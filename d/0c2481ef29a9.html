Wiki-LLaVA: Hierarchical Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Multimodal LLMs

Davide Caffagni · 2024 · 9 sections
"Wiki-LLaVA: Hierarchical Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Multimodal LLMs" by Davide Caffagni, Federico Cocchi, Nicholas Moratelli, Sara Sarto, Marcella Cornia, Lorenzo Baraldi, Rita Cucchiara (2024), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.1109/cvprw63382.2024.00188

The document presents Wiki-LLaVA, a retrieval-augmented multimodal large language model designed to answer visual questions and generate dialogues that depend on external knowledge. It begins by noting that LLMs and MLLMs perform strongly in zero-shot language and vision-language tasks but struggle with highly specific, compositional, and long-tail information not reliably stored in their parameters. Wiki-LLaVA addresses this limitation by augmenting LLaVA with retrieved text from an external multimodal knowledge source, such as Wikipedia, without substantially changing the underlying MLLM structure. The paper situates the method among LLMs, MLLMs, retrieval augmentation, and knowledge-based visual question answering. Its central mechanism is a hierarchical retrieval pipeline: CLIP-based image–title similarity first identifies relevant documents, then Contriever-based question-driven retrieval selects relevant passages. These passages are added to the model input alongside the visual and textual query. Training may fine-tune the system on knowledge-dependent question-answer pairs while mixing in examples that do not require external knowledge. Experiments evaluate Wiki-LLaVA on Encyclopedic-VQA and InfoSeek, studying Wikipedia retrieval, the contribution of retrieved context, and LLaVA-1.5 fine-tuning with dataset and LLaVA-Instruct samples. Results indicate that external knowledge substantially improves answers when the correct entity is retrieved, while incorrect retrieval can add harmful noise; oracle entities yield larger gains. The work also examines retention of general MLLM abilities. It concludes that hierarchical document and passage retrieval effectively augments MLLMs, while improved retrieval embeddings and document-selection methods remain key future directions. The work acknowledges CINECA, research grants involving Leonardo S.p.A. and Altilia s.r.l., FAIR, ITSERR, the European Commission, and NextGenerationEU.

Abstract body

The paper presents Wiki-LLaVA, an approach for enabling multimodal LLMs to answer questions that require external knowledge. Wiki-LLaVA integrates an external knowledge source of multimodal documents accessed through a hierarchical retrieval pipeline. Retrieved passages are provided as additional context to the LLM, improving the effectiveness and precision of generated dialogues. Experiments on datasets tailored for visual question answering with external data demonstrate the appropriateness of the approach.

1. Introduction body

The unit describes how Large Language Models have achieved strong zero-shot performance and how Multimodal Large Language Models extend these capabilities to vision-and-language tasks. It explains that MLLMs struggle with highly specific queries, compositional reasoning, and long-tail information that is difficult to encode in model parameters. The paper introduces Wiki-LLaVA, an MLLM augmented with a retrieval module that uses hierarchical retrieval to obtain relevant passages from an external knowledge base. The retrieved knowledge is provided to the MLLM without changing its structure, improving its ability to answer questions requiring external knowledge.

The unit reviews the development of large language models and multimodal large language models, including LLaVA and other systems that connect visual inputs with language generation. It describes retrieval augmentation as a way to provide language and vision-and-language models with external knowledge. It focuses on knowledge-based visual question answering, where systems retrieve entities and relevant passages to answer questions requiring specific knowledge. It presents Wiki-LLaVA, which uses a hierarchical retrieval pipeline based on CLIP and Contriever to augment a multimodal large language model with multimodal documents.

3. Proposed Method .. 3.2. Training body

Wiki-LLaVA equips multimodal large language models with external knowledge by augmenting LLaVA with retrieved textual context rather than substantially changing its design. It uses a visual encoder, an external knowledge base such as Wikipedia, and a hierarchical retrieval module to retrieve relevant documents and passages. The system first retrieves documents using image–title similarity and then retrieves passages using the user's question. Training can fine-tune the model on knowledge-dependent question–answer pairs while mixing them with pairs that do not require external knowledge.

4. Experiments / 4.1. Datasets .. 4.5. Limitations and Future Works body

This unit evaluates Wiki-LLaVA, a retrieval-augmented MLLM, on the Encyclopedic-VQA and InfoSeek visual question-answering datasets. It studies CLIP-based hierarchical retrieval from Wikipedia knowledge bases, the effects of retrieved textual context, and LLaVA-1.5 fine-tuning with dataset samples and LLaVA-Instruct data. External knowledge substantially improves performance when the correct entity is retrieved, while incorrect retrieval can introduce noisy context; oracle entities produce larger gains. The experiments also assess preservation of general MLLM abilities and identify improved retrieval embeddings and document-selection mechanisms as key future directions.

5. Conclusion body

The conclusion presents Wiki-LLaVA, an architecture for augmenting an existing MLLM with external knowledge. Wiki-LLaVA uses an external knowledge source of documents to improve the MLLM’s performance on questions and dialogues. Its hierarchical architecture retrieves documents and selects relevant parts for inclusion in the MLLM input context. Experiments demonstrate the solution’s effectiveness and its ability to maintain the MLLM’s proficiency across different tasks.
