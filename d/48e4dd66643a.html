The Marvelous Land of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1904-06 · 27 sections

The Marvelous Land of Oz is a sequel written by L. Frank Baum in response to children asking for more about the Scarecrow and Tin Woodman. It begins with Tip, a youth raised by the witch Mombi in the Gillikin Country. Fearing Mombi will make him drink a potion that turns him to marble, Tip escapes with Jack Pumpkinhead, a figure he has made and Mombi animates with the Powder of Life. Tip also brings a wooden Saw-Horse to life, and they head for the Emerald City. Jack reaches the Scarecrow, ruler of the city, just as General Jinjur and her all-girl Army of Revolt capture it without bloodshed. Tip warns the Scarecrow, who escapes with Jack and the Soldier with the Green Whiskers. They seek aid from Nick Chopper, the nickel-plated Tin Woodman and Emperor of the Winkies. Nick joins their effort to recover the city, but Mombi uses illusions to hinder them. Along the way they gain the help of the highly educated Woggle-Bug and the Queen of the Field Mice. Though they enter the city, Jinjur traps them; hidden mice drive away the girl soldiers, but the party remains surrounded. The Scarecrow devises an aerial escape. Nick constructs the Gump from sofas, a Gump’s head, palm-leaf wings, and a broom tail, and Tip animates it. The travelers fly south to consult Glinda the Good, surviving a crash in a Jackdaw’s nest and using wishing pills to repair the Gump. Glinda explains that Ozma, daughter of Pastoria, is the rightful ruler and suspects Mombi helped hide her. Glinda marches on the Emerald City, captures Mombi after the witch disguises herself as Jellia Jamb, a rose, a Shadow, an ant, and a Griffin, and compels her confession. Mombi reveals that the Wizard gave her the infant Ozma, whom she transformed into Tip. When Mombi reverses the spell, Tip becomes Princess Ozma. Jinjur is finally captured by the Gump, her army surrenders, and Ozma takes the throne. She restores stolen jewels, disbands the Army of Revolt, releases Jinjur after a promise of good behavior, and rules wisely. The Gump is dismantled at its request, and Ozma declares that contentment is the only true wealth.

The Marvelous Land of Oz body

L. Frank Baum explains that he wrote The Marvelous Land of Oz after receiving many letters from children asking for more about the Scarecrow and the Tin Woodman. He says he promised a little girl named Dorothy that he would write the book after receiving a thousand such letters, and he dedicates the book to David C. Montgomery and Frank A. Stone. The text includes a list of chapters concerning characters and events in the land of Oz, including Tip, Pumpkinhead, the Scarecrow, the Tin Woodman, Old Mombi, Glinda the Good, and Princess Ozma of Oz.

Tip Manufactures a Pumpkinhead body

Tip is a youth raised by the sorceress Mombi in the Country of the Gillikins. While Mombi is away, Tip carves a pumpkin face, builds a wooden body, and dresses it in clothing taken from Mombi’s chest. He names his creation Jack Pumpkinhead and plans to use it to frighten Mombi.

The Marvelous Powder of Life body

Tip places Jack Pumpkinhead at a bend in the road and repairs him after his arm falls off and his head twists around. Mombi returns with new magical supplies, uses the Powder of Life to animate Jack, and takes him home with Tip. Mombi then prepares a potion of milk, vinegar, herbs, and powders that she intends to make Tip drink so he will be transformed into a marble statue. Tip remains awake after Mombi locks the potion in her room, fearing what will happen at daybreak.

The Flight of the Fugitives body

Tip escapes from Mombi’s house after taking bread, cheese, and the Powder of Life because he fears being turned into a marble statue. He frees Jack Pumpkinhead, and the two travel south toward the Emerald City. Along the way, Tip explains the history and rulers of the Land of Oz to Jack, including the Scarecrow’s rule of the Emerald City.

Tip Makes an Experiment in Magic body

Tip travels with Jack Pumpkinhead, whose wooden joints are weak and wear out when he walks or sits. After Jack points out that a wooden sawhorse resembles a real horse, Tip decides to use the Powder of Life on it so Jack can ride it. Tip imitates Mombi’s words and gestures while sprinkling powder from the pepper-box, and the saw-horse comes to life.

The Awakening of the Saw-horse body

Tip brings the Saw-Horse to life, but the creature initially cannot control its stiff legs, hear commands, or understand its surroundings. Tip gives the Saw-Horse bark ears, teaches it commands, and equips it with a post for Jack Pumpkinhead to hold while riding. The group begins its journey to the Emerald City, following a yellow-brick roadway marked as nine miles from the city. At nightfall, Tip makes camp and asks the Saw-Horse to stand guard while he and Jack sleep.

Jack Pumpkinhead’s Ride to the Emerald City body

Tip, Jack Pumpkinhead, and the Saw-Horse resume their journey to the Emerald City and cross a broad river by using the Saw-Horse as a boat. Tip falls behind when the Saw-Horse races ahead, while Jack Pumpkinhead and the Saw-Horse reach the Emerald City. The Guardian of the Gates and the Soldier with the Green Whiskers bring Jack and the Saw-Horse into the city and escort them toward the royal palace. Jack is taken to meet the Scarecrow, who rules the Emerald City.

His Majesty the Scarecrow body

His Majesty the Scarecrow meets Jack Pumpkinhead, who has come from the Country of the Gillikins and is surprised by the Scarecrow’s appearance. Believing they speak different languages, the Scarecrow summons an interpreter, and the Soldier with the Green Whiskers brings Jellia Jamb. Jellia reveals that all of Oz speaks one language, making an interpreter unnecessary, while amusingly mistranslating their remarks. The Scarecrow and Jack become friends and go to the courtyard to play quoits.

Gen. Jinjur’s Army of Revolt body

Tip meets General Jinjur, who commands an Army of Revolt made up entirely of girls seeking to overthrow the Scarecrow and take control of the Emerald City. The army, armed with knitting-needles, marches to the city and demands its surrender. The Guardian of the Gate is overcome, and the Soldier with the Green Whiskers flees after admitting that his gun is unloaded. General Jinjur and her army capture the Emerald City without bloodshed.

The Scarecrow Plans an escape body

Tip follows the Soldier with the Green Whiskers into the conquered Emerald City and warns the Scarecrow that the invading girls plan to use his straw and stuffing. The Scarecrow, Jack Pumpkinhead, and the Soldier decide to escape on the Saw-Horse and ride toward the Tin Woodman’s kingdom. They break through the palace guards and the city gate, but the Saw-Horse carries them to a wide river and leaps into the water.

The Journey to the Tin Woodman body

Tip, Jack Pumpkinhead, the Scarecrow, and the Saw-Horse cross a river and reach the opposite bank. Jack’s pumpkin head floats away, but Tip retrieves it and replaces it, while he dries and repairs the Scarecrow. They resume their journey, recalling past adventures and heading to greet the Tin Woodman. At night, Tip sleeps on the grass while his companions keep watch because they do not sleep.

A Nickel-Plated Emperor body

Tip, the Scarecrow, Jack Pumpkinhead, and the Saw-Horse reach the Winkies’ capital and visit Nick Chopper, the Tin Woodman and Emperor of the Winkies. Nick Chopper is nickel-plated and welcomes the Scarecrow, though his putz-pomade rubs onto the Scarecrow before both are cleaned and polished. After learning that the girls of Oz have driven the Scarecrow from the Emerald City, Nick Chopper agrees to help recapture it. The group prepares for the journey and sets out toward the Emerald City, with Nick Chopper leading and the others accompanying him.

Mr. H. M. Woggle-Bug, T. E. body

General Jinjur summons Mombi to help defend the Emerald City against the Scarecrow and Tin Woodman, while Mombi uses magic to obstruct their Journey to the Emerald City. The travelers encounter an illusory field of sunflowers, then lose their way and suffer the Saw-Horse's broken leg. They meet Mr. H. M. Woggle-Bug, T. E., a highly magnified and thoroughly educated insect, who agrees to relate his history.

A Highly Magnified History body

The Woggle-Bug recounts how Professor Nowitall captured him, projected him in a highly magnified state with a magnifying-glass, and accidentally enabled his escape from the school house. He joins the Scarecrow, Tin Woodman, Saw-Horse, Pumpkinhead, and Tip on their journey toward the Emerald City. The Woggle-Bug helps solve the Saw-Horse’s broken-leg problem by suggesting that Pumpkinhead’s wooden leg be reshaped for the repair.

Old Mombi indulges in Witchcraft body

Tip and his companions travel toward the Emerald City while Old Mombi uses deceptive magic to obstruct them. The Queen of the Field Mice guides them and sends twelve Field Mice to hide in the Scarecrow so they can help him regain his throne. Mombi's illusions include a river, a granite wall, spinning roads, and a sheet of flame, but the travelers pass through them safely by following the Queen. The group reaches the gates of the Emerald City, and the Queen returns home.

The Prisoners of the Queen body

The Scarecrow, the Tin Woodman, Tip, and their companions enter the Emerald City to restore the Scarecrow to the throne, but General Jinjur and the Army of Revolt capture them in the throne room. The Tin Woodman frightens the guards at the gateway, yet the apparent ease of their progress proves to be a trap. General Jinjur orders the nonhuman prisoners destroyed, but the Scarecrow releases mice that panic the Army of Revolt and drive it from the palace. The Scarecrow's party bars the palace entrances and gathers in the throne room to plan what to do next.

The Scarecrow Takes Time to Think body

The Scarecrow realizes that Jinjur remains Queen and that he and his companions are prisoners in her palace, which is surrounded by girl soldiers. After learning about Tip’s nearly exhausted Powder of Life, he thinks of escaping through the air rather than through the guarded doors or underground. He proposes that Nick Chopper build a winged machine on the palace roof and that Tip bring it to life with the powder. Nick repairs Jack Pumpkinhead’s damaged leg, and the companions begin gathering materials for the aerial machine.

The Astonishing Flight of the Gump body

The adventurers assemble miscellaneous materials on the roof to build a flying machine. Nick Chopper binds two sofas together, attaches a Gump’s head, palm-leaf wings, and Jack Pumpkinhead’s broom as a tail, then Tip uses magic powder to animate it. The Gump’s head comes to life and directs the machine, which rises from the roof but returns when Tip commands it.

In the Jackdaw’s Nest body

Tip, the Scarecrow, the Tin Woodman, the Woggle-Bug, Jack Pumpkinhead, and their companions bring the newly revived Gump to life as a flying Thing and travel south to seek Glinda the Good’s advice. During the flight, they discover three of Dr. Nikidik’s wishing pills hidden in a false-bottomed pepper-box, but believe the directions cannot be followed because seventeen cannot be counted by twos. The Gump loses its way and crashes in a mountain range, leaving the travelers stranded in a Jackdaw’s nest. When the Jackdaws return, the Scarecrow hides Tip, Jack Pumpkinhead’s head, and the Woggle-Bug beneath straw.

Dr. Nikidik’s Famous Wishing Pills body

The Tin Woodman, Tip, the Woggle-Bug, the Scarecrow, the Saw-Horse, and the Gump survive a Jackdaw attack in their nest, but the Scarecrow loses all his straw. They restuff the Scarecrow with stolen money found in the nest. Tip’s first wish reverses his swallowing of a pill, while the Woggle-Bug successfully uses another pill to repair the Gump’s broken wings. The group learns that counting seventeen by twos can begin with one-half.

The Scarecrow Appeals to Glinda the Good body

The Scarecrow and his companions escape the Jackdaws' nest in the Gump but lose the pepper-box containing the remaining Wishing Pills. They reach Glinda the Good's palace and ask her to help remove Queen Jinjur from the Emerald City. Glinda explains that the throne rightfully belongs to Ozma, daughter of Pastoria, and begins investigating the Wizard of Oz's role in hiding her. After identifying Mombi as a likely accomplice, Glinda decides to march on the Emerald City and capture her.

The Tin-Woodman Plucks a Rose body

Glinda the Good leads her army to the Emerald City and demands that Queen Jinjur surrender Mombi. Mombi deceives Glinda by enchanting Jellia Jamb to resemble her and then transforming herself into a red rose. Glinda discovers the first deception, but her search of the city fails to find the disguised Mombi. The Tin Woodman unknowingly plucks the rose containing Mombi and carries her into Glinda’s camp.

The Transformation of Old Mombi body

Mombi hides as a rose in the Tin Woodman’s button-hole, believing this will help her escape Glinda. Glinda discovers her, but Mombi transforms into a Shadow, a Black Ant, and finally a Griffin to flee. Glinda pursues her on the Saw-Horse, and the exhausted Griffin collapses at the desert’s edge. Glinda uses a golden thread to destroy the transformation’s magic and reveal Mombi in her true form.

Princess Ozma of Oz body

Glinda captures Mombi and forces her to reveal that the Wonderful Wizard of Oz brought the infant Princess Ozma to her for concealment. Mombi admits that she transformed Ozma into a boy, whom she raised as Tip, and that Ozma is the rightful ruler of the Emerald City. Mombi reverses the spell, restoring Tip to Princess Ozma, who remains the same person despite her changed appearance.

The Riches of Content body

Queen Jinjur resists Glinda the Good and Princess Ozma after losing power, but the Gump flies over the Emerald City's walls and captures her, ending the war. The Army of Revolt surrenders, the Emerald City welcomes Glinda's army, and Princess Ozma takes the throne. Ozma restores the city's stolen jewels, disbands the Army of Revolt, releases Jinjur on promise of good behavior, and rules with wisdom and justice. The Gump is taken apart at its own request, while the Scarecrow and the Tin Woodman discuss whether brains or a good heart make someone truly rich; Ozma concludes that the riches of content are the only riches worth having.
