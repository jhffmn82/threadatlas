Achieving Higher Factual Accuracy in Llama LLM with Weighted Distribution of Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Zhenhua Gai · 2024-05-21 · 8 sections
"Achieving Higher Factual Accuracy in Llama LLM with Weighted Distribution of Retrieval-Augmented Generation" by 盖振华, Lianxin Tong, Quan Ge (2024), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.31219/osf.io/ctw8v

This paper by Zhenhua Gai, Lianxin Tong, and Quan Ge investigates improving the factual accuracy and contextual relevance of the Llama Large Language Model through a weighted distribution of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). It begins by identifying hallucination—the production of fluent but incorrect or nonsensical content—as a central limitation of LLMs, and presents RAG as a means of grounding generation in external documents. The literature review covers hallucination mitigation, RAG’s benefits and challenges, and benchmarks and metrics including Google BIG-Bench, GLUE, SuperGLUE, precision, recall, F1, and BLEU. The methodology integrates RAG with Llama: relevant documents are retrieved, and documents considered more relevant and reliable receive greater influence through weighting. Experiments use the Google BIG-Bench dataset and NVIDIA A100 GPUs with TensorFlow, PyTorch, Hugging Face Transformers, and FAISS; evaluation combines precision, recall, F1, BLEU, and human assessments. Results show that weighted RAG outperforms baseline Llama Large on all reported quantitative measures and on human-rated factual correctness and contextual relevance. Error analysis reports reductions in ambiguous-query errors, contextual irrelevance, superficial accuracy, specialized-knowledge and long-tail-query errors, and over-reliance on high-weight documents, though these issues remain. The discussion highlights possible NLP uses in legal, medical, news, education, customer support, and content generation, while stressing the value of human evaluation alongside metrics. The paper concludes that its scalable document-weighting framework improves reliability, but calls for adaptive and context-aware weighting, advanced retrieval, semantic understanding, disambiguation, multilingual settings, real-time feedback, and domain-specific corpora.
