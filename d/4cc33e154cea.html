VideoRAG: Retrieval-Augmented Generation over Video Corpus

Soyeong Jeong · 2025 · 16 sections
"VideoRAG: Retrieval-Augmented Generation over Video Corpus" by Soyeong Jeong, Kangsan Kim, Jinheon Baek, Sung Ju Hwang (2025), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.findings-acl.1096

The document presents VideoRAG, a retrieval-augmented generation framework that treats a video corpus as external knowledge. Rather than relying chiefly on text, images, or textual descriptions of videos, it dynamically retrieves videos relevant to a user query and generates answers grounded in both their visual and textual content. Its central motivation is that videos preserve temporal dynamics, spatial details, and multimodal cues that other RAG sources can miss. VideoRAG is enabled by Large Video Language Models, which represent and understand videos for retrieval and response generation. The pipeline retrieves query-relevant videos, uses adaptive frame selection to retain informative frames while reducing redundancy in long videos, and integrates selected visual material with available text. When subtitles or descriptions are unavailable, it uses automatic speech recognition to produce auxiliary transcripts. Frame-space reduction through clustering is also part of the approach. The evaluation builds question-answering data by linking WikiHowQA query-answer pairs with HowTo100M query-video pairs, alongside an automatically collected synthetic dataset. Across reported metrics, VideoRAG variants—especially VIDEORAG-V and VIDEORAG-VT—generally outperform textual, text-image, and text-video RAG baselines. Results indicate that visual features, textual features, and their combination improve retrieval and generation; adaptive frame selection benefits both; and larger LVLMs generally perform better. Human evaluation favors VIDEORAG-VT. Oracle retrieval results suggest that stronger video retrieval could yield further gains. A case study illustrates that retrieved video supports a detailed answer about baking cookies on a car dashboard. The document concludes that VideoRAG provides a more holistic video-based RAG system and that its experiments validate advantages over existing methods. It also notes limits: full-pipeline evaluation needs query-video-answer triples, but suitable datasets remain scarce. Future work includes developing and releasing benchmark datasets and improving frame selection. Finally, it warns that biased or harmful material in a video corpus, as well as biases inherited by LVLMs, can affect generated answers; practitioners should assess these risks and use measures such as bias detection and filtering. The work received support from several Korean government and institutional grant programs.

The conclusion presents VideoRAG, a framework that uses a video corpus as an external knowledge source for retrieval-augmented generation. VideoRAG retrieves videos by query relevance and integrates their visual and textual content, using adaptive frame selection to improve effectiveness and efficiency. The analysis finds that visual features, textual features, or both improve retrieval and generation performance, while automatic speech recognition can generate transcripts for videos lacking textual features. Experiments validate VideoRAG's superiority over existing RAG methods and support its use in holistic systems that utilize videos.

Limitations body

The unit concerns VideoRAG, which operationalizes a full RAG pipeline over video corpora by dynamically retrieving query-relevant videos and generating answers grounded in them. Evaluating this pipeline requires triples linking queries, relevant videos, and ground-truth answers, but such datasets are currently limited. The authors construct a dataset by associating WikiHowQA query-answer pairs with HowTo100M query-video pairs and also automatically collect a synthetic dataset. They identify developing and releasing a benchmark dataset, as well as further improving the frame selection strategy, as valuable future work.

Ethics Statement body

VideoRAG is designed to answer user queries by retrieving relevant videos from a large video corpus. The retrieval process depends on the corpus, so biased, harmful, or otherwise problematic examples may cause generated answers to reflect those issues. LVLMs power the generation process and may inherit or amplify biases from the vast multimodal datasets used to train them. Practitioners are advised to evaluate these potential risks and consider mitigation strategies such as bias detection and filtering.
