The Case-Book of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1921-10 · 37 sections

This document is a prefaced collection of Sherlock Holmes cases, framed by Arthur Conan Doyle’s farewell to Holmes and Watson. Doyle recalls Holmes’s progress from A Study in Scarlet and The Sign of Four through the Strand stories, explains that public demand brought Holmes back after The Memoirs, and thanks readers. The cases follow Holmes, usually with Dr. John Watson, through crimes, disappearances, threats, and apparent supernatural mysteries. Holmes prevents Violet de Merville’s marriage to the corrupt Baron Adelbert Gruner by obtaining Gruner’s incriminating record of women he ruined; Kitty Winter disfigures Gruner and the marriage is canceled. He finds that the secretly confined Godfrey Emsworth has non-infective pseudo-leprosy rather than leprosy, recovers the Mazarin Stone from Count Negretto Sylvius and Sam Merton, and protects Mrs. Maberley after Isadora Klein orders the destruction of Douglas Maberley’s manuscript. Several cases overturn misleading appearances: the supposed Sussex vampire is a mother removing poison after Jacky Ferguson attacks a baby; John Garrideb is the criminal James Winter, alias Killer Evans, seeking a counterfeiting cellar; and Miss Grace Dunbar is cleared when Holmes proves that Mrs. Gibson engineered evidence around her own death. Holmes discovers that Professor Presbury’s animal-like behavior comes from a rejuvenation treatment involving black-faced Langur serum, and that Fitzroy McPherson was killed accidentally by Cyanea capillata, the “Lion’s Mane,” rather than by Ian Murdoch. Later, Eugenia Ronder confesses her part in a plot against her abusive husband, ending the Abbas Parva matter; Holmes uncovers Lady Beatrice Falder’s death and Mr. Norlett’s impersonation of her at Shoscombe Old Place; and, in the final case, he exposes Josiah Amberley’s fabricated story that his wife and Dr. Ray Ernest fled with his money. Amberley had murdered them in his strong-room, but Holmes, police, and Inspector MacKinnon arrest him and stop his attempted suicide. Across the collection, Holmes repeatedly uses observation, disguises, experiments, and psychological pressure to reveal concealed motives and protect the innocent.

PREFACE body

Arthur Conan Doyle bids farewell to Sherlock Holmes, expressing hope that Holmes and Watson may find a place in an imagined Valhalla. He recounts Holmes's career from his debut in A Study in Scarlet and The Sign of Four through the short stories published in The Strand Magazine and the collections that followed. Doyle explains that he once ended Holmes's career after The Memoirs but later revived him in response to public demand. He thanks readers for their loyalty and for the distraction and change of thought provided by the stories.

During a visit to Tuxbury Old Hall, Dodd is rebuffed by the Colonel and hears the butler Ralph say that he wishes Godfrey were dead. THE ADVENTURE OF THE MAZARIN STONE body

Dr. John Watson visits Sherlock Holmes at Baker Street while Holmes investigates the burglary of the Crown diamond, also called the Mazarin Stone. Holmes explains that Count Negretto Sylvius and Sam Merton are involved, and that Sylvius has come intending to kill him. Using a wax dummy, disguises, and evidence of the criminals' activities, Holmes confronts Sylvius and persuades him that surrendering the stone may secure his freedom. Holmes sends Watson to fetch the police and brings Merton into the discussion before giving the two criminals five minutes to decide whether to surrender the stone or face arrest.

Holmes seizes the stone at the window, holds the criminals at gunpoint, and has the waiting police arrest them. Lord Cantlemere then visits, and Holmes secretly places the stone in his overcoat pocket to demonstrate how possession would establish a case against a receiver. Holmes retrieves the stone and sends Lord Cantlemere away, while arranging dinner with Watson.

A telegram summons Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson to The Three Gables after Mrs. Maberley is chloroformed during a burglary, which the police attribute to Barney Stockdale's gang. Holmes recognizes that the burglars were searching for Douglas Maberley's manuscript and traces the crime to Isadora Klein, Douglas's former lover. Klein admits ordering the burglary and destroying the manuscript, claiming it threatened her impending marriage and social position. Holmes agrees not to expose her in exchange for a £5,000 cheque to fund Mrs. Maberley's travels, while warning Klein to be careful.

Holmes and Watson receive a referral concerning Robert Ferguson, who seeks help for an unnamed friend whose Peruvian wife allegedly attacks his children and drinks the baby's blood. The husband has confined his wife to her room while Mrs. Mason guards the baby, but he remains worried about Jack, whom the wife has assaulted twice. Ferguson visits Holmes and Watson, explains the family circumstances, and asks for advice. Holmes identifies inconsistencies in the accounts and agrees to investigate at Cheeseman's in Lamberley.

Sherlock Holmes and Dr. John Watson visit Robert Ferguson's isolated farmhouse to investigate apparent vampirism involving Ferguson's wife and baby. Holmes determines that Ferguson's wife sucked poison from the baby's wound after Jacky Ferguson attacked the child with a poisoned arrow, likely dipped in curare or another drug. He also identifies Jacky's jealousy and hatred toward the baby and advises sending him to sea. Holmes's investigation resolves the Ferguson case and leads him to answer the original letter from Baker Street.

THE ADVENTURE OF THE THREE GARRIDEBS body

In June 1902, Sherlock Holmes and Dr. John Watson become involved in John Garrideb's search for two other men named Garrideb who can claim a fortune under Alexander Hamilton Garrideb's will. John tells Holmes that the inheritance requires three adult Garridebs, while Holmes suspects that John's story contains numerous lies. Nathan Garrideb, a reclusive collector living in London, confirms John's account and eagerly hopes to obtain the money. John then produces an advertisement for Howard Garrideb in Birmingham and persuades Nathan to travel there to verify Howard's identity.

Sherlock Holmes discovers that John Garrideb, an American lawyer, is James Winter, alias Killer Evans, a dangerous criminal who murdered Rodger Prescott. Holmes and Dr. John Watson discover that Evans invented the three Garridebs deception to remove Nathan Garrideb from his apartment, where Prescott had hidden a counterfeiting operation. Evans opens a secret cellar, fires at Watson, and is subdued and arrested after Holmes finds Prescott's printing equipment and counterfeit banknotes. Nathan Garrideb is left devastated when his hopes of inheriting a fortune collapse.

John H. Watson recounts how Sherlock Holmes is asked to defend Miss Dunbar, a governess accused of killing Neil Gibson's wife at Thor Bridge. The victim was found near Thor Mere with a revolver bullet through her brain, while a matching revolver was discovered in Miss Dunbar's wardrobe and a note from her arranged the meeting. Estate manager Marlow Bates warns Holmes that Gibson was brutal to his wife, while Gibson insists that Miss Dunbar is innocent and eventually reveals the history of his marriage to Maria Pinto. Holmes suspects that Gibson has concealed the true nature of his relationship with Miss Dunbar and uses a bluff to make him disclose further facts.

Holmes criticizes the millionaire for trying to ruin a defenseless young woman under his protection and says that the millionaire's conduct may be no better than what Grace is accused of doing. Trevor Bennett reports that Presbury received secret letters, guarded a wooden box, was attacked by his wolf-hound Roy, and was seen crawling through the house at night. Holmes sends Mercer to investigate Dorak and theorizes that Presbury takes a poisonous drug every nine days, a habit learned in Prague. Holmes and the others discover letters, a syringe, and serum bottles showing that Presbury has been receiving a treatment from H. Lowenstein of Prague involving black-faced Langur serum. Holmes concludes that the treatment was intended to rejuvenate Presbury but caused him to revert to animal behavior, and he plans to hold Lowenstein responsible.

THE ADVENTURE OF THE LION'S MANE body

After retiring to Sussex, Mr. Holmes investigates the death of Fitzroy McPherson, who is found dying near the beach after suffering terrible unexplained injuries. McPherson’s last words appear to identify the “Lion’s Mane,” while his body bears long, curved red weals resembling scourge marks. Suspicion falls partly on Ian Murdoch, who had visited The Haven, was dismissed by Harold Stackhurst, and may have had feelings for Maud Bellamy. Holmes and Stackhurst learn that McPherson and Maud were secretly engaged and begin examining the possible motives and circumstances surrounding the assault.

Sherlock Holmes connects the deaths of Fitzroy McPherson and McPherson's dog, along with Ian Murdoch's sudden illness, to a deadly jellyfish in the bathing-pool. He identifies the creature as Cyanea capillata, whose radiating filaments produce the distinctive reticulated sting pattern and severe pain. The phrase “Lion's Mane,” spoken by McPherson before his death, is recognized as a warning describing the creature. Cyanea capillata is crushed beneath a boulder, McPherson's death is explained as an accidental envenomation, and Ian Murdoch is exonerated.

Sherlock Holmes and Dr. John Watson investigate the long-unresolved Abbas Parva tragedy after Mrs. Merrilow brings them to her veiled lodger, Eugenia Ronder. Eugenia explains that her husband, Ronder, abused her, and that she and Leonardo plotted to kill him with a spiked club and make the death appear to be a lion attack. Leonardo struck Ronder, but fled when Sahara King escaped from the lion's cage, leaving the lion to mutilate Eugenia's face. Eugenia concealed the truth to protect Leonardo and herself, and has since lived in isolation under a veil.

When Holmes sees her disfigured face, he urges her not to take her own life. THE ADVENTURE OF SHOSCOMBE OLD PLACE body

Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson investigate troubling events at Shoscombe Old Place after John Mason reports that Sir Robert Norberton has become obsessed with winning the Derby, estranged from Lady Beatrice Falder, and visited a haunted crypt with an unknown man. Evidence includes Lady Beatrice's unexplained change in behavior, the removal of her beloved spaniel, mummy remains in the crypt, and a charred fragment of human bone from the central heating furnace. Holmes develops two possible lines of inquiry: a quarrel involving Lady Beatrice's maid, Carrie Evans, and the possibility that Sir Robert has killed Lady Beatrice and arranged for someone to impersonate her. Holmes and Watson travel to the Green Dragon near Shoscombe Old Place disguised as fishermen to investigate further.

Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson investigate the apparent personation of Lady Beatrice at Shoscombe Old Place. Holmes uses Lady Beatrice's black spaniel to establish that the carriage occupant is a stranger and later discovers Lady Beatrice's body hidden in an ancestral coffin in the chapel crypt. Sir Robert admits that Lady Beatrice died a week earlier and that Mr. Norlett had been personating her to delay his creditors until the Derby. Shoscombe Prince wins the Derby, allowing Sir Robert to pay his creditors, while the police and coroner treat the deception leniently.

THE ADVENTURE OF THE RETIRED COLOURMAN body

Sherlock Holmes asks Dr. John Watson to investigate Josiah Amberley's claim that Mrs. Amberley and Dr. Ray Ernest disappeared together after taking money and securities from his strong-room. Watson visits The Haven in Lewisham, hears Amberley's account, and observes the neglected house, Amberley's artificial limb, and a tall, dark man who appears to follow him. Holmes identifies several overlooked details and obtains information confirming Amberley's reputation, his wealth, and Dr. Ernest's association with him and Mrs. Amberley. Although the case initially seems simple, Holmes concludes that it has developed serious complications.
