Enhancing Retrieval-Augmented Large Language Models with Iterative Retrieval-Generation Synergy

Zhihong Shao, Yeyun Gong, Yelong Shen, Minlie Huang, Nan Duan, Weizhu Chen · 2023 · 15 sections
"Enhancing Retrieval-Augmented Large Language Models with Iterative Retrieval-Generation Synergy" by Zhihong Shao, Yeyun Gong, Yelong Shen, Minlie Huang, Nan Duan, Weizhu Chen (2023), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2023.findings-emnlp.620

This paper presents ITER-RETGEN, an iterative retrieval-augmented generation framework for improving Generative Large Language Models whose knowledge may be outdated and whose outputs may hallucinate. Given a question, a retrieval corpus, and an LLM, it repeats retrieval and generation for T iterations: it retrieves top-k paragraphs using the question and the previous generated output, then generates a new response from the question plus all retrieved knowledge. Its central idea is that an initial response, including its reasoning, can bridge semantic gaps between a question and supporting evidence, enabling better retrieval in later iterations. Unlike approaches that interrupt a single output by interleaving retrieval and generation, ITER-RETGEN processes retrieved knowledge as a whole and retains flexibility in generation. The work also introduces a generation-augmented retrieval adaptation that uses LLM generations and a re-ranker to distill improvements into a dense retriever’s query encoder. Across six datasets in multi-hop question answering, fact verification, and commonsense reasoning, ITER-RETGEN generally matches or exceeds retrieval-augmented baselines, with up to 8.6% absolute gains on four datasets. Two iterations often produce the strongest improvements, while requiring fewer API calls and retrieved paragraphs than ReAct and Self-Ask. Examples from HotPotQA and StrategyQA show that initially incomplete or distractive retrieval can still yield generated phrases that locate missing evidence in a second iteration and correct factual errors. The analysis argues that Acc† from text-davinci-003 can better capture semantic correctness than exact match. Failures chiefly stem from misleading initial reasoning or incomplete retrieval. The paper concludes that this simple workflow is a strong baseline for complex information needs, and that retrieval adaptation can further improve performance while reducing overhead. Limitations include use of a fixed black-box LLM that may not equally suit prompting methods, and no evaluation of long-form generation, which may need finer-grained retrieval. Zhihong Shao and Minlie Huang’s work received support from the National Science Foundation for Distinguished Young Scholars, NSFC projects, and the Guoqiang Institute of Tsinghua University.

Abstract body

The paper concerns ITER-RETGEN, an iterative method for retrieval-augmented generation that synergizes retrieval and generation to improve responses. It addresses limitations of large language models, including outdated knowledge and hallucinations, by using a model’s initial response to guide retrieval of more relevant knowledge. Unlike methods that interleave retrieval with generation within a single output, ITER-RETGEN processes all retrieved knowledge as a whole and preserves flexibility in generation. Evaluations on multi-hop question answering, fact verification, and commonsense reasoning show that it is superior or competitive with retrieval-augmented baselines while requiring fewer retrieval and generation operations.

1 body

The unit examines how Generative Large Language Models can be improved by grounding their outputs in external knowledge. It introduces ITER-RETGEN, which iteratively combines retrieval-augmented generation with generation-augmented retrieval so that generated responses help identify additional relevant knowledge. The method is evaluated on multi-hop question answering, fact verification, and commonsense reasoning, achieving up to 8.6% absolute gains over prior retrieval-augmented methods on four of six datasets. The unit also reports that LLM-based evaluation may be more reliable than exact match and that two retrieval-generation iterations often provide the strongest gains.

2 body

The unit reviews limitations of LLMs, including difficult continual updating and hallucination, and describes efforts to augment them with external tools. It focuses on retrieval augmentation, contrasting passive retrieval with methods in which LLMs generate queries or other context to improve relevance. ITER-RETGEN interleaves retrieval and generation across iterations without interrupting a single generation, using prior outputs to retrieve more relevant knowledge. The unit states that ITER-RETGEN is conceptually simple and empirically strong on multi-hop question answering, fact verification, and commonsense reasoning.

3 / 3.1 .. 3.4 body

ITER-RETGEN is an iterative framework that repeats retrieval-generation for T iterations using a question q, a retrieval corpus D, and an LLM M. Each iteration retrieves top-k paragraphs using the previous generation and q, then generates an output using the retrieved paragraphs and q; the final output is yT. Generation-Augmented Retrieval uses previous LLM outputs to bridge semantic gaps between questions and supporting knowledge, while Retrieval-Augmented Generation prepends retrieved knowledge to q during generation. The generation-augmented retrieval adaptation further uses an LLM generation and a re-ranker to optimize the query encoder of a dense retriever through distillation.

4 / 4.1 .. 4.7 body

The unit evaluates retrieval and reasoning methods across six datasets covering multi-hop question answering, fact verification, and commonsense reasoning. ITER-RETGEN generally outperforms or matches retrieval-augmented baselines, especially after two or more iterations, while using fewer API calls and retrieved paragraphs than ReAct and Self-Ask. The experiments show that generated reasoning can improve later retrieval, that Acc† from text-davinci-003 often better reflects semantic correctness than exact match, and that retrieval-generation synergy can correct initial factual errors. Error analysis finds that failures mainly arise from misleading first-iteration reasoning or incomplete retrieval.

6 body

The unit concludes that ITER-RETGEN is effective for answering questions with complex information needs. Despite having a simple workflow, ITER-RETGEN outperforms retrieval-augmented methods with more complex workflows. The authors suggest that ITER-RETGEN could serve as a strong baseline for future research on retrieval-augmented generation. They also find that generation-augmented retrieval adaptation can further improve ITER-RETGEN’s performance while reducing overheads.

Limitations body

The unit concerns ITER-RETGEN, a method for enhancing retrieval-augmented large language models by iteratively combining retrieval and generation. It reports that ITER-RETGEN performs strongly compared with structured prompting techniques such as Self-Ask, but the experiments used a fixed black-box large language model that may not have been equally optimized for different prompting methods. The work did not evaluate long-form generation, which may require more fine-grained retrieval, and identifies prompting-specific gradient-based optimization and long-form generation as areas for future research.
