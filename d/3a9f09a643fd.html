Integrating retrieval-augmented generation for enhanced personalized physician recommendations in web-based medical services: model development study

Frontiers in Public Health · 2025-01-29 · 15 sections
"Integrating retrieval-augmented generation for enhanced personalized physician recommendations in web-based medical services: model development study" by Yingbin Zheng, Yiwei Yan, Sai Chen, Yunping Cai, Kun Ren, Yishan Liu, Jiaying Zhuang, Min Zhao (2025), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1501408

This Frontiers in Public Health article by Yingbin Zheng, Yiwei Yan, Sai Chen, Yunping Cai, Kun Ren, Yishan Liu, Jiaying Zhuang, and Min Zhao develops and evaluates a Retrieval-Augmented Generation-Based Physician Recommendation (RAGPR) model for web-based medical services. It addresses the difficulty of matching patients to suitable physicians through personalized recommendations, contrasting RAG with manual triage, symptom checkers, decision trees, rule-based systems, collaborative filtering, and content-based filtering. The study uses 646,383 anonymized consultation records from the Internet Hospital of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xiamen University, covering 193,675 patients, 858 physicians, and 44 departments, plus 965 web-based medical documents from Hugging Face for testing. RAGPR embeds documents with Sentence-BERT, stores them in Chroma, retrieves six relevant queries using Maximal Marginal Relevance, and uses a locally constructed LLaMA-based large language model to generate department and physician recommendations. The authors assess performance with precision, recall, F1-score, and ratings by three triage staff members. The results compare FastText, SBERT, and OpenAI embeddings: OpenAI has the highest classification metrics, but SBERT is chosen for implementation because it balances performance with cost, data security, and ease of migration. Mistral, GPT-4o-mini, and GPT-4o are compared for recommendations. GPT-4o achieves the highest F1-score, while Mistral is selected as a practical, rational option. A case study indicates that RAGPR can infer relevant specialties from symptom descriptions and recommend corresponding departments and physicians. The discussion presents RAGPR as a means to improve the accuracy and efficiency of web-based consultation triage. It emphasizes implementation needs including infrastructure, staff training, privacy, interoperability, bias mitigation, and ongoing updating. Limitations are its single-hospital evaluation, irrelevant dataset questions, and limited transparency; proposed future work includes real-time data, multimodal inputs, and explainable models. The study was ethics-approved by the First Affiliated Hospital of Xiamen University Ethics Committee, used deidentified data, and reports no conflicts of interest.

4 Discussion / 4.1 Principal findings .. 4.5 Limitations and future directions body

Zheng et al. introduce the RAGPR model to improve the accuracy and efficiency of web-based medical consultations by matching patients’ queries with physicians’ specialties. The study finds that SBERT and Mistral are practical model choices, while SBERT provides a strong balance of performance, cost, data security, and ease of migration. The discussion addresses requirements for implementing RAGPR in healthcare, including infrastructure, training, privacy, interoperability, bias mitigation, and continuous updating. Limitations include evaluation in a single hospital, irrelevant questions in the dataset, and limited transparency, while future work includes real-time data, multimodal inputs, and explainable models.
