SimRAG: Self-Improving Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Adapting Large Language Models to Specialized Domains

Ran Xu, Hui Liu, Sreyashi Nag, Zhenwei Dai, Yaochen Xie, Xianfeng Tang, Chen Luo, Yang Li, Joyce C. Ho, Carl Yang, Qi He · 2025 · 16 sections

This unit is a paper in Volume 1: Long Papers of the Proceedings of the 2025 Conference of the Nations of the Americas Chapter of the Association for Computational Linguistics: Human Language Technologies, held April 29–May 4, 2025. Titled “SimRAG: Self-Improving Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Adapting Large Language Models to Specialized Domains,” it is by Ran Xu, Hui Liu, Sreyashi Nag, Zhenwei Dai, Yaochen Xie, Xianfeng Tang, Chen Luo, Yang Li, Joyce C. Ho, Carl Yang, and Qi He of Emory University and Amazon. The paper presents SimRAG, a self-training framework that adapts large language models for specialized-domain retrieval-augmented question answering in science, medicine, and computer science. SimRAG first conducts retrieval-oriented fine-tuning using general instruction, context-aware QA, and retrieval-related data. It then uses an unlabeled domain corpus: the same model generates candidate answers and questions, retrieves contexts, and applies round-trip-consistency filtering to retain pseudo-labeled question-answer tuples. These synthetic data are blended with general instruction and context-aware QA data for continued domain-adaptive training. The study evaluates SimRAG across 11 datasets, two backbone sizes, and three domains, including medical datasets from MIRAGE plus LiveQA and MedicationQA; scientific tasks from MMLU subsets, ARC-challenge, and SciQ; and CS-Bench. It compares GPT-3.5, GPT-4, Llama3-8B-Instruct, Gemma2-27B-Instruct, RAFT, EvidenceRAG, and SimRAG. Results show gains of 1.2%–8.6% over baselines; with Gemma2-27B, SimRAG approaches GPT-4 performance. Ablations find that both Stage-I retrieval-oriented fine-tuning and Stage-II self-synthesized tuples matter, while question diversity and filtering help further. Case studies show more relevant, self-contained, and challenging synthetic examples than Llama3-8B-it. Limitations include use of only one pseudo-labeling round, added training time, and the expense of stronger query generators. Research support came partly from the Emory Global Diabetes Center of the Woodruff Sciences Center at Emory University.

Abstract body

The abstract introduces SimRAG, a self-training approach for adapting large language models to specialized domains such as science and medicine. SimRAG fine-tunes a model on instruction-following, question-answering, and search-related data, then uses the same model to generate and filter domain-relevant questions from unlabeled corpora. The method uses high-quality synthetic examples to improve domain-specific retrieval-augmented generation tasks. Across 11 datasets, two backbone sizes, and three domains, SimRAG outperforms baselines by 1.2%–8.6%.

Introduction body

The unit concerns SimRAG, a self-improving retrieval-augmented generation framework for adapting large language models to question answering in specialized domains. It fine-tunes one model for both context-based question answering and question generation, then uses unlabeled domain corpora to produce and filter synthetic question-answer pairs for continued training. Experiments across biomedical, natural and social sciences, and computer science domains show that SimRAG outperforms baseline models by 1.2%–8.6% across 11 datasets.

Related Work body

The unit reviews retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), self-training, and domain-specific LLMs, and introduces SimRAG as a method for adapting LLMs to domain-specific RAG and question answering. RAG systems combine a retriever with an LLM generator, while recent work improves retrieval dynamics, context filtering, and retrieval-related instruction tuning. SimRAG uses a two-stage fine-tuning framework: retrieval-oriented fine-tuning is followed by domain-adaptive fine-tuning with self-training. In the second stage, the same LLM generates candidate answers and questions from corpus documents, and round-trip consistency filters the resulting pseudo-labeled tuples.

Methodology / Problem Setup .. Stage-II: Domain Adaptive Fine-tuning body

This unit describes a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) setup in which a retriever selects relevant contexts and an LLM generates an answer to a query. Stage-I retrieval-oriented fine-tuning trains instruction-tuned LLMs on general instruction, context-aware QA, and retrieval-related data. Stage-II domain adaptive fine-tuning uses self-training on an unlabeled specialized-domain corpus to generate, retrieve, and filter pseudo-labeled QA samples. The resulting samples are blended with general instruction and context-aware QA data to adapt the LLM to specialized domains.

Experimental Setup / Tasks and Datasets .. Implementation Details body

The Experimental Setup evaluates SimRAG and multiple baselines across medical, scientific, and computer science datasets, using task-specific metrics and retrieval-augmented inference. The medical evaluation includes the five datasets in the MIRAGE benchmark plus LiveQA and MedicationQA, while the scientific evaluation includes MMLU scientific subsets, ARC-challenge, and SciQ, and the computer science evaluation uses CS-Bench. The study compares general, domain-specific, and retrieval-augmented language models, including GPT-3.5, GPT-4, Llama3-8B-Instruct, Gemma2-27B-Instruct, RAFT, EvidenceRAG, and SimRAG. SimRAG achieves strong results in the medical and scientific tables, and removing Stage-II training lowers its average performance.

Experimental Results / Main Results .. Case Studies body

This unit evaluates SimRAG, an instruction fine-tuning framework for retrieval-augmented, domain-specific question answering across medical, scientific, and computer science domains. SimRAG consistently outperforms baseline models across multiple question-answering formats and approaches GPT-4 performance when using Gemma2-27B as its backbone. Ablation studies show that retrieval-oriented fine-tuning in Stage-I and self-synthesized training tuples in Stage-II both improve performance, while diverse question types and question filtering further benefit adaptation. Case studies indicate that SimRAG generates more relevant, self-contained, and challenging pseudo-labeled question-answer pairs than Llama3-8B-it.

Limitation body

The unit describes limitations of SimRAG. Its single round of pseudo-label generation may restrict refinement of pseudo-label quality, while iterative refinement could improve generated synthetic queries. Synthetic query generation and filtering add training time compared with baseline models, although inference time complexity is not increased relative to RAG approaches using the same backbone models. Stronger query generation models, including Llama-3.1-70B-it, could yield further gains but would require computational costs beyond the current budget.
