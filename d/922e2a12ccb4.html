Part of The Sherlock Holmes Canon

The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25 · 42 sections

This document is a collection of twelve Sherlock Holmes cases narrated or framed by Dr. John Watson, following Holmes’s investigations from Baker Street through London and the countryside. Holmes’s powers of disguise, observation, deduction, and occasional moral judgment drive each story, while Watson assists, records events, and often provides the human perspective. It begins with the King of Bohemia seeking recovery of a compromising photograph from Irene Adler. Holmes locates it, but Adler recognizes and outwits him, leaves with her husband Godfrey Norton, and becomes the woman whose wit defeats Holmes. In the Red-Headed League, Holmes exposes a fake employment scheme designed to clear Jabez Wilson’s shop while John Clay tunnels toward a bank. He next proves that James Windibank invented the fiancé Hosmer Angel to keep Mary Sutherland’s income in the family. The middle cases range from murder and inherited menace to mistaken identity and theft. Holmes clears James McCarthy by uncovering John Turner’s confession in the Boscombe Valley killing. He tries too late to save John Openshaw from the K. K. K., though he identifies the men pursuing the family papers. He reveals that beggar Hugh Boone is actually Neville St. Clair, hiding a profitable double life. The blue carbuncle is traced through a Christmas goose to James Ryder, whom Holmes releases after his confession. At Stoke Moran, Holmes discovers that Dr. Grimesby Roylott used a snake to kill Julia Stoner and endanger Helen; the snake kills Roylott instead. Later, Holmes investigates Victor Hatherley’s severed thumb and identifies Colonel Lysander Stark’s hydraulic press as part of a counterfeiting operation. He solves Hatty Doran’s apparent wedding disappearance by finding that she has reunited with her presumed-dead husband, Francis Hay Moulton. In the Beryl Coronet affair, Holmes clears Arthur Holder and shows that Mary Holder aided Sir George Burnwell’s theft. Finally, at the Copper Beeches, Holmes and Watson help Violet Hunter uncover Alice Rucastle’s imprisonment by her father, who used Hunter to impersonate her and control her inheritance. Across the collection, apparent scandals, crimes, disappearances, and mysteries are resolved through Holmes’s reconstruction of concealed motives and events, though some wrongdoers escape legal punishment or tragedy cannot be prevented.

Holmes accepts the case, receives Irene Adler’s address at Briony Lodge, and is given carte blanche and funds for expenses.
