RE-RAG: Improving Open-Domain QA Performance and Interpretability with Relevance Estimator in Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Kiseung Kim and Jay-Yoon Lee · 2024 · 21 sections

This is a paper in the Proceedings of the 2024 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, titled “RE-RAG: Improving Open-Domain QA Performance and Interpretability with Relevance Estimator in Retrieval-Augmented Generation,” by Kiseung Kim and Jay-Yoon Lee of the Graduate School of Data Science at Seoul National University. It addresses a weakness of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) for open-domain question answering: retrieved contexts may be irrelevant and distract a generator, reducing answer accuracy. Traditional RAG weights contexts using retriever relevance scores, while approaches such as RALM use concatenated contexts for large-language-model in-context learning. The authors propose RE-RAG, which adds a detachable relevance estimator (RE) to retrieval and generation. The RE estimates both contexts’ relative usefulness for answering a question and confidence in the retrieved context set. It is weakly supervised from question-answer data without labels identifying correct contexts. RE-RAG reranks retrieved contexts, replaces retriever similarity weights with normalized RE scores, and jointly trains generator, relevance-estimation, and token losses. It can be trained with a small language model and then used with larger language models. Experiments on Natural Questions and TriviaQA compare RE-RAG with traditional RAG, FiD, FiD-KD, MonoT5, and large language model approaches. RE-RAG evaluates 100 retrieved contexts and uses the top 25 at inference. Reported results show that it generally improves over comparable baselines, outperforms traditional RAG with the same total parameter count, competes with FiD-based models, and improves reranking and unanswerable-set classification. Applied to Llama2, RE surpasses Self-RAG, though TriviaQA results depend more on the generator. RE also generalizes better than FiD-KD on unseen datasets, and ablations indicate benefits from reranking, relevance scoring, normalization, and generator-informed loss. The paper further uses RE confidence for decoding: low-confidence sets can be classified as unanswerable, or the system can selectively favor an LLM’s parametric knowledge. This mixed strategy generally improves TriviaQA while preserving Natural Questions performance. The authors conclude that RE-RAG strengthens RAG and can augment modern large language models. They note that the work focuses on single-hop QA; multi-hop effectiveness remains unverified, and future work should assess whether parametric knowledge can answer a query before deeming it unanswerable. The research received support from the National Research Foundation of Korea, Seoul National University, and computing-resource contributors.

Abstract body

The Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) framework combines parametric and external knowledge and achieves strong performance on open-domain question answering, but its performance degrades when queries include irrelevant contexts. The proposed RE-RAG framework adds a relevance estimator (RE) that measures both the relative relevance and the confidence of contexts for answering a question. A weakly supervised method trains the RE using question-answer data without labels for correct contexts. The work also introduces decoding strategies that can identify unanswerable questions or favor an LLM’s parametric knowledge over unrelated retrieved contexts.

opening body

The unit concerns retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and proposes RE-RAG, which adds a relevance estimator to assess retrieved contexts. It argues that irrelevant contexts can distract language models and reduce answer accuracy, while traditional retrievers and rerankers may not determine whether top-ranked contexts are truly useful. RE estimates precise relative relevance and context confidence, supports weighted answers and reranking, and can be trained with weak supervision on a small language model before being applied to large language models. Its confidence assessments enable decoding strategies such as classifying queries as unanswerable or selectively relying on a language model’s parametric knowledge.

2 / 2.1 .. 2.3 body

This unit reviews traditional RAG and introduces RE-RAG, which combines retrieval, generation, and a relevance estimator. Traditional RAG uses a bi-encoder retriever to select contexts and a generator to produce answers, with RAG-sequence marginalizing answer probabilities across contexts. RE-RAG reranks retrieved contexts using a relevance estimator and replaces retriever similarity-based weights with normalized relevance scores. Its joint training uses generator, relevance-estimation, and token losses combined into a total loss.

3 .. 3.3 body

The unit describes experiments evaluating RE-RAG on the Natural Questions and TriviaQA open-domain question-answering datasets. It explains the datasets, context retrieval setup, exact match and additional evaluation metrics, and baseline comparisons involving FiD, FiD-KD, and large language models. RE-RAG uses an extra module to evaluate contexts, selects the top 25 from 100 retrieved contexts for inference, and generally outperforms comparable baselines in the reported results.

3.4 / 4 / 4.1 .. 4.2 body

This unit evaluates RE-RAG, a retrieval-augmented generation system that uses a relevance estimator (RE) with T5, Flan-T5, and several large language model generators. On Natural Questions (NQ) and TriviaQA (TQA), RE-RAG outperforms traditional RAG with the same total number of parameters and performs competitively with FiD-based models. RE improves context re-ranking and unanswerable set classification compared with FiD-KD and MonoT5 in overall F1 performance. When applied to Llama2, RE also surpasses Self-RAG, while performance on TQA depends more strongly on the generator model.

5 / 5.1 .. 5.3 body

This unit evaluates RE and RE-RAG for handling low-confidence context sets, including when to classify a query as unanswerable or rely on an LLM’s parametric knowledge. The mixed strategy generally improves TriviaQA performance and preserves Natural Questions performance, although gains depend on the availability of parametric knowledge. RE also generalizes better than FiD-KD to unseen datasets, with smaller drops in reranking recall and answer performance. Ablations show that RE’s reranking, relevance scoring, normalization, and generator-informed loss each contribute to performance.

6 body

Research on Question Answering has improved system performance by using external knowledge and more accurate retrieval methods. In open-domain QA, extractive systems and generative models such as T5 and BART have been studied, with RAG and FiD achieving strong performance. Subsequent work improved FiD's structural advantages, while Atlas achieved state-of-the-art performance through a better retriever and increased model scale. The development of large language models such as GPT and Llama has renewed interest in RAG models because these models can directly process context without requiring encoded data.

7 body

The RE-RAG framework extends traditional RAG by incorporating an RE module that measures the relative relevance and confidence of contexts. The authors show that RE-RAG can enhance traditional RAG performance and that the detachable RE module can be combined with modern large language models to improve their performance. They also explore decoding strategies that use RE-measured confidence to answer “unanswerable” or selectively use parametric knowledge when a low-confidence context set is retrieved. The authors hope this work inspires exploration of additional modules for retrieval-augmented generation.

8 body

The research primarily focuses on improving answer performance in single-hop QA tasks. Its effectiveness in multi-hop QA tasks has not been sufficiently verified, and future work may examine whether the RE-RAG framework can extend to them. The work also explores a decoding strategy that measures confidence in whether a context is useful for a query and classifies low-confidence contexts as unanswerable. The authors state that future research should determine whether parametric knowledge can adequately answer a query before classifying it as unanswerable.
