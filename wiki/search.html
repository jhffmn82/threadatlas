    How a search works

    This page runs retrieval's own search, the steps the retrieval notebook (version 0.21) takes before a reader model answers a question, in your browser, over the works on this site. What you type stays in the page: the store's records, their vectors and their links are files this site serves, and both models are downloaded once and run here. The settings above are the ones retrieval's arms change, so you can change one at a time and watch what it does to the list.

    What it searches

    The store holds five kinds of record, and each result is tagged with its kind.

    • fact: one statement the ingestor read from a work, a subject, a relation and an object, with the verbatim quote it came from. Its link opens the work's text with the quote highlighted.
    • cell: what one part of a work, a chapter or a section, says about one entity. Its link opens that part.
    • abstract: a short account of one entity across a whole work, or of the work itself. Its link opens the entity's page at that work, or the work's page.
    • chunk: a passage of a work's own text, cut by the store with no model in between. Its link opens the part of the work it starts in.
    • parent: one entity found in several works, linking its instances without merging them. A parent is never listed itself: a hit on it brings in its instances' abstracts (step 3).

    The In menu picks the collection to search. Each collection is a download of its own, its size beside its name; Every work loads them all. A search typed in the box at the top of any page, or on the front page, comes here and runs over every work.

    Step 1: meaning

    The question is turned into 384 numbers by bge-small-en-v1.5, the model the store was embedded with. Every record has one or more rows of 384 numbers made the same way from its text: a fact or a chunk has one, an abstract, a cell or a parent can have several. The question is compared with each row by their dot product (both are scaled to length 1, so this is the cosine of the angle between them), and a record scores its best row. The 80 records that score highest are the meaning list. This finds a record that says the same thing in other words, and can miss a rare name the model has no sense of.

    Step 2: keywords

    The question is split into words the way the store's keyword index splits text: lower case, accents dropped, runs of letters or digits. Every record that holds at least one of the words is scored by BM25, the formula SQLite's FTS5 index ranks by. A word counts for more when it is rare across the store, when it comes up more often in the record, and when the record is short. How rare a word is counts across the whole store's index, as retrieval counts it. The 80 best are the keyword list. Parents are not in the keyword index and are found by meaning only. This finds a name or an exact phrase, and misses the same idea in other words.

    Step 3: fusing the two lists

    The first 40 of each list go on. Each record is given 1/(60 + its place) from each list it is on, and the two are added: first place is worth 1/61, tenth place 1/70, so a record both searches found usually comes ahead of one found by only one. This is reciprocal rank fusion: it uses only the places, so the two kinds of score never have to be put on one scale. With keywords only or meaning only one list is empty and the order is the other list's.

    A hit on a parent is replaced by the abstracts of its instances, up to 10 of them, the most similar to the question first, at the parent's place. What comes out is the pool: the records the rest of the search works on.

    Step 4: the walk

    With 0 hops the pool is the hits. With 1 or 2, the search follows the store's links out from the 20 best-placed records of the pool:

    • from a fact to its cell, its abstract and the chunks that hold its quote;
    • from a cell to its abstract, the same entity's cells just before and after it, and its facts;
    • from an abstract to the entity's cells and facts (a work's own abstract leads to every fact in the work);
    • from a chunk to the facts whose quotes it holds;
    • from any record of an entity to the abstracts of the same entity in other works, through its parent.

    On each link it takes the 10 records most similar to the question, each valued at its similarity times 0.8 per hop. A second hop goes out again from the 20 best records the first hop reached. Everything reached that is not in the pool already is ranked by that value and joins the pool as a third list, given 1/(60 + its place) like the other two, so a well-placed record the walk found can come ahead of a low hit. The walk adds what the searches could not reach: the passage around a fact, the facts inside a cell, the same entity in another work.

    Step 5: reranking

    Without reranking the list is in the order of steps 3 and 4. With it, every record of the pool is read together with the question by ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2, a cross-encoder: a model that reads the question and the record side by side and gives one score for how well the record answers it, where the searches compared two sets of numbers made apart. The pool is then ordered by that score, higher first. It reads every record in full, so it is the slow step: several seconds for a pool, less on a graphics card the browser can use.

    Graph, chunks or both

    Graph and chunks searches every record. Graph only leaves the chunks out, as the store was before chunks were added. Chunks only is retrieval's chunks-only arm: both searches return chunks alone and no parent brings in abstracts, though a walk can still step from a chunk to the facts it holds and on from them. Comparing the two on one question sets what the graph made from a text against the text itself.

    Reading a result

    Each result shows its kind, where it sits (the work, and the entity or the part, linked), its text and, for a fact, its quote. The grey line under it says how it got into the pool:

    • a hit: meaning #3, keywords #12: its place on each list that found it;
    • brought in by the hit on a parent: one of that parent's instance abstracts;
    • 1 hop along cell from the fact: reached by the walk, along which link, from which record;
    • reranker 4.21: the cross-encoder's score, when reranking is on; higher is better, on no fixed scale.

    The first 30 of the pool are listed. The line above the list gives the size of the pool and the time the search took.

    Things to try

    • Search with the page as it opens, then set the walk to 1 hop: see which linked records join, and where.
    • Tick Rerank to see the same pool in the cross-encoder's order.
    • Ask the same question with graph only and then chunks only.
    • Search a name with keywords only, then describe the same thing in other words with meaning only.

    After the search

    In retrieval this ranked pool is where answering starts: the records at the top are packed, each with the passage its facts came from, into the reader model's prompt, and the reader answers from them. This page stops at the ranked list, so no model writes an answer.
