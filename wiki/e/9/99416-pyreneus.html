Abstract written by a model

Pyreneus is a dreadful, insolent prince and unjust ruler of Daulian and Phocean land in Phocis, backed by Thracian troops. Hostile to learning, he destroyed institutions of instruction. During a storm he invited the Muses into his house and attempted violence; they escaped by taking wing, and he lost his life.

Pyreneus is in one work on this site, The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

opening

Pyreneus had seized Daulian and Phocean land with Thracian troops and unjustly ruled it. He invited the Muses into his house during a storm, then attempted violence, but they escaped by taking wing.

notes .. notes

Pyreneus is presented in Plutarch’s interpretation as a ruler in Phocis who was hostile to learning. He allegedly destroyed institutions of instruction, pursued the Muses, and lost his life; Ovid is identified as the only writer who mentions him by name.
