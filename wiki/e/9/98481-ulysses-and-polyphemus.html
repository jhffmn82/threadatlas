Homeric story of Ulysses and Polyphemus in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Homeric story of Ulysses and Polyphemus first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Homeric story of Ulysses and Polyphemus is an ancient story type that survives in oral tradition and has modern versions among many widely separated peoples. Its central pattern involves a one-eyed giant’s cave, the capture and devouring of people, tricks with names such as “Nobody” and “Myself,” the giant’s blinding, and escape through or among a flock of sheep. Closely resembling versions are recorded in Sicily, Mingrelia, the Caucasus, Oghuz traditions, Armenia, Russia, and among the Lapps. Its common source may be the Odyssey, or more probably an older folk-tale incorporated by Homer.
