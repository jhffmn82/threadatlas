Cecil Barker in The Valley of Fear

Narrative, section by section

9 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter III

Cecil James Barker was a wealthy English bachelor and an intimate friend of John Douglas from America. He heard the shot, reached the room within about thirty seconds, reported the murder, and told Sergeant Wilson that the open window and blood mark on the sill indicated that the killer escaped across the moat.

Chapter IV

Cecil Barker is present at the Manor House and is described as tall, sunburned, capable-looking, and masterful. He reports that the suspected killer’s bicycle has been found concealed within a hundred yards of the hall door.

Chapter V

Cecil Barker says he believed the murderer escaped through the study window because of the bloodstain and the raised drawbridge. He describes Douglas's unexplained danger, his past in California, his habit of carrying a revolver, his jealousy, and his own loyalty to Douglas and Mrs. Douglas.

Inspector MacDonald had sent up a note to the effect that he would wait

Cecil Barker stopped Mrs. Douglas from entering the room after the shooting and went to summon the police. His carpet slippers were stained with blood, and one of them exactly matched the blood mark on the study window sill, indicating that he made the apparent outside footprint.

Chapter VI

Barker is seen sitting and laughing with Mrs. Douglas in the garden before assuming a solemn manner when Dr. Watson appears. He asks whether Sherlock Holmes acts independently of the police. Holmes says Barker’s account is a clumsy lie and claims that Barker deliberately placed the blood mark on the windowsill to mislead the police.

It was nightfall before Inspector MacDonald and White Mason came back

Sherlock Holmes suspects that Barker was involved with Mrs. Douglas in concealing something. Holmes believes Barker helped the murderer escape, or reached the room before the escape, and helped fabricate evidence involving the window.

Chapter VII

Cecil Barker is found in the study retrieving a weighted bundle from the moat. He refuses to explain the secret and says it is not his to reveal, but Mrs. Douglas tells him he has done enough.

Barker helped Douglas and Douglas's wife dress Ted Baldwin's body in Douglas's dressing gown and prepare the false murder scene. He also opened the window, made a mark on the sill, and rang the bell to suggest that the murderer had escaped.

Epilogue

Cecil Barker brought Sherlock Holmes the news of John Douglas's reported death and believed that the Scowrers had murdered him. He expressed anger and helplessness at the prospect of being unable to retaliate against Moriarty.
