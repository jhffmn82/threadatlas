plague at Ægina in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where plague at Ægina first appears

Abstract written by a model

The plague at Ægina is a contagion attributed to Juno’s anger and inflicted on Æacus’s country. It spreads through air and waters, then destroys dogs, birds, sheep, oxen, wild beasts, and people. Remedies fail; severe symptoms and mass death overwhelm burial and cremation, leaving the island desolate. When it ceases, hidden subjects return, though another tradition says the island must be repopulated.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

opening

The pestilence is attributed to Juno’s anger and spreads through the air, fountains, lakes, rivers, animals, countryside, and city. It kills animals and people, defeats medical remedies, causes severe physical symptoms, and overwhelms burial and cremation practices.

notes .. notes

The plague at Ægina forms part of the narrative associated with Minos’s preparations for revenge. The unit also links Æacus with Ægina and its temple.

notes .. notes

The plague, or contagion, afflicted the country of Æacus and made his island desolate. Its cessation allowed the hidden subjects to return, while another tradition says it created the need to repopulate the island.

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where plague of Aegina first appears

Abstract written by a model

Juno sends a plague to Aegina as punishment for its name; it kills animals and people, poisons land and water, defeats physicians and religious practices, and nearly empties Aeacus's kingdom.
