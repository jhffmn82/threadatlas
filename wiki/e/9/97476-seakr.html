Zijun Yao · 2025

This document is a long paper in the Proceedings of the 63rd Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics, titled “SEAKR: Self-aware Knowledge Retrieval for Adaptive Retrieval Augmented Generation,” by Zijun Yao, Weijian Qi, Liangming Pan, Shulin Cao, Linmei Hu, Weichuan Liu, Lei Hou, and Juanzi Li. It presents SEAKR, an adaptive retrieval-augmented generation system for answering natural-language questions with large language models. The paper begins by framing two central problems for adaptive retrieval: deciding when an LLM needs external knowledge and determining how retrieved knowledge should be incorporated. It situates the approach among retrieval-augmented generation, retrieval-augmented reasoning, and research showing that Transformer internal representations can reveal uncertainty or hallucination-related signals. SEAKR combines a search engine, an LLM, and a self-aware uncertainty estimator. During Chain-of-Thought-style iterative reasoning, it derives uncertainty from EOS-token hidden representations across sampled generations, using a Gram matrix and regularized Gram determinant. High uncertainty triggers retrieval. Retrieved snippets are then re-ranked according to their ability to reduce uncertainty, and for complex questions the system compares reasoning based on generated rationales with reasoning based on retrieved knowledge, selecting the less uncertain route for the final answer. The evaluation uses LLaMA-2-chat and BM25 retrieval over a 2018 English Wikipedia dump, without fine-tuning. It tests complex multi-hop QA on 2WikiMultiHopQA, HotpotQA, and IIRC, and simple QA on NaturalQuestions, TriviaQA, and SQuAD. SEAKR achieves the strongest overall complex-QA results, the best simple-QA F1 results on TriviaQA and SQuAD, and performance comparable to FLARE on NaturalQuestions. Ablations show that removing or changing uncertainty estimation, retrieval, re-ranking, or reasoning generally harms results. Further analyses compare LLM backbones and tune generation count, internal-state layer, and uncertainty threshold. A HotpotQA case study shows the system recognizing insufficient knowledge about Alejandro Jodorowsky and retrieving information for a comparison with Philip Saville. The paper concludes that self-aware uncertainty can guide both retrieval decisions and dynamic knowledge integration. It notes limits: dependence on internal model states, short-form QA evaluation, 20 pseudo-generations for uncertainty computation, models of at most 8 billion parameters, and possible future improvements in re-ranking. Ethical considerations emphasize improved factual correctness but acknowledge misuse risks; the authors plan to release code for transparency and risk monitoring.

SEAKR in SEAKR: Self-aware Knowledge Retrieval for Adaptive Retrieval Augmented Generation

Zijun Yao · 2025

SEAKR, titled “Self-aware Knowledge Retrieval for Adaptive Retrieval Augmented Generation,” is a tuning-free adaptive retrieval-augmented generation system and method by Zijun Yao, Weijian Qi, Liangming Pan, Shulin Cao, Linmei Hu, Weichuan Liu, Lei Hou, and Juanzi Li. Its central idea is to extract self-aware uncertainty from a large language model’s internal states, rather than its output, and use that uncertainty to decide when retrieval is needed and how retrieved knowledge should be used. For each natural-language question, its search engine, large language model, and self-aware uncertainty estimator iteratively generate Chain-of-Thought rationales while maintaining rationale and knowledge buffers. When uncertainty exceeds a threshold, it retrieves knowledge, re-ranks snippets according to how much they reduce uncertainty, and retains the most uncertainty-reducing snippet. For tasks requiring multiple retrievals, it uses uncertainty to choose reasoning strategies; it stops retrieval when generation signals “So the final answer is” and returns the lower-uncertainty answer between rationale-based and knowledge-based strategies. Evaluated with LLaMA-2-chat, BM25, and English Wikipedia, SEAKR outperforms existing adaptive methods on complex and simple question-answering datasets, including reported F1 scores of 36.0% on 2WikiMultiHopQA, 39.7% on HotpotQA, 23.5% on IIRC, 63.1% on TriviaQA, and 36.5% on SQuAD, while being comparable with FLARE on NaturalQuestions. Ablations indicate that uncertainty estimation, retrieval, re-ranking, and reasoning matter to performance, and it benefits from LLaMA-3. Its limitations are reliance on internal states and therefore open-sourced LLMs, 20 pseudo-generations for Gram-determinant computation, testing only up to 8-billion-parameter models, and evaluation mainly on short-form QA rather than long-form QA or creative writing. It aims to improve LLM factual correctness and trustworthiness and releases source code.
