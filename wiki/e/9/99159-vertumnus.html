Vertumnus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Vertumnus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Vertumnus is a shape-shifting god, a tutelary deity of traders at Rome, associated with seasonal change and said by some to have been an ancient king of Etruria. He has a temple in Vicus Tuscus. Loving Pomona above all other suitors and sharing her tastes for fruit and gardens, he repeatedly disguises himself to reach her, speaks as an old woman, tells of Anaxarete and Iphis, then returns to youthful form.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

FABLE XI. [XIV.609-697]

Vertumnus is enamoured of Pomona and exceeds the Satyrs, Pans, Sylvanus, and other suitors in his love for her. He repeatedly seeks access to her by changing his appearance, and in the form of an old woman he urges Pomona to choose him, saying that she would be his first and last love.

FABLES XII. AND XIII. [XIV.698-851]

Vertumnus relates to Pomona the story of Anaxarete, Iphis, and Anaxarete's transformation into stone. He then returns to his youthful form after speaking to Pomona.

Vertumnus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Vertumnus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Vertumnus is a young, handsome rural deity of the mountains who can assume any shape. Loving Pomona and her cultivation, he repeatedly enters her garden in disguises, tells her Iphis and Anaxarete's story as an old woman, reveals himself, and wins her mutual love.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter VI

Vertumnus loves Pomona more than her other suitors and repeatedly gains admission to her garden by disguising himself as a reaper, haymaker, ox-driver, vine-dresser, apple-gatherer, soldier, fisherman, and old woman. He tells Pomona the story of Iphis and Anaxarete, then drops his disguise and wins Pomona's mutual love.
