Abstract written by a model

In The Library, it belongs to the Sun and is drawn by fire-breathing horses; when driven by Phaethon, it leaves its prescribed course and causes celestial and earthly destruction. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, it is the magnificent solar vehicle made by Vulcan and driven by Phaëton, whose unstable course burns the earth before Jupiter's lightning destroys it. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, it is the golden, silver-spoked, jewel-seated vehicle entrusted to Phaeton for one day despite warnings.

Abstract written by a model

The Sun’s chariot, belonging to the Sun and drawn by fire-breathing horses, normally follows a prescribed course; driven by Phaethon, it runs wildly and causes celestial and earthly destruction.

XI.—PHAETHON AND THE CHARIOT OF THE SUN

The Sun’s chariot is drawn by fire-breathing horses and travels along a prescribed course. When Phaethon drives it, the horses run wild and the chariot moves too high, too low, northward, and southward, causing celestial and earthly destruction.

the Sun's chariot in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where the Sun's chariot first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Sun’s chariot is a magnificent solar vehicle made by Vulcan, with gold on its axle-tree, poles, and wheel circumference, silver spokes, and chrysolites and gems on its yoke. Suited to the Sun horses and carrying light, it is driven by Phaëton in the fable. Too lightly burdened, it becomes unstable, leaps and rises through the air, and its course burns mountains, rivers, and lands. Jupiter’s lightning destroys it, scattering its reins, axle-tree, broken wheels, spokes, and fragments.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK THE SECOND.

Phaëton’s chariot is a magnificent vehicle made by Vulcan, with a golden axle, poles, and wheel rim, silver spokes, and jewels set along the yoke. Because Phaëton is too light to provide its usual burden, it becomes unstable and bounds through the air.

notes .. notes

Phaëton’s chariot is driven improperly, causing it to make jumps into the air, rise without its usual weight, and seem empty. Its course contributes to the burning of mountains, rivers, and lands.

opening .. opening

The chariot is destroyed when Jupiter's lightning strikes Phaëton. Its reins, axle-tree, spokes, and other fragments are scattered far and wide.
