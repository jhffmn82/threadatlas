German sentimentalism

topic · 1 work · 3 quoted facts · 0 connections
Also called: Gallic feeling, a sentimental view of nature, human sympathy, religious and ethical sentimentalism, the analysis of human feeling, the display of emotion, the natural man, the sentimental life

Abstract written by a model

German sentimentalism was prepared by Brockes’s sentimental view of nature, fostered by Friedrich Gottlieb Klopstock’s emotional poetry, and spread by Christian Fürchtegott Gellert’s religious and ethical sentimentalism. The 1768 publication of the Sentimental Journey brought it to German soil, supplying practical sentimental life and releasing pent-up emotion.

] CHAPTER III THE PUBLICATION OF THE SENTIMENTAL JOURNEY On February 27, 1768, the Sentimental Journey was published in London,[1] less than three weeks before the author’s death, and the book was at once transplanted to German soil, beginning there immediately its career of commanding influence and wide-spread popularity.

German sentimentalism had been prepared by Brockes’s sentimental view of nature, Klopstock’s emotional poetry, and Gellert’s religious and ethical sentimentalism. The Sentimental Journey supplied this movement with a practical illustration of sentimental life and a release for pent-up emotion.
