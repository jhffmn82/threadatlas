the Scowrers in The Valley of Fear

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1914-09/1915-05

The Scowrers are a secret, organized criminal gang—also called the violent local members of the Eminent Order of Freemen—operating through lodges in the coal district and dominating Vermissa and the Valley of Fear. Said to have at least five hundred members, they are led by older members who order younger agents to act. They maintain power through terror, violence, loyal supporters, intimidation of witnesses, alibis, and money for legal defense, escaping convictions during ten years of outrage. Their repeated crimes include murder, shootings, beatings, mutilation, violent intimidation, an attack on the Herald office, and the blowing up of a family; their rule leaves the community afraid to speak openly. Opposition eventually gathers among citizens, corporations, railroads, and the Pinkerton organization. When the lodge plans to capture and kill Birdy Edwards as an informant, he reveals himself as a Pinkerton detective. The gang is surrounded, disarmed, arrested, tried, punished, and broken up, though sixty more members remain beyond those captured. In the epilogue, they are still considered capable of murdering John Douglas, but Holmes says they did not act alone.

8 sections; each heading opens the text

PART II / Chapter I

The Scowrers are described as a gang associated with murder and terror in the Vermissa district. People are afraid to speak of them openly, and the miner who guides McMurdo warns that men have been beaten to death for less.

Chapter II

The Scowrers are identified as the local form of the Eminent Order of Freemen and are feared throughout the valley. They are described as a murderous society whose members intimidate witnesses and escape prosecution through alibis and fear.

Chapter III

The Scowrers are a dangerous organization operating through several lodges in the coal district. Their older members give orders while younger agents carry them out, and their confidence comes from witnesses who will not testify, loyal supporters, and funds for strong legal representation.

Chapter IV

The Scowrers are identified as the violent members of the Freeman’s society who terrorize Vermissa and are blamed for the attack on the Herald office. McMurdo and other accused men are called Scowrers by hostile townspeople after their release.

McMurdo thought long and deeply over the commission which had been so

The Scowrers were responsible for deeds that spread a rule of fear across the great and rich district. Their crimes included shootings, murders, mutilation, and the blowing up of a family.

Chapter VI

The Scowrers are facing organized opposition from citizens, corporations, railroads, and the Pinkerton organization. Their lodge leaders respond to the investigation by planning to kill Birdy Edwards.

Chapter VII

The Scowrers gather at McMurdo’s house to capture and kill Birdy Edwards, believing him to be an informant. Edwards exposes himself as a Pinkerton’s detective, and the gang is surrounded, disarmed, arrested, tried, and broken up.

Epilogue

The Scowrers are described by Cecil Barker as a vindictive nest of criminals who might have murdered John Douglas. Holmes rejects the idea that they acted alone, attributing the crime to a more powerful hand.

the Eminent Order of Freemen in The Valley of Fear

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1914-09/1915-05

The Eminent Order of Freemen is a society organized through lodges, with a stated identity as a benefit club for charity and good fellowship. McMurdo, a member of Lodge No. 29 in Chicago, says its lodges throughout the country provide members with friends; membership is proved by a hand-grip, gesture, sign, and countersign, and Freemen settling in Vermissa must join the local lodge. In Vermissa, however, the Order is identified with the Scowrers. Its apparent social and charitable purpose gives way to a coercive secret criminal society: its bosses control members by threats, punishment, and violence, and opposition brings retaliation. Its members commit murder, intimidation, blackmail, mutilation, attacks, and explosions, terrorizing mine owners, other opponents, and the district as a whole. Killings at Crow Hill and Stake Royal deepen the shadow over Vermissa, while the local lodge is associated with the attack on the Herald office. McMurdo becomes an increasingly important adviser, even as citizens begin organizing and distributing firearms against the terror. In the later investigation of Jack Douglas’s death, Holmes, White Mason, and Inspector MacDonald treat the existence of such a secret society as a working hypothesis, and Barker believes it relentlessly pursued Douglas until his killing.

Narrative, section by section

9 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter IV

Holmes, White Mason, and Inspector MacDonald adopt the existence of a secret society as a working hypothesis based on the card marked V.V. 341 and the branded mark on Mr. Douglas’s forearm. They consider whether an agent of such a society entered the Manor House to carry out vengeance.

Chapter V

Cecil Barker believes that an implacable secret society was pursuing Jack Douglas and would not rest until it killed him. Barker did not know the society's name or the reason Douglas had offended it, but thought the legend on the placard referred to it.

PART II / Chapter I

The Eminent Order of Freemen has lodges throughout the country, and McMurdo says that membership will provide him with friends wherever there is a lodge. He proves his membership to Brother Scanlan through a hand-grip and a secret gesture.

Chapter II

The Eminent Order of Freemen is presented by McMurdo as a society for charity and good fellowship, and he says that he has belonged to it for over two years. In the valley, however, Mr. Shafter says its bosses are also the bosses of the Scowrers and that offending it brings violent retaliation.

Chapter III

Jack McMurdo is already a member of the Ancient Order of Freemen, belonging to Lodge No. 29 in Chicago. His sign and countersign establish his membership when he is questioned during the Vermissa lodge ceremony.

Chapter IV

The Freeman’s society is described as an organization that initially appears to be a benefit club and meeting place but has become a coercive criminal society in Vermissa. It controls its members through threats, punishment, and violence, and its local lodge is associated with the attack on the Herald office.

Chapter V

The Scowrers are a violent secret society whose members carry out murders, intimidation, and blackmail against mine owners and others who oppose them. They celebrate the killings at Crow Hill and Stake Royal, causing the shadow over Vermissa to fall darker.

McMurdo thought long and deeply over the commission which had been so

The Society of Freemen used organized violence to spread fear over the district. Its members and associates carried out attacks, killings, mutilations, and explosions against victims who opposed or challenged them.

Chapter VI

The Freemen regard McMurdo as an increasingly important adviser. Although they have terrorized Vermissa, citizens are beginning to organize against them and distribute firearms among law-abiding people.
