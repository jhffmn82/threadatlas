Daphne in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Daphne is a nymph and daughter of Peneus who loves woods, hunting, and perpetual virginity, rejecting marriage, love, and suitors. Pursued by Apollo after Cupid wounds her with a leaden arrow, she flees until exhausted and seeks divine aid. She is transformed into a laurel while fleeing, retaining her former elegance.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

front matter

Daphne is changed into a laurel while fleeing from Phœbus.

opening

Daphne, the daughter of Peneus, loves the woods, hunting, and perpetual virginity, and rejects all suitors. Wounded by Cupid’s leaden arrow, she flees from Apollo until her strength fails and she asks for divine aid. She is then transformed into a laurel tree, retaining only her former elegance.

notes .. notes

Daphne is the maiden pursued by Apollo toward the river Peneus. The explanation suggests that her name, which means laurel in Greek, may have helped give rise to the story of her transformation; Diodorus Siculus identifies her with Manto, the daughter of Tiresias.

Daphne in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Daughter of Ladon, linked to Arcadian and Elean traditions and the laurel, Daphne disliked males, befriended disguised Leucippus believing him a girl, bathed with maidens in the Ladon, and was reportedly the object of Apollo's love.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XX.

Daphne is associated with a tradition preserved in Arcadia and Elis. She disliked all males, befriended Leucippus while believing him to be a girl, and later bathed in the Ladon with the other maidens.

CHAPTER VII.

Daphne, the daughter of Ladon, was associated with the laurel because Apollo was reportedly enamoured of her. The text also notes that the name Daphne means laurel.

Daphne in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Daphne, Peneus's nymph daughter, rejects marriage for woodland hunting; after fleeing Apollo and praying to Peneus for escape, she becomes a laurel tree.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

PYTHON

Daphne was the daughter of the river god Peneus and preferred woodland sports and hunting to marriage. After Cupid caused her to reject love, she fled Apollo and prayed for escape, whereupon she was transformed into a laurel tree.
