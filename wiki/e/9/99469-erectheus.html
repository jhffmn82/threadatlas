Erectheus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Erectheus is king of Athens, receiving its sceptre and government. He sends for corn from Egypt during famine, fathers Orithyïa among eight children, and refuses Boreas’s marriage to her. Told he will win by sacrificing daughter Proserpine, he wars with Eleusinians and is killed alongside Eumolpus. Accounts differ on his relation to Pandion.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

notes .. notes

Erectheus was king of Athens and fought against the Eleusinians. He was told by an oracle that he would win if he sacrificed his daughter Proserpine, and he and Eumolpus were later killed in battle.

opening

Erectheus is king of Athens and receives the sceptre and government of the country. He refuses to consent to Boreas's marriage with Orithyïa.
