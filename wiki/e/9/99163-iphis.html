Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Iphis is a humble-born man whose unrequited love for Anaxarete leads him to plead through her household, letters, and garlands before he hangs himself before her door. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Iphis is a humble young man who loves Anaxarete, pleads with her, and hangs himself at her gate after her cruel rejection.

Abstract written by a model

Iphis is a humble-born man whose unrequited love for Anaxarete drives repeated pleas through her household, letters, and garlands; rejected, he hangs himself before her door, and his body is taken for burial.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLES XII. AND XIII. [XIV.698-851]

Iphis, born of a humble family, falls in love with Anaxarete and repeatedly seeks her favor through her nurse, servants, letters, garlands, and pleas. Unable to endure her rejection, he hangs himself before her door, and his body is carried to his mother's house and then through the city for burial.
