Clymene in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Clymene is a sea Nymph, daughter of Oceanus and Tethys, queen of the Ethiopians, and mother of Phaëton and his sisters. After hearing Phaëton’s account of reproaches, she swears that the Sun begot him and directs him to the Sun’s dwelling. After Phaëton dies, she searches for his limbs, finds his bones abroad, mourns at his tomb, and tries to save her daughters as they become trees.

opening

Clymene hears Phaëton’s account of Epaphus’s reproaches and confirms by an oath that Phaëton was begotten by the Sun. She tells him that he can visit the Sun’s dwelling and inquire of the Sun himself.

Clymene searches across the earth for Phaëton's lifeless limbs and finds his bones buried on a foreign bank. She lies beside the tomb, bathes his name with tears, and tries to rescue her daughters as they become trees.

Clymene in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Clymene, Phaeton’s mother, identifies the Sun as his father and sends Phaeton to seek the Sun’s confirmation.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

LATONA AND THE RUSTICS

Clymene told Phaeton that the Sun was his father and directed him to seek confirmation from the Sun himself. She called the Sun to witness the truth of her statement.
