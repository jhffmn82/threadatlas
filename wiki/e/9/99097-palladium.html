Abstract written by a model

In The Library, the Palladium is a three-cubit wooden image of Athena in the likeness of Pallas that fell from heaven, came to Ilium, protected Troy, and was stolen by Odysseus and Diomedes. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the statue of Minerva is a hidden statue believed to guard Troy and required by the Fates for its capture, which Ulysses takes from Troy and later reveals before the chiefs. In Description of Greece, the Palladium is a statue of Athene carried from Ilium to Italy by Æneas, whose removal the Argives claimed caused Ilium’s fall. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Palladium is a statue of Minerva believed to have fallen from heaven and to have protected Troy until Ulysses and Diomedes stole it.

Palladium in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Palladium first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Palladium is a three-cubit wooden image made by Athena in the likeness of Pallas, with an aegis around its breast, joined feet, an upright spear in its right hand, and a distaff and spindle in the other. Connected with Pallas, daughter of Triton, it was granted by Zeus to Ilus: it fell from heaven, appeared before Ilus’s tent after his prayer, and came into Ilium, where Ilus built it a temple and honored it. It protected Troy so long as it remained within the walls. During the ten-year war, Helenus said Troy could not be taken until it was stolen; Odysseus, Diomedes, and Ulysses are said to have taken it to the ships.

the statue of Minerva in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where the statue of Minerva first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Palladium is a hidden statue of Minerva believed to guard Troy’s safety and required by the Fates for Troy’s capture. During the night Ulysses takes it from Troy, later reveals it before the chiefs as his final appeal, and sails away; some writers link its possession to his dispute with Ajax Telamon.

Ulysses says that he carried the hidden statue of Minerva away from the middle of the enemy. He reveals the statue before the chiefs as a final appeal, asserting that Troy could not be captured without it.
