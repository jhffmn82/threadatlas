Cæneus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Cæneus, formerly the woman Cænis, is a Perrhæbean Lapith from Othrys who becomes a man, warrior, and famous invulnerable figure. At Pirithoüs’s wedding, he fights in the Lapith–Centaur battle, resisting Latreus’s sword and other weapons despite mockery of his former sex. The Centaurs bury him beneath tree trunks, killing him; afterward, a tawny-winged bird emerges from the heap, identified by Mopsus as Cæneus.

FABLES III. AND IV. [XII.146-535]

Cæneus is the male form of Cænis and is famous for being invulnerable. He attends Pirithoüs’s wedding, withstands the Centaurs’ attacks, and is eventually crushed beneath tree trunks.

Cæneus’s body resists Latreus’s sword and the weapons of the other Centaurs; he ultimately remains beneath a pile of trees, after which Mopsus says that a tawny-winged bird emerged from the heap and identifies it as Cæneus.
