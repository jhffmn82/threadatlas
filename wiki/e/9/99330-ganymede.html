Ganymede

person · 4 works · 11 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Ganymedes, Trojan boy, the youth Ganymede, the youth of Ilium
  • In The Library, Ganymede is the son of Tros and Callirrhoe, carried away by Zeus on an eagle because of his beauty and made cupbearer of the gods in heaven.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Ganymede is a beautiful youth of Ilium carried by Jupiter as an eagle to Heaven, where he becomes Jupiter’s cupbearer despite Juno’s opposition.
  • In The Fall of Troy, Ganymedes is Zeus’s immortal son from Troy who pleads from heaven for Troy’s safety or removal before witnessing its fall.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Ganymede is a Trojan boy seized by Jupiter disguised as an eagle from Mount Ida and carried to heaven as the gods’ cupbearer.
