Orpheus's descent to the underworld

event · 3 works · 3 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Orpheus's attempt to rescue Eurydice, Orpheus's journey to the underworld, descend to Styx, descended to the Infernal Regions, he went down alive to Hades, he went to Aornus, journey to that distant country, seek his wife in the regions of the dead, try once more for her release
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, it is Orpheus's descent through the Tænarian gate into the Infernal Regions to seek Eurydice, where his plea and music move Pluto and Persephone to grant her restoration subject to a condition.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, it is the Greeks' supposed belief that Orpheus went down alive to Hades to recover Eurydice, alongside an account that he went to Aornus to consult an oracle of the dead about her.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, it is Orpheus's attempt to rescue Eurydice by leading her from the underworld without looking back, which fails when he turns near the exit.

Orpheus's attempt to rescue Eurydice in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Orpheus descended to the underworld and sang before Pluto and Proserpine to ask for Eurydice's return. He was allowed to lead her away if he did not look back, but he turned near the exit to make sure she followed and caused her to be taken away a second time. He then tried to return, but the ferryman refused him passage.
