Hercules mythus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

The Hercules mythus is a mythic tradition of extreme antiquity, possibly magnifying a real Dorian, Theban, or Argive hero while absorbing other heroes’ exploits. It presents Hercules as human perfection, heroic power, or the Sun through the Zodiac, ending with his apotheosis.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

EXPLANATION.

The Hercules mythus is described as a very ancient tradition that may have begun with a real Dorian, Theban, or Argive hero and accumulated the exploits of similar heroes from other regions. Interpretations presented in the unit view Hercules as a personification of human perfection, the highest powers of the heroic age, or the Sun passing through the twelve signs of the Zodiac.
