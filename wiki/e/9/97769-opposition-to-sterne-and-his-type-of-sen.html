opposition to Sterne and his type of sentimentalism in Laurence Sterne in Germany

Harvey Waterman Thayer · 1905

Opposition to Sterne and his type of sentimentalism is a German current of antagonism tied to protests against slavish English imitation and calls for independent literary ideals. It chiefly attacks misunderstandings and adaptations of Sterne’s methods, not Sterne himself. Though some critics preferred Addison, defenders including Lessing, Goethe, and Herder valued Sterne as his influence gradually ceased to animate German letters.

NEW YORK, May 1, 1905.

Opposition to Sterne and his type of sentimentalism is identified in the contents as a separate subject. The introduction connects such opposition with German concerns about foreign imitation, national limitations, and the need for independent literary ideals.

] CHAPTER VII OPPOSITION TO STERNE AND HIS TYPE OF SENTIMENTALISM Sterne’s influence in Germany lived its own life, and gradually and imperceptibly died out of letters, as an actuating principle.

Opposition was directed chiefly against the misunderstanding and adaptation of Sterne’s methods rather than against Sterne himself. Some critics compared Sterne unfavorably with Addison, but defenders and major critics such as Lessing, Goethe, and Herder continued to express appreciation for him.
