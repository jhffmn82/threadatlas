Triptolemus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Triptolemus, in accounts identifying him with the fire child, is a child of Celeus. Distinguished from Demophon by some writers, he survives and receives Demeter’s mission to reveal corn to mankind, sowing wheat across the whole earth.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

J.—PuttTinc CHILDREN ON THE FIRE

Triptolemus is identified by several writers with the child whom Demeter placed on the fire, although Apollodorus and the Homeric hymn-writer distinguish him from Demophon. Accounts that call the child Triptolemus do not say that he perished, but instead describe his mission from Demeter to reveal corn to mankind.

Triptolemus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Triptolemus is a youth from Athens, priest of Ceres, and ruler at Eleusis when her mysteries were established. He carries Ceres’s seeds and corn to spread fruitful harvests, wholesome food, and her worship across different countries. He travels by ship and through the sky in Ceres’s chariot, associated with winged dragons, over Europe, Asia, and the earth; in Scythia he tells Lyncus that he comes to provide food. Traditions also say Ceres nursed and purified him.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

notes .. notes

Triptolemus is said to have established the worship of Isis at Eleusis and instituted the mysteries brought from Egypt. In the account of famine in Greece, he helped transmit the agricultural religious practices associated with the goddess.

opening

Triptolemus travels in Ceres's chariot over Europe, Asia, and the earth, carrying her gifts of seeds. In Scythia, he tells Lyncus that he comes from Athens to provide fruitful harvests and wholesome food.

notes .. notes

Triptolemus reigned at Eleusis when the mysteries of Ceres were established there. He carried corn to different countries by ship and introduced the worship of Ceres, whose priest he was; traditions also said that Ceres nursed and purified him.

Triptolemus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Triptolemus is represented by statues in temples near the Nine Springs and before the Eleusinium. Athenians and neighbouring tribes call him Celeus’s son and the first to sow corn; other traditions assign him to Ocean and Earth, Dysaules, or Rharus.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIV.

Triptolemus has a statue in the temple near the Nine Springs and another statue in front of the Eleusinium. The Athenians identify him as the son of Celeus and the first person to sow corn, while other traditions make him the son of Ocean and Earth, or of Dysaules or Rharus.
