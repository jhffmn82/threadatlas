Apollodorus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Apollodorus is the author of The Library and a source for several mythic accounts. His record of Troy includes its sack, Astyanax’s killing, Polyxena’s sacrifice, and the division of captive women among Greek leaders. In the account of the child on the fire, he names Celeus and Metanira as Demophon’s parents, distinguishes Demophon from Triptolemus, and says Praxithea’s interruption led to the child’s being consumed. He tells of Melampus and kine held by Phylacus, and reports that Leucus’s crimes and alliance led to Idomeneus’s expulsion. He also gives the Pleiades’ names in Aratus’s order.

Apollodorus’s account records the sack of Troy, the killing of Astyanax, the sacrifice of Polyxena, and the distribution of the captive women among the Greek leaders. The notes compare his account with several other traditions.

J.—PuttTinc CHILDREN ON THE FIRE

Apollodorus names Celeus and Metanira as the child’s parents, calls the child Demophon, and distinguishes him from his elder brother Triptolemus. He says that Praxithea interrupted Demeter’s rite and that the child was consumed in the fire.

IV.—MELAMPUS AND THE KINE OF PHYLACUS

Apollodorus tells a version of the story in which the kine are in Phylacus's possession and Phylacus releases Melampus. His account mentions a sacred oak rather than the wild pear-tree found in other versions and omits the knife's actual contact with Iphiclus's genitals.

XII.—TuE Vow or [DOMENEUS

Apollodorus says that Leucus debauched Meda, murdered her and her daughter, won ten cities of Crete to his allegiance, and expelled Idomeneus when he returned. He also records the exile story as the source for the first account discussed in the unit.

Apollodorus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Apollodorus is a person whose records mention Cycnus and Tiresias’s mother, place Ericthonius’s birth in Attica, preserve Europa’s genealogy, rank Semele as Thyone, reverse Philomela, Progne, and Tereus’s transformations, and recount Proserpine’s stays and Ascalaphus’s punishment and change into an owl.

Apollodorus says that Proserpine stayed nine months with Ceres and three months with Pluto. He also describes Ascalaphus as the son of Acheron and the Nymph Orphne or Gorgyra, and says that Ceres placed a rock upon him before he was changed into an owl after being liberated by Hercules.

Apollodorus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Apollodorus is identified as Aesacus’s son and as transformed into the didapper. It also provides an account of Atalanta: exposed, bear-nursed, found by hunters, beaten by Melanion’s golden-apple stratagem, then transformed with him for profaning Jupiter’s temple.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

[Footnote 52: _Pleases even Venus._--Ver. 524. According to

Apollodorus gives a version of Atalanta’s story in which her father exposes her as an infant, a she-bear nourishes her, and hunters later discover her. He says that Melanion defeats her in the race by using a stratagem involving golden apples, and that Melanion and Atalanta are transformed after profaning Jupiter’s temple. He gives her father’s name as Iasius in one account and Schœneus in another.
