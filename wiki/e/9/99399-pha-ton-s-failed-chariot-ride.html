Phaëton's failed chariot ride in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Phaëton's failed chariot ride first appears

Abstract written by a model

Phaëton’s one-day attempt to drive the solar chariot becomes an allegory of a rash young man’s wilful folly. Unable to control the horses or recognize the route, he drops the reins at the threatening Scorpion. The chariot wanders through unknown sky, rising and plunging near earth, and its uncontrolled course causes the world’s destruction.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

BOOK THE SECOND.

Phaëton cannot control the horses or recognize the route, and he eventually drops the reins when he sees the threatening Scorpion. The chariot then travels through unknown regions of the sky, alternately rising high and plunging near the earth.

notes .. notes

Phaëton’s adventure is presented as an allegory of wilful folly and rashness. It may preserve memories of destructive flames, an unusually hot period, an imperfect astronomical prediction, the ascent of Elijah in a chariot of fire, or Egyptian mourning for Osiris and Thammuz.
