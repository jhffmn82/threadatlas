fire-making by friction

Abstract written by a model

Fire-making by friction is a method of producing fire with wood by boring or rubbing. Stories describe it being learned through observation or instruction, including watching gods, spirits, birds, or insects. The Basongo Meno use a pointed stick to bore a Raphia-palm rib, a practice employed since fire was first elicited this way.

Fire-making is learned in several stories through observation or instruction, especially by rubbing two sticks, wood, a bamboo rope, or a bowstring together. In other accounts, people learn the process by watching gods, spirits, birds, or insects make fire.
