Tereus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Tereus, Ares’s Thracian son, helped Pandion defeat Labdacus, married Procne, and fathered Itys. After violating and mutilating Philomela, he pursued her and Procne after Itys was served to him, ending transformed into a hoopoe.

Pandion married Zeuxippe, his mother’s sister,!

Tereus, identified as the son of Ares and associated with Thrace, helped Pandion defeat Labdacus and married Procne, with whom he had Itys. He violated Philomela, cut out her tongue, and concealed her; after Procne served him Itys, he pursued the sisters with an axe and was transformed into a hoopoe.

Tereus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Tereus is a Thracian, Odrysian king, descended from Gradivus, powerful in wealth and men. He marries Progne and fathers Itys. After conquering barbarous troops at Athens, he travels there to bring Philomela to Thrace, lusts after her, rapes her, cuts out her tongue, and hides her in a remote dwelling, then falsely reports her death to Progne. Progne later serves him Itys’s remains at a banquet. Learning this, Tereus pursues Progne and Philomela with a sword, but is transformed into an epops, or lapwing, whose association with filth and impurity symbolizes his character.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

front matter

Tereus marries Progne, takes Philomela to his kingdom, and commits violence against her. He is eventually transformed into a hoopoe.

opening

Tereus marries Progne and becomes the father of Itys. He travels to Athens to bring Philomela to Thrace, then ravishes and imprisons her, cuts out her tongue, and falsely tells Progne that Philomela has died.

notes .. notes

Tereus is called the Odrysian king because he is associated with the Odrysæ, a people of Thrace.

opening

Tereus unknowingly eats Itys’s remains at the banquet prepared by Progne. When he learns what has happened, he pursues Progne and Philomela with a drawn sword, but is transformed into an epops, or lapwing.

notes .. notes

Tereus is represented by the lapwing, a bird associated with filth and impurity, symbolizing his unscrupulous character. The lapwing’s slow flight also signifies that Tereus could not overtake Progne and Philomela.
