Abstract written by a model

The inner moat surrounds the Manor House, is about forty feet wide, and is fed by a small stream that continues beyond it. It is normally two feet deep at the sides and three in the middle. An open window and a muddy, blood-like sill mark suggest the murderer waded across, though no clear far-side exit signs are found. Because it is nowhere over three feet deep, Barker says the assassin could not have drowned there.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter III

The inner moat was about forty feet wide, surrounded the Manor House, and was fed by a small stream. The open window and a muddy, blood-like mark on its sill led Barker and Sergeant Wilson to believe that the murderer had waded across it.

Chapter IV

The Manor House is surrounded by a broad moat that is about two feet deep at the sides and three feet in the middle. Holmes and White Mason find no clear signs on the far side that anyone climbed out, but agree that a person could cross it without drowning.

Chapter V

The moat was no more than three feet deep at any point. Barker therefore said that the assassin could not possibly have drowned in it.
