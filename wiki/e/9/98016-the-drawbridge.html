the drawbridge in The Valley of Fear

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1914-09/1915-05

The drawbridge is the wooden bridge over the moat and the only approach to the Manor House. By custom it is raised every evening or night and lowered every morning, though it can be lowered quietly. On the night of the murder it was raised after dusk, from nearly six until Barker lowered it afterward, restricting entry and escape and making the study window seem the only possible escape route; White Mason allows an earlier outsider entry.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter III

The drawbridge was the only approach to the Manor House and was raised every evening and lowered every morning. It was up from nearly six o’clock until Barker lowered it after the murder, making it appear difficult for an outsider to have entered or escaped.

Chapter IV

The drawbridge provides access to the Manor House and had been raised after dusk. White Mason reasons that an outsider could have entered between roughly four-thirty and six, before the bridge was raised.

Chapter V

The drawbridge was raised at night as an old custom of the house. After Douglas was found, Ames lowered it, while Barker argued that its being raised left the study window as the only possible escape route.
