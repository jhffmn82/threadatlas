Smilax in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Smilax is likely a Nymph associated with a herb, though the word may also denote a yew or oak. In accounts involving Crocus, Smilax is changed into a little flower: either as part of a chaste married couple or after loving Crocus and being rejected.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Smilax was changed together with Crocus into a little flower.

notes .. notes

Smilax was probably understood as a Nymph transformed into a kind of herb called smilax, although the word could also refer to a yew tree or a kind of oak. In one account, Smilax and Crocus were a chaste married couple changed into flowers; in another, Smilax loved Crocus, and both became flowers after he rejected her advances.
