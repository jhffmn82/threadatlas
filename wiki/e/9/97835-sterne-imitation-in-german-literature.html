Abstract written by a model

Sterne imitation in German literature is a literary phenomenon following Sterne’s popularity and tied to debates over German national identity, limitation, or disintegration. It was treated as a predictable result of admiration for foreign writing: German thinkers examined its causes and effects, while critics resisted heedless importation and urged national ideals. The imitation ranged from direct borrowing of plots, forms, headings, digressions, lists, interruptions, typographical marks, relationships, expressions, and sentimental motifs to looser allusion. Its strongest forms could be extensive yet retain independent events, German settings, and distinct moral or pedagogical aims. Across novels, travel narratives, poems, anecdote collections, and typographical experiments, the phenomenon moves from prominent disciples and intensive formal imitation to varied, weakening, incidental, or merely remote Sterne-related connections.

Literary imitation is presented as the predictable result of admiration for foreign writing. German thinkers studied its causes and effects, while other critics opposed the heedless importation of foreign views and promoted characteristically national ideals.
