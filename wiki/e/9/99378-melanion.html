Melanion in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Melanion defeats Atalanta in a race by a stratagem involving golden apples, marries her, then with Atalanta profanes Jupiter’s temple and is transformed into a lion.

[Footnote 52: _Pleases even Venus._--Ver. 524. According to

Melanion defeats Atalanta in the race by using a stratagem involving golden apples and becomes her husband. According to Apollodorus, Melanion and Atalanta are later transformed into a lion and a lioness after profaning Jupiter’s temple.

Melanion in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Melanion pursues and defeats Atalanta in a marriage race using Aphrodite’s golden apples, marries her, and fathers Parthenopaeus in one account; after intercourse with Atalanta in Zeus’s precinct, both are transformed into lions.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

IX. ᾿Αρκάδος δὲ καὶ Λεανείρας τῆς ᾿Αμύκλου

Melanion pursued Atalanta in her race for marriage, using golden apples from Aphrodite to distract her and win. He married Atalanta, and she bore him Parthenopaeus; another account names Ares as the father instead.
