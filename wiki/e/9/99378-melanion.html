Melanion

person · 2 works · 6 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Μελανίων, Μελανίωνος

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Melanion defeats Atalanta in a race using golden apples, marries her, profanes Jupiter’s temple with her, and is transformed into a lion. In The Library, Melanion pursues and defeats Atalanta in a marriage race using Aphrodite’s golden apples, marries her, fathers Parthenopaeus in one account, and is transformed into a lion with Atalanta after intercourse in Zeus’s precinct.

Melanion in The Library

Apollodorus · about AD 70 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Melanion first appears

Abstract written by a model

Melanion pursues and defeats Atalanta in a marriage race using Aphrodite’s golden apples, marries her, and fathers Parthenopaeus in one account; after intercourse with Atalanta in Zeus’s precinct, both are transformed into lions.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

IX. ᾿Αρκάδος δὲ καὶ Λεανείρας τῆς ᾿Αμύκλου

Melanion pursued Atalanta in her race for marriage, using golden apples from Aphrodite to distract her and win. He married Atalanta, and she bore him Parthenopaeus; another account names Ares as the father instead.
