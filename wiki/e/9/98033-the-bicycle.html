Abstract written by a model

A well-used Rudge-Whitworth bicycle, splashed from a considerable journey, is found concealed near the Manor House with a saddlebag holding a spanner and oilcan. Left as a supposed means of escape, it is investigated for its likely owner. Holmes doubts that a fugitive would abandon such a means of escape. The Eagle Commercial’s manager identifies it as Hargrave’s; MacDonald concludes Hargrave concealed and abandoned it because it could identify him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter IV

A well-used Rudge-Whitworth bicycle, splashed as if from a considerable journey, is found concealed in evergreens near the Manor House. It has a saddlebag containing a spanner and oilcan but provides no clue to its owner.

Chapter VI

A bicycle was left concealed outside the Manor House and is treated as evidence of an outsider’s escape. Holmes finds it suspicious that a fugitive would leave behind the means of escape, while his colleagues travel to Tunbridge Wells to seek its likely owner.

It was nightfall before Inspector MacDonald and White Mason came back

The bicycle is identified by the manager of the Eagle Commercial as belonging to Hargrave. MacDonald believes Hargrave left it concealed among the laurels near the house and abandoned it because it could identify him.
