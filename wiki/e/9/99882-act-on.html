Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Actæon is a skilled hunter who sees Diana bathing, is transformed into a stag, and is killed by his own dogs. In The Library, Actaeon is a hunter trained by Chiron who sees Artemis bathing, becomes a deer, and is killed by his own dogs. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Actæon is a hunter whose sighting of Artemis bathing leads to his dogs devouring him; his spectre later harms Orchomenus until his remains are buried and funeral rites established. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Actaeon is a hunting-party leader who enters Diana’s bathing place, becomes a stag, and is killed by his own dogs.

Actæon in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Actæon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Actæon is a skilled hunter, grandson of Cadmus and son of Autonoë and Aristæus, brought up by Chiron and owner of a large hunting pack. After postponing the rest of a hunt, he enters the Gargaphie grove and accidentally sees Diana bathing. Diana punishes him by transforming him into a stag. His own dogs pursue and tear him to pieces, and he dies, said to be killed on Mount Cithæron.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Actæon, the grandson of Cadmus and a skilled hunter, enters the Gargaphie grove after postponing the remainder of his hunt. He accidentally sees Diana bathing, is transformed into a stag, and is pursued and torn apart by his own dogs.

notes .. notes

Actæon is identified as the son of Autonoë and Aristæus and as a Boeotian youth, called Hyantian because the Hyantes were the ancient inhabitants of Boeotia. He was brought up by Chiron according to Apollodorus, and ancient accounts variously say that he was punished for seeing Diana bathing, showing contempt for Diana, attempting improper advances toward Semele, or approaching Diana out of curiosity.

Actæon in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Actæon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Actæon, son of Aristæus, is a hunter associated with a fawn, hound, and deerskin. After hunting, he slept near a spring and saw Artemis bathing; one account says she dressed him in deerskin so his dogs devoured him, while another attributes their madness without her intervention. At Orchomenus, his spectre harmed the land until his found remains were buried and annual funeral rites established.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

Actæon is said to have slept on a rock near a spring after hunting and to have seen Artemis bathing there. Stesichorus represented Artemis as dressing him in a deerskin so that his dogs would devour him, while the author believed madness came upon the dogs without the goddess’s intervention.

CHAPTER XXXVIII.

Actaeon is associated at Orchomenus with a spectre that injured the land. The Orchomenians buried whatever remains of him they could find and annually offer him funeral rites.

CHAPTER XXX.

Actæon, the son of Aristæus, is depicted seated with his mother on a deerskin and holding a fawn. A hunting hound is nearby, serving as an emblem of Actæon’s life and death.

Actaeon in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Actaeon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Actaeon, son of King Cadmus and leader of a hunting party, hunts, enters Diana’s bathing place, becomes a conscious but voiceless stag, and is pursued and killed by his own dogs.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter III

Actaeon, the son of King Cadmus, is hunting when he wanders into the place where Diana is bathing. Diana changes him into a stag, leaving his consciousness intact but taking away his human voice and form. His own hunting dogs and fellow-huntsmen pursue him, and the dogs tear him to death.
