Silver Age in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The Silver Age is the second age, succeeding the Golden Age under Jupiter's rule. Less just and happy than the Golden Age, yet more valuable than the Brazen Age, it divides the year into four seasons as men build primitive houses, bury seeds, and plow with oxen.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

The Silver Age succeeds the Golden Age under Jupiter's rule. It is less happy and less just than the Golden Age but more valuable than the Brazen Age; during it, the year is divided into four seasons, men build primitive houses, and they begin burying seeds and using oxen to plow.

notes .. notes

The Silver Age succeeded the Golden Age. During it, the year was divided into seasons.
