Athamas

person · 3 works · 36 quoted facts · 14 connections
Also called: Athamas the son of Æolus, son of Æolus, ᾿Αθάμαντα, ᾿Αθάμας
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Athamas is a husband and father whose marriages and madness shape his story; he fathers Learchus, Melicerta, Phryxus, and Helle, consults Delphi during famine, kills Learchus while maddened, and pursues Ino and Melicerta.
  • In The Library, Athamas is ruler of Boeotia who fathers Phrixus, Helle, Learchus, and Melicertes; during a dearth he consults Delphi and is forced to offer Phrixus, but Nephele rescues him, and after losing Learchus and Melicertes he is banished and settles Athamantia.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Athamas lived in Athamantium, was associated with Laphystian Zeus, received land near Laphystium from Andreus, adopted Haliartus and Coronus, and ultimately had his kingdom yielded to Phrixus and his descendants.

Athamas in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Athamas, son of Aeolus and great-grandson of Deucalion, is a husband and father whose marriages and madness shape his story. He marries Ino, divorces Nephele, later remarries Nephele, and is also described as taking Ino back; he fathers Learchus and Melicerta with Ino and Phryxus and Helle. During famine at Thebes he orders consultation of the oracle of Delphi. After discovering Ino’s deceit, and while maddened by Tisiphone’s poison, he pursues Ino, kills Learchus, and pursues Melicerta. After Phryxus’s death, Æetes seizes his treasures.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Athamas is struck by Tisiphone's poison and becomes mad. Believing that he sees a lioness with two cubs, he pursues Ino and kills Learchus by dashing him against the stones.

notes .. notes

Athamas, the son of Æolus and great-grandson of Deucalion, married Ino after divorcing Nephele and later took Ino back after divorcing her in turn. He ordered the oracle of Delphi to be consulted during the famine at Thebes, killed Learchus after discovering Ino's deception, and pursued Ino and Melicerta.

notes .. notes

Athamas is the son of Æolus and the father of Phryxus and Helle. After Phryxus’s death, Æetes seizes Athamas’s treasures, which becomes one of the stated reasons for the Greek expedition.

Athamas in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Athamas, son of Aeolus and ruler of Boeotia, fathers Phrixus and Helle by Nephele, then Learchus and Melicertes by his second wife Ino. Amid dearth he consults Delphi and is forced to offer Phrixus for sacrifice, but Nephele rescues him. After madness and Hera’s wrath cost him Learchus and Melicertes, he is banished and settles Athamantia.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

IX. Τῶν δὲ Αἰόλον παίδων ᾿Αθάμας, Βοιωτίας

Athamas rules Boeotia and has Phrixus and Helle by Nephele, and Learchus and Melicertes by Ino. He is induced to bring Phrixus to the altar for sacrifice, but Nephele rescues him. Later, Athamas loses Learchus and Melicertes, is banished from Boeotia, and settles in a land he names Athamantia.

Athamas in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Athamas lived in Athamantium, was associated with Laphystian Zeus, and received land near Laphystium from Andreus. Believing no male children survived, he adopted Haliartus and Coronus; after Phrixus or Presbon returned, Thersander’s sons yielded his kingdom to Phrixus and descendants.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXIV.

Athamas received land near Mount Laphystium, including what were later called Coronea and Haliartia, from Andreus. Believing he had no surviving male children, he adopted Haliartus and Coronus, but after Phrixus or Presbon returned, the sons of Thersander conceded Athamas’s kingdom to Phrixus and his descendants.
