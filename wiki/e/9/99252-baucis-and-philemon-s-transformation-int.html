Baucis and Philemon's transformation into trees

event · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Philemon and Baucis's transformation into trees, changing in like manner, made of their two bodies, made out of the two good old people, shooting into leaf, two trees

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, it is the divine end-of-life transformation that preserves Philemon and Baucis as adjoining trees after temple service, as they farewell each other before the sacred steps while branches cover their faces. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, it is the event in which Baucis and Philemon grow old, transform into trees before the temple, exchange farewell, and stand side by side.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE V. [VIII.547-610]

After a long life serving the temple, Philemon and Baucis begin to grow into trees while standing before its sacred steps. They exchange farewell as long as they can speak, and branches cover their faces at the same moment.
