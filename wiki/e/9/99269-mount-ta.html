Mount Œta

place · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: lofty Œta, Œta
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Œta is the mountain where Hercules suffers from the poisoned tunic, rages in pain, and cuts down trees for his funeral pile; its flames consume the mortal part of Hercules.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Mount Œta contains a narrow pass near Trachis and an easier route through Ænianes; Galati were defeated attempting one crossing, while Brennus used the other to reach the Greek force’s rear at Thermopylæ.

CHAPTER XXII.

Mount Œta had a narrow pass near the ruins of Trachis and another, easier route through the district of the Ænianes. Galati were defeated while attempting one crossing, but Brennus later used the other pass to reach the rear of the Greek force at Thermopylæ.
