Mount Œta

place · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: lofty Œta, Œta

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Œta is the mountain where Hercules suffers from the poisoned tunic, rages in pain, and cuts down trees for his funeral pile; its flames consume the mortal part of Hercules. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Mount Œta contains a narrow pass near Trachis and an easier route through Ænianes; Galati were defeated attempting one crossing, while Brennus used the other to reach the Greek force’s rear at Thermopylæ.
