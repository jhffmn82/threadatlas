Abstract written by a model

Schummel’s self-criticism judges his ninety-page autobiography a flawed, excessively imitative attempt at Sterne, failing to characterize the German people and relying more on Fielding and Richardson. It moves from admitting ill-conception and incoherence to condemning the second volume as still worse, while preserving praise for the preacher’s speeches and a few passages.

Schummel’s self-criticism identifies his work as a flawed imitation of Sterne, with few or no beauties of the original and many faults of its own. He condemns much of the work as ill conceived, incoherent, poorly told, beneath criticism, excessively imitative, indelicate, and painfully unsuccessful, while retaining approval of the preacher’s speeches and a few other sections.
