Johann Gottfried Herder in Laurence Sterne in Germany

Harvey Waterman Thayer · 1905

Johann Gottfried Herder is presented as a German literary figure with an early and enduring acquaintance with Laurence Sterne. In student-era notebooks he possibly quoted The Life and Opinions of Tristram Shandy, Gentleman and Don Quixote; later he called a travel account “ein Capitel meines Shandyschen Romans” and exchanged Shandean names with Johann Georg Hamann, referring to himself as Yorick. He owned Sermons of Yorick in Zürich translation and read Sentimental Journey enthusiastically in autumn 1768. In subsequent writings he continued to admire Sterne’s humor, sentiment, and characterization of the French people, including after arriving in Paris, while criticizing Sterne’s “Säuereien und Zweideutigkeiten.” He is ultimately characterized as a clear-minded critic who recognized Sterne’s significance, with no reported later withdrawal of appreciation.

Among German literati, Herder is another representative of acquaintance with Ste

Herder quoted from Tristram Shandy and Don Quixote, called an account of his travels “ein Capitel meines Shandyschen Romans,” and exchanged Shandean names with Johann Georg Hamann. He read the Sentimental Journey in autumn 1768, admired Sterne’s humor and sentiment, possessed the sermons of Yorick in a Zürich translation, and criticized Sterne’s “Säuereien und Zweideutigkeiten.”

[37] Herder’s early acquaintance with Sterne has been already treated.

Johann Gottfried Herder continued to admire Laurence Sterne after his early acquaintance with him. Soon after arriving in Paris, he praised Sterne’s characterization of the French people to Hartknoch, and his fifth “Wäldchen” refers to Sterne.

] CHAPTER VII OPPOSITION TO STERNE AND HIS TYPE OF SENTIMENTALISM Sterne’s influence in Germany lived its own life, and gradually and imperceptibly died out of letters, as an actuating principle.

Johann Gottfried Herder was identified as one of the far-seeing, clear-minded critics who recognized Sterne as a significant figure. The text does not report any later withdrawal of his appreciation.
