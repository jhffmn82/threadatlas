Abstract written by a model

In The Library, Cinyras comes to Cyprus, founds Paphos, marries Metharme, has several children, gives breastplates to absent Agamemnon, and deceptively sends only one ship to the Greek expedition. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Cinyras is king of Cyprus and Myrrha’s father who is deceived into incest with her, discovers the crime, attempts to kill her, and later curses Myrrha and Adonis.

Cinyras in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Cinyras first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cinyras, son of Sandocus and Pharnace, comes to Cyprus with his people, founds Paphos, and marries Metharme. They have Oxyporus, Adonis, Orsedice, Laogore, and Braesia. He gives breastplates to absent Agamemnon, promises fifty ships to the Greek expedition, but sends one under a son of Mygdalion and molds the rest from earth before launching them at sea.

Cinyras in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Cinyras first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cinyras, king of Cyprus, is Myrrha’s father and Cenchris’s lawful husband. While choosing a husband for Myrrha, he is deceived by her nurse into repeatedly receiving Myrrha in his bed. He discovers the incest, draws his sword to kill her, and later, after learning she told Ammon of his behavior, curses Myrrha and Adonis.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

FABLE VIII. [X.298-518]

Cinyras is Myrrha's father and is considering which of her suitors she should marry. Deceived by the nurse, he receives Myrrha into his bed, but later discovers that his paramour is his own daughter and draws his sword to kill her.

[Footnote 42: _The Panchæan land._--Ver. 309. Panchæa was a region

Cinyras, identified as the grandfather of Adonis, is said by Le Clerc to have become drunk and fallen asleep in an indecent posture. After learning that Myrrha had told Ammon of his behavior, he became indignant and cursed Myrrha and Adonis.
