This is a long paper in the Proceedings of the 63rd Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics, presenting MAIN-RAG, or Multi-Agent Filtering Retrieval-Augmented Generation, by Chia-Yuan Chang, Zhimeng Jiang, Vineeth Rakesh, Menghai Pan, Chin-Chia Michael Yeh, Guanchu Wang, Mingzhi Hu, Zhichao Xu, Yan Zheng, Mahashweta Das, and Na Zou. The paper addresses a central weakness of retrieval-augmented generation: although retrieval can ground Large Language Model responses in external, real-time information, retrieved sets often contain irrelevant or noisy documents that mislead models, lower reliability, and add computation. After motivating this problem and situating it among training-based and training-free RAG methods, the paper formalizes queries, retrieved documents, relevance scores, rankings, adaptive thresholds, and filtered document sets. It then introduces the training-free MAIN-RAG pipeline. Agent-1, the Predictor, produces answers using individual documents. Agent-2, the Judge, evaluates Document-Query-Answer Triplets with Yes/No judgments; relevance is measured by the difference between the log probabilities of “Yes” and “No.” These scores filter and reorder documents. Agent-3, the Final-Predictor, generates the final answer from the retained, ordered evidence. A query-specific adaptive judge bar is based on average document scores and can be adjusted with the candidate set’s standard deviation. Experiments in zero-shot settings compare MAIN-RAG with retrieval and non-retrieval baselines on ARC-Challenge, TriviaQA-unfiltered, PopQA, and ALCE-ASQA. Using three pre-trained LLM agents and a Contriever-MS MARCO retriever, MAIN-RAG generally surpasses the listed training-free and non-retrieval systems; MAIN-RAG-Llama38B has the strongest reported results among listed training-free systems. The reported gains are 2–11% in answer accuracy, alongside fewer irrelevant documents and more consistent responses. Ablations support the default adaptive judge bar and descending document order, though case studies show both successful and unsuccessful filtering. The paper concludes that multi-agent filtering and ranking improve relevant-information recall while limiting noise. It proposes finer-grained thresholds, uses beyond question answering, and human feedback or tuning-based filtering as future work. It also notes environmental risk from LLM inference and retrieved-document workflows, and seeks to reduce repetitive experiments through controlled settings.

Abstract

The abstract concerns Large Language Models (LLMs), Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), and the proposed Multi-Agent Filtering Retrieval-Augmented Generation (MAIN-RAG) framework. It explains that RAG uses external, real-time information to ground LLM responses, but existing RAG systems often retrieve irrelevant or noisy documents that reduce reliability and increase computational overhead. MAIN-RAG uses multiple LLM agents, adaptive relevance filtering, and inter-agent consensus to select higher-quality documents without training or fine-tuning. Across four QA benchmarks, MAIN-RAG improves answer accuracy by 2–11% while reducing irrelevant retrieved documents and improving response consistency.

The experiments evaluate MAIN-RAG and retrieval and non-retrieval baselines on ARC-Challenge, TriviaQA-unfiltered, PopQA, and ALCE-ASQA in zero-shot settings. MAIN-RAG uses three pre-trained LLM agents, a Contriever-MS MARCO retriever, adaptive document filtering, and document ranking. 5 / Conclusion and Future Work

The unit concludes by presenting MAIN-RAG, a training-free, multi-agent framework for addressing noisy document retrieval in RAG. MAIN-RAG uses multiple LLM agents to filter and rank retrieved documents, improving recall of relevant information while reducing irrelevant content. Its adaptive judge bar adjusts to the score distribution of relevant and noisy documents across queries, and experiments show that it outperforms training-free RAG baselines on various QA benchmarks. Future work includes developing a more fine-grained adaptive judge bar, applying the framework beyond question answering, and incorporating human feedback or tuning-based approaches to improve document filtering.

6 / Limitations

The authors report experiments on four datasets using two different pre-trained LLM architectures. The experiments primarily examine LLM inference with retrieved external documents under a RAG workflow. The authors acknowledge that this workflow contributes to carbon emissions and creates a potential environmental risk. They aim to mitigate this risk by reducing repetitive experiments through more predictable outcomes and controlled experimental settings.
