sunflower

plant · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Clytie's sunflower, Turn-sole, a sunflower, large yellow plant, larger flower, the Heliotrope, the sunflower, the true classical heliotrope

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the sunflower is associated with Clytie’s metamorphosis, remains rooted while turning toward the Sun, and is said by some naturalists to kill a nearby frankincense tree. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Clytie's sunflower is the flower formed from Clytie’s face after her limbs rooted in the ground; it turns on its stem to face the sun throughout the day and represents constancy.

Abstract written by a model

The sunflower is a plant associated with Clytie’s metamorphosis after she pines away for the Sun. Rooted in the ground, it continues to turn toward the Sun and retain her passion. Some naturalists say it kills a nearby frankincense tree; Pliny recorded other properties.
