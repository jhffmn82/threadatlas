Nyctimene in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Nyctimene, child of Nycteus, commits incest with her father and is changed into a bird of the night for the crime. Ashamed, she avoids human gaze and light, conceals herself in darkness, and is expelled from the sky by other birds. She succeeds the crow in a former honor.

opening

Nyctimene is described as having been made a bird for a horrid crime. The virgin says that Nyctimene has succeeded her in honor.

opening

Nyctimene defiled the bed of her father and was transformed into a bird. Conscious of her crime, she avoids human gaze and daylight, hides in darkness, and is expelled from the sky by the other birds.
