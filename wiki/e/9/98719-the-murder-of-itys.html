the murder of Itys

event · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Itys's murder, having murdered him, horrible deed, infernal murder, murder of Itys, murdered him, so barbarously murdered, the crime
  • In The Library, it is the crime Procne committed after learning of Philomela’s suffering, when she killed and cooked Itys and served him to Tereus unaware of the meal’s contents.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, it is Progne’s frantic act in which Progne and Philomela kill Itys, mangle and cook his body, with Progne striking him with a sword and Philomela cutting his throat.

the murder of Itys in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The murder of Itys is an event in which Progne and Philomela kill Itys in a remote part of the house: Progne strikes him with a sword, Philomela cuts his throat, and they mangle and cook his body. It is described as Progne's frantic act.

opening

Progne and Philomela murder Itys in a remote part of the house. Progne strikes him with a sword, and Philomela cuts his throat; they then mangle and cook his body.

notes .. notes

The murder of Itys is described as an act committed by Progne in a frantic fit, after which the swallow represents her restless search for her son. Homer preserves a different tradition in which Ædon mistakenly kills her son Itylus while intending to kill her nephew.
