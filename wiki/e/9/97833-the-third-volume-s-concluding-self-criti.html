the third volume’s concluding self-criticism in Laurence Sterne in Germany

Harvey Waterman Thayer · 1905

The third volume’s concluding self-criticism is an unusually candid end-of-book condemnation of Schummel’s three-volume work, admitting imitation of Sterne, incoherent narration, bad taste, indelicacy, failed whimsical mannerisms, and failure to characterize Germans as Sterne characterized English and French people.

The third volume’s concluding self-criticism is presented as an unusually candid condemnation of Schummel’s own extensive work. Schummel acknowledges imitation, bad taste, indelicacy, failed attempts at whimsical mannerisms, and the inadequacy of his effort to characterize the German people as Sterne characterized the English and French.
