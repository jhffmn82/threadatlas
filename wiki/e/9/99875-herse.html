Herse in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Herse is a daughter of Cecrops and sister of Aglauros, a chaste virgin who occupies the middle bed-chamber in Cecrops’s house. Returning from Minerva’s citadel, she surpasses the other virgins in beauty, drawing Mercury’s love. Her marriage to Mercury is fortunate; she is also recorded as Cephalus’s mother. Temples of Herse and Aglauros stand at Athens.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Herse is one of Cecrops's daughters and occupies the middle bed-chamber. Among the virgins returning from Minerva's citadel, she surpasses the others in beauty and is the ornament of the ceremony and her companions; Mercury falls in love with her.

notes .. notes

Herse was one of the daughters of Cecrops, king of Athens. One Mercury was probably in love with her.

opening

Herse is Aglauros’s sister. She is described as fortunate because of her marriage to Mercury.
