Inachus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Inachus is a river of Argolis, now known as Naio, and a God; accounts place its rise in Lycæus or Artemisium and name Phoroneus or Oceanus as parent. Greek authors call him Argos’s first king and Io’s father. He is said to settle in Greece as Isis worship is received, and hides in his deepest cavern, weeping for Io and swelling his waters.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Inachus is absent from the gathering of the rivers and remains hidden in his deepest cavern. He increases his waters with tears and mourns Io, fearing that she is dead or among the shades below.

notes .. notes

Inachus is described as the first king of Argos and the father of Io according to Greek authors. He is also said to have gone to settle in Greece, where the worship of Isis was received.

Inachus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Inachus, son of Ocean and Tethys, fathers Phoroneus and Aegialeus with Melia; a river in Argos is named after him.
