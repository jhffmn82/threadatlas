Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the ship leaves harbor carrying Ceyx and is wrecked and sunk by violent winds and waves in the Ægean Sea. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Ceyx's ship leaves the harbor and is destroyed at sea when lightning shatters its mast, its rudder breaks, and waves crush it into fragments.

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where the ship first appears

Abstract written by a model

A Trachinian ship, equipped with oars, rigging, sails, and crew, leaves harbor carrying Ceyx. In the Ægean Sea, increasingly violent South winds and waves open its seams, admit water, break mast and helm, shatter it, and sink it in a shipwreck.

FABLE VII. [XI.410-748]

The ship leaves harbor with its oars, rigging, sails, and crew, but encounters increasingly violent winds and waves. Its seams open, water enters, the mast and helm break, and it is eventually sunk by the storm.
