ants in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where ants first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ants are insects that gather corn in a long train, carry burdens in their little mouths, and follow tracks in an oak’s wrinkled bark. A report, attributed to similarity between their name and the Greek word μύρμηξ, says Jupiter transforms them into upright men, removing their leanness, many feet, and black color.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

The ants gather grain in a long train, carrying heavy burdens in their mouths and following tracks in the oak's wrinkled bark. Jupiter transforms them into upright human beings, removing their former leanness, many feet, and black color.

notes .. notes

Ants are connected with the Myrmidons through the similarity between their name and the Greek word μύρμηξ, meaning ‘an ant.’ This resemblance is said to have given rise to the report that Jupiter transformed ants into men.

Aeacus sees ants laboring on a sacred oak, carrying grains in a line, and asks Jupiter for citizens as numerous as they are. In his dream, the ants grow larger and take human form.
