Lycaon's transformation into a wolf

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Lycaon's transformation into a wolf is described as Jupiter's punishment after Lycaon kills and serves the hostage. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, wolf transformation is described as Lycaon's change from a man into a wolf after sacrificing a baby to Zeus Lycæus.

wolf transformation in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where wolf transformation first appears

Abstract written by a model

Wolf transformation is a change from a man into a wolf, said to occur to Lycaon after sacrificing a baby to Zeus Lycæus; human form returns after ten months without meat, but tasting meat leaves him a beast.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

Lycaon was said to have become a wolf immediately after sacrificing a baby to Zeus Lycæus. A later tradition held that a person transformed into a wolf at the Lycæa could become human again after abstaining from meat for ten months, but would remain a beast if he tasted meat.
