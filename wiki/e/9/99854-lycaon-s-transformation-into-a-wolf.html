Lycaon's transformation into a wolf in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Lycaon's transformation occurs as Jupiter's punishment after Lycaon kills and serves the hostage. His garments become hair, his arms become legs, and he becomes a wolf while retaining vestiges of his former form.

wolf transformation in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Wolf transformation is a change from a man into a wolf, said to occur to Lycaon after sacrificing a baby to Zeus Lycæus; human form returns after ten months without meat, but tasting meat leaves him a beast.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

Lycaon was said to have become a wolf immediately after sacrificing a baby to Zeus Lycæus. A later tradition held that a person transformed into a wolf at the Lycæa could become human again after abstaining from meat for ten months, but would remain a beast if he tasted meat.
