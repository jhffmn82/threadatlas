the flood of Deucalion

event · 4 works · 8 quoted facts · 7 connections
Also called: Deucalion's flood, Deucalion’s flood, Flood in Deucalion’s time, Noah's flood, Noah’s flood, flood, flood in the age of Deucalion, flood of Deucalion, great flood, the Flood, the flood, the flood in the age of Deucalion, the universal deluge, ὁ ἐπὶ Δευκαλίωνος κατακλυσμὸς
  • In The Library, it is said to occur during Cranaus’s reign and is caused by heavy rains.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, it covers the earth with sea and heavenly rain, while Deucalion and Pyrrha survive; as its waters recede, slime produces reptiles before the sun dissipates noxious exhalations.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, it is compared with Noah’s flood; both accounts include virtuous survivors, a solemn offering or altar after deliverance, and a promise or claim that the flood would be the last.
  • In The Library, it occurs after Nyctimus takes the kingdom, when Zeus pours heavy rain from heaven, possibly because of Lycaon’s sons’ impiety; it destroys most of Greece and its people, but Deucalion and Pyrrha survive in their chest.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, it is said to have destroyed the town built by Parnassus; the human beings who escaped follow wolves and other wild beasts to Mount Parnassus and found Lycorea.

the flood of Deucalion in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The flood of Deucalion, subject of Ovid’s narrative, covers the earth with sea and heavenly rain; Deucalion and Pyrrha survive. As its waters recede, they leave slime, from which reptiles arise before the sun dissipates noxious exhalations.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

notes .. notes

The flood of Deucalion is the subject of Ovid’s narrative in this unit. Its waters are said to have included both the sea and rain from heaven, and its survivors were Deucalion and Pyrrha.

notes .. notes

The flood is described as leaving slime as its waters receded. Reptiles were believed to have been produced from this slime and to have disappeared when the sun’s heat dissipated the resulting noxious exhalations.
