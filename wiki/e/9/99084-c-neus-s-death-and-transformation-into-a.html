Cæneus's death and transformation into a bird

event · 1 work · 3 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Cæneus’s death, a bird with tawny wings, go forth into the liquid air, his body was hurled to roomy Tartarus, pressed to death

Abstract written by a model

Cæneus's death and transformation occur when the Centaurs, unable to wound him with weapons, bury him beneath piled tree trunks. The weight covers his face and head and deprives him of air, pressing him to death. Afterward, Neptune changes his body into a tawny-winged bird seen emerging from the pile.

The Centaurs cannot wound Cæneus with their weapons, so they pile tree trunks upon him. The weight presses him to death, after which Neptune changes his body into a bird.

The Centaurs bury Cæneus under trees until the weight covers his face and head and leaves him without air. Some say that the wood sent his body to Tartarus, but Mopsus sees a tawny-winged bird emerge from the pile and declares it to be Cæneus, once the greatest of men and now the only bird of its kind.
