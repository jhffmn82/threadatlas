Philomela in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Philomela is a daughter of Pandion and sister of Progne, described as a Cecropian or Athenian Nymph. Wanting to visit Progne, she is taken by Tereus to Thrace, where he rapes her, cuts out her tongue, and imprisons her in an ancient wood. Unable to speak, she reveals his crime to Progne through a woven message. Rescued and brought to Tereus's house, she helps Progne murder Itys and throws Itys's bloody head at Tereus. After his pursuit, Philomela is transformed into a nightingale; other accounts call her a swallow. Her bird form’s hiding in woods and thickets represents her concealed misfortunes and sorrows.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

front matter

Philomela is taken by Tereus to his kingdom and suffers violence from him. She is eventually changed into a nightingale.

opening

Philomela eagerly asks Pandion to let her visit Progne, but Tereus ravishes her after bringing her to Thrace. He imprisons her and cuts out her tongue; she later finds a way to tell Progne about her suffering by weaving a message into cloth.

opening

Philomela is rescued from captivity and brought to the house of Tereus. She helps Progne murder Itys, throws his bloody head at Tereus, and becomes a nightingale.

notes .. notes

Philomela is described as being transformed into a nightingale in the principal account, where the bird’s concealment among woods and thickets represents the concealment of her misfortunes and sorrows. Anacreon and Apollodorus instead say that Philomela was changed into a swallow.
