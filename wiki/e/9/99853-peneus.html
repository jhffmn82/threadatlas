Peneus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Peneus is a divine river of Thessaly, flowing from the bottom of Pindus through Tempe, where it has a rocky cavern home, gives law to water Nymphs, and runs with foaming, far-heard waves, though Ælian calls its stream gentle and sluggish. Its course was probably stopped by an earthquake before Thessaly was inundated. As Daphne’s father, it urges her to marry; she seeks its aid while fleeing Apollo.

opening

Peneus is Daphne’s father, and he repeatedly urges her to accept a husband and give him grandchildren. Daphne appeals to him for aid when Apollo is about to overtake her.

notes .. notes

Peneus is identified as a river of Thessaly. Daphne is said to have fled toward its bank, where one explanation holds that she was accidentally precipitated into the river and perished in Apollo’s sight.

opening

Peneus flows from the bottom of Pindus through Tempe with foaming waves. Its fall gathers clouds and spray, and its noise reaches places far away.

Peneus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Peneus was the river god and Daphne's father. When Apollo pursued Daphne, she called upon Peneus to open the earth or change her form, and she was changed into a tree.
