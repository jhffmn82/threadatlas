Abstract written by a model

The Sibyls are women of antiquity who, in frenzied enthusiasm, utter obscure prophetic sentences. Ancient writers variously counted one to ten, naming several regional Sibyls; Plato and others regarded them as benefactors of mankind.

FABLE III. [XIV.101-153]

The Sibyls were women who, in a state of frenzied enthusiasm, uttered obscure sentences regarded as prophecies. Ancient writers differed about their number, identifying anywhere from one to ten, including the Delphian, Erythræan, Cumæan, Samian, Hellespontine, Libyan, Persian or Babylonian, Phrygian, and Tiburtine Sibyls.
