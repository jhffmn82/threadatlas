Pierides

Also called: foolish sisters, his daughters, the Pierides

Abstract written by a model

The Pierides are nine sisters, daughters of Pierus and Evippe. They challenge the Muses in a contest of voice and skill, are defeated, and become magpies, increasing the number of birds; the change symbolizes poor, empty stories associated with Pierus’s works.

Pierides is in one work on this site, The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

opening

The Pierides are nine sisters, daughters of Pierus and Evippe, who challenged the Muses to a contest of voice and skill. After being defeated, they increased the number of the birds by becoming magpies.

notes .. notes

The Pierides challenged the Muses, although this challenge is not mentioned by writers before Ovid. They were said to have been changed into magpies, symbolizing the poor and empty stories associated with Pierus’s works.
