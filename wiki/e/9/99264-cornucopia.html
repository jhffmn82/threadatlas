Cornucopia

object · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: my horn, one horn, the Horn of Plenty, the horn of plenty, this horn
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the Horn of Plenty, also called Cornucopia, is a horn Hercules breaks from Acheloüs that Naiads fill with Autumn fruits and flowers, making it a symbol of abundance.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Cornucopia is Achelous’s severed horn, consecrated and flower-filled by Naiades, adopted and named by Plenty, and able to fill with whatever its possessor wished.

Achelous’s severed horn, consecrated and flower-filled by Naiades, was adopted and named Cornucopia by Plenty; it could fill with whatever its possessor wished.
