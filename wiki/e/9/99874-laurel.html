laurel in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Laurel, a tree, begins absent at the first Pythian games and becomes the form Daphne takes after praying for rescue. Apollo adopts it as his sacred tree, wears its lasting foliage, and links it to his lyre and quiver. It accompanies Roman triumphs, guards oak at Augustus’s gate-posts, and stands faithfully before his doors.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

The laurel is the form Daphne takes after her prayer for rescue. Apollo adopts it as his tree and says it will adorn his hair, lyre, and quiver, accompany triumphal processions, and stand as a guardian before Augustus’s doors.

notes .. notes

Laurel is associated with Daphne’s transformation and with Apollo’s lyre, triumphs, and civic honors. The explanation proposes that laurels growing near the place where Daphne perished, or the Greek meaning of her name, may have inspired the transformation story.

laurel tree in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

remains_green eternal youth [by Apollo's promise] The laurel tree was Daphne's transformed form. Apollo declared that he would wear it as his crown and use it to decorate his harp and quiver, and that it would remain eternally green without decay.
