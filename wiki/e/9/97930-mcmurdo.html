Abstract written by a model

McMurdo is a young Irishman, a well-educated, sociable, quick-witted traveler from Chicago who arrives in Vermissa as a self-confessed fugitive from justice. Fresh-complexioned and middle-sized, with brown hair, large gray eyes, spectacles, and a loaded navy revolver, he is charming and communicative but prone to fierce anger and bitterly contemptuous of police. He is also a counterfeiter and murderer: in Chicago he made false dollars and killed Jonas Pinto after Pinto threatened to expose their work. At Jacob Shafter’s boarding house, McMurdo becomes conspicuous through his wit, singing, education, and fearlessness. He loves Ettie Shafter and clashes with Ted Baldwin over her. His prior membership in Lodge 29 of the Eminent Order of Freemen brings him to Boss McGinty and Lodge 341 in Vermissa. He proves himself dangerous by admitting his crimes, holding McGinty at pistol point during a test, enduring branding and initiation, swearing secrecy and fidelity, and accepting McGinty’s authority. McMurdo rapidly gains influence among the lodge. He approves its violence, assists in the attack on James Stanger while preventing Baldwin from killing him, survives arrest and acquittal, and earns a reputation for fighting and for planning violent work. Though Ettie asks him to leave, he refuses, promising to find an honourable way out within a year. He accepts an assignment against Chester Wilcox, leads Manders and Reilly in an unsuccessful killing attempt, and continues pursuing Wilcox after an ensuing shooting. By the end, McMurdo is Inner Deacon, necessary to lodge councils, and considered a possible successor to McGinty. As danger from the Pinkerton investigation rises, he destroys incriminating papers, warns the lodge, recognizes Birdy Edwards under another name, and helps plan an ambush. At the same time, he and Ettie prepare to escape the valley together.

McMurdo in The Valley of Fear

Narrative, section by section written by a model

PART II / Chapter I

John McMurdo is a young Irishman traveling from Chicago to Vermissa. He carries a fully loaded navy revolver, belongs to the Eminent Order of Freemen, and admits he left Chicago because he was in deep trouble, possibly involving a penitentiary sentence or worse. He angrily confronts two policemen on the train, then takes a room at Jacob Shafter’s house and immediately attracts attention in Vermissa.

Chapter II

McMurdo becomes the most important person among the boarders at Shafter's, gaining attention through his wit, singing, education, charm, and capacity for sudden anger. He falls in love with Ettie, refuses to leave the valley despite threats from Baldwin and the Scowrers, and reveals that he is himself a member of the Freemen. After Baldwin threatens him, McMurdo decides to go at once to see Boss McGinty.

McMurdo visits Boss McGinty at McGinty's saloon after being directed there by Brother Scanlan of Lodge 341. He proves that he belongs to Lodge 29 in Chicago and admits that he made counterfeit dollars and killed Jonas Pinto before coming to the coal country. He reveals that he was covering McGinty with a cocked pistol during McGinty's test and later challenges Ted Baldwin over a young lady.

Chapter III

Jack McMurdo moves from old Jacob Shafter’s lodgings to the Widow MacNamara’s, where he lives with Scanlan. He becomes popular with the local gang because of his fearlessness, fighting ability, and reputation from Chicago, and he is initiated into Lodge 341 after enduring its tests. He accepts the lodge’s oath, secrecy, and the authority of the Bodymaster, and receives a brand on his forearm.

When the drinks which followed the ceremony of initiation had all been

McMurdo attends his first lodge meeting and openly approves of its violent methods. He volunteers to assist the lodge when needed, sings to win the members' approval, and joins Ted Baldwin's group in attacking James Stanger. During the attack, he draws his pistol and stops Baldwin from continuing to beat Stanger, arguing that the Bodymaster ordered that the editor not be killed.

Chapter IV

McMurdo suffers a headache and a swollen, branded arm after his lodge initiation, then meets Brother Morris on Miller Hill. He conceals the meeting from Boss McGinty, is arrested by Captain Marvin for the attack on the Herald office, and is released after the court finds the evidence insufficient.

Chapter V

Jack McMurdo gains prestige among the Scowrers after his arrest and acquittal, and they regard him as capable of devising and carrying out violent schemes. He refuses Ettie Shafter’s request that he leave the lodge, but promises that within a year he will find an honourable way to leave the valley. After witnessing the Crow Hill murders, he accepts Boss McGinty’s assignment to attack Chester Wilcox.

McMurdo thought long and deeply over the commission which had been so

McMurdo planned and carried out an attempt to kill Chester Wilcox with Manders and Reilly. After learning that Wilcox had escaped, he vowed to continue pursuing him, and he was believed to be responsible for the later ambuscade in which Wilcox was shot at.

Chapter VI

McMurdo has become Inner Deacon and an influential adviser to the Freemen. After learning of the Pinkerton investigation, he destroys incriminating papers, warns the lodge, identifies Birdy Edwards as Steve Wilson, and plans to lure him into an ambush. He also arranges an escape plan with Ettie Shafter.
