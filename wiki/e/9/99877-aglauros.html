Aglauros in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Aglauros, daughter of Cecrops and sister of Herse, first calls her sisters cowards and unties a basket, revealing Ericthonius with a dragon beside him; she has also seen Minerva’s hidden mysteries with a profane hand. In Cecrops’s left-hand bed-chamber, she notices Mercury seeking Herse, demands gold to assist him, and sends him away. Envy infects her, turning her jealousy of Herse’s fortune into obstinate resistance at Herse’s threshold. When Mercury opens the door, Aglauros stiffens and gradually becomes a bloodless stone statue. Temples of Herse and Aglauros later stand at Athens.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Aglauros calls her sisters cowards and unties the knots of the basket. She and her sisters then see a child with a dragon extended beside him.

opening

Aglauros occupies the left-hand bed-chamber and is the first to notice Mercury approaching. She asks who he is and why he has come, then demands gold for helping him approach Herse and makes him leave the house.

notes .. notes

Aglauros became jealous of Herse.

opening

Aglauros is infected by Envy and becomes consumed by jealousy of Herse’s good fortune. She blocks Mercury at the threshold, but as he opens the door, her body stiffens and gradually becomes a bloodless statue.
