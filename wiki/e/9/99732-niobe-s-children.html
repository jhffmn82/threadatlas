Niobe's children in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Niobe's children are victims of the plague in one historical interpretation; mythically Apollo kills the sons and Diana the daughters. Ancient sources disagree on their number and describe deaths during horse management, hunting on Mount Cithæron, or at Thebes.

Ancient sources give different numbers for Niobe's children, including twelve, five, fourteen, and twenty. Ovid says that her sons were killed while managing their horses, while Pausanias places their deaths during hunting on Mount Cithæron and says that her daughters died at Thebes.

Niobe's children in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Niobe's children are a group listed here as seven sons and seven daughters, including Ethodaia, Cleodoxa, Astyoche, Phthia, Pelopia, Astycratia, and Ogygia. Artemis killed the daughters and Apollo the sons, though accounts differ on survivors and total numbers.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Γαμεῖ δὲ Ζῆθος μὲν Θήβην, ad ἧς ἡ πόλιες

Niobe’s children are listed in this account as seven sons—Sipylus, Eupinytus, Ismenus, Damasichthon, Agenor, Phaedimus, and Tantalus—and seven daughters—Ethodaia, Cleodoxa, Astyoche, Phthia, Pelopia, Astycratia, and Ogygia. Artemis killed the daughters and Apollo killed the sons, although Amphion, Chloris, or in another account Amyclas and Meliboea were said to have survived; other writers gave different numbers of children.
