Yuba Bill in From Sand Hill to Pine

Bret Harte · 1894-04-24

Yuba Bill is a stagecoach driver and stage driver, known for skillful driving, supreme control of things, and well-known coolness. Rough and imperious toward passengers and strangers, he nevertheless acts squarely toward Snapshot Harry and has a duty to kill him if given the chance. When a fallen tree blocks the road, Bill stops the coach, orders a stranger to fetch a wood axe, impatiently directs the clearing, and resumes the journey. At a later obstruction, a masked horseman halts the coach and takes the express box; Bill prevents the expressman from firing, accepts the surrender, and urges Brice to stay calm. He suspects sneaks or traitors among the passengers rather than Snapshot Harry. Local press praises his composure and notes Hiram Tarbox's warning about the obstruction. Bill learns at the station of an earlier robbery, meaning his coach was robbed twice that evening, a fact Henry enjoys. He also reports Brice's conduct to the company.

BILSON'S HOUSEKEEPER FROM SAND HILL TO PINE A NIECE OF SNAPSHOT HARRY'S I There was a slight jarring though the whole frame of the coach, a grinding and hissing from the brakes, and then a sudden jolt as the vehicle ran upon and recoiled from the taut pole-straps of the now arrested horses.

Yuba Bill, the stagecoach driver, stops the coach when a fallen tree blocks the road and orders the stranger to bring a large wood axe. He is impatient with the passengers and the cabin’s occupants, but leads the road-clearing effort and returns after the obstruction is removed.

Five minutes after Bill had gathered up the reins, they reached the scene of obs

Yuba Bill guides the coach through the obstructed road and stops when a masked horseman orders him to halt. He prevents the expressman from using his rifle, has him surrender the express box, and later urges Brice to remain calm. Bill believes Snapshot Harry was not involved in the theft of the treasure and suggests that sneaks or traitors among the passengers took it.

II The robbery furnished the usual amount of copy for the local press.

Yuba Bill is praised in the local press for his well-known coolness during the robbery. The newspaper account also says that Hiram Tarbox timely warned him about the obstruction.

When Brice had partaken of the whiskey, the stranger, who had kept silence, poin

Yuba Bill did not know about the first robbery when it occurred, but Brice told him at the station. Henry takes pleasure in the fact that Yuba Bill and his coach were robbed twice in one evening.
