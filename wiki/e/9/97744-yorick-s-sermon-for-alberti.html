Yorick’s sermon for Alberti in Laurence Sterne in Germany

Harvey Waterman Thayer · 1905

Yorick’s sermon for Alberti is a work written by Gotthold Ephraim Lessing as a trick connected with Alberti’s controversy with Goeze. Privately printed in part by Bode, it opens with Uncle Toby and Corporal Trim aiding a wounded French veteran, imitating Yorick’s sympathetic, tolerant, whimsical simplicity.

Yorick’s sermon for Alberti was written by Lessing as a trick connected with Alberti’s controversy with Goeze. Its title and part of its introduction were privately printed by Bode, and Nicolai later recalled an introduction in which Uncle Toby and Trim aid a wounded French veteran in a deliberately Sternean manner.
