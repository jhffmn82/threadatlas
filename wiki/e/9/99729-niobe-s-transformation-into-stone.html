Niobe's transformation into stone

event · 1 work · 3 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: became hardened by her woes, borne on a whirlwind, changed into a rock, she dissolves

Abstract written by a model

Niobe's transformation into stone is a divine punishment after the deaths of her husband and children. Her body becomes rigid and lifeless, losing color and movement, before a mighty wind carries her to her native land. Fixed on Mount Sipylus, she becomes marble or rock resembling a grieving woman and still distils tears.

Niobe's transformation into stone is in one work on this site, The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

opening

After the deaths of her children and husband, Niobe's body becomes rigid and lifeless: her color disappears, her eyes no longer move, her tongue and palate harden, and her limbs cannot move. A mighty wind carries her to her native land, where she is fixed upon a mountain and becomes marble that still distils tears.

notes .. notes

After the deaths of her husband and children, Niobe was said to have been carried by a whirlwind to a Lydian mountain and changed into a rock. The rock was described as resembling a woman grieving.
