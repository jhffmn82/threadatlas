Abstract written by a model

Sentimental journeying is a widely imitated German literary form associated with Yorick, Jacobi, Bock, Schummel, and later writers. It centers on emotional encounters, sympathy for poor people and animals, self-questioning about trivial acts, and seeking sentimental scenes. Influenced by Sterne, it was adopted by Thümmel with less whimsy; by the late eighteenth century it was satirized and called a forced mixture of names, wit, and weak humor.

The sentimental journey became a widely imitated German literary form associated with Yorick, Jacobi, Bock, Schummel, and later writers. Its conventions included emotional encounters, sympathy for the poor and animals, self-questioning over trivial actions, and the search for sentimental scenes. By the late eighteenth century, writers such as Hedemann were treating the form satirically, and a contemporary review defined it as a forced mixture of familiar names, wit, and weak humor.
