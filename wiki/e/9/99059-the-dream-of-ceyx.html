the dream of Ceyx

event · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: a dream, a vision, the sad vision, the undoubted and the real ghost of my husband, the vision of Ceyx

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, it appears to Halcyone after Ceyx’s shipwreck and reveals that he has died. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, it reveals Ceyx’s shipwreck death to Halcyone and asks her to mourn him.

Abstract written by a model

A vision of Ceyx, sent by Morpheus to Halcyone under Juno’s command, appears pale, wet, and grieving after shipwreck, revealing that Ceyx has died.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

In the meantime, the daughter of Æolus, ignorant of so great

The vision of Ceyx is sent to Halcyone by Morpheus under Juno’s command. It appears as a pale, wet, grieving image of the dead Ceyx and tells Halcyone that he has perished in the shipwreck.

Abstract written by a model

CEYX AND HALCYONE: OR, THE HALCYON BIRDS

The dream is sent to Halcyone through Morpheus, who takes Ceyx's appearance, voice, gestures, and manner. In the dream, the pale and water-soaked figure tells Halcyone that Ceyx is dead and asks her to mourn him.
