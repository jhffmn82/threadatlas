Roman Sibylline books in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

The Roman Sibylline books were a collection of verses attributed to the Sibyls, kept beneath the temple of Jupiter Capitolinus and consulted during public calamities, including pestilence; they directed bringing Æsculapius from Epidaurus to Rome. Guarded by successive officials and repeatedly revised, the original burned with the Capitol, and the remaining books were ultimately burned.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE III. [XIV.101-153]

The Romans kept a collection of verses attributed to the Sibyls and consulted them during public calamities. The books were first guarded by Duumvirs, later by Decemvirs and Quindecimvirs, and were repeatedly revised; the original collection was believed to have been destroyed when the Capitol burned, and the remaining books were ultimately ordered to be burned.
