Memnonides

group · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Memnon's birds, birds, children of the air, flying fowl, swift birds

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Memnonides are a flock of birds born from Memnon's funeral pyre that divide into opposing groups and perish fighting each year in honor of their parent. In The Fall of Troy, Memnon's birds are called Memnons by human tribes; they circle Memnon's tomb, wail, scatter dust, and battle around his barrow, with the victors striking the dead.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

FABLES III. AND IV. [XIII.439-622]

The Memnonides arise from the ashes and embers of Memnon's funeral pyre. They divide into two opposing groups, fight with beaks and claws, and are destined to perish in battle each year in honor of their parent.
