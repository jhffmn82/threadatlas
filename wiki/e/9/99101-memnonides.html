Memnonides

group · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Memnon's birds, birds, children of the air, flying fowl, swift birds
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Memnonides are a flock of birds born from Memnon's funeral pyre that divide into opposing groups and perish fighting each year in honor of their parent.
  • In The Fall of Troy, Memnon's birds are called Memnons by human tribes; they circle Memnon's tomb, wail, scatter dust, and battle around his barrow, with the victors striking the dead.

Memnonides in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Memnonides are a flock of birds born from Memnon's funeral pyre; each year, after the Sun passes through twelve signs, they divide into opposing groups and perish fighting in honor of their parent.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLES III. AND IV. [XIII.439-622]

The Memnonides arise from the ashes and embers of Memnon's funeral pyre. They divide into two opposing groups, fight with beaks and claws, and are destined to perish in battle each year in honor of their parent.

Memnon's birds in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

Memnon's birds are called Memnons by human tribes; they circle Memnon's tomb, wail, scatter dust, and battle around his barrow, victors striking the dead.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Then to the side of Aeacus' mighty son

Memnon's birds fly around his tomb with wailing cries and scatter dust onto his grave. They continue their battles around the barrow in memory of Memnon, with victors striking the vanquished dead.
