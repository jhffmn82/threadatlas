Tmolus

person · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: aged umpire, king of Lydia, mountain God, mountain-god, sacred Tmolus, umpire
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Tmolus was a mountain deity and king of Lydia who judged the contest between Pan and Apollo, favoring Apollo and displeasing Midas.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Tmolus was the mountain-god umpire of the contest between Pan and Apollo, awarding victory to Apollo after hearing his lyre.

Tmolus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Tmolus is a mountain deity and, according to Clitophon, king of Lydia; as umpire between Pan and Apollo, he hears both performances and favors Apollo, displeasing only Midas.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE III. [XI.146-193]

Tmolus serves as umpire of the contest between Pan and Apollo. After hearing both performances, he favors Apollo, and his judgment pleases everyone except Midas.
