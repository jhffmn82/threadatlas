  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII: Phaëton is hurled headlong and carried in a long tract through the air, like a star that appears to fall from a clear sky. Eridanus receives him in a distant part of the world. Phaëton’s fate caused his mother and sisters great grief. Their grief was said to have led to their deaths and transformation.
