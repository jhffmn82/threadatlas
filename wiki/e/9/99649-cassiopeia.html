Cassiopeia

person · 3 works · 3 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Cassiope, his queen, mother, that starred Aethiop queen, the mother, Κασσιεπείας
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, she is Andromeda's mother who boasts of her beauty against the Nereids, suffers punishment, mourns at the rock, and rejoices when Perseus rescues Andromeda.
  • In The Library, she is the wife of Cepheus and mother of Andromeda who claims to be more beautiful than the Nereids, angering them.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, she is Aethiopia’s queen and Andromeda’s mother who compares her beauty to the Sea-Nymphs’, provoking anger and a sea-monster, and becomes an upside-down constellation after death.

PERSEUS AND MEDUSA

Cassiopeia, queen of Aethiopia, compares her beauty with that of the Sea-Nymphs, provoking their anger and the sending of a sea-monster. After her death she is placed among the stars as the constellation Cassiopeia, where she is made to appear upside down for part of each night.
