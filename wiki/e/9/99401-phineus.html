Phineus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Phineus, brother of Cepheus, king of Bithynia, and Andromeda’s uncle and former betrothed, is described as most righteous. Angered when Andromeda is given to Perseus, he leads an attack, then surrenders after his followers are petrified. Perseus petrifies him with the Gorgon head, fixing his fearful, supplicant expression.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK THE FIFTH.

Phineus attacks Perseus because Andromeda, his former betrothed, has been given to Perseus. He leads a large force against Perseus but eventually begs for his life after seeing his followers turned to stone. Perseus turns him into a stone statue, preserving his fearful and supplicant expression.

Phineus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Phineus, a Thracian sage and Andromeda’s former betrothed, is blinded for cruelty, attacks her wedding, begs mercy after failing, and is turned to stone.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

PERSEUS AND MEDUSA

Phineus is the former betrothed of Andromeda and interrupts the wedding feast with armed followers, claiming her as his own. After his attack fails, he begs Perseus for mercy, but Perseus turns him to stone with Medusa's head.
