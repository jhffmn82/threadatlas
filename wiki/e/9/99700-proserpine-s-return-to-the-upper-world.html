Proserpine’s return to the upper world

event · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Proserpine is restored to her mother, Proserpine return to heaven, her complete release, her return, pass half the time with her mother, and the rest with her husband Pluto, the seasonal return of Proserpine, their separation

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, it is the return Jupiter permits if Proserpine has eaten no food in the Infernal Regions, prevented by her eating seven pomegranate seeds. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, it is the Fates-governed seasonal cycle in which Proserpine divides the year between Ceres and Pluto, bringing Spring and restored fertility.

the seasonal return of Proserpine in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where the seasonal return of Proserpine first appears

Abstract written by a model

Proserpine's return is a Fates-governed seasonal cycle symbolizing seed-corn's reappearance: after eating pomegranate pulp in the lower world, she must divide the year between Ceres and Pluto; her return brings Spring and restored fertility.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

PROSERPINE

Jupiter orders that Proserpine be released if she has eaten no food in the lower world, but she has eaten pulp from several pomegranate seeds offered by Pluto. As a compromise, she spends half the year with Ceres and half with Pluto. Her return to the upper world is associated with Spring and the restoration of the earth's fertility.
