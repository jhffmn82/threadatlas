Proserpine’s return to the upper world

  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, it is the return Jupiter permits if Proserpine has eaten no food in the Infernal Regions, prevented by her eating seven pomegranate seeds.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, it is the Fates-governed seasonal cycle in which Proserpine divides the year between Ceres and Pluto, bringing Spring and restored fertility.

Jupiter agrees that Proserpine may return to heaven if she has eaten no food in the Infernal Regions. Her eating of seven pomegranate seeds prevents the return permitted by that condition.

the seasonal return of Proserpine in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Proserpine's return is a Fates-governed seasonal cycle symbolizing seed-corn's reappearance: after eating pomegranate pulp in the lower world, she must divide the year between Ceres and Pluto; her return brings Spring and restored fertility.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

PROSERPINE

Jupiter orders that Proserpine be released if she has eaten no food in the lower world, but she has eaten pulp from several pomegranate seeds offered by Pluto. As a compromise, she spends half the year with Ceres and half with Pluto. Her return to the upper world is associated with Spring and the restoration of the earth's fertility.
