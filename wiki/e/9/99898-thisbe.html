Thisbe

Also called: Thisbe herself, the Babylonian Thisbe, the fairest maiden, the other preferred before {all} the damsels that the East contained, thy own Thisbe

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Thisbe is Pyramus’s beloved maiden, favored among the damsels of the East, who flees a lioness, returns to find Pyramus dead, and kills herself; their story warns youth and parents. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Thisbe is Babylonia’s fairest maiden who loves Pyramus, flees a lioness, loses her veil, and kills herself with his sword after finding him dying.

Abstract written by a model

Thisbe is a maiden and Pyramus’s beloved, favored among the damsels of the East. She reaches their meeting place first, flees from a lioness, returns to find Pyramus dead by his own hand, and kills herself; their story warns youth and parents.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

BOOK THE FOURTH.

Thisbe is described as the most favored of the damsels of the East and as Pyramus's beloved. She reaches the meeting place first, flees from a lioness, returns after Pyramus has killed himself, and then kills herself.

notes .. notes

Thisbe was one of the two lovers in the story of Pyramus and Thisbe. The story takes place in Babylon and is interpreted as a warning to youth and parents.
