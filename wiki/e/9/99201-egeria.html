Egeria

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Egeria is a nymph and Numa’s wife who advises him on laws, grieves after his death, and is transformed by Diana into a fountain. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Egeria is a Camena nymph who teaches Numa wisdom and law, then pines after his death and is changed into a fountain.

Nymph Egeria in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Nymph Egeria first appears

Abstract written by a model

Egeria is a nymph and Numa’s wife, associated with advising him on laws. After Numa’s death, she withdraws to Aricia’s groves and grieves so deeply that she disrupts Diana’s rites. Unconsoled, she is transformed by Diana into a cool, ever-flowing fountain in her grove.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

FABLES IV. V. AND VI. [XV.479-621]

Egeria, Numa's wife, withdrew to the groves of the valley of Aricia after his death and lamented so intensely that she disturbed Diana's sacred rites. She could not be consoled by Hippolytus's account of his own misfortunes, and Diana transformed her grieving body into a cool, ever-flowing fountain.

EXPLANATION.

The Nymph Egeria was said to have advised Numa on the laws he promulgated. The Romans believed in Numa’s conferences with her, and when they found only a fountain in the Arician grove, they explained her absence by claiming that she had been transformed.
