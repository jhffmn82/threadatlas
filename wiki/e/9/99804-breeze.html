breeze

natural phenomenon · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Sweet Air, most delightful {breeze}, sweet breeze, the breeze, the wandering wind
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the breeze refreshes Cephalus after hunting and heat, and his affectionate address to it leads Procris to believe he loves another.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the breeze is mistaken for a lover by an informer who deceives Procris; Cephalus's affectionate calls to it lead Procris to suspect he loves another, resulting in her death.

Ovid · 0008~

The breeze refreshes Cephalus after hunting and heat, coming from cool valleys; his affectionate address to it leads Procris to believe he loves another.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

opening

Cephalus sought the breeze as a refreshing relief from the heat and his hunting exertions. He addressed it with words of affection, and those ambiguous words led Procris to believe that he loved another.
