Gorgon’s head in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Gorgon’s head is Medusa’s snake-bearing head, cut from her neck by Perseus while she slept and carried as his memorable spoil after victory. Its most important power is petrification: in the fable, those who look at it become stone. Perseus sets it face-down on sea plants, whose poisonous touch changes them into coral. When surrounded by Phineus and a thousand followers, he reveals it, petrifying many attackers and finally Phineus; he also exposes it to Atlas, transforming Atlas into a mountain. It is said to produce serpents when Medusa’s bloody drops reach the ground. Notes interpret its effects as fear caused by Perseus’s fame or display of the head.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

The Gorgon’s head is the memorable spoil carried by Perseus after his victory. When Perseus exposes it to Atlas, its horrible features transform Atlas into a mountain.

notes .. notes

The myth says that Medusa’s head turned those who looked upon it into stone. One interpretation explains this as the terror produced by Perseus’s fame and by the Gorgon statue or its head, which he placed on the prow of his ship.

opening

Perseus carries the snake-bearing Gorgon's head and places it face-down on sea plants softened by leaves. He later explains that he cut it from Medusa's neck while she slept.

BOOK THE FIFTH.

Perseus produces the Gorgon head when he is surrounded by Phineus and a thousand followers. Those who look toward it become immovable stone, including many of Phineus’s men and eventually Phineus himself.

notes .. notes

The Gorgon’s head is said to have been the means by which Perseus allegedly petrified his enemies. The explanation presents this as a metaphorical account of the fear caused by his victories.

Gorgon’s head in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Gorgon’s head is Medusa’s head, taken after her beheading and carried by Perseus in a wallet. Ordered by Polydectes, Perseus uses it to turn Phineus, conspirators, Polydectes, and his friends to stone, then gives it to Athena for her shield.
