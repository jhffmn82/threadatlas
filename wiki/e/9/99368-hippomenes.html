Hippomenes in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Hippomenes, son of Megareus and a fourth-generation descendant of Neptune, falls in love with Atalanta and risks her deadly race. Venus gives him golden apples, which he uses to delay Atalanta and win her favor. After failing to thank Venus or offer frankincense, he and Atalanta defile a sanctuary and are transformed into lions.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

FABLE IX. [X.519-707]

Hippomenes becomes enamored of Atalanta and enters her deadly race despite the fate of earlier suitors. He invokes Venus, uses the golden apples she gives him to slow Atalanta, wins the race, and later incurs Venus's anger by failing to offer her thanks or frankincense.

[Footnote 52: _Pleases even Venus._--Ver. 524. According to

Hippomenes wins Atalanta’s favor by using golden apples to influence the outcome of their race. The explanation says that the story may be founded on the idea that he used bribes to gain the favor of his mistress.

Hippomenes in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Hippomenes accepts Atalanta's deadly racing challenge, uses Venus's golden apples to win her, then Cybele transforms them into lion and lioness after they offend her.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

MEDEA AND AESON

Hippomenes accepts Atalanta's deadly racing challenge after seeing her beauty and prays to Venus for help. He uses the three golden apples Venus gives him to make Atalanta stop during the race and wins her as his prize.
