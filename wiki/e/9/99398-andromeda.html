Andromeda in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Andromeda, daughter of Cepheus and Cassiope, is bound by her arms to a rock and exposed to a sea monster to be devoured, as punishment for her mother’s boast about beauty. Though betrothed to Phineus, she is rescued from sacrifice by Perseus, liberated after he turns Atlas into a mountain. After sea-weed is changed into coral, she marries Perseus and enters his native city with him. She is central to a story associated with Æthiopia or the coast of Africa near Joppa, where chain marks reportedly remained on a rock.

Andromeda is found with her arms bound to a rock, awaiting the sea monster because of her mother's boast about beauty. Perseus rescues her, and she becomes his wife.

notes .. notes

Andromeda is identified as the daughter of Cepheus and Cassiope. She is exposed to be devoured by the sea monster, but Perseus rescues her.

BOOK THE FIFTH.

Andromeda is identified as Phineus’s former betrothed and Perseus’s new wife. Her father, Cepheus, says that Perseus saved her from being sacrificed to the sea monster. She later enters Perseus’s native city with him.

notes .. notes

Andromeda is the central figure in the story associated by some writers with Æthiopia and by others with the coast of Africa near Joppa. Josephus reportedly said that the marks of the chains with which she was fastened remained on a rock in his time.

Andromeda in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Andromeda, daughter of Cepheus and Cassiepea, was first betrothed to Phineus. During a calamity, she was exposed as sea-monster prey and bound to a rock. Perseus saw and loved her, killed the monster, rescued and married her, and had children with her.
