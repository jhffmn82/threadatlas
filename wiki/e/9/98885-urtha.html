In Grampa in Oz, Urtha is a flower maiden and flower fairy brought to life in Gorba’s garden who joins the travelers, survives their dangers, and helps them reach Oz. In Grampa in Oz, Urtha, Princess of Perhaps City, is a flower fairy who disappears with Abrog, is mistreated by Play Fellows, and escapes through a wicket gate. In Grampa in Oz, Urtha is the lost Princess of Perhaps City, transformed into a flower fairy by Abrog’s spell until Grampa restores her to her Princess form.

Urtha in Grampa in Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1924 · the work’s page · read it where Urtha first appears

Abstract written by a model

Urtha is a little flower maiden and flower fairy, called a Lady of Flowers: her face, body, hair, and clothing are made of blossoms, vines, flowering fern, and posies. The old wizard names her Urtha because she is made of earth. Before awakening, she grows in Gorba’s garden, remembers nothing beyond it, and is brought to life by a gold watering can. She is not the Princess, though she is later described as a Princess of Ragbad. Urtha joins Tatters, Grampa, and Bill in leaving the garden. Fresh, cheerful, gentle, and anxious for her companions, she comforts Grampa, makes a daisy chain for Bill, and refuses to be left behind. Protected by the wizard’s medicine, she survives the underground lake, fire, volcano, and snow. Wherever she steps, flowers grow; in the frozen land her feet warm the snow, though this angers the islanders. She weeps for Enorma and is comforted by Tatters. At Isa Poso, Urtha warns Tatters and Grampa to flee and recalls the medicine. Yaga’s tobacco transforms her into a vari-colored crow, allowing her to fly toward Oz with the company. Even in the sky, her movement brings forth flowers. She welcomes new friends, helps resolve a disagreement about Princess and Prince, gathers berries, and plays scrum. Reunited with Mrs Sew-and-Sew in Ragbad, she leads the Ragbad Quadrille with the Prince and is happy with Tatters.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

9 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 6

Urtha is a little flower maiden whose face, body, hair, and clothing are made from blossoms, vines, and flowering fern. She says that the old wizard called her Urtha because she is made of earth and that she remembers nothing beyond the garden. After being brought to life, she joins Tatters, Grampa, and Bill on their journey through the garden.

CHAPTER 7

Urtha remains fresh and cheerful in the garden, weaving a daisy chain for Bill. She comforts Grampa about his chimney, watches while Tatters cures him, and anxiously follows the group toward the stairway.

CHAPTER 10

Urtha falls into the underground lake and floats safely after the wizard’s medicine protects her from the heat. She enjoys Fire Island, dances on its burning sands, handles fire flowers without being burned, and refuses to remain behind when the others leave for Blazes.

CHAPTER 11

Urtha is frightened while entering the dark tower and volcano but survives the eruption without crushing any of her posies. She delights in seeing the stars for the first time, falls asleep while counting them, and reaches the snowy land with Grampa and Tatters.

CHAPTER 12

Urtha travels with the others and warms the frozen land wherever her feet touch it, causing flowers to grow in the snow. Her flowers and footprints anger the islanders, but Grampa defends her when Chin Chilly objects to her presence. She weeps after Enorma is put out and is comforted by Tatters.

CHAPTER 13

Urtha warns Tatters and Grampa to run from the Princess of Isa Poso and reminds them about the wizard's medicine. Her flowers blossom across the iceberg, and she dances and plays scrum with Tatters. After the tobacco is smoked, she becomes a crow with vari-colored feathers and flies toward Oz.

CHAPTER 15

Urtha flies as a crow with the company and notices the gun sticking through Grampa's feathers before the transformation ends. In the sky, she skips over the clouds, causing flowers to spring up, and meets Maribella. She travels down the rainbow with Polychrome and the others.

CHAPTER 17

Urtha helps settle the disagreement by proposing that the group believe Dorothy is a Princess if Dorothy's companions believe Tatters is a Prince. She happily welcomes the new friends, gathers berries for supper, and joins Dorothy, Tatters, and Percy Vere in a game of scrum.

CHAPTER 22

Urtha is reunited with Mrs Sew-and-Sew and joins the festivities in Ragbad. She leads off the Ragbad Quadrille with the Prince and is later described as happy with Tatters.

Urtha in Grampa in Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1924 · the work’s page · read it where Urtha first appears

Abstract written by a model

Urtha is the only daughter of Peer Haps and the lost Princess of Perhaps City, described as a sweet young Princess with rosy cheeks and yellow hair. While gathering roses, she disappears amid the strange events involving Abrog, the Prophet, and her whereabouts become unknown. Prince Tatters and the other travelers seek her. Abrog’s spell changes her into a flower fairy rather than mud, and she forgets that she was the Princess. As a flower fairy, she has flower-like colors and fragrance, is found at Maybe Mountain, and is loved by Tatters, who feels she suits him exactly as she is. Urtha reveals her name and identity, but still needs unbewitching. Grampa applies the cure for enchantment, restoring her to Princess form, after which Peer Haps joyfully embraces her.
