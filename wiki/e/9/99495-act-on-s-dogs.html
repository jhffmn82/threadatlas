Actæon’s dogs in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Actæon’s dogs first appears

Abstract written by a model

Actæon’s dogs are a pack of hunting dogs, said to number fifty though lists differ. After Actæon is transformed into a stag, they pursue, wound, and tear apart their master without recognizing him, urged on by his companions. Named members include Melampus, Ladon, Tigris, Thoüs, and Lycisca. According to Apollodorus, they later die of grief.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Actæon's numerous hunting dogs pursue him after he has been transformed into a stag. They wound and tear apart their master, failing to recognize him, while his companions urge them on and call aloud for Actæon.

notes .. notes

Actæon’s pack is said to have consisted of fifty dogs, although Ovid names thirty-six and other writers preserve different lists. Their names often describe their qualities or origins, such as Melampus ('Black-foot'), Ichnobates ('Tracer'), Pamphagus ('Glutton'), and Lælaps ('Tempest'); Apollodorus says that the dogs died of grief after losing their master.

Actaeon's hunting dogs in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Actaeon's hunting dogs first appears

Abstract written by a model

Actaeon’s pack, including Melampus, pursues its changed master as a stag without recognizing him; Melampus first barks, then the dogs seize and tear Actaeon until he dies.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter III

Actaeon's hunting dogs recognize and pursue him after he has been changed into a stag, although they do not recognize him as their master. Melampus gives the first bark, followed by Pamphagus, Dorceus, Lelaps, Theron, Nape, Tigris, and the rest of the pack. They seize and tear Actaeon until he dies.
