Dodona

place · 3 works · 3 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: forest of Dodona, oracle of Dodona, oracle of Jupiter at Dodona
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Dodona is a land whose vocal oaks are associated with an oracle, with traditions variously connecting its responses with oaks, beeches, pigeons, or ringing cauldrons.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Dodona is an oracle believed to give responses from doves and an oak, and it conveyed Zeus’s instruction that Athenians respect and spare conquered Lacedæmonian suppliants.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the oracle of Jupiter at Dodona is described as the most ancient Grecian oracle, whose priest-interpreted responses came through rustling oak branches.

Dodona in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Dodona is an oracle believed by mainland peoples to give responses from doves and an oak. Consulted by the Calydonians, it later conveyed Zeus’s instruction that Athenians respect and spare conquered Lacedæmonian suppliants.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXI.

Dodona was consulted by the Calydonians because the Ætolians, Acarnanians, Epirotes, and other inhabitants of the mainland believed in its oracular responses from doves and the oak.

CHAPTER XXV.

Dodona was the place from which Zeus gave the Athenians an exhortation to respect suppliants. Its message instructed them to treat conquered Lacedæmonians as suppliants and not to kill or harm them.

oracle of Jupiter at Dodona in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The oracle of Jupiter at Dodona, the most ancient Grecian oracle, was said to begin with a black dove’s command and gave priest-interpreted responses through rustling oak branches.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

MYTH OF OSIRIS AND ISIS

The oracle of Jupiter at Dodona was said to have been established after a black dove from Thebes in Egypt commanded the inhabitants to create it. Its priests interpreted responses conveyed by the rustling branches of oak trees.
