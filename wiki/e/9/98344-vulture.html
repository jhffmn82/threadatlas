vulture in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Vulture succeeds where other birds cannot, telling Melampus that a knife in a wild pear-tree causes Iphiclus's childlessness and prescribing rust in wine for ten days; it also eats Tityus's heart.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

IV.—MELAMPUS AND THE KINE OF PHYLACUS

The vulture explained that Iphiclus's childlessness resulted from the knife that Phylacus had stuck into a wild pear-tree. It advised removing the knife, scraping off its rust, and giving the rust in wine to Iphiclus for ten days.
