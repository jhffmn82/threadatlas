  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Amphion was Niobe's husband and the father of her seven sons and seven daughters; after their destruction, he killed himself in grief.
  • In The Library, Amphion was the son of Antiope and Zeus, a lyre-player who helped fortify Thebes, killed Lycus with Zethus, succeeded to Thebes, and later married Niobe.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Amphion was Antiope's and Acestor's son, a renowned lyre-player, singer, and founder of Thebes's joined city, who was later punished in Hades and buried near Zethus.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Amphion was a musician, Niobe's husband, king of Thebes, and builder of its wall through the power of his lyre.

Amphion in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Amphion, Niobe’s husband and ruler of Thebes, fathers seven sons and seven daughters with her. After witnessing his children’s destruction, he kills himself in grief; he and his entire race are described as extinct, and people mourn him.

Amphion in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Amphion, son of Antiope and Zeus, is exposed with Zethus but raised by a neatherd. Given a lyre by Hermes, he practises minstrelsy and helps fortify Thebes as stones follow his music. After recognizing Antiope, he and Zethus kill Lycus; he later succeeds to Thebes, marries Niobe, dies, and Laius takes the kingdom.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Ὁ δὲ Κάδμος pera ‘Appovias Θήβας ἐκλιπὼν

Amphion is exposed with Zethus but is found and raised by a neatherd. He practises minstrelsy with a lyre given to him by Hermes, recognizes Antiope as his mother, kills Lycus with Zethus, kills Dirce, and helps fortify Thebes, with the stones following his lyre.

Γαμεῖ δὲ Ζῆθος μὲν Θήβην, ad ἧς ἡ πόλιες

Amphion married Niobe and was the only male said to have survived Apollo’s attack in one account. After Amphion’s death, Laius took over the kingdom; another account says that Amphion was also killed by Artemis and Apollo.

Amphion in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Amphion, described as Antiope’s and Acestor’s son and Zethus’s brother, is a Cretan famed for lyre, music, and singing. After receiving a lyre from Hermes and discovering nete, he and Zethus invade Thebes, defeat Lycus, and join the lower town to Cadmea. Later punished in Hades, he is buried near Zethus.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER V.

Amphion and his brother Zethus invaded Thebes and defeated Lycus, while those loyal to Cadmus’s line withdrew Laius. Amphion and Zethus joined the lower town to Cadmea and named it Thebes after Thebe. Amphion was famous for music, was said to have expanded the lyre from four strings to seven, and was later punished in Hades for railing against Leto and her sons.

CHAPTER XVII.

Amphion is buried with Zethus in a mound of earth. His funeral pile lies about half a stade from his tomb, and stones near the tomb were said to have followed his music.

Amphion in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Amphion, son of Jupiter and Antiope and twin brother of Zethus, was raised among shepherds and taught the lyre by Mercury. He became musician, husband of Niobe, and king of Thebes, then used his lyre’s sound to move stones and magically build the city’s wall.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XVII

Amphion was the son of Jupiter and Antiope and the twin brother of Zethus. Mercury taught him to play the lyre, and when Amphion became king of Thebes, the stones moved into place by themselves when he played, allowing him to fortify the city with a wall.
