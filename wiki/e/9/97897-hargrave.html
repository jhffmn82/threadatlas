Hargrave in The Valley of Fear

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1914-09/1915-05

Hargrave is an American traveler and suspected assassin who stays at the Eagle Commercial in Tunbridge Wells under his own name, with no papers, letters, or address recorded. About five foot nine, around fifty, grizzled and gray-moustached, he is fierce-looking and wears several heavy garments. He travels by bicycle and carries a valise concealing a sawed-off shotgun. After leaving the hotel he disappears; Barker believes he escaped through a study window, leaving his bicycle behind. Later cyclist reports conflict, and Holmes dismisses tracing him as unhelpful.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter V

The assassin was believed by Barker to have escaped through the study window. Barker could not explain what became of him or why he had not taken the bicycle believed to be his.

It was nightfall before Inspector MacDonald and White Mason came back

Hargrave is identified as an American who stayed at the Eagle Commercial in Tunbridge Wells under that name, carrying only a bicycle and a small valise. He is described as about five foot nine, around fifty years old, slightly grizzled, gray-moustached, curved-nosed, and fierce-looking, and he disappears after leaving the hotel.

Chapter VII

The mysterious cyclist is reported from numerous places, producing conflicting descriptions and several arrests. Sherlock Holmes says that tracing him will not help solve the investigation and urges the detectives to abandon that line of inquiry.
