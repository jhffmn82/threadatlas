multimodal RAG in Ask in Any Modality

Mohammad Mahdi Abootorabi, Amirhosein Zobeiri, Mahdi Dehghani, Mohammadali Mohammadkhani, Bardia Mohammadi, Omid Ghahroodi, Mahdieh Soleymani Baghshah, Ehsaneddin Asgari · 2025

Multimodal RAG is an extension of Retrieval-Augmented Generation for multimodal data and models. It incorporates text, images, audio, video, and sometimes structured data as contextual sources, using cross-modal cues to enrich grounding and generation. Given a multimodal query, modality-specific encoders map documents from a multimodal corpus into a shared semantic space; a retrieval model computes relevance scores, selects, filters, and may re-rank documents, and a generative model produces a multimodal response from the query and retrieved context. Its development includes modality-specific retrieval, efficient similarity search, document understanding, fusion, augmentation, training, and generation. Further methods use in-context learning, chain-of-thought reasoning, instruction tuning, evidence attribution, and agentic systems to improve reasoning, ranking, evidence integration, hallucination reduction, and visual-textual interaction. It applies to healthcare, software engineering, fashion, and entertainment. Its central difficulties are cross-modal alignment, reasoning, modality selection, effective fusion, relevance management, domain adaptation, modality bias, compositional reasoning, explainability, and robustness. Systems often over-rely on text, attribute responses to broad sources, vary in answer quality by modality, and degrade with low-quality or outdated sources. They can be vulnerable to adversarial perturbations, hallucinate, and propagate misinformation when retrieval fails or sources are unreliable. They also raise concerns about bias, misinformation, privacy, and intellectual property. The survey examines advances, applications, datasets, benchmarks, and evaluation, but does not comparatively evaluate systems because their tasks, metrics, implementation details, and computational requirements differ substantially.

Introduction & Background

Multimodal RAG incorporates sources such as images, audio, and structured data to enrich contextual grounding and improve generation quality. It leverages cross-modal cues for reasoning but introduces challenges involving modality selection, effective fusion, and cross-modal relevance. In the presented formulation, modality-specific encoders map documents into a shared semantic space, a retrieval model selects documents using relevance thresholds, and a generative model produces a response from the query and retrieved context.

Key Innovations and Methodologies / 3.1

Multimodal RAG systems encode diverse input modalities into unified embedding spaces to support cross-modal retrieval. The unit describes their use of modality-specific retrieval, efficient similarity search, document understanding, re-ranking, relevance scoring, and filtering.

3.4

Multimodal RAG is improved through in-context learning, chain-of-thought reasoning, instruction tuning, evidence attribution, and agentic systems. The discussed methods address reasoning, retrieval, ranking, evidence integration, hallucination reduction, and interaction across visual and textual information.

Open Problems and Future Directions

Multimodal RAG systems often struggle with domain adaptation, modality biases, compositional reasoning, explainability, and robustness. They may over-rely on text, produce different-quality answers depending on the modalities used, and degrade when sources are low-quality or outdated.

Multimodal RAG is reviewed through key advancements in retrieval, multimodal fusion, augmentation, generation, training strategies, and agents. Its task-specific applications, datasets, benchmarks, and evaluation methods are also examined.

multimodal retrieval-augmented generation in VisDoM: Multi-Document QA with Visually Rich Elements Using Multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Manan Suri, Puneet Mathur, Franck Dernoncourt, Kanika Gowswami, Ryan A. Rossi, and Dinesh Manocha · 2025

Multimodal retrieval-augmented generation is a RAG system for multi-document question answering with visually rich elements. It retrieves and combines textual and visual evidence, including visual data obtained through vision-based retrieval, but end-to-end results can be limited by LLM visual reasoning. In VisDoMRAG, textual and visual pipelines are processed independently and fused late for answer generation; this outperforms early fusion, especially in cross-modal reasoning.
