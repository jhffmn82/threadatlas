stones as ancestors of humanity in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Stones, interpreted as the bones of earth and as children of Mount Parnassus survivors, are cast behind Deucalion and Pyrrha after the inundation. They gradually lose hardness, their earthy and humid parts becoming human bodies while solid parts become bones and veins remain veins. Deucalion’s stones become men; Pyrrha’s become women.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

The stones cast behind Deucalion and Pyrrha gradually lose their hardness and stiffness and assume human forms. Those thrown by Deucalion become men, while those thrown by Pyrrha renew the female race.

notes .. notes

The children of those preserved from the inundation were said to be the stones mentioned by the Poet. The explanation connects this fable with words that could mean either “stone” or “child,” and notes that a word meaning “people” could also mean “stone.”
