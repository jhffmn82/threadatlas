Acis

person · 2 works · 8 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: hero, my beloved Acis, son of Symæthis, the beautiful youth, youth

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Acis is a Sicilian youth who loves Galatea, is crushed by Polyphemus, and becomes the Acis River. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Acis is the beautiful son of Faunus and a Naiad who loves Galatea, is crushed by Polyphemus, and becomes the river Acis.

Acis in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Acis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Acis, a Sicilian youth, son of Faunus and Symæthis, mutually loves Galatea. Discovered with her by Polyphemus, he flees and calls for help, but is crushed by a mountain fragment and becomes the Acis River.

FABLE VII. [XIII.719-897]

Acis is the son of Faunus and the Nymph Symæthis and is loved by Galatea. When Polyphemus discovers Acis with Galatea, Acis flees and calls for help, but a rock hurled by Polyphemus crushes him.

Chapter XIX

Acis was the son of Faunus and a Naiad, and a beautiful youth who loved Galatea. Polyphemus crushed him beneath a rock after discovering him with Galatea. Galatea changed him into a river, which retained the name Acis.
