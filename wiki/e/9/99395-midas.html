Midas in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Midas is a king of the Phrygian people and Greater Phrygia, son of Gordius and Cybele, who rules a kingdom, possesses great wealth and frugality, and is associated with the Berecynthian hero. After hospitably hosting Silenus for ten days and nights and restoring him to Bacchus, Midas receives the power to turn touches to gold. When food and drink become gold, he repents and is purified at the Pactolus's source. Later, for preferring Pan's music in a contest, Apollo gives him ass's ears, which he hides with a purple turban.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

FABLE II. [XI.85-145]

Midas, king of the Phrygian people, hospitably receives Silenus and restores him to Bacchus. He receives the power to turn whatever he touches into gold, but the power leaves him rich and wretched when it transforms his food and drink; after repenting, he bathes at the source of the Pactolus and is freed from it.

FABLE III. [XI.146-193]

Midas is present during the musical contest and foolishly prefers Pan's music to Apollo's. Apollo punishes him by changing his human ears into the ears of an ass, which Midas conceals beneath a purple turban.

Midas in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Midas is a king of Phrygia and son of Gordius. After hospitably entertaining Silenus, he receives from Bacchus the power to turn touches into gold, but rejects it when he cannot eat or drink. He abandons wealth, worships Pan, and Apollo gives him ass ears.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter IV

Midas, king of Phrygia and son of Gordius, hospitably entertained Silenus and was rewarded by Bacchus with a wish. He chose the power to turn whatever he touched into gold, but later rejected the gift because it prevented him from eating and drinking. After Bacchus removed the power, Midas lived in the country as a worshipper of Pan, and Apollo gave him ass ears for questioning the judgment in Pan's music contest.
