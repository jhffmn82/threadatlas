Cecrops in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Cecrops, autochthonous and compounded of man and serpent, is Attica’s first king, renaming it Cecropia. He testifies for Athena’s claim to Attica and her planting of the olive-tree. He marries Agraulus, has Erysichthon and daughters Agraulus, Herse, and Pandrosus, and is father of Pandion. After his death, Cranaus becomes king.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

XIV. Κέκροψ αὐτόχθων, συμφυὲς ἔχων σῶμα

Cecrops was autochthonous and had a body compounded of a man and a serpent. He was the first king of Attica, renamed the country Cecropia after himself, and testified that Athena had been the first to plant the olive-tree.

Κέκροπος δὲ ἀποθανόντος Κραναὸς «ἐβασί-

Cecrops dies, after which Cranaus becomes king.

When Cecrops died, Cranaus came to the throne?!;

When Cecrops died, Cranaus came to the throne. Pandrosus, to whom Athena entrusted Erichthonius, was a daughter of Cecrops.
