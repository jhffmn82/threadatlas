transformation of Io into a cow in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where transformation of Io into a cow first appears

Abstract written by a model

The transformation of Io into a sleek, snow-white cow begins when Jupiter changes Inachus’s daughter to hide her from Juno. Unable to speak or use her arms, Io communicates by lowing and writing with her foot. Greek writers link her cow-shaped passage to a ship’s cow figure and the name Bosphorus.

The Greek writers cited in the unit explain the cow imagery by saying that Jupiter placed Io aboard a vessel with a cow-shaped figure at its head. They also connect the passage of Io in the shape of a cow with the name of the Bosphorus.

opening

Jupiter's transformation changes the daughter of Inachus into a sleek, snow-white cow so that Juno will not recognize her. The transformation leaves Io unable to use human speech or arms, though she can communicate by lowing and writing with her foot.

Io's transformation in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Io's transformation first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jupiter changes Io from a mortal maiden into a beautiful white heifer to hide her from Juno. When Io is restored, her coarse hairs fall away, her horns shrink, her eyes and mouth regain their human shape, and hands and fingers replace her hoofs.
