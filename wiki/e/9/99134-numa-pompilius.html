Numa Pompilius in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Numa Pompilius is a Roman king, destined by Fame for sovereignty and later voluntarily invited to rule the Roman people. Seeking knowledge, he investigates the nature of things, travels to a city associated with Hercules, asks about its Grecian founder, and is drawn by Pythagoras’s reputation to hear his discourses. Knowing Sabine ceremonials, with Egeria as wife and the Muses as guides, he teaches sacrificial rites and arts of peace to people accustomed to savage warfare. He consults Egeria in the Arician grove about laws, and is said to use divine communications, secret interviews, consultations with the Muses, and hydromancy to confirm belief in communication with heaven and secure obedience. Some accounts call this a political fiction. Romans consider his governing inspired; his reign is marked by profound peace, piety, justice, and civilization. After a long life and completed reign, he dies, mourned by matrons, people, and Senators.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK THE FIFTEENTH.

Numa is destined by Fame to become king and seeks a deeper understanding of the nature of things. He travels to the city associated with Hercules and asks about the founder of the Grecian city on the Italian shores.

FABLES II. AND III. [XV.60-478]

Numa Pompilius is drawn by Pythagoras's reputation to hear his discourses.

FABLES IV. V. AND VI. [XV.479-621]

Numa returned to his country and, being voluntarily invited, received sovereignty over the Roman people. With a Nymph for his wife and the Muses as guides, he taught sacrificial rites and led a people accustomed to savage warfare toward peaceful arts; after a long life, he died and was mourned by the matrons, people, and Senators.

EXPLANATION.

Numa is said to have retired to the Arician grove to consult the Nymph Egeria about laws for his subjects. His alleged divine communications were presented as a means of securing obedience to his laws, though some accounts describe them as a political fiction. He is also said to have reigned in profound peace and to have encouraged piety, justice, and civilization.
