magpies in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Magpies are nine birds, transformed from the Pierides or Emathian sisters after defeat in a dispute. In woods and groves, they perch on lofty branches or hang in the air, lamenting their fate while retaining garrulity, chattering, and imitation with distinct human-like voices.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

The magpies are nine birds that imitate everything and speak with distinct human-like voices. They are the transformed Pierides and remain perched on lofty branches while lamenting their fate.

opening

The transformed Emathian sisters hang poised in the air as magpies, becoming the scandal of the groves. As magpies, they retain their talkativeness and love of chattering.
