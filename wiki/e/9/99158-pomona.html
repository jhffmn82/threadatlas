Pomona in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Pomona is a Nymph and Hamadryad of Latium, the finest tender of gardens, devoted to fruit trees, country, pruning, grafting, and watering roots. She initially rejects love, marriage, and male approach, enclosing her orchard and fearing rustics. Under Proca, she has a priest called Flamen Pomonalis. After hearing Vertumnus’s account of Anaxarete, she is captivated by his youthful form and returns his love.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

FABLE XI. [XIV.609-697]

Pomona is the most skilled of the Hamadryads of Latium at tending gardens and caring for fruit trees. She prunes, grafts, and waters the trees, but has no desire for love, fearing the violence of rustics and avoiding the approach of males.

FABLES XII. AND XIII. [XIV.698-851]

Pomona hears Vertumnus's account of Anaxarete's cruelty and is urged to abandon prolonged disdain and unite herself with one who loves her. She is subsequently captivated by Vertumnus's youthful form and returns his love.

Pomona in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Pomona is a Hamadryad and patroness of orchards, devoted to gardens, fruit trees, apples, and cultivation. She guards her locked orchard with a pruning knife, initially rejecting men and Vertumnus. After he appears in his true form and argues for love, she returns his love.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter VI

Pomona is a Hamadryad who excels in the love and cultivation of gardens and fruit. She keeps her orchard locked against men and initially rejects Vertumnus, but after seeing him in his true form and hearing his arguments, she returns his love.
