Janus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Janus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Janus is the porter of heaven, guardian deity of gates, and opener of the year, which gives its first month his name. Represented with two heads because every door looks two ways, he has numerous temples in Rome. His principal temple’s gates open in war and close in peace, having closed only once between Numa and Augustus.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

ROMAN DIVINITIES

Janus was the porter of heaven, the guardian deity of gates, and the deity who opened the year, with the first month named after him. He was commonly represented with two heads because every door looks two ways; in Rome, the gates of his principal temple were open in war and closed in peace, though they were shut only once between the reign of Numa and that of Augustus.
