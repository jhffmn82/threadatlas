Mænades in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Mænades are Thracian women who, during orgies, attack and kill Orpheus in a rite-inspired fury; Lyæus punishes the murder by fastening them to the ground and transforming them into trees.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK THE ELEVENTH.

During their orgies, the women of Thrace attack Orpheus because of his indifference toward their sex. They throw weapons at him and finally kill him. Lyæus punishes them by fastening them to the ground and transforming them into trees.

Bacchanals in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Bacchanals accompanying Dionysus are captured with Satyrs by Lycurgus, suddenly released, and compelled into Bacchic frenzy on Cithaeron.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

V. Διόνυσος δὲ εὑρετὴς ἀμπέλου γενόμενος,

The Bacchanals accompanying Dionysus were captured with the multitude of Satyrs when Lycurgus expelled him. They were later suddenly released, and Dionysus compelled the women to engage in Bacchic frenzy on Cithaeron.

Thracian women in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Thracian women alone may enter the temple of Hercules at Erythræ. They plot against and, after drinking wine, kill Orpheus because he led their husbands on wanderings; the account links this to men entering battle drunk.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXX.

The Thracian women are said to have plotted against Orpheus because he persuaded their husbands to accompany him in his wanderings. After strengthening themselves with wine, they carried out the deed, and the text connects this story with the custom of men going drunk into battle.

Thracian maidens in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Thracian maidens, rejected by Orpheus as he dwells on Eurydice, attack in Bacchic rites, overcome his lyre by drowning out its music, and tear him limb from limb.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XVII

The Thracian maidens tried to captivate Orpheus, but he rejected their advances while dwelling on Eurydice. During the rites of Bacchus, they attacked him; although his lyre first caused their weapons and stones to fall harmlessly, they drowned out his music and tore him limb from limb.
