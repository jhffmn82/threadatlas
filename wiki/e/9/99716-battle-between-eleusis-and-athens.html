battle between Eleusis and Athens

event · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: battle, battle between the Eleusinians and Athenians, battle between the people of Eleusis and the Athenians
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the battle between the Eleusinians and Athenians results in the deaths of Eumolpus and Erectheus, after which Erectheus's descendants rule Athens and Eumolpus's descendants retain the office of Hierophant.
  • In Description of Greece, the battle between Eleusis and Athens kills Erechtheus and Immaradus; peace makes the Eleusinians Athenians while preserving their private management of the Mysteries.

battle between the Eleusinians and Athenians in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The battle between the Eleusinians and Athenians resulted in the deaths of Eumolpus and Erectheus. It was then agreed that Erectheus's descendants would rule Athens and Eumolpus's descendants would retain the office of Hierophant.

CHAPTER XXXVIII.

In the battle between Eleusis and Athens, Erechtheus, king of Athens, and Immaradus, son of Eumolpus, were killed. The resulting peace made the people of Eleusis Athenians in other respects but preserved their private management of the Mysteries.
