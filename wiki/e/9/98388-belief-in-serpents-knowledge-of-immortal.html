belief in serpents' knowledge of immortality plants

Also called: A belief in the actual existence of a plant endowed with such magical virtue, a popular belief

Abstract written by a model

A popular Armenian belief holds that serpents know life-extending plants and springs; old, sick, or wounded snakes seek them, shed their skins, eat a flower, drink, and regain healing, youth, and immortality.

VII.—THE RESURRECTION OF GLAUCUS

The unit records a belief that serpents know the plants and springs that provide healing, renewed youth, and immortality. In the Armenian account, old, sick, and wounded snakes visit such places, shed their skins, eat a flower, drink from the spring, and become healed and young again.
