the second volume in Laurence Sterne in Germany

Harvey Waterman Thayer · 1905

The second volume of Schummel’s three-volume work becomes more incoherent and less eventful than the first. It mixes the rescued baker’s wife’s sentimental story with Shandean borrowings, digressions, typographical experiments, mock-scientific passages, whimsy, and anticipated censure, while its changed manner foreshadows the third volume.

” The second volume is even more incoherent in narration, and contains less genu

The second volume is described as more incoherent in narration and less genuinely eventful than the first, with numerous ill-considered attempts at whimsy. It contains sentimental stories, direct and indirect borrowings from Sterne, digressions, unusual typography, mock-scientific passages, and anticipations of critical censure.
