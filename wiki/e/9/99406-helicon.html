Helicon in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Helicon is a fertile, tree-filled mountain in Greece, sacred to the Muses. Its purslane feeds goats, its grass and roots are harmless to people, and its pastures weaken snake venom. At its foot lies Ascra; on the way to the Muses’ grove are Aganippe, Eupheme’s image, and Linus’s statue. Its summit holds Hippocrene and Lamus, while its grove and festival site are linked with the Thespians and traditions of Hesiod, the Muses, Eros, and Narcissus.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXVIII.

Helicon is described as the most fertile and tree-filled of the mountains of Greece. Its purslane bushes provide excellent food for goats, and its grass and roots are said not to injure humans. Its pastures make the venom of snakes less potent.

CHAPTER XXIX.

Helicon is described as a sacred mountain of the Muses, with Ascra at its foot. On the way to the grove of the Muses are the fountain Aganippe, the image of Eupheme, and a statue of Linus.

CHAPTER XXXI.

Mount Helicon contains the grove and festival site of the Thespians, as well as the spring Hippocrene and the small river Lamus on its summit. The surrounding traditions concern Hesiod, the Muses, Eros, and Narcissus.
