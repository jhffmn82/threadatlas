the John Douglas case in The Valley of Fear

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1914-09/1915-05

The John Douglas case concerns Douglas's murder and an investigation at the Manor House. Investigators consider whether the killer was an insider or outsider, examine physical clues, statements, timing, a blood mark, wedding ring, bicycle, and missing dumb-bell, while Holmes tests how sound travels through the house. Holmes says their approach is hopeless because they misunderstand the case, but pursues a shorter path to the truth. Referred to a higher court, Douglas is acquitted for self-defense; his later reported death suggests a planned murder organized from London.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter IV

The investigation considers whether the killer was an insider or an outsider and examines the physical clues at the Manor House. Holmes insists on gathering complete evidence before presenting a final theory.

Chapter VI

The investigation examines the statements of Barker and Mrs. Douglas, the timing of the gunshot and alarm, the blood mark on the windowsill, the wedding ring, the bicycle, and the missing dumb-bell. Holmes conducts experiments about how sounds travel through the house and plans further examination of the study.

Chapter VII

Sherlock Holmes tells Inspector MacDonald and White Mason that their case is hopeless because they have not understood what they are investigating. He believes the truth can still be found through a shorter course than tracing the mysterious cyclist.

Epilogue

The John Douglas case was referred to a higher court, and Douglas was acquitted at the Quarter Sessions for acting in self-defense. The later reported death of Douglas leads Holmes to conclude that the case involves a planned murder organized from London.
