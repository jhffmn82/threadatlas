Abstract written by a model

Johann Joachim Christoph Bode was a Braunschweig-born Hamburg resident, born in 1730 and arriving in Hamburg in 1756–57, who became a prominent German translator and promoter of Sterne and Yorick. Already conversant with English language and literature, he may have read Tristram Shandy in English before 1764 and may have edited the Hamburgischer unpartheyischer Correspondent in 1762–63; his possible connection with that paper’s severe review of Zückert’s translation cannot be proved. He also privately printed and circulated material from Lessing’s Yorick’s sermon for Alberti. After Sentimental Journey appeared, Bode rapidly translated and publicized it, first through extracts in the Hamburgische Adress-Comptoir-Nachrichten and then as the complete Yoricks Empfindsame Reise in 1768. The translation’s perceived excellence and power linked him with sentimental travelers, helped make him a German apostle of Yorick, and earned him the description “Yorick’s rescuer.” Its success led him to undertake a complete translation of Tristram Shandy, and he responded to Goeze in that work’s introduction. Bode also obtained from Alberti and translated the third and fourth volumes of Yorick’s Journey, a continuation by Eugenius. By publishing them without explaining their status or his attitude toward them adequately, he was often mistaken for their author. His version substantially omitted, altered, and added material, including Das Mündel, Das Hündchen, and Die Moral, and commonly softened or removed indecent passages. He translated Stevenson’s continuation and was probably the translator of 1775 Hamburg volumes of Yorick’s and Eliza’s letters and miscellaneous letters. Reviewers praised these translations, while Benzler later charged Bode with many errors and revised his work. His stated vocation was to translate everything Sterne had written. He also purchased Gotthold Ephraim Lessing at Lessing’s Hamburg auction.

] CHAPTER II STERNE IN GERMANY BEFORE THE PUBLICATION OF THE SENTIMENTAL JOURNEY It is no exaggeration to assert that the works of Yorick obtained and still retain a relatively more substantial position of serious consideration and recognized merit in France and Germany than in the countries where Sterne’s own tongue is spoken.

Johann Joachim Christoph Bode was a Hamburg resident who was highly proficient in English and was reportedly editor of the Hamburgischer unpartheyischer Correspondent in 1762–63. He may have written or supervised the paper’s severe 1764 review of Zückert’s translation, although the text states that this cannot be proved. He later became the successful translator of A Sentimental Journey Through France and Italy and undertook a complete translation of Tristram Shandy.

The first mention of Sterne in the _Göttingische Gelehrte Anzeigen_ is in the nu

Bode may have known the English original of Tristram Shandy before 1764. He later privately printed and circulated the title and part of the introduction to Lessing’s Yorick’s sermon for Alberti.

] CHAPTER III THE PUBLICATION OF THE SENTIMENTAL JOURNEY On February 27, 1768, the Sentimental Journey was published in London,[1] less than three weeks before the author’s death, and the book was at once transplanted to German soil, beginning there immediately its career of commanding influence and wide-spread popularity.

Johann Joachim Christoph Bode rapidly translated and publicized the Sentimental Journey after it reached Germany. He published extracts in the Hamburgische Adress-Comptoir-Nachrichten and later issued the complete Yoricks Empfindsame Reise, becoming an important promoter of the Yorick cult.

Bode’s translation of the third and fourth volumes of Yorick’s Journey,[38] that

Bode translated the third and fourth volumes of Yorick’s Journey without explaining that they continued Eugenius’s work, which caused him to be regarded by many as their author. He made extensive omissions, alterations, and original additions, including Das Mündel, Das Hündchen, Die Moral, and other sentimental episodes, while often softening or removing indecent material.

] CHAPTER IV STERNE IN GERMANY AFTER THE PUBLICATION OF THE SENTIMENTAL JOURNEY The publication of the Sentimental Journey, as implied in the previous chapter, brought Sterne into vital connection with literary impulses and emotional experiences in Germany, and his position as a leader was at once recognized.

Johann Joachim Christoph Bode translated the Sentimental Journey, Stevenson’s continuation, and Tristram Shandy into German. His translations were widely admired for their style and fidelity, although Benzler later accused him of numerous errors and revised his work.

Naturally Sterne’s letters found readers in Germany, the Yorick-Eliza correspond

Johann Joachim Christoph Bode is identified as the probable translator of the 1775 Hamburg volumes containing Yorick’s letters to Eliza, letters from Eliza, and miscellaneous letters. His translations were praised by German reviewers, and his translator’s notes show an intention to render Sterne’s writings and related materials into German.
