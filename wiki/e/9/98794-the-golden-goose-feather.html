the golden goose feather

Abstract written by a model

The golden goose feather is a golden magic quill given by Pastoria to Pajuka on his last birthday in the castle. It warns Pajuka or the King when either is in danger. After Snip pulls it from a well, it returns to the place where the old castle had stood; a golden feather then separates from Pajuka’s feathers and flies toward the Emerald City carrying a magic message. It flies into a garden, writes five words on the marble walk, and falls lifeless, leaving Ozma’s company unable to identify its sender. Later, after being kept in Sir Hokus’s shirt, it escapes and writes on his shield that the King of Oz is in the palace and summons Ozma, Betsy, Trot, and the Wizard before again falling lifeless.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 8

A golden goose feather flies into the garden, rights itself, and writes five words on the marble walk before falling lifeless. Sir Hokus identifies it as a goose quill, and the Wizard finds no information about such feathers in his magic-message book.

CHAPTER 9

The quill is the unexplained object whose sender Ozma wants to identify. Trot suggests that Fairy Lurline may have sent it, but the company does not learn who actually sent it in this unit.

CHAPTER 13

A golden feather separates from the white feathers Snip pulled from Pajuka's wing and flies through Tora's window toward the Emerald City. It is the feather that carries the magic message to the Emerald City, although Snip and Tora do not know this.

CHAPTER 18

The golden goose feather escapes from Sir Hokus's shirt and writes on his shield that the King of Oz is in the palace, followed by a summons for Ozma, Betsy, Trot, and the Wizard. It then falls lifelessly into the dust, and the Scarecrow picks it up.

CHAPTER 20

The magic quill was given by Pastoria to Pajuka on his last birthday in the castle. It warned Pastoria or Pajuka when either was in danger, and after Snip pulled it from a well it returned to the place where the old castle had stood.
