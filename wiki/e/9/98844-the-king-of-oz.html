the King of Oz in The Lost King of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1925

The King of Oz is Ozma's long-lost biological and royal father. Mombi's failed magic transformed him into Humpy, a bewitched dummy with flying ears; he encounters Lurline during this enchantment. Though Mombi doubts Humpy is the King, a forgotten green magic formula on Humpy's collar confirms it. Attempts to restore him initially fail, but the Wizard of Oz's magic restores him when he wears the King's robe, revealing him as the tired tailor Tora. He reunites with Ozma, abdicates in her favor, and becomes a retired royal tailor in the Emerald City, where he owns a shop and employs Snip.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 17

The King is represented by a stuffed figure beside Pajuka during the journey. Mombi had initially doubted that Humpy was the King, but the forgotten green magic formula on Humpy's collar convinced her.

CHAPTER 19

The King of Oz is revealed to be Humpy in a bewitched dummy form. The efforts to restore him to his natural shape fail in this unit.

CHAPTER 20

Pastoria is revealed to be the tired tailor Tora when he wears the King's robe and the Wizard of Oz's magic restores his natural shape. He reunites with Ozma, explains his enchantment and flying ears, and later abdicates in her favor to open a tailoring shop.

CHAPTER 21

The King of Oz rides proudly on the Elegant Elephant beside Humpy, with both dressed exactly alike. He later measures Snip for a suit and agrees absently to Ozma's plan for punishing Mombi.

Humpy in The Lost King of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1925

Humpy is the lost King of Oz, a kind, gentle former ruler who never punished anyone, but Mombi transformed and hid him, leaving him a stuffed dummy with no memory of his past. He wears a green robe and a forgotten green magic formula on his collar, and his crown helps Pajuka and Mombi identify him. After joking with Kabumpo and others, he eagerly accepts being King, orders Kabumpo about, briefly sentences Mombi, then reconsiders. He travels on Kabumpo to the Emerald City, occupies the emerald throne, and Mombi's attempted restoration instead sinks the palace. He then complains that the fallen kingdom has freed him from kingship and tries to catch Tora's flying ears. Although Ozma recognizes him as her father, the Wizard's attempt leaves him a dummy.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 4

The King was transformed by Mombi and hidden somewhere, but Mombi cannot remember where she put him. Mombi believes green magic can restore him and plans to find him near the Emerald City.

CHAPTER 5

Pajuka says that the King of Oz was a kind and gentle ruler who never punished anyone and would believe evil of no one. Mombi transformed him in a little wood, and Pajuka hopes to find him and restore him.

CHAPTER 16

Humpy jokes with Kabumpo and the others after being thrown from the elephant. Pajuka and Mombi identify him as the lost King of Oz, and Humpy accepts the role enthusiastically, ordering Kabumpo about and sentencing Mombi before reconsidering. He travels toward the Emerald City on Kabumpo's back.

CHAPTER 17

Humpy travels to the Emerald City while holding onto his crown and Kabumpo. He sits on the emerald throne, and Mombi's attempted transformation spell instead triggers the sinking of the palace.

CHAPTER 19

Humpy complains that the fallen kingdom has released him from being King and tries to capture Tora's flying ears. Although Ozma recognizes him as her father, Humpy remains a dummy after the Wizard places the royal robe on him and performs the supposed incantation.
