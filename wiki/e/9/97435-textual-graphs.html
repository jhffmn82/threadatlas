Yuntong Hu, Zhihan Lei, Zheng Zhang, Bo Pan, Chen Ling, Liang Zhao · 2025 · the work’s page · read it where textual graph first appears

Abstract written by a model

A textual graph is a graph of networked documents with text-attributed nodes and edges, formally represented by node and edge sets and their natural-language attributes. It preserves textual and topological information for GRAG retrieval and generation. Graph G supplies subgraphs: its K-hop ego-graphs are indexed and ranked as candidate textual subgraphs, then semantically relevant candidates are retrieved and refined for a query. Retrieved subgraphs become hierarchical descriptions and graph embeddings. For LLMs, the graph is presented through complementary graph and text views to provide graph context.

textual graphs in GRAG: Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Yuntong Hu, Zhihan Lei, Zheng Zhang, Bo Pan, Chen Ling, Liang Zhao · 2025-07-12 · the work’s page · read it where textual graphs first appears

Abstract written by a model

Textual graphs are networked documents, common in citation graphs, social media, and knowledge graphs, that may contain social media postings, research papers, knowledge items, and product reviews. They matter because network information, including citations, social interactions, entity relations, and purchasing links, provides context for retrieval, generation, and prompting Large Language Models, beyond retrieval of individual documents. Formally, a textual graph is G = (V, E, {Tn}n∈V, {Te}e∈E): a graph whose nodes and edges have natural-language attributes. For textual subgraph retrieval, it is represented through graph and text views to preserve connected structure and narrated connections. Retrieved ego-graphs can become hierarchical descriptions while retaining information for lossless conversion between K-hop ego-graphs and text descriptions. Textual graphs provide the scenarios and contexts for GRAG, particularly when detailed, multi-hop reasoning is required.
