Hyacinthian festival in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

An annual Amyclæ festival honoring Hyacinthus and probably Apollo, beginning and ending with mourning and sacrifices, with a middle day of joyous songs, races, processions, and feasting.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE III. [X.106-142]

The Hyacinthian festival returned each year with prescribed ceremonies in honor of Hyacinthus. Its first and last days involved mourning and sacrifices, while the middle day featured rejoicing, amusements, songs praising Apollo, horse races, processions, and feasting.
