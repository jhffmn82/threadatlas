Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Leto is a divine queen and mother of Artemis and Apollo who secures Delos, gives birth to Apollo, and tends him thereafter. In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Leto is the daughter of Koios and mother of twins who comes to Delos in childbirth, gives birth there, and beholds her blessed children. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Latona is a goddess and Titaness, mother of Apollo and Diana, whose persecution and encounters with the Lycians and Niobe drive episodes of the narrative. In The Library, Leto is the daughter of Coeus and mother of Artemis and Apollo who reaches Delos, gives birth, and incites her children against Niobe's offspring. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Latona is the persecuted mother of Apollo and Diana who transforms the hostile Lycian rustics and seeks protection from her children after Niobe insults her.

Leto in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · about 699 BC · the work’s page · read it where Leto first appears

Abstract written by a model

Leto is a queenly, divine goddess, daughter of Coeus and rich-haired mother of Artemis and Apollo. After traveling many lands, she secures Delos, endures nine days and nights of labor, and gives birth to Apollo beside a palm tree. She later remains beside him in Zeus’s house, tending his bow and quiver.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

III. TO DELIAN APOLLO

Leto alone remains beside Apollo when he enters Zeus’s house, where she unstrings his bow and removes his quiver. During her labor she travels among many lands before securing Delos as Apollo’s birthplace, and she gives birth while holding a palm tree on the island.

Latona in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Latona first appears

Abstract written by a model

Latona is a goddess and Titaness, daughter of Cæus and mother of Apollo and Diana, her twins. Banished by Juno, she wanders with her children, is received by floating Delos, and leans against a palm during their birth. Honored by Theban women, she is mocked by Niobe, whose insult prompts Apollo and Diana to avenge her. Later, exhausted and thirsty in Lycia, Latona seeks water to bathe her children; after countrymen refuse, insult her, and muddy the water, she curses them to remain in the pool, later changing them into frogs, then travels toward Xanthus.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Latona is honored by the women of Thebes at Manto's command, but Niobe mocks her as a wandering Titaness who had difficulty finding a place to give birth. Latona tells Apollo and Diana that Niobe has insulted both her and her children, and they avenge the affront.

opening

Latona, wearied by her prolonged journey and the burden of her twins, becomes intensely thirsty in Lycia. She humbly asks the countrymen for water, but after they refuse, insult her, and muddy the lake, she curses them to live forever in the pool.

notes .. notes

Latona sought water from a lake or stream and was churlishly repulsed by a shepherd or by country peasants. According to Antoninus Liberalis, she needed the water to bathe her children, and she later changed the peasants into frogs.

Latona in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Latona first appears

Abstract written by a model

Latona is a goddess and mother of Apollo, Diana, and infant twins. Persecuted by Juno, she is driven to Lycia, where thirsty and denied pond water by rustics, she transforms them into water-dwelling creatures. Honored at Thebes, she is insulted by Niobe, who rejects her rites, and she appeals to her children for protection.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

LATONA AND THE RUSTICS

Latona, driven from land to land by Juno, reached Lycia carrying her infant twins and asked the rustics for water. When they refused her and stirred up the pond, she transformed them into creatures that would live in the water.

Chapter IX

Latona is honored by the people of Thebes as the mother of Apollo and Diana. Niobe insults her and orders the people to abandon Latona's sacred rites, after which Latona appeals to her children for protection.
