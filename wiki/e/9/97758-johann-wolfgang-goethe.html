Johann Wolfgang Goethe in Laurence Sterne in Germany

Harvey Waterman Thayer · 1905

Johann Wolfgang Goethe is presented through his lifelong, discerning engagement with Laurence Sterne and Sterne’s German reception. In Strassburg, under Johann Gottfried Herder’s guidance, Goethe first read Sterne. He then carried Sterne into his own literary circle: recommending him to Jung-Stilling, reading from Tristram Shandy, including the story of poor Le Fevre, to the Darmstadt circle, and repeatedly referring to Sterne and Yorick in letters, works, and conversation. He acknowledged an impulse from Yorick in his travel writing and adopted Sterne’s direct appeal to readers, while distinguishing his own travel accounts from A Sentimental Journey Through France and Italy by their objectivity. He regarded Sterne as an adversary of pedantry and philistinism, valued Yorick’s liberating humor and intellectual penetration, and described Sterne as encouraging humane knowledge and tolerance. Goethe also recognized risks in Sterne’s delicately handled indecency. His admiration increased over time: he called himself infinitely indebted to Shakespeare, Sterne, and Goldsmith, reread Sterne near life’s end, and remained unequivocally appreciative even when a new century mocked the earlier fashion. Yet he rejected derivative sentimentality. In 1772 he harshly reviewed Schummel’s work as a contrived imitation, a thistle on Yorick’s grave; Der Triumph der Empfindsamkeit likewise satirized the sentimental movement and imitators of Werther, without being directly tied to Sterne. The record also notes his publication of Koran material without identifying its source, accusations of literary plagiarism involving Sterne and the Koran, and possible Koranic influence on Graf von Gleichen in Stella.

Johann Wolfgang Goethe was identified as a clear-minded critic who expressed unequivocal appreciation for Sterne. His significant later words about Sterne came from the last years of his life, when the new century viewed the earlier literary fashion with almost incredulous amusement.
