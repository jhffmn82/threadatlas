Cyane in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Cyane is a celebrated Sicilian Nymph, guardian and namesake of her pool, wife of Anapis, and companion of Proserpine. She rises to oppose Pluto's seizure of Proserpina, but after he forces through her stream, grief dissolves her into its waters.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

opening

Cyane, a celebrated Sicilian Nymph, rises from her stream and tries to stop Pluto, arguing that Proserpina should have been asked of Ceres rather than carried away. After Pluto forces his chariot through the stream, Cyane grieves, dissolves into tears, and melts into the waters she had guarded.
