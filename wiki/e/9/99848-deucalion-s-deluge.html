Deucalion's deluge

event · 1 work · 6 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Deluge, Deucalion’s deluge, deluge, destroy the world, destruction, late deluge, punish the earth by a deluge, that deluge, the deluge, the destruction of the world, the last, the race of mortals be cut off, universal deluge

Abstract written by a model

Deucalion's deluge is a universal punishment and destruction sent by Jupiter because of universal corruption, intended to destroy the mortal race and the world. Jupiter calls the council of the Gods and determines that mankind must be destroyed. The deluge then covers the land, erases the distinction between sea and land, and carries away crops, groves, animals, people, houses, and temples. It overflows Thessaly, transforms Phocis into sea and a wide plain of waters, and destroys men and animals. Deucalion and Pyrrha alone survive, as the only saved persons. It is described as the last deluge.

Deucalion's deluge is in one work on this site, The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

front matter

The Deluge is sent by Jupiter to destroy the race of men and animals, except for Deucalion and Pyrrha.

opening

The destruction of the world is the purpose for which Jupiter calls the council of the Gods. Jupiter determines that the mortal race must be destroyed, though the unit does not describe the destruction itself taking place.

notes .. notes

The deluge is presented as the punishment by which the earth and mankind are to be destroyed because of universal corruption.

opening

The universal deluge is the punishment Jupiter uses to destroy mankind. It covers the land, removes the distinction between sea and land, and carries away crops, groves, animals, people, houses, and temples.

notes .. notes

Deucalion’s deluge is described as one of the two most celebrated floods mentioned by Pausanias. It was generally said to have covered Thessaly, was treated by Ovid as a universal flood, and was to be the last deluge.

opening

The deluge covers the land and leaves Deucalion and Pyrrha as the only surviving man and woman. It transforms Phocis into part of the sea and a wide plain of waters.
