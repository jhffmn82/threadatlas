Yuba Bill stops the stagecoach after a fallen tree blocks the road near the site of a robbery six months earlier. While Bill and several passengers work on clearing the road, other members of the coach party take shelter in a cabin occupied by Hiram, Sophy, and Flo, the niece of Snapshot Harry, the alleged coach robber. Flo’s bold humor eases the tension caused by the family’s resentment of investigators and travelers. After the road is cleared, the passengers return to the coach, while the quiet, observant passenger and the box-seat occupant are said to have joined the clearing party.

Yuba Bill's coach is stopped by a masked horseman and armed accomplices, who take the express box during a stage robbery. Brice discovers that the treasure, a packet of greenbacks, had already been removed from the box, apparently while he was away at a settler's cabin. Bill believes the thieves were sneaks or traitors among the passengers rather than Snapshot Harry, who would likely pursue them after discovering the deception. Brice decides to seek out Snapshot Harry, while Bill promises to protect him in his report to the company.

II The robbery furnished the usual amount of copy for the local press.

Mr. Ned Brice visits Hiram Tarbox and his wife, using a flattering newspaper account of the robbery to gain their cooperation and learn how to reach Flora Tarbox and Snapshot Harry's secluded home. Brice follows a narrow mountain trail marked at times by Flora's footprints, crossing the ridge and the North Fork canyon into an isolated hollow. Near the hollow, an unidentified rifleman shoots Brice's hat, dispatch bag, and handkerchief without killing him. The rifleman then approaches, recognizes Brice as the expressman, and offers him whiskey at a cabin while Brice says he wants to see Snapshot Harry.

Brice tells Henry Dimwood, alias Snapshot Harry, that he knew the coach's lock had been forced and accepts blame for failing to warn anyone. Henry explains that two men from the gang secretly robbed the coach before the gang stopped it, making Yuba Bill and the coach victims of two robberies in one evening. Brice proposes recovering the greenbacks and turning the thief over to the express company, but Henry must consult his gang before agreeing. Henry sends Flora with Brice as a guide, and the two discuss their growing familiarity while traveling away from the gang's hideout.

"I am going to be a real guide to you now," she said suddenly.

Flora Dimwood secretly leads Brice through a buckeye thicket and along a narrow trail toward a hidden route up the mountain to the stage road. They find the cynical traveler dead beneath debris after apparently falling from the road, and Brice recovers the stolen greenbacks from his treasure belt. Flora persuades Brice not to return immediately to uncle Harry, warning that the gang may endanger him and the money. She directs Brice up a fallen pine and through an old waterway, then disappears among the trees as he escapes.

III In twenty-four hours Edward Brice was in San Francisco.

Edward Brice reaches San Francisco with the recovered treasure and reports to the manager, who keeps the affair out of the newspapers while arranging Brice's promotion. After receiving no replies to his letters, Brice follows a clue sent with his mended handkerchief and learns from Hiram Tarbox that Flora Dimwood is safe and attending a Catholic convent school. Flora reunites with Brice, explains that she buried the actual thief and burned the empty belt, and they begin courting. Tarbox reveals that the newspaper paragraph helped him become a land and timber agent and that Snapshot Harry may eventually sell his valley and water-power rights.

" A TREASURE OF THE REDWOODS PART I Mr.

Jack Fleming, a youthful prospector, becomes lost in the redwood forest while returning to camp and discovers a spring and quartz outcrop. He borrows a battered bread pan from Katinka Jallinger at her isolated cabin, leaving his gold specimen ring as a deposit. Fleming washes soil from the outcrop but finds only a few tiny flakes of gold, then returns the pan and retrieves his ring. He learns that Katinka's father, Henry Boone Jallinger, dislikes miners and lives by hunting, while Katinka is a seventeen- or eighteen-year-old young woman.

PART II Mr.

Mr. Jack Fleming receives a letter and gold flakes from Katinka Jallinger, who claims that gold escaped from his broken prospecting pan and appeared in biscuits made at the Jallinger cabin. Fleming returns to the spring and outcrop, finds no significant gold, and meets Katinka, who confesses that she salted the pan to make him come back. They investigate the Run and discover gold-bearing quartz, leading to Fleming and Katinka's engagement. Their discovery is associated with the Jallinger gold strike, while Fleming's partners remain skeptical of the story.

" A BELLE OF CANADA CITY Cissy was tying her hat under her round chin before a small glass at her window.

Cissy Trixit, the acknowledged belle of Canada City and daughter of banker Montagu Trixit, prepares to go out wearing her new hat. She and Miss Piney Tibbs parade through town, receiving the attention of miners, townsmen, and other residents before visiting the Reverend Mr. Windibrook's parsonage. Cissy dismisses the young engineer, Masterton, while Piney finds him attractive and respectable. On her return, Cissy notices that the townspeople are unusually preoccupied and that crowds have gathered around her father's bank and the newspaper office, leaving her vaguely frightened and confused.

The excitement over the failure of Montagu Trixit's bank did not burn itself out

The failure of Montagu Trixit's bank caused widespread excitement, distrust, and suspicion in Canada City, though the reported defalcations were exaggerated. Montagu Trixit was linked to the suspended Excelsior Bank and had fled eastward across the Sierras, but was believed to be hiding near the Summit. The directors of the New Mill sent Dick Masterton to locate Trixit and, if possible, obtain an interview with him. They hoped to recover securities that Trixit held privately for money advanced to the New Mill, because he might declare them as assets of his bank.

A few hours later he was on the coach.

Mr. Masterton travels by mountain wagon toward the next station while pursuing a mission connected with Montagu Trixit, whose financial scandal has prompted a staged search. During a severe snowstorm, the coach becomes stuck, and Masterton learns that Trixit may be hiding nearby and that Jake Poole, a deputy sheriff, is also involved. Suspecting that Chinese travelers are acting as spies through a signaling system, Masterton takes a saddled mustang and follows one of them toward a prospector's ruined hut. His horse breaks through the snow near the summit, throwing him into a suffocating depth and rendering him unconscious.

How long he lay there thus he never knew.

Masterton regains consciousness in a Chinese hut after falling into a prospector's hole while pursuing Cissy Trixit. Cissy, disguised as a Chinaman, saved him and explains that she traveled secretly to find her father, Montagu Trixit. Montagu tells Masterton that the proceedings against him have stopped and offers to return the securities if the directors send renewed notes to Sacramento within three days. Masterton and Cissy travel toward the station, and Masterton agrees to follow her to Sacramento.

The editor of the Mountain Clarion, Mr. Grey, discusses Colonel Starbottle's reported horse accident with his foreman, Richards, who suspects the colonel was attacked. Colonel Starbottle insists that he was thrown by the mustang he rode after leaving the Ramierez fonda, but his uneasy response to questions about choking leaves Mr. Grey curious. Richards investigates the fonda and is himself nearly strangled while watching the road, apparently by an unseen assailant. His encounter renews Mr. Grey's concern about the Ramierez family and the identity of a fair-skinned girl seen at the fonda.

PART II The next afternoon he went to the fonda.

Mr. Grey visits Ramierez's fonda to investigate the assault on Richards and Colonel Starbottle, but learns nothing useful about the assailants. He meets Ramierez's daughter, Cota, who returns from riding a distinctive pinto mustang. In a playful contest, Grey mounts the mustang and uses his spurs to stop its attempts to throw him, though the animal is injured. Cota then tricks Grey into the stable, and he leaves without solving the assault.

Mr. Grey learns from Enriquez Saltillo about a Spanish-California legend involving Friar Pedro, a lasso, and a spirit horse that strangles people. '" A JACK AND JILL OF THE SIERRAS It was four o'clock in the afternoon, and the hottest hour of the day on that Sierran foothill.

Ned Bray of the Eureka Mining Company fetches water from a spring on a brutally hot Sierran foothill. At the spring, Eugenia Neworth falls from the stage road and lands on Bray, who regains consciousness, rescues her from the ledge, and helps her climb back to the road. Bray and Eugenia compare their linked escape to Jack and Jill, while her family searches for and retrieves her. Eugenia thanks Bray despite her relatives’ condescension toward him.

Edmund Bray travels to San Francisco to thank Eugenia Neworth for her note and explain why he did not answer it sooner. After Eugenia initially treats him coldly, Bray enters the Neworths' garden by climbing the wall, and they clarify the misunderstandings surrounding her note and its delivery to the spring. Bray continues visiting San Francisco and eventually enters a new partnership with Eugenia, whom he says made the first strike on Eureka mountain.

Mr. Bilson's Housekeeper

After Joshua Bilson’s wife dies, he hires Miss Euphemia Trotter as housekeeper at the Summit House in Buckeye Hill, where her competence and severity make her respected and feared. Trotter, a poor and highly experienced working woman, nurses Chris Calton after he fractures his arm and collar-bone, while Calton becomes interested in the gentler chambermaid Frida Pauline Jansen. Frida’s attention helps Calton’s spirits during his recovery, and he returns to Roanoke Ledge after improving. He sends Trotter a valuable watch and chain in gratitude, while the nature of his gifts to Frida remains undisclosed.

Jim Calton visits Miss Trotter to object to his brother Chris's engagement to Frida Jansen, arguing that Frida seeks Chris's wealth and is an unsuitable match. Miss Trotter rejects Jim's attempt to make her interfere, but later agrees to speak with Frida after Chris asks for her help. In the Summit woods, Chris sees Frida with Mr. Bilson and returns changed, releasing Miss Trotter from her promise after discovering her faithlessness. Mr. Bilson then tells Miss Trotter that he and Frida will marry, while Chris leaves without revealing the discovery to his brother.

III When the news of the marriage reached Buckeye Hill, it did not, however, make much scandal, owing, possibly, to the scant number of the sex who are apt to disseminate it, and to many the name of Miss Jansen was unknown.

The news of Mr. Bilson’s marriage to Frida causes little scandal at Buckeye Hill, but Miss Trotter’s assumption of control of the Summit Hotel creates a stir. Chris Calton returns to the hotel after an illness and relapse, confesses that he never loved Frida, and declares his love for Miss Trotter. Miss Trotter resigns, leaves the Summit House, and later encounters Baroness X. at a foreign Spa, where she learns that Frida is now Mrs. Chris Calton. Miss Trotter leaves the Spa before Chris can return, and the nature of her relationship with him remains unresolved in the unit.
