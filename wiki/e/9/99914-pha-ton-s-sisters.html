Phaëton's sisters in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Phaëton's sisters, daughters of the Sun, mourn Phaëton day and night near his tomb, calling his name and beating their breasts. Overcome by grief at his fate, they end their lives as their bodies gradually transform into poplars; some writers say they become larch trees instead.

opening .. opening

Phaëton's sisters mourn him day and night, calling his name and beating their breasts. During their mourning, their bodies are gradually transformed into trees.

notes .. notes

Phaëton’s sisters were said to have ended their lives near his tomb after being overcome with grief. The story says that they were changed into poplars, though some writers instead said they became larch trees.

the Heliades in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The Heliades are Phaeton’s sisters, who mourn his death, become poplar trees by a river, and shed tears that turn to amber in the stream.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

LATONA AND THE RUSTICS

The Heliades mourned Phaeton after his death and were transformed into poplar trees on the banks of the river. Their continuing tears became amber as they fell into the stream.
