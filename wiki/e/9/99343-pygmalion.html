Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Pygmalion is a virtuous man who carves and loves an ivory statue, then marries it after Venus brings it to life. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Pygmalion is a sculptor who creates and loves an ivory statue, marries it after Venus brings it to life, and has a child with her.

Abstract written by a model

Pygmalion is a man of virtuous principles who, shocked by the Propœtides’ conduct, rejects female sex and lives without a wife. He carves and loves an ivory statue, prays to Venus for a wife like it, then marries the animated statue.

Pygmalion in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Pygmalion first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pygmalion, a sculptor who abhors women and plans to remain unmarried, creates and loves an ivory statue. After Venus brings Galatea to life, he marries her, and they have a child, Paphos.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter V

Pygmalion is a sculptor who rejects women and resolves to remain unmarried. He creates an exceptionally beautiful ivory statue, falls in love with it, and asks Venus to give him a wife like it. Venus brings the statue to life, and Pygmalion marries her; their child is Paphos.
