Atalanta's race

event · 3 works · 4 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Atalanta and Hippomenes's race, Atalanta's Race, attempt, course, race, that race, the contest, the contest of speed, the last course, the race, the usual race, this unreasonable race, δρόμον

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the race is a footrace between Atalanta and her suitors in which Hippomenes uses Venus's golden apples to win and marry her. In The Library, Atalanta's race is a contest in which wooers race against Atalanta for the right to marry her; Melanion wins by using golden apples. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Atalanta and Hippomenes's race is a contest for marriage that Hippomenes wins with three golden apples, though the lovers are later transformed into lions after offending Cybele.

the race in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where the race first appears

Abstract written by a model

The race is a footrace between Atalanta and her suitors: a suitor must outrun her to win marriage, while a slower contestant dies. Hippomenes competes, and Venus causes Atalanta to stop repeatedly for golden apples, allowing him to win and marry her.
