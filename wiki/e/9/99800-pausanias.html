Pausanias in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Pausanias is an ancient author who writes Arcadica, Attica, Laconica, Corinthiaca, and Bœotica. Across these works he records traditions, places, tombs, deluges, and accounts of figures including Lycaons, Cecrops’s daughters, Pentheus, Tereus, Orithyïa, Alpheus, Arethusa, and Narcissus. He weighs such stories critically: supporting some historical bases, accepting Alpheus’s course because of an oracle, and treating the passions of Alpheus and Narcissus’s transformation as fables or fiction.

notes .. notes

Pausanias, in his Attica, gives a version of the story in which the daughters of Cecrops, running mad, threw themselves from the top of a tower. He also records that Pandrosos had a temple built near Minerva's temple because she had remained faithful to the Goddess.

notes .. notes

Pausanias gives a different version of Narcissus’s story in his Bœotica. He places the fountain near Donacon, in the country of the Thespians, and regards Narcissus’s transformation into a flower as a fiction.
