frogs (transformed countrymen of Lycia) in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The frogs are transformed countrymen of Lycia, identified as peasants whom Latona transformed after they repulsed her. They live in a pool, diving, swimming, sitting on its bank, and leaping into the stream. Still quarrelsome and abusive, they have hoarse voices, bloated necks, heads joined to green backs, and mostly white bellies; their croaking is debated.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

The transformed countrymen live in the pool, plunging into the water, swimming, sitting on the bank, and leaping back into the stream. They continue to quarrel and abuse one another, but their voices are hoarse, their necks are bloated, their backs are joined to their heads, and their bodies are green-backed and white-bellied.

notes .. notes

The frogs are identified with the peasants whom Latona transformed after they repulsed her. Their croaking is discussed in connection with the repeated phrase “sub aqua,” though the proposed resemblance is regarded as fanciful.

the frogs in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Transformed rustics in the pond, frogs have bloated throats, stretched mouths, short necks, green backs, white bellies, croaking harshly and railing.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

LATONA AND THE RUSTICS

The frogs had bloated throats, stretched mouths, shortened necks, green backs, and white bellies. They lived in the water, croaked harshly, and continued to rail as the rustics had done.
