the fortune in Grampa in Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1924

The fortune is a hardship and a sought-after reward in Prince Tatters’s journey. Pudge first urges Tatters to seek it while searching for the King’s head, hoping he may avoid life as a common rag-picker, perhaps through a Princess with a fortune. Tatters, Grampa, Bill, and the adventurers pursue it through possible gold, jewels, land, a key, treasure, Fire Island, and Isa Poso. Grampa expects it at the rainbow’s end, while Bill repeatedly believes they have found it. Even after finding King Fumbo’s head, the fortune remains missing, and Tatters says they can be happy without it. At last, Peer Haps provides it as Tatters’s reward: a thousand gold bricks, beginning with one laid by the yellow hen.

Narrative, section by section

7 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 2

Pudge tells Prince Tatters to seek his fortune while searching for the King’s head. He suggests that Tatters might find a Princess with a fortune so he will not end as a common rag-picker.

CHAPTER 3

The fortune is another object of Prince Tatters’s search. Grampa jokes that they might find it along the road, and Bill joins them hoping to help locate it.

CHAPTER 5

The fortune is something Prince Tatters and his companions are seeking, possibly gold, jewels, or land. Bill first mistakes the golden key for the fortune, and Prince Tatters wonders whether it might open the bandit's treasure chest.

CHAPTER 10

The fortune is still being sought by Tatters, and Bill repeatedly announces that they have reached it when they arrive at the golden-looking Fire Island. Prince Forge John the First offers Tatters a different way to make a fortune through fireworks.

CHAPTER 13

The fortune remains undiscovered after the party finds no golden ship or diamonds at Isa Poso. Bill insists that they must continue looking for it, and Grampa agrees that they should not give up because Oz contains fortunes and other Princesses.

CHAPTER 15

The fortune remains undiscovered while the company finds King Fumbo's head instead. Grampa continues to believe that a fortune is waiting at the end of the rainbow, while Tatters says they can be happy without one.

CHAPTER 21

Peer Haps intends Tatters to receive a fortune as a reward, consisting of a thousand gold bricks. The yellow hen begins providing the fortune by laying one gold brick.
