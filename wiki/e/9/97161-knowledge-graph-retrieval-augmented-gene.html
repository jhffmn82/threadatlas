Knowledge Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation for LLM-based Recommendation / Abstract

Shijie Wang and colleagues study LLM-based recommendation and its use in recommender systems. They identify hallucinations and insufficient up-to-date, domain-specific knowledge as limitations of Large Language Models in this setting. They propose K-RagRec, a retrieval-augmented framework that retrieves high-quality, up-to-date structural information from an external knowledge graph to improve recommendation generation. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of their method.

1

The unit examines how Large Language Models are being used in recommender systems and the challenges faced by LLM-based recommender systems, especially hallucinations and missing up-to-date or domain-specific knowledge. It explains that Retrieval-Augmented Generation can address these problems, while vanilla RAG may add noise and overlook structural relationships. The proposed K-RagRec framework retrieves and reranks relevant knowledge sub-graphs from item knowledge graphs, selectively using popularity to improve efficiency. It then uses a GNN and projector to align the retrieved graph information with the LLM’s semantic space for recommendation generation.

2

The unit reviews RAG, which combines retrieval systems and language models to generate coherent and informative text. Early methods such as REALM, RETRO, and DPR retrieve relevant fragments from large corpora, but standard RAG can be affected by missed relevant chunks, noise, and harmful document disturbances. Recent studies retrieve structured and faithful knowledge from knowledge graphs, including sub-graphs converted into text or used to reduce hallucinations. The unit notes that RAG for recommendations remains under-explored and proposes retrieving knowledge sub-graphs from knowledge graphs to improve recommendation performance with language models.

3 / 3.1 .. 3.8

This methodology unit introduces K-RagRec, a framework that uses knowledge graphs and a frozen large language model for recommendations. It builds hop-field knowledge sub-graphs, indexes them semantically, selectively retrieves knowledge based on item popularity, and re-ranks the retrieved sub-graphs. K-RagRec encodes the selected sub-graphs with graph neural networks and maps them into the large language model’s embedding space as soft prompts. During training, only the graph neural networks and MLP projector are optimized with cross-entropy loss, while the large language model remains frozen.

4 / 4.1 / 4.1.1 .. 4.5

This unit evaluates K-RagRec, a knowledge-graph retrieval framework for large language model recommendation, on MovieLens-1M, MovieLens-20M, and Amazon Book using LLama-2-7b, LLama-3-8b, and QWEN2-7b. K-RagRec is compared with inference-only, prompt-tuning, and LoRA fine-tuning baselines using Accuracy and Recall@3/5 under a leave-one-out candidate-ranking setup. It achieves the strongest or near-strongest recommendation results in most settings, while also reducing inference time through popularity selective retrieval. Ablation and parameter analyses show that the framework's GNN encoding, retrieval components, and choices of retrieval thresholds and sub-graph counts affect performance.

5

The paper proposes K-RagRec, a framework that augments the recommendation capability of large language models by retrieving reliable and up-to-date knowledge from knowledge graphs. It uses a graph neural network and a pretrained language model for semantic indexing, supporting coarse- and fine-grained retrieval. A popularity selective retrieval policy improves efficiency by deciding whether an item should be retrieved based on its popularity. Experiments on three real-world datasets show that K-RagRec is effective, while its graph encoding helps large language models use structural information without requiring long context inputs.

6

This unit discusses limitations of a pioneering study on knowledge-graph RAG for LLM-based recommendations. The proposed framework was evaluated only on 7b and 8b models because of GPU resource constraints. The study used Freebase as its only external knowledge graph and used popularity to select items for retrieval. Future work will evaluate larger models and other knowledge graphs, and will investigate more flexible selective retrieval mechanisms such as reinforcement learning.
