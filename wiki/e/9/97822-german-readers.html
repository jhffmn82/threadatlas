German readers in Laurence Sterne in Germany

Harvey Waterman Thayer · 1905

German readers favored English literature when its English provenance assured consideration and were already interested in travel literature. Before and during the publication of the Sentimental Journey, they had some knowledge and appreciation of Laurence Sterne and responded strongly to the work’s emotional appeal, especially the Maria incident. After its 1768 publication, the book quickly gained commanding influence and wide popularity in Germany. Readers also showed particular interest in Sterne’s letters, notably the Yorick-Eliza correspondence, and often accepted translations and purported works, including The Koran, with little questioning of genuineness.

] CHAPTER III THE PUBLICATION OF THE SENTIMENTAL JOURNEY On February 27, 1768, the Sentimental Journey was published in London,[1] less than three weeks before the author’s death, and the book was at once transplanted to German soil, beginning there immediately its career of commanding influence and wide-spread popularity.

German readers already possessed some knowledge and appreciation of Laurence Sterne before the Sentimental Journey appeared. They were also strongly interested in books of travel and proved highly responsive to the work’s emotional appeal, especially the Maria incident.

Naturally Sterne’s letters found readers in Germany, the Yorick-Eliza correspond

German readers showed strong interest in Sterne’s letters, especially the Yorick-Eliza correspondence. They also encountered numerous translations and purported works that were more extensively recognized in Germany than in England.
