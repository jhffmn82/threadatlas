Orpheus's music

object · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 0 connections
Also called: Orpheus's singing, his music, his singing, his voice, melody, sing, songs, the harmony of his voice and his lyre, the sound of the lyre, thy song
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Orpheus's song and lyre charm woods, rocks, birds, serpents, and wild beasts, overcoming weapons until women's clamour, instruments, handclapping, and cries drown out the lyre.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Orpheus's singing is represented at Helicon by animals listening to him; a shepherd later sang some of Orpheus's verses aloud in his sleep, drawing shepherds and husbandmen together and causing them to overturn the pillar beside Orpheus's tomb.

BOOK THE ELEVENTH.

Orpheus's music charms woods, rocks, birds, serpents, and wild beasts. It initially overcomes the weapons thrown by the women, but their clamour, instruments, handclapping, and Bacchanalian cries prevent the lyre from being heard.
