Ames in The Valley of Fear

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1914-09/1915-05

Ames is the prim, respectable, capable butler of Manor House, a faithful servant who had served Sir Charles Chandos for ten years before working for Jack Douglas for five years. During the investigation of Douglas’s murder, he gives a clear, sincere account: he hears the bell, goes with Mrs. Allen to the front of the house, helps enter the room, returns to the study with Barker, and lowers the drawbridge. Shocked and quivering, he identifies the missing wedding ring and Douglas’s branded forearm, describes Douglas as restless and excited, explains the plaster as a shaving cut, and denies seeing the shotgun in the house.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter III

Ames was the prim, respectable, and capable butler of the Manor House. He stated that he had raised the drawbridge at nearly six o’clock, helped enter the room after the murder, identified the missing wedding ring, and confirmed that Douglas had long borne the brand on his forearm.

Chapter IV

Ames, the butler, is described as white and quivering from shock and is questioned about the house and Mr. Douglas. He confirms that Mr. Douglas often had the branded triangle mark, identifies the plaster on his jaw as a shaving cut, recalls that he seemed restless and excited, and says he has never seen the shotgun in the house.

Chapter V

Ames had been Jack Douglas's butler for five years and considered his account of the night clear and sincere. He heard the bell, went to the front of the house with Mrs. Allen, later returned to the study with Barker, and lowered the drawbridge.
