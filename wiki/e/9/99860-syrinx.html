Syrinx

person · 2 works · 12 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Naiad, Nymph, body of the Nymph, damsel, the nymph

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Syrinx is a famous Naiad and Nymph devoted to Diana and her virginity who repeatedly escapes Satyrs and Gods, flees Pan, seeks the Naiads’ help at the Ladon, and becomes reeds. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Syrinx is a nymph devoted to Diana who rejects woodland suitors, flees Pan, becomes reeds through water nymphs, and inspires Pan’s named instrument.

Chapter III

Syrinx is a nymph who rejects the satyrs and spirits of the wood and remains a faithful worshipper of Diana. When Pan pursues her, the water nymphs change her into reeds. The sound of Pan's sigh through the reeds inspires the instrument called Syrinx.
