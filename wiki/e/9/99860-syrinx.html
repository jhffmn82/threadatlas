Syrinx in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Syrinx, a famous Naiad and Nymph among the Hamadryads at Nonacris, is devoted to Diana and her virginity. Dressed like Diana, she repeatedly escapes Satyrs and Gods, flees Pan, seeks Naiads’ help at the Ladon, and becomes reeds.

1 section; each heading opens the text

opening

Syrinx is a famous Naiad among the Hamadryads of Nonacris who values her virginity and follows the Ortygian Goddess. She flees from Pan, asks the Naiads for help at the river Ladon, and is changed into reeds.

Syrinx in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Syrinx is a nymph devoted to Diana who rejects woodland suitors, flees Pan, becomes reeds through water nymphs, and inspires Pan’s named instrument.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter III

Syrinx is a nymph who rejects the satyrs and spirits of the wood and remains a faithful worshipper of Diana. When Pan pursues her, the water nymphs change her into reeds. The sound of Pan's sigh through the reeds inspires the instrument called Syrinx.
