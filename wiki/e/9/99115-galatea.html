Galatea in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Galatea, daughter of Nereus and Doris, is a sea Nymph protected by the Nereids. She loves Acis and rejects Polyphemus; after Polyphemus kills Acis, she changes his blood and remains into a river.

Galatea in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Galatea, queen of the deep and daughter of Nereus, is protected among Sea-Nymph sisters. She loves Acis and hates Polyphemus, escaping Polyphemus’s passion in the sea. After Polyphemus kills Acis, she transforms his blood into a clear, murmuring river and grants him his river-god grandfather’s honors.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XIX

Galatea was a daughter of Nereus who had escaped Polyphemus’s passion by hiding in the sea. She loved Acis and hated Polyphemus, who became jealous after seeing them together. After Polyphemus killed Acis with a rock, Galatea transformed Acis’s blood into a clear, murmuring river and gave him the honors of his river-god grandfather.
