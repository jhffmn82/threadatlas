Scython in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Scython is said to have been first a man and then a woman after the law of nature was altered, changing to the opposite sex. The report may instead arise from Thrace, once called Scython, later taking a feminine name.

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Scython is said to have been of both sexes, first a man and then a woman, after the law of nature was altered.

notes .. notes

Scython was said to have changed sex. This may have arisen because the country of Thrace, once called Scython, later took a feminine name, leading to the report that Scython had changed sexes.
