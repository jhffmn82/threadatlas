Danae

person · 3 works · 9 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: his own daughter, the captive girl, Δανάην, Δανάης
  • In The Library, Danae is confined in a brazen chamber, conceives Perseus by Zeus, is cast into the sea and reaches Seriphus, is loved by Polydectes, and returns with Perseus to Argos.
  • In The Library, Danae is confined by Acrisius in a brazen chamber, is said to have been seduced by Proetus or to have had intercourse with Zeus in the form of gold, and gives birth to Perseus.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Danae is confined in a brass tower, visited by Jupiter as gold, and sent to sea with Perseus in a chest.

Danae in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Danae, daughter of Acrisius and mother of Perseus, is confined in a brass tower, visited by Jupiter as gold, then sent to sea with Perseus in a chest.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

PERSEUS AND MEDUSA

Danae is the daughter of Acrisius and the mother of Perseus. Acrisius confines her in a brass tower, but Jupiter visits her in the form of a shower of gold; later, she is placed with Perseus in a chest and sent to sea.
