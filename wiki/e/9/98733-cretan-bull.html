In The Library, the Cretan Bull is an animal from Crete that was variously identified as the bull that ferried Europa for Zeus or one Poseidon sent from the sea; after Hercules released it, it wandered to Marathon and harmed inhabitants.

Cretan Bull in The Library

Apollodorus · about AD 70 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Cretan Bull first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Cretan Bull is an animal from Crete, variously identified as the bull that ferried Europa for Zeus or one Poseidon sent from the sea. It rose at Minos’s request as proof of his kingdom, but Minos kept it instead of sacrificing it; Poseidon made it savage and caused Pasiphae to desire it. After Hercules released it, it wandered through Sparta and Arcadia to Marathon, harming inhabitants. A bull also reached Eryx after swimming from Rhegium and was mingled with Eryx’s herds.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

THE LIBRARY, II. v. 4-5

The Cretan Bull is variously identified as the bull that carried Europa to Zeus or the bull that Poseidon sent from the sea. After Hercules releases it, it wanders through Sparta and Arcadia, crosses the Isthmus, and reaches Marathon, where it harms the inhabitants.

THE LIBRARY, II. v. 9

A bull escapes from Hercules' herd at Rhegium, plunges into the sea, and swims across to Sicily. It reaches the plain of Eryx, where Eryx mingles it with his own herds.

THE LIBRARY, 111. 10

A bull rose from the sea at Minos’s request to prove that he had received the kingdom from the gods. Minos spared that bull for his herds instead of sacrificing it, and Poseidon consequently made it savage and caused Pasiphae to desire it.
