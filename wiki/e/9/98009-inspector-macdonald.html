Inspector MacDonald in The Valley of Fear

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1914-09/1915-05

Inspector MacDonald is a young but trusted Scotland Yard inspector and C.I.D. investigating officer, a London detective from Scotland with a good Aberdeen upbringing. He visits Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson after recognizing Douglas and Birlstone in a decoded message, and reports the murder of Mr. Douglas at Birlstone Manor House. Though he first wants to find Porlock in London, he travels by cab to Birlstone after expecting details from White Mason. At the manor, MacDonald investigates John Douglas's murder closely. He questions Cecil Barker and Mrs. Douglas about Douglas's California past, a secret society, marriages, Barker's relationship with Mrs. Douglas, and the missing wedding ring. After Holmes identifies Barker's slipper as the source of a blood mark, MacDonald concludes Barker created the apparent window footprint. Working with White Mason, he traces a suspected outsider and reports a bicycle identification and possible sightings, though these produce arrests in several places. Finally, he is astonished that the man investigated was not John Douglas, angrily challenges Holmes over the concealed inquiry, and learns that the earlier search had been misguided.

Narrative, section by section

8 sections; each heading opens the text

PART I / Chapter I

Alec MacDonald, an Inspector of Scotland Yard, visits Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson. He recognizes the names Douglas and Birlstone on their decoded message and reports that Mr. Douglas of Birlstone Manor House was murdered the previous night.

Chapter II

Inspector MacDonald discusses the warning letter and the Birlstone case with Sherlock Holmes. He initially favors finding Porlock in London but agrees to travel to Birlstone, where he expects to receive fuller details from White Mason.

Chapter IV

Inspector MacDonald challenges the idea that an outsider entered the house, arguing that a deliberate killer would not choose a noisy shotgun when escape was difficult. He agrees that suicide is impossible and accepts that the evidence creates serious difficulties for both an inside and an outside killer.

Chapter V

Inspector MacDonald questions Cecil Barker about Jack Douglas's past in California, the secret society, Douglas's first wife, his marriage to Mrs. Douglas, Barker's friendship with Mrs. Douglas, and the missing wedding ring. He presses Barker when Barker refuses to answer whether Douglas approved of his friendship with his wife.

Inspector MacDonald questions Mrs. Douglas about the shooting, Mr. Douglas's American past, his marriage, and the missing wedding ring. After Holmes matches Cecil Barker's slipper to the blood mark on the window sill, MacDonald concludes that Barker made the apparent window footprint.

It was nightfall before Inspector MacDonald and White Mason came back

Inspector MacDonald returns with White Mason, reporting that the bicycle has been identified and that a description of the suspected outsider has been obtained. He traces the suspect from Tunbridge Wells to the house and theorizes that the man shot Mr. Douglas and escaped.

Chapter VII

Inspector MacDonald reports that supposed sightings of the mysterious cyclist have led to arrests in East Ham, Leicester, and Liverpool. He initially objects to abandoning the case and to the moat-draining plan, but agrees to follow Holmes’s instructions and witnesses Cecil Barker retrieve the sodden bundle.
