Abstract written by a model

In The Lost King of Oz, Mombi's magic powers are lost or forgotten witchcraft used for transformations, whose absence leaves her unable to restore Pajuka, remember the King's transformation, or change Snip, and whose attempted restoration causes the palace to sink. In The Lost King of Oz, green magic transformed the King and may restore him, but its enchantments are weakened by kindness and generosity. In The Giant Horse of Oz, Mombi's enchantments cause the good witch's disappearance, bewitch Agnes, drive Prince Philador's troubles, change Mombi's appearance, abduct Orin, and ultimately turn Mombi into a good, powerful witch.

Mombi's magic powers in The Lost King of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1925 · the work’s page · read it where Mombi's magic powers first appears

Abstract written by a model

Mombi's magic powers are her lost or forgotten witchcraft, used for transformations. At the journey's start, their absence leaves Mombi a cook, unable to restore Pajuka, remember the King's transformation, or turn Snip into a muffin. She seeks Ozma's magic belt to recover her own magic, plans to find the old King with Pajuka's help, and hopes to use restored powers to rule Oz. Later, her magic is meant to return Humpy to his proper form after a counting-and-button ritual, but instead makes the palace sink amid darkness and confusion.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 2

Mombi has lost or forgotten her witchcraft and says that she would not still be a cook if she retained her magic powers. Her loss of magic prevents her from restoring Pajuka to his proper form and leaves her unable to remember the King's transformation.

CHAPTER 4

Mombi's magic powers are gone, so Pajuka says she cannot turn Snip into a muffin and eat him. Mombi nevertheless plans to steal Ozma's magic belt and use its magic to restore the King and transform others.

CHAPTER 5

Mombi says that she has forgotten her magic and wants to steal some of Ozma's magic so that she can remember it. She still knows practical tricks, including using purple pepper to repel a weenix.

CHAPTER 7

Mombi believes she needs Pajuka in order to recognize the old King and regain her magic powers. She intends to use those restored powers in her larger ambition to rule Oz.

CHAPTER 17

Mombi's magic is intended to make Humpy assume his proper form after she counts seven figures and eats two buttons from Kabumpo's robe. Instead, it causes the palace to grind and crash downward, producing darkness and confusion.

Mombi's enchantments in The Giant Horse of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1928 · the work’s page · read it where Mombi's enchantments first appears

Abstract written by a model

Mombi's enchantments cause the good witch's disappearance, bewitch Agnes, and drive Prince Philador's troubles. Her magic changes her appearance, abducts Orin on a black eagle, and tries to make Orin an old witch, but instead makes her a good, powerful witch. Orin's recognition through the blue window breaks the spell, and the Wizard plans to undo the damage.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 17

Mombi's enchantments are described by Prince Philador as the cause of his troubles and are among the mysteries Ozma and the Wizard must address. The Wizard intends to undo as much of their damage as possible.

CHAPTER 18

Mombi's magic allows her to change her appearance, abduct Orin on a black eagle, and attempt to transform Orin into an old witch. Her spell instead leaves Orin a good and powerful witch, and the spell is broken when Orin recognizes Cheeriobed and Philador through the blue window.
