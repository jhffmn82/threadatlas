Ariadne's crown constellation

object · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Constellation, a constellation, crown constellation, shape of the crown, the stars
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, it is a constellation formed from Ariadne's crown to immortalize her name, placed between Hercules and Ophiuchus.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, it is fixed in the heavens after Bacchus places Ariadne's crown in the sky, lies between Hercules and the man who holds the serpent, and preserves the crown's form.

Ariadne's crown constellation in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Ariadne’s crown constellation, fixed in the heavens after Bacchus places her crown in the sky, lies between Hercules and the man who holds the serpent, preserving the crown’s form.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XIV

Ariadne’s crown constellation remains fixed in the heavens after Bacchus places her crown in the sky. It appears between the kneeling Hercules and the man who holds the serpent, and its stars preserve the crown’s form.
