Coyote in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Coyote first appears

Abstract written by a model

Coyote is an animal repeatedly described as a helper or thief who brings fire to people. It steals fire from Spider’s house, carries a lit cedar brand tied to its tail, becomes exhausted, and passes the fire to bat; across accounts it leads, steals, carries, and organizes fire’s transfer to humans.

Coyote repeatedly appears as a helper or thief who brings fire to humans. For the Sia Indians he stole fire from Spider, for the Navahoes he carried fire from the animals, for the Uintah Utes he led the theft and passed the fire among animal runners, and for the Karok Indians he stole fire and organized its transfer.

Abstract written by a model

Coyote is the first thief of fire in a Thompson Indians myth. Wearing a headdress of combustible shavings, Coyote dances around a fire, flees when it ignites, and passes fire along a chain of animals to Fox, Wolf, and Antelope.

In a myth of the Thompson Indians, Coyote is the first thief of fire. He dances around a fire wearing a headdress of combustible shavings, then runs away when the shavings ignite and passes the fire along a chain of animals including Fox, Wolf, and Antelope.
