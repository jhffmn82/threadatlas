Halcyone in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Halcyone, daughter of Æolus and wife of Ceyx, loves and shares a bed with him. Fearing the sea, she begs him not to voyage, watches his ship vanish, then waits, prepares for his return, and prays to Juno. Morpheus reveals Ceyx’s death; she finds his body, mourns, and divine pity transforms them into birds. As a kingfisher, she broods on a sea-floating nest during seven calm winter days.

FABLE VII. [XI.410-748]

Halcyone fears Ceyx’s voyage because of the dangers of the sea and begs him either to remain or to take her with him. She watches the ship depart until it disappears from sight, then returns to their bed in grief and anxiety.

In the meantime, the daughter of Æolus, ignorant of so great

Halcyone waits for Ceyx, prepares garments for his return, and prays to Juno that he may be safe and prefer no other woman. After Morpheus reveals Ceyx’s death in a vision, she mourns, finds his body on the shore, and is transformed with him into a bird. As a kingfisher, she broods on a nest floating on the sea for seven calm winter days.

Halcyone in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Halcyone, daughter of Aeolus and devoted wife of Ceyx, fears his dangerous sea voyage and begs him not to go or to take her. After learning in a dream of his death and finding his body, she embraces him; pitying gods transform them into halcyon birds, which she broods over for seven placid winter days.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CEYX AND HALCYONE: OR, THE HALCYON BIRDS

Halcyone, the daughter of Aeolus and wife of Ceyx, fears his voyage and begs either to accompany him or for him not to go. After Ceyx's death, she learns the truth through a dream, finds his body floating toward shore, and flies out to embrace it. The pitying gods transform her and Ceyx into birds.
