Abstract written by a model

The tomb of Phaëton stands on the banks of the river Po, containing his buried body and marked by marble bearing his name. The Hesperian Naiads place him there after his failed chariot attempt; Clymene mourns, and the tomb is linked to his mother’s and sisters’ grief and deaths.

The Hesperian Naiads place Phaëton's body in the tomb and inscribe verses saying that he was the driver of his father's chariot and failed in a great attempt. Clymene finds his bones buried on a foreign bank and mourns at the marble bearing his name.

notes .. notes

Plutarch places the tomb of Phaëton on the banks of the river Po. The tomb is associated with the grief and deaths of Phaëton’s mother and sisters.
