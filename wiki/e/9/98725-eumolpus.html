Eumolpus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Eumolpus is a son of Poseidon and Chione, thrown into the sea by his mother, rescued by Poseidon, carried to Ethiopia, and raised by Benthesicyme. He is associated with Ismarus, fluteplaying, and cleansing and initiating Hercules at Eleusis. Banished, he goes to Tegyrius and Thrace, later flees to the Eleusinians, returns to Tegyrius, and becomes king of Thrace. He fights for the Eleusinians against the Athenians and is killed by Erechtheus.

XV. Πανδίονος δὲ ἀποθανόντος οἱ παῖδες τὰ

Eumolpus was born to Chione and Poseidon and was raised by Benthesicyme. After being banished, he went to Tegyrius, later fled to the Eleusinians, and eventually returned to Tegyrius and succeeded to the kingdom. He fought for the Eleusinians against the Athenians and was killed by Erechtheus after Erechtheus sacrificed his youngest daughter.

After being banished, he goes to Thrace, becomes its king, fights for the Eleusinians against the Athenians, and is killed by Erechtheus; he is the father of Ismarus.

Eumolpus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Eumolpus, a noble man in Homer’s poem, was said to come from Thrace as the son of Poseidon and Chione. He was killed in the Eleusinian-Athenian battle after his son Immaradus died; Athenians and Euboeans showed his tomb.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXVIII.

Eumolpus was said to have come from Thrace as the son of Poseidon and Chione, and Homer called him a noble man. His tomb was shown by both the Athenians and Eubœans; he was killed in the battle between the Eleusinians and Athenians through the death of his son Immaradus, and Ceryx was said by heralds not to have been his son.
