Tethys

deity · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 3 connections

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Tethys is a deity married to Ocean; with Oceanus, she purifies Glaucus, pities the falling Æsacus, receives him in the sea, covers him with feathers, and prevents his sought death through transformation. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Tethys is one of the Titans who ruled over the sea before Neptune and Amphitrite succeeded to dominion of the waters.

FABLE VIII. [XI.749-795]

Tethys pities Æsacus as he falls from the rock and receives him softly. She covers him with feathers as he swims, preventing him from obtaining the death he seeks and enabling his transformation into a didapper.
