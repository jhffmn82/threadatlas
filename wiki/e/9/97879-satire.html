satire in Laurence Sterne in Germany

Harvey Waterman Thayer · 1905

Satire deliberately targets sentimentalism, especially Sterne’s sentimental influence, exposing conflicts between universal sympathy and immediate inhumanity; Timme grows burlesque, while Wezel’s earnest version falters by making its heroine pitiable rather than ridiculous.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

A former acquaintance of Frau Kurt, whose favorite reading was Shandy, Wieland’s

The unit describes satire as arising chiefly from the contradiction between elevated claims of universal sympathy and failures to act humanely in immediate situations. Timme’s satire becomes increasingly burlesque, while Wezel’s comparable satire is more earnest but less effective because it makes its heroine pitiable rather than ridiculous.
