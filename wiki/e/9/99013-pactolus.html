Pactolus

place · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: river, river Pactolus, the river, the river adjoining to great Sardis, the stream
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Pactolus is the river adjoining great Sardis where Midas plunges into its bubbling source, transferring his golden power to the stream and leaving gold in its sands.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the river Pactolus is where Bacchus instructs Midas to wash away his golden power and punishment, causing gold to pass into its waters and sands.

Pactolus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Pactolus is a river adjoining great Sardis. Bacchus sends Midas there; when Midas plunges into its bubbling source, his golden power enters the stream, leaving gold in its sands.
