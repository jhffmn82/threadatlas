Abstract

The abstract introduces HopRAG, a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework designed to address imperfect retrieval caused by retrievers focusing on lexical or semantic similarity rather than logical relevance. HopRAG constructs a passage graph in which text chunks are vertices and LLM-generated pseudo-queries establish logical connections as edges. During retrieval, its retrieve-reason-prune mechanism starts with similar passages and explores multi-hop neighbors using pseudo-queries and LLM reasoning to identify relevant passages. Experiments on multiple multi-hop benchmarks show that this approach expands retrieval through logical connections and improves final answer quality.

1 / Introduction .. Contributions

The unit examines retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) for knowledge-intensive tasks and argues that retrieval should account for logical relevance in addition to lexical and semantic similarity. It identifies imperfect retrieval, including low precision and recall, as a major problem for multi-hop or multi-document QA tasks. It introduces HopRAG, which builds a passage graph using pseudo-queries and performs reasoning-augmented graph traversal through retrieval, reasoning, and pruning. Experiments reportedly show that HopRAG improves answer metrics and retrieval F1 over conventional information retrieval approaches.

2 / Related Work .. Tree&Graph-structured RAG

Retrieval-Augmented Generation improves large language models by retrieving relevant information from external knowledge sources. Retrieval models have progressed from sparse methods such as TF-IDF and BM25 to dense neural retrievers and adaptive methods such as Self-RAG and FLARE, while the knowledge index remains logically unstructured. Tree- and graph-structured RAG methods model logical relations through hierarchical summaries, mindmaps, knowledge graphs, and graph indices, but tree methods are limited to single documents and graph methods can be costly and return triplets rather than plain text. HopRAG is presented as a lightweight, downstream-task-friendly alternative supporting flexible logical modeling, cross-document organization, and efficient construction and updating.

3 / Method .. Algorithm

This unit introduces HopRAG, a logic-aware RAG system for answering queries that require information from multiple passages. It constructs a graph-structured index in which vertices store passages and directed logical edges connect related passages, using Query Simulation and Edge Merging. At retrieval time, HopRAG applies a Retrieve-Reason-Prune pipeline that combines hybrid retrieval, reasoning-augmented graph traversal, and Helpfulness-based pruning to form the context for a large language model.

4 / Experiments / 4.1 / Experimental Setups / Datasets .. Ablation on Traversal Model

The unit evaluates HopRAG for multi-hop question answering on HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and MuSiQue using EM and F1 score, comparing it with sparse, dense, tree-structured, and graph-structured retrieval baselines. HopRAG generally achieves the strongest results, although SiReRAG on HotpotQA and HippoRAG on 2WikiMultiHopQA outperform it in some settings. Ablations show that increasing topk improves answer quality but can reduce retrieval F1, while increasing nhop improves retrieval at higher LLM cost. Traversal-model experiments show that HopRAG remains effective with Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct or non-LLM graph traversal, with GPT-4o-mini producing the best reported results.

5 / Conclusion

The conclusion concerns HopRAG, a RAG system with a logic-aware retrieval mechanism. HopRAG connects related passages through pseudo-queries to identify relevant passages in multi-hop neighborhoods, improving retrieval precision and recall. On MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and HotpotQA, it outperformed conventional RAG systems and state-of-the-art baselines, achieving over 36.25% higher answer accuracy and 20.97% improved retrieval F1 than conventional information retrieval approaches. The paper concludes that logical reasoning benefits retrieval and identifies broader-domain scaling and lower-cost indexing and traversal as future directions.

Limitations

This unit describes limitations of HopRAG and its current evaluation. The evaluation focuses on multi-hop or multi-document QA tasks, leaving HopRAG's generalization capabilities across broader domains to be explored. The text also identifies potential improvements through more sophisticated query simulation and edge merging strategies. Although six degrees of separation and small-world networks inspired HopRAG, the degree distribution of its passage graph vertices does not exhibit a power-law characteristic.
