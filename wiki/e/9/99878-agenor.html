Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Agenor is the father of Cadmus, Phœnix, Cilix, and Europa, and orders his sons to search for Europa after her abduction. In The Library, Agenor goes to Phoenicia, marries Telephassa, fathers four children, and orders his sons to search for Europa after her disappearance.

Agenor in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Agenor first appears

Abstract written by a model

Agenor is a father, identified as the son of Neptune and Libya according to Apollodorus, and husband of Telephassa. He has Cadmus, Phœnix, Cilix, and Europa. After Europa is ravished and carried away, he orders his sons to search for her and forbids their return without her, threatening Cadmus with exile. They fail to recover Europa and establish themselves in different countries.

Agenor commands Cadmus to seek Europa, who has been ravished and carried away. He threatens Cadmus with exile if he does not find her.
