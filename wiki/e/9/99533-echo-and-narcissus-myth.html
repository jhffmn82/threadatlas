Echo and Narcissus myth

topic · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: ECHO AND NARCISSUS, Narcissus's story, The Fable, The fable of Narcissus, account of his change into the flower which bears his name, the story of Echo and Narcissus, this story

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Narcissus's story presents a moral lesson showing self-love's fatal effects: Narcissus pursues an image that continually withdraws, likened to the limited reality of eagerly pursued pleasures. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Echo and Narcissus myth follows Echo's unreturned love for Narcissus, her loss into a voice, and Narcissus's self-love, death beside a fountain, and transformation into a flower.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter VIII

The myth tells how Echo loved Narcissus but could speak only in reply, while Narcissus rejected her and later fell in love with his own reflection. Echo became only a voice, Narcissus died beside the fountain, and a flower grew in his place.
