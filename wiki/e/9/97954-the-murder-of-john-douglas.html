Abstract written by a model

The murder of John Douglas at Birlstone Manor House is a perplexing, extraordinary apparent homicide and the central crime investigated by the police. Douglas is reported killed shortly before midnight, found with his head almost destroyed by a close-range blast from a sawed-off shotgun. The scene suggests that an attacker hid behind curtains, shot him, and escaped through an open window; it also contains a blood mark, footprints, a missing wedding ring, and a card marked “V.V. 341.” Inspector Alec MacDonald treats the death as murder rather than suicide and later reconstructs Hargrave as the gunman. Holmes challenges the account, arguing that the ring, card, and reported sequence are impossible and that the shot was fired about half an hour before the alarm raised by Barker and Mrs. Douglas. He concludes that a conspiracy concealed the truth. The investigation’s apparent victim is ultimately revealed to be alive, leaving the supposed murder not fully explained.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

PART I / Chapter I

The murder of Mr. Douglas is reported by Inspector Alec MacDonald at the end of the unit. Mr. Douglas, of Birlstone Manor House, was horribly murdered the previous night.

Chapter II

The Birlstone murder concerns the killing of John Douglas shortly before midnight. The available report states that it is undoubtedly murder, involves a shotgun wound to the head, has produced no arrest, and presents perplexing and extraordinary features.

Chapter III

John Douglas’s murder occurred at about half-past eleven in the Manor House. He was found in a room with his head almost destroyed by a shotgun blast, while evidence suggested that a person had hidden behind the curtains, killed him, and escaped through the window.

Chapter IV

The murder of Mr. Douglas is treated as a homicide rather than suicide. The circumstances include a close-range shotgun blast, a missing wedding ring, an open window, a blood mark, footprints, and a card marked V.V. 341, but the killer’s identity and escape remain unexplained.

Chapter VI

Holmes believes the reported sequence surrounding the murder is impossible, particularly the removal and replacement of the wedding ring and the placement of the card. He reasons that the gunshot probably occurred around a quarter to eleven, about half an hour before Barker and Mrs. Douglas raised the alarm.

It was nightfall before Inspector MacDonald and White Mason came back

MacDonald attributes the murder of Mr. Douglas to Hargrave, who allegedly waited near the house and shot him with a concealed shotgun. Holmes disputes the reported timing, saying that the crime was committed half an hour earlier than reported.

Chapter VII

The supposed murder of John Douglas is the investigation that Inspector MacDonald and White Mason believe they are conducting. The revelation that John Douglas is alive shows that the apparent murder has not been fully explained.
