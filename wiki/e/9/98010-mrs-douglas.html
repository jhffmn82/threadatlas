Though reticent about that past and never having been in America, she believed powerful enemies were tracking him, heard him mention the Valley of Fear and Bodymaster McGinty during a fever, and regarded their wedding as romantic rather than mysterious. After a shot, she came downstairs but was stopped by Cecil Barker, told that Jack was dead, and removed without seeing the body. Trembling and greatly excited, she made no outcry; later she appeared pale and drawn but composed. She told investigators of the enemies and the missing wedding ring. Her laughing privately with Barker and concern about Holmes sharing confidential information raised suspicion. Holmes concluded that she and Barker concealed the truth, conspired to hide the crime, fabricated evidence of an escape through the window, and aided Hargrave's escape. During the later confrontation, she told Barker he had done enough, embraced the man revealed as John Douglas, and urged him to tell his story. She is subsequently described as John Douglas's widow, and Ivy Douglas sent a cable reporting that Jack Douglas was lost overboard near St. Helena in a gale.

Mrs. Douglas in The Valley of Fear

Narrative, section by section

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter III

Mrs. Douglas was a tall, dark, slender Englishwoman about twenty years younger than John Douglas. She was retiring and devoted to her household, but had sometimes shown acute uneasiness when her husband was late returning; after the shot, she came downstairs and was taken away by Mrs. Allen before seeing the body.

Chapter V

Mrs. Douglas came downstairs after the bell rang, but Cecil Barker stopped her and told her that Jack was dead. She returned to her bedroom with Mrs. Allen, where she remained trembling and greatly excited but made no outcry.

Inspector MacDonald had sent up a note to the effect that he would wait

Mrs. Douglas tells the investigators that she was stopped by Cecil Barker on the stairs after hearing the shot and never entered the room where the tragedy occurred. She says Mr. Douglas had powerful enemies connected with his American past and repeatedly referred to the Valley of Fear. She also recalls that Bodymaster McGinty was a name he spoke during a fever and that his wedding ring had been taken.

Chapter VI

Mrs. Douglas is seen laughing with Barker behind a hedge in the garden, apparently having put aside her show of grief. She asks Dr. Watson whether Sherlock Holmes must pass confidential information to the detectives and says that his answer could greatly affect her. Holmes believes that she and Barker are lying and concealing the truth about the murder.

It was nightfall before Inspector MacDonald and White Mason came back

Sherlock Holmes suspects that Mrs. Douglas was involved in a conspiracy to conceal something and helped the murderer escape, or at least reached the room before he escaped. He believes she participated in fabricating evidence of an escape through the window.

Chapter VII

Mrs. Douglas listens to the confrontation in the study and tells Cecil Barker that he has done enough. She then embraces the man revealed to be John Douglas and says that it is best for him to tell his story.

Epilogue

Ivy Douglas sent a cable reporting that Jack Douglas had been lost overboard in a gale off St. Helena and that no one knew how the accident had occurred.
