Abstract written by a model

Imitators of Sterne were German writers and a major subject in the study of Sterne’s German reception. They arose within a broader tendency in Germany to imitate admired English authors and movements, and historians recognized their connection with Sterne, though earlier accounts were incomplete or inexact. Typical disciples included Johann Georg Jacobi and Johann Gottlieb Schummel. Their works repeated sentimental journeys and Sterne’s formal devices, while adapting them to German settings, moral teaching, professional concerns, stronger emotion, or less whimsy. Sobering critics condemned their incapacity, lack of originality, and failed reproduction of Yorick’s manner; Lichtenberg ridiculed their eccentricities and sentimental travel. Ultimately, their influence gradually ceased to operate as a living principle in German letters.

NEW YORK, May 1, 1905.

Imitators of Sterne are identified in the contents as a major subject of the study. The introduction places their activity within Germany’s broader tendency to imitate admired English authors and literary movements.

] CHAPTER II STERNE IN GERMANY BEFORE THE PUBLICATION OF THE SENTIMENTAL JOURNEY It is no exaggeration to assert that the works of Yorick obtained and still retain a relatively more substantial position of serious consideration and recognized merit in France and Germany than in the countries where Sterne’s own tongue is spoken.

German imitators of Sterne are identified as part of the broader history of his reception in German literature. The unit notes that historians had recognized the connection between Sterne and his German imitators, although earlier scholarship had generally offered only incomplete or inexact general statements.

] CHAPTER VI IMITATORS OF STERNE Among the disciples of Sterne in Germany whose literary imitation may be regarded as typical of their master’s influence, Johann Georg Jacobi is perhaps the best known.

The imitators of Sterne discussed in this unit include Johann Georg Jacobi and Johann Gottlieb Schummel. They reproduce Sterne’s sentimental travel patterns and formal devices but often alter them through German settings, moral instruction, professional concerns, heightened emotionality, or reduced attention to whimsy.

] CHAPTER VII OPPOSITION TO STERNE AND HIS TYPE OF SENTIMENTALISM Sterne’s influence in Germany lived its own life, and gradually and imperceptibly died out of letters, as an actuating principle.

German imitators of Sterne were condemned for their incapacities, lack of originality, and unsuccessful reproduction of Yorick’s manner. Lichtenberg repeatedly ridiculed their efforts and satirized their eccentricities and sentimental journeying.
