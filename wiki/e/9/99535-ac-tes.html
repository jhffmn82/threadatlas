Acœtes

person · 2 works · 8 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Acetes, one, who, from the Etrurian nation, the Etrurian Acœtes, the pilot, the prisoner, this one

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Acœtes is a Mæonian sailor, pilot, and attendant of Bacchus who survives his fellow sailors’ transformation into dolphins and recounts their punishment before Pentheus. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Acetes is a Maeonian sailor, fisherman, and pilot who resists the sailors seeking to sell Bacchus, brings Bacchus to Naxos, and vanishes from prison after Pentheus orders his execution.

Acœtes in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Acœtes first appears

Abstract written by a model

Acœtes is a humble Mæonian sailor, pilot, and attendant of Bacchus who survives when his fellow sailors are transformed into dolphins. Captured before Pentheus, he recounts Bacchus’s punishment of the deceptive crew; Pentheus orders torture, but Acœtes’s chains fall away and the prison opens.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

opening

Acœtes, an Etrurian sailor and follower of Bacchus, is captured and brought before Pentheus. He tells how Bacchus was taken aboard his ship and how the god punished the sailors who tried to deceive him. Pentheus orders Acœtes tortured, but the prison doors open and his chains fall away by themselves.

Acetes in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Acetes first appears

Abstract written by a model

Acetes, a Maeonian sailor, fisherman, and pilot, alone resists sailors seeking to sell Bacchus. Surviving their transformation, he brings Bacchus to Naxos, celebrates his rites, then vanishes from prison after Pentheus orders his execution.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XIV

Acetes is a fisherman and pilot from Maeonia who opposes his fellow sailors’ attempt to seize Bacchus and sell him as a slave. After Bacchus transforms the sailors into dolphins, Acetes steers him to Naxos and celebrates his sacred rites. Pentheus imprisons him and orders his execution, but Acetes disappears when his chains fall away and the prison doors open.
