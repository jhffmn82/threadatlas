marriage of Cephalus and Procris in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The marriage of Cephalus and Procris, arranged by Erectheus and joined by love, begins with mutual kindness, equal affection, and happiness. Cephalus’s suspicion and deceptive test threaten it, but his confession and request for forgiveness restore them to harmony. As Procris dies, she invokes their marital ties and asks Cephalus not to share them with the supposed Nymph Aura.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Erectheus united Cephalus and Procris, and their love and recent marriage initially made Cephalus happy. Their marriage is threatened by Cephalus’s suspicion and deceptive test, but after he confesses his fault and asks forgiveness, they are restored to one another and live harmoniously.

notes

The son of Deïoneus married Procris, the daughter of Erectheus, king of Athens.

opening

Cephalus and Procris’s marriage was initially marked by mutual kindness, social love, and equal affection. As she was dying, Procris invoked their marriage ties while asking Cephalus not to share them with the supposed Nymph Aura.
