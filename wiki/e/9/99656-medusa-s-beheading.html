Medusa's beheading

event · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Medusa’s beheading, beheaded her, cut off the head, killed Medusa, the decapitation of Medusa, took the head from off the neck, ἐκαρατόμησεν αὐτήν

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Perseus beheads Medusa and her serpents while they sleep using his bronze shield’s reflection; Pegasus emerges from her blood. In The Library, Perseus decapitates the sleeping Medusa while looking at her image in a bronze shield and following Athena’s direction; Pegasus and Chrysaor spring from her after her head is cut off. In The Library, Perseus beheads Medusa with Athena guiding his hand while he looks at her image in a brazen shield; some accounts say he does so for Athena’s sake because Medusa wished to compete with Athena in beauty.
