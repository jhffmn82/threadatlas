Polydectes

person · 3 works · 14 quoted facts · 6 connections

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Polydectes is the ruler and king of Seriphus, brother of Dictys, who loves Danaë, threatens Danaë and Dictys, hates Perseus, demands Medusa’s head, and is turned to stone by Medusa’s face. In The Library, Polydectes is king of Seriphus and Dictys’s brother who loves Danae and contrives to send the grown Perseus after the Gorgon’s head. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Polydectes is king of Seriphus who sends Perseus after Medusa’s head to kill him, but is shown the head when Perseus returns.

Polydectes in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Polydectes first appears

Abstract written by a model

Polydectes is the ruler and king of Seriphus, brother of Dictys. While preparing to marry Hippodamia, he loves Danaë and violently threatens Danaë and Dictys. He hates Perseus, dismisses Perseus’s victory over Medusa, and demands Medusa’s head. When Perseus returns, he uses Medusa’s face to transform Polydectes into stone.

opening

Polydectes, ruler of Seriphus, continues to hate Perseus and claims that Perseus’s victory over Medusa is a fiction. Perseus answers by using Medusa’s face to turn Polydectes into stone.

notes .. notes

Polydectes was king of Seriphus and became enamored of Danaë while preparing to marry Hippodamia. He demanded that Perseus obtain the head of Medusa, but when Perseus returned and found Danaë and Dictys taking refuge from Polydectes’ violence, he changed Polydectes into stone.
