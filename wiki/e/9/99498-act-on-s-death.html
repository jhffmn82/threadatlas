Actæon’s death

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, it is the killing of Actæon by his own hunting dogs, which surround him and bury their jaws in his body while his companions keep seeking him. In The Library, it is Actaeon’s death after he sees Artemis bathing; Artemis changes him into a deer and causes his dogs to become mad and devour him.

Abstract written by a model

Actæon’s death is the killing of Actæon by his own hunting dogs, which surround him and bury their jaws in his body while his companions keep seeking him. Some accounts say the dogs became mad; Apollodorus places the death on Mount Cithæron after Actæon saw Diana bathing.

opening

Actæon dies after his own dogs surround him and bury their jaws in his body. His companions do not understand what is happening and continue to seek Actæon as though he were absent.

notes .. notes

Actæon’s death is variously attributed to his own hunting dogs, which some authors say became mad and devoured him. Apollodorus places his death on Mount Cithæron after he saw Diana bathing, while other ancient authorities connect it with his contempt for Diana or his advances toward Semele.
