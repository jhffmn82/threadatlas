Actæon fable in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Actæon’s transformation is a fable attributed most probably to Diana Britomartis: Diana punishes Actæon after he sees her naked, with accounts differing between transformation and death by mad dogs, financial ruin from hounds, or offenses involving Diana or Semele.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

notes .. notes

The fable presents Diana as punishing Actæon for the misfortune or indiscretion of seeing her naked. Ancient accounts differ about the cause and meaning of the story, including literal transformation and death by his dogs, a symbolic account of financial ruin from maintaining hounds, and claims that Actæon had offended Diana or pursued Semele.
