person · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 3 connections

  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Hesione is chained to rocks for a sea monster, rescued by Hercules, and later married to Telamon.

Hesione in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Hesione, Laomedon’s daughter, is chained to rocks for a sea monster, rescued by Hercules, and later married to Telamon.

FABLE IV. [XI.194-220]

Hesione, the daughter of Laomedon, is demanded for a sea monster and is chained to rugged rocks. Hercules delivers her, and she is later given in marriage to Telamon.

Hesione in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Hesione, daughter of Laomedon, is fastened to rocks for a sea monster but rescued by Hercules; after Heracles captures the city, she is awarded to Telamon.

THE LIBRARY, II. v. 9

Hesione was Laomedon's daughter. Laomedon fastened her to rocks near the sea to be devoured by the sea monster, but Hercules rescued her.
