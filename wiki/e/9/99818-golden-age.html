In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Golden Age is the first age of humanity, marked by innocence, happiness, truth, right, and nature's abundance during Saturn's reign.

Golden Age in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Golden Age first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Golden Age is the first of the four ages, a period of greater happiness associated with the more happy days of the first parents. Founded first and governed by Innocence and Justice, it is marked by people practising faith and rectitude without laws, punishment, war, or fear, while Earth provides abundant food. Perpetual spring prevails and seasons are unknown. Its people are more just and happier than those of the Silver Age. Yet the account is inconsistent: wars and crimes are also said to occur during it.

The Golden Age was the name the poets gave to the more happy days of the first parents, a time associated with innocence.

opening

The Golden Age is presented as a time when men are more just and consequently happier than in the Silver Age.

notes .. notes

During the Golden Age, perpetual spring reigned on the earth, and the division of the year into seasons was not known. The account is presented as inconsistent because wars and crimes are also said to have occurred during Saturn’s Golden Age.

Golden Age in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Golden Age first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Golden Age is the first age of humanity, an age of innocence, happiness, truth, and right without laws or magistrates. During Saturn’s reign in Italy after his flight from Jupiter, nature provided abundantly without labor: perpetual spring, necessities from the earth, milk and wine rivers, and honey from oaks.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ROMAN DIVINITIES

The Golden Age was the period during which Saturn was said to have reigned in Italy after fleeing there following his dethronement by Jupiter.

CHAPTER II

The Golden Age was the first age of humanity, marked by innocence, happiness, truth, and right without laws or magistrates. The earth supplied necessities without labor, perpetual spring prevailed, and rivers flowed with milk and wine while honey came from the oaks.
