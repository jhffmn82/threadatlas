Abstract written by a model

In The Library, Pandion is a king associated with Athens and later Megara, whose reign, family, wars, and succession are recounted. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Pandion is king of Athens and father of Progne and Philomela; he entrusts Philomela to Tereus, urges her return, and dies in Tartarus before old age.

Pandion in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Pandion first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pandion is a king associated with Athens and later Megara. One account makes him king after Erichthonius died; another says he succeeded Cecrops, as Cecrops’ son by Metiadusa. During his reign Demeter and Dionysus came to Attica. He married Zeuxippe and had Procne, Philomela, Erechtheus, and Butes. In war with Labdacus over boundaries, he sought Tereus’ help, won, and gave Procne to Tereus. After his death, Erechtheus received the kingdom and Butes the priesthoods. Another account says he was expelled by Metion’s sons, went to Megara, married Pylia, became king there, and had Aegeus, Pallas, Nisus, and Lycus.

Pandion married Zeuxippe, his mother's sister, and had Procne, Philomela, Erechtheus, and Butes. When war broke out with Labdacus over boundaries, he called on Tereus for help and, after the victory, gave Procne to Tereus in marriage.

XV. Πανδίονος δὲ ἀποθανόντος οἱ παῖδες τὰ

When Pandion died, his sons divided his inheritance: Erechtheus received the kingdom and Butes received the priesthoods of Athena and Poseidon Erechtheus. A later Pandion, identified as the son of Cecrops and Metiadusa, succeeded to the kingdom after Cecrops.

Pandion in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Pandion first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pandion, king of Athens and father of Progne and Philomela, entrusts Philomela to Tereus, urges her swift return, and dies in Tartarus before old age.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

opening

Pandion allows Philomela to accompany Tereus and entrusts her to Tereus's care. He urges Tereus to protect her and asks Philomela to return quickly, while giving both of them his hands as a pledge of fidelity.
