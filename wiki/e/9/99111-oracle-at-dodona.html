oracle at Dodona

institution · 2 works · 8 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Dodonaean oracle, oracle, oracle from Dodona, responses, the following message from Zeus at Dodona, the god at Dodona, the oracle at Dodona, the oracular responses

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the Dodonaean oracle is described as famous for ambiguous, ingenious responses, with accounts of its three priestesses, later two priests, Selli, sacred trees, pigeons, and wind-struck cauldrons. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the oracle at Dodona attributes the Calydonians’ affliction to Dionysus, requires Callirhoe’s sacrifice or a volunteer’s death to end it, exhorts Athenians to respect suppliants, commands mercy toward conquered Lacedæmonians, and instructs the Athenians to colonize Sicily.

Abstract written by a model

The Dodonaean oracle, famous for ambiguous, ingenious responses, originated with three priestesses and later two priests; accounts describe Selli, sacred trees, pigeons, and wind-struck cauldrons.

FABLES V. AND VI. [XIII.623-718]

The Dodonaean oracle became famous for its responses, which its priests delivered ambiguously and with considerable ingenuity. The text gives several accounts of its origin and operation, including priestesses, priests called Selli, sacred trees, pigeons, and wind-struck cauldrons.

oracle at Dodona in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where oracle at Dodona first appears

Abstract written by a model

The oracle at Dodona attributes the Calydonians’ affliction to Dionysus and requires Callirhoe’s sacrifice or a volunteer’s death to end it. Later it exhorts Athenians to respect suppliants, commands mercy toward conquered Lacedæmonians, and instructs the Athenians to colonize Sicily.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXI.

The oracle at Dodona declared that Dionysus's wrath had caused the Calydonians' trouble. It said that the trouble would not end until Coresus sacrificed Callirhoe or someone volunteered to die instead of her.

CHAPTER XXV.

The oracle at Dodona delivered a message to the Athenians in the days of Aphidas. It commanded them to spare conquered Lacedæmonians who sought protection as suppliants.
