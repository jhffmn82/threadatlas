Pittheus

person · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Πιτθέως, Πιτθεὺς

Abstract written by a model

In The Library, Pittheus, son of Pelops and king of Troezen, hosted Aegeus, understood an oracle, intoxicated him, and arranged his union with Aethra. In Description of Greece, Pittheus was associated with a three-seat monument at Troezen where he and two others were said to judge; he built the oldest-known temple of the Thearian Apollo, taught language, erected an altar to the Muses, and united Hyperea and Anthea into Troezen.

Abstract written by a model

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIX.

Pittheus was associated with the monument bearing three seats where he and two others were said to give judgments. He was also credited with building the temple of Thearian Apollo, teaching the art of language, erecting an altar to the Muses, and joining Hyperea and Anthea into the city of Troezen.
