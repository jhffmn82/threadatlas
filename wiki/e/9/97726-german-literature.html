German literature

topic · 1 work · 3 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: German letters, home literature, native German literature, the literature of France and Germany

Abstract written by a model

German literature is presented as developing through successive connections with foreign literatures, with English influence becoming predominant in the later eighteenth century. In the 1760s it was especially receptive to British authors and forms, through extensive reading, translation, imitation, and arguments about originality. Sterne’s works entered this environment in translation, influenced German sentimental culture, and helped establish conventions such as “Leben und Meynungen” in novel titles. Although contemporary thinkers complained of a lack of originality, the account also says German literature increasingly had native worth that could overshadow Sterne’s influence on Herder and Lessing. Thus Sterne remains a significant extending force rather than the whole story.

NEW YORK, May 1, 1905.

German literature is presented as developing through successive connections with foreign literatures, with English influence becoming predominant in the latter half of the eighteenth century. The period is characterized by extensive reading, translation, imitation, and debate over originality.

] CHAPTER II STERNE IN GERMANY BEFORE THE PUBLICATION OF THE SENTIMENTAL JOURNEY It is no exaggeration to assert that the works of Yorick obtained and still retain a relatively more substantial position of serious consideration and recognized merit in France and Germany than in the countries where Sterne’s own tongue is spoken.

German literature in the 1760s was highly receptive to British authors and forms, including Richardson, Fielding, Shakespeare, Young, Goldsmith, Ossian, Percy, and Sterne. Sterne’s works entered this literary environment through translation and helped establish conventions such as the use of “Leben und Meynungen” in novel titles.

[37] Herder’s early acquaintance with Sterne has been already treated.

German literature increasingly possessed native worth that may have overshadowed Laurence Sterne’s influence on writers such as Johann Gottfried Herder and Gotthold Ephraim Lessing. The unit nevertheless presents Sterne as a force in extending literary conventions and shaping German sentimental culture.
