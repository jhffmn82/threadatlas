textual subgraphs in GRAG: Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Yuntong Hu, Zhihan Lei, Zheng Zhang, Bo Pan, Chen Ling, Liang Zhao · 2025 · the work’s page · read it where textual subgraphs first appears

Abstract written by a model

A textual subgraph is a subgraph structure in a textual graph, defined by subsets of nodes and edges and their natural-language attributes. It is the retrieval target of Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation rather than discrete entities or individual documents. Its high-dimensional textual nodes and edges make selecting the subgraph that maximizes generation quality a combinatorial optimization problem. Beginning with selected important or key nodes, it unfolds as a union of induced partial neighborhoods and selected K-hop neighbors. For a relevant query, GRAG retrieves an approximate optimal textual subgraph through divide-and-conquer, K-hop ego-graphs, and soft pruning in linear time.

Abstract written by a model

Textual subgraphs are subgraph structures of a textual graph, containing selected nodes and edges with their natural-language features and networked documents’ textual and topological relationships. They matter because GRAG retrieves them rather than discrete entities, preserving relationships and improving retrieval precision, robustness, and coverage. GRAG uses divide-and-conquer to approximate an optimal query-relevant subgraph in linear time, then supplies it to LLMs.

Abstract

Textual subgraphs are retrieved by Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation using a divide-and-conquer strategy that retrieves the optimal subgraph structure in linear time.

Introduction

A textual subgraph contains relevant networked documents together with their textual and topological relationships. GRAG retrieves textual subgraphs instead of discrete entities and uses them to preserve relationships and improve the precision, robustness, and coverage of retrieval.

Problem Formalization

Textual Subgraphs are subgraph structures of a textual graph. They are formed from subsets of the graph’s nodes and edges while retaining the corresponding natural language attributes.

Conclusion

Textual subgraphs are retrieved according to query relevance and supplied to LLMs by GRAG. The retrieval framework uses a divide-and-conquer strategy to approximate the optimal textual subgraph.
