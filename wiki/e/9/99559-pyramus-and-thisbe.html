Pyramus and Thisbe in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Pyramus and Thisbe is a Babylon-set moral tale, known to Ovid and Hyginus, contrasting strongly and affectingly with the loves of Mars and Venus; it warns young people against rash engagements and parents against rigorous resentment.

Pyramus and Thisbe was a story known to Ovid and Hyginus, both of whom placed it in Babylon. It is interpreted as a moral tale warning young people against rash engagements and parents against enforcing resentment too rigorously.

Pyramus and Thisbe in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

communicate_through wall crack [the crack separates their adjoining houses] Pyramus and Thisbe were young lovers in Babylonia whose parents forbade them to marry. A misunderstanding involving a lioness led Pyramus and then Thisbe to kill themselves, and they were buried together while the mulberry tree preserved the memory of their blood.

Pyramus and Thisbe were young lovers in Babylonia whose parents forbade them to marry. A misunderstanding involving a lioness led Pyramus and then Thisbe to kill themselves, and they were buried together while the mulberry tree preserved the memory of their blood.
