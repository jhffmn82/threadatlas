the Chimæra in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The Chimæra is described by poets as a monster with a lion’s head, goat’s body, and dragon’s tail; it may instead be a volcanic mountain in Lycia, occasionally erupting flame, whose regions were linked to lions, goats, and serpents.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

notes .. notes

The Chimæra is described by the poets as a monster with the head of a lion, the body of a goat, and the tail of a dragon. It may instead have been a volcanic mountain in Lycia, whose upper, middle, and lower regions were associated with lions, goats, and serpents.

Chimera in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

A fire-breathing monster with lion forepart, goat middle head, and dragon tail, begotten by Typhon on Echidna and bred by Amisodares; it devastated country and cattle until Bellerophon, riding Pegasus, shot it down from height.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE LIBRARY, 11. 1. 5—u. 1

The Chimera had the forepart of a lion, the tail of a dragon, and a goat's middle head, which belched fire. It devastated the country and harried the cattle until Bellerophon shot it down from the height while riding Pegasus.

Chimaera in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

A fire-breathing monster with lion, goat, and dragon parts ravages Lycia until Bellerophon, aided by Pegasus, defeats it.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter X

The Chimaera was a fire-breathing monster whose forepart combined a lion and a goat and whose hind part was that of a dragon. It ravaged Lycia until Bellerophon defeated it with the aid of Pegasus.
