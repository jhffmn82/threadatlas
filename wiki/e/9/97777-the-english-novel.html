the English novel in Laurence Sterne in Germany

Harvey Waterman Thayer · 1905

The English novel, characteristic of England and distinct from romance, guides German fiction through Robinson Crusoe’s everyday extraordinary, Richardson’s sensibilities, and Fielding’s realistic resistance to emotional excess.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

NEW YORK, May 1, 1905.

The English novel is described as a decisive influence on the development of German fiction. Robinson Crusoe connected the extraordinary with everyday life, Richardson emphasized the analysis of human sensibilities, and Fielding strengthened the representation of real and ordinary life while opposing excessive emotionalism.
