White Mason in The Valley of Fear

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1914-09/1915-05

White Mason is a quiet, comfortable-looking detective in a loose tweed suit, serving as the local officer at Birlstone and chief Sussex detective. A personal friend of Inspector MacDonald, he recognizes that Mr. Douglas's murder is exceptionally perplexing and asks MacDonald to bring Sherlock Holmes. He briefs Holmes, Dr. Watson, and MacDonald, leads them through the Manor House, examines evidence, and favors an outsider and possible secret-society explanation. He later helps investigate the bicycle and suspect's movements, questions links to Mrs. Douglas, follows Holmes's advice, joins the vigil, and witnesses the recovery of the sodden bundle.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter II

White Mason is the local officer at Birlstone and a personal friend of Inspector MacDonald. He sends MacDonald a private message describing the case as exceptionally perplexing and asks that Sherlock Holmes be brought to investigate.

Chapter IV

White Mason, the chief Sussex detective, briefs Holmes, Dr. Watson, and Inspector MacDonald on the murder and leads them through the Manor House. He examines the hammer and shotgun, favors the theory that an outsider entered the house, and suggests that the card and branded mark may indicate a secret society.

It was nightfall before Inspector MacDonald and White Mason came back

White Mason accompanies Inspector MacDonald in identifying the bicycle and investigating the suspect's movements. He questions why the suspect did not return to the hotel and expresses difficulty explaining how Mrs. Douglas could be connected with an American assassin.

Chapter VII

White Mason participates in the discussion with Inspector MacDonald and agrees to follow Sherlock Holmes’s advice. He accompanies the others during the vigil outside Birlstone Manor and witnesses the recovery of the sodden bundle.
