Icarus's fall and death

event · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Icarus’s fall and death, drowned, he fell, he fell into the sea, he perishes, perished, the middle of the air
  • In The Library, it is the event in which Icarus, after flying too high against Daedalus's injunction, loses his wings when the glue melts in the sun and perishes in the sea thereafter called the Icarian Sea.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, it is the event caused by the Sun melting the wax of Icarus's wings, leading to his fall into the sea and burial by Dædalus.

Icarus's fall and death in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Icarus's fall and death is an event caused by the Sun melting the wax of his wings. Unable to catch the air, he falls into the sea calling for Dædalus; Dædalus buries him, and Icaria bears his name.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE III. [VIII.183-259]

When the Sun melts the wax of Icarus's wings, he shakes his bare arms and can no longer catch the air. He falls into the sea while calling for Dædalus, and Dædalus later buries his body; the sea and nearby land are associated with his name.
