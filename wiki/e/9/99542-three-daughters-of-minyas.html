  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII: The daughters of Minyas are women who reject Bacchus’s orgies and worship. During his festival, they stay indoors at textile work, carding, spinning, weaving, and directing handmaids, and tell stories, including Pyramus and Thisbe. As they continue to despise Bacchus and desecrate the festival, fires and light drive them to hide in the smoking house; they gain membranes and wings, lose their former shape, and become small night-flying creatures, said to be bats. Another account suggests secret death or flight instead.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK THE FOURTH.

The daughters of Minyas refuse to celebrate the festival of Bacchus and remain indoors, carding wool, spinning thread, weaving, and directing their handmaids. They pass the time by telling stories, including the story of Pyramus and Thisbe.

notes .. notes

The daughters of Minyas, including Alcithoë, refused to join the rites of Bacchus and were said to have been changed into bats. The explanation suggests that they may instead have been secretly killed or forced to flee, with their absence later explained through the fable.

opening

The daughters of Minyas continue working while despising Bacchus and desecrating his festival. They hide throughout the smoking house as fires and light appear, then acquire membranes and wings, lose their former shape, and become small night-flying creatures.
