Abstract written by a model

In The Library, Serpent is a reptile believed in Armenia to know magical life-giving herbs and springs, and in resurrection stories revives a dead or wounded serpent with a herb, teaching humans about plants' healing, renewing, and potentially immortal power. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, snakes on Helicon are said to have less potent venom because of the mountain's pastures, while wild snakes generally have venom deadly to both humans and animals.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

VII.—THE RESURRECTION OF GLAUCUS

Serpents repeatedly serve as the source from which humans learn the power of life-giving plants. In the stories, a serpent applies a herb to a dead or wounded serpent, and in Armenian belief serpents know springs and flowers that heal them, renew their youth, and may confer immortality.
