Glaucus

person · 4 works · 40 quoted facts · 11 connections

Abstract written by a model

In The Library, Glaucus of Anthedon is a fisherman who discovers a life-giving herb, becomes immortal, and lives as a marine deity. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Glaucus is a mortal fisherman who becomes a sea God, loves Scylla, and rejects Circe’s advances. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Glaucus is a fisherman who becomes a marine god and is believed by seafarers to predict the future. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Glaucus is a fisherman transformed into a sea god who loves Scylla and seeks Circe’s help.

Glaucus of Anthedon in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Glaucus of Anthedon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Glaucus of Anthedon, a fisherman distinguished from Glaucus son of Minos, discovers a life-giving herb by watching an animal revive, tastes it, becomes immortal, leaps into the sea, and lives there as a marine deity.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

VII.—THE RESURRECTION OF GLAUCUS

Glaucus of Anthedon learned the life-giving property of a herb or grass by observing a dead or dying fish or hare revive after contact with it. After tasting the herb, he became immortal and leaped into the sea, where he continued to live as a marine deity.

Glaucus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Glaucus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Glaucus is a mortal fisherman, later a sea God, associated with Anthedon. He discovers grass that revives fish; after eating it, strange passions drive him into the sea, where he is transformed and then purified by Oceanus and Tethys. He has a green beard and finny tail. Loving Scylla, he seeks Circe’s help after Scylla rejects him, but rejects Circe’s advances, remains devoted to Scylla, mourns her transformation, and flees alliance with Circe. Accounts also variously name his father, say he drowned, make him Triton, and portray him as appearing to the Argonauts, foretelling Jason, interpreting Nereus, and teaching Apollo prediction.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

FABLE VIII. [XIII.898-968]

Glaucus was formerly a mortal fisherman who discovered that a certain grass revived dead or dying fish. After chewing the grass, he became overwhelmed by strange passions, plunged into the sea, and was transformed into a sea God. He pursued Scylla because he loved her, but after she rejected him, he went to the court of Circe.

BOOK THE FOURTEENTH.

Glaucus, the Eubœan plougher of the waves, asks Circe to help him because Scylla has rejected his love. He refuses Circe’s advances, remains devoted to Scylla, mourns her transformation, and flees from an alliance with Circe.

Abstract written by a model

Glaucus begins as a fisherman, then becomes a marine god after eating transforming grass. Especially seafaring men believe he predicts the future; Pindar and Aeschylus celebrated him, and Aeschylus made him a play’s subject.

Glaucus was a fisherman who became a marine god after eating a certain grass. He is believed, especially by seafaring men, to predict the future, and Pindar and Aeschylus celebrated him, with Aeschylus making him the subject of one of his plays.

Glaucus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Glaucus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Glaucus begins as a fisherman, discovers an herb that revives dead fish and makes him long for water, and is transformed by water-gods into a sea god. With sea-green hair and a fish’s tail, he loves rejecting Scylla and seeks Circe’s help.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

PROSERPINE

Glaucus is a fisherman who discovers an herb that revives dead fish and causes him to long for the water. The water-gods wash away his mortal nature and transform him into a sea-god with sea-green hair and a fish's tail. He loves Scylla, but she rejects him, so he asks Circe to make Scylla return his love.
