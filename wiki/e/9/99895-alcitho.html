Alcithoë

person · 1 work · 7 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Alcathoë, Alcithoë, the daughter of Minyas

Abstract written by a model

Alcithoë, daughter of Minyas and sister of Aristippe/Arsinoë and Leucippe, rejects Bacchus’s festival and rites, denying his parentage, alongside her sisters. While weaving at a hanging web after her sisters fall silent, she takes up the telling of transformations to entertain her listeners; she is associated with the daughters of Minyas being changed into bats.

Alcithoë is in one work on this site, The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

BOOK THE FOURTH.

Alcithoë, daughter of Minyas, rejects the rites of Bacchus and rashly denies that Bacchus is the progeny of Jupiter. She joins her sisters in this impiety.

notes .. notes

Alcithoë was the daughter of Minyas and one of the women who refused to join the rites of Bacchus. She is associated with the story in which the daughters of Minyas were said to have been changed into bats.

opening

Alcithoë is called upon after her sisters become silent. While running her shuttle through the warp of the hanging web, she recounts several transformations and says that she will entertain her listeners with a pleasing novelty.
