In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Clytie is a nymph and Leucothoë’s rival who loves the Sun, exposes Leucothoë’s affair, and is transformed into a sunflower. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Clytie is a water-nymph who loves Apollo without return, watches the sun for nine days, and becomes a sunflower.

Clytie in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Clytie first appears

Abstract written by a model

Clytie, a nymph and Leucothoë’s rival, passionately loves the Sun and envies Leucothoë for his love. She reveals Leucothoë’s affair to her father; when the Sun rejects her, she fasts nine days and watches his path across the sky. Rooted in the ground, she is transformed into a sunflower while retaining her passion.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Clytie envies Leucothoë because of the Sun's love for her and reveals their affair to Leucothoë's father. After the Sun rejects her, Clytie fasts for nine days, watches him as he moves across the sky, and gradually becomes rooted in the ground before being changed into a sunflower.

notes .. notes

Clytie is described as Leucothoë's rival and as having been metamorphosed into a sunflower. Her jealousy may have been based on the claim that the sunflower kills the frankincense tree when growing near it.

Clytie in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Clytie first appears

Abstract written by a model

Clytie, a water-nymph, loves Apollo without return, pines watching the sun for nine days without food or drink, roots in the ground, and becomes Clytie's sunflower.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter VIII

Clytie was a water-nymph who loved Apollo without receiving his love in return. She sat for nine days without food or drink while watching the sun, and her limbs eventually rooted in the ground and her face became a sunflower.
