Adonis

Abstract written by a model

In The Library, Adonis is a boy born from the myrrh tree, whose disputed parentage and divided year are recounted; he is concealed by Aphrodite, gives his share of the year to her, and is killed by a boar while hunting. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Adonis is a beautiful youth and hero, loved by Venus, who is fatally wounded by a boar and transformed after death into a flower, thereafter dividing his time between earth and the Infernal Regions. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Adonis is a youth beloved by Venus who joins her hunting, ignores her warning, and is killed by a wild boar.

Adonis in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Adonis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Adonis is a boy and infant in accounts of disputed parentage: Hesiod names Phoenix and Alphesiboea, while another account names Cinyras and Metharme. He is born from the myrrh tree ten months after Smyrna's transformation, then concealed by Aphrodite. Zeus divides his year among himself, Persephone, and Aphrodite; Adonis gives his own share to Aphrodite. While still a boy, he is hunted, wounded, and killed by a boar during a hunt.

XIV. Κέκροψ αὐτόχθων, συμφυὲς ἔχων σῶμα

Adonis was a son of Cinyras and Metharme according to this account. While still a boy, he was wounded and killed by a boar while hunting because of the anger of Artemis.

Adonis in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Adonis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Adonis is a beautiful youth, hero, and son of Cinyra, also described as born to Ammon and Myrrha and cursed with Myrrha by Cinyras. He spends youth in Arabia, is educated by the Naiads, and is loved by Venus. Though Venus warns him against dangerous hunting, he pursues wild beasts and is fatally wounded in the groin by a boar. Venus changes his blood into a crimson flower. He thereafter divides time between earth with Venus and the Infernal Regions, marked by an annual return festival.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

[Footnote 42: _The Panchæan land._--Ver. 309. Panchæa was a region

Adonis is described as the son of Ammon and Myrrha and the grandson of Cinyras. Cinyras curses him together with Myrrha, and he spends part of his youth in Arabia; poets consequently said that Myrrha gave birth to him there.

FABLE IX. [X.519-707]

Adonis grows from a beautiful infant into a youth and then a man whose beauty attracts Venus. Venus advises him to avoid hunting lions, boars, and other dangerous wild beasts because his courage could bring harm to them both.

FABLE X. [X.708-739]

Adonis ignores Venus’s advice and pursues a wild boar. The boar wounds him in the groin with its tusks and leaves him dying on the sand, after which Venus changes his blood into a crimson flower.
