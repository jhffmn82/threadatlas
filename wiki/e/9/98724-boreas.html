Boreas in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Boreas carries off Orithyia by the Ilissus and fathers Cleopatra, Chione, Zetes, and Calais, including winged sons.

XV. Πανδίονος δὲ ἀποθανόντος οἱ παῖδες τὰ

Boreas carried off Orithyia while she was playing by the Ilissus and had intercourse with her. Their children were Cleopatra, Chione, Zetes, and Calais.

Boreas in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Boreas is a winged god and north wind, also called Aquilo, governing northern regions and king of Thrace. After Erectheus refuses his marriage suit to Orithyïa, he uses his winds to carry her by force from near the Ilissus to Ciconian lands; they become spouses and parents of Calaïs and Zethes.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Boreas seeks Orithyïa in marriage but, after Erectheus refuses his consent, abandons persuasion and carries her away by force. He uses his winds to shake the earth, ruffle the sea, sweep the ground, and fly with Orithyïa to the people and walls of the Ciconians.

notes .. notes

Boreas was described as the king of Thrace. According to Apollodorus and Pausanias, he seized Orithyïa near the river Ilissus and carried her into his dominions.

Boreas in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Boreas, also called Aquilo, is a rude north wind who raises storms, loves and carries off Orithyia, fathers Zetes and Calais, and is chained during a flood.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XV

Boreas, also called Aquilo, is the north wind and is characterized by rudeness. He loves Orithyia, cannot breathe gently or sigh, and finally seizes and carries her off; their children are Zetes and Calais.
