Schummel in Laurence Sterne in Germany

Harvey Waterman Thayer · 1905

Schummel, born in May 1748, was a German author, writer, and pedagogue who later attained some eminence. As a young man, after reading Laurence Sterne, he wrote Empfindsame Reisen durch Deutschland as an imitator of Sterne, using quotations from Sterne’s Journey and adopting sentimental relationships, digressions, interruptions, typographical devices, experiments, coined words, and Yorickian borrowings. Its central events were generally independent, but the second volume became especially incoherent; by the third, he largely left Sterne’s manner behind, mixed narratives and dramatic pieces, and condemned weaknesses in his own work. He also wrote the fourth part of Die Gleichheit der menschlichen Herzen, published Uebersetzer-Bibliothek, Fritzen’s Reise nach Dessau, and, about fifteen years later, Reise durch Schlesien. The later travel works were largely free of Sterne-like whim and sentiment, and Reise durch Schlesien contrasts his earlier sentimental extravagance with a more worldly attitude.

] CHAPTER VI IMITATORS OF STERNE Among the disciples of Sterne in Germany whose literary imitation may be regarded as typical of their master’s influence, Johann Georg Jacobi is perhaps the best known.

Johann Gottlieb Schummel wrote Empfindsame Reisen while still a young man, apparently drawing his inspiration directly from reading Sterne. His narrative imitates the forms, incidents, sentimental relationships, digressions, interruptions, typographical devices, and expressions of Sterne’s works, although its central events are generally independent of the English models.

Schummel imitates Sterne in the second volume through sentimental episodes, digressions, typographical experiments, mock-scientific descriptions, coined words, and borrowings from Yorickian scenes. In the third volume he largely abandons Sterne’s stylistic manner, incorporates narratives and dramatic pieces of different kinds, and openly condemns many weaknesses of his own work.

Schummel wrote the fourth part of Christian Opitz’s novel Die Gleichheit der menschlichen Herzen, possibly during or before the composition of the third volume of his own work. His Uebersetzer-Bibliothek contained passages in which Sterne was visible, including a reference to Slawkenberg’s book on noses, but his later Fritzen’s Reise nach Dessau and Reise durch Schlesien were largely free of Sterne-like whim and sentiment. In Reise durch Schlesien, he explicitly contrasted his former sentimental extravagance with his later, more worldly attitude.
