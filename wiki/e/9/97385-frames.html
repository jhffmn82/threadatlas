  • In Fact, Fetch, and Reason: A Unified Evaluation of Retrieval-Augmented Generation: FRAMES, or Factuality, Retrieval, And reasoning MEasurement Set, is a high-quality dataset, benchmark, evaluation set, and unified evaluation framework for end-to-end Retrieval-Augmented Generation. It contains 824 human-annotated test questions built from Wikipedia, each requiring information from 2–15 articles. Its central purpose is to assess factuality, retrieval, reasoning, and accurate synthesis together rather than separately, using realistic multi-document queries that reveal capabilities and limitations of Large Language Models and RAG systems. The questions are challenging multi-hop or multi-step tasks that require retrieval and integration across sources, coherent final responses, and temporal disambiguation. Reasoning types include multiple constraints, numerical reasoning, tabular reasoning, temporal reasoning, and post-processing; multiple constraints are the largest category at approximately 36% of samples, followed by numerical reasoning at approximately 20%. FRAMES begins by positioning itself as a response to limitations of existing benchmarks, which do not provide its integrated assessment of factual retrieval, reasoning, and synthesis. It then evaluates systems under complex, multifaceted end-to-end conditions and is intended to guide next-generation RAG system development. It is planned for open-source public use. Its stated limitations are possible pretraining-data contamination and limited coverage of real-world queries and scenarios; future iterations should consider newer or synthetic data and methods to quantify and account for contamination. Users are encouraged to pursue efficient, accessible implementations and transparent methodologies.

FRAMES in Fact, Fetch, and Reason: A Unified Evaluation of Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Satyapriya Krishna, Kalpesh Krishna, Anhad Mohananey, Steven Schwarcz, Adam Stambler, Shyam Upadhyay, Manaal Faruqui · 2025

FRAMES, or Factuality, Retrieval, And reasoning MEasurement Set, is a high-quality dataset, benchmark, evaluation set, and unified evaluation framework for end-to-end Retrieval-Augmented Generation. It contains 824 human-annotated test questions built from Wikipedia, each requiring information from 2–15 articles. Its central purpose is to assess factuality, retrieval, reasoning, and accurate synthesis together rather than separately, using realistic multi-document queries that reveal capabilities and limitations of Large Language Models and RAG systems. The questions are challenging multi-hop or multi-step tasks that require retrieval and integration across sources, coherent final responses, and temporal disambiguation. Reasoning types include multiple constraints, numerical reasoning, tabular reasoning, temporal reasoning, and post-processing; multiple constraints are the largest category at approximately 36% of samples, followed by numerical reasoning at approximately 20%. FRAMES begins by positioning itself as a response to limitations of existing benchmarks, which do not provide its integrated assessment of factual retrieval, reasoning, and synthesis. It then evaluates systems under complex, multifaceted end-to-end conditions and is intended to guide next-generation RAG system development. It is planned for open-source public use. Its stated limitations are possible pretraining-data contamination and limited coverage of real-world queries and scenarios; future iterations should consider newer or synthetic data and methods to quantify and account for contamination. Users are encouraged to pursue efficient, accessible implementations and transparent methodologies.

Abstract

FRAMES, or Factuality, Retrieval, And reasoning MEasurement Set, is a high-quality dataset for testing factual responses, retrieval capabilities, and reasoning in final-answer generation. It contains challenging multi-hop questions and is designed to evaluate end-to-end retrieval-augmented generation.

1

FRAMES, or Factuality, Retrieval, And reasoning MEasurement Set, is introduced as a dataset and evaluation framework with 824 test samples. It evaluates fact retrieval, reasoning across multiple constraints, and accurate synthesis of information in an integrated, end-to-end setting.

2

FRAMES is an evaluation set of 824 questions designed to provide an end-to-end assessment of Retrieval Augmented Generation systems. It evaluates factuality, retrieval, and reasoning, including multi-hop or multi-step questions and temporal disambiguation.

3 .. 3.2

FRAMES is the evaluation dataset used to assess model performance on questions requiring factuality, retrieval, and reasoning. Its questions include multiple-constraint, numerical, post-processing, tabular, and temporal reasoning types.

4

FRAMES addresses limitations of existing benchmarks through a unified evaluation framework. It evaluates factual retrieval, reasoning, and synthesis, including complex multi-hop queries, information integration from various sources, temporal disambiguation, and the production of coherent responses.

5

FRAMES is a comprehensive evaluation dataset designed to test Retrieval-Augmented Generation systems across factuality, retrieval accuracy, and reasoning. It provides an integrated framework using realistic, multi-document queries to reveal the capabilities and limitations of LLMs.

Limitations

FRAMES provides a comprehensive evaluation framework for RAG systems, but its effectiveness may be reduced by pretraining data contamination and limited coverage of real-world queries and scenarios. Future iterations should explore newer or synthetic data and methods to quantify and account for contamination.

Ethical Considerations

FRAMES was developed with recognition of the importance of addressing ethical considerations in retrieval-augmented generation research. Researchers using it are encouraged to explore efficient, accessible implementations and maintain transparency in their methodologies.
