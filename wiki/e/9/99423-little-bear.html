Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the Little Bear is identified with the constellation formed when Jupiter places Arcas among the stars. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Little Bear is one of two constellations formed when Jupiter places Callisto and Arcas in the heavens.

Little Bear in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Little Bear first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Little Bear is one of two constellations formed when Jupiter places Callisto and Arcas in the heavens. It circles the heavens without sinking beneath the ocean at Juno’s request; its tail ends in the Pole star, the Cynosure.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter III

The Little Bear is one of the two constellations formed when Jupiter places Callisto and Arcas in the heavens. It moves around the heavens but never sinks beneath the ocean because of Juno's request to Tethys and Oceanus. The last star in its tail is the Pole star, also called the Cynosure.
