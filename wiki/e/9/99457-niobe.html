Niobe

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Niobe is the proud queen of Thebes who insults Latona, loses her family to Apollo and Diana, and becomes a weeping stone on Mount Sipylus. In The Library, Niobe is the daughter of Tantalus, wife of Amphion, and mother of seven sons and seven daughters who becomes a weeping stone at Sipylus after claiming superiority over Latona. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Niobe is the daughter of Tantalus who becomes stone and weeps on Mount Sipylus during summer. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Niobe is the proud queen of Thebes whose fourteen children are slain after she boasts over Latona, and who becomes a weeping stone.

Niobe in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Niobe first appears

Abstract written by a model

Niobe is a daughter of Tantalus, sister of Pelops, wife of Amphion, and queen of Thebes, also described as descended from Atlas. She possesses immense wealth and takes particular pride in her lineage, beauty, and children, whose number is variously given as twelve, fourteen, or twenty. During worship of Latona, Niobe claims superiority over her and contemptuously orders Theban women to abandon Latona’s sacrifice. Apollo and Diana then slay all her sons and daughters, and Amphion dies. Bereft of her family, Niobe retires to Mount Sipylus in Lydia, is borne to a mountain by a whirlwind, and hardens into a statue, rock, or stone that continues to shed tears.

Niobe in The Library

Apollodorus · about AD 70 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Niobe first appears

Abstract written by a model

Niobe is described as a daughter of Tantalus, and elsewhere of Phoroneus and Teledice; she bears Argus, and according to Acusilaus Pelasgus, by Zeus, and marries Amphion. With seven sons and seven daughters in one account, she claims superiority over Latona, provoking Artemis and Apollo, prays to Zeus, and becomes a weeping stone at Sipylus.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK ITI

Niobe was the daughter of Phoroneus and the nymph Teledice. She had Argos by Zeus and, according to Acusilaus, also had Pelasgus by him.

Γαμεῖ δὲ Ζῆθος μὲν Θήβην, ad ἧς ἡ πόλιες

Niobe, daughter of Tantalus, married Amphion and bore seven sons and seven daughters in this account, though other writers gave different numbers. After claiming to be more blessed with children than Latona, she provoked Artemis and Apollo, and later became a stone at Sipylus whose tears flowed day and night.

Niobe in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Niobe first appears

Abstract written by a model

Niobe, daughter of Tantalus, is queen of Thebes through Amphion and inherits Phrygia. Proud of her fourteen children, she boasts over Latona; Apollo and Diana kill them, Amphion dies, and Niobe becomes a weeping stone, later represented by a celebrated statue.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter IX

Niobe is the queen of Thebes and boasts that her fourteen children make her more fortunate and secure than Latona, who has only two children. After Apollo and Diana kill all her children, and her husband Amphion dies, Niobe is changed into stone, though tears continue to flow from her.
