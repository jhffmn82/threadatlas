Æsacus

person · 1 work · 20 quoted facts · 10 connections
Also called: The Trojan hero, The lover, the brother of Hector, the son of a king

Abstract written by a model

Æsacus is a Trojan hero, son of Priam and Alexirhoë, brother of Hector, and member of the Trojan royal lineage. Secretly born under Mount Ida, he avoids Ilium’s crowds for lonely mountains and fields, and foretells the Trojan War from Hecuba’s pregnancy. After seeing Hesperia by the Cebrenus, he loves and pursues her until her flight leads to her death. Despairing, he throws himself into the sea and becomes a didapper, whose love of the sea and repeated dives continue his attempts to die.

Æsacus is in one work on this site, The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

FABLE VIII. [XI.749-795]

Æsacus is presented as a son of Priam, brother of Hector, and descendant of Ilus, Assaracus, Ganymede, Laomedon, and Priam. He avoids cities and courts, falls in love with Hesperia, and pursues her until her flight leads to her death. In despair, he throws himself into the sea, is transformed into a didapper, and repeatedly dives in attempts to die.
