Dryope in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Dryope is an exceptionally beautiful Œchalian maid, daughter of Eurytus, sister of Iole, wife of Andræmon, and mother. While offering garlands to the Nymphs with her infant son, she plucks lotus flowers, becomes a tree, and entrusts her son and tree to her family.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE IV. [IX.324-425]

Dryope, the beautiful daughter of Eurytus and sister of Iole, went to offer garlands to the Nymphs while carrying her infant son. After plucking flowers from the lotus, she became rooted and was gradually enclosed in bark, leaves, and branches. Before her mouth was covered, she asked her family to protect her tree and care for her son.

Dryope in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Dryope, wife of Andraemon, sister of Iole, and mother of a young child, picks flowers from transformed Lotis, becomes rooted and changes into a lotus tree, pleading for her child’s care and the tree’s protection.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter V

Dryope, the wife of Andraemon and mother of a young child, picks flowers from the transformed nymph Lotis. As punishment, she becomes rooted to the ground and gradually changes into a lotus tree while pleading that her child be cared for and that the tree be protected.
