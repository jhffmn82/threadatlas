Anthedon

place · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Bœotian city Anthedon

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Anthedon is Glaucus’s native city near Eubœa across Euripus, where he was transformed, worshipped as a sea God, and remembered through Glaucus’ Leap. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Anthedon is a Boeotian city on the sea at the foot of Messapium, named after Nymph Anthedon according to one account, and containing sacred sites, tombs, and the leap of Glaucus.

CHAPTER XXII.

Anthedon is a Boeotian city on the sea at the foot of Mount Messapium. It has a grove and temple of the Cabiri, temples of Demeter and Proserpine and of Dionysus, the tombs of Otus and Ephialtes, and the place called the leap of Glaucus.
