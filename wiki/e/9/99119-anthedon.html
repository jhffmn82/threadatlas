Anthedon in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Anthedon, near Eubœa across Euripus, is Glaucus’s native city, where he was transformed, worshipped as a sea God, and remembered through Glaucus’ Leap.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE VIII. [XIII.898-968]

Anthedon is near Euboea, and Glaucus is said to have been transformed there. The explanation identifies Anthedon as Glaucus's native city and says that it worshipped him as a sea God.

Anthedon in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Anthedon is a Boeotian city on the sea at the foot of Messapium, named after Nymph Anthedon according to one account; it contains sacred sites, tombs, and the leap of Glaucus.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXII.

Anthedon is a Boeotian city on the sea at the foot of Mount Messapium. It has a grove and temple of the Cabiri, temples of Demeter and Proserpine and of Dionysus, the tombs of Otus and Ephialtes, and the place called the leap of Glaucus.
