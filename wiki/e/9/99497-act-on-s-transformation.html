Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Actæon’s transformation is Diana’s punishment after he sees her bathing, changing him into a fearful, spotted stag with horns and altered limbs while he retains his understanding but cannot speak as a man. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Actaeon's transformation is Diana’s changing of Actaeon into a stag, with antlers, altered limbs, and a spotted hide; he retains his consciousness but loses the ability to speak.

Actæon’s transformation in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Actæon’s transformation first appears

Abstract written by a model

Actæon’s transformation is a punishment in the Actæon fable, caused by Diana after he sees her bathing. Diana’s avenging water changes him into a fearful, spotted stag with horns and altered limbs. Though his understanding remains, he cannot speak as a man; notes also give nonliteral alternatives involving his dogs or ruined hunting establishment.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Diana's avenging water gives Actæon the horns, neck, ears, feet, legs, and spotted coat of a lively stag, and fear is added to his condition. His former understanding remains, but he can no longer speak as a man.

notes .. notes

Actæon’s transformation is treated as a punishment inflicted by Diana after he saw her bathing. The explanation notes alternative interpretations in which Actæon was not literally transformed, but was said to have been devoured by his dogs after they became mad or after his hunting establishment ruined him.
