event · 2 works · 0 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Phaëton’s conflagration, conflagration, conflagration of the earth, excessive heat, flames, such a fate, the devouring flame, the world on fire
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Phaëton’s conflagration ravages countries with flames caused by Phaëton’s fall and intensifies the fires of Ætna.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the conflagration caused by Phaeton burns Earth, scorching the heavens and earth, destroying cities and nations, drying rivers and seas, and forcing aquatic inhabitants into deeper waters.

burns Earth [across mountains, cities, forests, and fields] The conflagration caused by Phaeton scorched the heavens and earth, burned mountains and forests, destroyed cities and nations, dried rivers and seas, and cracked the earth open. It also darkened the air and forced the inhabitants of the waters to seek refuge in deeper places.
