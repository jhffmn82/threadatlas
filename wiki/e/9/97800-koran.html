Abstract written by a model

The Koran is a spurious, purportedly posthumous work attributed to Laurence Sterne, though probably written by Richard Griffith. English critics considered it manifestly spurious, while Germany gave it greater recognition: it was translated in 1771 and 1778, reprinted in Vienna by Sammer in 1795, and accepted or praised by reviewers despite doubts. Not clearly sundered from the original in Germany, it contains Graf von Gleichen and supplied twenty sayings and reflections for the appendix to Wilhelm Meister’s Journeyman Years.

Naturally Sterne’s letters found readers in Germany, the Yorick-Eliza correspond

The Koran was a purportedly posthumous work that English critics considered manifestly spurious, but it received considerably greater recognition in Germany. German translations appeared in 1771 and 1778, and several German reviewers accepted or warmly praised it despite acknowledging, in some cases, doubts about its authorship.

[37] Herder’s early acquaintance with Sterne has been already treated.

The Koran was identified as the source of twenty sayings and reflections printed in the appendix to Wilhelm Meister’s Journeyman Years. It was also considered a possible source for the story of the Graf von Gleichen, which may have contributed to Johann Wolfgang Goethe’s Stella.
