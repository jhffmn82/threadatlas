river Marsyas in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

River Marsyas is the clearest river in Phrygia, near Celenæ, running within steep banks toward the rapid sea. It begins when tears for Marsyas enter the earth’s lowest veins and emerge as a stream. After Marsyas’s defeat and death, it bears his name; its noisy fall and later smooth course are linked to Apollo’s vengeance.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

The tears shed for Marsyas moisten the earth, which receives them into its lowest veins and turns them into a stream. The stream emerges in Phrygia and bears the name of Marsyas.

notes .. notes

The river Marsyas was said by Livy and Quintus Curtius to have inspired the story of Apollo and Marsyas. Its fall from a precipice made a loud and unpleasant noise, while the later smoothness of its course was explained as the result of Apollo's vengeance. Suidas says that Marsyas threw himself into it after his defeat and that it then bore his name.
