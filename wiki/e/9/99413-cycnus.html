Cycnus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Cycnus is a king of Liguria, son of Sthenelus, ruler of the Ligurians and their cities, and a friend and maternal-side relative of Phaëton. After leaving his kingdom to mourn Phaëton beside the Eridanus and the wood augmented by the sisters, he may die of grief and is changed into a swan. As a swan, he avoids the heavens and air, abhors fire, and frequents pools, lakes, and streams.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Cycnus, king of the Ligurians and related to Phaëton, leaves his kingdom to mourn Phaëton beside the Eridanus and in the wood augmented by the sisters. His human voice becomes shrill, gray feathers cover his hair, and he is transformed into a new bird.

notes .. notes

Cycnus may have died from grief at Phaëton’s loss. The poets were said to have honored his attachment by describing his transformation into a swan.
