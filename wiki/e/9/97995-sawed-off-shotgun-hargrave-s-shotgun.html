sawed-off shotgun (Hargrave's shotgun) in The Valley of Fear

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1914-09/1915-05

Hargrave’s sawed-off shotgun is a weapon and an American shotgun made by the Pennsylvania Small Arms Company, identified from the surviving letters “P-E-N.” Its barrel was cut about a foot before the triggers, leaving it under two feet long and concealable in an overcoat or valise. Loaded with buckshot, its triggers were wired together so that pulling the rear trigger discharged both barrels at once. After being fired at close range, it lay across John Douglas’s chest. The discharge caused Mr. Douglas’s death; MacDonald believes Hargrave deliberately brought and used it in the murder.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter III

The sawed-off shotgun lay across John Douglas’s chest after being fired at close range. Its barrel had been cut off about a foot in front of the triggers, and the triggers had been wired together so that the whole charge would discharge simultaneously.

Chapter IV

The shotgun is loaded with buckshot cartridges and has its triggers wired together so that pulling the rear trigger fires both barrels. It is less than two feet long, bears the partial name P-E-N, and is identified by Holmes as an American shotgun made by the Pennsylvania Small Arms Company.

It was nightfall before Inspector MacDonald and White Mason came back

The sawed-off shotgun was less than two feet long and could have been carried inside Hargrave's overcoat or concealed in his valise. MacDonald believes Hargrave brought it deliberately and used it to shoot Mr. Douglas.
