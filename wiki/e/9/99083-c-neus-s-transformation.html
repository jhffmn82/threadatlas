Cæneus's transformation

event · 1 work · 1 quoted fact · 2 connections
Also called: Cænis’s transformation, changed, changed to the opposite sex, the false resemblance to a man, transformed, turned into the other sex

Abstract written by a model

Cæneus’s transformation is Cænis’s change from woman to man. After asking Neptune to prevent another similar assault, Cænis is granted the request; the voice becomes hoarser and seemingly male. Later, Cæneus appears as an unwoundable male warrior, though Latreus calls his male resemblance false.

Cæneus's transformation is in one work on this site, The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

FABLES III. AND IV. [XII.146-535]

Cænis asks Neptune to make her no longer a woman so that she cannot suffer a similar assault again. Neptune grants the request, and her voice becomes hoarser and seemingly male as she becomes Cæneus.

Latreus refers to Cæneus as having been born a woman and having received a reward that gave him the false resemblance of a man. The unit does not otherwise explain the transformation, but it presents Cæneus as a male warrior whose body cannot be wounded by the Centaurs’ weapons.
