Pierus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Pierus is a very bad poet whose injurious, idle, tiresome, and unmeaning narratives include a work on the war of the Gods with the Giants; he is father of the Pierides, whose transformation into magpies represents his narratives.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

notes .. notes

Pierus is described as a very bad poet whose works contained stories injurious to the credit of the Gods. The explanation suggests that the transformation of his daughters into magpies represented the idle, tiresome, and unmeaning character of his narratives.
