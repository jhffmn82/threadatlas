Phaeton's death

event · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Phaeton untimely dead, Phaëton's death, death of Phaëton, deprived him both of his life, his death, his fate, his son, the death of Phaëton

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Phaëton's death occurs when Jupiter's lightning strikes him and his chariot, prompted by the Sun's grievance; his smoking body is entombed by the Hesperian Naiads, while Cycnus mourns beside the Eridanus and his father abandons labor and blames the steeds. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Phaeton's death occurs when Jupiter strikes him with a lightning-bolt to stop the runaway chariot's destruction; he falls burning into Eridanus, is cooled, and receives a tomb.

Phaeton's death occurred when Jupiter struck him with a lightning-bolt to end the destruction caused by the runaway chariot. Phaeton fell burning into Eridanus, where the river cooled his body, and the Italian Naiads erected a tomb for him.
