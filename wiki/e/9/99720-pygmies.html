Pygmies in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Pygmies are a supposedly diminutive people described by ancient writers, variously placed in Ethiopia, India, Scythia, and parts of Africa, and traditionally said to wage continual warfare with Cranes; later explanations seek a historical or natural basis for this story.

notes .. notes

The Pygmies are described as a supposedly diminutive people whom ancient writers placed in various regions, including Ethiopia, India, Scythia, and parts of Africa. They were traditionally said to be in continual warfare with the Cranes, and several later explanations attempt to identify the historical or natural basis of the story.

Pygmies in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Pygmies are a nation of dwarfs about a cubit tall, said to live near the Nile, defending cornfields from cranes and once attacking sleeping Hercules.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter X

The Pygmies were a nation of dwarfs whose height was said to be about a cubit. They defended their cornfields against migrating cranes, and an army of them once tried to attack the sleeping Hercules.
