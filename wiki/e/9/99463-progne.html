Progne in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

front matter

Progne is the daughter of Pandion and the wife of Tereus. After Tereus’s crimes against Philomela, Progne becomes a swallow.

opening

Progne asks Tereus to let her see Philomela and persuades him to bring her sister from Athens. Believing Tereus's false account of Philomela's death, she mourns her with black garments and an honorary sepulchre, until Philomela's woven message reveals the truth and moves her toward revenge.

opening

Progne delivers Philomela from captivity by disguising herself in the rites of Bacchus and bringing her to the royal house. She plans revenge against Tereus, helps murder Itys, and serves his remains to Tereus before fleeing and becoming a swallow.

notes .. notes

Progne is described as being transformed into a swallow, whose presence around human dwellings symbolizes her restless search for the son whom she has murdered in a frantic fit. Anacreon and Apollodorus reverse this account and say that Progne became a nightingale.
