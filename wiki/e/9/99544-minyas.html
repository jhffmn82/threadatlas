Ovid · 0008~

Minyas is a king of the Bœotians, associated with their name Minyæ, and has daughters. Accounts conflict over whether Orchomenus was his father or his son.

Minyas in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Minyas, king of Orchomenus and son of Chryses, gained great wealth through rule; his revenues surpassed earlier rulers’, and he built the first known Treasury for his money. He later had a tomb at Orchomenus, and Orchomenians were called Minyae.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXVI.

Minyas was the son of Chryses and succeeded to great wealth through his rule. His revenues surpassed those of his predecessors, and he was the first known ruler to build a Treasury for his money.

CHAPTER XXXVIII.

Minyas has a tomb at Orchomenus. The Orchomenians are called Minyae in the inscription concerning Hesiod’s remains.
