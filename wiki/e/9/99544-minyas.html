Minyas

person · 2 works · 6 quoted facts · 4 connections

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Minyas is a king of the Bœotians, associated with their name Minyæ, and has daughters; accounts conflict over whether Orchomenus was his father or his son. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Minyas is king of Orchomenus and son of Chryses, whose great wealth surpassed that of earlier rulers; he built the first known Treasury, later had a tomb at Orchomenus, and gave the Orchomenians the name Minyae.

Minyas in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Minyas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Minyas, king of Orchomenus and son of Chryses, gained great wealth through rule; his revenues surpassed earlier rulers’, and he built the first known Treasury for his money. He later had a tomb at Orchomenus, and Orchomenians were called Minyae.

CHAPTER XXXVI.

Minyas was the son of Chryses and succeeded to great wealth through his rule. His revenues surpassed those of his predecessors, and he was the first known ruler to build a Treasury for his money.

CHAPTER XXXVIII.

Minyas has a tomb at Orchomenus. The Orchomenians are called Minyae in the inscription concerning Hesiod’s remains.
