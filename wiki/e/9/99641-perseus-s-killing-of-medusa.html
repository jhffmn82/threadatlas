Perseus's killing of Medusa in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Perseus’s killing of Medusa is Medusa’s death, later explained as her defeat by Perseus. He and companions may surprise Medusa and her escort at night, or he acts after seizing her sisters’ minister and golden statue. Using Minerva’s reflecting shield to avoid looking directly at her, Perseus takes Medusa by the hair and cuts off her head. He carries it as spoil; its blood produces serpents in Libya, and its face petrifies Polydectes, proving the death was real.

Perseus's killing of Medusa enables him to carry the Gorgon’s head as a spoil. Blood from the head falls onto the Libyan sands, where the ground quickens it into various serpents.

notes .. notes

In the myth, Perseus killed Medusa and took her head. In Pausanias’s account, Perseus and companions surprised Medusa and her escort by night; in another interpretation, he killed Medusa after seizing the common minister of her sisters and obtaining their golden statue.

notes .. notes

The slaying of Medusa involves Perseus using a reflecting shield from Minerva, taking Medusa by the hair, and cutting off her head while avoiding direct sight of her. The explanation suggests that Pegasus and Chrysaor may have been ships associated with the event rather than literal winged horses.

opening

Polydectes calls the death of Medusa a fiction, but Perseus demonstrates its reality by using Medusa’s face to petrify him.
