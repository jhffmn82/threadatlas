the missing dumb-bell in The Valley of Fear

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1914-09/1915-05

The missing dumb-bell, one of a pair, becomes central after its mate is found under a side table and no one knows whether there was another. Sherlock Holmes says the case hangs on it. Its absence suggests something sunk in nearby water; it is found in the moat attached to a sodden bundle, used to weight it, and removed.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter IV

One dumb-bell is found under the side table in the room. Holmes asks where the other is, but Ames does not know whether there had ever been a pair.

Chapter VI

Sherlock Holmes says that the case hangs upon the missing dumb-bell. He jokes about the danger of an athlete exercising with only one dumb-bell, while indicating that the other investigators have not grasped its importance.

Chapter VII

The missing dumb-bell leads Sherlock Holmes to suspect that something has been sunk in the nearby water. He finds it attached to the sodden bundle and later removes it from the bundle.
