Göschen wrote Reise von Johann, a late sentimental journey in which a master and servant travel through southern Germany. Its whimsical, buoyant, episodic narrative includes sentimental scenes, found papers, description, information, and suggestiveness. Though compared with Yorick and La Fleur and misclassified as an imitation of von Thümmel, it was discussed by Schiller and Goethe for the Xenien but not used.

Göschen wrote Reise von Johann, in which a master and servant travel through southern Germany in a relationship compared with Yorick and La Fleur or Captain Shandy and Corporal Trim. The book uses forced buoyancy, intentional inconsequence, narrative whimsy, sentimental episodes, accidental found papers, and occasional suggestiveness, but is burdened by German tendencies toward description and information. Ebeling incorrectly classed it as an imitation of von Thümmel; Schiller and Goethe discussed it in connection with the Xenien, but the proposed use of it was never carried out.
