Jupiter's abduction of Europa

event · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: abduction of Europa, adventure, borne off, carries his prize over the expanse of the midst of the ocean, carrying her off by force, carrying off Europa, conveyed her through the sea to Crete, present Fable

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, it is described as Jupiter Asterius's forced voyage carrying Europa away after she sits on a bull's back and is carried across the ocean toward Crete. In The Library, it is described as Zeus, disguised as a tame bull, carrying Europa over the sea to Crete, after which her brothers and others search for her unsuccessfully.

Jupiter's abduction of Europa in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Jupiter's abduction of Europa first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jupiter's abduction of Europa is an abduction, explained as Jupiter Asterius's forced voyage carrying Europa away. It begins when Europa unknowingly sits on a bull's back; he enters the water, carries her from shore across the ocean toward Crete, and may reflect a bull-figurehead ship or related names and words.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Jupiter's abduction begins when Europa sits on the back of the bull without knowing who he is. He gradually enters the water, carries her away from the shore, and takes her across the ocean toward Crete.

notes .. notes

The abduction of Europa is explained as a voyage by Jupiter Asterius to carry her away by force. The story may have developed from a ship with a bull-shaped figurehead, a general named Taurus, or the Phoenician word Alpha or Ilpha, which could mean either ship or bull.
