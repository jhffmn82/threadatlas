Christian Friedrich Timme in Laurence Sterne in Germany

Harvey Waterman Thayer · 1905

Christian Friedrich Timme was a novelist and reviewer for the Erfurtische gelehrte Zeitung. He wrote Der Empfindsame as an extensive burlesque satire of Empfindsamkeit, attacking the followers of Werther, Siegwart, and Sterne and exposing the gap between claims of universal sympathy and failures to aid nearby people or animals. Timme distinguished Sterne’s sentimental influence from his whimsicality: he criticized the Sentimental Journey translation and German imitators while admiring Sterne as gentle, compassionate, and whimsically inventive. The novel reached a second edition, but contemporary reviews often ignored, misunderstood, or condemned it.

Christian Friedrich Timme wrote Der Empfindsame as an extensive satire of sentimental excess, targeting the followers of Werther, Siegwart, and Sterne. He insisted that his criticism was directed at Yorick’s mistaken imitators rather than at Sterne himself, whom he portrayed as genuinely gentle and compassionate.

Timme satirizes sentimentalism through the actions of Frau Kurt and Pankraz, repeatedly contrasting declarations of universal sympathy with failures to help people or animals immediately in need. He adapts Sterne’s whimsical narrative methods with apparent admiration, while directing ridicule and burlesque chiefly against sentimentalism. His novel was popular enough to require a second edition, though most contemporary reviews either ignored it, misunderstood it, or condemned its attacks on popular German writers.
