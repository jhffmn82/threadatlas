HopRAG in HopRAG: Multi-Hop Reasoning for Logic-Aware Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Hao Liu, Zhengren Wang, Xi Chen, Zhiyu Li, Feiyu Xiong, Qinhan Yu, Wentao Zhang · 2025 · the work’s page · read it where HopRAG first appears

Abstract written by a model

HopRAG is a novel, logic-aware, graph-structured retrieval-augmented generation framework and system that augments retrieval with logical reasoning. During indexing, it builds a graph-structured knowledge index: passages are vertices, and directed edges represent similarity and logical relations. It generates pseudo-queries through query simulation and merges edges to connect related passages, supporting cross-document organization, flexible logical modeling, and efficient construction and updating. During retrieval, it first uses similarity matching, then reasoning-augmented graph traversal to expand into multi-hop neighborhoods, including indirectly relevant passages that may lead to supporting facts. Its retrieve-reason-prune mechanism combines textual similarity and logical relations, retrieves information within limited context length, and applies Helpfulness-based pruning to retain useful passages. This is intended to improve retrieval precision and recall and final answer quality, while providing a lightweight, downstream-task-friendly alternative to existing structured RAG methods. HopRAG was evaluated on validation-set samples from HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and MuSiQue with 20 retrieved passages and nhop=4. It generally achieved the best answer performance; its GPT-4o-mini traversal outperformed its non-LLM variant and was comparable to or better than other structured RAG baselines. It outperformed conventional RAG systems and conventional information retrieval, reporting answer accuracy over 36.25% higher and retrieval F1 score 20.97% improved than conventional information retrieval approaches. Its evaluation is mainly on multi-hop or multi-document QA, and broader-domain generalization plus improved query simulation and edge merging remain future work.

Abstract

HopRAG is a RAG framework that augments retrieval with logical reasoning through graph-structured knowledge exploration. It constructs a passage graph during indexing and uses a retrieve-reason-prune mechanism during retrieval.

1 / Introduction .. Contributions

HopRAG is introduced as a graph-structured RAG system that treats passages as vertices and logical relations as directed edges. It connects passages with pseudo-queries and uses reasoning-augmented graph traversal to retrieve, reason over, and prune passages, including indirectly relevant passages that can lead to supporting facts.

2 / Related Work .. Tree&Graph-structured RAG

It is presented as a more lightweight and downstream-task-friendly alternative to existing tree- and graph-structured RAG methods. It supports flexible logical modeling, cross-document organization, and efficient construction and updating.

3 / Method .. Algorithm

HopRAG is a logic-aware RAG system that stores passages and their similarity and logical relations in a graph. It retrieves passages through similarity matching, graph traversal, and Helpfulness-based pruning.

4 / Experiments / 4.1 / Experimental Setups / Datasets .. Ablation on Traversal Model

HopRAG is evaluated on HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and MuSiQue with 20 retrieved passages and nhop=4. It generally achieves the best answer performance, and its graph traversal integrates textual similarity with logical relations; its GPT-4o-mini version outperforms its non-LLM traversal variant and performs comparably to or better than the other structured RAG baselines.

5 / Conclusion

HopRAG is introduced as a RAG system with a logic-aware retrieval mechanism that connects related passages through pseudo-queries. It outperformed conventional RAG systems and state-of-the-art baselines on multi-hop QA benchmarks, while its ablation studies examined hyperparameter and model sensitivity.

Limitations

HopRAG is currently evaluated mainly on multi-hop or multi-document QA tasks. Its generalization capabilities across a broader range of domains, as well as more sophisticated query simulation and edge merging strategies, remain areas for future work.
