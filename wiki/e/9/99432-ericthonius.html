Ericthonius in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Minerva aids his birth, encloses him in a basket, and gives him to three virgins; another account says he was exposed and found in a temple of Minerva and Vulcan. Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Ericthonius is described as an offspring born without a mother. Minerva encloses him in a basket and gives it to three virgins to keep.

notes .. notes

Ericthonius was fabled to be the son or foster-child of Athene, and traditions variously describe him as the son of Vulcan and the Earth or as born in Attica. He disputed the crown of Athens with Amphictyon, eventually succeeded him, and was said to have invented chariots to conceal his deformed legs. He was also credited with instituting the Panathenæa, and Hyginus says that after his death he became the constellation Auriga, the Charioteer.
