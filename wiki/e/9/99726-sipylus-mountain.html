Sipylus mountain in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Sipylus is a mountain in Lydia and Niobe’s native mountain, where she lived before marriage. It is chiefly known for a distant-visible rock resembling a grief-stricken woman: Niobe is carried there by a whirlwind, fixed on its summit, and changed into marble or stone.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Sipylus is named as a place where Niobe lived before her marriage, when she inhabited Mæonia and Sipylus. The text later says that Niobe is carried to her native land and fixed on the top of a mountain, where she becomes marble.

notes .. notes

Mount Sipylus in Lydia was said to contain a rock resembling a woman overwhelmed with grief. According to the myth, Niobe was borne there by a whirlwind and changed into that stone.
