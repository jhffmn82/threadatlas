Orchamus

person · 1 work · 6 quoted facts · 2 connections

Abstract written by a model

Orchamus rules the Achæmenian cities as a king of Persia, seventh in descent from Belus and in the stated line from Achæmenes. Identified as Leucothoë’s father, he buries her alive and may have introduced the frankincense tree into his kingdom; ancient writers otherwise mention him only here.

Orchamus is in one work on this site, The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Orchamus is identified as Leucothoë's father and as the king of Persia who buried her alive in the story. The explanation suggests that he may represent the ruler who first introduced the frankincense tree into Persia; he is otherwise mentioned by no ancient writers except here.
