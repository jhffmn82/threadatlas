Latona's lake

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, it is a Lycia lake whose clear water, ash-blackened altar, reeds, and countrymen's work are described; after the countrymen muddy it and are transformed, it becomes the frogs' pool. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, it is a clear pond where country people gather willows and osiers; after they stir its mud, it becomes inhabited by them as frogs.

opening

The lake lies in a valley in Lycia and contains clear water, an altar blackened by sacrificial ashes, reeds, and the countrymen's work. The countrymen muddy it by jumping about and stirring up the soft mud, and after their transformation it becomes the pool in which the frogs live.
