Latona's lake

place · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: a lake, a pond, that pool, the lake, the pond of Lycia, the pool, the pool and the lake, the slimy pool, the very pond and marsh, this pond of clear water
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, it is a Lycia lake whose clear water, ash-blackened altar, reeds, and countrymen's work are described; after the countrymen muddy it and are transformed, it becomes the frogs' pool.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, it is a clear pond where country people gather willows and osiers; after they stir its mud, it becomes inhabited by them as frogs.

Latona's lake in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

A Lycia lake in a valley has clear water, an ash-blackened altar, reeds, and countrymen's work; countrymen muddy it by jumping and stirring soft mud, and after their transformation it becomes the frogs' pool.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

opening

The lake lies in a valley in Lycia and contains clear water, an altar blackened by sacrificial ashes, reeds, and the countrymen's work. The countrymen muddy it by jumping about and stirring up the soft mud, and after their transformation it becomes the pool in which the frogs live.

LATONA AND THE RUSTICS

The pond of Lycia was a clear body of water where country people gathered willows and osiers. After the rustics stirred up its mud, it became the place where they lived as frogs.
