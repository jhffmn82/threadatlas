Ladon

river · 2 works · 21 quoted facts · 10 connections
Also called: River Ladon, river Ladon, sandy Ladon, the Ladon, the river Ladon

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the river Ladon is described as the sandy river that stops Syrinx's flight from Pan and flows into the Alpheus. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Ladon is described as a famed Arcadian river that flows through several settlements and sanctuaries before joining the Alpheus.

Ladon in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Ladon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ladon is a river, said to surpass all rivers in Greece, and to be second to no Greek or barbarian river, for the beauty of its stream. It is famous in poets’ songs about Daphne, though it is not wide enough to form navigable islands. Its springs are near Clitor, 60 stades from the town by a narrow road along the Aroanius. It flows through Lucasium, Mesoboa, Nasi, Oryx, Halus, and Thaliades; it lies fifty stades beyond Nasi. Reaching Thelpusa, it passes the temple of Eleusinian Demeter, then the temple of Demeter at Onceum and other sanctuaries, before joining the Alpheus at the Island of Crows.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XX.

The Ladon is said to surpass all the rivers in Greece for the beauty of its stream. It is also famous in connection with what poets have sung about Daphne.

CHAPTER XXI.

The springs of Ladon are 60 stades from the town of Clitor by a narrow road along the Aroanius.

CHAPTER XXIII.

The river Ladon lies fifty stades beyond Nasi. After crossing it, the traveler reaches the oak-coppice Soron.

CHAPTER XXV.

Ladon rises near Clitor and flows through several places before reaching Thelpusa, where it passes the temple of Eleusinian Demeter. It then flows past the temple of Demeter at Onceum and other sanctuaries before joining the Alpheus at the Island of Crows. Though renowned for its beauty, it is not wide enough to form navigable islands.
