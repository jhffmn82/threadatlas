Python in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Python is an enormous, unheard-of serpent produced by the Earth, occupying much of a mountain and terrifying the new race of men. Apollo slays it with a thousand arrows. Philosophically it represents reptiles born from flood slime and destroyed by the sun’s heat; historically, it may represent a robber near Parnassus.

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Python is an enormous serpent produced by the Earth, so vast that it occupies a great part of a mountain. It terrifies the new race of men, but Apollo kills it with a thousand arrows.

notes .. notes

The serpent Python is explained philosophically as representing reptiles produced from slime left by the receding flood and then destroyed by the sun’s heat. Historically, it may have represented a robber who troubled people traveling near Parnassus to offer sacrifices.

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855
