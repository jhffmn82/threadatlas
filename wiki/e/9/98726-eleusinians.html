Eleusinians

group · 3 works · 2 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: people of Eleusis
  • In The Library, the Eleusinians were at war with the Athenians, calling on Eumolpus and his Thracian force to fight on their side.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the Eleusinians fought against Erectheus, and their conflict ended with Eumolpus and Erectheus killed in battle.
  • In Description of Greece, the Eleusinians retained private management of the Eleusinian Mysteries under the peace settlement.

Eleusinians in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

have_private_management_of Eleusinian Mysteries [under the peace settlement] The Eleusinians fought the Athenians, and peace left them Athenian in other respects while allowing them private management of the Mysteries. Their women were said to have first danced and sung to Demeter at the Well of Callichorus, and their territory contained temples, tombs, and other sacred sites.

CHAPTER XXXVIII.

The Eleusinians fought the Athenians, and peace left them Athenian in other respects while allowing them private management of the Mysteries. Their women were said to have first danced and sung to Demeter at the Well of Callichorus, and their territory contained temples, tombs, and other sacred sites.
