Abstract written by a model

Johann Christian Bock was a theater-poet with the Ackerman Company in Hamburg in 1772 and an author active around 1770. He published Die Tagereise in 1770, later reissued as Die Geschichte eines empfundenen Tages, and anonymously wrote several Empfindsame Reise volumes for holiday visits. His works adapted sentimental encounters associated with Sterne, directly and through Jacobi. Critics judged Die Tagereise tiresome and unsuccessful, though it included German patriotism, praise of Gellert, and moral and religious observations.

Johann Christian Bock published Die Tagereise in 1770, later reissued as Die Geschichte eines empfundenen Tages, and wrote several anonymous sentimental travel volumes in the early 1770s. His work drew directly and indirectly through Jacobi on Sterne, adapting episodes involving a soldier-beggar, a peasant girl, a dead dog, a lady’s companion, and other sentimental encounters. Critics called Die Tagereise an unsuccessful and tiresome imitation, though it also contained original German patriotism, praise of Gellert, and moral and religious observations.
