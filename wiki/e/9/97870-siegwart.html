Siegwart in Laurence Sterne in Germany

Harvey Waterman Thayer · 1905

Siegwart, published in 1776, is a popular sentimental novel that renewed and redirected sentimentalism by offering an outlet for emotions linked to Sterne’s influence. It became a favorite of Frau Kurt and a target of Timme’s satire, especially through its followers and imitators; Timme distinguishes the work itself from their excesses and from reading that ignores people in need.

Siegwart, published in 1776, is said to have renewed and redirected the sentimental movement by providing an outlet for emotions associated with Sterne’s influence. Timme treats Siegwart and its followers as targets of satire, while distinguishing the work from the excessive behavior of its imitators.

Siegwart is one of Frau Kurt’s favorite sentimental books and is used as an example of the reading that absorbs her attention while people in need are ignored. The unit also identifies it as one of the popular German works attacked by Timme.
