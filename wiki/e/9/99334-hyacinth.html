Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the hyacinth is a purple, lily-shaped flower that sprang from Hyacinthus’s blood and bears Apollo’s “ai, ai” lamentation. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the hyacinth flower springs from Hyacinthus’s blood, commemorates him, revives each spring, and bears Apollo’s “Ah! Ah!” as a sign of his sorrow.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE III. [X.106-142]

The hyacinth sprang from the blood of Hyacinthus and appeared brighter than Tyrian purple, with a form resembling lilies but with a purple hue. Apollo inscribed his lamentations on its leaves, including the letters ‘ai, ai.’
