Also called: Hyacinth, a flower, a new flower, hyacinth flower, the flower, the hyacinth
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the hyacinth is a purple, lily-shaped flower that sprang from Hyacinthus’s blood and bears Apollo’s “ai, ai” lamentation.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the hyacinth flower springs from Hyacinthus’s blood, commemorates him, revives each spring, and bears Apollo’s “Ah! Ah!” as a sign of his sorrow.

hyacinth in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Hyacinth is a purple flower, brighter than Tyrian purple and lily-shaped, that sprang from Hyacinthus’s blood and bears Apollo’s “ai, ai” lamentation.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE III. [X.106-142]

The hyacinth sprang from the blood of Hyacinthus and appeared brighter than Tyrian purple, with a form resembling lilies but with a purple hue. Apollo inscribed his lamentations on its leaves, including the letters ‘ai, ai.’

hyacinth flower in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

commemorates Hyacinthus [reviving every spring] The hyacinth flower springs from the blood of Hyacinthus and is described as more beautiful than the Tyrian hue, resembling a lily but purple rather than silvery white. Apollo marks its petals with "Ah! Ah!" as a sign of his sorrow, and the flower revives each spring.
