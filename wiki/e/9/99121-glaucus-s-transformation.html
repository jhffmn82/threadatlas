Glaucus's transformation

event · 2 works · 1 quoted fact · 1 connection
Also called: his metamorphosis, metamorphosed into a Triton, sea-change, transformation of Glaucus, transformed into a sea God

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Glaucus's transformation is the event in which he changes from a mortal fisherman into a sea god with a green beard, flowing hair, azure arms, and a fish's fin. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Glaucus's transformation is the event in which he becomes a sea-god with sea-green hair and a fish's tail after Oceanus and Tethys remove his mortality.

Glaucus's transformation in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Glaucus's transformation first appears

Abstract written by a model

After tasting an herb that revives his fish, Glaucus enters the water; Oceanus and Tethys remove his mortality, and he emerges a sea-god with sea-green hair and a fish's tail.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

PROSERPINE

After tasting an herb that revives his fish, Glaucus plunges into the water and is accepted by the water-gods. Oceanus and Tethys have a hundred rivers wash away everything mortal in him. He emerges with sea-green hair, broad shoulders, and a fish's tail, becoming a sea-god.
