Proserpine’s girdle in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Proserpine’s girdle is a fastening worn around the loins to hold up a tunic, sometimes money or a purse, and part of a warrior’s cuirass. Worn by both sexes, young women discontinued it at marriage. It falls into Cyane’s sacred stream; Cyane shows it, Ceres recognizes it, and its continued wearing indicates violence against Proserpine.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Proserpine’s girdle has fallen into the sacred stream at Cyane. Cyane points it out on the surface of the water, and Ceres recognizes it.

notes .. notes

The girdle was worn by both sexes to fasten and raise the tunic, and it could also hold money or a purse. Proserpine's continued wearing of her girdle is treated as evidence of the violence committed against her, since young women discontinued wearing it on their wedding day.
