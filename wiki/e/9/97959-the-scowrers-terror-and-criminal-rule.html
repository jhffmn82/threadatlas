the Scowrers' terror and criminal rule in The Valley of Fear

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1914-09/1915-05

The Scowrers’ terror and criminal rule is their oppressive control of Vermissa and the Valley of Fear through fear, organized assaults, and murder. Over ten years, they attack even people who have not harmed or been seen by them, face no convictions, and boast about fatal violence. Their rule also coerces members into killing and makes departure dangerous, threatening families. By early summer 1875 their dominance is at its height: they are armed, powerful, and confident. It begins to weaken when organized law and investigation make their leaders fear retribution.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter III

The Scowrers’ crimes include organized assaults and murders carried out against people who often have never harmed or even been seen by the attackers. Over ten years of outrage, there has been no conviction, and the members sometimes compete over who delivered the fatal blow while describing the victim’s suffering.

Chapter IV

The Scowrers’ terror and criminal rule force members to participate in violence and prevent them from leaving the society safely. Brother Morris recounts that a murder committed by the group left him excommunicated, morally condemned, and afraid that his wife and children would be killed.

McMurdo thought long and deeply over the commission which had been so

The Scowrers imposed a rule of fear over the district and kept the men and women of the Valley of Fear under an oppressive yoke. Their terror was described as especially dark and hopeless in early summer 1875.

Chapter VI

The reign of terror is at its height, with the Scowrers powerful, armed, and confident that their opponents cannot seriously challenge them. The threat of organized law and investigation begins to make their leaders fear retribution.
