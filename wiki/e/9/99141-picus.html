Picus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Picus is a young, handsome king of Ausonia, son of Saturn, devoted to horses, hunting, augury, and his wife Canens. Carrying javelins and wearing a purple, gold-fastened cloak, he rejects Circe despite other Nymphs' desire. Circe transforms him into a woodpecker, his clothing becoming plumage; after death he is ranked among Latium's Dii Indigetes.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE VI. [XIV.320-440]

Picus is described as a young, handsome king of Ausonia who loves horses and remains faithful to his wife, Canens, despite the many Nymphs who desire him. He rejects Circe's advances, after which she transforms him into a woodpecker; his purple cloak becomes the bird's purple wings, and his golden buckle becomes feathers around his neck.
