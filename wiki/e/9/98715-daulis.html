Daulis in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Daulis is an ancient town in Phocis, about seven stades from Panopeus, though the distance may be corrupt. Despite few inhabitants, it is renowned among the Phocians for size and strength. It was said to be named for the nymph Daulis or for former dense trees, and was traditionally associated with the first cannibalism among mankind. It has a temple of Athene, an older wooden statue brought by Procne, and Tronis with a hero-chapel. From Daulis, routes lead up Parnassus and along the high road to Delphi; after being taken, it was razed to the ground.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IV.

Daulis is about seven stades from Panopeus, though the distance may be corrupt, and its inhabitants are few but renowned among the Phocians for their size and strength. The town is said to have been named after the nymph Daulis, daughter of Cephisus, or after the dense trees once found there. It contains a temple and ancient statue of Athene, an older wooden statue said to have been brought by Procne, and the nearby place called Tronis, where a hero-chapel was built.

CHAPTER V.

Daulis is the starting point for an ascent to the heights of Parnassus and lies on the high road to Delphi. After leaving Daulis, travelers reach the Phocicum on the left side of the road.
