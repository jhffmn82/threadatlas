Cerastæ in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

The Cerastæ are people of Cyprus, named for two horns on their foreheads; they pollute the island by sacrificing strangers at Jupiter Hospes’s altar, until Venus transforms them into bulls.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE VI. [X.220-242]

The Cerastæ are inhabitants of Cyprus whose foreheads are said to have borne two horns. They stain an altar of Jupiter Hospes with the blood of strangers offered in sacrifice, and Venus transforms them into bulls.
