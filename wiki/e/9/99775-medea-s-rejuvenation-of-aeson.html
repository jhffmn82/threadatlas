Medea's rejuvenation of Aeson

event · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: become young again, rejuvenation, renewed, restore, restored, the recovery of the golden Fleece, the same operation, what Medea had done for AESON
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, it is the rejuvenation performed on Æson through Medea's rites and magical mixture, while the transformation of the ram falsely serves as proof that Pelias can also be rejuvenated.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, it is Medea's restoration of Aeson to youthful vigor through nine nights of plants, sacrifices, prayers, sleep, and a magical caldron.

Medea's rejuvenation of Aeson in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Medea restores Aeson to youthful vigor through nine nights of plants, sacrifices, prayers, sleep, and a magical caldron; he emerges forty years younger.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

MEDEA AND AESON

Medea's rejuvenation of Aeson requires nine nights of gathering potent plants, sacrifices and prayers, a deep sleep, and a caldron of magical ingredients. After the operation, Aeson appears as he did forty years earlier.
