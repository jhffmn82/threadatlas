Cycnus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Cycnus, after having children by Proclia, marries Philonome; believing her false accusation, he casts Tenes and his sister adrift, then kills Philonome and buries her alive on learning the truth, before Achilles kills him.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

2] ᾿Αναχθέντων δὲ αὐτῶν ἀπ᾽ Ἄργους καὶ παραγε-

Cycnus believed Philonome’s false accusation against Tenes and cast Tenes and his sister adrift in a chest. After learning the truth, he killed the fluteplayer and buried Philonome alive.

ES 28 | ᾿Αναχθέντες δὲ ἀπὸ τῆς Τενέδου 1 προσέπλεον

Cycnus was killed by Achilles, who threw a stone at his head. After Cycnus died, the barbarians fled into Troy.

Cycnus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Cycnus, son of Poseidon and king of Colonæ, has Tennes and Hemithea with Proclea. After second wife Phylonome falsely accuses Tennes, he casts both children into the sea, learns the truth, and sails after Tennes seeking forgiveness.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIV.

Cycnus was said to be the son of Poseidon and king of Colonæ in the Troad. After his second wife falsely accused his son Tennes, Cycnus cast Tennes and his sister Hemithea into the sea in a chest, later learned the truth, and sailed after Tennes to seek forgiveness.
