Ascalaphus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Ascalaphus, son of Orphne and Acheron and a courtier of Pluto, advises Pluto to carry away Proserpine. After seeing Proserpine eat seven pomegranate grains, he reveals it, obstructing her restoration to Ceres. Ceres or Proserpine punishes him; accounts make him a rock, an owl with great eyes, or a lizard.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Ascalaphus sees Proserpine eat seven grains of a pomegranate and reveals this fact, preventing her return. Proserpine punishes him by transforming him into an owl.

notes .. notes

Ascalaphus revealed that Proserpine had eaten pomegranate seeds and thereby hindered her complete restoration to Ceres. According to different accounts, Ceres punished him with a rock, and he was transformed into an owl or a lizard.
