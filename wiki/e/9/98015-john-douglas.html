Abstract written by a model

John Douglas is a wealthy, vigorous American-made gentleman of about fifty who lives at Birlstone Manor House in England. Known as fearless, generous, and considerate, he made money in the California gold fields, bears a distinctive branded mark on his forearm, and is shadowed by a long-standing hidden menace from America. Though he is usually fearless, he is nervous about fire, makes nightly rounds of the house, orders the drawbridge raised each night, and believes powerful enemies have pursued him from Chicago and California. He is married to Mrs. Douglas and is jealous of Cecil Barker. At first, Douglas appears to have been horribly murdered at Birlstone: a sawed-off shotgun has inflicted fatal-looking injuries to the face, his wedding ring is missing, and a V.V. 341 card lies nearby. His unease after visiting Tunbridge Wells and the warning sent through Porlock suggest that the danger has finally reached him. The apparent victim, however, is later revealed to be alive and concealed in the Manor House. Ted Baldwin attacked him, but Douglas survived; Baldwin's body was substituted for his own, while Douglas prepared a written account of the Valley of Fear. That account identifies him with Birdy Edwards, Pinkerton's best detective. Under the names Steve Wilson and McMurdo, he infiltrated the Scowrers, gathered evidence, prevented or limited crimes, and helped Captain Marvin arrest the gang. After the Scowrers' convictions, surviving members sought revenge. Edwards changed names, including Douglas and John Douglas, escaped America, amassed a fortune with Barker in California, and settled in England, but remained hunted. Douglas is acquitted for acting in self-defense after the Birlstone affair. Holmes urges him to leave England, yet his flight does not end the pursuit: he travels toward South Africa aboard the Palmyra and is later reported lost overboard near St. Helena.

John Douglas in The Valley of Fear

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1914-09 to 1915-05 · the work’s page · read it where John Douglas first appears

Narrative, section by section written by a model

13 sections; each heading opens the text

PART I / Chapter I

Mr. Douglas is identified in the decoded warning as a rich country gentleman residing at Birlstone. Alec MacDonald reports that he was horribly murdered the previous night.

Chapter II

John Douglas is the man whose approaching fate was known through Porlock’s warning. He has been murdered at Birlstone, suffering fatal head injuries from a shotgun discharge.

Chapter III

John Douglas was about fifty, vigorous, genial, generous, and reputed to have made his money in the California gold fields. He was found dead on the floor of the Manor House in a pink dressing gown after receiving a shotgun blast in the face, and he bore a distinctive brand on his right forearm.

Chapter IV

Mr. Douglas has been murdered in the Manor House by a shot from a sawed-off shotgun. His wedding ring is missing, a card marked V.V. 341 lies near him, and he bears a branded triangle inside a circle on his forearm.

Chapter V

Jack Douglas was a rich American-made gentleman who had lived at Birlstone for five years. He was described as fearless, kind, and considerate, but he had shown unusual restlessness and irritability on the day before his murder.

Inspector MacDonald had sent up a note to the effect that he would wait

Mr. Douglas regularly made a nighttime round of the house because he feared fire. His wife says he believed he was being pursued by powerful enemies from his American life and used the expression “the Valley of Fear” for the danger surrounding him.

Chapter VI

Mr. Douglas is the dead man whose body was found in the study. Holmes considers the possibility that he had a shameful secret in his past and that an outsider murdered him as an avenger.

It was nightfall before Inspector MacDonald and White Mason came back

Mr. Douglas had seemed disturbed after visiting Tunbridge Wells, which Holmes and MacDonald treat as evidence that he had become conscious of danger. MacDonald theorizes that he was shot during his usual nightly round, while Holmes argues that the crime happened earlier than reported.

Chapter VII

John Douglas is revealed to be alive and hiding in the Manor House rather than dead as previously believed. He has spent two days preparing a written account and gives it to Dr. Watson as the story of the Valley of Fear.

“That’s the past, Mr. Douglas,” said Sherlock Holmes quietly. “What we

Douglas reveals that he survived Ted Baldwin's attack and hid at Birlstone Manor while Baldwin's body was presented as his own. He says that enemies pursued him from Chicago to California and out of America, and he asks how he stands under English law.

Chapter VI

Birdy Edwards is described as Pinkerton’s best man and as the detective investigating the Scowrers for the corporations and railroads. He is operating under the name Steve Wilson and lodging at Hobson’s Patch. McMurdo and the lodge plan to lure and kill him.

Chapter VII

Birdy Edwards reveals that he has been posing as McMurdo while infiltrating the Scowrers for Pinkerton’s. He traps the gang at his house, helps Captain Marvin arrest them, and explains that he prevented or reduced several crimes while gathering evidence against the organization. After the trial, he is pursued by surviving Scowrers, changes his name several times, and eventually lives in England as John Douglas.

Epilogue

John Douglas was acquitted at the Quarter Sessions on the ground that he had acted in self-defense. Holmes urged that he be taken out of England, but he was later reported lost overboard from the Palmyra near St. Helena.
