English literary interests in Laurence Sterne in Germany

Harvey Waterman Thayer · 1905

English literary influence on Germany unfolds through reading, translation, and imitation. English works, including Robinson Crusoe, Richardson, Fielding, and Edward Young, circulated in originals and popular translations. In the 1760s, British literary supremacy shaped German reading and writing. Sterne, or Yorick, held especially substantial serious esteem in Germany and France, reinforcing English literature’s status as a body of established classics.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

NEW YORK, May 1, 1905.

English literature supplied Germany with influential authors and literary movements, including Robinson Crusoe, Richardson, Fielding, and Edward Young. English works were read in the original and in numerous popular translations.

] CHAPTER II STERNE IN GERMANY BEFORE THE PUBLICATION OF THE SENTIMENTAL JOURNEY It is no exaggeration to assert that the works of Yorick obtained and still retain a relatively more substantial position of serious consideration and recognized merit in France and Germany than in the countries where Sterne’s own tongue is spoken.

English literature was viewed from the Continent as containing established classics, with Sterne’s position reinforced partly by the magnitude of his influence in France and Germany. The unit presents the 1760s as a period when British literary supremacy strongly shaped German reading and writing.
