Johann Karl Wezel is credited with wit, narrative ability, knowledge of human nature, and a conscious plan in Tobias Knaut, but contemporary reviewers criticized his imitation of Sterne as obscuring his originality. His novel combines Sternean technique with satire directed against sentimentalism, exaggerated originality, and the German form of Sterne worship.

A former acquaintance of Frau Kurt, whose favorite reading was Shandy, Wieland’s

Wezel wrote Wilhelmine Arend, oder die Gefahren der Empfindsamkeit as a more earnest attack on sentimentalism than Timme’s novel. His satire is judged unsuccessful because Wilhelmine is unconvincing and pitiable rather than ludicrous, leaving the book between effective satire and serious psychological presentation.
