Greek fire-passing customs protect against evil spirits: by the fifth or seventh day after birth, a child was run round the hearth, possibly replacing an older practice of passing the child over fire in houses suggested to have central fireplaces.

J.—PuttTinc CHILDREN ON THE FIRE

Greek custom included running round the hearth with a child on the fifth or seventh day after birth, possibly as a substitute for the older practice of passing the child over the fire. Ancient Greek houses are suggested to have had a central fireplace, with smoke escaping through a hole in the roof.
