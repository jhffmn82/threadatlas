Leucothoë's love affair with the Sun in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The Sun’s intense love affair with Leucothoë begins when Venus retaliates: he enters her chamber disguised as her mother, reveals himself, and Leucothoë submits to his embrace, while his devotion provokes Clytie’s enduring envy.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

The Sun falls in love with Leucothoë after Venus retaliates against him. He enters her chamber disguised as her mother, reveals that he is the Sun, and, after Leucothoë is overcome by his beauty, she submits to his embrace.

opening

The Sun's love for Leucothoë is described as having been intense, and it causes Clytie to envy her. Clytie's own passion for the Sun remains after her transformation.
