Abrog in Grampa in Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1924

Abrog, revealed to be Gorba by reversing his name, is an aged, silent High Sky prophet and wizard of Perhaps City, described as dreadful, scheming, and villainous. He lives in a tower, speaks only once yearly to prophesy, and keeps magical appliances in a hidden garden associated with patent medicine. He predicts that a monster will marry the Princess, offers to marry her himself, and disappears from his tower with her while claiming he will seek another solution. Later, he condemns Peer Haps for the monster marriage, uses magic to give Tatters the appearance of two heads, and tries to seize the Princess's cloak before Tatters catches him. He admits he bewitched Urtha to save Pretty Good from the monster and marry her himself; instead of turning Urtha to mud, his spell makes her a flower fairy. He is punished by being transformed into a mouse.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 5

The wizard is associated with the patent medicine and with the Garden of Gorba. Grampa suspects that the wizard may be present in the garden and warns the others not to trust a wizard.

CHAPTER 8

Abrog is the silent High Sky prophet of Perhaps City who normally speaks only once a year. He warns that a monster will marry the Princess in four days, proposes marrying her himself, then says he will use magic to find another solution before disappearing from his tower along with the Princess.

CHAPTER 19

Abrog enters the throne room and calls Peer Haps an idiot for marrying his daughter to a monster. He places Fumbo's head on Tatters's shoulders to make Tatters appear to have two heads, then tries to reach the Princess and snatches away her cloak. Tatters catches him after he reveals the Princess.

CHAPTER 20

Abrog confesses that he bewitched Urtha because he wanted to save Pretty Good from the monster and marry her himself. He intended to change Urtha into mud, but the spell made her a flower fairy; he is later punished and transformed into a mouse.
