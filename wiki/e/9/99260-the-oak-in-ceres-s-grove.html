the oak in Ceres's grove

object · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Deoïan oak, a venerable oak, oak, sacred oak, the oak, the pride of the forest, the tree, tree

Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, it is an ancient, revered oak in Ceres' grove, home of a Nymph, that is surrounded by offerings, bleeds, groans, warns Erisicthon, and is finally felled. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, it is a sacred, inhabited and garlanded oak that shudders and bleeds under Erisichthon's blows before falling and crushing much of the grove.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

EXPLANATION.

The sacred oak in Ceres’ grove was ancient and surrounded by offerings, memorial tablets, garlands, and the dances of Dryads. Erisicthon ordered it cut down despite its holiness; it bled, groaned, and warned him of punishment before he finally felled it.

Abstract written by a model

A sacred, inhabited, garlanded oak, vast as a wood, shudders and bleeds under Erisichthon’s blows before falling and crushing much of the grove.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XV

The oak is a venerable tree so large that it seems like a wood by itself, and votive garlands and inscriptions are placed on it. When Erisichthon strikes it, it shudders and appears to bleed; repeated blows finally bring it down, prostrating much of the grove.
