Greek and Roman mythology in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Greek and Roman mythology is presented as a compendium of antiquity’s learning, traditions, manners, and customs, and as the subject of Ovid’s Metamorphoses, Books I–VII. Its narratives may embellish Phoenician or Egyptian origins, as with Io, and include Europa’s famous abduction.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

front matter

The introduction describes the work as a compendium of Greek and Roman mythology and says that it conveys information about the learning, traditions, manners, and customs of antiquity.

notes .. notes

Greek mythology is described as frequently embellishing narratives of Phoenician or Egyptian origin. The story of Io is given as an example of this process.

notes .. notes

The unit identifies the story of Europa's abduction as one of the most famous events in ancient Mythology.
