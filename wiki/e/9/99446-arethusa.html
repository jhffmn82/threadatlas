Abstract written by a model

Arethusa is a nymph of the Alpheian waters and of Achaia, native to Pisa and born from Elis, later a sacred spring, river, and fountain on Ortygia in Sicily. A robust, beautiful hunter and Diana’s protected armor-bearer, she hunted through glades and set nets. While bathing, she was pursued by Alpheus; Diana hid her in a cloud, after which she became a stream. Carried beneath spacious seas and through Earth’s lowest caverns, she flowed through Styx, saw Proserpine as queen and spouse of the Infernal King, and told Ceres of her fate. She finally reached Ortygia’s upper air, where she has her abode as a fountain.

Arethusa is in one work on this site, The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Arethusa in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Arethusa first appears

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Arethusa, the Alpheian Nymph, tells Ceres that Sicily did not willingly provide a path for Proserpine’s abduction. She explains that, while traveling beneath the earth, she saw Proserpine in the Stygian stream’s vicinity, already a queen and the spouse of the Infernal King.

notes .. notes

Arethusa discovered and reported to Ceres what had happened to Proserpine. Ovid makes Arethusa the informant in this account, although in the Fourth Book of the Fasti he instead represents the Sun as giving Ceres the information.

opening

Arethusa tells Ceres that she was an Achaian nymph who enjoyed hunting and was pursued by Alpheus while bathing. After Diana concealed her in a cloud, Arethusa changed into a stream and was carried underground to Ortygia.

notes .. notes

Arethusa is a Sicilian fountain surrounded by willows. In the poetic story, she is a Nymph pursued by the river god Alpheus, and the oracle of Delphi describes Alpheus as mixing his waters with her.
