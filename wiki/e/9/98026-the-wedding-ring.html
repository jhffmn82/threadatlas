the wedding ring in The Valley of Fear

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1914-09/1915-05

The wedding ring is John or Mr. Douglas’s plain gold ring, worn on the little finger of his left hand beneath a nugget ring. After Douglas’s death, it is missing: an unknown person removed it, while the nugget ring was removed and replaced, suggesting deliberate taking. White Mason supposes the killer demanded it before shooting Douglas, but its purpose is unknown. MacDonald finds the removal extraordinary and considers whether it connects Douglas’s marriage to the tragedy, though Barker rejects any reflection on Mrs. Douglas’s honour. Holmes doubts the reported removal-and-replacement account and considers that an avenger may have taken it in connection with Douglas’s first marriage.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter III

John Douglas’s plain gold wedding ring was missing from the little finger of his left hand. Ames explained that it had been worn beneath a nugget ring, which had been removed and then replaced, indicating that someone had deliberately taken the wedding ring.

Chapter IV

Mr. Douglas’s wedding ring is missing from his finger. White Mason’s reconstruction supposes that the killer demanded the ring before shooting him, but the reason for its removal remains unknown.

Chapter V

Jack Douglas's wedding ring had been removed from his finger. Inspector MacDonald suggested that its absence could connect the marriage with the tragedy, while Barker said it did not reflect upon Mrs. Douglas's honour.

Inspector MacDonald had sent up a note to the effect that he would wait

Mr. Douglas's wedding ring was taken from him. Inspector MacDonald considers its removal extraordinary and questions what motive an enemy could have for taking it.

Chapter VI

The wedding ring was reportedly removed from Mr. Douglas’s finger and replaced beneath another ring, which Holmes considers an implausible part of the account. He also considers the possibility that an avenger took it because the vendetta might relate to Mr. Douglas’s first marriage.
