The German literary world initially showed limited and often belated recognition of Sterne and Yorick. Although periodicals treated Sterne as a fitful object of English taste, individual figures within the inner circle of German culture became acquainted with Tristram Shandy and learned to esteem him.

German literati such as Herder, Hamann, Riedel, and Wieland formed selective circles of readers who knew, admired, translated, or discussed Sterne. Their early interest in Tristram Shandy remained largely confined to educated book-lovers until the Sentimental Journey greatly expanded Sterne’s reputation.
