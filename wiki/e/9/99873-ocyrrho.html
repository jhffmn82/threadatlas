Ocyrrhoë in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Ocyrrhoë, daughter of Chiron and Chariclo, is a centaur’s daughter and prophetess trained in riding and managing horses by her father. She sings the Fates’ secrets and foretells the destinies of Æsculapius and Chiron. Prevented from speaking further, she is punished for prophetic arts by transformation into a mare and renamed Enippe.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Ocyrrhoe, the daughter of Chiron and Chariclo, learns her father’s arts and sings the secrets of the Fates. She prophesies the fates of Æsculapius and Chiron, but the Fates prevent her from speaking further; she is then transformed into a mare and named Enippe.

notes .. notes

Ocyrrhoë was the daughter of a Centaur. Her skill in riding and managing horses under her father’s instruction may have provided the basis for the story that she was transformed into a mare.
