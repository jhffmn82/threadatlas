Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, metempsychosis is described as a doctrine of continual soul transmigration between bodies that leads people to protect animals. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, metempsychosis is described as an ancient doctrine of the transmigration of immortal souls among human and animal bodies.

metempsychosis in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where metempsychosis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Metempsychosis is an ancient doctrine of the transmigration of immortal souls. Souls pass among human and animal bodies according to purification, corruption, and the reward or punishment of actions. Purified souls receive new bodies; souls too corrupted for human bodies may enter animals, and sufficiently purified souls eventually return to their divine source.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXV

Metempsychosis is the ancient doctrine of the transmigration of souls. It teaches that purified souls receive new bodies, while souls considered too corrupted for human bodies may enter animals such as lions, tigers, cats, dogs, or monkeys.

Chapter XXVII

Metempsychosis is the doctrine that the soul is immortal and passes from one body to another, including human and animal bodies. The soul eventually returns to its divine source when sufficiently purified.
