house of Pelops

topic · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: the house

Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the house of Pelops is presented as having lost its entire line and former happiness, with its ruin traced through the crimes of Pelops, the golden lamb, Thyestes' feast, and the marriage of Ærope. In The Library, the house of Pelops is represented through the conflict among Pelops’s sons Atreus and Thyestes, the adultery of Aerope, the murder of Thyestes’ sons, and the later killing of Atreus by Aegisthus.
