the sand-boat in The Road to Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1909

The sand-boat is a twelve-foot-long, four-foot-wide boat designed to travel over sand. Built at the edge of the Deadly Desert by Johnny Dooit from boards, a pole, rope, and canvas, it has two sharp runners, a pointed front, a mast and sail, and a stern rudder. Its runners and sails let wind carry travelers over the Deadly Sands without contact with them, provided it does not tip. It glides rapidly across the desert, then strikes rocks at the far edge, is crushed into splinters, and loses its sail to a tall tree.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

11. Johnny Dooit Does It

The sand-boat is designed with runners like a sled and sails like a ship, allowing the wind to carry travelers over the desert without contact with the sand. Johnny Dooit builds it rapidly from boards, a pole, rope, and canvas, and says it will carry them across if they avoid tipping over.

12. The Deadly Desert Crossed

The sand-boat has two sharp runners, a pointed front, and a rudder, and it glides rapidly over the sand when its sail catches the wind. It crashes squarely into rocks at the far edge of the desert and is crushed into a mass of splinters. Its sail is torn away and carried to the top of a tall tree.
