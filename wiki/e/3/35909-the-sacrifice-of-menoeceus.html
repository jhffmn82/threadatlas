the sacrifice of Menoeceus

Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the sacrifice of Menoeceus is his voluntary stabbing in the dragon’s cave, giving his blood to the earth to appease Mars’s wrath, preserve Thebes, and fulfill Tiresias’s prophecy of Theban victory. In The Library, the self-sacrifice of Menoeceus consists of his offering himself to Ares by killing himself before the gates of Thebes, bringing victory to the Thebans.

ETEOCLES, CHORUS, MESSENGER.

The sacrifice requires Menoeceus to be stabbed in the cave where the earth-born dragon once lay, with his blood given to the earth in return for the dragon's blood. Menoeceus accepts this fate voluntarily, believing that his death will save Thebes and prevent him from becoming a cowardly deserter.
