character in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

High character requires reliable, principled habits: good feelings and deeds cannot outweigh repeated unjust, rude, cruel, or unreliable conduct, and it likely cannot coexist with a temper like Touchwood's.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

VI.

High character is said to require qualities that can be safely calculated on and that have become principles or laws habitually obeyed. Good feelings and good deeds do not compensate for repeated unjust judgments, rude behaviour, cruel words, or unreliable conduct. The unit doubts that a high order of character can coexist with a temper like Touchwood's.
