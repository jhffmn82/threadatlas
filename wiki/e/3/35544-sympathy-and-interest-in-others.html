2 sections; each heading opens the text

I.

The narrator tries to widen his personal sensitiveness into a beneficent sympathy by becoming interested in the experiences and fortunes of others. This habit grows stronger as he restrains his desire to redirect their confidences toward himself.

VIII.

Sympathy and fellow-feeling should provide checks on actions by making people consider those immediately affected rather than relying only on distant, grand motives. The narrator argues that actions beneficial to future ages will usually also show beneficence and grace toward contemporaries.
