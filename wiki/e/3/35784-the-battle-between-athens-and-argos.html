Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, it is the contest in which Athenian and Argive forces clash and the Athenians and descendants of Hercules win. In Hecuba and other plays, it is the conflict between Athens and Argos that culminates in Athens routing Argos.

the battle between Athens and Argos in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where the battle between Athens and Argos first appears

Abstract written by a model

The battle between Athens and Argos is a contest between Athenian and Argive forces that begins as both armies prepare under Hyllus and Eurystheus. After single combat is rejected, they clash with spears and chariots. Following a hard fight, the Athenians and descendants of Hercules rout the Argives, winning victory.

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHOR. This land is ever willing to aid in a just cause those in difficulty;

The battle between Athens and Argos is imminent. Hyllus has brought an army to support Athens, while Eurystheus has arrayed the Argives, and both sides are preparing their victims and formations.

SERV. O mistress, I bring news most concise for you to hear, and to myself

The battle begins after a rejected proposal for single combat between Hyllus and the opposing general. The armies clash with spears and chariots; after a difficult fight, the Athenians and the descendants of Hercules put the Argive force to flight.

Argive invasion of Athens in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · about 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Argive invasion of Athens first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Argive invasion of Athens is a conflict between Athens and Argos, threatened after Copreus fails to seize the children of Hercules. Eurystheus promises an attack, prompting Demophoon to gather troops, spies, seers, and victims. As battle nears, Hyllus marshals Athens’ forces and Iolaus seeks to help despite age. After failed single combat and a virgin’s sacrifice, the armies fight; Athens ultimately routs Argos.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

THE CHILDREN OF HERCULES.

The threatened Argive invasion of Athens follows Copreus’ failure to seize the children of Hercules. Copreus promises that Eurystheus and a large force will attack, and Demophoon begins gathering troops, spies, seers, and sacrificial victims to meet them.

DEMOPHOON, IOLAUS, CHORUS.

The battle between the Athenian and Argive forces is imminent when Hyllus marshals the troops. Iolaus insists that honor requires him to assist his friends, though the servant warns that his age and weakness make him unable to fight effectively.

SERVANT, ALCMENA, CHORUS.

The battle begins after single combat fails and a sacrificial virgin is killed as an auspicious victim. The armies clash with heavy losses, and the Athenian side eventually puts the Argive forces to flight.
