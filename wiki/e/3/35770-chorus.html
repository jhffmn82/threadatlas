Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Chorus defends suppliants, witnesses violence, demands their protection, urges restraint, warns of the approaching Argive army, praises Athens’s justice, reflects on fortune and the Gods, questions Iolaus’s aged fight, and calls for news. In Hecuba and other plays, Chorus of Athenian old men protects Hercules’ suppliant children, condemns Copreus’s force, supports Demophoon’s refusal, and urges preparation for Argos’s threatened invasion. In Hecuba and other plays, Chorus celebrates victory and Zeus’s justice, urges Alcmene to release Eurystheus, and approves his killing.

Chorus in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Chorus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Chorus is a group defending suppliants, especially the Heraclids, after receiving them and facing danger for its country and house. It witnesses Copreus's violence toward Iolaus, demands protection for the Gods' suppliants, urges restraint, and warns of the approaching Argive army. It praises Athens's justice and Macaria's courage, reflects on fortune and the Gods, questions Iolaus's aged fight, and finally calls on earth, moon, divine rays, and Minerva for news.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

THE HERACLIDÆ.

The Chorus witnesses Copreus's violence against Iolaus and insists that the suppliants of the Gods must be protected. It urges Demophoon and Copreus to avoid violence and warns that the Argive army is approaching.

CHOR. This land is ever willing to aid in a just cause those in difficulty;

The Chorus praises Athens for aiding those in a just cause but warns the Argive visitor against threatening the city. It honors Macaria's courage and noble reputation, reflects that human fortune depends on the Gods, and questions Iolaus's attempt to fight despite his age.

CHOR. O earth, and moon that shinest through the night, and most brilliant

The Chorus calls on the earth, moon, divine rays, and Minerva to bring news and announce the speaker's situation in heaven and at Minerva's throne.

Chorus of Athenian old men in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · about 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Chorus of Athenian old men first appears

Abstract written by a model

A group of elderly Athenian citizens protects Hercules’ suppliant children, condemns Copreus’ force, supports Demophoon’s refusal, and urges preparation for Argos’ threatened invasion.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE CHILDREN OF HERCULES.

The Chorus discovers Copreus using force against Iolaus and the children of Hercules and declares that the suppliants of the gods must be protected. It supports Demophoon’s refusal to surrender them and warns that Argos’ threatened invasion requires careful preparation.

Chorus in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · about 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Chorus first appears

Abstract written by a model

A choral group celebrates victory, reversal, Zeus's justice, and Hercules among the gods; it urges Alcmene to release Eurystheus, then approves his killing.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

SERVANT, ALCMENA, CHORUS.

The Chorus celebrates the victory and the reversal of fortune, praises Zeus's justice, and says that Hercules now dwells among the gods with Hebe. It advises Alcmene to release Eurystheus because Athens desires it, but later approves her decision to have him killed.
