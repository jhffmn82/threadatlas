image of Artemis in Iphigenia in Tauris

Euripides · -0413/-0411

The image of Artemis, believed to have fallen from heaven, stands in the Taurian shrine and is associated with the sacrifice of strangers. Apollo commands Orestes to seize it and carry it to Attic earth for freedom, so it will no longer be stained with human sacrifice. Iphigenia removes it during a false cleansing, claiming it is defiled, takes it aboard the Greek ship, and Athena orders its installation at Halae.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

PREFACE

Artemis’s image among the Tauri is believed to have fallen from heaven and is associated with the sacrifice of strangers. Orestes is sent by Apollo to take the image to Attica so that it will no longer be stained with human sacrifice.

Iphigenia in Tauris

The divine image of Artemis is said to have fallen from heaven into the Taurian shrine. Orestes has been commanded to seize it and carry it to Attic earth.

LEADER.

The image of Artemis is the object Orestes must take to Greece in order to gain freedom. Iphigenia carries it from its shrine, claiming that it has been defiled by contact with the guilty strangers and must be purified.

CHORUS. [STROPHE.]

Iphigenia steals the image of Artemis during the false cleansing and carries it aboard the Greek ship. Athena commands that it be taken to Greece and installed at Halae, where its history will be remembered.

Diana's image in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Diana's sacred image, said to have fallen from heaven, belongs to Artemis and stands in the Tauric temple. When strangers approach, it turns away and closes its eyes, prompting Iphigenia to remove it under pretense of purification. Orestes and Iphigenia carry it to a Grecian ship; by Phœbus's and Minerva's commands, it is taken and enshrined at Athens.

2 sections; each heading opens the text

IPHIGENIA IN TAURIS.

Diana's image is said to have fallen from heaven into the Tauric temple. Phœbus commands Orestes to take it and give it to the land of the Athenians.

THOAS. Where is the Grecian woman who keeps the gate of this temple? Has

The sacred image turns away from its seat and closes its eyes when the strangers approach, which Iphigenia interprets as a sign of pollution. She carries it from the temple under the pretense of purification, and Orestes and Iphigenia take it aboard the Grecian ship. Minerva commands that it be enshrined at Athens.

Taurian wooden image in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

A wooden image in the land of the Taurians is sought by Orestes on divine instruction to free him from disorder. He carries it away with Iphigenia to Athens, where it is called Artemis Tauropolus; some place it on Rhodes.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ES 23 [᾿Αγαμέμνων δὲ καταντήσας εἰς Μυκήνας μετὰ

The god tells Orestes to fetch the wooden image in the land of the Taurians as a means of being freed from his disorder. Orestes carries it away with his sister, and it is taken to Athens, where it is called the image of Tauropolus; some say it is instead dedicated in a fortification wall on Rhodes.
