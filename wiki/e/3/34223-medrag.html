MEDRAG in Benchmarking Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Medicine

Guangzhi Xiong, Qiao Jin, Zhiyong Lu, Aidong Zhang · 2024 · the work’s page · read it where MEDRAG first appears

Abstract written by a model

MEDRAG is an easy-to-use medical retrieval-augmented generation toolkit for evaluating RAG systems and answering medical questions. It systematically combines three major components, Corpora, Retrievers, and LLMs, covering five corpora, four retrievers, and six general or domain-specific LLMs. It prepends retrieved snippets to model inputs, uses chain-of-thought prompting, and sets temperature to 0 for deterministic outputs. The work evaluates MIRAGE through 41 combinations and varies LLMs, corpora, retrievers, retrieved-snippet counts, and context positions. Across six LLMs, MEDRAG improves accuracy relative to chain-of-thought prompting by 1% to 18%, elevating GPT-3.5 and Mixtral to GPT-4-level performance. PubMed or MedCorp broadly perform best, while too few or too many snippets can hurt results. MEDRAG supports evaluation-based research and real-world deployment recommendations, though its coverage could grow with PubMed Central full-text articles and trustworthy FAQs; generated rationales remain unevaluated.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Abstract

MEDRAG is introduced as a toolkit for evaluating retrieval-augmented generation systems. Using MEDRAG, the study ran large-scale experiments across 41 combinations of corpora, retrievers, and backbone LLMs, and found accuracy improvements of up to 18% over chain-of-thought prompting for six LLMs.

Introduction

MEDRAG is an easy-to-use medical RAG toolkit covering five corpora, four retrievers, and six general or domain-specific Large Language Models. Its configurations improve performance on MIRAGE relative to chain-of-thought prompting, with relative gains of 1% to 18% across different models.

The MEDRAG Toolkit

The MEDRAG Toolkit provides systematic implementations of retrieval-augmented generation for medical question answering. It consists of Corpora, Retrievers, and LLMs, prepends retrieved snippets to model inputs, uses chain-of-thought prompting, and sets temperatures to 0 for deterministic outputs.

Results .. Practical Recommendations

MEDRAG is evaluated on MIRAGE with different LLMs, corpora, retrievers, numbers of retrieved snippets, and context positions. It substantially improves several backbone models, performs best broadly with PubMed or MedCorp, and can suffer when too few or too many snippets are supplied.

Conclusion

The MEDRAG toolkit is introduced to evaluate RAG systems in medicine. It supports comprehensive evaluations that inform research and real-world deployment recommendations.

Limitations

MEDRAG has reasonably comprehensive coverage of corpora, retrievers, and LLMs, but could be expanded with full-text articles from PubMed Central and FAQs from trustworthy sources. It may also benefit knowledge-intensive tasks such as claim verification, although its generated rationales remain unevaluated.

MedRAG in Rationale-Guided Retrieval Augmented Generation

Jiwoong Sohn · 2025 · the work’s page · read it where MedRAG first appears

Abstract written by a model

MedRAG is a retrieval-augmented generation method, baseline, and toolkit integrating indexing, retrieval, and reranking. It extracts MedCorps documents through a hybrid of sparse and dense retrievers, uses four retrievers over MedCorp, and reranks retrieved results with Reciprocal Rank Fusion. Its retrieval methods and validated top-k values yield inconsistent performance across top-k settings; it often underperforms LLMs without retrieval and is generally surpassed by RAG2 and the balanced retrieval method.
