altar

object · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 8 connections
Also called: Diana's altar, God's altar, altar of Diana, altar of the Goddess, altar of the sanguinary Goddess, altar-stone, altars, ancient altar, her altar, high Altar, temple and the altar, the altar
  • In Iphigenia in Tauris, the altar is used for Iphigenia's sacrifice at Aulis and is stained with the blood of sacrificed strangers, with Iphigenia expected to prepare captured Greeks for sacrifice upon it.
  • In Iphigenia in Tauris, the altar is the sacred place where the Taurian rite is performed and where Iphigenia says Orestes may be killed, while Orestes imagines dying there and Iphigenia recalls being taken to an altar at Aulis.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the altar is a memorial for Iphigenia and the focal place of sacrifice, where she is told to stand at Aulis and where slain strangers' blood marks Diana's temple.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the altar of Diana is where the girl is to be sacrificed, where the stag appears in her place, and where the stag's blood wets the altar before the victim is reduced to ashes.

altar in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Diana’s altar is a memorial for Iphigenia and the focal place of sacrifice: at Aulis she is told she will stand by it as preparations and songs proceed; at Diana’s temple it is marked by slain strangers’ blood while Iphigenia prepares sacrifices and others kill within the shrine.

Diana's altar is the place where Iphigenia is to be sacrificed and where she says her memorial will stand. The ritual preparations and songs are directed toward this altar.

IPHIGENIA IN TAURIS.

The altar at Diana's temple is marked by the blood of slain strangers. Iphigenia performs the preparation for the sacrifices, while others within the shrine carry out the killing.
