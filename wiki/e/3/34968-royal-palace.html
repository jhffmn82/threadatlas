Royal Palace in The Road to Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1909

Royal Palace is a magnificent royal palace, described as standing in central Foxville and as a royal residence in the Emerald City. Marked by a crowned fox’s head, it has richly decorated rooms, fox servants, and a golden throne room where King Dox receives travelers. It is vast and elegant, surrounded by gardens and ample grounds behind a green-marble wall. Dorothy and companions arrive in Ozma’s chariot, are welcomed by servants, and receive prepared rooms; its interiors include halls, drawing-rooms, guest apartments, and the shaggy man’s lavish quarters.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

3. A Queer Village

The royal palace stands in the center of Foxville and is marked by a crowned fox's head over its entrance. Inside are richly decorated rooms, fox servants, and a golden throne room where King Dox receives the travelers.

18. The Emerald City

The Royal Palace is a vast, noble, and elegant building with gardens and grounds enclosed by a separate wall of green marble. Dorothy and her companions arrive there in Ozma's royal chariot and are welcomed by gorgeously dressed servants, with rooms prepared for their use.

19. The Shaggy Man's Welcome

The palace contains a great hall, a velvet-carpeted staircase, wide corridors, magnificent drawing-rooms, and luxurious guest apartments. The shaggy man's apartments include gold furniture, tapestries, a fountain, fine clothing, jewels, and a marble bathing pool.
