Abstract written by a model

Temper is primarily the proportion and mode in which qualities are mingled, and commonly an habitual state or general tendency. Popular judgment may excuse bad temper as separate from character, yet it can cause rude, cruel, inconsistent, and unjust behaviour, disrupting healthy mental habits and harmony among perception, conviction, and impulse.

1 section; each heading opens the text

VI.

Temper is described primarily as the proportion and mode in which qualities are mingled, but in common usage it often means an habitual state or general tendency. Bad temper is frequently treated as separate from character and excused as a mood, although it can produce rude, cruel, inconsistent, and unjust behaviour. A temper of this kind interrupts the formation of healthy mental habits by disturbing the harmony between perception, conviction, and impulse.
