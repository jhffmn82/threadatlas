the English countryside in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

The English countryside is a national landscape of midland uplands and plains, woodlands, pastures, hedgerows, roadsides, rivers, villages, markets, cottages, barns, and churches. Readily altered by human labour yet marked by permanence and railway-driven world-wide change, it roots national life and language, inspires affectionate joy and sympathy with labour, and fosters tender attachment to the past and conservative prepossessions.

II.

The national landscape, especially the midland uplands, hedgerows, grassy roadsides, villages, and markets, provided a deep root of the narrator's national life and language. Its memory continues to give him affectionate joy and has mingled with his conservative prepossessions.

The English countryside consists of midland plains, woodlands, pastures, hedge-parted fields, commons, rivers, villages, cottages, barns, churches, and other familiar features. Because these features are readily altered yet retain signs of permanence, they create sympathy with human labor and encourage tender attachment to the past.
