Antigone’s marriage

event · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: bridal bed, love was plighted, marriage-bed, marriage-song, the marriage of Antigone and Haemon, thy marriage, thy son's espousal, unwed, wedlock

Abstract written by a model

In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Creon treats Antigone’s promised marriage to Haemon as no reason to spare her and says that Haemon may find another bride; Ismene warns that Antigone’s death will also destroy the espousal, while Haemon says he knows whom her death will ruin. In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, Antigone’s marriage is denied by Creon and prevented by her execution; she dies unwed, calling her tomb her bridal chamber.
