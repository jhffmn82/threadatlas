Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Iphigenia and Orestes's escape is their flight by sea with the sacred image after Iphigenia frees Orestes and Pylades through a false purification, despite Thoas's men and rough water, with Minerva ensuring their success. In Iphigenia in Tauris, Iphigenia and Orestes's escape is their joint flight from Tauris to Greece with Pylades and the Greek women, achieved by deceiving King Thoas and taking Artemis's image to a ship before Athena secures their departure.

escape of Iphigenia and Orestes in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where escape of Iphigenia and Orestes first appears

Abstract written by a model

The escape of Iphigenia and Orestes from Tauris is a flight by sea with the sacred image. Orestes and Pylades, captured near the temple, are freed when Iphigenia says they must be purified away from it. At the shore she releases them, boards the Grecian ship with Orestes and the image, and escapes despite Thoas's men and rough water, with Minerva ensuring success.

Iphigenia and Orestes's escape in Iphigenia in Tauris

Euripides · 413 to 411 BC · the work’s page · read it where Iphigenia and Orestes's escape first appears

Abstract written by a model

Iphigenia and Orestes’s escape is the joint flight from Tauris to Greece after Iphigenia recognizes Orestes and abandons her earlier plan to save only Pylades. To avoid the Taurian sacrifice, Iphigenia, Orestes, Pylades, and the Greek women deceive King Thoas with a false purification, secretly take Artemis’s image to a ship, and seek passage to Argos. Tauric forces and a storm nearly prevent them, but Athena intervenes and secures their departure.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

PREFACE

Iphigenia’s meeting with Orestes leads her to risk herself in order to save him and the other Greek stranger. Their escape from the Taurian sacrifice forms part of the play’s movement through peril to a happy ending.

[Enter ORESTES and PYLADES, bound, and guarded by

Iphigenia intends to save Pylades by having him carry her tablet to Argos and persuade the king to let him sail safely away. After recognizing Orestes, she realizes that the goal must become their joint return to Argos and urgently searches for a route before the executioners arrive.

LEADER.

The escape to Greece depends on secretly taking the image of Artemis to the ship while King Thoas believes that a purification is being performed. Iphigenia, Orestes, Pylades, and the Greek women all contribute to the plan.

CHORUS. [STROPHE.]

Iphigenia’s false cleansing enables her and Orestes to reach the Greek ship with the image of Artemis. Their escape is nearly stopped by the Tauric forces and the storm, but Athena intervenes and secures their departure.
