Oedipus pronounces a curse on Laius's murderer: exile from Thebes and human fellowship, denial of shelter, speech, and religious rites, and divine punishment for the killer or concealers. After recalling his actions at the crossroads, he realizes the curse may condemn himself.

[OEDIPUS _has entered during the Choral song_

Oedipus curses the murderer with a withered life of hopeless misery and commands that no one shelter, speak with, or share religious rites with him. He further calls for the murderer and anyone who conceals him to suffer divine punishment.

CHORUS.

Oedipus's curse calls for Laius's murderer to be driven from the land and denied fellowship, shelter, and speech. After recounting his own actions at the crossroads, Oedipus realizes that he may have pronounced the curse against himself.
