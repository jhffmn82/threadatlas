Hercules's rescue of Alcestis in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Hercules's rescue of Alcestis is a rescue at Alcestis's tomb. Hercules plans to ambush Death and force Alcestis's release, or seek her from the underworld's rulers. He confronts Death, brings Alcestis into the light, returns her to Admetus, and requires her silence until the third day.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHOR. Desist, for the present calamity is sufficient; and do not, O son,

Hercules plans to ambush Death near Alcestis's tomb and force him to release her. If Death does not appear there, Hercules intends to descend to the underworld and ask its rulers to return Alcestis to Admetus.

ADMETUS, CHORUS.

Heracles rescues Alcestis by confronting Death at the tomb and bringing her from beneath into the light. He gives her to Admetus but explains that she must remain silent until she is released from her consecrations to the Gods below and the third day has come.
