Melissa in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

Melissa pities Sir Gavial Mantrap after his commercial disgrace, judging him moral for family respectability and local charity. She condemns Mr Barabbas’s private sexual conduct and later regards the narrator as violent-tempered and insufficiently strict about morality.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

XVI.

Melissa pities Sir Gavial Mantrap after his commercial disgrace, saying that his conduct regarding the Companies was unfortunate but that he is a thoroughly moral man because he is an excellent family man and charitable near Tiptop. She distinguishes him favorably from Mr Barabbas, whose personal life she considers objectionable, and later regards the narrator as violent-tempered and insufficiently strict about morality.
