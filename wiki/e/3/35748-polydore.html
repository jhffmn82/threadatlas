Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Polydore is Hecuba and Priam’s youngest son, secretly sent to Polymestor for protection, but murdered by him for gold; his body is cast into the sea and later discovered and buried by Hecuba. In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the Ghost of Polydore leaves the underworld to recount his murder and his family’s fate, worries for Hecuba, withdraws as she approaches, and is to appear before a female slave for burial. In Hecuba and other plays, Polydore is Priam and Hecuba’s youngest son, murdered by Polymestor for gold; after appearing as a ghost seeking burial, his remains are recognized and permitted by Agamemnon to be buried with Polyxena. In The Aeneid, Polydore is Priam’s son, sent to Thrace for safety but murdered by its faithless ruler for gold; his buried body causes blood to flow from a wounded myrtle. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the story of Polydorus concerns his being sent by Priam to Polymnestor for secret upbringing and then murdered after the Phrygians’ fortunes collapsed, with an alternative version in which Ilione raises him, Polymnestor accidentally kills his own son, and Polydorus later blinds Polymnestor.

Polydore in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Polydore first appears

Abstract written by a model

Polydore is the youngest son of Hecuba and Priam and brother of Polyxena. During danger to Troy, Priam secretly sends him, along with gold for his future support, to Polymestor for concealment, protection, and upbringing. Polymestor, entrusted with both the child and the gold, murders Polydore for the gold after Troy’s fall. His mutilated body is thrown into the sea and carried to the shore, remaining unburied until Hecuba discovers and buries him.

HECUBA.

Polydore is the youngest son of Priam and Hecuba. Priam secretly sent him with gold to Polymestor for protection, but Polymestor murdered him for the gold and threw his body into the sea. His body is being carried onto the shore, where he hopes to receive burial and fall into Hecuba’s hands.

FEMALE ATTENDANT, CHORUS, HECUBA.

Polydore was sent away from Troy by his father for protection and was placed with Polymestor, who was entrusted with his safety and with gold. Polymestor murdered him after learning of Troy's fall and cast his mutilated body into the sea.

POLYMESTOR, HECUBA, CHORUS.

Polydore is identified as the youngest of Priam's children and Hecuba's son. Priam sent him to Polymestor for protection and upbringing, but Polymestor killed him.

Polydore in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · about 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Polydore first appears

Abstract written by a model

Polydore is the youngest son of Priam and Hecuba. As a child and royal refugee, Priam secretly sends him to Polymestor in Thrace, entrusting him to the Thracian king’s protection and fostering and providing gold for his support. After Troy’s fall, Polymestor, Polydore’s supposed friend and host, murders him for the gold, also preventing him from restoring Troy. His body is cast into the sea and lies unburied on the Thracian shore, denied funeral rites. After death he appears as a ghost, seeking burial from Hecuba. A servant finds his corpse, Hecuba recognizes Polymestor’s crime, and Agamemnon permits Polydore’s remains to be buried with Polyxena.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

HECUBA.

Polydore’s ghost says that Priam secretly sent him to Polymestor in Thrace with gold for his support. Polymestor murdered him for the gold and cast his body into the sea; Polydore returns so that Hecuba may give him burial.

CHORUS.

Polydore is Priam’s son, secretly placed by his aged father with the Thracian king for protection. The attendant finds his corse on the shore, and Hecuba concludes that the Thracian king murdered him for gold.

AGAMEMNON, HECUBA, CHORUS.

Hecuba’s dead son had been sent to Polymestor for protection when Troy was destroyed. Polymestor murdered him for the gold and threw his mangled body into the sea, where a servant found it. Agamemnon agrees that his remains may be buried with Polyxena.

CHORUS.

Polydore was the youngest son of Priam and Hecuba, entrusted by his father to Polymestor for protection and upbringing. Polymestor murdered him after the fall of Troy so that he could retain Polydore’s gold and prevent him from restoring Troy.
