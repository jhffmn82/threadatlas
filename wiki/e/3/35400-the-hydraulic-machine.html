the hydraulic machine

object · 1 work · 5 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: This press, his machine, hydraulic press, hydraulic stamping machine, so powerful an engine, the hydraulic press, the machine, the machinery
  • In The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes: The hydraulic machine is a gigantic press at Stark’s property, with an iron trough floor, ceiling, lateral water cylinders, and a descending piston capable of many tons of force. Ostensibly defective and used to compress fuller’s-earth into bricks, it is inspected by Hatherley. In fact, the coining gang uses it to form an amalgam replacing silver in counterfeit half-crowns. Stark locks Hatherley inside and activates it, but he escapes through a concealed panel. The machine is later destroyed by fire, leaving twisted cylinders and iron piping.

the hydraulic machine in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

The hydraulic machine is a gigantic press at Stark’s property, with an iron trough floor, ceiling, lateral water cylinders, and a descending piston capable of many tons of force. Ostensibly defective and used to compress fuller’s-earth into bricks, it is inspected by Hatherley. In fact, the coining gang uses it to form an amalgam replacing silver in counterfeit half-crowns. Stark locks Hatherley inside and activates it, but he escapes through a concealed panel. The machine is later destroyed by fire, leaving twisted cylinders and iron piping.

IX. THE ADVENTURE OF THE ENGINEER’S THUMB

The hydraulic press at Stark’s property is said to have become defective and is the stated subject of Hatherley’s inspection. Stark claims that the press is used to compress fuller’s-earth into bricks for removal.

The hydraulic press is a gigantic machine capable of exerting enormous pressure, with an iron floor and ceiling and lateral water cylinders. Stark locks the narrator inside it and turns it on, causing the ceiling to descend until the narrator escapes through a concealed panel.
