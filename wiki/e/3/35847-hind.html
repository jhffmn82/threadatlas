hind

animal · 2 works · 1 quoted fact · 3 connections
Also called: a hind, hind substituted for Iphigenia

Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the hind is the animal that the Goddess gave in exchange for Iphigenia at the sacrifice; Agamemnon sacrificed it while believing that he was sacrificing Iphigenia. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the hind substituted for Iphigenia is placed in her position at the altar when Diana casts a mist before the participants; its death appeases Diana, allowing the Greek fleet to sail.
