Abstract written by a model

Creon’s royal palace contains the bridal room where Jocasta dies and becomes a site of concealed, unpurgeable horror: Eurydicè emerges from it, Haemon and Eurydicè’s bodies are brought or revealed there, and Oedipus is taken back inside.

CHORUS.

The palace is the place from which Eurydicè emerges and to which the Messenger suggests returning to discover what grief she conceals. Creon later brings Haemon’s corpse toward the palace, where Eurydicè’s body is revealed.

CHORUS.

The palace is described as hiding a horror that cannot be purged. Its bridal room contains Jocasta's body, and Oedipus is eventually taken back inside it.
