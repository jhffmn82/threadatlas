Marathon

place · 4 works · 6 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: MARATHON, forum at Marathon, plain, township of Marathon
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Marathon is a city in the Athenian dominions where the children of Hercules and Iolaus seek refuge at the altar of Jupiter.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Marathon is the territory under Athenian rule where Iolaus and the Heraclids seek refuge at the altars.
  • In Description of Greece, Marathon is a plain where the Persians landed and were defeated, commemorated by tombs, monuments, and other landmarks.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Marathon is the site of the victory whose spoil taken from Datis provided the basis for the Athenian treasuries at Delphi.

Marathon in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Marathon is a plain, said to be named from the hero Marathon, about equidistant from Athens and Carystus in Eubœa. Persians landed there and were defeated; the battle is depicted in the Painted Chamber. It honours Echetlæus and Hercules and preserves tombs and monuments for Athenians, Platæans, slaves, and Miltiades, along with a lake where fleeing Persians fell and a nearby cave of Pan.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIV.

Marathon is the plain from which the hero Marathon is said to have taken his name. The Painted Chamber depicts the battle there, including the Athenians, the Plataeans, Callimachus, Miltiades, and the hero Echetlus.

CHAPTER XXXI.

Marathon lies about equidistant from Athens and Carystus in Eubœa and was the place where the Persians landed and were defeated. It has the tomb of the Athenians who died in the battle, a monument to Miltiades, a monument for the Platæans and their slaves, a lake into which many fleeing Persians fell, and a cave of Pan on a nearby mountain.
