Elmsley in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Elmsley is a commentator whose notes focus on grammar, vocabulary, and textual criticism. He begins by reading φυγη, then argues for alternatives such as και and μογερα, approves Porson’s reading despite printing another, and ends by preferring Kalends for the final item.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

notes

Elmsley proposes, approves, or discusses numerous readings and interpretations in the notes, including φυγη, και, several passages concerning marriage, παντες, παυρον δε γενος, ανθηπτετο, νιν, and μενε και γηρας. He also explains grammatical constructions, vocabulary, and textual alternatives throughout the commentary.
