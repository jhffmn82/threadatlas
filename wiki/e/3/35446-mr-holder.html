Mr. Holder in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

Alexander Holder is a widowed banker and senior partner of Holder & Stevenson, father of Arthur and uncle and adoptive father of Mary. He owns a house at Streatham and Fairbank. After accepting the Beryl Coronet as security for a £50,000 advance, he takes it home and locks it in his dressing-room bureau. He later finds Arthur holding the damaged coronet, assumes Arthur stole it, and threatens police involvement. As Sherlock Holmes investigates, Holder learns that Mary betrayed him with Sir George Burnwell and that Arthur was innocent. Holmes recovers the three gems; Holder pays for them, rejoices, regrets his judgment, and prepares to apologize to Arthur while grieving Mary's flight.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

XI. THE ADVENTURE OF THE BERYL CORONET

Alexander Holder is the senior partner of Holder & Stevenson and the guardian of the Beryl Coronet after accepting it as security for a £50,000 loan. He takes the coronet from the bank to his house, locks it in his dressing-room bureau, and later sees Arthur holding the damaged coronet. Holder accuses Arthur of theft and threatens to involve the police.

“Do you receive much company?” he asked.

Mr. Holder believes he saw Arthur Holder holding the coronet and initially regards this as proof of Arthur’s guilt. After Sherlock Holmes recovers the three gems, Mr. Holder pays for them, rejoices at being saved, and learns that he owes Arthur an apology.

“For Heaven’s sake, tell me, then, what is this extraordinary mystery!”

Mr. Holder learns that Mary betrayed him with Sir George Burnwell and that Arthur was innocent of stealing the coronet. He regrets misjudging Arthur and prepares to apologize to him, while grieving over Mary's flight.
