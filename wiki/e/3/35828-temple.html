the temple in Iphigenia in Tauris

Euripides · -0413/-0411

The temple is a sanctuary on a barbaric, desolate sea-coast, housing Artemis’s divine image in a sacred shrine and an altar stained with sacrificial blood and the spoils of slain men. Iphigenia serves there as priestess. Orestes and Pylades arrive to steal the image, are freed under its holy rule, and preparations are made within. It becomes the site of a supposed purification as Iphigenia leads the strangers and image toward the sea, while Thoas attempts to answer their escape; Athena orders a new temple at Halae for the image.

Iphigenia in Tauris

The sanctuary stands on a barbaric, desolate sea-coast and contains an altar stained with blood and the spoils of slain men. Iphigenia serves there as priestess, and Orestes and Pylades come intending to enter it and steal the divine image.

The temple houses the image of Artemis and serves as the setting for the supposed purification. Thoas remains there to purge it with fire while Iphigenia leads the bound strangers and the image toward the sea.

CHORUS. [STROPHE.]

The temple in Tauris is the setting for the chorus, the Messenger’s report, and Thoas’s attempted response to the escape. Athena directs that a new temple be built at Halae for the image of Artemis.

temple of Artemis in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

The temple of Diana in Tauris is a sacred place containing an altar where Greek blood is shed and the image Orestes is commanded to take. Orestes and Pylades observe it, consider hiding nearby, and are brought there for rites prepared by Iphigenia, its gatekeeper and sacrificial priestess. She orders their release and sacrificial preparations, then plans to remove the image under a claim of purification from the strangers’ pollution, despite sacred keepers. Minerva finally directs that the image be taken to Athens and assigns Iphigenia to guard the Goddess’s temple at Brauron.
