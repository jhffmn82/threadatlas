Phaedra's love for Hippolytus in Hippolytus; The Bacchae

Euripides · -0427

Phaedra's secret, divinely caused passion for Hippolytus begins when she sees him in Athens. It wastes her away, drives her to fast and fear dishonor, and, despite her resistance and the Nurse's intervention, ends in her false accusation of him.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

HIPPOLYTUS

Phaedra's love for Hippolytus began when she saw him in Athens during his visit to the Mysteries. Aphrodite caused the love, which has led Phaedra to waste away, fast, and fear that her desire will bring dishonor.

love in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Love is Phaedra’s fierce, secret passion for Hippolytus, inflicted by Venus after she sees him. Though delightful and painful, it wounds her with shame, illness, fasting, and a wish to die despite her efforts at silence and chastity. It culminates in a false accusation that destroys Hippolytus and helps ruin Theseus’s household.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

HIPPOLYTUS.

Love afflicts Phaedra after she sees Hippolytus, according to Venus’s design. Phaedra’s love is described as fierce, secret, and destructive.

PHÆDRA, NURSE, CHORUS.

Love wounds Phædra and causes her to suffer shame, illness, fasting, and the desire to die. She says that she has tried to conquer love through silence and chastity, while the Nurse calls it a common affliction caused by Venus.

DIANA, THESEUS, CHORUS.

Phaedra fell in love with Hippolytus after being affected by Venus, the deity hostile to those who delight in virginity. Her passion led to the false accusation that destroyed Hippolytus and contributed to the ruin of Theseus's household.
