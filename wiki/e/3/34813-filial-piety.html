Also called: affection, duty, faithful, filial care, filial duty, filial love, holy shame, honor those who bear thee, honour him, love, love so great, loyalty to Heaven's eternal cause, piam, pietas

  • In Heroides and Amores, piety and filial duty lead Hypermestra to treat her refusal to kill Lynceus as an act of faithfulness rather than a crime, preferring accusation of guilt to pleasing her cruel father through murder.
  • In The Aeneid, filial love motivates Lausus to protect Mezentius through self-sacrifice, leading to Lausus’s death and Mezentius’s anguished recognition of his son’s sacrifice and his own guilt.
  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, filial piety is the duty to care for and honor one’s parents, motivating Electra’s remembrance and mourning of Agamemnon, her requests to Chrysothemis, and her prayers for Orestes to avenge him and purge family shame.
  • In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, filial duty is shown in Antigone and Ismene’s care for Oedipus, Oedipus’s condemnation of Polyneices and Eteocles for failing to honor him, and Polyneices’ request for burial from his sisters.

filial love in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Filial love motivates Lausus to protect Mezentius from Aeneas through self-sacrifice; Lausus dies, and Mezentius anguishedly recognizes his son’s sacrifice and his own guilt.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Meantime, by Jove’s impulse, Mezentius arm’d,

Lausus’s filial love leads him to shield Mezentius from Aeneas even at the cost of his own life. Mezentius recognizes this sacrifice with anguish, calling Lausus’s death the price of preserving him and blaming his own past guilt for staining his son’s name.

filial piety in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Filial piety is the duty to care for and honor one's parents, modeled by birds; it motivates Electra's sacred remembrance and mourning of Agamemnon, her request that Chrysothemis offer hair and maiden-zone, and prayers for Orestes to avenge him and purge family shame.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ELECTRA

Electra treats remembrance and mourning for Agamemnon as sacred duties of a daughter. She asks Chrysothemis to honor him with their own hair and maiden-zone and to pray for Orestes to avenge him.

_Enter_ CHRYSOTHEMIS.

The Chorus presents care for one's parents as a model of proper conduct, comparing human duty to the care birds provide for those who raised them. It praises Electra for remaining loyal to her father and for seeking to purge the family's shame.
