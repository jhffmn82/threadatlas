death in Hippolytus; The Bacchae

Euripides · -0427

Death is the feared and desired end of Phaedra’s suffering, love, and disgrace: she imagines dying without thought or knowledge, while Aphrodite foresees it in her vengeance against Hippolytus. Phaedra says she will die soon, the Chorus expects hanging in the bridal room, and the Nurse discovers her caught in a noose. Theseus and the women call it a bitter calamity for the household.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

HIPPOLYTUS

Phaedra's fasting and wasting seem likely to lead to death, and she wishes at moments that she could die without thought or knowledge. Aphrodite also foresees death as the outcome of Phaedra's suffering and vengeance against Hippolytus.

CHORUS

Phaedra repeatedly presents death as the best or only remaining response to her love and disgrace. She says that she will die soon, and the Chorus anticipates that her death will come by hanging in the bridal room.

Phaedra's death is discovered when the Nurse cries that the Queen is caught in a noose. Theseus calls it the bitterest outcome of his labours, while the women describe it as a calamity that threatens the entire household.
