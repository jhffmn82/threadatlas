Mr. Rucastle in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

Mr. Rucastle is the owner of the Copper Beeches, husband of Mrs. Rucastle, and father of Alice, a six-year-old son, and Edward. He employs Violet Hunter as governess under strange conditions: she must cut her hair, wear a blue dress, sit as directed, and face away from a window. Though often jovial, he is judged cunning and dangerous. His central scheme is to imprison Alice in a locked chamber and use Hunter to impersonate her, deceiving Mr. Fowler while coercing Alice to surrender control of her money. When Holmes and others confront him, Rucastle releases Carlo, the mastiff guarding the property; the starving dog attacks and mauls him. He survives permanently broken and is cared for by Mrs. Rucastle.

XII. THE ADVENTURE OF THE COPPER BEECHES

Jep hro Rucastle offers Violet Hunter a governess position at the Copper Beeches with a salary of £120 a year. He describes unusual household requirements, including wearing a particular dress, sitting where indicated, and cutting her hair short, and says that his wife wants these conditions.

By eleven o’clock the next day we were well upon our way to the old

Mr. Rucastle employs Miss Hunter and drives her from Winchester to the Copper Beeches. He is usually amiable, jovial, and boisterous, but he makes Miss Hunter wear a blue dress, sit facing away from the window, and entertain him; he also becomes angry when she approaches the forbidden suite.

Holmes and I had listened spellbound to this extraordinary story. My

Mr. Rucastle imprisoned his daughter Alice and brought Violet Hunter to the house to impersonate her so that Mr. Fowler would believe Alice was content and no longer wanted his attentions. He returns to confront Holmes and the others, then releases the starving dog, which attacks and horribly mauls him. He survives but remains a broken man, cared for by his devoted wife.
