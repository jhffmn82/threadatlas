Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, women are regarded as evil by Hippolytus and condemned by Phædra for hidden dishonor and adultery; Phædra fears womanhood and disgrace to her children, while the Chorus laments women’s unhappy fate amid accusations of danger and household loss. In Hecuba and other plays, women are presented as subject to a proposed law that any woman who betrays her husband should die, and Menelaus says Helen’s ruin should warn all women to be chaste.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

PHÆDRA, NURSE, CHORUS.

Phædra condemns women who appear chaste in speech while secretly acting dishonorably, and she calls the pollution of marriage with other men a hated evil. She also says that being a woman is hated by all and fears that her conduct could disgrace her children.

CHORUS, PHÆDRA.

Hippolytus calls women a great evil, blames them for draining household wealth, and says that even a clever woman is dangerous. The Chorus instead laments the unhappy fate of women.
