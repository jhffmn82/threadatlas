Theban citizens in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Theban citizens are a group subject to Creon’s royal authority and order, with stoning threatened for disobedience and malcontents said to resent his rule. They suffer a plague marked by death, failed crops, dying cattle, and fruitless labor; Oedipus promises relief by finding Laius’s murderer. Tiresias’s warning links that murderer to a true-born Theban. Later, Thebans are said to send Creon to bring Oedipus back to Thebes, under threat of war.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

ANTIGONE

Theban citizens are subject to Creon’s order and are threatened with stoning if they disobey it. Creon also speaks of malcontents among them who resent his rule.

CHORUS.

Creon claims that Antigone's disobedience threatens the authority of the State and says that he must not break his word before it. He maintains that whoever is just in ruling a household will also act rightly in the commonwealth.

KING OEDIPUS

The Theban people suffer from pestilence, with deaths, failing crops, dying cattle, and women undergoing fruitless labor. Oedipus promises to address their need by finding Laius's murderer.

[OEDIPUS _has entered during the Choral song_

Tiresias tells Oedipus to stop speaking to any Theban after Oedipus's own ordinance has condemned the murderer. The murderer is prophesied to be an alien believed to have come from elsewhere but ultimately certified as a true-born Theban.

_Enter_ THESEUS.

The Thebans are said to have sent Creon to bring Oedipus back to Thebes. Creon also threatens that harming him will bring war with Thebes.

the Theban commons in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912

The Theban people suffer plague, gather at altars, and seek Oedipus’s aid. Oedipus demands knowledge of Laius’s murder and exclusion of its murderer. Thebans later claim Oedipus back and mourn him; they support Antigone against Creon’s judgment and mourn her condemned death.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

OEDIPUS THE KING

The Theban people suffer from plague, gather at altars, and seek aid from Oedipus. Oedipus orders them to reveal anything they know about Laius’s murder and to deny the murderer shelter, speech, sacrifices, and rites.

[Enter CREON with attendants]

Creon says that the Thebans claim Oedipus back and that he has mourned Oedipus's misfortunes as his kinsman. Oedipus accuses Creon of acting for Thebes's advantage rather than out of genuine welcome.
