Phaedra's suicide by hanging in Hippolytus; The Bacchae

Euripides · -0427

Phaedra's suicide by hanging is her self-inflicted death in the Castle, intended to preserve her and her children's honour. After deciding to kill herself and considering how, she goes alone to her bridal room, ties a noose, and hangs herself; the women later see her body beneath the rafters.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS

Phaedra decides that she will kill herself, though she initially asks how she should die. The Chorus later describes the intended suicide as Phaedra going alone to her bridal room and placing her neck in a noose.

[_The Voice of the_ NURSE _is heard from within, crying,

Phaedra dies by a noose that she tied with her own hands. The women see her body hanging beneath the rafters, and the Nurse calls for a blade before it becomes clear that the Queen is already dead.

Phædra's suicide in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Phædra hangs herself with a noose from the bridal-chamber ceiling, devastating Theseus, confusing his house, and orphaning his children.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHORUS, PHÆDRA.

Phædra's suicide is carried out by hanging herself with a noose in her bridal chamber. Her death devastates Theseus and leaves his house in confusion and his children orphans.
