Phaedra's suicide by hanging

event · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Phaedra’s suicide, Phædra's suicide, a fair white neck shall creep to the noose, a high death-knot, a noose of death, a rope swing slow in the rafters’ gloom, death, die right soon, hanging, having quitted life, her own hand's knotting, strangled herself, the deed, this knot about her throat
and 2 more, to die, violent death

Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Phædra's suicide is her hanging by a noose from the bridal-chamber ceiling, devastating Theseus, confusing his house, and orphaning her children. In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, Phaedra's suicide by hanging is her self-inflicted death in the Castle, intended to preserve her and her children's honour; she ties a noose in her bridal room and hangs herself.

Phaedra's suicide by hanging in Hippolytus; The Bacchae

Euripides · 427 BC · the work’s page · read it where Phaedra's suicide by hanging first appears

Abstract written by a model

Phaedra's suicide by hanging is her self-inflicted death in the Castle, intended to preserve her and her children's honour. After deciding to kill herself and considering how, she goes alone to her bridal room, ties a noose, and hangs herself; the women later see her body beneath the rafters.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS

Phaedra decides that she will kill herself, though she initially asks how she should die. The Chorus later describes the intended suicide as Phaedra going alone to her bridal room and placing her neck in a noose.

[_The Voice of the_ NURSE _is heard from within, crying,

Phaedra dies by a noose that she tied with her own hands. The women see her body hanging beneath the rafters, and the Nurse calls for a blade before it becomes clear that the Queen is already dead.
