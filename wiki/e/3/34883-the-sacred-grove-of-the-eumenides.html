the sacred grove of the Eumenides

Abstract written by a model

In The Seven Plays in English Verse, the grove of the Eumenides is a sacred, forbidden sanctuary at Colonos where Oedipus rests near its shrine before being directed to make an offering from a permitted place. In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, the sacred grove is inviolable holy ground containing the Eumenides’ sanctuary and offerings, and becomes Oedipus’s destined resting-place.

Abstract written by a model

The sacred grove is inviolable, untrod holy ground containing the Eumenides’ sanctuary and ritual offerings. A wilderness of laurel, olive, vine, and nightingales, it becomes Oedipus’s destined resting-place despite the Chorus ordering him out.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

OEDIPUS AT COLONUS

The sacred grove is a wilderness of laurel, olive, and vine where nightingales sing. It is holy and normally not to be entered, but Oedipus sits there because he believes it is his destined resting-place. The Chorus orders him to leave its precincts.
