grove of the Eumenides in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

A sacred, forbidden sanctuary at Colonos receives Oedipus unknowingly; after he rests near its shrine, the Chorus directs him beyond it to make an offering from a permitted place.

2 sections; each heading opens the text

OEDIPUS AT COLONOS

The grove of the Eumenides is a sacred and forbidden place at Colonos. Oedipus enters it unknowingly, rests near its shrine, and is directed to move to a permitted place outside it.

_Enter_ ISMENE.

The grove received Oedipus when he first entered the land. The Chorus directs him to perform the offering beyond the grove.

the sacred grove of the Eumenides in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912

The sacred grove is inviolable, untrod holy ground containing the Eumenides’ sanctuary and ritual offerings. A wilderness of laurel, olive, vine, and nightingales, it becomes Oedipus’s destined resting-place despite the Chorus ordering him out.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

OEDIPUS AT COLONUS

The sacred grove is a wilderness of laurel, olive, and vine where nightingales sing. It is holy and normally not to be entered, but Oedipus sits there because he believes it is his destined resting-place. The Chorus orders him to leave its precincts.
