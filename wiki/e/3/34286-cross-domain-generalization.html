Cross-domain generalization

topic · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 0 connections
Also called: cross-domain applications, cross-domain generalization, cross-domain shift, cross-domain transfer, generalizability

Abstract written by a model

In ARES: An Automated Evaluation Framework for Retrieval-Augmented Generation Systems, cross-domain generalization is a strength within KILT and SuperGLUE when query or document types change, but it weakens for language, text-to-code, and retrieval-to-extraction shifts, with Kendall’s tau values of 0.33, 0.28, and 0.38. In VISA: Retrieval Augmented Generation with Visual Source Attribution, cross-domain generalization is evaluated on web and medical scientific papers with passages, tables, and figures; zero-shot transfer has reasonable bounding-box accuracy but trails in-domain effectiveness and omits scanned or handwritten documents.

Abstract written by a model

Cross-domain generalization is a strength of ARES within KILT and SuperGLUE when query or document types change, but it weakens for language, text-to-code, and retrieval-to-extraction shifts, with Kendall’s tau values of 0.33, 0.28, and 0.38.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Results & Analysis / 5.1 .. 5.4

ARES shows strong cross-domain generalization when query type, document type, or both change within KILT and SuperGLUE. Its performance is substantially weaker for shifts across languages, from text to code, and from text retrieval to entity, webpage, or citation extraction, with reported Kendall’s tau values of 0.33, 0.28, and 0.38 respectively.
