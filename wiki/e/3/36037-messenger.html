Messenger

person · 3 works · 2 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: I, MESS., MESSENGER, a Messenger, a Messenger from the Mountain, messenger, the Messenger, we

Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the messenger reports witnessing the Bacchae’s rites, miraculous sources of water and other substances, destruction of cattle, and victory over armed men on Cithaeron, and urges Pentheus to receive Bacchus as a powerful god. In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, the Messenger reports that the wild women performed miraculous acts, slaughtered cattle, defeated armed villagers, and ravaged nearby settlements, and urges Pentheus to accept Dionysus while warning that destroying the god would destroy joy and love. In The Bacchae of Euripides, Messenger comes from Kithaeron to report to Pentheus on the Bacchae’s worship, miracles, attacks on herds and villages, and victory over villagers, and urges Pentheus to honor Dionysus.
