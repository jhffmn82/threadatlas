Messenger

person · 3 works · 2 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: I, MESS., MESSENGER, a Messenger, a Messenger from the Mountain, messenger, the Messenger, we
  • In The Bacchae of Euripides, Messenger comes from Kithaeron to report to Pentheus on the Bacchae’s worship, miracles, attacks on herds and villages, and victory over villagers, and urges Pentheus to honor Dionysus.
  • In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, the Messenger reports that the wild women performed miraculous acts, slaughtered cattle, defeated armed villagers, and ravaged nearby settlements, and urges Pentheus to accept Dionysus while warning that destroying the god would destroy joy and love.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the messenger reports witnessing the Bacchae’s rites, miraculous sources of water and other substances, destruction of cattle, and victory over armed men on Cithaeron, and urges Pentheus to receive Bacchus as a powerful god.

Messenger in The Bacchae of Euripides

Euripides · -0406/-0405

Messenger comes from Kithaeron to report to Pentheus, describing the Bacchae’s worship, miracles, attacks on herds and villages, and victory over villagers, then urges Pentheus to honor Dionysus.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

_Enter_ PENTHEUS _in fury_.

The Messenger arrives from Kithaeron and gives Pentheus a detailed account of the Bacchae's conduct. He describes their worship, miraculous springs, destruction of herds and villages, and victory over armed villagers. He urges Pentheus to receive Dionysus in glory and let him live.
