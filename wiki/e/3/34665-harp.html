harp in Musical Instruments

Carl Engel · 1875-08

The harp is a more or less triangular stringed instrument among the Anglo-Saxons. Especially popular in central and northern Europe, it was favored by German and Celtic bards and Scandinavian skalds. Medieval forms include twelve strings and two sound holes, though some early forms lacked a front pillar. Small harps appear in the St. Georges de Boscherville orchestra, played by crowned musicians with fingers or a plectrum.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VII.

The harp was especially popular in central and northern Europe and was favored by German and Celtic bards and Scandinavian skalds. Medieval representations include a harp with twelve strings and two sound holes, while some early forms lacked the front pillar.

[Illustration] CHAPTER IX.

Small harps appear in the St. Georges de Boscherville orchestra, including one played by a crowned musician. Another crowned musician plays a harp with a plectrum in the right hand and the fingers of the left hand.
