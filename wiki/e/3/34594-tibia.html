CHAPTER IV.

The tibia was the Roman flute, originally made from a shin bone and having a mouth-hole and four finger-holes. Romans developed many varieties, including the tibia gingrina, tibia obliqua, tibia vasca, tibia longa, tibia curva, and tibia ligula, as well as single and double forms. Performers used a capistrum around the cheeks, and a Roman mosaic in the British museum shows a girl playing the tibia with its holmos or mouth-piece.
