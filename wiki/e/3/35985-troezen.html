Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Trœzene is Hippolytus’s homeland, where Theseus lives in banishment, Phaedra resides, and Pittheus raises Hippolytus. In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, Trozên is the setting and Hippolytus’s beloved youthful home, where Phaedra and Theseus reside, Hippolytus rejects Aphrodite, and Hippolytus is honored after his death. In The Library, Troezen is the city on Aegeus’s route to Athens where he lodged with Pittheus and encountered Aethra. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Trœzen honors Hippolytus after his death with a temple, a priest, annual sacrifices, and the hair-cutting rite of its young women. In Description of Greece, Troezen is a proud country whose people preserve temples, altars, monuments, sanctuaries, landmarks, and traditions, along with yearly sacrifices and local customs.

Trozên in Hippolytus; The Bacchae

Euripides · 427 BC · the work’s page · read it where Trozên first appears

Abstract written by a model

Trozên is the play’s setting, Pelops’ land, and Hippolytus’s beloved youthful home, with a broad plain and surrounding mountains, springs, woods, and shore. There, after Theseus’s exile, Phaedra and Theseus reside; Trozên’s daughters witness Phaedra’s illness and keep her secret. Hippolytus rejects Aphrodite, bids Trozên farewell, is destroyed and carried there after the wreck, and is honored through rites, songs, and maidens’ hair-cutting.

Trœzen in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Trœzen first appears

Abstract written by a model

Trœzen is a proud country with a remembered line of kings from Orus to Pittheus. Its people preserve temples, altars, monuments, sanctuaries, landmarks, and traditions for Artemis, Apollo, the Muses, Orestes, Hippolytus, Theseus, Athene, Poseidon, Aphrodite, Pan, Isis, Demeter, and others. Its customs unfold through yearly sacrifices, maidens’ offerings, Stonethrowing, Saronia, and routes through its surrounding district.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIX.

Troezen was described as a proud country whose traditions included the kings Orus, Althepus, Saron, Hyperes, Anthas, Aetius, Troezen, and Pittheus. Its people built and preserved many temples, altars, monuments, and traditions associated with Artemis, Apollo, the Muses, Orestes, and other figures.

CHAPTER XXXII.

Trœzen had numerous sanctuaries and landmarks connected with Hippolytus, Theseus, Athene, Poseidon, Aphrodite, Pan, Isis, Demeter, and other deities. Its people maintained customs including yearly sacrifices to Hippolytus, offerings by maidens before marriage, the festival called Stonethrowing for Lamia and Auxesia, and the Saronia festival for Artemis. The surrounding district contained places associated with Theseus, Hippolytus, Methana, and the route to Hermione.
