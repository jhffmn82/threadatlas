Alcestis's death in Alcestis

Euripides · -0437

Alcestis’s death is a voluntary substitution undertaken in place of Admetus, saving him. After giving final instructions and farewells to Admetus and their children, she dies and goes alone to the hungry dead. Her death overwhelms Admetus, the children, the Handmaid, the Servant, and the Chorus with grief; Admetus conceals it from Heracles, feels shame, rejects entering his house, and will not accept another wife. The Chorus celebrates her courage and love as worthy of lasting song, fame, and blessing.

Alcestis's death in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Alcestis's death is the destined death and self-sacrifice through which she accepts death in exchange for Admetus's life. At Admetus's palace, she gradually loses strength while bidding farewell to Admetus, their children, servants, and the sun. Her devotion to Admetus and their children brings profound grief to Admetus, the children, and the whole household. Admetus honors her death as exceptional but finds his own remaining life bitter and burdensome; Pheres cites it against him, the Chorus vows remembrance in song, and Hercules resolves to attempt her recovery.

ADMETUS, CHORUS.

Alcestis's death leaves Admetus convinced that his own life has become bitter and burdensome. He says that she has escaped future grief and toil, while he must continue living with the consequences of her loss.
