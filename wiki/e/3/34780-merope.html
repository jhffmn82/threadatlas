Merope

person · 3 works · 4 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Meropè, Queen Meropè, my mother, my mother, Dorian Meropè, the consort of your king, the maiden, wife of Polybus

Abstract written by a model

In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Merope is Polybus’s consort and queen of Corinth, believed by Oedipus to be his Dorian mother; fearing an oracle’s prophecy of incest with her, he flees Corinth, though she is only his believed mother. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Merope adopts Oedipus; believing her to be his mother, he leaves Corinth to escape the prophecy. In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, Merope is identified by Oedipus as his mother and as a Dorian.

Abstract written by a model

Merope is Polybus’s consort and queen of Corinth, believed by Oedipus to be his Dorian mother. Oedipus, fearing an oracle’s prophecy of incest with her, flees Corinth, though she is only his believed mother.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS.

Merope is identified by Oedipus as his Dorian mother. Oedipus fled Corinth because the oracle said that he would commit incest with his mother, though he believed Merope and Polybus were his true parents.

_Enter_ JOCASTA.

Merope is identified as Polybus's consort and the woman whom Oedipus believed to be his mother. Oedipus says that fear of the prophecy concerning her marriage-bed helped drive him into exile.

Abstract written by a model

Merope, Oenopion’s daughter and Polybus’s wife, adopts OEdipus; believing her his mother, he leaves Corinth to escape prophecy.

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter X

Merope, the wife of Polybus, adopted OEdipus because she and Polybus had no children. OEdipus believed that she was the mother named in the oracle and left Corinth to escape the prophecy.
