Sir Henry Baskerville in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901/1902

Sir Henry Baskerville is the young baronet, nephew and heir of Sir Charles Baskerville, the last representative of the Baskerville family, and inheritor of the Baskerville estate, title, Hall, and seven hundred and forty thousand pounds. Formerly farming in Canada and spending much of his time in America and Canada, he arrives in England to claim his inheritance. In London, after a warning to keep away from the moor and the strange theft and return of his boots, he insists on visiting Baskerville Hall despite danger. An unknown pursuer shadows him, and he asks Dr. John Watson to accompany him to Devonshire. At Baskerville Hall, Sir Henry is both eager to improve the place, including with electric lamps, and unsettled by its atmosphere and the moor. He investigates local concerns with Watson, confronts Barrymore over a telegram, learns the Barrymores have aided Selden, and is alarmed by the cry believed to be the Hound of the Baskervilles. He becomes strongly attracted to Miss Stapleton, proposes marriage, quarrels with Stapleton, and agrees to wait three months while remaining her friend, unaware that she is Stapleton's wife. Sir Henry is the intended victim of Stapleton's plot to gain the estate. Selden is first mistaken for him after wearing Sir Henry's discarded clothing, which draws the hound. Holmes then has Sir Henry dine with the Stapletons and walk home alone as bait to expose Stapleton. The hound attacks Sir Henry on the moor, but Holmes and the others rescue him before he is wounded. He is brought back to Baskerville Hall with a high fever and severe shock, though the doctors expect his illness to be temporary.

Narrative, section by section

18 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2.

Henry Baskerville is identified as the likely heir to Sir Charles Baskerville because he is the son of Sir Charles's younger brother. He was last heard of in America, and inquiries were being made to inform him of his inheritance.

Chapter 3.

Sir Henry Baskerville is the heir to Sir Charles Baskerville and the last of the Baskervilles. He has been farming in Canada and is due to arrive at Waterloo Station, after which Dr. Mortimer plans to take him to Holmes.

Chapter 4.

Sir Henry Baskerville meets Sherlock Holmes after receiving a warning telling him to keep away from the moor. He reports that one of a pair of new tan boots disappeared from outside his hotel room, and he insists that he will go to the home of his own people despite the possible danger. He asks for time to consider the information and invites Holmes and Dr. Watson to lunch.

We heard the steps of our visitors descend the stair and the bang

Baskerville leaves with Dr. Mortimer and is followed through the streets by an unknown person. Holmes concludes that Baskerville has been closely shadowed since arriving in town, including after choosing the Northumberland Hotel.

Chapter 5.

Sir Henry Baskerville is angered by the disappearance of one brown boot and one black boot, though the brown boot later reappears under a cabinet. He plans to go to Baskerville Hall at the end of the week and asks Dr. Watson to accompany him. He has not yet made a will and says that the money should remain with the title and estate.

Chapter 6.

Sir Henry Baskerville travels with Dr. Watson and Dr. Mortimer to Devonshire to take possession of Baskerville Hall. He is delighted by the Devon scenery and eager to see the moor, but the atmosphere of Baskerville Hall makes him uneasy. He plans to install powerful electric lamps along the dark avenue.

Chapter 7.

Sir Henry Baskerville agrees that the gloomy impression of Baskerville Hall may have been caused partly by fatigue and the journey. He remains at the Hall after breakfast to examine papers while Dr. Watson investigates the neighborhood.

A short walk brought us to it, a bleak moorland house, once the

Sir Henry is the new member of the Baskerville family living at Baskerville Hall. Beryl is distressed that he has come to the moor and believes he will face danger there, while Stapleton intends to visit him.

Chapter 8.

Sir Henry Baskerville becomes strongly attracted to Miss Stapleton and repeatedly refers to her after meeting her. He questions Jack Stapleton about supernatural interference in human affairs, confronts Barrymore about the test telegram, and works with Watson on a plan of campaign.

Chapter 9.

Sir Henry agrees with Dr. Watson to watch Barrymore at night, but later insists on going alone onto the moor. He meets Miss Stapleton, proposes marriage to her, quarrels with Stapleton over the relationship, and agrees to let the matter rest for three months while cultivating her friendship.

So there is one of our small mysteries cleared up. It is

Sir Henry Baskerville helps Watson confront the Barrymores and learns that they have been assisting Selden. He joins Watson in trying to capture Selden, becomes frightened by the cry on the moor, and believes it may have been the Hound of the Baskervilles.

Chapter 10.

Sir Henry is shaken by the sound on the moor and argues that Selden remains a danger to isolated houses. He agrees not to inform the police about Selden after Barrymore promises that the convict will soon be sent to South America, and he becomes interested in tracing the woman identified as L. L.

Chapter 12.

Sir Henry Baskerville is identified as the intended target of the murder plot. Sherlock Holmes warns Dr. John Watson that Stapleton may strike against him before the investigation is complete.

A terrible scream—a prolonged yell of horror and anguish—burst

Sir Henry Baskerville is initially believed to be the man killed on the moor. It is learned that his discarded boots, shirt, cap, and other clothing had been passed to Selden, whose wearing of them apparently caused the hound to pursue him.

“We’re at close grips at last,” said Holmes as we walked together

Sir Henry is expected to dine with Stapleton and the others the next day. Holmes wants him kept unaware of the hound so that he will have a better nerve for the ordeal and will attend the dinner alone.

Chapter 13.

Sir Henry Baskerville agrees to follow Holmes's instructions despite feeling deserted. He is to dine with the Stapletons, send back his trap, and walk home from Merripit House along the straight path to the Grimpen Road.

Chapter 14.

Sir Henry Baskerville is left in the dining-room with Stapleton and later walks onto the moor. The hound attacks him, but Holmes and the others rescue him before he is wounded; afterward, he is taken to Baskerville Hall and develops a high fever.

Chapter 15.

Sir Henry was the heir whom Stapleton intended to kill in order to obtain the Baskerville estate. Holmes used him as apparently unprotected bait, and although he suffered a severe shock after encountering the hound, the doctors expected his condition to be temporary.
