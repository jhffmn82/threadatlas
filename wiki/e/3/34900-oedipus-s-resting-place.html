Abstract written by a model

In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Oedipus's resting-place is a sacred, secret spot in Athens whose secrecy protects Athens from enemies. In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, Oedipus’s prophesied final resting-place is the sacred grove of the Eumenides, where his suffering ends in a divinely caused, painless death. In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, Oedipus’s secret burial place is a hidden, sacred tomb in a foreign land whose location Theseus must conceal.

Oedipus’s prophesied final resting-place in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912 · the work’s page · read it where Oedipus’s prophesied final resting-place first appears

Abstract written by a model

Oedipus’s prophesied final resting-place is the sacred grove of the Eumenides, the distant land Apollo foretold where his long suffering would end in rest. Oedipus recognizes it through the promised signs and circumstances. Repeated thunder and lightning signal that his appointed end has come, and he goes toward Hades. After a divine summons, the gods cause his painless death; an unseen power takes him away, and only Theseus knows exactly how.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

OEDIPUS AT COLONUS

Oedipus says that Apollo foretold a distant land where, after his long suffering, he would find rest. He recognizes the grove of the Eumenides as that place because the promised signs and circumstances have led him there.

[Enter ANTIGONE and ISMENE with THESEUS]

Oedipus interprets the repeated thunder and lightning as divine signs that his appointed end has come. He tells Theseus that he will soon die and sets out toward the place where he is to enter Hades.

[Enter MESSENGER]

Oedipus's death occurs after a divine summons and is described as painless and without wailing, disease, fire, or a whirlwind. He is taken away by an unseen power, possibly a messenger from heaven or a gentle opening of the earth. Only Theseus knows the exact manner of his death.

Oedipus’s secret burial place in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912 · the work’s page · read it where Oedipus’s secret burial place first appears

Abstract written by a model

Oedipus’s secret burial place is a hidden, sacred tomb in a foreign land. As he prepares to end his life, Oedipus leads Theseus there and commands him to conceal its location except from a chosen heir, promising the secret will protect Theseus and his land. Theseus guards it accordingly: Antigone and Ismene cannot approach or perform rites. Thebes fears the tomb could curse it, yet cannot bury Oedipus because of kinship blood-guilt.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

[Enter ISMENE]

Ismene reports that Oedipus's tomb could bring a curse upon Thebes if he dies outside its control. Thebes therefore wants him kept near its land, though it cannot bury him there because kinship blood-guilt forbids it.

[Enter ANTIGONE and ISMENE with THESEUS]

Oedipus tells Theseus that he will lead him to a place where he must end his life. He orders Theseus to keep the place and its location secret from everyone except his chosen heir, saying that the secret will protect Theseus and his land.

[Enter MESSENGER]

Oedipus's tomb is a hidden, sacred burial place that Antigone and Ismene are forbidden to approach. Theseus refuses to lead them there because he promised Oedipus that no one would come near it or perform funeral rites.
