laws of Greece

topic · 2 works · 1 quoted fact · 2 connections
Also called: Athenian law against executing captured enemies, decision, law, laws of the Greeks, mandates of th’ Athenian realm, not any one whom they have taken alive in battle

Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the Athenian law against executing captured enemies forbids the death of Eurystheus because he was captured alive. In Hecuba and other plays, the laws of Greece forbid the death of Eurystheus after his capture alive in battle.

Abstract written by a model

forbids death of Eurystheus [because he was captured alive] The leaders of Athens hold that an enemy captured alive must not be put to death. Eurystheus says that killing him would bring pollution under the laws of the Greeks, and the messenger says that the city has released him for this reason.
