Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Medea's revenge is the punishment of Jason for abandoning her through poisoned gifts, charms against Creon, and the killing of her children. In Medea, Medea's revenge is a planned retaliation for Jason's betrayal and her exile, unfolding through a plan to kill his new bride and their children and leave him childless and devastated.

Medea's revenge in Medea

Euripides · 430 BC · the work’s page · read it where Medea's revenge first appears

Abstract written by a model

Medea’s revenge is a planned retaliation for Jason’s betrayal and her exile. She confides in Corinthian women, demands secrecy, and warns of a wronged woman’s violence. It unfolds as a plan to kill Jason’s new bride and their children, leaving Jason childless and devastated.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

MEDEA

Medea tells the Corinthian women that she seeks a way to requite Jason for his betrayal. She asks them not to reveal her intentions and declares that a woman deprived of her rights in love may become a bloodier spirit than anyone expects.

CHORUS.

Medea’s revenge is directed against Jason, his new bride, and the household that has caused her exile. She plans to kill the royal bride and the children so that Jason will be left childless and suffer the greatest possible grief.
