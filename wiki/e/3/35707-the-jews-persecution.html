the Jews' persecution

Abstract written by a model

The Jews’ persecution is a long pattern of exploitation and violence: restrictions, distinctive dress, torture, robbery, ritual-crime accusations, forced baptism, burnings, massacres, expulsions, and exile. It disperses Jews and is tied to Europe’s treatment of them as exceptional rather than comparable to other persecuted peoples. Though it could foster bitterness or isolation, the record emphasizes the virtues Jews preserved.

XVII.

The Jews’ persecution is connected to Europe’s failure to compare similar national situations and its tendency to treat the Jews as exceptional. The text argues that the national memories of persecuted peoples should evoke sympathy and that persecutors may themselves claim blessings derived from national memory.

But to consider a people whose ideas have determined the religion of half the wo

Jewish persecution included restrictions on agriculture and handicrafts, compulsory distinctive dress, torture, robbery, accusations of ritual crimes, forced baptism, burning, massacres, and mass expulsions. The text argues that persecution could foster bitterness, isolation, deceitful conformity, or vice, but expresses greater wonder at the virtues the Jews preserved under such conditions.
