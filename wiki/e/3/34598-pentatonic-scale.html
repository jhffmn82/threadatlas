pentatonic scale in Musical Instruments

Carl Engel · 1875-08

The pentatonic scale is a five-tone scale, heard on a pianoforte by omitting f and b from C major or playing successive black keys from f-sharp. It is presented as a deliberately organized interval system in Aztec, Mexican, and Peruvian instruments, including the pito, huayra-puhura, pipe, and syrinx. It is also associated with Peruvian cachua, Scotch national dances, Chinese instruments, and ancient Asian peoples.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER V.

The pentatonic scale is described as a five-tone scale. Its character can be heard on a pianoforte by omitting f and b from the scale of C major, or by playing the black keys successively from f-sharp to the next f-sharp.

CHAPTER VI.

The pentatonic scale characterized the intervals of the Aztec pito and the tones of a Peruvian huayra-puhura from a tomb at Arica. The text presents this arrangement as evidence that these instruments' intervals were deliberately organized.
