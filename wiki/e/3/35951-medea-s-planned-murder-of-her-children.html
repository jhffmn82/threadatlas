Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, it is Medea's murder of her children to make Jason suffer before she flees Corinth. In Medea, it is the killing of Medea's sons by their mother with a sword to wound Jason and deny her enemies mockery. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, it is Medea's murder of her children by cutting their throats, except for one who escaped.

Medea's planned murder of her children in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Medea's planned murder of her children first appears

Abstract written by a model

Medea, driven by anger and revenge over Jason's new marriage, plans and carries out the murder of her children with her own hand to make Jason suffer. Though she knows the act is dreadful and briefly considers sparing them, she decides they must die before fleeing Corinth. She kills them with a sword, tells Jason why, and plans burial rites and sacrifices.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

JASON, MEDEA, CHORUS.

Medea declares that she will kill her children so that Jason will suffer most severely and cannot see them alive. She recognizes that the deed is dreadful but intends to complete it before fleeing Corinth.

JASON, MEDEA, CHORUS.

Medea debates whether to spare her sons but decides that they must die rather than be insulted by her enemies. The sons call for help, yet she kills them with the sword.

JASON, CHORUS.

The children's murder is revealed to Jason by the Chorus and is attributed to Medea. Medea says she committed it because Jason dishonored her bed and entered a new marriage, and she plans to perform burial rites and sacrifices to expiate the murder.

the killing of Medea's children in Medea

Euripides · 430 BC · the work’s page · read it where the killing of Medea's children first appears

Abstract written by a model

The killing of Medea's sons by their mother is perpetrated by Medea with a sword. She resolves to kill her children to wound Jason and deny her enemies mockery, briefly hesitates, then sends them inside and kills them. Their cries are heard outside, leaving Jason broken and childless.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS.

Medea repeatedly declares that she will kill her children in order to wound Jason and prevent her enemies from mocking them. She briefly considers abandoning the plan when she sees them, but finally sends them inside and maintains that the killing cannot be escaped.
