Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Orestes' madness is caused by the murder of Clytemnestra and pursuit by the Eumenides, bringing memory loss, violent agitation, visions of attacking Furies, alternating fits and weakness, and an attack on cattle in imagined self-defense. In Iphigenia in Tauris, Orestes' madness began after the bloodshed he refuses to name, led him through exile and suffering, and continued as some of the Furies pursued him despite his being freed from death at Athens. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Orestes’s madness is caused by the pursuit of Orestes by the Furies of Clytæmnestra after he murdered his mother; it began at Maniæ, was associated with his gnawing off a finger, and ended at Ace when the Eumenides appeared white to him.

Orestes' madness in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Orestes' madness first appears

Abstract written by a model

Orestes' madness, caused by the murder of Clytemnestra and pursuit by the Eumenides, brings memory loss, violent agitation, and visions of attacking Furies. Heaven-inflicted fits alternate with sleep, weakness, grief, and awareness. Near the sea, he attacks cattle in imagined self-defense, collapses, recovers, and is captured, while Pylades aids him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

ORESTES.

Orestes' madness causes him to lose his memory, become violently agitated, and see the Eumenides as attacking figures. His fits alternate with periods of sleep, weakness, grief, and restored awareness.

PYLADES, ORESTES, CHORUS.

Orestes is described as overcome by heaven-inflicted madness and as a prey to the Eumenides. He fears that the goddesses may stop him, and Pylades takes responsibility for assisting him despite the danger.

IPHIGENIA IN TAURIS.

Orestes becomes violently mad near the sea, seeing a Fury and believing that it is attacking him. He kills cattle while imagining that he is defending himself, then collapses and is captured after recovering his senses.
