the disappearance investigation's false murder charge in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

The supposed murder charge treats Hugh Boone as Neville St. Clair’s killer, alleging that Boone pushed him through a window, removed his clothes, weighted his coat with coins, and let the river carry away the body. It begins after blood and missing clothing suggest murder, but ends when Holmes reveals that Neville St. Clair had been disguised as Boone; Bradstreet then recognizes that no murder occurred.

The working hypothesis is that Hugh Boone thrust Neville St. Clair through the window, removed his clothes, and weighted Neville's coat with coins so it would sink. The hypothesis suggests that the body was then carried away by the river while Boone had no time to dispose of the other garments.

The supposed murder charge results from Neville St. Clair's decision to remain disguised as Hugh Boone after his wife saw him. Once Holmes reveals his identity, Inspector Bradstreet recognizes that no murder has occurred.
