Sir George Burnwell in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

Sir George Burnwell is Arthur Holder’s older friend, a ruined gambler with a cynical manner, an evil reputation among women, and a dangerous influence on Arthur. Though Alexander Holder admires his charm, he distrusts him. Burnwell, Mary’s lover, manipulates her into the coronet theft; Arthur wounds him in the struggle. Burnwell possesses the stolen gems, sells the three missing stones to a receiver, and Holmes later forces this disclosure.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

XI. THE ADVENTURE OF THE BERYL CORONET

Sir George Burnwell is Arthur Holder's older friend and a man whose influence repeatedly draws Arthur back into dangerous company. Alexander Holder admires Burnwell's charm but distrusts his cynical speech and the look in his eyes.

“For Heaven’s sake, tell me, then, what is this extraordinary mystery!”

Sir George Burnwell is identified as a ruined gambler and dangerous villain who manipulates Mary into helping steal the coronet. Arthur wounds him during the struggle, and Holmes later forces him to reveal that he sold the three missing stones to a receiver.
