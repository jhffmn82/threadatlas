the audience in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

The imagined audience is a hazy, far-off, multitudinous assemblage, nearer in idiom than the Cherokees and more numerous than the One. Its imagined approval turns the speaker's reticence into garrulous writing; a distinct member would inhibit it.

The imagined audience is hazy, distant, and multitudinous, making an approving chorus to the speaker's sentences and paragraphs. A distinct audience member would be critical and would inhibit the speaker.
