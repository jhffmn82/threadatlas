Phrygia

place · 4 works · 3 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Dardania, Phrygian, Phrygian city, Phrygians, city of the Phrygians, greater Phrygia, the Phrygian mountains, Δαρδανίαν, Φρυγίαν

Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Phrygia is represented through the Phrygian attendants and the destroyed city of Ilion, while a Phrygian narrator laments the ruin of his country and the defeat of its people by the Greeks. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Phrygia is described as the birthplace of Cybele, whose worship the Phrygians later established at Pessinunte. In The Library, Dardania was the name Dardanus gave to the whole country after Teucer died. In The Library, Ilus went to Phrygia, won a wrestling contest held by its king, and followed the dappled cow to the hill of the Phrygian Ate, where he founded Ilium. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Phrygia is a kingdom and region ruled by Midas, where Gordius became king, Rhea cured Bacchus, and Jupiter and Mercury visited Baucis and Philemon.
