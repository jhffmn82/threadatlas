bad-tempered man in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

A bad-tempered man may have good feelings, do good deeds, and be fundamentally well disposed, yet repeated temper makes his conduct ugly and undermines character. Apologies, amends, friendliness, and promises cannot erase injuries; only a substantial lasting benefit to present and future generations could compensate.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

VI.

A bad-tempered man may possess good feelings, perform good deeds, and have a fundamentally good disposition, but his behaviour remains ugly when temper repeatedly causes harm. He cannot make past injuries harmless through later excessive friendliness or promises of future gentleness. If he wants to compensate on a larger scale, he must prove himself a substantial benefit to contemporary and future generations through a beneficent discovery, stimulating work of genius, or powerful process.
