Abstract written by a model

A carillon is a musical instrument made of differently sized, tuned bells, popular in the Netherlands and Belgium and found in Germany and Italy. It may have thirty-seven bells, hand keys and pedals spanning over three octaves, and is played, often by a church organist, or operated by clockwork. Its distant sound is its central purpose.

CHAPTER X.

The carillon consists of a set of differently sized and tuned bells and was especially popular in the Netherlands and Belgium, though it also appeared elsewhere in Europe. It could be operated by clockwork or by a performer using manual keys and pedals; its sound was intended to be heard from a distance, since rapid passages could become confused when heard nearby.
