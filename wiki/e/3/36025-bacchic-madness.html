Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Bacchic madness seizes the women of Cadmus's house, drives them to the mountains, and can bring prophetic insight. In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, Bacchic madness overshadows Pentheus, clouds his reason, and fills him with excited, violent, and deluded thoughts. In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, Bacchic frenzy drives Cadmus's daughters to Kithaeron, where they mistake Pentheus for a wild beast and attack him, before it clears when Cadmus reveals the head Agave carries. In The Bacchae of Euripides, Bacchic frenzy spreads among the women of Thebes, deepens Pentheus's madness, and later possesses Agave and the Maenads.

the Bacchic frenzy in The Bacchae of Euripides

Euripides · 406 to 405 BC · the work’s page · read it where the Bacchic frenzy first appears

Abstract written by a model

Bacchic frenzy, a widespread disturbance in Thebes likened by Pentheus to a smothered fire, spreads among women, mocks the city, and deepens Pentheus’s madness into disguise. It later possesses Agave and Maenads, doubling Pentheus’s visions and preventing Agave from recognizing or sparing him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

_Enter_ PENTHEUS _in fury_.

Pentheus describes the Bacchic frenzy as a smothered fire that has spread through the land and made the women mock Thebes. Dionysus invokes divine power behind the frenzy and works to deepen Pentheus's madness until he accepts the disguise.

CHORUS.

Bacchic madness overshadows Pentheus as he sees the sun, Thebes, and the Wall of Seven Gates doubled and imagines horns on Dionysus. It later possesses Agave and the other Maenads, preventing Agave from recognizing or sparing her son.
