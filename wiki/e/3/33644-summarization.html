Retrieval Augmented Generation and Representative Vector Summarization for Large Unstructured Textual Data in Medical Ed

Summarization of large documents is difficult because the content may exceed a model's context window, and performance can decline as context length increases. Large contexts can also worsen the Lost in the Middle problem, in which facts in the middle receive less attention than information at the beginning and end.

2 / 2.1 .. 2.2.2

Summarization selects a token-budget-limited set of representative chunks and then reduces those chunks further through keyword generation and mapping. This process is used because the representative text may still exceed the model's context window.

Abstract written by a model

Summarization is a method for compressing retrieved documents in the RAG pipeline, reducing redundant information and prompt length to speed inference. It can be extractive, selecting important sentences, or abstractive, synthesizing multiple documents. It is the final module in iterative optimization, evaluated on NQ, TriviaQA, and HotpotQA. Tested with Recomp and LongLLMLingua, Recomp was preferred for preserving original-content integrity; removal is investigated and can reduce latency when length constraints do not limit the generator.

Abstract written by a model

Document summaries serve as pseudo-instructions in PIC for semantic document chunking. A summarizer creates a concise, informative summary that preserves a document’s key information and main theme, while also providing an embedding for sentence comparisons. PIC uses this summary to group sentences by relevance to the central theme and potential user instructions. Analysis finds that summary-based pseudo-instructions outperform random summaries, randomly selected sentences, and mean sentence embeddings, though limitations are noted in the conclusions.
