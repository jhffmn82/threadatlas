Abstract written by a model

In Alcestis, Admetus's house is desolate after Alcestis's death, but becomes fortunate again when she returns and Admetus leads her inside. In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Admetus's palace is his residence and household, where Apollo serves, Alcestis prepares to die, Death comes to claim her, Hercules is received during mourning, and the house becomes widowed and deserted after Alcestis's death.

Admetus's palace in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Admetus's palace first appears

Abstract written by a model

Admetus's palace is his residence and household, protected by Apollo and marked by hospitality. Apollo served there; Alcestis prepares to die there, and Death comes to claim her. At first its silence and missing funeral preparations leave the Chorus unsure of her condition. During mourning, it receives Hercules because Admetus hides the calamity. After Alcestis's death, it becomes a widowed, deserted house haunted by her empty places and grieving children.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

ALCESTIS.

Admetus's palace is the place where Apollo served, Alcestis prepares for death, and Death arrives to claim her. Its silence and lack of ordinary funeral preparations initially leave the Chorus uncertain about Alcestis's condition.

CHOR. Desist, for the present calamity is sufficient; and do not, O son,

The house of Admetus receives Hercules as a guest even while it is suffering the death of Alcestis. Its servants mourn Alcestis, while Admetus conceals the calamity from Hercules so that he will not be turned away.

ADMETUS, CHORUS.

Admetus's house has become a widowed and deserted place filled with reminders of Alcestis, including her empty bed, seat, and household floor. Admetus fears entering it because his children will mourn their mother there.
