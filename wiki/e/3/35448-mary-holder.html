Mary Holder in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

Mary Holder is Alexander Holder’s niece and adopted daughter, a quiet young woman of four-and-twenty who capably manages his household as his right hand. Though Arthur Holder twice proposes to her, she refuses, while remaining affectionate toward him and strongly maintaining his innocence. She notices Lucy Parr’s return through the back door and checks the house’s security. Secretly in love with Sir George Burnwell, Mary tells him about the coronet and brings it to him, making her involved in its theft. After the coronet is recovered, she faints, leaves Fairbank permanently in a note, and flees with Sir George.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

XI. THE ADVENTURE OF THE BERYL CORONET

Mary Holder is Alexander Holder's niece and adopted daughter, whom he describes as gentle, capable, and his right hand in the household. She has twice refused Arthur's marriage proposals, notices that Lucy Parr has returned through the back door, and checks that the house is secured.

“Do you receive much company?” he asked.

Mary Holder insists that Arthur Holder is innocent and asks Mr. Holder to have him liberated. After Mr. Holder says that Arthur might have been saved if he had married her, Mary leaves Fairbank and writes that she will not be happy under his roof and that he should not search for her.

“For Heaven’s sake, tell me, then, what is this extraordinary mystery!”

Mary secretly meets Sir George Burnwell at the hall window and tells him about the coronet. She brings it to him, faints when the coronet is recovered, and flees with Sir George.
