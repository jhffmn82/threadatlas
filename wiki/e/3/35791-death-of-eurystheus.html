Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the death of Eurystheus is ordered by Alcmena for his many wrongs, but the text does not narrate its completion. In Hecuba and other plays, the death of Eurystheus is intended by Alcmene as revenge, ordered despite Athens's rule against killing a captive taken alive, and accepted by Eurystheus as bringing Athens the oracle's foretold protection.
