death of Eurystheus

event · 2 works · 1 quoted fact · 2 connections
Also called: breathless corse, destroy me, die, dying, having slain him, perish, perish miserably, put him to death, slain, slay him
  • In Hecuba and other plays, the death of Eurystheus is intended by Alcmene as revenge, ordered despite Athens's rule against killing a captive taken alive, and accepted by Eurystheus as bringing Athens the oracle's foretold protection.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the death of Eurystheus is ordered by Alcmena for his many wrongs, but the text does not narrate its completion.
