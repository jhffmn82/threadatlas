Medea's banishment in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Medea's banishment first appears

Abstract written by a model

Medea's banishment is Creon's decree that Medea and her children leave Corinth, dishonored. Although Jason accepts the decision and offers aid that Medea rejects, she secures a one-day delay from Creon and seeks permission for the children to remain. She uses the postponement to arrange escape and carry out revenge, then prepares to flee.

Medea's exile in Medea

Euripides · 430 BC · the work’s page · read it where Medea's exile first appears

Abstract written by a model

Medea’s exile is a one-day, swift banishment from Corinth, ordered by Creon under royal authority and imposed through Jason. Creon commands Medea and her two children to leave and never return. Medea accepts but gains a day to seek counsel and comfort the children. Facing loss of home and feared hostility after the murders, she refuses Jason’s aid, secures Aegeus’s refuge in Athens, and tries unsuccessfully to keep the children from being separated from her.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

MEDEA

Creon declares that Medea and her two children must leave Corinth and not return. Medea accepts the exile but asks for one day to find counsel and comfort for the children.

MEDEA.

Medea says that Jason has granted her one long day before exile, giving her time to act. She fears that after the murders no friend, land, or trustworthy door will shelter her from her enemies.

_Enter from the right_ JASON.

Medea and her children are to be driven from Corinth without the security of their home. Jason offers provisions and assistance for their exile, but Medea refuses his gifts and instead obtains Aegeus's promise of refuge in Athens.

CHORUS.

Medea agrees to leave Corinth and describes herself as going into exile. She tries to secure permission for the children to remain, while recognizing that she will be separated from them.
