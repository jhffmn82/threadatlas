the Servant

person · 2 works · 8 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: SERV., SERVANT, a henchman, a henchman's office, domestics, servant of Admetus
  • In Alcestis, the Servant is a slave attendant in Admetus's household who serves its hospitality, loves Alcestis like a mother, obeys orders to conceal mourning from Heracles, and angrily reveals Alcestis's death and grieves her loss.

the Servant in Alcestis

Euripides · -0437

A slave attendant in Admetus's household, the Servant serves its hospitality and loves Alcestis like a mother. While obeying orders to conceal mourning from Heracles, the Servant angrily reveals Alcestis's death and grieves her loss.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

[_As the song ceases there enters a stranger, walking strongly, but

The Servant angrily condemns Heracles for feasting and singing while the household mourns, though he admits that Admetus ordered the grief to be concealed from the guest. He reveals to Heracles that the dead woman is Alcestis and expresses his own love and grief for her.

servant of Admetus in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

A servant in Admetus's household obeys orders to host Hercules despite resenting his revelry during mourning for Alcestis. He ultimately tells Hercules that Alcestis has died and directs him to her tomb.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHOR. Desist, for the present calamity is sufficient; and do not, O son,

The servant resents Hercules for reveling while Alcestis is mourned, but explains that he had been ordered by Admetus to provide hospitality. He tells Hercules that Alcestis has died and directs him toward her tomb.
