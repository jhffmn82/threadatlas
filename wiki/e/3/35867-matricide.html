matricide in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where matricide first appears

Abstract written by a model

Matricide is Orestes' killing of Clytæmnestra, carried out under Apollo's oracle to avenge Agamemnon, with his sister aware of the resulting pollution. Orestes defends it as necessary, but Tyndarus calls it worse than Clytæmnestra's crime. The act brings blood pollution, madness, exile from Greece, and a purported purification arranged by Iphigenia.

the planned matricide in The Electra of Euripides

Euripides · 419 to 409 BC · the work’s page · read it where the planned matricide first appears

Abstract written by a model

The planned matricide is Orestes' proposed killing of Clytemnestra, Agamemnon's murderer. It is the story's central moral horror: a sin even if commanded by a god, yet treated by Aeschylus as a duty restoring Agamemnon. Orestes fears the oracle's charge while Electra urges the killing; Euripides condemns it and emphasizes its destructive aftermath.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Introduction[1]

Matricide is treated as the central moral horror of the story. Aeschylus presents it as both sinful and divinely required, while Euripides concludes that it should not have been committed and portrays its aftermath as psychologically destructive.

CHORUS.

The planned matricide is the proposed killing of Clytemnestra by Orestes. Orestes questions whether the oracle commanding it was an evil charge and fears the sin, while Electra insists that killing her would restore their father.
