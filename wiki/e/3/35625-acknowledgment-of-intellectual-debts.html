acknowledgment of intellectual debts in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

A topic arguing that acknowledgment of intellectual debts should be treated as a moral obligation like debts of honour, even when public opinion cannot enforce it. It criticizes unnamed use of fellow-workers’ ideas, while ending with caution that omissions may arise from forgetfulness rather than deliberate theft.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

XI.

The unit argues that acknowledgment should extend to intellectual debts that are unlikely to be detected or enforced. It compares such recognition to traditional susceptibility concerning debts of honour and criticizes the failure to name fellow-workers whose ideas have been used. At the same time, it cautions that a person’s failure to acknowledge a source may result from ordinary forgetfulness rather than deliberate theft, since what matters intensely to the originator may have little lasting importance to the borrower.
