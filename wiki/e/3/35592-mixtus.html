Abstract written by a model

Mixtus is a wealthy commercial man whose growing wealth supports a London mansion kept as a little palace and liberal spending. Known among fashionable acquaintances as a rich man, he chiefly distinguishes himself through genial hospitality and usefulness. In youth he was religious and philanthropic, visiting the poor as an exhorter and helper and attending Mr Apollos’s lectures; he later married Scintilla despite opposing ideals. He values knowledge and collects works on colonisation, supports commerce and sometimes war as means to spread Christianity, and retains private, bashful religion and benevolence. Success leaves him divided, uncertain, and bitter toward obstructive workers.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

IX.

Mixtus becomes very wealthy through commercial activity, maintains a London mansion, and gains recognition chiefly as a genial and useful rich man. He retains substantial knowledge, kindness, religious memories, and intellectual interests, but his earlier convictions have been displaced by worldly success and he no longer knows how to understand himself as a whole.
