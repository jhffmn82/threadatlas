the air space around mermaids and visitors in The Sea Fairies

L. Frank Baum · 1911

A hair’s-breadth air space separates mermaids and visitors from water, continually replenished by air contained in it, keeping their bodies and gowns warm and dry while enabling breathing.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

[Illustration: The PALACE OF QUEEN AQUARINE

A very small air space remains between the mermaids and visitors and the surrounding water, so the water does not touch their bodies or gowns. The air space is continually replenished from air contained in the water, allowing them to breathe freely and remain warm and dry.
