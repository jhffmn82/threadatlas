Abstract written by a model

Sacho is a boy, formerly Prince Sacho of Sacharhineolaland, who becomes a slave and castle attendant in Zog's domain after surviving a shipwreck. Saved by Zog, he is magically able to live underwater and is taught to serve, including carrying Zog's messages. Though he knows the castle and its maze and serves Zog as a subordinate, he is cheerful, rejects fear and hatred, and believes happiness is the purpose of time. He receives and escorts the prisoners, shows them rooms and the goldsmiths' shop, explains their guarded confinement, warns that Zog may intend to kill them, and offers comfort and help with escape if possible. He refuses to leave alone because he is happy among the other slaves. Afterward, he urges the former slaves to stay together, labor for themselves, and elect a ruler; declining kingship, he agrees to attend and serve as an officer to Cap'n Joe and may seek Aquareine's help.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

[Illustration: _Chap. 12._

Prince Sacho of Sacharhineolaland is now known in Zog's domain only as a slave. After surviving a shipwreck, he was saved by Zog, magically adapted to live underwater, and taught to serve in the castle. He receives the group, warns them about Zog, and guides them through the castle and maze.

[Illustration: _Chap. 13._

Sacho is a boy who serves Zog and escorts the prisoners through the castle. He shows them the Rose Chamber and Peony Room, explains that they may roam the castle but cannot leave, and offers to look after their comfort.

[Illustration: _Chap. 16._

Sacho leads the prisoners back to their rooms and tells them that Zog has no time for vengeance but may be planning to kill them. He explains that the great dome's opening and all other entrances are guarded, and refuses to leave because he is happy among his fellow slaves.

[Illustration: _Chap. 17._

Sacho tells the group that Zog's hot water was foolish and that both vengeance and fear are wasting time. He takes them to the goldsmiths' shop without asking Zog's permission, because his orders allow them liberty in the castle. He later warns them that Zog intends to stop them from living and advises them to be careful.

[Illustration: _Chap. 21._

Sacho tells the former slaves that they should remain together, labor for themselves, and choose a ruler. He declines to become king, agrees to serve as Cap'n Joe's attendant and officer, and promises to seek Aquareine's help if necessary.
