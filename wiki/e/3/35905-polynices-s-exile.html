Polynices’s exile is his voluntary departure from Thebes after Eteocles refuses to yield the throne under their agreement. He endures the speechlessness and uncertainty of exile, flees to Argos and marries into Adrastus’s family, then returns with an army to reclaim his home and share of the land.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE PHŒNICIAN VIRGINS.

Polynices's exile results from Eteocles's refusal to surrender the throne after the agreed period of rule. Polynices flees to Argos, marries there, and later returns with an army to reclaim his home and share of the land.

POLYNICES, CHORUS.

Polynices describes exile as the greatest misfortune, especially because an exile lacks freedom of speech and dependable friends. He survives uncertainly before marrying into Adrastus's family and says that exile has made him long intensely for his country.
