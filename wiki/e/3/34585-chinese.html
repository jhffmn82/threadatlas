Chinese in Musical Instruments

Carl Engel · 1875-08

The Chinese are a people described as possessing musical instruments long before the Christian era. Traditions attribute the musical scale to foung-hoang, stringed instruments to Fohi, and instruments and sound arrangements to Niuva. By 2200 years before the Christian era they had a sacred king, for whom various sonorous stones were constructed; famous selected stones were especially valued musically. They also had copper-and-tin tchung bells arranged in scale-forming sets, kou drums, and hiuen tones using a pentatonic scale. Their music employed twelve lu intervals and served religious, ceremonial, military, rhythmic, and melodic contexts.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER V.

The Chinese are described as possessing musical instruments long before the Christian era and as attributing many of them a fabulously high antiquity. They used sonorous stones, bells, wood instruments, drums, bamboo pipes, clay instruments, and stringed instruments in religious, ceremonial, military, rhythmic, and melodic contexts. Their musical intervals were called lu, with twelve such intervals in the compass of an octave, while some instruments used the pentatonic scale.
