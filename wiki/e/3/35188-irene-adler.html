Irene Adler in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

Irene Adler is a well-known adventuress, former contralto and concert singer, trained actress, and woman born in New Jersey in 1858. She performed at La Scala, lived in Warsaw during a lengthy visit by the King, and later lives at Briony Lodge in London. Wronged by the King of Bohemia, she holds compromising letters and a photograph of herself with him, intending the photograph as protection if he marries another woman. She drives out daily, has one male visitor, and marries the man she loves, Godfrey Norton, at the Church of St. Monica. When Holmes stages a fight and false fire alarm at Briony Lodge to locate the concealed photograph, she recognizes his disguise but lets him expose its hiding place behind a sliding panel. She then flees England with Norton, retains the incriminating photograph, and leaves another photograph for the King.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

I. A SCANDAL IN BOHEMIA

Irene Adler is a former opera singer and adventuress who lives in London. She possesses a photograph of herself with the King of Bohemia and threatens to send it to the family of his intended bride if he marries another woman.

II.

Irene Adler lives at Briony Lodge, sings at concerts, drives out daily, and is regarded by local men as exceptionally attractive. She travels to the Church of St. Monica and marries Godfrey Norton, then plans to return to her house after driving in the park.

It was a quarter past six when we left Baker Street, and it still

Irene Adler arrives at Briony Lodge in her carriage and is caught amid a staged fight outside her house. She permits the apparently injured Holmes to be carried into her sitting-room and responds to the fire alarm by rushing to the photograph's hiding place. She briefly draws the photograph from behind a sliding panel, replaces it after Holmes announces the alarm is false, and then leaves the room.

III.

Irene Norton, née Adler, explains in a letter that she recognized Sherlock Holmes’s disguise and discovered the purpose of his investigation. She flees with her husband, keeps the incriminating photograph as protection, and leaves another photograph for the King.
