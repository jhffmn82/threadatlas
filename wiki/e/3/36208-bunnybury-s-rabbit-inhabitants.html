Bunnybury's rabbit inhabitants in The Emerald City of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1910

Bunnybury's rabbit inhabitants are pink-eyed white rabbits from the forest, living as Ozma of Oz's subjects in a city Glinda the Good created and protects. They wear gem-decorated silks and satins, dislike dogs above all else, and elected the unwilling Rabbit King because none wanted to rule. When Dorothy arrives, curiosity yields to orderly passage and respectful bows; they then serve their king as soldiers, attendants, and court members.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

19. How Bunnybury Welcomed the Strangers

The rabbit inhabitants wear splendid silks and satins decorated with jewels, with the lady rabbits dressed especially magnificently. They are initially curious about Dorothy, make way for her after the Keeper's announcement, and bow respectfully as she passes.

20. How Dorothy Lunched With a King

The Bunnybury rabbits elected the Rabbit King because they did not want the position themselves, even though they knew he did not want to rule. They serve him as soldiers, attendants, and court members, and they live in the city created for them by Glinda the Good.
