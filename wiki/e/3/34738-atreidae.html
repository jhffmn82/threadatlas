Atreidae in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Atreidae are twin, high-sceptred kings and rulers of the Greek army. Their authority and actions provoke hostility: they use craft to award Achilles’ armor to a wit-proud man, depriving Aias and Neoptolemus of it and earning Aias’s enmity. They abandon Philoctetes on Lemnos because of his plague, casting him away and destroying his life. Later, after Helenus’ prophecy, they pursue Philoctetes because he is required for Troy’s capture. Philoctetes fears renewed harm from them, though he must aid their cause.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Of Aias, and his fortunes as they are. [AIAS _is discovered_

The Atreidae, described as the twin or high-sceptred kings, are blamed by Aias for using craft to award Achilles' armor to a wit-proud man and for despising his prowess. Aias says he will henceforth submit to their authority, although he has wished to kill them.

_The scene changes to a lonely wooded spot._

Aias asks that the Atridae beware after his death. Tecmessa says that Aias's suffering began when the contest over the prize was decided, and Teucer accuses the Atridae of authority that he does not recognize over Aias.

_Enter_ PHILOCTETES.

The Atreidae are blamed by Philoctetes and Neoptolemus for cruelly abandoning Philoctetes and for depriving Neoptolemus of Achilles' armour. They now seek Philoctetes because his presence is required for the capture of Troy.

MONODY.

The Atreidae are blamed by Philoctetes for casting him onto Lemnos and for destroying his life. He fears returning to Troy because he expects further harm from them, although Heracles' command requires him to aid their cause.

Atreus' sons in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

Atreus' sons are Argive, Danaan, and Achaean kings, warriors, and leaders in the war. They abandon Philoctetes on Lemnos, later halt the war on Calchas' counsel and send Odysseus and Diomede to recover him, welcoming his recovery. They return Penthesileia's body to Troy, permit burial for Trojans and Amazons, withstand surrounding enemies with allies' aid, and are driven back again by Eurypylus.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Then did the warrior sons of Argos strip

Atreus' sons marvel at Penthesileia's beauty and return her body to the men of Troy at Priam's request. They also allow the Trojans to recover and bury their own dead and the slain Amazons.

By this to Podaleirius tidings came

The Atreus' sons are nearly left alone amid enemies who surround and attack them from every side. They hold their ground and kill foes, then receive help from Teucer, Idomeneus, Thoas, Meriones, and Thrasymedes before Eurypylus again drives the Danaans back.

At last by Calchas' counsel Achaea's sons

The Atreus' sons stayed the war after Calchas' prophecy and sent Odysseus and Diomede to retrieve Philoctetes. They marveled at Philoctetes' recovery and welcomed him among the Achaeans.
