Servant in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

A messenger reports the Athenian army’s victory, recounts the battle and Eurystheus’s capture, then asks Alcmene to honor her promise and free him.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

SERVANT, ALCMENA, CHORUS.

The Servant tells Alcmene that the forces opposing Eurystheus have prevailed. He recounts Hyllus's challenge, the battle, Iolaus's rejuvenation, and the capture of Eurystheus, then asks Alcmene to fulfill her promise and set him free.

Servant in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Servant, a messenger, brings Alcmena news of victory: Iolaus became young again and captured Eurystheus; Servant reminds her that she promised freedom for this news.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

SERV. O mistress, I bring news most concise for you to hear, and to myself

The servant brings Alcmena news of the victory and reports that Iolaus has become young again and captured Eurystheus. He reminds Alcmena that she promised to make him free for bringing the news.
