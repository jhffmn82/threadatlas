Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the prophecy declares that Iphigenia must be sacrificed to Diana so the Greek army can sail, threatens to expose Agamemnon’s promise, prompts his fear, and is distrusted by Achilles despite the chorus recalling prophecies about his Trojan role. In The Library, the oracle declared that the Greeks could not sail unless Agamemnon’s fairest daughter was sacrificed to Artemis.

prophecy concerning Iphigenia in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where prophecy concerning Iphigenia first appears

Abstract written by a model

The prophecy, interpreted by Calchas, declares that Iphigenia must be sacrificed to Diana so the Greek army can sail. It threatens to expose Agamemnon’s promise and prompts his fear. Achilles distrusts such prophecy, though the chorus recalls prophecies about his Trojan role.

[_Enter a Messenger_.[29]]

The prophecy concerning Iphigenia requires her sacrifice for the Greek army's purposes. Agamemnon fears that Calchas will announce it to the army and expose Agamemnon's promise to offer her.

[_Enter_ ACHILLES.]

Calchas is said to have declared that Iphigenia must be sacrificed to Diana so that the army can sail. Achilles distrusts the prophet, saying that prophets tell a few things truly but many falsely and are undone when they fail. The chorus also recalls that Apollo and Chiron once prophesied Achilles' great role in the Trojan land.
