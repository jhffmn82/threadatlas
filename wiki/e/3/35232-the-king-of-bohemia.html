Abstract written by a model

The hereditary King of Bohemia, also Grand Duke of Cassel-Felstein, is engaged to Clotilde Lothman von Saxe-Meningen but fears exposure of his past relationship with Irene Adler. He wrote compromising letters, appears with Adler in a compromising photograph, and seeks to recover it before his betrothal is proclaimed. Disguised as Count Von Kramm, he employs Sherlock Holmes after failed searches and burglary attempts. He accompanies Holmes to Briony Lodge, believes the photograph has been left behind, and gratefully permits Holmes to keep it.

I. A SCANDAL IN BOHEMIA

Wilhelm Gottsreich Sigismond von Ormstein visits Holmes in disguise as Count Von Kramm and reveals that he is the hereditary King of Bohemia. He explains that Irene Adler possesses a compromising photograph and hires Holmes to recover it before the King’s betrothal is publicly proclaimed.

It was a quarter past six when we left Baker Street, and it still

The King is seeking to recover the photograph and has previously had Irene Adler waylaid and searched. Sherlock Holmes plans to return with him to Briony Lodge the next morning so that he may potentially regain the photograph with his own hands.

III.

The King of Bohemia accompanies Sherlock Holmes to Briony Lodge in search of the photograph. He is relieved when Irene Adler leaves the photograph behind, declares himself immensely indebted to Holmes, and gives Holmes permission to keep it.
