the will of the deceased wife in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

The deceased wife’s will provides each daughter £250 upon marriage from an estate now yielding £750 after agricultural prices fell. Holmes concludes that either daughter’s marriage would sharply reduce Roylott’s means, especially if both marry.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

“And what do you think of it all, Watson?” asked Sherlock Holmes,

The will of the deceased wife provides each daughter with an income of £250 in case of marriage. Holmes calculates that the reduced agricultural income would leave Roylott with very little money if both daughters married and would seriously reduce his means even if one married.
