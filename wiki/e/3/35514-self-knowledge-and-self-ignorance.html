self-knowledge and self-ignorance

Also called: Introspection, consciousness, describe oneself, give an account to myself, inward experience, know myself better, knowing mankind, natural history of my inward self, self-betrayal, self-ignorance, self-knowledge

Abstract written by a model

Self-knowledge is an incomplete natural history of one’s inward self, limited by self-ignorance and possible self-betrayal. It begins with recognizing that others may see hidden powers, appearance, delusion, and betrayal; it unfolds through examining one’s absurdities and others’ tendencies, despite unawareness and outdated self-traditions.

2 sections; each heading opens the text

I.

The narrator believes he knows himself better than casual acquaintances do, yet recognizes that other people may know aspects of his powers and appearance that remain hidden from him. He learns through observing others that he is also capable of unconscious self-delusion and self-betrayal.

XIII.

Self-knowledge is pursued by examining one’s own absurdities and using observations of other people as clues to inward tendencies. It is difficult because people may be unaware of their deliberate actions or may preserve an outdated tradition about their own character.
