Henry Baker in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

Henry Baker is a large, learned but ill-used married man of limited means, with a grizzled beard, worn black coat, and signs of drinking. Unknown to Sherlock Holmes at first, he loses his battered black felt hat and a Christmas goose marked for Mrs. Henry Baker after roughs assault him during a confrontation on Tottenham Court Road, where a window is broken and he flees. The goose had been carried over his shoulder and came through the Alpha Inn goose club, of which he is a paying member. Baker later visits Holmes to recover the lost property, believing the assailants took it. He accepts a replacement goose when told the original was eaten. Holmes concludes that Baker knows nothing of the theft and follows the goose’s route through the club’s supply chain.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

VII. THE ADVENTURE OF THE BLUE CARBUNCLE

Henry Baker is identified as the owner of the battered black felt hat and the Christmas goose marked for Mrs. Henry Baker. He drops the goose and flees after a window is broken during a confrontation on Tottenham Court Road; Sherlock Holmes considers him likely to be innocent but seeks to question him.

I had been delayed at a case, and it was a little after half-past six

Henry Baker comes to Sherlock Holmes' rooms to recover his hat and goose, believing that roughs who assaulted him had taken them. He accepts the replacement goose after learning that his original was eaten and says that he received it through a goose club at the Alpha Inn.

“So much for Mr. Henry Baker,” said Holmes when he had closed the door

Henry Baker is identified as a member of the Alpha Inn’s goose club. Sherlock Holmes concludes that he knows nothing about the theft and traces his goose through the club’s supply chain.
