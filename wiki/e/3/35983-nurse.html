the Nurse of Phaedra in Hippolytus; The Bacchae

Euripides · -0427

The Nurse of Phaedra is Phaedra’s handmaid, watcher, and caregiver from youth, loving her as mistress and child. Seeking to relieve Phaedra’s illness, she presses her to confess love for Hippolytus, blames Aphrodite, and argues surrender to Cypris is wiser than resistance. She offers love-philtres and breaks her oath by seeking a word from Hippolytus, who condemns her; Phaedra curses and dismisses her.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

HIPPOLYTUS

The Nurse cares for Phaedra, tries to relieve her physical distress, and repeatedly urges her to reveal the cause of her illness. She finally mentions Hippolytus and draws from Phaedra the confession of her love, then blames Aphrodite for bringing ruin upon Phaedra and the household.

CHORUS

The Nurse argues that Phaedra's love is not extraordinary and that yielding to Cypris is wiser than resisting a divine power. She offers love-philtres and attempts to obtain a sign or word from Hippolytus, but he discovers what she has done, condemns her, and throws her from him; Phaedra then curses her and sends her away.

Nurse in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Nurse is an aged, faithful attendant in Phædra’s household, devoted to Phædra and distressed by her suffering. She brings Phædra outside, tends her illness, urges her to live for her children, and learns that she loves Hippolytus. Believing Venus caused the passion, Nurse offers a love potion and seeks an honorable cure. She reveals the love to Hippolytus; he condemns her, Phædra blames her, and Nurse regrets her unwise attempt while maintaining it came from affection.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

HIPPOLYTUS.

The Nurse brings Phaedra out of the palace before the gates. The Chorus observes that Phaedra’s sorrowful condition appears to have increased.

PHÆDRA, NURSE, CHORUS.

The Nurse tends Phædra, tries to discover the cause of her illness, and urges her to preserve her life for the sake of her children. After learning that Phædra loves Hippolytus, she attributes the passion to Venus and proposes a love potion, asking Phædra to help her keep the matter honorable.

CHORUS, PHÆDRA.

The Nurse tries to cure Phædra's love by revealing it to Hippolytus, but Hippolytus angrily condemns her and Phædra blames her for destroying her. The Nurse admits that her attempt was unwise but says she acted from affection and a desire to find a remedy.
