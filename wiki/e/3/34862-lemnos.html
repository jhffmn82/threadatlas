Lemnos in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · -0299/-0200

Lemnos, the Sintian isle in the Aegean Sea, is a fertile island whose men were massacred by its women. Reached by the heroes after the wind fails, it receives the Minyae; Hypsipyle offers Jason sovereignty, and the Argonauts later depart, with an alliance intended to reinhabit it.

Lemnos in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Lemnos is a desert, sea-girt island, uninhabited and untrodden by men, with no proper harbour. It contains Philoctetes’ cave, the sacred Lemnian fire, and the earth, waters, rocks, creeks, and precipices that witness his suffering. After the fleet leaves him there from Chrysa, Philoctetes lives unwillingly in exile for ten years, threatens to die rather than sail, then agrees to depart, blessing and bidding farewell to the island.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

PHILOCTETES

Lemnos is the deserted island where Philoctetes has been cast away. Its shore is described as uninhabited and untrodden by men.

_Enter_ PHILOCTETES.

Lemnos is the deserted island where Philoctetes was left after the fleet touched there from Chrysa. It has no proper harbour or sea-port, and Philoctetes has lived there in isolation for ten years.

CHORUS.

Lemnos is the island where Philoctetes has lived in abandonment and where he threatens to die rather than sail. Its earth, flame, rocks, creeks, precipices, and familiar presence are invoked by Philoctetes as witnesses to his suffering.

MONODY.

Lemnos has been Philoctetes' place of exile and suffering, but he finally agrees to leave it. He blesses and bids farewell to its earth, waters, shores, and surrounding landscape before beginning the voyage.

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

Lemnos is an island of stately mansions, marked by wives' jealous slaughter of their husbands in the distant past and by Philoctetes' years of suffering in a rocky cave before his return to the Achaean host.
