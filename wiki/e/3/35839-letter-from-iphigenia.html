letter from Iphigenia in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Iphigenia’s letter is a message for her friends in Argos, especially Orestes, carried by Pylades if he is spared. Beginning with her supposed death at Aulis, it reveals that Iphigenia survived because the Goddess substituted a hind, and insists that she did not willingly become a murderess. Written for a cenotaph at Mycenæ when she expected to die, it now asks Orestes to rescue her from the barbarian land before she dies. Iphigenia retrieves it from Artemis’ shrine to send it.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHOR. Thou hast told wondrous things concerning him who has appeared,

Iphigenia has written a letter to a friend in Argos explaining that she lives and that she did not willingly become a murderess. She plans to give it to Pylades to carry after he is spared, and she goes to retrieve it from Artemis’ shrine.

CHOR. I deplore thee now destined to the gory streams of the lustral

Iphigenia's letter is addressed to Orestes and says that the woman sacrificed at Aulis, Iphigenia, is alive. It asks Orestes to bring her from the barbarian land before she dies and explains that the Goddess saved her by substituting a hind.

notes

The letter from Iphigenia is said to begin with a reference to her having been sacrificed at Aulis. It was intended for a cenotaph at Mycenae because she believed she was about to die at Aulis, and the notes clarify that it was sent with that purpose.
