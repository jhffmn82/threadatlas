Orestes' death

Abstract written by a model

Orestes' death is a fabricated report, falsely presented as a chariot accident in the Pythian course. Delivered by the Old Man as a deceptive message from Phanoteus, it says Orestes' body was burned and his ashes placed in a bronze urn. Clytemnestra welcomes the news as freedom from fear, while Electra mourns the loss of her last hope for vengeance and seeks Chrysothemis's help. In fact Orestes is alive: the deception conceals his return, disarms Clytemnestra and Aegisthus, and lets him enter the palace safely with Pylades and the urn.

Orestes' death is in one work on this site, The Seven Plays in English Verse. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

ELECTRA

The report of Orestes' death is to be fabricated as a chariot accident during the Pythian course. The false report is intended to disarm Clytemnestra and Aegisthus while Orestes secretly returns to punish them.

_Enter_ CLYTEMNESTRA.

The Old Man reports Orestes' death to Clytemnestra and Electra, saying that his body was burned and his ashes placed in a bronze urn. Clytemnestra welcomes the news as freedom from fear, while Electra mourns it as the destruction of her last hope.

_Enter_ CHRYSOTHEMIS.

Electra says that Orestes is dead and that she learned this from a man who witnessed his destruction. His death ends her hope that he will avenge their father's blood and leads her to seek Chrysothemis's help.

_Enter_ ORESTES _and_ PYLADES, _with the urn_.

The death report falsely states that Orestes died in a chariot race and that his remains were brought in an urn. It deceives Clytemnestra and Aegisthus and allows Orestes to enter the palace safely.
