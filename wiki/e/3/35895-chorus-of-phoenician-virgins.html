Abstract written by a model

The Chorus of Phoenician Virgins is a group of Phoenician women, enslaved attendants sent as first-fruits from Phoenicia to serve Apollo at Thebes. Their journey is halted by the Argives' march against Thebes. They identify with both Phoenician and Cadmean peoples, fear the war, comment on the brothers' dispute, and pray for reconciliation. After celebrating Thebes' victory, they dread the combat's result, mourn Eteocles, Polynices, and Jocasta, and witness Creon's grief.

3 sections; each heading opens the text

THE PHŒNICIAN VIRGINS.

The Chorus consists of Phoenician virgins sent from Phoenicia to serve Apollo at Thebes. It describes its journey and its connection to the Cadmean and Phoenician peoples, while fearing the war surrounding Thebes.

POLYNICES, CHORUS.

The Phoenician women form the chorus and say that Phoenicia is their paternal country. They were sent by the descendants of Agenor as first-fruits to Apollo, but their journey was interrupted when the Argives marched against Thebes; they comment on the brothers' dispute and pray for reconciliation.

MESSENGER, JOCASTA, CHORUS.

The Chorus celebrates Thebes' victory while fearing the consequences of the brothers' proposed combat. It mourns the deaths of Eteocles, Polynices, and Jocasta and observes Creon's arrival in grief.
