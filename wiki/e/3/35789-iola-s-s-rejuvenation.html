Iolaüs's rejuvenation

event · 3 works · 2 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Iolaus's rejuvenation, augmented the number of their years, be young for one day, became young, changed from an old into a young man again, chief grown young again, grown young again, recover the pristine vigour of his youth, restored to youth, showed forth a youthful image

Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Iolaus's rejuvenation follows his prayer to Juno and Jupiter to become young for one day and take vengeance on his enemies; two stars and a dim cloud conceal the chariot, and he emerges with the appearance and arms of a young man. In Hecuba and other plays, Iolaus's rejuvenation is granted by Hebe after he prays to Hebe and Zeus while pursuing Eurystheus; Hercules and Hebe are seen as stars above his chariot, and he temporarily regains the strength of youth. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Iolaüs's rejuvenation changes him from an aged man into someone almost boyish, with the appearance of early manhood; Hebe grants it, and Themis's prophecy establishes that Calirrhoë's sons will later receive a similar gift.
