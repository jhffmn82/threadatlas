the Nurse in Medea

Euripides · -0430

Medea's elderly, trusted nurse guards Jason and Medea's children, recounts Medea's history with Jason, fears her enraged revenge against herself, Jason, the bridegroom, or the king, and keeps the children away while seeking the Chorus's understanding.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

MEDEA

The Nurse explains Medea's history with Jason and fears that Medea may kill herself, Jason, the bridegroom, or the king. She tries to keep the children away from Medea while Medea's anger is intense, and she asks the Chorus to understand Medea's suffering.

The Nurse describes Medea's grief, isolation, and violent anger after Jason's betrayal. She fears that Medea may kill herself, Glauce, Jason, or others, and tries to keep Medea's sons away from her while warning the Chorus about her state.
