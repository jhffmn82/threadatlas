India in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

India is a country, called the world’s greatest nation and richest tract of land in the account. Quadrangular in form, it is bounded by the Great Ocean east and south, by Hemodus and Scythia north, and by the Indus west; the Indus also divides it from the rest of Asia. It extends 28,000 furlongs east to west and 32,000 north to south. Much lies under the Tropic of Capricorn, with remote shadowless regions. Its fruit-bearing mountains, pleasant river-watered plains, constant summer rains, navigable rivers, and two yearly crops support millet, pulse, rice, Bosphorus, and sesamus. Mines yield gold, silver, brass, iron, and tin, and Indian elephants are incredibly numerous. Many native nations inhabit it, organized into seven social ranks; some regions have monarchy, and the account says it neither received nor sent colonies. Indian antiquity attributes conquest to Bacchus and claims Hercules as a birthplace. Semiramis invaded without provocation. Dionysius made a three-year expedition, returned with rich spoils, and was said to be first to triumph on an Indian elephant. Later Lambulus reached India after four months at sea, lost his companion in sandy shallows, and was taken inland to the king at Polyboihia.
