Pentheus's persecution of Dionysus

event · 2 works · 1 quoted fact · 0 connections
Also called: Pentheus's persecution of the Bacchæ, bound, drives me from sacrifices, hunt from the mountain, our persecutor, persecuted those who celebrated his revels, taken, the day he troubled Pentheus with his God, this work

Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Pentheus's persecution of the Bacchæ includes imprisoning women in public strongholds, ordering the women absent from the city to be hunted, and seeking the arrest and execution of the stranger associated with Bacchus; Tiresias and Cadmus oppose his actions. In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, Pentheus's persecution of Dionysus is carried out by Pentheus's bodyguard under Pentheus's orders, beginning with his commands to arrest Dionysian worshippers, seek the girl-faced stranger, wreck Teiresias's sanctuary, and threaten the stranger with imprisonment, judgment, and death.
