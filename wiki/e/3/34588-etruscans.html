Tyrrhenians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Tyrrhenians were an ancient people in a large country in Italy, once powerful warriors who founded famous cities, maintained a navy, and controlled the sea named after them. They practiced piracy and raided seas around Lipara, though the Liparans later defeated them in sea battles. Credited with inventing the trumpet and studying natural events, especially thunder and lightning, they later became luxurious, drunken, and less warlike. Their sea power was finally broken when Hiero defeated their fleet near Cuma.

The Tyrrhenians were formerly powerful warriors who occupied a large country, founded famous cities, maintained a navy, and controlled the sea named after them. They were credited with inventing the trumpet and with giving generals ivory thrones and purple robes. They also studied natural philosophy, especially thunder and lightning, but later became luxurious, drunken, and less warlike.

CHAP. VII.

The Etruscans of Tyrrhenia fought Hiero's fleet after the citizens of Cuma requested assistance. Several of their ships were sunk, and their sea power was broken.

Tyrrhenian pirates in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Tyrrhenian pirates hire a trireme to ferry Dionysus from Icaria to Naxos but plan to sell him in Asia; after Dionysus transforms the ship, they go mad, leap into the sea, and become dolphins.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

V. Διόνυσος δὲ εὑρετὴς ἀμπέλου γενόμενος,

The Tyrrhenian pirates hired out a trireme and carried Dionysus aboard, intending to sail to Asia and sell him instead of taking him to Naxos. Dionysus turned the mast and oars into snakes and filled the ship with ivy and the sound of flutes; the pirates went mad, leaped into the sea, and became dolphins.

Etruscans in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The Etruscans are a group beyond the Tiber, not poetical or imaginative. Rejecting Mezentius for cruelty, they seek his punishment; priests require a foreign victorious leader, so they offer Aeneas leadership. Led by Tarchon, they warmly receive Aeneas’s followers against Turnus.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXVI

The Etruscans live beyond the Tiber and have rejected Mezentius because of his cruelty. They seek to punish him and offer leadership to Aeneas because their priests say that their victorious leader must come from across the sea. Their leader Tarchon receives Aeneas and his followers warmly.

Etruscans in Musical Instruments

Carl Engel · 1875-08

The Etruscans excel early in the arts, possess Greek-like instruments, and may invent the hydraulic organ; they use flutes and trumpets of varied materials and are said to transmit popular instruments to the Romans.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IV.

The Etruscans possessed instruments similar to those of the Greeks and are said to have transmitted some popular instruments to the Romans. Their flutes were made from materials including ivory, box-wood, bone, bronze, and silver, and a bronze flute and a large bronze trumpet have been found in tombs. Some attribute the invention of the hydraulic organ to them.
