the people of Thebes

Also called: Dwellers in our native Thebè, all Thebes, citizen, mankind, mortal tribes of men, my country, thy people

Abstract written by a model

The people of Thebes once regard Oedipus as king and protector, but are later invoked as witnesses to his crimes. Under Creon, they privately praise Antigone’s burial of her brother and condemn her death sentence, yet fear prevents open opposition.

CHORUS.

According to Haemon, the people of Thebes privately condemn Antigone's punishment and believe that she deserves praise for burying her brother. Their fear of Creon's authority keeps them from speaking openly.

CHORUS.

The people of Thebes are invoked as those who will see and hear of Oedipus's crimes. Oedipus believes he can no longer face their eyes after condemning the impious and impure.
