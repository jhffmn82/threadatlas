Abstract written by a model

The warning letters are messages sent to Colonel Openshaw and his successors, containing five orange pips and the initials K. K. K. Posted from Pondicherry, Dundee, and East London, some demand that destroyed papers be put on the sundial. They serve as threats or tokens before persecution of the Openshaws, with their timing preceding attacks by roughly a sailing ship’s travel time.

V. THE FIVE ORANGE PIPS

The warning letters received by the Openshaws contain five orange pips and the initials K. K. K. Elias’s letter came from Pondicherry, Joseph’s from Dundee, and John’s from London; the latter two also state, “Put the papers on the sundial.”

The warning letters were sent to Colonel Openshaw and his successors from Pondicherry, Dundee, and East London. Holmes concludes that their senders were traveling by sailing ship and that the letters preceded the attacks by roughly the time needed for the ship to arrive.
