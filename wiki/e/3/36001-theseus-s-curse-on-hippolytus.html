Theseus's curse on Hippolytus in Hippolytus; The Bacchae

Euripides · -0427

Theseus's curse on Hippolytus is a prayer to Poseidon, spoken by Theseus, demanding Hippolytus's death or friendless exile. Using one of Poseidon's three granted prayers, Theseus invokes the god; Poseidon fulfills it when terrified horses destroy Hippolytus, although Artemis calls the curse unrighteous because Hippolytus is innocent.

Theseus's curse in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Theseus's curse is a punishment directed at Hippolytus, calling on Poseidon to destroy him through a promised wish. Despite the Chorus's plea to withdraw it, Theseus acts without proof. Poseidon fulfills it through a sea-born bull that panics Hippolytus's horses, causing his destruction; Theseus later realizes he was deceived.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS, PHÆDRA.

Theseus's curse calls on Neptune to destroy Hippolytus through one of the promised curses. Theseus says Hippolytus will either die or wander in exile, although the Chorus urges him to withdraw the prayer.

HIPPOLYTUS, THESEUS, CHORUS.

Theseus asks the ruler of the ocean to destroy Hippolytus, and the curse is fulfilled through the sea-born bull and the horses' panic. Hippolytus recognizes the disaster as his father's imprecation while he is being dragged by the chariot.

DIANA, THESEUS, CHORUS.

Theseus used one of the wishes granted by his father to curse Hippolytus, acting without examining proof or awaiting the voice of prophets. The curse brought about Hippolytus's destruction, and Theseus later recognizes that he was deceived.
