document chunking in Document Segmentation Matters for Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Zhitong Wang, Cheng Gao, Chaojun Xiao, Yufei Huang, Shuzheng Si, Kangyang Luo, Yuzhuo Bai, Wenhao Li, Tangjian Duan, Chuancheng Lv, Guoshan Lu, Gang Chen, Fanchao Qi, Maosong Sun · 2025

Document chunking, or document segmentation, divides lengthy source documents into manageable units for retrieval and generation and is a critical, underexplored challenge in retrieval-augmented generation. Rule-based approaches can produce overly broad or semantically incomplete chunks. The work evaluates rule-based, semantic-based, generation-based, and PIC methods for retrieval and downstream question answering, comparing sentence, paragraph, fixed-size, semantic, proposition, and PIC chunking. Results suggest that reducing irrelevant information helps LLMs find answers and reduces hallucinations. Analysis uses pseudo-instructions and real instructions when available, finding chunk text structure more influential than length. PIC dynamically groups sentences by relevance to the document topic.

Abstract

Document segmentation, also called document chunking, is identified as a critical and underexplored challenge in retrieval-augmented generation.

1 / Introduction

Document segmentation divides lengthy source documents into manageable units for retrieval and generation. The unit argues that fixed-length and other rule-based approaches may create chunks that are either too broad or semantically incomplete, and proposes PIC as an adaptive alternative.

4 / Experimental Settings .. 4.4 / Implementation Details

Document chunking is evaluated through rule-based, semantic-based, generation-based, and PIC methods. The resulting chunks are used for retrieval and downstream question answering.

5 / Main Results / 5.1 / Retrieval Performance .. 5.2 / End-to-end QA Performance

The unit compares sentence, paragraph, fixed-size, semantic, proposition, and PIC chunking methods for retrieval and QA. The results suggest that chunking with less irrelevant information helps LLMs identify answers more effectively and reduces hallucinations.

6 / Further Analysis / 6.1 / Impacts of the Pseudo-Instruction .. 6.4 / Chunk Analysis

Document chunking is evaluated using pseudo-instructions and, when available, real instructions. The analysis concludes that the structure of the text within each chunk has a greater effect on downstream performance than chunk length itself.

7 / Conclusions / Limitations

The paper proposes PIC as a novel document chunking method that dynamically groups sentences according to their relevance to the document topic.
