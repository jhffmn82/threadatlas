  • In Sky Island, Button-Bright travels from Philadelphia to Sky Island by means of the Magic Umbrella, is enslaved as the Royal Bootblue, escapes with the Royal Record Book, helps defeat the Boolooroo, recovers the umbrella, and returns home.
  • In The Scarecrow of Oz, Button-Bright travels with Trot and the others through Jinxland, repeatedly gets lost, helps free the Scarecrow from the Ruby Cavern, and disappears again during Ozma's reception.
  • In The Lost Princess of Oz, Button-Bright repeatedly gets lost while traveling with the Wizard and others, eats an enchanted peach, and unknowingly carries Ozma in its golden pit until she emerges from his pocket.
  • In Glinda of Oz, Button Bright joins the rescue expedition, is protected by Glinda from beasts, torments them, wanders away, and returns with the Glass Cat.

Button-Bright in The Road to Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1909

Button-Bright is a little boy, prettily dressed in sailor clothes, with curls, short legs, and a sailor hat. Though his mother calls him Button-Bright because his father said he was bright as a button, he does not know his real name, home, origin, or how he came to be lost on the seventh road. His people love him and take pains with his appearance, but worry because he is lost. He is younger than Dorothy and becomes her travelling companion and friend, joining Dorothy, the Shaggy Man, and Toto after Dorothy finds him digging a large hole beneath a chestnut tree. He is curious, clever, and repeatedly answers difficult questions with uncertainty, often saying that he does not know. He asks many questions, enjoys Foxville and its royal drum, likes Allegro da Capo and his music, and is interested in strange sights and events. He eats readily, especially pie, but fears being made into soup when the Scoodlers capture the travelers. During their escape and further journey, he is sometimes frightened, hungry, weary, tearful, or hurt, but continues with the group. In Foxville, King Dox changes his head into a little fox head. Button-Bright cries at first, though praise from the fox-princesses partly comforts him. He remains a boy, not a fox, and travels in his sailor suit with the fox head hidden or covered by his sailor hat. In Dunkiton, others mistake him for a fox. His fox head is later stuck in a gopher hole after the sand-boat crashes, but he is pulled free. At the Truth Pond, he is frightened by his reflection, falls into the water, and regains his proper chubby face, blue eyes, and curls. He reaches the Emerald City, grows less afraid of the Cowardly Lion and Hungry Tiger, meets Ozma and other guests, and is recognized by Santa Claus as a boy whose home he has visited. After the birthday celebration, Button-Bright asks to go home. The Wizard sends him westward in a giant soap bubble, where he sits cross-legged and waves his sailor hat as he returns to Kansas.

Narrative, section by section

18 sections; each heading opens the text

2. Dorothy Meets Button-Bright

Button-Bright is a little boy in sailor clothes who is digging a large hole beneath a chestnut tree. He says that his mother calls him Button-Bright because Papa said he was bright as a button, but he does not know his real name, where he lives, how he came there, or what he will do next. He is invited to travel with Dorothy, the Shaggy Man, and Toto.

3. A Queer Village

Button-Bright travels with Dorothy and the Shaggy Man and asks many questions about prisoners, captives, sovereigns, and communities. He is fascinated by Foxville and enjoys pounding the royal drum until the fox-captain stops him.

4. King Dox

Button-Bright answers King Renard's questions with repeated uncertainty, which the King interprets as unusual cleverness. After the King gives him a fox's head, he cries and feels the new head, but the fox-princesses' praise comforts him somewhat. He travels onward with Dorothy and the shaggy man while wearing the fox head.

5. The Rainbow's Daughter

Button-Bright walks with the others and does not tire easily. Dorothy explains to Polychrome that the Fox-King changed his head into a fox head and that she hopes to have him changed back.

6. The City Of Beasts

Button-Bright eats a leg of turkey during luncheon. In Dunkiton, the donkeys mistake him for a fox because he is wearing a fox head, but the shaggy man explains that he is only wearing it until he can get his own head back.

7. The Shaggy Man's Transformation

Button-Bright travels with the group while wearing a fox head. He is fascinated by the shaggy man's new donkey ears, eats apple pie, and shares Dorothy's beefsteak at breakfast.

8. The Musicker

Button-Bright calls Allegro da Capo a "musicker" and asks what would happen if he swallowed a mouth-organ. He likes Allegro and considers him nice, even though the others want to leave the constant music behind.

9. Facing the Scoodlers

Button-Bright stumbles on the rough ground and shares an apple during the travelers' meager luncheon. He does not know what lies ahead and becomes frightened and tearful when the Scoodlers demand the travelers for soup.

10. Escaping the Soup-Kettle

Button-Bright is knocked down during the Scoodlers' attack and repeatedly protests that he does not want to be soup. The shaggy man carries him toward the cave entrance, and he crosses the narrow bridge to safety.

11. Johnny Dooit Does It

Button-Bright becomes hungry and weary during the journey, then sits on the grass and digs in the earth with a stick while the others consider how to proceed. He watches with absorbed interest as Johnny Dooit builds the sand-boat.

12. The Deadly Desert Crossed

Button-Bright in Sky Island

L. Frank Baum · 1912

Button-Bright is a boy from Philadelphia, an Earth person whose formal name is Saladin Paracelsus de Lambertine Evagne von Smith. He is a descendant and member of the family that rightfully owns the old Magic Umbrella, a precious family relic with an elephant-head handle. After finding it in an attic, he discovers that it can carry him through the air and flies from Philadelphia across several places to the Pacific Ocean, arriving by parachute-like descent and meeting Trot and Cap'n Bill. Though he says he dislikes most girls and boys, he considers Trot one of the good ones and becomes her companion. The umbrella matters most to Button-Bright: it enables the journey, is repeatedly lost or sought, and is his means of returning home. He directs it toward Sky Island, but its literal obedience brings the travelers into danger. In the Blue Country, the Boolooroo takes the umbrella, enslaves Button-Bright, and makes him Royal Bootblue, responsible for blueing and shining royal footwear. Button-Bright plans an escape, steals the Treasure Chamber key, enters the chamber despite the Blue Wolf, fails to find the umbrella, and escapes with the Royal Record Book concealed in his blouse. With Trot, Cap'n Bill, and later the parrot, he crosses the Fog Bank into the Pink Country. He tells their story, fears their uncertain fate, wants to return to Earth, and recognizes Polychrome as an old friend when she helps save them. During the conflict with the Boolooroo, he leads the Pinkies, supports Ghip-Ghisizzle if he will rule, and makes return of the Magic Umbrella a condition of his help. The Royal Record Book establishes that the Boolooroo has completed his lawful reign. After the victory, Button-Bright searches for and recovers the umbrella, carries it through its brief transformation involving its elephant head, and finally travels with Trot and Cap'n Bill back to Earth. He stays at Trot's house, flies home to Philadelphia, and reports that he arrived safely.

Narrative, section by section

25 sections; each heading opens the text

Sky Island

Button-Bright is named in the contents in connection with an encounter with the Blue Wolf.

A LITTLE TALK TO MY READERS

Button-Bright previously had an adventure with Dorothy in Oz. In Sky Island, he travels with Trot and his Magic Umbrella, which Baum says is essential to the story, and he eventually returns to Earth with Trot and Cap'n Bill.

A MYSTERIOUS ARRIVAL

Button-Bright introduces himself as being from Philadelphia and says he does not like most girls or boys, though he considers Trot one of the good ones. He carries an old umbrella with an elephant-head handle and says he dropped from the sky with it. He agrees to go with Trot and Cap'n Bill and says he will tell them his story.

THE MAGIC UMBRELLA

Button-Bright is taken by Trot to Mrs. Griffith's cottage, where he is given bread-and-butter and invited to stay overnight. He explains that he found his family's old umbrella in an attic and discovered that it could carry him through the air. He says he flew from Philadelphia to Buffalo, then Chicago, Denver, and finally the Pacific Ocean, where he met Trot.

A WONDERFUL EXPERIENCE

Button-Bright carries and operates the Magic Umbrella, taking Trot to town and back. He helps Cap'n Bill and Trot make a trial flight to Smuggler's Cove before directing the umbrella toward Sky Island.

THE ISLAND IN THE SKY

Button-Bright tells the Magic Umbrella to take them to Sky Island, intending to name the island they had approached. He realizes that the umbrella is following his exact words and cannot be redirected, then confidently expects it to land them on the island in the sky.

THE BOOLOOROO OF THE BLUES

Button-Bright closes the Magic Umbrella after the travelers land and later holds it while they prepare to leave. He tries to recover it when the Boolooroo takes it, and he seizes it after Cap'n Bill makes the Boolooroo drop it. A blue cord then winds around Button-Bright and makes him helpless.

THE SIX SNUBNOSED PRINCESSES

Button-Bright says he would not mind remaining on the island if the Magic Umbrella had not been taken. The Boolooroo makes him the Royal Bootblue, ordering him to polish the royal family's footwear with blue paste and Moonshine, and sends him to the shoeblue den for instruction.

GHIP-GHISIZZLE PROVES FRIENDLY

Button-Bright is brought to the Royal Palace with Cap'n Bill and is viewed with dislike by many of the royal servants. He learns about patching, is assigned duty as the Royal Bootblue, and goes with Ghip-Ghisizzle and Cap'n Bill to see the Blue City.

THE BLUE CITY

Button-Bright learns about the Pink Country, the Great Fog Bank, and the law requiring Blueskins to enter the Arch of Phinis at the end of their lives. He plans to enter the Treasure Chamber to recover his Magic Umbrella and agrees to try to bring Ghip-Ghisizzle the Book of Records.

THE KING'S TREASURE CHAMBER

Button-Bright returns to the palace with Trot and Cap'n Bill and plans to recover his Magic Umbrella from the Treasure Chamber. He polishes the Boolooroo's shoes, steals the key to the Treasure Chamber from the sleeping king, and tricks Jimfred into leaving his post. Button-Bright then enters the chamber carrying a sofa pillow to face the Blue Wolf.

BUTTON-BRIGHT ENCOUNTERS THE BLUE WOLF

Button-Bright enters the Treasure Chamber and stuffs a sofa pillow into the Blue Wolf's mouth when it attacks him. He searches unsuccessfully for the Magic Umbrella, conceals the Royal Record Book inside his blouse, and escapes with Cap'n Bill and Trot after the palace alarm is raised.

THROUGH THE FOG BANK

Button-Bright worries that the Fog Bank may lead to a terrible country, but he travels onward with Trot and Cap'n Bill. He helps support Cap'n Bill when the sailor trips and rides on the common frog's back. He is carried out of the Fog Bank and lands with the others in a meadow.

THE PINK COUNTRY

Button-Bright quickly gets to his feet when the parrot warns that people are approaching. He tells the Pinkies that the travelers came from the Earth, flew to the Blue Country with a Magic Umbrella, and escaped after the Boolooroo stole it and made them slaves.

TOURMALINE THE POVERTY QUEEN

Button-Bright accompanies Trot and Cap'n Bill before Tourmaline and helps tell the story of their journey. He says that a powerful ruler ought to be wealthy and live in a splendid palace, and he asks Tourmaline about witches.

THE SUNRISE TRIBE AND THE SUNSET TRIBE

Button-Bright wishes to return to the Earth and regrets that the Boolooroo stole his umbrella, which might have allowed them to fly. He walks freely with Trot and Cap'n Bill and faces the same uncertain fate before the Council.

ROSALIE THE WITCH

Button-Bright becomes frightened when the strangers' fate is discussed and presses Trot's hand to encourage her. Through Rosalie's vision, he learns that the Boolooroo had cast the Magic Umbrella beneath a cabinet, explaining why he could not find it in the Treasure Chamber.

THE ARRIVAL OF POLYCHROME

Button-Bright remains sad while he, Trot, and Cap'n Bill are led toward execution. He recognizes Polychrome, asks her to help them, and explains their adventures with the Magic Umbrella. He is grateful when Polychrome's interpretation of the laws saves them.

MAYRE, QUEEN OF THE PINK COUNTRY

Button-Bright witnesses Trot's coronation and agrees that returning to Earth is preferable to remaining in the Pink Country. He explains that the Boolooroo has the Magic Umbrella and says that the Royal Record Book proves the Boolooroo has already reigned for his lawful three hundred years.

THE CAPTURE OF CAP'N BILL

Button-Bright mourns Cap'n Bill's capture and is told to stay outside to lead the army and seek a way to rescue Trot and Rosalie. Trot gives him her polly before climbing the wall, and he promises to do his best.

THE GIRL AND THE BOOLOOROO

Button-Bright sees Cap'n Bill escaping along the wall and alerts Coralie and the Pinkies, but they arrive too late to prevent his capture. He gives Ghip-Ghisizzle the Royal Record Book and asks that the Magic Umbrella be returned so they can fly home.

TROT CELEBRATES THE VICTORY

Button-Bright arrives with the Pinkies and is astonished to find Trot and Cap'n Bill standing safely before the Blues. He tells the parrot to stop its long verse because he cannot hear himself think.

THE FATE OF THE MAGIC UMBRELLA

Button-Bright searches for the Magic Umbrella and questions the former Boolooroo about it. He eagerly recognizes Tiggle's description, celebrates when the umbrella is found, and carries it during the departure from the Blue City.

THE ELEPHANT'S HEAD COMES TO LIFE

Button-Bright carries his Magic Umbrella and holds it over himself and Trot. He drops it when it begins transforming, then follows the elephant and later picks up the umbrella after it returns to its original form.

THE JOURNEY HOME

Button-Bright checks the Magic Umbrella before the departure and travels with Trot and Cap'n Bill to Earth. He stays overnight at Trot's house, then flies to Philadelphia and later writes that he arrived home safely.

Button-Bright in The Scarecrow of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1915

Button-Bright is a small, little boy and child: one of three mortal adventurers, a traveler in Jinxland, Trot's companion, and a member of the Scarecrow's traveling party. Formerly from Philadelphia, he has no home because he cannot get back there after losing his Magic Umbrella. He is cheerful, brave, composed, almost without nerves, and habitually content merely to be alive. He accepts good or bad fortune with a quiet smile and no complaint. Fond of Trot and Cap'n Bill and favorite of those who know him, he is nevertheless careless about where he wanders and repeatedly gets lost, usually assuming someone will find him. He is found buried in popcorn snow, calmly eating it and asking for his cap, then joins Trot and the others. During the flight and journey through Jinxland, he follows Trot, eats eagerly, stocks his pockets with food, and says thinking tires him. He notices King Krewl's plan for Princess Gloria, helps Trot find shelter after they are expelled from the castle, and stays at Pon's hut. After wandering off for blackberries and a butterfly, he is lost again but stays cheerful; the Ork carries him back to Pon's hut and Trot. He is later found playing a bass drum and joins the travel toward the Land of Oz. With the group, he discovers the cave behind the waterfall, leads them down its steps, helps remove the Scarecrow from the Ruby Cavern, and carries the Scarecrow's boots and hat. At Glinda's palace he recognizes and embraces the Sawhorse. During Ozma's reception, he disappears once more, and Trot says he is lost.

Button-Bright in The Lost Princess of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1917

Button-Bright is a venture-some boy, described as an American boy from Philadelphia and as coming from Emerald City. What matters most about him is his repeated habit of getting lost, then being found again, alongside his curious, skeptical questions and his unwitting role in carrying Ozma. Traveling with the Wizard on the Wooden Sawhorse and later in the Red Wagon, he supports entering dangerous country, notices the reappearing mysterious city, and argues that what can be seen and heard must exist somewhere. He follows Scraps through a wall, becomes invisible until entering the city, and asks about its dragon, the Herkus, and its music. In the Great Orchard he wanders away, eats an enchanted peach, and keeps its golden pit. He questions claims about Herku's strength and Ugu the Shoemaker. Lost again while the travelers prepare for Ugu's castle, he falls into a deep hole while wandering in moonlight and is rescued by Dorothy and the others; he jokes that he has been found again. Finally, while emptying his pocket of a peg top, string, rubber ball, and the golden pit, he reveals the pit that had unknowingly carried Ozma. Ozma emerges, and Button-Bright recalls the peach as the best he ever ate and tries to tell how he rescued her.
