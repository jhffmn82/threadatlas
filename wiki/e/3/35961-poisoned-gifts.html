poisoned gifts in Medea

Euripides · -0430

Poisoned gifts are fine bridal robes and a gold wreath or crown sent by Medea through her children to Jason’s bride, the princess. Offered as marriage gifts, they are identified as instruments of her death: the crown becomes consuming fire, the robes burn her flesh, and the king dies when he embraces her.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS.

The gifts consist of fine robes and a golden carcanet, which Medea sends through the children to Jason’s bride. Medea says that such gifts can persuade gods and people, while the Chorus identifies them as instruments of the bride’s death.

[_Enter_ MESSENGER.

The poisoned gifts consist of fine robes and a golden crown given to the princess through Medea's children. The crown melts into consuming fire and the robes burn the princess's flesh, also killing the king when he embraces her.
