the letter from Neville St. Clair in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

A pencil message on a book fly-leaf, in Neville St. Clair’s handwriting, arrives for Mrs. St. Clair when Holmes visits. Posted at Gravesend that day or the day before, its coarsely addressed envelope bears signs of a dirty thumb and chewing tobacco.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

“Mrs. St. Clair had fainted at the sight of the blood upon the window,

The letter from Neville St. Clair is delivered to Mrs. St. Clair on the day Holmes visits, bearing a Gravesend postmark and the date of that day or the previous day. Its coarse envelope appears to have been addressed by someone unfamiliar with the address, and Holmes observes signs that the writer or sender had a dirty thumb and used chewing tobacco.
