Egyptian drums in Musical Instruments

Carl Engel · 1875-08

Egyptian drums were chiefly barrel-shaped, including long parchment-covered forms and darabouka-like types; they were played by hands or sticks, with surviving examples braced by cords and catgut bands.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

Egyptian drums included barrel-shaped types, some two to three feet long and covered with parchment at both ends, as well as a type resembling the modern darabouka. They could be beaten with the hands or with slightly bent or straight drumsticks, and surviving examples used cords and catgut bands for bracing.
