Oedipus's daughters in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912

Oedipus’s daughters, Antigone and Ismene, are sisters born from the woman who was both Oedipus’s wife and mother, making them also their father’s sisters. They fulfill filial duties: Antigone guides and supports Oedipus, while Ismene performs ritual offerings. Oedipus praises them for caring for and preserving him as his sons seek power. They accompany him toward his death, mourn him afterward, and become orphans. Entrusted to Theseus, they receive his promise of protection before deciding to return to Thebes.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

[Enter ISMENE]

Oedipus praises his daughters for bearing his hardships while his sons pursued power. Antigone stays to guide and support him, while Ismene undertakes the ritual offerings on his behalf.

CHORUS.

Oedipus identifies his two daughters as having been born from the woman who was both his wife and mother. He calls them two curses and states that they are also their father's sisters.

[Enter ANTIGONE and ISMENE with THESEUS]

Antigone and Ismene return safely and are described by Oedipus as the children who have cared for and preserved him. They accompany him toward his final destination, although he ultimately asks them not to touch or guide him.

[Enter MESSENGER]

Oedipus's daughters, Antigone and Ismene, mourn their father's death after having cared for and guided him. Before dying, Oedipus entrusts them to Theseus, who promises not to forsake them. They later decide to return to Thebes.
