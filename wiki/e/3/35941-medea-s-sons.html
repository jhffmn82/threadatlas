Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Medea's sons are Medea and Jason's children, used to carry poisoned gifts before Medea kills them and takes their bodies to bury at Juno's shrine. In Medea, the two children of Jason and Medea are threatened with exile, carry Medea's gifts to the royal bride, and are later killed by Medea, who takes their bodies in her winged-dragon chariot. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Medea and Jason's children are twins Thessalus and Alcimena and Tisandrus; Medea kills all but one, who escapes by flight. In Heroides and Amores, the children of Medea and Jason are two shared children whose resemblance to their father moves Medea to tears as she fears mistreatment by a stepmother. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Medea and Jason's children are killed by Medea, though some accounts say the Corinthians killed them and later sacrificed at their tomb.

Medea's sons in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Medea's sons first appears

Abstract written by a model

Medea's sons are the children of Medea and Jason. Initially threatened with exile under Creon's decree, they are later reprieved and used by Medea to carry poisoned gifts, a robe and golden chaplet, to Jason's bride. Although Medea weeps for them and Jason values them as part of his family's future, Medea plans their deaths despite the Chorus's pleas. The boys cry for help from inside the house before Medea kills them. She carries their bodies and says she will bury them at Juno’s shrine, denying Jason contact or burial rights.

MEDEA.

Medea's sons are children of Jason and are threatened with exile along with their mother. The Nurse fears that they may suffer because of Jason's actions and Medea's grief, while Medea says that she weeps for them even though she is planning violence against their family.

JASON, MEDEA, CHORUS.

Jason says that the children are sufficient for him and that he wants to give them royal brothers and advance the family through his new marriage. Medea intends to use them to deliver the poisoned gifts and then plans to kill them, despite the Chorus's pleas.

JASON, MEDEA, CHORUS.

Medea's sons carry the robe and golden chaplet to Jason's bride and are reportedly reprieved from banishment. They later cry for help from inside the house, but Medea kills them.

JASON, CHORUS.

The children have been killed by Medea. Medea says she will carry and bury them at Juno’s shrine, while Jason is denied the chance to touch or bury them.

the two children of Jason and Medea in Medea

Euripides · 430 BC · the work’s page · read it where the two children of Jason and Medea first appears

Abstract written by a model

The entity is the two sons of Medea and Jason. They are ordered into exile from Corinth with Medea and face a threat of death if they remain after the appointed day. Jason says his new marriage was intended to secure their future and connect them with royal children, while Medea refuses his aid. During her feigned reconciliation, they greet Jason, are said to be spared exile, and carry Medea’s robe and golden carcanet to the royal bride. Unaware of Medea’s distress, they later return, are sent inside, and are killed by Medea. Their bodies leave with her in the winged-dragon chariot, beyond Jason’s reach.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

MEDEA

The two children return from playing unaware of their mother's distress, but the Nurse and the Attendant try to keep them apart from her. Creon's decree orders them into exile with Medea, and Creon later threatens that they will die if they remain in Corinth after the appointed day.

_Enter from the right_ JASON.

Jason says that he sought his new marriage partly to give Medea and their children security, to raise their sons in a manner worthy of his race, and to connect them with royal children. He offers aid for the children's exile, but Medea refuses it.

CHORUS.

The children greet Jason during Medea’s feigned reconciliation and are told that they have been spared exile. They carry Medea’s robe and golden carcanet to the royal bride, then return to Medea before she sends them back into the house.

[_Enter_ MESSENGER.

Medea's children bring the poisoned gifts to the princess and later are killed by Medea inside the house. Their bodies are carried with Medea in the winged-dragon chariot, and Jason is denied the chance to touch or bury them.

Medea and Jason's children in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Medea and Jason's children first appears

Abstract written by a model

Medea and Jason’s two sons are killed in accounts centered on Medea’s departure: the opening says Medea murders them before Jason, and the main account says she leaves them in Juno’s temple, burns Creon’s palace, then kills them. Other accounts blame Corinthians, who later sacrifice at their Corinth tomb.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Medea and Jason's two children are murdered by Medea before Jason's face.

notes .. notes

Medea left her two sons in the temple of Juno before setting fire to Creon’s palace and killed them according to the main account. Euripides’s chorus says that the Corinthians committed the murder, while Pausanias likewise reports that the Corinthians stoned the children to death and later sacrificed at their tomb to appease their ghosts.
