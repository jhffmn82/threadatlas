Polydore's gold

object · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Polydore’s gold, Priam’s treasure, a large quantity of gold, abundant gold, gold, goods, massive gold, the ancient treasures, the gold, the hidden treasures, those treasures, treasure
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Polydore's gold was kept by Polymestor in his palace, while Hecuba used the promise of additional Priamite treasure to lure him into the tent and Agamemnon judged greed for the gold to be his motive for murdering Polydore.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Polydore's gold was sent by Priam with Polydore to sustain surviving children if Troy fell, and its possession motivated Polymestor's murder of Polydore.

Polydore's gold in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Polydore’s gold is a large quantity sent by Priam with Polydore to sustain surviving children if Troy fell. Brought from Troy and kept guarded in Polymestor’s house, it motivates Polymestor’s murder of Polydore and remains in his possession.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

HECUBA.

Priam sent a large quantity of gold with Polydore so that his surviving children would have support if Troy fell. Polymestor murdered Polydore in order to possess the gold.

POLYMESTOR, HECUBA, CHORUS.

Polydore's gold was brought from Troy and is said by Polymestor to be safe and guarded in his house. Hecuba argues that Polymestor killed Polydore to possess the gold, which he still keeps.
