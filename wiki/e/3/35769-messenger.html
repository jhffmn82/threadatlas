Messenger

person · 2 works · 1 quoted fact · 2 connections
Also called: MES., MESS., MESSENGER, messenger

Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the Messenger brings Eurystheus before Alcmena after Hyllus and Iolaus defeat the Argive forces, explains that Athenian authorities forbid executing an enemy captured alive, and warns that Alcmena will incur blame if she kills Eurystheus. In Hecuba and other plays, the Messenger brings Eurystheus before Alcmene, reports that Hyllus and Iolaus have captured him, and warns that the rulers of Athens forbid killing a captive taken alive in battle.
