the unwritten laws of Heaven

and 1 morethese laws
  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, the law is an infallible and everlasting unwritten law of Heaven; Creon insists that the law and the ruler’s commands must be obeyed, while Antigone distinguishes his published edict from the eternal unwritten laws of Heaven.
  • In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, the unwritten laws of Heaven are immutable divine laws superior to Creon’s interdict, requiring Antigone to bury her brother and justifying her defiance of mortal rule.
  • In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, divine law is the eternal law of right and wrong superior to human ordinance, which Antigone obeys over Creon’s command and which the Chorus says must be honored along with the laws of the land.
  • In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, the law is opposed by Creon when it conflicts with his decree, while Antigone invokes it to explain her duty to honor her dead brother and says the law of conscience led her to bury Polyneices.
