burlesque in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

Burlesque, when indiscriminately applied, spoils and degrades sentiment by caricaturing sacred, heroic, and pathetic subjects; repeated mockery may poison children’s associations with great works, people, and solemn events.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

X.

Burlesque is criticized for appropriating sacred, heroic, and pathetic themes and reducing noble forms to caricature. The unit warns that repeated exposure to such mockery can poison children’s primary associations with great works, people, and solemn events.
