Abstract written by a model

In Alcestis, Alcestis sacrifices herself so Admetus can escape death and preserve his life, giving up her youth and happiness and securing his promise not to remarry. In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Alcestis agrees to die so Admetus can escape his destined death, and Admetus later calls her the noblest of women. In The Library, Alcestis voluntarily dies in Admetus’s stead after Apollo obtains the Fates’ condition that someone may die for him. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Alcestis generously offers herself as Admetus’s substitute when no warrior, servant, or parent will die for him.

Alcestis's sacrifice for Admetus in Alcestis

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Alcestis's sacrifice for Admetus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Alcestis’s sacrifice for Admetus is a self-sacrifice made so that he can escape death and preserve his life. When no one else will die in his place, she undertakes to die, giving up her youth and happiness because she cannot bear separation from Admetus and their children. She also seeks to protect the children by securing Admetus’s promise not to remarry. Afterward, Alcestis is remembered as brave and faithful, while Admetus regrets not dying with her.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

INTRODUCTION

Alcestis undertakes to die for Admetus when he is allowed to escape death if another person volunteers in his place. Her sacrifice is presented as a response to the danger of Admetus’s death and, according to the suggested interpretation, as a way to protect their children from being left fatherless; she also tries to protect them from a future stepmother by making Admetus promise never to remarry.

ALCESTIS

Alcestis sacrifices herself because no other person willing to die for Admetus can be found. She says that she has given him her youth and all her happiness, and that she dies because she could not live separated from him and their children.

Alcestis is remembered as having died to save Admetus, while Admetus regrets that he did not die with her. Her sacrifice is treated as brave, faithful, and worthy of reverence.

substitution of Alcestis for Admetus in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where substitution of Alcestis for Admetus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Alcestis substitutes herself for Admetus, agreeing to die so he can escape his destined death when neither his father nor mother will. After her death, Admetus calls her the noblest of women and vows her tomb divine honor.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ALCESTIS.

Alcestis substitutes herself for Admetus by agreeing to die so that he may live. Neither Admetus's father nor his mother is willing to make the same sacrifice.

ADMETUS, CHORUS.

Alcestis died in Admetus's place, allowing him to escape his own destined death. Admetus considers her sacrifice proof that she was the noblest of women and says that her tomb should be honored equally with the Gods.
