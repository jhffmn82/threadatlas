Greek funeral customs

topic · 1 work · 7 quoted facts · 0 connections
Also called: burial, custom, customary to bury, funeral, preparations for the funeral

Abstract written by a model

Greek funeral customs were elaborate preparations for the dead, called κοσμος. The corpse lay in state with a lustral water vessel before the house doors; mourners lamented, treated their hair, addressed the dead with χαιρε, and received lustral-water purification when leaving after the funeral. The elderly were buried with greater magnificence, and tombs could be magnificent roadside landmarks or chapels.

notes

Greek funeral customs included elaborate preparations for the dead, greater magnificence for those who died at an advanced age, and the display of a corpse with lustral water placed before the house. Mourners used ritual lamentations and farewells, cut or otherwise treated their hair, and observed purification after funerals; tombs were often magnificent structures near highways that could serve as landmarks or chapels.
