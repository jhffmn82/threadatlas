Assyrians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Assyrians were a long-ruling Asian group that fought in Ninus’s conquests and under Sardanapalus against revolting Medes, Persians, Babylonians, and allies. After an early defeat by Bactrians, they prevailed through numbers; later, despite early victories, they were defeated near Nineveh and the Euphrates.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

DIODORUS SICULUS. / BOOK IL

The Assyrians served in Ninus's armies during his conquests. In the first battle against Bactria, the Bactrians defeated and pursued them, killing a hundred thousand, but the Assyrians later overcame the Bactrians by superior numbers.

CHAP. IL

The Assyrians served under Sardanapalus's command against the revolting Medes, Persians, Babylonians, and their allies. Their forces won several early battles, but were later defeated, with many killed in the fighting near Nineveh and the Euphrates.
