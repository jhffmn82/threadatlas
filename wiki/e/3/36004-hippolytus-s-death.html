  • In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, Hippolytus's death results from the wreck of his chariot after the Sea-Bull terrifies his horses; before dying, he forgives Theseus and is promised lasting honor by Artemis.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Hippolytus's death results from Theseus's unjust curse and his horses' panic after a sea-born bull frightens them; his chariot overturns, he is dragged by the reins and fatally injured on rocks, and before dying he forgives Theseus as Diana promises lasting honors.
  • In The Library, the death of Hippolytus occurs after his horses are frightened by a bull sent from the sea; his chariot is dashed to pieces, and he is dragged to death after becoming entangled in the reins.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the death of Hippolytus follows his father's curse and exile toward Trœzen; a sea-monster frightens his horses, his chariot breaks against a stump, and his body is dragged and torn apart by the reins and surrounding obstacles.

HIPPOLYTUS, THESEUS, CHORUS.

Hippolytus's death follows the fulfillment of Theseus's imprecation. He is dragged by the reins after the chariot overturns and is fatally injured against the rocks.

DIANA, THESEUS, CHORUS.

Hippolytus dies from the injuries inflicted when his horses destroyed him after Theseus's curse. Before dying, he forgives Theseus, and Diana promises that he will receive great honors and lasting remembrance among the virgins of Troezene.
