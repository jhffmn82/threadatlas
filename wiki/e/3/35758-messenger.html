Messenger

person · 3 works · 5 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: MESS., MESSENGER, man

Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the Messenger, Pentheus’s slave and attendant, reports Pentheus’s death on Cithaeron and the Maenads’ tearing him apart, then leaves to avoid Agave’s return. In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, the Messenger reports Pentheus’s death to the Leader and Chorus, describing how Dionysus bends a pine tree for Pentheus to climb and reveals him to the Maenads, who tear him apart. In The Bacchae of Euripides, the Messenger, Pentheus’s mountain attendant and witness, reports Pentheus’s death after Dionysus summons the Maenads, who tear Pentheus apart despite his appeal to Agave.
