Dindorf

Also called: Dind.

Abstract written by a model

Dindorf is a textual critic whose recorded work consists of judging verses, words, readings, grammar, punctuation, and suspected interpolations. He repeatedly proposes omissions, including a verse, words, and lines; condemns a messenger’s speech and adjacent lines as spurious; and criticizes grammar and textual criticism. He also restores, prefers, supports, retains, or approves particular Greek readings and conjectures, often alongside Elmsley, Reiske, Brunot, Orelli, Musgrave, and others. Across the records, his activity moves between rejecting disputed material and selecting or explaining preferred textual alternatives.

Dindorf is in one work on this site, The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

notes

Dindorf proposes omitting or altering several verses and readings, including a disputed verse, εκφρωσ', πυκνοισι, and a punctuation correction. He approves Musgrave's interpretation of στεφανοισι, explains the sense of an old man's need for assistance, and condemns certain lines as the work of an interpolator.

notes

Dindorf retains or adopts several readings proposed by Elmsley, including passages concerning the bride's gifts, marriage, παυρον δε γενος, and μενε και γηρας. He is also associated with the reading το γαρ ειθισθαι and with textual alternatives discussed in the notes.
