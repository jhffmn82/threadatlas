In The Seven Plays in English Verse, the murder of Laius is the crime polluting Thebes and is to be avenged by punishing those responsible; Oedipus undertakes its investigation. In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, the murder of Laius is the crime polluting Thebes, which Oedipus vows to investigate and avenge. In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, the murder is the bloodshed whose undiscovered doer is sought and over which the Chorus declines to join a blood-feud.

the murder of Laius in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · 449 to 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where the murder of Laius first appears

Abstract written by a model

The murder of Laius, at a crossroads in Phocis where the roads from Daulia and Delphi meet, pollutes Thebes and is to be avenged by punishing those responsible. It was reported as an attack by foreign robbers, with one servant surviving, but the killers and witness remain unavailable. Oedipus undertakes an investigation, though the opening account states that he unknowingly killed Laius.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

KING OEDIPUS

Laius was reportedly attacked by a group of robbers while traveling, with only one survivor escaping. Oedipus undertakes to investigate the murder, although the opening account states that Oedipus himself unknowingly killed Laius.

[OEDIPUS _has entered during the Choral song_

The murder of Laius remains unresolved because the reported band of murderers has not been found and the surviving witness is unavailable. Oedipus treats the investigation as a duty that should have been completed even before the oracle spoke.

CHORUS.

The murder of Laius was reported as an attack by foreign robbers at a crossroads in Phocis. Only one servant survived, and his account is expected to clarify whether the killers were many or whether one man was responsible.

the murder of Laius in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912 · the work’s page · read it where the murder of Laius first appears

Abstract written by a model

The murder of Laius is the crime polluting Thebes. Oedipus vows to investigate and avenge it as if Laius were his father. Teiresias says Laius’s killer is present in Thebes and will be revealed as a native-born Theban. Though the charge is laid on Oedipus without proof accepted by the Chorus, he recalls killing an old man and attendants in a crossroads confrontation matching the reported attack on Laius.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

OEDIPUS THE KING

The murder of Laius is identified as the pollution afflicting Thebes. Oedipus promises to investigate it as though Laius were his father and to avenge the crime.

CHORUS.

Teiresias identifies the man whom Oedipus has sought to arrest for murdering Laius as present in Thebes. He says that this man will be shown to be a native-born Theban and will be revealed as Laius's assassin.

CHORUS.

A crime is laid on Oedipus, but the Chorus asks how it can assent to the charge without proof.

CREON.

Laius’s murder was reported as an attack by robbers at a crossroads, with Laius riding in a mule-car and accompanied by five attendants. Oedipus recounts killing an old man and the others during a road confrontation and wonders whether the old man was Laius.
