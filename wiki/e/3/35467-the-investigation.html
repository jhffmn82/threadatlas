the investigation in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

The investigation concerns stolen coronet gems. It begins with questions about the household, night noise, locks, and entry routes, then examines the grounds, windows, bureau, coronet, snow, footprints, blood, and window marks. Holmes questions a valet, obtains shoes, confronts Sir George, finds the receiver, works in disguise, and recovers the gems.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

“Do you receive much company?” he asked.

The investigation begins with questions about the household, the noise heard during the night, the locked windows and doors, and possible routes into the house. Sherlock Holmes examines the grounds, the lower windows, the bureau, and the coronet, then continues investigating in disguise before recovering the stolen gems.

“For Heaven’s sake, tell me, then, what is this extraordinary mystery!”

The investigation follows physical evidence at the house and in the snow, including footprints, blood, and marks on the hall window. Holmes supplements this work by questioning Sir George's valet, buying the cast-off shoes, confronting Sir George, and locating the receiver.
