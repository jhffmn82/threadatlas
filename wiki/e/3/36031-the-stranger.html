the stranger in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

A Lydian worshiper and divine representative of Bacchus brings Bacchic rites to Greece. Marked by long hair and carefully protected white skin, he is seized by Pentheus, imprisoned, escapes through Bacchus's intervention, returns to the Chorus, and warns that Bacchus will pursue Pentheus.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

SERVANT. Pentheus, we are here; having caught this prey, for which you sent

The stranger says that he comes from Lydia and that Bacchus persuaded him to bring the god's rites to Greece. Pentheus arrests and imprisons him, but he escapes through the intervention of Bacchus and returns to the Chorus, warning that Bacchus will pursue Pentheus.
