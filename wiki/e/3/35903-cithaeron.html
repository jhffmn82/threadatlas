  • In The Bacchae of Euripides, the mountain is where Pentheus goes to spy on the Maenads and where the Messenger reports that he is killed amid the rocks, trees, and wild dances.
  • In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, Kithaeron is the mountain region where cattle graze and wild women perform Bacchic rites, slaughter cattle, and attack villages; Pentheus hides there to observe the Maenads, who tear him apart and scatter his body through its ravines and woodland.
  • In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, the mountain wilderness is the place where Bacchic maidens gather for dancing, prayer, revelry, and visions, while Pentheus sends men to hunt them and spies there under Dionysus’s lead.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Cithaeron is the mountain region where the Bacchæ and women of Cadmus’s house dwell and hold revels; Pentheus travels there in disguise and is killed there, and Agave later wishes to avoid seeing or remembering it.
  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Cithaeron is the mountain wilderness and pasture where Oedipus is exposed, found, and fostered, and which Oedipus later wishes had killed him.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Mount Cithæron is the Bœotian mountain famed for Bacchic orgies, where Pentheus goes to interrupt the Bacchantes’ festival and is dismembered in its woods.
  • In The Library, Cithaeron is the mountain where Bacchanals rave, Pentheus spies and is torn apart, Niobe’s male children hunt before Apollo kills them, and Tydeus delivers a succession message.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Mount Cithæron is the sacred mountain of Zeus of Cithæron, with ruins, a tomb, traditions of Pentheus and Œdipus, the Dædala procession and altar, and Sphragidion below its summit.
  • In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, Cithaeron is the mountain region where Laius’s Herdsman tends flocks and the infant Oedipus is found; the Chorus calls it his nurse and foster-mother, and Oedipus names it his intended tomb.

Mount Cithæron in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Mount Cithæron is a sacred mountain of Zeus of Cithæron, holding ruins, a tomb, and traditions of Pentheus and Œdipus. It received the Dædala procession and altar; below its summit, Sphragidion was linked to Nymphs’ prophecies.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

Mount Cithæron contains the ruins of Hysiæ and Erythræ, the alleged tomb of Mardonius near the road, and traditions concerning where Pentheus met his fate and where Œdipus was exposed. It is sacred to Zeus of Cithæron.

CHAPTER III.

Mount Cithæron was the destination of the Dædala procession and the place where the festival altar was built. About fifteen stades below its summit was the cave called Sphragidion, where tradition said the Nymphs had prophesied.
