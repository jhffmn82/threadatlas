dragon in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where dragon first appears

Abstract written by a model

The dragon is a golden, crested creature sacred to Mars, hidden in a cavern in an ancient grove and guarding Mars's fountain. It has fiery eyes, a poison-swollen body, three tongues, and teeth in three rows; notes describe it as enormous and able to kill by sting, coils, or poisonous breath. When Cadmus's companions disturb the grove, it emerges, attacks them, and battles Cadmus. After leaving Sidon, Cadmus pierces its throat with a spear, driving it into an oak and killing it. Cadmus then sows its teeth as an unknown seed and later questions whether it was sacred. Accounts alternatively call it a serpent, or a king named Draco and a son of Mars.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK THE THIRD.

The dragon is sacred to Mars and hides in a cavern within an ancient grove. It has crests, a golden color, fiery eyes, a body swollen with poison, three tongues, and teeth arranged in three rows.

opening

The dragon guarding the fountain of Mars emerges from its cave when Cadmus's companions disturb the grove. It attacks them, battles Cadmus, and is finally killed when Cadmus drives his spear through its throat and into an oak.

notes .. notes

The dragon slain by Cadmus was described in the notes as enormous and capable of killing prey by its sting, coils, or poisonous breath. Other writers identified it as a king named Draco, a son of Mars, while Heraclitus maintained that Cadmus really killed a serpent that troubled Bœotia.

opening

The dragon was pierced and killed by Cadmus's spear after he left Sidon. Cadmus sowed its teeth in the ground as an unknown seed and later wonders whether it was sacred.
