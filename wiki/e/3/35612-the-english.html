Abstract written by a model

In Impressions of Theophrastus Such, the English are portrayed as a people whose national life is worth defending and who are urged to preserve their distinctiveness through individual excellence. In Scientific American Supplement No. 360, November 25, 1882, the English people are described as naturally serious and as possessing reverence, respect, and love for the past. In His Last Bow, the British people are portrayed as docile and apparently indifferent to war, though their simplicity conceals firm limits and strong conventions that help authorities detect naval-code changes and pursue enemy agents.

the English in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879 · the work’s page · read it where the English first appears

Abstract written by a model

The English are portrayed as a people with a national life worth defending, encouraged to identify with old English seamen whose conquests and institutions benefited later generations. They praise this ancestry yet overlook its violence and non-Christian religion, are falsely reputed to know Shakspere, and are urged to preserve national distinctiveness through greater excellence among individual natives rather than exclusion.

His Last Bow: The War Service of Sherlock Holmes

The British people are described by Von Bork as docile and simple, but Baron Von Herling warns that their apparent simplicity conceals firm limits and strong conventions. Their political and public indifference to the possibility of war is mocked, although the text also suggests that British authorities have detected changes to their naval codes and are pursuing enemy agents.
