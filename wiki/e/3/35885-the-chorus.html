Women of the Chorus in Iphigenia in Tauris

Euripides · -0413/-0411

Women of the Chorus are Greek women in captivity, exiled from their heart’s home among savage people and cruel gods. Their sea- and water-filled lyrics express longing for Hellas. As Iphigenia’s comrades, they promise Zeus to keep her planned escape secret and aid Iphigenia, Orestes, and Pylades. Accused by Thoas, they are declared faithful by Athena, praise the escape, and depart peacefully.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

PREFACE

The Women of the Chorus are among the exiles in the play, separated from their heart’s home among savage people and cruel gods. Their lyrics, like much of the play’s poetry, are filled with sea-light, waters, and longing for Hellas.

LEADER.

The Greek women listen to Iphigenia's plea and promise before Zeus that they will keep her secret. They are therefore committed to helping Iphigenia, Orestes, and Pylades escape.

CHORUS. [STROPHE.]

The women of the chorus are accused by Thoas of knowing the plot, but Athena later declares that they are faithful and orders that they be allowed to depart peacefully. They praise the escape and obey Athena’s command.

Chorus of Captive Greek Women in Iphigenia in Tauris

Euripides · -0413/-0411

The Chorus of Captive Greek Women is a group of Greek handmaids enslaved at Tauris and assigned to Iphigenia. Formerly from Hellas, they serve as witnesses and commentators, mourning the house of Atreus, recalling Orestes and Pylades’ voyage, and longing for Greece and freedom. When Orestes and Pylades are brought bound for slaughter, they pity them yet rejoice at a captive’s possible return.

the Chorus in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

The Chorus is a group of Grecian captive women serving Iphigenia as attendants in Tauris. They mourn the troubles of the house of Atreus, lament Orestes’ expected sacrifice, recall Iphigenia’s unhappy history, and long for Greece and Iphigenia’s return to Argos. They describe the route from Greece, announce Orestes and Pylades’ arrival, and witness Orestes’ reunion with Iphigenia. As Iphigenia plans escape, they faithfully keep the secret, accompany her as accomplices, support her account to Thoas, and deny sharing responsibility when accused by a messenger. In the end, Minerva commands that the women be permitted to depart from Tauris to Greece.
