  • In Hecuba and other plays, friendship is valued by Hercules as steadfast support in misfortune; Theseus demonstrates it by helping Hercules, offering refuge, wealth, purification, and lasting honor, leading Hercules to conclude that a steadfast friend is more valuable than wealth or dominion.
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Amphitryon, Megara, and the Chorus remain loyal to Hercules and his children despite danger, while Amphitryon observes that misfortune exposes false friends and the Chorus insists that friends are needed even when the dead can no longer defend their families.

friendship and loyalty in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Friendship and loyalty are valued above riches and kingdoms when genuine. They unfold as shared danger and refusal to abandon a companion after a killing, then as a dispute over whether loyalty belongs first to household and children or to a brother, expedition, and sworn oath.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ELECTRA, ORESTES, PYLADES, CHORUS.

Orestes praises Pylades as a friend more valuable than riches or kingdoms. Pylades refuses to abandon Orestes in misfortune, saying that he shared in the killing of Clytemnestra and must share its consequences. Orestes, Electra, and Pylades describe their friendship as a single bond exposed to one common danger.

IPHIGENIA IN AULIS.

The dispute between Agamemnon and Menelaus centers on whether a friend and brother must suffer and act for another’s cause. Agamemnon values loyalty to his household and children, while Menelaus demands loyalty to the Greek expedition and the oath sworn to Tyndarus.
