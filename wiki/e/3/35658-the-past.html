the past in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

The past mixes beauty with ugliness and social limitations; rather than idealizing it as majestic, the narrator values it through personal memory and inheritance.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

II.

The past is often imagined as majestic and graceful, but the narrator maintains that its beauty must be considered alongside its ugliness, ignorance, bores, detractors, and social limitations. He values it chiefly through personal memory and inherited experience rather than through an indiscriminate idealisation.
