Agave in The Bacchae of Euripides

Euripides · -0406/-0405

Agave, daughter of Cadmus, wife of Echion, and mother of Pentheus, is queen of Thebes, a Bacchanal, and priestess of rites of blood. She leads one band of Bacchae on Kithaeron, awakening, directing, and leading the women in worship and attacks. When Dionysus calls them to punish Pentheus, divine madness possesses her; with her sisters she tears him apart and carries his head toward Thebes, believing it a lion’s. Cadmus restores her understanding, and she recognizes Pentheus’s head and body, accepts guilt, and leaves Thebes in exile.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

_Enter_ PENTHEUS _in fury_.

Agave leads one of the three bands of women on Kithaeron. She awakens the women with the God's wild cry, directs them in their worship, and leads them when they attack the herds and later move toward the villages.

CHORUS.

Agave comes with the Maenads when Dionysus calls them to punish Pentheus. Possessed by Bacchios, she tears off Pentheus's arm, helps destroy him, and later carries his head on a wand toward Thebes while exulting in what she believes is a deed of blood.

CHORUS.

Agave returns from Kithaeron in a state of ecstatic madness, carrying Pentheus's severed head and celebrating it as a lion's head won by her own strength. Cadmus restores her understanding, after which she recognizes the head and body as those of Pentheus, accepts her guilt, and prepares to leave Thebes in exile.

Agave in Hippolytus; The Bacchae

Euripides · -0427

Agave, daughter of Cadmus and mother of Pentheus, leads one band of wild women on Kithaeron. As a Bacchanal hunter, she awakens the women with the god’s cry, directs worship, and joins attacks on cattle and villages. In ecstatic Bacchic frenzy, she carries out Pentheus’s foretold punishment, kills him, and returns carrying his head on a wand while believing it is a lion’s. When Cadmus restores her recognition, she understands that she and her sisters killed Pentheus and that Dionysus punished their house. She is then exiled with her sisters from Thebes.

Agave in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Agave is Cadmus’s daughter and Pentheus’s mother, a Bacchant and Mænad who leads one company of Bacchae. Driven into Bacchic madness, she lives on the mountain with Cadmus’s other women, awakens and leads them, and helps repel men who ambush them. Under Bacchus’s possession, she fails to recognize Pentheus, leads the attack that kills him, tears off his shoulder, and carries his head to Thebes as if it were a lion’s head, using it as an ornament on her thyrsus. Cadmus restores her reason; she recognizes that she has killed her son, and Bacchus decrees her exile.

Agave leads the attack on Pentheus and tears off his shoulder, later carrying his head to Thebes as though it were the head of a lion. Cadmus helps her recover her reason, and she recognizes that she has killed her son; Bacchus then decrees her exile.

notes

Agave is one of the women associated with the Bacchanalian rites and is described as boasting that she is undomestic. In the catastrophe, she participates in Pentheus’s destruction and treats his head as an ornament on the thyrsus before recognizing what has happened.
