Polybus in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Polybus is the king of Corinth and Oedipus’s believed father. Oedipus flees Corinth to avoid killing him after an oracle. Polybus later dies from sickness and old age, and a Corinthian Shepherd reveals that he adopted, not begot, Oedipus.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS.

Polybus is identified by Oedipus as his Corinthian father. Oedipus says he fled Corinth after receiving the oracle that he would kill his father, intending to prevent the prophecy from being fulfilled.

_Enter_ JOCASTA.

Polybus, king of Corinth and the man Oedipus believed to be his father, dies from sickness and old age. The Corinthian Shepherd reveals that Polybus had no part in begetting Oedipus but had adopted him.

Polybus in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912

Polybus is Corinth’s king, Merope’s husband, and Oedipus’s adoptive father. He raises Oedipus, is regarded as his father, and dies naturally in old age. His death briefly suggests the oracle failed, until the Messenger reveals he was not Oedipus’s biological father.

CREON.

Polybus is identified by Oedipus as his father, who ruled or lived in Corinth and raised him.

Polybus, who was regarded as Oedipus’s father and king of Corinth, dies naturally after reaching old age. His death leads Oedipus and Jocasta to think that the oracle concerning Oedipus’s parricide has failed, but the Messenger later reveals that Polybus was not Oedipus’s biological father.
