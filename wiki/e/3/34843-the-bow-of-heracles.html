the bow of Heracles in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

The bow of Heracles is a unique, sacred weapon with unerring, deadly arrows. Descended from Heracles and held by Philoctetes on Lemnos, it is his sole great possession and supports his survival by helping him hunt birds. It is also the decisive power required to capture and conquer Troy, though Odysseus says it is useless without Neoptolemus. Philoctetes temporarily entrusts it to Neoptolemus, who deceitfully keeps it for the Achaean cause. After repenting, Neoptolemus returns it to Philoctetes; Heracles identifies it as Troy's conquering weapon.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

PHILOCTETES

The bow of Heracles is in Philoctetes' possession and is described as having arrows that cannot miss and bring death. Odysseus says that the bow alone has the power to take Troy, though it is also useless without Neoptolemus.

_Enter_ PHILOCTETES.

The bow that descended from Heracles is Philoctetes' sole great possession and has enabled him to hunt birds for food. Neoptolemus asks to handle it, and Philoctetes permits him to take it temporarily.

CHORUS.

The bow, described as the sacred gift and reward of Zeus-born Heracles, is entrusted by Philoctetes to Neoptolemus during his attack. Neoptolemus uses deceit to retain it, and Odysseus ultimately decides that the bow can serve the Achaean cause without Philoctetes.

MONODY.

Philoctetes' bow is taken from him through Neoptolemus' deceit and is treated as the essential support of his life and power. Neoptolemus returns it, and Heracles identifies it as the weapon by which Troy will be conquered.
