  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Oedipus's daughters are brought to him, whom he embraces and mourns, and for whom he asks Creon to provide protection from poverty, abandonment, and reproaches attached to their parents.
  • In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, Oedipus's children share his ills, face social disgrace from their parentage, and are the children for whom he asks Creon to care, especially Antigone and Ismene.

Oedipus's children in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912

Oedipus’s children, also his siblings through their mother, share his ills and face social disgrace from their parentage. His sons can fend for themselves, but Oedipus asks Creon to care for his daughters Antigone and Ismene, fearing all his children will suffer.

Oedipus’s sons are treated as able to fend for themselves, but he asks Creon to care for his daughters, Antigone and Ismene. He fears that all his children will suffer disgrace because he killed his father and fathered them by his own mother.
