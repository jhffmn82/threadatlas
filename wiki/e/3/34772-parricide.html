parricide in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Parricide is Oedipus’s unwitting killing of Laius, his father, during a quarrel on the road. Foretold as part of Oedipus’s destiny, the act is later recognized by Oedipus and Creon as a crime that makes him accursed, impure, and defiled. Oedipus accepts the deed but stresses that he acted without knowing either identity, under a divine oracle, and claims divine law frees him from guilt.

4 sections; each heading opens the text

KING OEDIPUS

The opening account states that Oedipus's destiny included parricide and that he unknowingly killed Laius, his father, during a quarrel on the road.

CHORUS.

Parricide is the killing of Laius by Oedipus. Oedipus calls himself a parricide and accepts that the deed has made him accursed and impure.

_Enter_ ISMENE.

Oedipus acknowledges that he filled his mother's bed and that his daughters are both his children and his sisters. He also admits that he killed his father, but says the killing was committed unknowingly and that divine law frees him from guilt.

_Enter_ THESEUS.

Creon identifies Oedipus's crimes as parricide, incest, and the defilement of his mother's bed. Oedipus answers that he committed these acts unknowingly, fulfilling a divine oracle and acting without knowledge of his father's or mother's identity.
