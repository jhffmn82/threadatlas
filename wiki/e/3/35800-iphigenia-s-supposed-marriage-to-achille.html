Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

IPHIGENIA IN AULIS.

Agamemnon invents a marriage between Iphigenia and Achilles to persuade Clytaemnestra to send their daughter to Aulis. Achilles is unaware that the marriage is only a pretext and does not know what Agamemnon and the other informed leaders are planning.

[_Enter a Messenger_.[29]]

Iphigenia's wedding is presented publicly as a marriage to Achilles, and Clytaemnestra brings dower-gifts and asks about the ceremony. In reality, Agamemnon connects the supposed wedding with Iphigenia's sacrifice and death.

[_Enter_ ACHILLES.]

Agamemnon falsely presented Iphigenia's arrival as a marriage to Achilles, causing Clytæmnestra to bring her daughter to the Greek camp. Neither Achilles nor Clytæmnestra had known the truth, and the deception is exposed when the Old Man reveals the intended sacrifice.

The supposed marriage of Iphigenia and Achilles is used to bring Iphigenia to Aulis, but it is connected with Agamemnon's plan to sacrifice her. Iphigenia says that the failed marriage has become a source of shame, while Achilles claims that Agamemnon had promised her as his bride.

notes

The proposed marriage is connected with the double meaning of terms for preliminary marriage and sacrificial rites. The notes explain language describing Iphigenia as being led to Achilles for marriage and discuss how the supposed wedding functions within the deception surrounding her sacrifice.
