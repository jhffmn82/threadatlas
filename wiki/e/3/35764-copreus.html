Copreus

person · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Argive, COPREUS, herald, stranger

Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Copreus is an Argive herald serving Eurystheus, sent to remove the Heraclids; Demophoon stops him, and he threatens to return with Eurystheus and a large Argive army. In Hecuba and other plays, Copreus is an Argive herald and emissary of Eurystheus who comes to seize Hercules’ children for Mycene, threatens Athens after Demophoon refuses, and withdraws promising armed return.

THE HERACLIDÆ.

Copreus, an Argive herald sent by Eurystheus, attempts to remove the Heraclids from the altar by force. After Demophoon prevents him, he threatens to return with a large Argive army and Eurystheus.

THE CHILDREN OF HERCULES.

Copreus comes from Argos as Eurystheus’ herald to seize the children of Hercules and return them to Mycene. He argues that Argos may execute its own subjects and threatens Athens with war, but Demophoon rejects his demands. Copreus withdraws, declaring that he will return with an armed Argive force.
