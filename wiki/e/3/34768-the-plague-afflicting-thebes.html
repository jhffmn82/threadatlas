the plague afflicting Thebes in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

The plague is a disaster afflicting and wasting Thebes, bringing death, damaged crops, failing herds, and fruitless childbirth. According to Apollo's oracle, it is caused by the pollution of Laius's murder and will continue until Laius's murderer is discovered, punished, and removed. Thebes's woes are further increased by Oedipus and Creon's quarrel.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

KING OEDIPUS

The plague brings death, damaged crops, failing herds, and fruitless childbirth to Thebes. The oracle declares that the plague will continue until Laius's murderer is punished.

[OEDIPUS _has entered during the Choral song_

The plague is described as a disaster afflicting the land and wasting Thebes. Its release depends on discovering and removing those responsible for Laius's murder.

CHORUS.

The plague afflicting Thebes is not described directly in this unit, but the city is repeatedly said to be suffering and to have its present and past woes increased by the quarrel between Oedipus and Creon.

the plague afflicting Thebes in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912

The plague ravages Thebes during Oedipus and Creon’s dispute, bringing death, mourning, failed harvests and herds, and barren childbirth. As the land and State lie stricken and distressed, the gods are invoked to drive it away amid fears discord will worsen suffering.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

OEDIPUS THE KING

The plague ravages Thebes, damaging harvests and herds, causing barren childbirth, and filling the city with death and mourning. The gods are repeatedly called upon to drive it away.

CREON.

The plague afflicting Thebes is reflected in the statement that the whole land lies stricken and that the State is distracted and in distress. The Chorus fears that continued discord between Oedipus and Creon will worsen the city’s suffering.
