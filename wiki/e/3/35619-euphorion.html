Euphorion

person · 1 work · 11 quoted facts · 0 connections
Also called: he, himself

Abstract written by a model

Euphorion is an accomplished theorist whose central concern is ownership in material and intellectual production. He opposes communistic principles for material property, yet supports them for ideas, treating claims of original authorship as egoistic while recognising spiritual proprietorship. He insists that ancient, medieval, and eighteenth-century writers receive credit, but quietly appropriates newer philosophic or scientific ideas and may not acknowledge debts. He explains omissions as memory lapses or as effects of unfashionable sources, weakened material, or thought’s universal inheritance, subordinating self to ideas and using Universal Utilitarianism toward helpful fellow-workers.

Euphorion is in one work on this site, Impressions of Theophrastus Such. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

XI.

Euphorion opposes communistic principles regarding material property but accepts them readily regarding ideas. He credits ancient and earlier writers when defending their priority, yet may present recently borrowed ideas as his own by appealing to the universal, inherited, and diffusive nature of thought. His omissions may arise from faulty memory, reluctance to associate good material with an unfashionable source, or a belief that naming the source would weaken the material.
