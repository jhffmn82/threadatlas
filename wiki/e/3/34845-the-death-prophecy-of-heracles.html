The death prophecy of Heracles is delivered at Dodona through the sacred oak and Doves, foretelling his death. It links the end of his undertaking in Euboea and a fifteen-month appointed time to either death or prosperity. Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE TRACHINIAN MAIDENS

The prophecy says that Heracles will either end his life after completing his undertaking in the land of Euboea or, if he survives the appointed time, enjoy a prosperous and trouble-free life. Dêanira connects this prophecy with the fifteen-month period recorded in the scroll.

CHORUS.

Heracles recalls a prophecy from his father that he would die through the will of someone already dwelling among the dead, which he identifies with the dead Nessus. He also recalls oracles from the sacred tree stating that his labors would end when a new period of life began, and he now understands this as a prophecy of death.
