George Eliot · 1879

Felicia is a clever, sensible, vivacious, kindly-disposed woman who helps her distinguished husband and welcomes visitors. She finds Babes in the Wood enchanting; speaking with Hinze, his reverence embarrasses her, and she resists being treated as an oracle.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

V.

Felicia is described as a clever, sensible, vivacious, and kindly disposed woman who helps her distinguished husband and makes her receptions agreeable. When first speaking with Hinze, she offers conventional observations on subjects including Italian travel, Ariosto, French politics, German music, art, and Shakespeare. Hinze’s reverent attention embarrasses and confuses her, causing her to stumble in her replies and resist being treated as an oracle.
