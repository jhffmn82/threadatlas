Haemon's death

event · 2 works · 1 quoted fact · 4 connections
Also called: Haemon is dead, His blood shed, They are dead, death, the loud lamented death of Haemon, two corpses

Abstract written by a model

In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Haemon’s death is caused by Creon’s decree through the murder of Antigone, and follows Haemon’s discovery of the maiden’s body and confrontation with Creon; he plunges his sword into his side and dies beside the maiden, joined to her in Hades. In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, Haemon’s death is caused by Creon’s guilt and punishment through his treatment of Antigone, and occurs in Antigone’s tomb after Haemon finds her dead, attacks Creon, misses, and drives a sword through his own side while holding Antigone.
