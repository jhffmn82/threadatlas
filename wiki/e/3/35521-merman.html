Merman in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

Merman is a lawyer, conveyancer, writer, and editor whose early promise and budding practice fail to advance. Married to Julia with two children, he supports himself partly through miscellaneous periodical writing while pursuing moral, physical, linguistic, and historical studies. For many years he investigates ancient civilisations and proposes a connection among symbolic monuments of widely scattered races. Convinced that Grampus is wrong about Magicodumbras and Zuzumotzis, he writes a book attacking Grampus's interpretation. A review answers with epigrams and ridicule, making Merman widely notorious; the learned know him more through the attack than by reading him. He remains civil but firm in controversy, studies German print and Pali intensely, and publishes a condensed sixty-page rejoinder, admitting mistakes while defending his central theory. The dispute damages his former reputation as a sober thinker, safe writer, and sound lawyer, and causes him to neglect his legal and editorial career. Under want he accepts a post far beneath earlier prospects. In later life he is conspicuously poor, becomes a bore through repeating his arguments, and grows bitter toward the world after public and opponents' treatment.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

III.

Merman is a versatile conveyancer and periodical writer who studies many moral, physical, linguistic, and historical questions. He eventually concentrates on the comparative history of ancient civilisations and develops a theory challenging Grampus, but his published work is publicly ridiculed and he becomes notorious as a supposedly shallow and incompetent scholar.

Meanwhile, how did he like it?

Merman becomes convinced that his counter-theory is fundamentally right and spends months studying German works and oriental languages to defend it against Grampus. He publishes a sixty-page rejoinder, but public ridicule, financial hardship, and social isolation follow; he eventually takes a post far below his earlier prospects and remains marked by bitterness and premature weariness.
