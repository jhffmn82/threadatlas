the globe in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

is_a heavy element [borne downward by its weight] Earth is one of the four elementary bodies and is heavy, moving downward. It can dissolve into water and can also be formed again from water made dense; its surface changes as seas become land, plains become valleys, and hills rise.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLES II. AND III. [XV.60-478]

Earth is one of the four elementary bodies and is heavy, moving downward. It can dissolve into water and can also be formed again from water made dense; its surface changes as seas become land, plains become valleys, and hills rise.

the earth in The Sea Fairies

L. Frank Baum · 1911

The earth is a dry, airy place where humans, horses, automobiles, and other creatures with legs live. Harry Hustle calls it stupid and nearly dies there, though he enjoys a circus. It is Trot and Cap'n Bill's natural home, preferred over the ocean, where their sea forms are changed back on return.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

[Illustration: _Chap. 7._

The earth is the dry, airy place where Trot and Cap'n Bill came from and where humans, horses, automobiles, and other creatures with legs live. Harry Hustle says he nearly died there because it was stupid, dry, and airy, although he enjoyed seeing a circus.

[Illustration: _Chap. 20._

The earth is described as the natural home of Trot and Cap'n Bill, whose sea forms are to be changed back when they return. Anko says that the ocean is not a proper dwelling place for earth people because they remain constantly in danger underwater.
