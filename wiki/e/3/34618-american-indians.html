American Indians in Musical Instruments

Carl Engel · 1875-08

American Indians possessed and made varied musical instruments before European contact, including pipes, flutes, syrinxes, trumpets, and sound-producing vessels. Their music accompanied ceremonies, warfare, funerals, dances, signaling, and entertainment. They also had songs, dances, ceremonies, and musical institutions; their present-day accomplishments are described as below those of their ancestors.

CHAPTER VI.

American Indians made and used a wide variety of musical instruments before European influence, including pipes, flutes, syrinxes, trumpets, and sound-producing vessels. Some instruments were used in religious ceremonies, warfare, funerals, dances, signaling, and entertainment.

The American Indians are described as having possessed varied musical instruments, songs, dances, ceremonies, and musical institutions before European discovery. Their musical traditions are compared with those of peoples in Asia, Africa, and other regions, while their present-day musical accomplishments are said to be below the standard of their ancestors.
