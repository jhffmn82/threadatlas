Aztecs in Musical Instruments

Carl Engel · 1875-08

The Aztecs are a group sharing a race with the Tezcucans and described as having many claims to civilization, splendid living, and magnificent structures. Their preserved musical instruments include pipes, flutes, huehuetl, pottery whistles, pito, and yotl-bells. They used flutes in religious observances and solemn orations, made pipes with pentatonic intervals, and depicted tribute bells in picture-writings. They were said to lack stringed instruments, though that claim is questioned.

CHAPTER VI.

The Aztecs made pottery whistles and pipes, including the pito, and used flutes in religious observances and solemn orations. Their instruments included a variety of pipes, some of which followed intervals conforming to the pentatonic scale.

The Aztecs belonged to the same race as the Tezcucans, who were described as surpassing them in knowledge and social refinement. The Aztecs used yotl-bells and represented them in picture-writings as objects paid in tribute to their sovereigns.
