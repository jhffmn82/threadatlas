  • In The Road to Oz, love and kindness replace money in the Land of Oz, forming the basis of happiness and inspiring the Love Magnet and Ozma's plan to spread love.
  • In Tik-Tok of Oz, love is required by the Land of Oz for retaining the land's love and is shown through loyalty, devotion, and welcoming friendship.
  • In The Scarecrow of Oz, love is portrayed as stubborn and difficult to conquer or destroy, decided by the heart rather than reason or worth, and capable of returning again and again like a cat with nine lives.
  • In The Tin Woodman of Oz, love is presented as dependent on a heart, with losing a meat heart leaving one unable to love, while kindness may replace or outweigh love.

love and kindness in The Road to Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1909

Love and kindness, together with the desire to please one another, replace money in the Land of Oz. Nick Chopper presents these qualities as the reason there are no rich or poor people and as the basis of their happiness. The shaggy man steals the Love Magnet because he wants to be loved, while the girl in Butterfield is unhappy because too many young men love her. Ozma teaches that in Oz people are loved for themselves, their kindness to one another, and their good deeds, and she plans to spread love through the Love Magnet at the Emerald City's gates.

love in The Tin Woodman of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1918

Love is presented as dependent on a heart. Nick Chopper and Captain Fyter say losing their meat hearts left them unable to love, even with tin or kind hearts. Nick cannot love Nimmie Amee as before, but recognizes her faithfulness and plans to marry her through kindness, prompting debate over whether kindness is better than love.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Two

Nick explains that his tin body had no heart and therefore could not love Nimmie Amee. Even after receiving the Wizard's Kind Heart, he still does not love her, though he recognizes her faithfulness and wants to make her happy.

Chapter Three

Woot observes that the Tin Woodman is going to marry Nimmie Amee through kindness rather than because he loves her. The question of whether kindness is preferable to love becomes part of the travelers' discussion about the marriage.

Chapter Sixteen

Captain Fyter says that he lost his ability to love when he lost his meat heart, despite receiving a tin heart. The Tin Woodman also says that he cannot yet love Nimmie Amee as he did before becoming tin.
