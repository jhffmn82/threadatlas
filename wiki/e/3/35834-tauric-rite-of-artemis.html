Tauric rite of Artemis in Iphigenia in Tauris

Euripides · 413 to 411 BC · the work’s page · read it where Tauric rite of Artemis first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Tauric rite of Artemis is a sacrificial ritual for foreign strangers, especially Greeks brought to the sanctuary. Captives such as Orestes and Pylades are sent to Iphigenia, who performs preliminary acts with the blood-bowl and holy spray. At the altar, victims are purified with water, struck by attendants, touched on the throat with a sword, and released; the rite also sends them toward a rocky gulf and holy fire.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

PREFACE

The relevant ritual brings a human victim to the altar, touches the victim’s throat with a sword, and then releases the victim. The preface links this rite to the stories of Iphigenia at Aulis and Orestes among the Tauri.

Iphigenia in Tauris

The Taurians sacrifice strangers to Artemis, especially Greeks who are brought to the sanctuary. The herdsman reports that the people capture Orestes and Pylades for this purpose and send them to Iphigenia for the blood-bowl and holy spray.
