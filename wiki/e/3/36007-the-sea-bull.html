the Sea-Bull

creature · 3 works · 3 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Thing, a bull, a fell and marvellous Thing, a fierce monster, a wild Sea-Bull, enormous mass of waters, horned bull, hornèd Horror of the Tide, monster, sea-monster, the bull from the sea, the woeful monster

Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the bull from the sea emerges from the Saronic Sea, terrifies Hippolytus’s horses, overturns his chariot, and causes his death. In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, the Sea-Bull rises from the sea, terrifies and drives Hippolytus’s horses toward the rocks, wrecks the chariot, and disappears. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the sea-monster appears as the sea rises and divides, sending forth a horned bull that vomits seawater and causes Hippolytus’s horses to drive his chariot over the rocks.
