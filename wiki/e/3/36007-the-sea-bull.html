the Sea-Bull

creature · 3 works · 3 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Thing, a bull, a fell and marvellous Thing, a fierce monster, a wild Sea-Bull, enormous mass of waters, horned bull, hornèd Horror of the Tide, monster, sea-monster, the bull from the sea, the woeful monster
  • In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, the Sea-Bull rises from the sea, terrifies and drives Hippolytus’s horses toward the rocks, wrecks the chariot, and disappears.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the bull from the sea emerges from the Saronic Sea, terrifies Hippolytus’s horses, overturns his chariot, and causes his death.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the sea-monster appears as the sea rises and divides, sending forth a horned bull that vomits seawater and causes Hippolytus’s horses to drive his chariot over the rocks.

the bull from the sea in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

A monstrous bull sent with a wave emerges from the Saronic Sea, terrifies Hippolytus's horses, overturns his chariot, and causes his death.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

HIPPOLYTUS, THESEUS, CHORUS.

The bull from the sea rises with a vast wave and a triple surge, bellowing fearfully. It follows the chariot's movements and drives the horses into panic until the chariot is destroyed.
