Polychrome in The Road to Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1909

Polychrome is the Rainbow's Daughter, a cloud fairy and traveler who lives on the rainbow among fleecy clouds, where magic has no use. She wishes for her father to find her and offers rainbow life to travelers, while watching Johnny Dooit build a sand-boat. Nick Chopper calls her the loveliest vision his tin eyes have seen; she eats dewdrops and dances gracefully. She greets Ozma without jealousy, delights in the Wizard's piglets, joins Oz's birthday procession, and finally dances up a settling rainbow to return to her father, melting into its colors.

Polychrome dances in to greet Ozma and recognizes that her own dainty beauty cannot match Ozma's, without feeling jealous. She enjoys the Wizard's trained piglets and fondles them as they pass near her.

Polychrome joins the procession and is warmly received by the people of Oz. She returns home when a rainbow settles onto the platform, allowing her to dance upward along it until she melts into its colors.

Polychrome in The Road to Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1909

Polychrome, also called Polly, is the beautiful Daughter of the Rainbow: radiant and fairy-shapely, with flowing spun-gold hair and gauzy, fleecy, rainbow-hued robes striped in violet, rose, topaz, olive, azure, and white. She is the daughter of Polychrome's father and fell or slid from her father's rainbow, becoming lost, afraid, worried, and lonely on the road. She joins Dorothy's group to seek help and regains a cheerful mood as they travel. What matters most to her is her distinctive rainbow nature: she dances often, sometimes to keep warm, enjoys beautiful colors, and prefers freshly gathered dewdrops and mist-cakes to ordinary food. At luncheon and later dinners she eats little, misses mist-cakes, and asks for dewdrops, mist-cakes, or cloudbuns. She dances along the road, to music, and for banquet guests; she also warns the group about a nearby city and asks whether the road leads to the Emerald City. She stays close to Dorothy when frightened by the Scoodlers, is captured with the others, and helps create an escape opportunity by slapping a guard and moving away quickly. Crossing the desert, she clings to the bow, sees the green tableland ahead, and warns the shaggy man to slow down; her gown tears in the crash, but she is unhurt. Near the Emerald City she admires rainbow-colored leaves, is frightened by the Lion and Tiger until Dorothy introduces them, then delights in the city's beauty and rides in Ozma's royal chariot.

Narrative, section by section

10 sections; each heading opens the text

5. The Rainbow's Daughter

Polychrome is the beautiful Daughter of the Rainbow, with golden hair and multicolored flowing robes. She became lost after sliding from her father's rainbow, joins Dorothy's group to seek help, and regains her cheerful mood while traveling.

6. The City Of Beasts

Polychrome is hungry at luncheon and tries turkey, bread and butter, and cold tea after asking for dewdrops, mist-cakes, or cloudbuns. She eats only a small amount, then dances along the road before warning the others that a large city is nearby.

7. The Shaggy Man's Transformation

Polychrome travels with Dorothy's group and asks for dewdrops and mist-cakes, which King Kik-a-bray provides. She dislikes the shaggy man's repeated braying and suggests that he might amuse the guests at Ozma's celebration.

8. The Musicker

Polychrome tries to dance to Allegro da Capo's music and feels sorry for him because he cannot help producing it. She goes ahead along the road while the others follow.

9. Facing the Scoodlers

Polychrome stops dancing because the rough walking keeps her warm and asks whether the road leads to the Emerald City. She eats only a small bite of an apple, stays close to Dorothy because she dislikes the Scoodlers, and wonders whether the creatures are friendly.

10. Escaping the Soup-Kettle

Polychrome escapes the first shower of heads and is captured with the others. She dances to keep warm in the cave, then slaps the guard and quickly moves away, creating the opportunity for the shaggy man to seize the guard's head.

12. The Deadly Desert Crossed

Polychrome leans over the bow and clings to it while crossing the desert. She sees the rocks and green tableland ahead and warns the shaggy man to slow down. The crash tears her gauze gown, but she is otherwise unhurt.

15. The Emperor's Tin Castle

Polychrome eats little at the tin dinner because her mist-cakes were mistakenly mislaid. She tastes a few dewdrops and dances to the orchestra, and Nick Chopper promises to have mist-cakes ready for her breakfast.

17. The Royal Chariot Arrives

Polychrome, also called Polly, admires the rainbow-colored, ostrich-plume-like leaves of the trees near the Emerald City and thinks they are almost as lovely as her father's rainbows. She is frightened when the Cowardly Lion and Hungry Tiger approach, but Dorothy introduces her to them.

18. The Emerald City

Polychrome is invited to ride in Ozma's royal chariot with Dorothy and Button-Bright. She is delighted by the beauty of the Emerald City and receives special attention from Jellia Jamb because she appears accustomed to splendid palaces.

Polychrome in Sky Island

L. Frank Baum · 1912

Polychrome is the Rainbow's Daughter, a fairy of the sky who lives in sky country and has powers far stronger than Rosalie the Witch's. She arrives by descending from the Rainbow to aid her old friend Button-Bright and the travelers, protecting Trot from threatened execution. Challenging Tourmaline, she interprets the Great Book of Laws in the travelers' favor, makes Trot Queen by placing Tourmaline's circlet on her, and leads her before the Pinkies. After asking Rosalie to protect Trot and promising to watch over Button-Bright, Polychrome leaves with her sisters on the Rainbow.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Sky Island

Polychrome is named in the contents in connection with her arrival.

A LITTLE TALK TO MY READERS

Polychrome is the Rainbow's Daughter and lives in the sky. She meets Trot and Button-Bright during their adventures there.

THE ARRIVAL OF POLYCHROME

Polychrome arrives by descending from the Rainbow to help her old friend Button-Bright and the other travelers. She challenges Tourmaline's decision to destroy them, interprets the Great Book of Laws as protecting the travelers, and declares Trot the rightful Queen because of her lighter skin. She places Tourmaline's circlet on Trot and commands the Pinkies to greet their new Queen.

MAYRE, QUEEN OF THE PINK COUNTRY

Polychrome helps establish Trot as Queen by leading her before the Pinkies and then departs with her sisters on the Rainbow. She asks Rosalie the Witch to protect Trot and promises Button-Bright that she will keep watch over him.

Polychrome in Tik-Tok of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1914

Polychrome is the Rainbow's Daughter, a lovely, dainty sky fairy and daughter of the Rainbow, with her home in the skies and Cloud Palaces among her radiant sisters. Long-lived, beautiful, and reckless, she prefers dewdrops for food and is beyond Ruggedo's power to injure. After accidentally losing her Bow, she is stranded on earth when the Rainbow vanishes before she can return to it. Unable to find home, she joins the travelers opposing Ruggedo, despite not knowing the way to his cavern. She remains spirited and curious through danger: she dances when Hank routs the Army of Oogaboo, tests the Rubber Country by dancing, bounds over its stream, laughs during the Hollow Tube journey, and takes interest in the strange Kings and Queens gathered before Tititi-Hoochoo. As Queen Erma's guest, she rests, questions the inhabitants of the secluded fairyland, and receives dewdrops at the feast. Her most important role is aiding her earth friends against Ruggedo. She refuses his offer to stay, escapes his efforts to capture her, leaves to awaken Quox after Shaggy Man is transformed, and warns Quox that their friends are in danger. Watching daily for her father, she observes Ruggedo's hidden spring and leads the party through the passage he opened. Finally, out of gratitude for Shaggy Man's unselfish love for his brother, she kisses the Ugly One and breaks the charm of ugliness. Having finished helping her friends, she longs for the sky; when the Rainbow returns, she rides it home to her sisters, bidding her companions goodbye.

Polychrome in The Tin Woodman of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1918

Polychrome is the Rainbow's Daughter, a powerful, exquisite Sky Fairy who belongs to the rainbow and longs to return to her home in the skies. She has floating golden hair, cannot keep still, feels nervous and ill at ease in four-walled rooms, dances in moonlight, and feeds only on dewdrops. Separated from her fairy sisters after losing her Rainbow, she searches for it, hoping to find it at Mount Munch while traveling with others. Mrs. Yoop captures Polychrome while she is asleep, transforms her into a canary-bird, and imprisons her in a diamond-studded gold bird-cage. Polychrome initially refuses to speak, but tells the Scarecrow that she cannot break the yookoohoo transformations. She explains how Mrs. Yoop's apron opens the enchanted castle, warns Woot that Mrs. Yoop reads thoughts, and asks him to take her when he escapes. Woot carries her cage from the bedroom, and Polychrome guides the escape from Mrs. Yoop's Valley. Though her learned fairy magic is limited against the enchantment, she conjures food for the Jaguar and seeks restoration from Glinda or Ozma. Ozma restores Polychrome after changing her through several animal forms. Once again a Sky Fairy, Polychrome helps devise a plan to exchange Mrs. Yoop's and Woot's forms. She then joins the Tin Woodman’s journey while awaiting her Rainbow. Along the way she advises the travelers about Nimmie Amee, recognizes a Tin Soldier, helps free it from rust, leads companions through an invisible region, persuades the Hip-po-gy-raf to carry them across a ditch, repairs Captain Fyter's leg and Nick Chopper's dent, helps the Scarecrow, and uses shrinking and enlarging magic to get the travelers through a rabbit's burrow.
