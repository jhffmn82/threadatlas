Hinze in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

Hinze is an Alsatian man from a family settled in England for more than one generation. Well off and gentlemanly educated, he seeks above all to stand well with the justly distinguished and to present himself correctly. He cultivates low-toned tete-a-tete, praises wisdom, elevates ordinary remarks, and cites distinguished opinions. His deference serves neither ambition nor gain, including with Tulpian, and he has no doubleness toward Felicia. Lacking genuine judgments or lasting impressions, he is not consciously false or fairly a hypocrite.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

V.

Hinze is an Alsatian whose family has been settled in England for more than one generation. He is superlatively deferential, habitually praising others’ wisdom, treating ordinary remarks as remarkable revelations, and citing the opinions of distinguished people. He is well off, has no evident ambition or specific gain in view, and lacks enough independent judgment to distinguish his flattering words from his thoughts.
