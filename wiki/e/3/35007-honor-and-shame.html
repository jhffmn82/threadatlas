honor and shame

topic · 2 works · 1 quoted fact · 2 connections
Also called: dishonour, fame, honour, honourable, shame
  • In Hippolytus and The Bacchae, honor and shame govern Phaedra’s choices as she values honor above survival, conceals love to turn shame into honor, resists comforting falsehood, and chooses death after exposure to preserve honor for her husband, children, and family. (Hippolytus; The Bacchae)
  • In Alcestis, honor and shame govern Admetus’s judgment of remarriage and survival, as he fears being called shameful for allowing Alcestis to die in his place and for continuing to live, insists that remaining unmarried will honor her, and is supported by the Chorus, which holds that her sacrifice deserves lasting honor.

honor and shame in Hippolytus; The Bacchae

Euripides · -0427

Honor and shame govern Phaedra’s choices: she values honor above survival, conceals love to turn shame into honor, resists comforting falsehood, and finally chooses death after exposure to preserve some honor for her husband, children, and family.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

HIPPOLYTUS

Phaedra tries to preserve honor by concealing her love, believing that she must build honor out of shame. The Nurse urges her to speak, while Phaedra fears that revealing the truth will disgrace her and her family.

CHORUS

Phaedra insists that honour, rather than comforting false words, must be preserved. She chooses death to avoid dishonouring her husband, children, and family, and seeks to save at least a fragment of honour even after her secret has been exposed.
