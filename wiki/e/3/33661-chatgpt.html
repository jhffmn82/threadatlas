ChatGPT is a large language model with extensive knowledge, powerful deduction capability, and skill at following instructions, though it is a black box with extensive parameters. It performs well in generate-then-ground, generating and adjusting answers for instructional grounding distillation. ChatGPT in RAGAS: Automated Evaluation of Retrieval Augmented Generation

Shahul Es, Jithin James, Luis Espinosa-Anke, Steven Schockaert · 2024 · the work’s page · read it where ChatGPT first appears

Abstract written by a model

ChatGPT is a closed model with inaccessible language-model probabilities and no token probabilities. It is used to suggest answerable questions for selected pages, answer generated questions with introductory-section context or without added context, generate lower-relevance candidate answers, and sometimes complete contexts lacking back-links. It also evaluates generated answers through numerical or star ratings, assigning 0–10 scores across three quality dimensions, though its evaluations are sensitive to prompting strategies. In GPT Score and GPT Ranking baselines, it scores answers and selects preferred answer/context, but often struggles to identify crucial sentences in long contexts. Assessing its outputs therefore may require sampling multiple answers.

ChatGPT is software used in the WikiEval Dataset to suggest non-trivial questions answerable from Wikipedia introductory sections and to answer them with or without added context. It also produced incomplete answers, completed unavailable contexts, and supplied baseline scores and rankings. It does not provide closed-model language-model or token probabilities and often struggles to identify crucial context sentences, especially in longer contexts.
