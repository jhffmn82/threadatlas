Chrysothemis in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Chrysothemis is the daughter of Agamemnon and sister of Electra and Orestes. Prudent and fearful of danger, she obeys Clytemnestra to preserve her liberty, carrying libations to Agamemnon’s tomb while protecting Electra through silence. She believes Electra is right and joyfully takes tomb offerings as evidence that Orestes has returned, yet refuses to join a killing of Aegisthus because she expects destruction.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ELECTRA

Chrysothemis admits that she believes Electra is right but obeys the stronger powers in order to preserve her freedom. She reports Clytemnestra's dream and prepares to take offerings to Agamemnon's tomb, though Electra instructs her to conceal them and offer their own tokens instead.

_Enter_ CHRYSOTHEMIS.

Chrysothemis arrives joyfully, believing that Orestes has returned because she saw offerings and a newly cut lock of hair at the ancient tomb. She refuses Electra's proposal to kill Aegisthus, warning that their weakness and lack of support would make the attempt fatal.
