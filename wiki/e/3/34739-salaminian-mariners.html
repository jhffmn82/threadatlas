Abstract written by a model

The Salaminian Mariners are Aias’s Greek sailor-companions and chorus. Loyal to him, they fear hostile reports from the Greek army, question Tecmessa, sympathize with Aias, and consider fleeing the Atreidae’s anger. They mourn his ruin, receive his charge to protect Eurysakes, and search for him after danger is reported. Finding Aias dead, they lament their failure to guard him, support Teucer’s burial effort, and grieve Aias, the Trojan War’s hardships, and the loss of their former security.

Salaminian Mariners is in one work on this site, The Seven Plays in English Verse. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

AIAS

The Salaminian Mariners fear for Aias and for themselves because the Greek army is spreading hostile reports about him. They question Tecmessa about the raid, express sympathy for Aias' suffering, and consider flight from the threatened anger of the Atreidae.

Of Aias, and his fortunes as they are. [AIAS _is discovered_

Aias's sailors mourn his ruin and struggle to decide whether to let him speak or silence him. He charges them to protect Eurysakes and obey his instructions, and they later prepare to search for him after Tecmessa reports the danger.

_The scene changes to a lonely wooded spot._

The Chorus searches for Aias, then laments when he is found dead and blames itself for failing to guard him. It supports Teucer's effort to secure burial and sings of Aias's loss, the hardships of the Trojan War, and the end of its own former joys and security.
