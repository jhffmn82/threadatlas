Antigone’s condemnation

event · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Creon's death sentence against Antigone, Death must come, her death, judged By Creon guilty, my bane, she may die, the irrevocable doom, the worst of deaths, this maiden doomed
  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Creon confirms that Antigone must die after she admits burying Eteocles and refuses to repent, deciding that she will be hidden alive in a cave-like place with enough food to prevent pollution from reaching the city.
  • In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, Creon condemns Antigone for burying Polyneices, treating her as a criminal and sentencing her to lose marriage, motherhood, companionship, and life among the living.
