Abstract written by a model

In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Colonos is the god-guarded sacred haunt and Attic deme where Oedipus arrives as a suppliant seeking Theseus’s protection, and where the Chorus defends the refuge when Creon removes the children. In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, Colonus is the sacred, horse-famed deme of Athens where Oedipus arrives and the events occur, and whose people defend Oedipus and his daughters after Creon’s abduction.

Colonos in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · 449 to 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Colonos first appears

Abstract written by a model

Colonos is a god-guarded sacred haunt and deme in Attic land, a suburb of Athens. Fertile and divinely favored, it is associated with Poseidon, Prometheus, and Colonos. Oedipus arrives seeking to remain as a suppliant under Theseus’s protection; the Chorus defends the refuge when Creon removes the children.

Colonus in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912 · the work’s page · read it where Colonus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Colonus is a sacred, horse-famed deme and part of Athens, where Oedipus arrives and the events occur. Its people worship the local hero Colonus and protect its hallowed land, marked by the nightingale’s bower, fountains, flowers, olive tree, and associations with the Bacchic god, Muses, Cyprian queen, and Poseidon. Praised as beautiful and fit for rest, it becomes the place where its people, Chorus, and Theseus defend Oedipus and his daughters after Creon’s abduction.

3 sections; each heading opens the text

OEDIPUS AT COLONUS

Colonus is identified as a deme of Athens and as the place where Oedipus has arrived. Its people worship the local hero Colonus and regard the surrounding land as sacred.

Colonus is praised as a beautiful, horse-famed land suited for rest. Its sacred landscape includes the nightingale's grove, fountains feeding the Cephisus, narcissi and crocuses, the olive tree, and places associated with the Bacchic god, the Muses, and the Cyprian queen.

[Enter CREON with attendants]

Colonus is identified as the place where the Chorus and its people defend Oedipus and his daughters. Theseus is called away from the altar of Poseidon, lord of Colonus, to respond to the abduction.
