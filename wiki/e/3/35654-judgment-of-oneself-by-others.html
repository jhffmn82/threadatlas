judgment of oneself by others in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

Judgment of oneself by others is judging oneself through others; practiced impartially and keenly, it reveals kinship with frailty and vice, enfeebling indignation, scorn, and active heroism against wrongdoing.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

XIII.

Judgment of oneself by others, understood as judging oneself through what one observes in others, can weaken indignation and scorn when practiced with too much impartiality and discernment. By making a person too conscious of kinship with frailty and vice, it may diminish the active heroism needed to oppose wrongdoing.
