excessive deference (Hinze's behavior)

topic · 1 work · 3 quoted facts · 0 connections
Also called: A TOO DEFERENTIAL MAN, Hinze's deferential bearing, complimentary parentheses, make-believe reverence, the superlatively deferential man, worshipful tones

Abstract written by a model

Hinze’s excessive deference is a habitual, studious and deliberate insincerity: make-believe reverence without deep motive or a calculated search for patronage. It unfolds through lowered voice, downcast eyes, hat handling, worshipful tones, compliments, grand questions, inflated commonplace opinions, and eager attention to Tulpian and others’ authority, without sneering or backbiting.

V.

Hinze’s excessive deference appears in his lowered voice, downcast eyes, careful handling of his hat, worshipful tones, and repeated compliments. He asks grand questions, recapitulates commonplace opinions as profound insights, and eagerly attends to the crotchets of Tulpian while appealing to others’ authority. The behavior is a habitual form of attitudinising rather than a calculated attempt to obtain patronage, and it has no contrasting sneering or backbiting side.
