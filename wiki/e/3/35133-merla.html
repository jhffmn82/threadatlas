Merla

Also called: a prettier lady, blonde mermaid, pretty Merla, the blonde mermaid, the mermaid, their conductor

Abstract written by a model

Merla is a blonde mermaid, Princess Clia’s cousin, and ruler of the mermaid palaces when Queen Aquareine is absent. Kind especially to Cap’n Bill, she escorts him into the palace, promises his protection, and explains sea serpents. She offers to escort Mayre to King Anko’s palace. Later she leads Trot and Cap’n Bill on an afternoon swim and through the sea, identifies and explains creatures they meet, orders snapping eels not to threaten the visitors, and returns the travelers to the palace before dark.

Merla is in one work on this site, The Sea Fairies. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Merla in The Sea Fairies

L. Frank Baum · 1911 · the work’s page · read it where Merla first appears

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

[Illustration: _Chap. 3._

Merla, a blonde mermaid and Princess Clia's cousin, guides Cap'n Bill by holding his hand so he will not lose the way. She promises to protect him during the journey and explains that the three sea serpents have gray whiskers and visit one another every hundred years.

[Illustration: _Chap. 6._

Merla warns Cap'n Bill not to spoil his appetite, explains how the mermaids cook without fires, and leads Trot and Cap'n Bill on an afternoon swim. She identifies the jellyfish, singing barnacles, sea pig, and snapping eels they encounter. She orders the eels to stop threatening the visitors and warns that Queen Aquareine may punish them.

[Illustration: _Chap. 7._

Merla guides Trot and Cap'n Bill through the sea, identifying the creatures they encounter and explaining their behavior. She describes codfish as aristocratic, the crabs as varied in nature, and the mackerel's belief that being caught means going to glory. She leads the travelers back to the palace before dark.
