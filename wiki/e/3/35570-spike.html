Spike in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

Spike is a wealthy cotton manufacturer, elector, and self-styled Liberal who votes for Progress, strongly supports the Reform Bill and repeal of the Corn Laws, and favors foreign markets. Trade is central: he understands his own affairs, resists legislation that hampers them, and becomes public-spirited because his economic interests coincide with those of his neighbours, making him a representative of class needs rather than a radical. He is tall, cleanly, abstemious, and melancholy, with leisure-born ennui after making his fortune. Though he aspires to intellectual society, keeps an orderly library, and welcomes beneficed clergymen, his knowledge is limited and his mind resists it. He might have become a member of Parliament or Government under different birth and eligibility conditions.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

VII.

Spike is a wealthy cotton manufacturer and elector who votes on the side of Progress, though he is not inwardly attached to that name. He prides himself on being a Liberal, supports the Reform Bill and the Repeal of the Corn Laws, and becomes a political element because his trade-related self-interest is shared by his neighbours. His private economic concerns are thereby transformed into a demand for general public benefit.
