the castle in Hippolytus; The Bacchae

Euripides · -0427

The castle, Pittheus's castle and royal residence, is Theseus's home in Trozên. After a hunt, Hippolytus and hunters enter while Phaedra is brought out; later it holds her bier and mourning handmaids, opens for Theseus, then closes behind him.

2 sections; each heading opens the text

HIPPOLYTUS

The castle is Theseus's residence in Trozên. Hippolytus and the hunters enter it after the hunt, while Phaedra is brought out from its central door and placed on a couch.

[_The Voice of the_ NURSE _is heard from within, crying,

The Castle contains Phaedra's body on a bier, surrounded by wailing Handmaids. Its gates are opened so Theseus can see the body, and later its doors close after Theseus enters.

house (Phædra's household) in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Phædra's household is threatened by her illness and feared disgrace, which may harm her children. Her passion brings new misfortune; her suicide, accusation, and Theseus's curse plunge Theseus's house into confusion, desolation, and orphanhood.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

PHÆDRA, NURSE, CHORUS.

The Chorus says that Phædra’s passion has brought a new misfortune upon the house. Phædra fears that her disgrace will affect the household and that her children will suffer from it.

CHORUS, PHÆDRA.

Theseus's house is thrown into confusion by Phædra's suicide, her accusation, and Theseus's curse. The Chorus fears that the house of its kings is ruined, while Theseus laments that it has been left desolate and that his children are orphans.
