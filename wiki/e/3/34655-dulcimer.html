dulcimer in Musical Instruments

Carl Engel · 1875-08

The dulcimer is a stringed instrument, called santir by Arabs and Persians, cimbal by some Europeans, and cimbalom by Magyars in Hungary. Beginning as strings stretched across a wooden board, it gradually developed into a more advanced instrument, taking trapezium shape from the fourteenth century. Its early Assyrian representation is imperfect, and it served as the prototype of the pianoforte.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER I.

The dulcimer was known at an early time to the Arabs and Persians, who called it santir, and it was also called cimbal by some European nations. Its gradual development from a wooden board with strings into a more advanced instrument can be traced through instruments from nations at different stages of civilization, and it served as the prototype of the pianoforte.

CHAPTER III.

The Assyrian dulcimer is imperfectly represented on a damaged and poorly repaired bas-relief. A Hebrew dulcimer may have been designated by the nebel or psanterin, though these identifications remain conjectural.
