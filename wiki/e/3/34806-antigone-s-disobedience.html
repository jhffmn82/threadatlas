Antigone's disobedience

Abstract written by a model

Antigone’s disobedience is her deliberate defiance of Creon’s command: she buries Polynices, is initially unidentified by the watchman, then openly admits and refuses to regret the act, placing Heaven’s eternal laws and her brother’s burial above mortal authority and death.

Antigone's disobedience is in one work on this site, The Seven Plays in English Verse. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

ANTIGONE

Antigone openly resolves to disobey Creon’s command by burying Polynices. In this unit, the watchman reports that the burial has occurred, but he does not identify the person who performed it.

CHORUS.

Antigone knowingly transgresses Creon's edict and refuses to deny or apologize for doing so. She says that no mortal command can overrule the eternal laws of Heaven and that she would rather die than leave her brother unburied.
