Trachis

Also called: Trachis' market-place, here in Trachis, ridge of Trachis, Τραχῖνα

Abstract written by a model

In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Trachis is a place of exile near the Mahae Gulf, home to Heracles and Deianira, where the action unfolds through Hyllus’s search for a magician and ends with Deianira’s death. In The Library, Trachis is the destination to which Hercules travels with Deianira, where Ceyx receives him; Hercules later returns there after being afflicted by the poisoned tunic, and the sons of Hercules leave it when fleeing from Eurystheus through Greece.

Trachis in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · 449 to 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Trachis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Trachis is a place of exile near the Mahae Gulf, home to Heracles and Deianira. Its household receives captive women, including Iole, after Oechalia’s conquest. The action unfolds there through Hyllus’s search for a magician and ends with Deianira’s death.

THE TRACHINIAN MAIDENS

Trachis is the place where Dêanira and Heracles have been living in exile and where the action occurs. Heracles's household there receives the captive women, including Iole, after the conquest of Oechalia.

CHORUS.

Trachis is the place where Heracles's household is located and where Deianira dies. Hyllus refers to Trachis when asking whether it contains a magician capable of producing the love charm.
