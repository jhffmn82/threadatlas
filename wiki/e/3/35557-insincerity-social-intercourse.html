insincerity (social intercourse)

Also called: falsity, forms of insincerity, hypocrisy, insincerity, unpremeditated insincerity

Abstract written by a model

Insincerity in social intercourse is a common incidental frailty: people may seek to be agreeable, avoid offense, lack exact words, or fill gaps with familiar opinions. Little unpremeditated insincerity merits lenience, but deliberate, studied, persistent make-believe, especially without appetite, purpose, or admiration, deserves impatience.

V.

The unit presents a little unpremeditated insincerity as an unavoidable part of social intercourse, since people often speak to avoid offending, lack the exact words for their thoughts, or fill conversational gaps with familiar opinions. It argues that this common frailty should be treated leniently, while deliberate and studied forms of insincerity deserve impatience. Hinze’s behavior is more troubling because his deference is a persistent make-believe without a strong appetite, clear purpose, or genuine admiration to excuse it.
