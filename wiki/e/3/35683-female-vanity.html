female vanity in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

Female vanity is presented as native to humanity, more immediately revealing, and apt to store small revenges; though first linked to women’s small authorship, it is ultimately judged not superior to male vanity after stronger male examples appear.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

XV.

Female vanity is described as equally native to human beings but more immediately revealing and more inclined to store up small revenges, like articles in a needle-case. The narrator initially supposes that the self-enlarging disease of small authorship is especially common among women, but abandons that view after observing more powerful examples of male vanity.
