James McCarthy in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

James McCarthy is Charles McCarthy’s eighteen-year-old son, a young man described as not quick-witted but sound at heart. He follows his father toward Boscombe Pool carrying his gun and uses “Cooee!” as their usual signal. After finding Charles injured, James has blood on his hand and sleeve, is arrested, and is accused of murdering his father on circumstantial evidence. At the inquest he testifies but refuses to disclose their quarrel, maintaining that he left Charles before the fatal attack. Though Inspector Lestrade charges him, Miss Turner defends his tender nature, and Holmes accepts James’s account, judges him innocent, and finds him genuinely puzzled. James is madly in love with Miss Turner, but records also state that he secretly married a barmaid in Bristol and is expected to live with Alice Turner in ignorance of their shared past.

IV. THE BOSCOMBE VALLEY MYSTERY

James McCarthy, Charles McCarthy’s eighteen-year-old son, was seen following his father toward Boscombe Pool with a gun. He was found near his injured father with blood on his right hand and sleeve, arrested, and charged after the inquest, but he maintained that he was innocent and had left Charles shortly before the fatal attack.
