Abstract written by a model

In ARES: An Automated Evaluation Framework for Retrieval-Augmented Generation Systems, synthetic data generation uses FLAN-T5 XXL and other LLMs to create positive and negative training examples from corpus passages via few-shot prompting and negative strategies. In Fact, Fetch, and Reason: A Unified Evaluation of Retrieval-Augmented Generation, synthetic data generation was explored as a cost-effective alternative to human annotation, but coherent questions had over 30% hallucination, needed heavy cleaning, and rarely spanned more than four articles.
