Messenger

person · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: MESSENGER, good friend, runner

Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Messenger, Jason’s servant, brings Medea news that her charms killed Creon’s daughter and Creon, describes the poisoned robe and chaplet, and warns her to flee. In Medea, the Messenger warns Medea to flee because the princess and the king have been killed by her poison, describes how the princess’s golden crown and fine robes burned her flesh, and how the king died while embracing her.
