hydraulic organ

Also called: pneumatic organ

Abstract written by a model

The hydraulic organ, or hydraulos/organum hydraulicum, was a primitive portable air-powered organ. Water provided pressure for its bellows and regulated them, but air through pipes made the sound. Placed on a table, it likely used stops and may have had ten pipes without a keyboard. It was later supplanted by the pneumatic organ by the early fourteenth century.

CHAPTER IV.

The hydraulic organ, called the hydraulos by the Greeks and the organum hydraulicum by the Romans, used water to provide pressure for bellows and regulate their action, while air passing through pipes produced the sound. It was a primitive, portable instrument placed on a table, with pipes probably controlled by stops. A representation on a coin of Nero shows an instrument with ten pipes and no indicated keyboard.

[Illustration] [Illustration] CHAPTER VIII.

The pneumatic organ was sculptured on an obelisk erected in Constantinople under Theodosius the Great toward the end of the fourth century. Men standing on its bellows pressed them to supply wind. By the beginning of the fourteenth century, it appears to have entirely supplanted the hydraulic organ.
