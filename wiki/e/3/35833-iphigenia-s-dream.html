Iphigenia's dream

event · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: dark dreams, dream, dream of Iphigenia, dreams, mine eyes astream, vision, visions

Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the dream of Iphigenia depicts her ancestral house collapsing and leaving a golden-haired, human-voiced pillar, which she interprets as Orestes and takes as proof of his death before preparing funeral offerings. In Iphigenia in Tauris, Iphigenia's dream depicts her father's house collapsing and leaving a human-haired, speaking oaken pillar, which she takes with cleansing drops as signs of Orestes' death.

Iphigenia dreams that the house of her father in Argos collapses, leaving one oaken pillar with human hair and a human voice. She interprets the pillar as Orestes and the cleansing drops she pours over it as signs that he is dead.
