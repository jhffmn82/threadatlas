Thebes

Also called: THEBES, Thaba, Thebai

Abstract written by a model

In "Musical Instruments," Thebes is associated with depictions and discoveries of Egyptian instruments, including harp frescoes and an actual drum specimen. In "Vestiges of the Mayas, or, Facts tending to prove that Communications and Intimate Relations must have existed, in very remote times, between the inhabitants of Mayab and those of Asia and Africa," Thebes is described as a place in Egypt and the capital of Upper Egypt during the XVIII dynasty, also known by several Egyptian and Greek names.

Abstract written by a model

Thebes is a place in Egypt, capital of Upper Egypt during the XVIII dynasty. It is called No or Na-Amun by Jeremiah and Nahum, written AP or APE in Egyptian hieroglyphs, and formed as TAPE or Taba; Greeks converted its name into Thebai and Thebes.

Thebes was the capital of Upper Egypt during the XVIII dynasty and was called No or Na-amun in the books of Jeremiah and Nahum. Its Egyptian form is given as AP or APE with the feminine article T, producing TAPE or Taba, which the Greeks converted into Thebai and Thebes.
