the palace of Pentheus in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

The palace of Pentheus, the royal residence in Thebes, is where the women of Cadmus’s house are driven into Bacchic madness. Bacchus orders drums around it, shakes it, surrounds Semele’s tomb with apparent fire, defeats servants’ efforts, and throws the palace down shattered.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE BACCHÆ.

The palace of Pentheus is the place from which the women of Cadmus's house have been driven into Bacchic madness. Bacchus directs his Chorus to beat their drums around the palace so that Thebes may witness the rites.

SERVANT. Pentheus, we are here; having caught this prey, for which you sent

Bacchus causes Pentheus's palace to shake, and fire appears around the sacred tomb of Semele. Pentheus's servants labor unsuccessfully to extinguish the apparent fire, and the palace is then thrown down and shattered.
