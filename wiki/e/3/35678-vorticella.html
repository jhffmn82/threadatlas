Abstract written by a model

Vorticella is a portly lady in silk attire who lives in Pumpiter and is married to an important townsman. She authored only The Channel Islands, with Notes and an Appendix, but treats it as a chronic social and moral preoccupation. She lends it to new acquaintances, requires them to read her album of reviews, defends its arguments on Jersey fisheries and drag-nets, and rejects disagreeing critics. Her authorship magnifies her self-importance and makes her tiresome.

Vorticella is in one work on this site, Impressions of Theophrastus Such. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

XV.

Vorticella wrote only one book, but made it a persistent subject of conversation and social display. She kept an album of critical opinions, expected visitors to read it, defended her arguments about the Jersey fisheries and drag-nets, dismissed or attacked critics who disagreed with her, and continued lending the book to new acquaintances. Her single publication made her more tiresome because it enlarged her sense of self-importance.
