Boscombe Pool in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

Boscombe Pool is a little, reed-girt lake or sheet of water in Boscombe Valley, about fifty yards across, formed by a spreading stream and bordered by woodland, grass, and reeds. At the boundary of Hatherley Farm and Turner’s private park, it becomes the site of Charles McCarthy’s death; Holmes examines its wet ground and woods for tracks and evidence leading to the murderer.

IV. THE BOSCOMBE VALLEY MYSTERY

Boscombe Pool is a small lake formed by the spreading stream in Boscombe Valley. Charles McCarthy was found dead beside it after being seen there with James during an apparent quarrel.

There was no rain, as Holmes had foretold, and the morning broke bright

Boscombe Pool is the site where McCarthy senior’s body was found. Holmes examines the surrounding wet ground and woods, finding tracks and other evidence that lead him to the murderer.
