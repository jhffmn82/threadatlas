Aegeus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Aegeus recognized and accepted Theseus through the tokens that Theseus brought to Athens. He sacrificed the Marathonian bull to Apollo. He sent a pilot with Theseus and ordered that white sails be used if Theseus survived and black sails if he perished. Seeing the returning black sails, Aegeus believed Theseus had died and threw himself from the top of the citadel.

Aegeus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Aegeus, described chiefly as Pandion’s son, holds the whole power after a divided government, though some accounts call Scyrius his father. After marriages to Meta and Chalciope and having no child, he consults an oracle, goes to Troezen, and fathers Theseus with Aethra. He leaves a sword and sandals beneath a rock for the child, but later fails to recognize Theseus and sends him against the Marathonian bull. Theseus’s sword reveals him; Aegeus knocks away Medea’s poisoned cup and expels her. He orders white sails for Theseus’s safe return, sees black instead, believes him dead, and dies.

Ægeus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Ægeus is king and ruler of Athens, brother of Pallas, father of Theseus, and husband of Medea. Jealous of Androgeus and aware that Pallas’s sons conspire against him, he causes Androgeus’s assassination. After sheltering and marrying Medea, he unknowingly offers Theseus a poisoned cup, recognizes him by his sword’s hilt, and saves him. He then celebrates Theseus with sacrifices, gifts, and a festival, later receiving help against Minos.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

front matter

Ægeus marries Medea and nearly has Theseus poisoned when Theseus returns home. He later receives assistance from the Myrmidons sent by Æacus against Minos.

opening

Ægeus shelters Medea and marries her, though the text blames him for this deed. He unknowingly presents the poisoned cup to Theseus, then recognizes his son by the markings on his sword and knocks the cup away. Rejoicing that Theseus is safe, he offers sacrifices and gifts to the gods and celebrates with the people.

notes .. notes

Ægeus, the king of Athens, became jealous of Androgeus, particularly because he knew that the sons of Pallas were conspiring against him. He caused Androgeus to be assassinated near Œnoë.
