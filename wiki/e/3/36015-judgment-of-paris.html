Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, it is Paris’s judgment among three goddesses that leads to the Trojan War, Troy’s destruction, and mourning. In Hecuba and other plays, it is the beauty contest in which Paris awards victory to Venus for promising Helen, a choice presented as causing the Trojan War. In The Trojan Women of Euripides, it is Paris’s choice of Cypris as fairest after she promises him Helen’s love, a judgment that leads to Helen’s removal from Sparta. In Heroides and Amores, it is the beauty contest in which Paris judges Venus, Juno, and Minerva, awards victory to Venus, and accepts her promise of Helen. In The Library, it is the judgment for which Zeus sends Hermes to bring Hera, Athena, and Aphrodite before Alexander, who chooses Aphrodite because she promises him Helen. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, it is the competition among Juno, Venus, and Minerva for the golden apple, which Paris decides in Venus’s favor.

the judgment of Paris in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · about 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where the judgment of Paris first appears

Abstract written by a model

The judgment of Paris is a contest over beauty among Hera, Aphrodite, and Athena, also called Juno, Venus, and Minerva. Paris judges the rivals and awards victory to Venus, who promises him Helen as his reward. The choice is presented as bringing vengeance from a foreign state and causing the Trojan War, though Hecuba rejects it as an excuse for Helen’s conduct.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS.

The chorus recalls that Paris judged three rival goddesses and awarded the prize to bright Venus. It presents this choice as the source of the woes that brought vengeance and war upon Troy.

MENELAUS, HECUBA, CHORUS.

The Judgment of Paris is described as the contest in which Paris awarded the prize of beauty to Venus over Juno and Minerva. Helen says that Venus then promised him Helen as his reward, while Hecuba rejects this account as an excuse for Helen’s conduct.

HELEN.

The Judgment of Paris was the contest in which Hera, Aphrodite, and Pallas asked Paris to decide which goddess was most beautiful. Aphrodite won by promising Paris Helen, leading to the events that caused the Trojan War.

the Judgment of Paris in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · about 24 to 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where the Judgment of Paris first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Judgment of Paris is a beauty contest in which Jupiter’s messenger commands Paris to judge Venus, Juno, and Minerva/Pallas, who appear before him, reportedly naked, on Mount Ida. Juno offers kingdoms, Pallas valor, and Venus Helen. Paris accepts Venus’s promise, awards her victory, and the event is recalled as beginning a disastrous change in his love, though Helen doubts his account and the alleged prize.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Oenone Paridi 2

The Judgment of Paris is recalled as the day when Venus, Juno, and Minerva appeared before Paris to be judged. Oenone says that this event marked the beginning of the disastrous change in Paris’s love.

XVI

Paris is commanded by Jupiter's messenger to judge the beauty of Venus, Pallas, and Juno. Juno offers kingdoms, Pallas offers valor, and Venus offers Helen; Paris chooses Venus and gives her the victory.

XVII

Paris claims that three goddesses appeared naked before him on Mount Ida and that Venus offered him Helen as the reward for choosing her. Helen doubts that divine beings submitted their beauty to Paris's judgment and suggests that the claim about her being the prize is fabricated.
