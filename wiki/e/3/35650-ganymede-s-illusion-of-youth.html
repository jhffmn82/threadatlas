XII.

Ganymede's illusion of youth developed through his family's treatment of him, repeated astonishment from strangers, critical commentary, and his own identification with the role of the young genius. The illusion survives his aging, changed physique, marriage, and government employment, and is reinforced by his manners, clothing, assumptions about others' impressions, and the continued descriptions of him as young.
