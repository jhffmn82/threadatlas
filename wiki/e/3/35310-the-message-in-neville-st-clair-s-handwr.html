the message in Neville St. Clair's handwriting in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

A hurried pencil message in Neville St. Clair's handwriting, written on an octavo book's fly-leaf, reassures Mrs. St. Clair not to fear, says a huge error will take time to rectify, and asks patience. Neville gives it, with his ring, to the Lascar; it reaches her only after a probable sailor-caused delay.

The message reads: "Dearest do not be frightened. All will come well. There is a huge error which it may take some little time to rectify. Wait in patience.—NEVILLE." It is written in pencil on the fly-leaf of an octavo book, and Mrs. St. Clair identifies it as Neville's hurried handwriting.

Neville St. Clair writes his wife a hurried note saying that she has no cause to fear and gives it to the Lascar along with his ring. The note reaches her only after a delay, probably because a sailor carried it from the Lascar.
