Nessus

person · 6 works · 18 quoted facts · 8 connections
Also called: Centaur, The Centaur Nessus, the Centaur, the Centaur-Thing, the centaur, the shagg'd Centaur, the weird Thing, this Centaur, Νέσσον, Νέσσος, Νέσσου, Νέσσῳ
  • In Heroides and Amores, Nessus is a centaur who dies in the river Euenus after Hercules strikes him with an arrow, and he tells Deianira that his blood possesses the power of love.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Nessus is a centaur and hired ferryman who attempts to ravish Dejanira after carrying her across the Evenus, and he gives her instructions for a substance meant to compel Hercules’s love as he dies.
  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Nessus ferries Deianira across the Evenus, touches her wantonly, is killed by Heracles, and deceives her with his blood as a supposed love charm.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Nessus carries Deïanira across the swollen Evenus, tries to abduct her, and gives her his blood-stained tunic as revenge after Hercules kills him.
  • In The Library, Nessus operates a paid ferry across the Evenus, attempts to assault Deianeira, and deceives her with instructions to use his blood as a love charm after Hercules shoots him.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Nessus is a Centaur who carries travelers across a river for a fee, tries to run away with Dejanira, and tells her to preserve some of his blood as a charm to maintain her husband's love after Hercules shoots him.

Nessus in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Nessus is a hairy-breasted, deceptive Centaur who ferries wayfarers across the Evenus. While carrying Deianira, he touches her wantonly, and Heracles kills him with an arrow. Dying, he gives Deianira his blood as a supposed love charm; she later uses it, realizing too late that Nessus deceived her to destroy Heracles.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS.

Nessus ferried travelers across the Evenus and, while carrying Deianira, touched her wantonly. Heracles killed him with an arrow, and while dying Nessus told Deianira to collect the blood from his wound as a charm to preserve Heracles' love. Deianira later realizes that Nessus deceived her in order to destroy Heracles.

CHORUS.

Nessus is identified as the Centaur whose deceptive advice led Deianira to use the robe as a love charm. Though Nessus is dead, Heracles understands that Nessus has thereby caused his living death.

Nessus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Nessus is a centaur who operates a paid ferry across the Evenus, claiming divine authorization for his justice. While ferrying Deianeira, he attempts to assault her. Hercules shoots him through the heart, and, dying, Nessus deceives Deianeira by giving instructions to use his blood as a love charm.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE LIBRARY, II. vir. 6

Nessus the Centaur ferried passersby across the river Evenus for payment, claiming that the gods had granted him this service because of his justice. While carrying Deianeira, he attempted to assault her, and as he was dying he gave her instructions for preparing a love charm for Hercules.

THE LIBRARY, II. vu. 6-7

Nessus ferries Deianira across the river Evenus and attempts to violate her. Hercules shoots him through the heart, and while dying Nessus deceives Deianira by presenting his blood as a love charm.
