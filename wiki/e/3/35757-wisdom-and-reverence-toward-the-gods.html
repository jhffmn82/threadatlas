Wisdom and reverence toward the gods is a mortal virtue and the most honorable, wisest way of life. It requires respecting divine law and avoiding plans beyond mortal limits, bringing freedom from grief. The Chorus contrasts it with Pentheus's forceful resistance to a god; the Messenger and Bacchus conclude that Cadmus's family would have been happy through such wisdom and acceptance.

The Chorus identifies wisdom with respecting divine law and avoiding plans that exceed what is proper for mortals. It presents reverence toward the gods as the source of a life free from grief and condemns Pentheus's attempt to overcome a god by force.

The Messenger states that wisdom and reverence for the gods are the most honorable and wisest course for mortals. Bacchus adds that Cadmus's family would have been happy if it had known how to be wise and accepted him as the son of Jupiter.
