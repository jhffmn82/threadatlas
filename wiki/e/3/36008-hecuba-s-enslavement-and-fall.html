enslavement of the Trojan women in The Trojan Women of Euripides

Euripides · 414 BC · the work’s page · read it where enslavement of the Trojan women first appears

Abstract written by a model

The enslavement of the Trojan women is a captivity imposed after Troy’s defeat in the Trojan War. Captured women are allotted to Greek leaders and territories, facing forced lives as bondmaids, household workers, water carriers, nurses, or unwilling bedmates. Hecuba is assigned to Odysseus, while Andromache is chosen for Achilles’ son’s household and fears both betraying Hector and provoking further suffering. The women mourn Troy, are ordered from their ruined land, and depart toward Greek ships and bondage.
