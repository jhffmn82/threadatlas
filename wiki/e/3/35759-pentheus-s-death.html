Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Pentheus’s death is a divine punishment for contempt of Bacchus; the Mænads tear him from a pine, attack him, scatter his body, and Agave carries his head on a thyrsus. In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Pentheus’s dismemberment is caused by the Mænads, including Agave, Ino, Autonoe, and the Bacchic crowd, who tear him apart and scatter his limbs and flesh. In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, Pentheus's death is divine justice for opposing Dionysus and occurs when, at Dionysus's command, the Maenads pull down his pine tree and tear him apart. In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, the tearing apart of Pentheus is carried out by Agave, Ino, Autonoë, and the Maenads, who scatter his body parts through the mountain woodland while Agave carries away his head. In The Bacchae of Euripides, Pentheus's death is a divine punishment for denying and mocking Dionysus; after Dionysus maddens the Maenads, Pentheus falls from a pine tree and is torn apart. In The Bacchae of Euripides, Pentheus's dismemberment is inflicted by the Maenads in a state of religious frenzy, as Agave, Ino, Autonoe, and the others tear him apart and scatter his flesh, limbs, and bones. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Pentheus’s dismemberment fulfills Tiresias’s prophecy, as Agave, Autonoë, Ino, and the other Bacchantes rip apart his body and remove his head. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Pentheus's death occurs during the rites on Mount Cithaeron, where Agave, Autonoe, Ino, and the other Bacchanals mistake him for a wild boar and tear him apart.

Pentheus’s dismemberment in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Pentheus’s dismemberment first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pentheus’s dismemberment is an event caused by the Mænads, including Agave, Ino, Autonoe, and the Bacchic crowd. Agave tears off a shoulder, Ino the other side, and the others rip him apart, scattering his arms, feet, sides, and remaining flesh.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

MESS. O house, which wast formerly prosperous in Greece! house of the

Agave tears off one of Pentheus's shoulders, Ino tears the other side, and Autonoe and the other Mænads join in ripping him apart. His arms, feet, sides, and remaining flesh are separated and scattered.

notes

Pentheus is torn apart by Agave and the Mænads in the catastrophe of the Bacchae. The notes connect this dismemberment with Dionysus’s epithet Ωμαδιος and cite traditions in which Pentheus is transformed into a bull before being destroyed.
