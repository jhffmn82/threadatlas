slavery and captivity in The Trojan Women of Euripides

Euripides · -0414

Slavery and captivity are the imposed fate of the defeated Trojan women: confined near ruined Troy, separated from their city, and awaiting lots assigning Greek masters and destinations. It unfolds from confinement and uncertainty to Hecuba’s expectation of humiliating servile labor.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE TROJAN WOMEN

The Trojan women remain confined in huts near the ruined walls while they await the lots determining their Greek masters and destinations. Their captivity is presented as forced separation from Troy and an uncertain life as slaves.

[TALTHYBIUS, _followed by some Soldiers, enters from the left_.

Slavery is the fate imposed on the defeated Trojan women. Hecuba expects to perform the basest tasks, including opening and shutting doors and grinding meal, despite having once been queen.

slavery in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Slavery is the condition imposed on conquered Trojan women after Troy’s destruction. Polyxena rejects survival under it, choosing death to preserve freedom from dishonor. Hecuba, Andromache, and others face degrading service or forced relationships despite noble birth; Andromache’s enslavement to Neoptolemus silences her and makes resistance dangerous.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

HECUBA.

Polyxena contrasts her former life as a free princess with the servitude that awaits her if she survives. She chooses death because she believes it preserves her freedom from dishonor and slavery.

CHOR. Attendants of the aged Hecuba,

Slavery is the fate awaiting Hecuba, Andromache, and the other surviving Trojan women after the city’s destruction. Their noble birth offers no protection, and they are assigned to degrading service or forced relationships in Greece.

ANDROMACHE.

Slavery is Andromache’s present condition after she was taken from Troy and awarded to Neoptolemus. She fears that her status prevents her from speaking freely and makes resistance to Hermione dangerous.

slavery in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Slavery is a condition of bondage imposed by superior force. For Hecuba and the captured Trojan women, it follows lost royal prosperity and the fall of their city, bringing sale, forced labor, sexual subjection, captivity, and an unbearable loss of children, home, and status.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

HECUBA. CHORUS.

Slavery is presented as the fate of the captured Trojan women, who may be purchased, forced to labor, and subjected to masters' sexual power. Hecuba and the Chorus describe slavery as degrading and unbearable.

FEMALE ATTENDANT, CHORUS, HECUBA.

Hecuba describes herself as once a queen but now a slave, childless, cityless, and captive. The Chorus describes the Trojan women as dragged from their city and carried across the sea into captivity.
