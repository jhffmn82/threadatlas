Abstract written by a model

Neville St. Clair is a thirty-seven-year-old former reporter who lives with his wife, Mrs. St. Clair, and their two children at The Cedars, a large villa in Lee. He is described as temperate, popular, a good husband and affectionate father, with interests in several companies, modest debts of £88 10s., and £220 in bank credit; there is no apparent reason to suspect money trouble. He normally commutes from Cannon Street on the 5:14. Unknown to his family, however, he has adopted the identity and disguise of Hugh Boone, a beggar, and earns his income by begging in London, averaging less than £700 a year and even making £2 on a very bad day. After backing a friend's bill, receiving a writ for £25, and using the disguise, he is seen by Mrs. St. Clair at an upstairs window of the Swandam Lane opium den, agitated and beckoning, then disappears. Blood at the window, clothing in the building, and his coat in river mud lead to fears that he entered the den and never left; a hurried note in his handwriting asks his wife to wait because a huge error is being rectified. Arrested as Hugh Boone and initially tied to a false murder charge, he is ultimately revealed as Boone himself. He explains that he concealed his begging from his family and maintained the disguise after his wife saw him at the den.

Neville St. Clair is described as a thirty-seven-year-old man of temperate habits, a good husband, an affectionate father, and a popular local resident with sufficient money. He was seen by Mrs. St. Clair at an upstairs window of the opium den, appearing terribly agitated and beckoning frantically, after which he vanished; his clothes, apart from his coat, were later found in the building.

Neville St. Clair remains missing after Mrs. St. Clair apparently sees him at the window in Swandam Lane. His coat is found in the river mud, but his body is not recovered. A letter apparently written by him says that a huge error is being rectified and asks Mrs. St. Clair to wait patiently.
