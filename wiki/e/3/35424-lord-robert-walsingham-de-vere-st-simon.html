Lord Robert Walsingham de Vere St. Simon in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

Lord Robert Walsingham de Vere St. Simon is an English peer, nobleman, and second son of the Duke of Balmoral, born in 1846 and forty-one at the time of the account. He has served as Under-Secretary for the colonies, owns Birchmoor, and is described as pleasant and cultured, pale, carefully and foppishly dressed, with golden eyeglasses. After travelling in the United States, he meets the wealthy American mining heiress Hatty Doran in San Francisco and marries her. When she disappears during the wedding breakfast, depriving him of the wedding and causing public humiliation, he becomes Sherlock Holmes's client while Lestrade investigates. He admits Flora Millar had been very friendly with him, and remains angry and cold when Holmes reveals Mrs. Francis Hay Moulton and Francis Hay Moulton.

X. THE ADVENTURE OF THE NOBLE BACHELOR

Lord Robert Walsingham de Vere St. Simon, the second son of the Duke of Balmoral, marries Miss Hatty Doran. After she disappears during the wedding breakfast, he contacts Sherlock Holmes for assistance, while Mr. Lestrade of Scotland Yard is already acting in the matter.

Lord Robert St. Simon consults Sherlock Holmes about his bride’s disappearance. He describes Hatty Doran as a wealthy American mining heiress with a wild, fearless, impetuous nature, and admits that Flora Millar had previously been on a very friendly footing with him.

Lord St. Simon believes he has been publicly slighted when his bride disappears immediately after their wedding ceremony. Holmes introduces him to Mrs. Francis Hay Moulton and Francis Hay Moulton, but he remains angry, coldly shakes the woman’s hand, and leaves without joining the supper.
