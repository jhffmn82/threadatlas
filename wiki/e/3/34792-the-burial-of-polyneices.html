Abstract written by a model

In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Polynices’ burial is the forbidden burial rite performed by an unidentified person, consisting of dust placed over his body and the religious rites required for the dead. In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, the burial of Polyneices is the funeral rite requested by Polyneices and ultimately performed despite Creon’s prohibition.

the burial of Polyneices in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912 · the work’s page · read it where the burial of Polyneices first appears

Abstract written by a model

The burial of Polyneices is a funeral rite he requests from Antigone and Ismene if he dies at Thebes. Though Creon forbids it, divine law requires it. Someone begins the ritual by sprinkling dust on the corpse, Antigone vows to finish it, and, after Teiresias warns him, Creon reverses his decision and his henchmen perform the burial rites.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

[Enter ANTIGONE and ISMENE with THESEUS]

Polyneices asks Antigone and Ismene to grant him burial and proper funeral rites if he dies in the expedition against Thebes. He says that their care in death will add to the praise they have earned by serving their father.

ANTIGONE

The burial of Polyneices is forbidden by Creon but undertaken in part when someone sprinkles dust over the corpse and performs the proper ritual. Antigone claims that she will complete the burial despite the danger.

[Enter TEIRESIAS and BOY]

Creon initially refuses to allow Polyneices’s burial, even in the face of divine signs. After Teiresias’s warning, he orders his men to bury the body, and the first Messenger reports that the burial rites have been performed.
