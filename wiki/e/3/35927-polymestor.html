Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Polymestor is a Thracian king, Priam’s friend, and guardian of Polydore who murders the boy for his gold and is punished by Hecuba, the Trojan women, and Agamemnon. In Hecuba and other plays, Polymestor is a Thracian king, Priam’s friend, and fosterer of Polydore who murders him for entrusted gold, is blinded by Hecuba and the Trojan women, and is exiled by Agamemnon. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Polymnestor is a greedy and treacherous Thracian king who murders his fosterchild Polydorus for wealth and is blinded by Hecuba and the Trojan matrons.

Polymestor in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Polymestor first appears

Abstract written by a model

Polymestor is a Thracian king who rules Thrace and is Priam’s friend. Priam and Hecuba entrust Polydore and gold to his care, making him the boy’s guardian and guest-friend of Hecuba. He betrays that trust: he murders Polydore for the gold, keeps it in his house, casts the body into the sea, and denies it burial. He first claims Polydore is alive, then admits the killing. His acts violate hospitality, human laws, and Jupiter Hospitalis, and are judged injustice and impiety. Hecuba and the Trojan women kill his two sons and blind him. Helplessly pursuing them, he appeals to Agamemnon, who finds him guilty and orders his exile to a desert island.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

HECUBA.

Polymestor is the Thracian friend of Priam and ruler of a warlike people in the Chersonese. Although he had received Polydore into his care, he murdered him to seize the gold and threw his body into the sea.

FEMALE ATTENDANT, CHORUS, HECUBA.

Polymestor, king of the Thracians, was the guardian of Polydore and his gold. He murdered Polydore for the gold, despite having shared hospitality with Hecuba, and failed to provide the body with a tomb.

POLYMESTOR, HECUBA, CHORUS.

Polymestor says that Polydore is alive and well, but he later admits that he killed him. Hecuba and the Trojan women kill his children and blind him, after which he pursues them helplessly and appeals to Agamemnon. Agamemnon judges him guilty and orders him cast out on a desert island.

notes

Polymestor is described as guilty of both injustice and impiety: he violated human laws and profaned the deity of Jupiter Hospitalis. The notes say that Agamemnon indicates he is to receive punishment on both accounts.

Polymestor in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · about 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Polymestor first appears

Abstract written by a model

Polymestor is a Thracian king, Priam’s friend, Hecuba’s guest-friend, and fosterer of Polydore after Priam sends the boy from Troy with gold. Once Troy falls, he murders Polydore with a sword to keep the entrusted gold, casts the body into the sea without a tomb, and violates hospitality. He later admits the killing to Hecuba. In revenge, Hecuba and Trojan women kill his sons and blind him. He appeals to Thracians and Grecian troops, but Agamemnon rejects his justification and orders him cast onto a deserted island. His friendship with Hecuba becomes enmity through his betrayal, murder, and punishment.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

HECUBA.

Polymestor, ruler in the Thracian Chersonesus and Priam’s friend, fostered Polydore after Priam sent him away from Troy. After Troy fell, Polymestor killed Polydore for the gold entrusted to him.

CHORUS.

The Thracian king receives Polydore into his household after Priam entrusts him with the boy. He murders Polydore with a sword, apparently to possess the gold, violating the laws of hospitality.

AGAMEMNON, HECUBA, CHORUS.

Polymestor, the Thracian king and former friend of Hecuba, murdered her son after learning that Troy had fallen, apparently to seize the gold entrusted to him. He cast the youth’s body into the sea and failed to provide him a tomb. Hecuba plans to punish him.

CHORUS.

Polymestor comes to Hecuba after returning from Thracia and admits that he murdered Polydore and kept the gold entrusted to him. Hecuba and the Trojan women kill his sons and blind him, after which he calls upon the Thracians and Grecian troops for help. Agamemnon rejects his justification and orders him cast onto a deserted island.

Polymnestor in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Polymnestor first appears

Abstract written by a model

Polymnestor is a king of Thrace, ruling Bistonia and described as an Odrysian king, greedy and treacherous. He keeps Polydorus in his rich palace to be raised, but after Phrygian fortunes fail, murders his fosterchild for entrusted riches and casts the body into water. Lured by Hecuba’s promise of treasure, he is attacked by Hecuba and captive Trojan matrons, who tear out his eyes.

FABLES III. AND IV. [XIII.439-622]

Polymnestor, the Odrysian king, murders Polydorus and is lured by Hecuba with the promise of a hidden treasure. Hecuba and the captive Trojan matrons attack him, tear out his eyes, and leave the sockets empty.
