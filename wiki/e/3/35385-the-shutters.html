the shutters in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

The shutters are a barred window covering, secured by metal bars and impassable when bolted; Holmes examines Helen’s room and finds their iron hinges fixed in masonry, with no opening to raise the bar.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

“And what do you think of it all, Watson?” asked Sherlock Holmes,

Holmes examines the shutters of Helen’s room and finds that they cannot be forced open when barred. Their iron hinges are firmly built into the masonry, and there is no opening through which a knife could raise the bar.
