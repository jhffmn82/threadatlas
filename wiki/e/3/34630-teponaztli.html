teponaztli

Abstract written by a model

The teponaztli is a drum, generally made from a single block of very hard wood, hollowed into a somewhat oblong-square body with a thin sound-board and two vibrating wooden tongues. Beaten with two drumsticks covered at the ends with ule, its tongues produce two distinct sounds; shaving the wood regulates pitch. Their interval may be a third, fourth, fifth, sixth, or octave and conforms to a diatonic series excepting the seventh. It is often carved with fanciful designs and ranges from small strap-carried forms to Mexican specimens over five feet long, audible for three miles. Some are preserved by Indians in Mexico.

The teponaztli was generally made from a single block of very hard wood, hollowed to leave a thin sound-board with two vibrating tongues. The tongues produced two distinct sounds whose interval could be a third, fourth, fifth, sixth, or octave, and their pitch could be regulated by shaving the wood. The instrument was carved with fanciful designs, played with sticks covered in elastic ule, and ranged from small neck-carried drums to specimens over five feet long that could be heard three miles away.
