Trost in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

Trost, the narrator’s friend, argues that machines will replace human labour yet remain dependent on human skill and humanity’s control, freeing minds from grosser work for higher speculation; he rejects their becoming a self-reproducing race that supersedes humanity.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

XVII.

Trost believes that machines will remain dependent on the human race and that their increasing refinement will release human minds from grosser labour for higher speculation. He rejects the narrator’s vision of machines becoming an unconscious, self-reproducing race that supersedes humanity.
