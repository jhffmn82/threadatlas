the narrator's childhood in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

The narrator's childhood is a warm, formative period rooted in family affection, his father's sermons, stories, popularity, and England. After his mother's death, he lives among parishioners at different homesteads, preferring their variety to the Hall. Riding his pony beside his father through surrounding parishes, he absorbs people and landscapes, imagines crises with swords and firearms, and develops lasting loves of rural scenery and inherited traditions.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

II.

The narrator's childhood was shaped by his father's stories, his father's popularity among parishioners, and periods of care at different homesteads after his mother's death. He spent years accompanying his father on horseback through the surrounding parishes, where he formed lasting impressions of people and landscapes.
