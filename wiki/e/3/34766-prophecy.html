Abstract written by a model

In The Seven Plays in English Verse, prophecy foretells Oedipus’s parricide, incest, exile, curses, loss of sight, and beggary, and is later recognized as fulfilled. In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, prophecy concerns Oedipus’s hidden identity, crimes, relationships, and fate, foretelling his parricide, incest, and the consequences of possessing him.

the prophecy of Oedipus's parentage and crimes in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · 449 to 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where the prophecy of Oedipus's parentage and crimes first appears

Abstract written by a model

The prophecy foretells that Oedipus will kill his father, commit incest with his mother, suffer exile and curses, lose sight after prosperity, and become beggared. Told of this fate, he flees Corinth in fear. Later, after learning that he killed Laius and married Jocasta, he recognizes that the prophecies have been fulfilled.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

KING OEDIPUS

The opening account states that Oedipus was told he would commit parricide and incest. His fear of this destiny led him away from Corinth, but the account says that both prophecies were fulfilled.

[OEDIPUS _has entered during the Choral song_

The prophecy declares that Oedipus will be driven from the land by his father's and mother's curses. It foretells that he will become blind while having sight, beggared after prosperity, and revealed as the husband-son of his mother and the murderer and rival of his father.

_Enter_ JOCASTA.

This prophecy declared that Oedipus would know his mother's shame and shed his true father's blood. Oedipus fled Corinth to avoid fulfilling it, but after learning that he killed Laius and married Jocasta, he recognizes that it has been fulfilled.

prophecy in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912 · the work’s page · read it where prophecy first appears

Abstract written by a model

Prophecy concerns Oedipus’s hidden identity, crimes, relationships, and fate. Through Teiresias and later revelations, it foretells that Oedipus will kill his father, Laius, and marry his mother, Jocasta, defiling her bed and fathering abhorrent children. Oedipus initially rejects Teiresias’s charge as slander, but fears his crossroads encounter may have fulfilled it; learning Laius and Jocasta are his parents confirms the prophecy. Later oracles say Oedipus’s country depends on possessing him, alive or dead, prompting Creon to seek control of his location.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

OEDIPUS THE KING

Prophecy is represented through Teiresias, whom the elders regard as able to uncover hidden truth. Teiresias’s prophecy identifies Oedipus as Laius’s murderer, though Oedipus rejects it as slander.

CREON.

The prophecy of Oedipus’s fate said that he would defile his mother’s bed, father abhorrent children, and kill the father who raised him. Oedipus fears that his encounter at the crossroads may have fulfilled part of it.

[Enter JOCASTA.]

The prophecy states that Oedipus will shed the blood of his father and marry his mother. The revelation that Laius was his father and Jocasta his mother shows that the prophecy has come to pass.

[Enter ISMENE]

The latest oracles say that Oedipus's country will eventually yearn to possess him for its welfare, alive or dead, and that its sovereignty depends on him. They also lead Creon to seek control over Oedipus's location, and Oedipus regards them as confirming the older prophecies concerning him.
