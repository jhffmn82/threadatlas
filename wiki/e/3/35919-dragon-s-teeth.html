dragon’s teeth in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · -0299/-0200

Dragon’s teeth from an Aonian dragon slain by Cadmus, once sown at Thebes to produce an earthborn people, are given by Aeetes to Telamon and Aethalides as seed for Jason’s contest.

Aeetes gives the dragon’s teeth to Telamon and Aethalides for Jason’s contest. They came from the dragon slain by Cadmus, who sowed such teeth at Thebes and produced an earthborn people.

the dragon's teeth in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The dragon’s teeth are sown by Cadmus in furrows under turned-up earth at Minerva’s direction. They produce earth-born armed men, emerging with spears, helmets, shields, and other weapons. In one interpretation they are the dragon’s subjects; another treats them as symbols of soldiers or brass-pointed javelins.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

At Minerva's direction, Cadmus scatters the dragon's teeth in furrows under the earth. They produce armed men, who emerge first with spears and helmets and then with shields and other weapons.

notes .. notes

The dragon’s teeth were sown by Cadmus and were said to produce armed men. The explanation proposes that they symbolized soldiers or short brass-pointed javelins because related words in the Phoenician language could have both meanings.

dragon’s teeth in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Dragon’s teeth, sown by Cadmus on Athena’s advice after he killed the dragon, produced armed men called the Sparti, who fought one another.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE LIBRARY, 111. 10

Athena advised Cadmus to sow the dragon’s teeth after he killed the dragon. Armed men called the Sparti grew from them and fought one another.
