Philoctetes' wound in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Philoctetes' wound is an ancient, festering foot injury, caused by the poisonous bite of a basilisk identified as Chrysa's guardian serpent after Philoctetes approached Chrysa. Chronic and worsening, it brings severe pain, bleeding, dark flow, weakness, and difficulty traveling or living among others. The army rejects and casts him away, leaving him isolated, hungry, and without a healer. During an eruption he collapses and must sleep; a cave leaf can lull the malady, and physicians connected with Asclepius are promised to heal it at Troy.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

PHILOCTETES

Philoctetes' wound is an ancient, festering injury in his foot that makes his movement difficult and causes severe pain. It led the army to reject and cast him away, and he has no healer to relieve it.

_Enter_ PHILOCTETES.

Philoctetes' plague is a fierce and worsening malady caused by the bite of a murderous basilisk. It causes severe suffering, and a leaf kept in the cave can quiet its rage and lull it to rest.

CHORUS.

Philoctetes' wound suddenly erupts in severe pain and bleeding, producing a gory flow and dark blood from his foot. It weakens him so completely that he collapses and requires sleep before he can recover.

MONODY.

Philoctetes' wound causes his isolation, hunger, and inability to live among other mortals. Heracles explains that it came from approaching Chrysa's sentinel and promises that the physicians connected with Asclepius will heal it at Troy.

Philoctetes' wound in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

An incurable, venomous water-snake bite left Philoctetes ulcerous, foul, and agonized for years until Podaleirius healed it with salves, restoring his health and strength.

At last by Calchas' counsel Achaea's sons

Philoctetes' wound was caused by the venomous bite of a water-snake and remained ulcerous, foul, and agonizing for years. Podaleirius healed it with salves, after which Philoctetes regained health, strength, and a healthy appearance.
