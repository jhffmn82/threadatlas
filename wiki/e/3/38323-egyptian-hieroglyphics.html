Egyptian hieroglyphics

writing system · 3 works · 4 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Egyptian hieroglyphs, Egyptian letters, ancient Egyptian characters, hieroglyph, hieroglyphics, hieroglyphs, those which the Egyptians call hieroglyphics
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Egyptian hieroglyphics are discussed as an image-based writing system alongside the Ethiopian letters, using symbolic representations such as the kite for speed, the crocodile for malice, the eye for justice and bodily protection, and different hands for plenty or preservation.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Egyptian hieroglyphics are found on monuments and manuscripts in tombs and were deciphered by Champollion the younger, enabling modern study of worship, mythology, rituals, and priestly literature.
  • In Vestiges of the Mayas, or, Facts tending to prove that Communications and Intimate Relations must have existed, in very remote times, between the inhabitants of Mayab and those of Asia and Africa, Egyptian hieroglyphs are described as representing Egypt with a land character and another character read as K; Thebes is written as AP or APE with the feminine prefix T.
