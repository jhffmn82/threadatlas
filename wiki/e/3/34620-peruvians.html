Peruvians in Musical Instruments

Carl Engel · 1875-08

The Peruvians are described through their music and musical roles. They made bone flutes, syrinxes called huayra-puhura, other pipes, and bells possibly called chanrares; they also possessed the five- or seven-stringed tinya. Their songs used different interval orders, with indications of a pentatonic scale. Music accompanied organized military trumpeters and drummers, victory celebrations, festivals, funerals, agricultural songs, and elegiac haravis, composed by haravecs.

CHAPTER VI.

The Peruvians made bone flutes, syrinxes called huayra-puhura, and other pipes. Their songs apparently used different orders of intervals for different kinds of tunes, and some instruments were arranged according to the pentatonic scale.

[Illustration] The Tezcucans (or Acolhuans) belonged to the same race as the Azt

The Peruvians were known to have used bells, since a small copper specimen was found in an old Peruvian tomb. They called their bells chanrares, although it remains uncertain whether that term referred instead to horse bells.

The Peruvians possessed the five- or seven-stringed tinya and used trumpeters and drummers in their carefully organized military divisions. They held musical celebrations after victories, performed music at festivals and funerals, composed agricultural songs and elegiac haravis, and employed haravecs as minstrel-composers.
