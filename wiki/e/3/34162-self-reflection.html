self-reflection in SELF-RAG: Learning to Retrieve, Generate, and Critique through Self-Reflection

Akari Asai, Zeqiu Wu, Yizhong Wang, Avirup Sil, Hannaneh Hajishirzi · 2023-10-17

Self-reflection is a mechanism for deciding whether retrieval is needed, filtering irrelevant retrieved passages, and judging whether model output is relevant, supported, useful, and factual. It helps users verify factual errors and supports robust, controllable generation without external models at inference.

self-reflection in HYBGRAG: Hybrid Retrieval-Augmented Generation on Textual and Relational Knowledge Bases

Meng-Chieh Lee, Qi Zhu, Costas Mavromatis, Zhen Han, Soji Adeshina, Vassilis N. Ioannidis, Huzefa Rangwala, Christos Faloutsos · 2025

Self-reflection is a model or agent capability for retrieval, generation, critique, and problem solving. It matters because it corrects incorrect first attempts by iteratively optimizing outputs or question routing from critic feedback, improving retrieval accuracy, interpretability, and HYBGRAG retrieval performance. In HYBGRAG, gains are strongest from one to two iterations, while later iterations yield diminishing improvements. Critics may use pre-trained LLMs, external tools, or fine-tuned LLMs, but poorly designed pre-trained-LLM critics can evaluate suboptimally and give overly implicit feedback. Existing approaches do not generalize to HQA without suitable retrieval tools and retrieval-refinement guidance.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

HYBGRAG uses self-reflection to iteratively improve question routing based on feedback from a critic module. This refinement is presented as contributing to retrieval accuracy and interpretability.

4 .. 4.1.2

Self-reflection improves HYBGRAG's retrieval performance, especially when increasing the number of iterations from one to two. Further iterations provide diminishing improvements, and a few iterations are sufficient.

5

Self-reflection addresses incorrect first attempts by iteratively optimizing an output based on feedback. Its critics can be implemented with pre-trained LLMs, external tools, or fine-tuned LLMs, but existing approaches do not generalize to HQA because they lack appropriate retrieval tools and effective retrieval-refinement guidance.
