Chorus of Argive Women in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · 449 to 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Chorus of Argive Women first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Chorus of Argive Women is a chorus that comments on the conflict and Electra’s grief. It comforts Electra with sympathy and counsel, urging her to restrain excessive mourning, trust divine justice, and accept its support. It observes hostility with Clytemnestra and laments the royal line’s reported destruction. After Orestes’ apparent death, revelation, and killings, it celebrates the avenger’s progress and declares the curse has reached those responsible for earlier bloodshed.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

ELECTRA

The Chorus of Argive Women comforts Electra but repeatedly urges her to restrain her excessive mourning and trust in divine justice. It acknowledges that Electra's grief is understandable and promises to remain on her side.

_Enter_ CLYTEMNESTRA.

The Chorus of Argive women observes the hostility between Clytemnestra and Electra and laments the reported destruction of the old royal line. It tries to comfort Electra after the report of Orestes' death, but Electra rejects its attempts to raise hope.

The Chorus of Argive women reacts to Orestes' apparent death, his revelation, and the killings. It celebrates the progress of the avenger and declares that the curse has overtaken those responsible for earlier bloodshed.

Chorus in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Chorus first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Chorus is a group of Mycenian virgins and Argive women, intimate associates of Electra and commentators on the calamities inherited by the house of Pelops. It comes quietly to inquire about Orestes, laments his suffering and madness, condemns murder, Helen, and the house’s wickedness, mourns Electra and the descendants of Pelops, then guards the palace roads and sustains the deception around Helen’s attack.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

ORESTES.

The Argive women form the chorus and come to inquire about Orestes' calamity. They approach quietly so as not to disturb his sleep, lament his suffering, and describe the destructive power of the Eumenides.

PYLADES, ORESTES, CHORUS.

The Chorus describes the inherited calamities of the house of Atreus and condemns both the murder of a mother and the wickedness surrounding it. It laments Orestes' madness, Electra's suffering, and the destruction of the descendants of Pelops.

ELECTRA, ORESTES, PYLADES, CHORUS.

The Chorus condemns Helen as an object of hatred among women and blames her for filling Greece with tears. It guards the roads approaching the palace, watches for allies and enemies, and helps maintain the deception surrounding Helen's attack. It hears Helen's cries and awaits news from a Phrygian servant.
