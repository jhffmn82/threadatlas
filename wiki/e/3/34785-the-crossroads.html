the crossroads

place · 3 works · 3 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Branch roads from Delphi and from Daulis meet, Cross-roads, Cross-roads here, a cross-way, a spot where three roads meet, the cross-road, the crossroad where Laius was killed, the meeting of three roads, the parted ways, the place where the 3 roads meet, the scene of this unhappy blow, the triple-branching roads
  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, it is the Phocian crossroad where roads from Dauha and Delphi meet and where Jocasta's description prompts Oedipus to recall killing an older man and his attendants.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, it is where three roads meet, where Oedipus is said to have murdered his father, and where the tombs of Laius and his attendant stand.
  • In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, it is where roads from Delphi and Daulis meet and where Laius was reportedly murdered; Oedipus recalls killing an older man and his attendants there.

the crossroad where Laius was killed in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

The crossroad where Laius was killed is in Phocis, where roads from Dauha and Delphi meet. Jocasta's description of the place prompts Oedipus to recall that he killed an older man and his attendants at the same location.

Cross-roads in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Cross-roads, where three roads meet, is said to be where Oedipus murdered his father; tombs of Laius and his attendant stand at its center.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER V.

The Cross-roads are said to be the place where Oedipus murdered his father. The tombs of Laius and his attendant stand in the middle of the place where the three roads meet.
