Abstract written by a model

Merman's bitterness and decline is a chronic, irrevocable deterioration caused by prolonged disappointment and resistance. His certainty and resentment grow amid criticism and ridicule; he becomes inflexible, loses friends and opportunities, and suffers mental weakening. Though he later speaks less and admits errors, the damage endures.

Meanwhile, how did he like it?

Merman's certainty in his own correctness grows under resistance, and the scorn of competent critics and ridicule from incompetent ones become a chronic source of resentment. He turns from a flexible, sociable man into a bore, loses friends and opportunities, and develops an aged, weakened mind; although he later speaks less about the controversy and recognizes some former errors, its bitter effects remain irrevocable.
