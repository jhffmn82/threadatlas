CHAPTER IV.

The harp was called kinyra by the Greeks and may have influenced the translation of the Hebrew kinnor as harp. A Greek vase in the Munich museum depicts Polyhymnia playing a thirteen-stringed harp resembling the Assyrian harp, with both hands used for treble and bass. This representation is regarded as an exceptional instance, and the instrument may have been imported from Asia rather than Egypt.
