Philoctetes' cave in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Philoctetes' cave is an isolated rocky shelter on a cliff by the shore on Lemnos, with two openings that give sunshine in winter and breezes in summer. It has been Philoctetes' bare, companionless dwelling since the Greeks left him: inside are a bed of leaves, a wooden cup, fire embers, and festering rags for his sore. It holds his pain, hunger, and exile; as Neoptolemus leads him away, Philoctetes gathers his few belongings, threatens to return if abandoned, and finally bids it farewell.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

PHILOCTETES

Philoctetes' cave has two openings, offering sunshine in winter and breezes in summer. Neoptolemus finds a bed of leaves, a wooden cup, fire embers, and festering rags used as dressings for a malignant sore.

_Enter_ PHILOCTETES.

Philoctetes' cave on the cliff by the shore has been his bare dwelling-place since the Greeks left him. He goes inside with Neoptolemus to collect the few things he cannot leave behind.

CHORUS.

Philoctetes' cave is the place where he has lived companionless and from which Neoptolemus leads him during the attempted departure. Philoctetes threatens to return to it, robbed of his arms and food, if he is left behind.

MONODY.

Philoctetes' cave is his lonely shelter and the place where he has endured pain, hunger, and exile. He bids farewell to it as he prepares to leave Lemnos.
