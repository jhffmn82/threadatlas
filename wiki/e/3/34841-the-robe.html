  • In Heroides and Amores, Nessus's poisoned robe is sent by Deianira to Hercules and causes his death through its poison.
  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, the robe is Deianira's poisoned gift to Heracles, whose wearing of it causes his death.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the tunic of Nessus is a garment dyed with poison that Deïanira sends to Hercules and that kills him when he wears it.

the robe in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

The robe is a gift Deianira sends to Heracles, coated and anointed with the Centaur's blood as a supposed love-charm to regain his affection. She instructs that it remain unexposed until he displays it during sacrifice. At Cenaeum, Heracles wears it; its ointment, carrying Lernaean Hydra's poison through the Centaur's device, adheres to him, consumes and tears his flesh, and causes violent convulsions, poisoning, and suffering that leads to his death.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

THE TRACHINIAN MAIDENS

The introductory account states that Dêanira later commissions Lichas to present Heracles with a robe anointed with the blood-charm of Nessus, hoping to regain his affection. The robe is not yet presented or used in the dramatic action of this unit.

CHORUS.

Deianira sends the finewoven robe to Heracles with instructions that it not be worn before him or exposed to sun, temple, or household flame until he displays it during sacrifice. Heracles wears it at Cenaeum, where it becomes glued to his body and causes tearing pains and violent convulsions.

CHORUS.

Nessus's robe is the garment Deianira sends to Heracles as a supposed charm to restore his love. When Heracles wears it, it clings to his body, consumes his flesh, and causes the agonizing suffering that will lead to his death.
