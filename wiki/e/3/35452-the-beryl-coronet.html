Abstract written by a model

The Beryl Coronet is a magnificent, nationally important precious gold diadem containing thirty-nine enormous beryls, worth at least £100,000. It is pledged to Alexander Holder as security for a £50,000 advance and kept in a case in his dressing-room bureau. It is then found ruined: a gold corner holding three beryls has been torn away, leaving thirty-six gems and a cracked edge. The coronet’s status as national property makes the loss public. It was handed through a hall window to Sir George, then recovered by Arthur in a struggle, twisted and incomplete. Holmes later retrieves the three missing gems.

XI. THE ADVENTURE OF THE BERYL CORONET

The Beryl Coronet is described as a magnificent and nationally important piece of jewellery containing thirty-nine enormous beryls and valuable gold chasing. It is left with Alexander Holder as security for a loan, carried to his house, and later found with one gold corner and three beryls missing.

The coronet is found ruined, with three stones missing. The narrator states that it is national property, making the matter public rather than private.

“Do you receive much company?” he asked.

The coronet is kept in a case inside a bureau in Mr. Holder’s dressing-room. It has thirty-six remaining gems and a cracked edge where a corner holding three gems was torn away; Holmes determines that removing such a corner would normally make a loud noise.

“For Heaven’s sake, tell me, then, what is this extraordinary mystery!”

The coronet is taken from Mr. Holder's dressing-room and handed through the hall window to Sir George. Arthur recovers it during the struggle, but it is twisted and missing three gems until Holmes later retrieves them.
