In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Phædra's accusation against Hippolytus is a written charge that Hippolytus forced himself upon her and defiled Theseus's bed. In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, Theseus's accusation of Hippolytus claims Hippolytus's guilt for violating Phaedra. In The Library, the false accusation against Hippolytus is made by Phaedra after she fears that Hippolytus will report her advances to Theseus.

Phædra's accusation against Hippolytus in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Phædra's accusation against Hippolytus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Phædra's accusation is a written charge in her letter to Theseus that Hippolytus forced himself upon her and defiled Theseus's bed. Though Hippolytus denies it and the charge is later presented as deceptive, Theseus believes it without investigation, curses and banishes his son, and thereby causes his death.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS, PHÆDRA.

Phædra's accusation appears in the letter she leaves for Theseus, claiming that Hippolytus approached her bed by force. Theseus believes the accusation and reacts by cursing his son.

HIPPOLYTUS, THESEUS, CHORUS.

The accusation is that Hippolytus defiled Theseus's wife. Hippolytus rejects it as false, but Theseus accepts the deceased woman's letter as proof and banishes him.

DIANA, THESEUS, CHORUS.

Phaedra's accusation is presented as false: she wrote a deceptive letter claiming wrongdoing by Hippolytus and persuaded Theseus to believe it. Theseus acted on the accusation without investigation, causing Hippolytus's death.
