Also called: Herald, LICH., LICHAS, Lychas, attendant, faithful minister, poor Lichas, the herald, the herald Lichas, your herald Lichas

Abstract written by a model

In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Lichas serves as Heracles' faithful minister and messenger, escorting captive women, reporting that Heracles is alive, and concealing then admitting Heracles' love for Iole. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Lichas unknowingly carries Nessus' tunic to Hercules and is hurled into the Eubœan waves, where he becomes a stone.

Lichas in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · 449 to 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Lichas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Lichas is Heracles' faithful minister and messenger, escorting captive women from Oechalia to Heracles' household. He tells Dêanira that Heracles is alive and sacrificing after conquering Oechalia, but conceals Iole's identity and Heracles' love to spare Dêanira pain. Pressed by her, he admits that love for the maiden caused the conquest and that his own fear led him to hide the truth.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE TRACHINIAN MAIDENS

Lichas arrives with captive women and tells Dêanira that Heracles is alive, healthy, and sacrificing in Euboea after conquering Oechalia. He presents the women as captives taken for the service of the gods and Heracles' household, but conceals or denies Iole's identity and the role of Heracles' love in the conquest. When confronted, he continues to give evasive answers.

DÊ. Nay, I entreat thee by His name, whose fire

Lichas confesses that the maiden's love for Heracles caused the conquest of Oechalia. He says that Heracles did not order him to conceal or deny this, but that he hid it because he feared wounding DÊ's heart.
