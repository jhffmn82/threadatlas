the illusion of an audience in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

An illusion of a liberal, far-off, hazy assembly, numerous and idiom-near, is kept as manuscripts face judgment outside imagination, but is fatalized when a distinct auditor or foreground physiognomy emerges.

The illusion is of a liberal, far-off, hazy assembly more numerous than the visionary One and nearer to the speaker's idiom than the Cherokees. The speaker keeps this illusion while leaving the manuscripts to judgment outside the imagination.
