Thracian Chersonese

place · 3 works · 2 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Chersonese, Chersonesus, Chersonesus land, Chersonesus of Thrace, THE THRACIAN CHERSONESUS, Thracian Chersonesus, the Chersonesus, the Chersonesus of Thrace, this land of Thrace, this territory of the Chersonese
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Chersonesus of Thrace is invaded and ravaged by large bodies of Thracians, until Dercyllidas drives them out and builds a wall from one sea to the other.
  • In Hecuba and other plays, the Thracian Chersonesus is the fruitful Thracian coast where the Greek army is stationed after leaving Troy, where Polymestor rules, and where Polydore’s unburied corpse lies on the shore.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the Thracian Chersonese is the fertile Thracian territory ruled by Polymestor where the Greeks have anchored after the capture of Troy and where Hecuba and the captive women are held near the Greek tents.

the Chersonesus of Thrace in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Chersonesus of Thrace was invaded and ravaged by large bodies of Thracians, who seized its fortified cities. Dercyllidas drove them out and built a wall from one sea to the other, protecting the region from further incursions.
