Hyllus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Hyllus, son of Hercules, helps command Heraclid forces, kills Eurystheus during his flight, then dies in single combat against Echemus.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

After the translation of Hercales to the gods, hb childraa dwelt

Hyllus, the son of Hercules, helped command the Heraclid forces with Iolaus and Theseus and killed Eurystheus after Eurystheus's chariot broke during his flight. He later fought Echemus of Tegea in single combat and was killed.

Hyllus in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Hyllus is the son of Heracles and Deianira. Urged by Deianira, he seeks news of Heracles, learns of his suffering, and witnesses the poisoning caused by the robe. Believing Deianira guilty, he condemns her and seeks death and vengeance against her. He then supports Heracles, learns that Deianira acted innocently under Nessus's influence, and obeys his father by carrying him to Mount Oeta, preparing the funeral pyre, and accepting Heracles' command to marry Iole.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

THE TRACHINIAN MAIDENS

Hyllus tells Dêanira that Heracles is said to have been enslaved to a Lydian girl and is now besieging Oechalia in Euboea. After hearing the prophecy concerning Heracles' fate, he agrees to go and investigate his father's situation.

CHORUS.

Hyllus witnesses Heracles' poisoning and reports that the robe caused his father's convulsions, agony, and furious actions. He tells Deianira that she has killed Heracles and invokes Right and Vengeance against her, rejecting the claim that she has acted as a mother. He then leaves her.

CHORUS.

Hyllus brings and supports his suffering father, Heracles, and explains that Deianira acted innocently under Nessus's influence. He swears to carry Heracles to Mount Oeta and prepare the pyre, though he initially resists marrying Iole because he blames her for the disaster. He ultimately agrees to obey Heracles.

Hyllus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Hyllus, elder son of Hercules by Deianira, marries Iole as Hercules commands. He kills the fleeing Eurystheus near the Scironian cliffs and gives Eurystheus's severed head to Alcmena. Seeking the Heraclids' return, he consults Delphi, returns after three years with an army, is defeated, and killed.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE LIBRARY, IIL. vir. 7

Hyllus pursued Eurystheus as he fled in a chariot near the Scironian cliffs and killed him.

THE LIBRARY, II. vir. 2

Hyllus marries Iole according to Hercules's commands and asks at Delphi how the Heraclids can return. He interprets the third crop as three years, returns with an army, and is defeated and killed.
