the attack on the herds and villages

event · 3 works · 4 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Bacchae's attack on the cattle, Destroying, a live steer riven asunder, a wreck they made, rending asunder a fatted lowing calf, rent open cows, strange deeds, tearing of the Bacchæ, the slaughter of the cattle, wounded them

Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, it is the Bacchae’s attack on cattle with their bare hands, tearing apart calves and cows and scattering their remains. In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, it is carried out by wild women during their mountain rites, as they tear cattle apart with their bare hands. In The Bacchae of Euripides, it involves the Bacchae tearing apart cattle, scattering remains, driving off herdsmen, and attacking Hysiae and Erythrae.

the attack on the herds and villages in The Bacchae of Euripides

Euripides · 406 to 405 BC · the work’s page · read it where the attack on the herds and villages first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Bacchae, with supernatural violence, tear apart cattle, scatter remains, drive off herdsmen, then attack Hysiae and Erythrae, destroying homes and weapons, carrying children, resisting villagers’ weapons, and striking attackers down with wands.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

_Enter_ PENTHEUS _in fury_.

The Bacchae tear cattle apart with their hands, scatter the remains through the forest, and drive away the herdsmen. They then attack Hysiae and Erythrae, destroy homes and weapons, carry children, and handle fire without being harmed; villagers' weapons cannot injure them, while the Bacchae's wands strike down the attackers.
