John Openshaw in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

John Openshaw, a young man living at the family estate near Horsham, is the son of Joseph Openshaw and nephew of Elias Openshaw. After Elias and Joseph die following warnings marked K. K. K. and five orange pips, John inherits the estate and receives the same threat, demanding papers be placed on the sundial. 2 sections; each heading opens the text

V. THE FIVE ORANGE PIPS

John Openshaw tells Sherlock Holmes that his uncle Elias and father Joseph died after receiving warnings marked K. K. K. and accompanied by five orange pips. He has now received the same kind of warning from London instructing him to put the papers on the sundial, and he seeks Holmes’s help.

John Openshaw is instructed to return home, place the remaining paper and a note in the brass box, and put it on the sundial. He leaves Holmes's rooms armed, but is found dead in the water near Waterloo Bridge that night.
