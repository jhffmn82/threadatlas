  • In The Trojan Women of Euripides, Achilles’ tomb is the place where Polyxena is said to rest apart after the lots are cast.
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Achilles’ tomb is the sepulchre where Achilles’ ghost demands a Trojan captive and where Polyxena is taken and killed as a gift to Achilles.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the tomb of Achilles is a site of honor for the dead where Achilles appears, demands Polyxena as a Trojan victim and tribute, and causes the Greeks to halt their departure for her sacrifice.
  • In The Library, Achilles’ grave is the site of Polyxena’s slaughter.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the tomb of Achilles receives Polyxena’s sacrifice as an offering to Achilles’ shade.
  • In The Fall of Troy, Achilles’ tomb is the place where Polyxeina is brought and sacrificed, and where Achilles’ son prays for his father’s favor and the Argives’ safe return.

Achilles’ tomb in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Achilles’ tomb is a sepulchre and the site claimed for Polyxena’s sacrifice. Achilles’ ghost appears there, demanding a Trojan captive. Polyxena is assigned there, first thought to be sepulchral service, then taken and killed as a gift to Achilles.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

HECUBA.

Achilles’ tomb is the place where Polyxena is ordered to be sacrificed. Achilles’ ghost appears upon it and demands a Trojan captive as an offering.

THE TROJAN CAPTIVES.

Achilles’ tomb is the place where Polyxena has been assigned to minister. Hecuba initially misunderstands the assignment as service for sepulchral rites before learning that Polyxena’s fate is death.

CHOR. Attendants of the aged Hecuba,

The tomb of Achilles is the place where Polyxena is taken and killed. Her death is presented as a gift to the deceased Achilles.

tomb of Achilles in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Achilles's tomb is a site associated with honor for the dead, where Achilles appears, demands Polyxena as a Trojan victim and tribute, and causes the Greeks to halt their departure for her sacrifice.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

HECUBA.

Achilles appears above his tomb and demands that Polyxena be given as a victim and tribute of honor. The Greeks stop their departure in response to this demand.

HECUBA. CHORUS.

Achilles's tomb is the place designated for Polyxena's sacrifice. Achilles's apparition appears above it and demands the honor of a Trojan victim.
