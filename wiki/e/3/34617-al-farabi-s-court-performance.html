THE PERSIANS AND ARABS.

During Al-Farabi's court performance, he played the lute in successive musical modes that made the audience laugh, sigh and weep, become enraged, and finally fall into profound sleep. He then departed while the listeners slept; the story is compared with the account of Timotheus performing before Alexander the Great in Dryden's "Alexander's Feast."
