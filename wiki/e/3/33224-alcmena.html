  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Alcmena is a mortal woman who bears Hercules by Jupiter, exposes him out of fear of Juno, nurses him when Jupiter returns him, and is later worshiped by the Thebans as a goddess.
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Alcmena is the aged mother of Hercules and grandmother of his children who defends them, learns of their survival and victory, and orders Eurystheus’ death in revenge for his persecution.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Alcmena is the aged mother of Hercules and protector of his descendants who preserves the children, rejoices at their survival, confronts Eurystheus, and orders his death.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Alcmena is Electryon’s daughter and Amphitryon’s wife who bears Heracles by Zeus and Iphiclus by Amphitryon.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Alcmena is the mother of Hercules, wife of Amphitryon, and confidante of Iole, who shares grief over Dryope before Iolaüs’ rejuvenation interrupts their sorrow.
  • In The Library, Alcmena is given to Amphitryon on the condition that he avenge her brothers, bears Heracles by Zeus and Iphicles by Amphitryon, later marries Rhadamanthys, and gouges out Eurystheus’ eyes.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Alcmena is associated with the bridal-bed in Amphitryon’s ruined house and gives birth to a boy after Historis deceives Hera’s Sorceresses.

Alcmena in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Alcmena is a royal, aged Greek mother of Hercules and grandmother of his children. She defends the children when an Argive herald threatens to seize them, fears for their safety, and depends on foreign suppliants as Hyllus approaches with forces. After learning that her loved ones survived, the battle was won, and Iolaus regained youth, she confronts Eurystheus and seeks revenge for his persecution, ordering his death and the casting out of his corpse.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

DEMOPHOON, IOLAUS, CHORUS.

Alcmena is described as the aged mother of Hercules and the grandmother of the children. She fears that an Argive herald has come to seize them, but learns that Hyllus is approaching with forces. She relies on the foreign suppliants and worries about being saved if Iolaus and the children are overcome.

SERVANT, ALCMENA, CHORUS.

Alcmene learns from the Servant that her loved ones are alive, that the battle has been won, and that Iolaus has regained his youth. She confronts Eurystheus, accuses him of persecuting Hercules and his children, and insists that he die despite the decree of Athens. She orders her servants to kill him and cast out his corpse.

Alcmena in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Alcmena is an aged woman, daughter of Electryon, mother of Hercules, and grandmother and protector of Hercules’ children. She preserves the female descendants in a temple and at its altars, anxiously fearing threats while Iolaus leads the male children. Though she loves Athens, she is hostile to Eurystheus for pursuing her family. After Hyllus arrives and victory is reported, she rejoices that Iolaus and Hercules’ descendants survive. She then confronts Eurystheus over his treatment of Hercules, herself, and the children, and, determined on vengeance despite Athenian law, orders his death and that his body be given to dogs.

Alcmena in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Alcmena is an Argive, daughter of Electryon, wife of Amphitryon, and mother of Hercules; some accounts also make her mother of Iphiclus. After Jupiter deceives her by taking Amphitryon’s form, she endures severe, obstructed labor carrying Hercules until Galanthis intervenes. In old age, anxious for Hercules, she is Iole’s confidante, sharing grief over Dryope before rejuvenated Iolaüs interrupts their sorrow.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

FABLE III. [IX.273-323]

Alcmena undergoes prolonged and severe labor while carrying Hercules. She invokes Ilithyïa and the two Nixi, but Ilithyïa, corrupted by Juno, obstructs her delivery until Galanthis intervenes.

EXPLANATION.

Alcmena, daughter of Electryon and cousin of Amphitryon, refused to marry Amphitryon until he avenged her father’s losses against the Teleboans. Jupiter was said to have assumed Amphitryon’s form and fathered Hercules with her, while some accounts state that she bore twins, Hercules and Iphiclus.

FABLE IV. [IX.324-425]

Alcmena listens while Iole recounts Dryope's fate and dries Iole's tears while weeping herself. She is present when the sight of rejuvenated Iolaüs interrupts their sorrow.

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Alcmena is given to Amphitryon with a kingdom, under Electryon’s requirement that she remain a virgin until his return. She makes marriage depend on Amphitryon avenging her brothers’ deaths. She becomes mother of Heracles/Hercules by Zeus and Iphicles by Amphitryon, later marries Rhadamanthys after Amphitryon’s death, and gouges Eurystheus’s eyes after Hyllus brings his severed head.
