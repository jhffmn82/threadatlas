Dante in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

Dante is a precise yet imaginative seer who distinguishes entranced vision from ordinary external fact. His strongest insight joins sustained creative energy to sane expectation; he also expresses a sentiment on courtesy and villainy and places Judas at Malebolge’s bottom.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

opening

Dante is presented as both precise in reproducing actual objects and expansive in imaginative combination. He distinguishes the truths of an entranced vision from the external facts recognized when his mind returns to ordinary experience.
