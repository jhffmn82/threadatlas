Cadmus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Cadmus is presented as Agenor’s son, allegedly born at Thebes in Egypt, who was sent from Phoenicia to seek Europa and forbidden to return without her. After failing, he came to Samothracia, was initiated into sacred mysteries, and reached Boeotia, where an oracle commanded him to build Thebes. He lived there and married Hermione, or Harmonia; with Hermione he fathered Semele, Ino, Autonoe, Agave, and Polydorus. After hearing of Semele’s unusual child, he reportedly wrapped it in gold, established sacrifices to it as Osiris, and said Jupiter was its father.

Cadmus in The Bacchae of Euripides

Euripides · -0406/-0405

Cadmus, founder and former king of Thebes, is father of Semele and Agave and grandfather of Dionysus and Pentheus. After yielding his throne to Pentheus, he preserves Semele’s sanctuary and honors Dionysus despite old age, warning Pentheus against defying divine power. He finds Pentheus’s scattered body, helps Agave learn the truth, mourns, is exiled, and is decreed to become serpent-like beside Harmonia.

Cadmus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Cadmus is a son of Agenor, brother of Europa, Sidonian stranger, exile, and founder and long-time ruler of Thebes. Sent from Phœnicia under the demand that he find Europa, he wanders in search of her and cannot recover her. After living in Thrace until his mother Telephassa dies, he goes to Delphi and consults the oracle of Phœbus. Obeying its direction, he follows an unyoked heifer to its resting place, prepares a sacrifice to Jupiter, and sends servants for water. When his companions are destroyed by a dragon, he kills it with his spear. On Minerva's advice he sows its teeth; earth-born armed warriors arise, destroy one another, and the five survivors help him build Thebes and its walls. He settles in Bœotia and, according to one account, serves Mars for a year to expiate killing the dragon. He later prospers through his wife Hermione, children, and grandchildren, though calamities begin with Actæon's death. He is father of Semele, Ino, and Agave, and is said to have caused Semele and her son to be thrown into the sea. After many woes, sorrow, prodigies, and a conspiracy that drives him from Thebes, he retires or is exiled with Hermione to Illyria. There he commands an army, is chosen king, assists the Enchelians, and receives the crown of Illyria. In old age he recalls the dragon, wishes to become a serpent, and transforms: scales cover his skin, his body becomes black with azure spots, his tongue divides and hisses, though his arms remain. Other accounts describe him as leading a colony into Greece, establishing Europe, building Nygnis, bringing cuirasses and brazen darts, or as an officer of Agenor who eloped with a singing girl.

Cadmus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Cadmus is a leader and founder associated with a Phoenician army, Cadmea, Thebes, dragon’s teeth, and a journey directed by an oracle. He received an oracle at Delphi, travelled toward Phocis following a marked cow, and settled where it lay down, erecting an altar and statue of Athene. He invaded the land, defeated the Hyantes, allowed submissive Aones to mix with the Phoenicians, and built Cadmea. He was said to slay a dragon at a well and sow its teeth near Thebes’ gates, producing armed men. He chose Echion as son-in-law, married Aphrodite and Ares, migrated to the Illyrians and Enchelians, and was succeeded by Polydorus.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER V.

Cadmus invaded the land with a Phoenician army, defeated the Hyantes, and allowed the submissive Aones to remain and mix with the Phoenicians. He built Cadmea and chose Echion as his son-in-law because of Echion’s valour. Cadmus later migrated to the Illyrians called Enchelians and was succeeded by his son Polydorus.

CHAPTER X.

Cadmus is said to have slain a dragon by a well and sown its teeth in the ground near the gates of Thebes. The ground supposedly produced a crop of armed men from the teeth.

CHAPTER XII.

Cadmus was said to have travelled from Delphi toward Phocis with a cow purchased from Pelagon's herds, whose markings resembled full moons. Following an oracle, he settled with his army where the cow lay down, and he is credited with erecting an altar and statue of Athene; Polydorus later adorned a heavenly piece of wood connected with Cadmus and called it Dionysus Cadmus.

CHAPTER XXVI.

Cadmus received the oracle at Delphi that was later used by the Sphinx to test those claiming royal descent.

Cadmus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Cadmus, son of Agenor and brother of Europa, comes from Phoenicia, searches unsuccessfully for Europa, and follows Apollo’s oracle and a guiding cow to found Thebes. After killing Mars’s sacred serpent, sowing its teeth, and building with five survivors, he marries Harmonia. He later rules the Enchelians, introduces alphabetic knowledge into Greece, and ends life transformed into a serpent with Harmonia.

Chapter VII

Cadmus searches unsuccessfully for his sister Europa and follows Apollo's oracle, which leads him to the place where he is to build Thebes. He kills the serpent sacred to Mars, sows its teeth, and builds Thebes with five survivors born from them. He marries Harmonia, becomes king of the Enchelians after leaving Thebes, and eventually changes into a serpent along with Harmonia.
