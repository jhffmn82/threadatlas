Thracians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Thracians are a people of Thrace who possessed territory, forts, and castles. Allied with Scythians against Lysimachus, they captured Agathocles and returned him with gifts seeking protection and restoration. Their soldiers spared Lysimachus for the public good. Later they suffered Diegylis’s brutality, and nobles fled to Attalus.

Thracians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Thracians first settled and seized Strongyle under Butes, practiced piracy and carried off women, and occupied the island for more than two hundred years. Drought and famine compelled their departure, and Otus and Ephialtes defeated and expelled them. They revolted from Athens over control of mines but were subdued. They occupied Amphipolis after ejecting earlier inhabitants. Under Sitalces they entered Macedonia with a very large army, brought Amyntas, captured a castle, gained city surrenders, plundered Macedonia, and moved toward Chalcidica. Near Salmydessus they seized shipwrecked merchants; Xenophon defeated them and burned many towns and villages. Other Thracians invaded the Chersonesus of Thrace.

Thracians in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

The Thracians are a warlike, horse-riding, spear-brandishing nation ruled by Mars, with Polymestor as their king and host; Hecuba summons him and his sons, and he later appeals to them while punished.

The Thracians include the host to which Polymestor belongs. Hecuba asks that Polymestor and his sons be summoned to hear her words.

POLYMESTOR, HECUBA, CHORUS.

The Thracians are described as a warlike, spear-brandishing nation that rides horses and is ruled by Mars. Polymestor appeals to the Thracian nation while suffering his punishment.
