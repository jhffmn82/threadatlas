capture of Eurystheus

event · 2 works · 0 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: bound his hands in fetters, brought Eurystheus hither, come into my power, seized Eurystheus, taken him captive, takes the four-horse chariot of Eurystheus
  • In Hecuba and other plays, after the Athenian victory, Iolaus pursues Eurystheus, seizes him in his chariot near the rocks of Scyron, binds him with chains, and brings him before Alcmene.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Iolaus seizes Eurystheus's four-horse chariot at the Scironian rocks, binds Eurystheus in fetters, and brings him alive before Alcmena.
