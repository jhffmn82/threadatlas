Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Talthybius serves as an aged Greek herald sent by Agamemnon to Hecuba, reporting Polyxena’s sacrifice and the Greeks’ pity and admiration while carrying orders to dismiss the army. In Hecuba and other plays, Talthybius serves as a Greek herald, messenger, and troop leader, conveying Agamemnon’s orders, announcing the captives’ assignments and executions, preparing Astyanax’s grave, and ordering the burning of Troy. In The Trojan Women of Euripides, Talthybius serves as a Greek herald and agent of Agamemnon, conveying the Greeks’ decisions, reporting Astyanax’s death, preparing his grave, and ordering Troy’s destruction.

Talthybius in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · about 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Talthybius first appears

Abstract written by a model

Talthybius is a herald, messenger, and Greek troop leader serving the Greeks. He carries Agamemnon’s orders and announces the captives’ assignments, Cassandra’s delivery to Agamemnon, Polyxena’s sacrifice, and Astyanax’s execution. Though reluctant when telling Andromache of the decree against her son, he urges compliance so Astyanax may receive funeral honors. After Neoptolemus leaves Astyanax’s body with Hecuba, Talthybius washes it, prepares and helps dig the grave, and directs the Greeks’ work on the tomb. At the end he orders Greek troops to burn Troy and commands Hecuba to follow Ulysses.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS.

Talthybius, herald of the Greeks, tells Hecuba how Polyxena was sacrificed. He describes Achilles’ son’s libations, the army’s silence, Polyxena’s courage, and the honors given to her corse, and he is instructed to prevent the Greeks from disturbing it.

THE TROJAN CAPTIVES.

Talthybius announces the results of the captives’ assignments to Hecuba and the chorus. He is ordered to deliver Cassandra to Agamemnon’s hands and then leads her toward the fleet.

CHOR. Attendants of the aged Hecuba,

Talthybius reluctantly tells Andromache that the Greeks have decreed Astyanax’s death. He says Ulysses persuaded the assembled Greeks that the son of Hector must not be raised, and he urges Andromache not to resist if she wants her son to receive funeral honors.

TALTHYBIUS, HECUBA, CHORUS.

Talthybius tells Hecuba that Neoptolemus has departed for Phthia with Andromache and has left Astyanax’s body in Hecuba’s care. He says that he washed the body and will help mark out and dig the grave, then later orders the Greek troops to burn Troy and commands Hecuba to follow Ulysses.

Talthybius in The Trojan Women of Euripides

Euripides · 414 BC · the work’s page · read it where Talthybius first appears

Abstract written by a model

Talthybius is a Greek herald and agent of the Greek army who serves Agamemnon. He conveys the Greeks’ decisions after the lots assign Cassandra to Agamemnon, Hecuba to Odysseus, and Andromache to Pyrrhus, escorting Cassandra toward the fleet. Reluctantly bearing bad news, he reports the council’s order for Astyanax’s death, takes the child from Andromache, and later delivers his body to Hecuba, cleanses his wounds, and prepares his grave. At the end, he orders the Greek captains to burn Troy and has Hecuba taken away.
