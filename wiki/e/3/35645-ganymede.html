Ganymede in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

Ganymede is a distinguished writer and boldly scheming administrator, formerly a precocious youth and the youngest child of his family. Commended to elder siblings’ care and called the youngest darling by his mother, he produced A Comparative Estimate of European Nations before leaving his teens. He founded and headed a literary organisation, continually generating schemes and writings. After an Antinoues period of chestnut locks, he grew self-conscious, married a considerably older wife, became a father, and served in and advocated reform of his government department. Though aging and growing heavier, he persistently believed and appeared youthful.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

XII.

Ganymede was the youngest child in his family and became known as a precocious writer after producing A Comparative Estimate of European Nations before he was well out of his teens. He later founded and presided over a literary organisation, continued producing schemes and writings, married an older woman, and took a government post. As he aged, grew heavier, and became more self-conscious, he still presented himself as a remarkably young man.
