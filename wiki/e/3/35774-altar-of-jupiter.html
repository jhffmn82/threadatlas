altar of Jupiter in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

The altar of Jupiter is a protective sanctuary erected by Hercules to memorialize victory over the Minyans. It provides asylum to Hercules’ children and other threatened suppliants, including Amphitryon and Megara, despite their deprivation. At Marathon, Copreus tries to remove them, but the Chorus and Demophoon defend its inviolability.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

HERCULES DISTRACTED.

The altar of Jupiter is the refuge where Amphitryon, Megara, and the children remain while threatened by Lycus. They lack food, drink, clothing, and shelter but continue to rely on the altar as their only protection.

THE CHILDREN OF HERCULES.

The altar of Jupiter at Marathon provides the children of Hercules and Iolaus with sacred protection. Copreus attempts to remove them by force, but the Chorus and Demophoon declare that no one may violate the altar or drag suppliants from it.
