  • In The Fall of Troy, Polyxeina’s sacrifice is performed at Achilles’s tomb to appease Achilles, after which the storm is stilled and the Argives hope to return home safely.

Polyxena’s sacrifice in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Polyxena’s sacrifice is a rite at Achilles’ tomb, demanded by his appearing ghost and approved by the Greek army to propitiate him and allow the Greeks to depart Troy safely. Though Hecuba tries to replace or save her, Polyxena freely accepts her assigned death. Achilles’ son performs the sacrifice; Polyxena bleeds and dies, then the Greeks honor her while Andromache shrouds her.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

HECUBA.

Polyxena’s sacrifice is demanded by Achilles’ ghost and approved by the Greek army. Polyxena accepts it willingly, while Hecuba unsuccessfully attempts to take her place or prevent her death.

CHORUS.

Polyxena’s sacrifice is performed by Achilles’ son at the sepulchral hillock of Achilles so that the Greeks may safely depart Troy. Polyxena accepts death freely, and the assembled Greeks honor her after she dies.

THE TROJAN CAPTIVES.

Polyxena’s sacrifice at Achilles’ tomb is reported to Hecuba as her assigned fate. Talthybius calls Polyxena fortunate because death releases her from the afflictions suffered by the surviving captives.

CHOR. Attendants of the aged Hecuba,

Polyxena’s sacrifice occurs at the tomb of Achilles, where she bleeds and dies by impious hands. Andromache witnesses the death, covers Polyxena with a shroud, and says Polyxena’s fate is more enviable than that of the living captives.

sacrifice of Polyxena in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

The sacrifice of Polyxena is a funerary offering at Achilles's tomb, requested by Achilles as honor to his tomb and approved by the Greeks after debate. Achilles appears above the tomb and demands Polyxena; she is taken from Hecuba, accepts her fate, and is sacrificed willingly and nobly by Neoptolemus with a sword after libations from a golden goblet. The offering is meant to secure the Greeks' prosperous return from Troy. Afterward, burial is allowed, but construction of Polyxena's tomb is delayed so she may be buried beside Polydore.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

HECUBA.

Achilles demands the sacrifice of Polyxena after appearing above his tomb. The Greeks will take her from Hecuba and offer her as an honor to Achilles.

HECUBA. CHORUS.

The sacrifice of Polyxena is approved by the Greeks after debate. Polyxena accepts it and is taken away to die by the sword or pyre of Achilles.

TALTHYBIUS, HECUBA, CHORUS.

Polyxena is sacrificed at Achilles' tomb so that the Greeks may gain a prosperous return from Troy. She dies willingly and nobly, asking to remain free in death; afterward, the Greeks prepare funeral honors for her.

FEMALE ATTENDANT, CHORUS, HECUBA.

Polyxena has been sacrificed, and Talthybius has reported that the Greeks will not prevent her burial. Agamemnon orders that her tomb's construction be delayed so that she can be buried beside Polydore.
