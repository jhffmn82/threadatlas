lonely road in The Road to Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1909

The lonely road is the only road in its part of the country, running through empty fields and groves. It begins as a place where Polychrome is found dancing and weeping, then becomes rough and uneven across a rocky plain toward low mountains, seeming to end at rocks where Scoodlers block it.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

5. The Rainbow's Daughter

The lonely road runs through charming fields and groves with no houses or people nearby. Polychrome is found dancing and weeping in the middle of it after falling from the rainbow.

9. Facing the Scoodlers

The road becomes rough and uneven as the travelers cross a rocky plain and approach low mountains. It appears to end soon and passes through an opening in the rocks, but the Scoodlers block the travelers' way along it.
