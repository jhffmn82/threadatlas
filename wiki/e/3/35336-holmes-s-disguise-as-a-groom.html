Holmes's disguise as a groom in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

Holmes’s disguise as a drunken, ill-kempt groom lets him investigate Briony Lodge, gain horsemen’s and cabmen’s trust, and learn Irene Adler’s routine and connection with Godfrey Norton.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

II.

Holmes uses the disguise of a drunken-looking, ill-kempt groom to investigate Briony Lodge and learn about Irene Adler from local horsemen and cabmen. In this role, he examines the house and discovers details about Adler's routine and her association with Godfrey Norton.
