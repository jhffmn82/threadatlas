  • In The Bacchae of Euripides, Pentheus's disguise is a Bacchic costume consisting of a trailing robe, snood, fawn-skin, wand, and long hair, arranged by Dionysus after Pentheus agrees to observe the Bacchae in disguise.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Pentheus's disguise is a linen robe, turban, thyrsus, and dappled deer-hide costume used by Bacchus to transform him until he resembles a Bacchant or Cadmus's daughter.

Pentheus's disguise in The Bacchae of Euripides

Euripides · -0406/-0405

Pentheus’s intended Bacchic disguise includes a trailing robe, snood, fawn-skin, wand, and long hair; after entering in fury, he is persuaded to observe the Bacchae disguised, and Dionysus arranges it.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

_Enter_ PENTHEUS _in fury_.

Dionysus persuades Pentheus to observe the Bacchae in disguise rather than attack them openly. Pentheus is to wear a long trailing robe, a snood, a fawn-skin, and a wand, with a long tress of hair beneath his shoulders, and Dionysus goes to arrange the costume.

Pentheus's woman's disguise in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

A costume of linen robe, turban, thyrsus, and dappled deer-hide, used by Bacchus to transform Pentheus’s hair and dress until he resembles a Bacchant or Cadmus’s daughter.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

MESSENGER. Pentheus, ruler o'er this Theban land, I come, having left

Bacchus dresses Pentheus in a linen garment, a robe reaching his feet, a turban, a thyrsus, and a dappled deer-hide. He arranges Pentheus's hair and clothing so that he resembles a Bacchant or one of Cadmus's daughters.
