Abstract written by a model

In “ARES: An Automated Evaluation Framework for Retrieval-Augmented Generation Systems,” answer relevance is an evaluation capability and criterion assessed by a dedicated binary judge and reported as a component-level ARES score. In “HyPA-RAG: A Hybrid Parameter Adaptive Retrieval-Augmented Generation System for AI Legal and Policy Applications,” Answer Relevancy measures alignment between a generated answer and the original question through mean cosine similarity of related embeddings. In “RAGAS: Automated Evaluation of Retrieval Augmented Generation,” Answer Relevance is used as a quality dimension and relevance metric measuring how directly and appropriately a response addresses an initial question or instruction. In “Investigating Retrieval-Augmented Generation in Quranic Studies: A Study of 13 Open-Source Large Language Models,” Answer Relevance is used as a human-evaluated metric of whether model responses directly and appropriately address a query’s intent. In “OG-RAG: Ontology-grounded retrieval-augmented generation for large language models,” Answer Relevance measures how easily the original question can be inferred from a generated response and evaluates pertinence to the query and ground-truth answer. In “RAGBench: Explainable Benchmark for Retrieval-Augmented Generation Systems,” answer relevance evaluates generative-model response quality, with RAGAS assessing it using an LLM judge and ARES predicting it using fine-tuned smaller NLI models. In “Ragas: Automated Evaluation of Retrieval Augmented Generation,” Answer Relevance is presented as a central reference-free quality aspect and defined as a metric for measuring whether generated answers directly and appropriately address questions.

answer relevance in ARES: An Automated Evaluation Framework for Retrieval-Augmented Generation Systems

Jon Saad-Falcon · 2024 · the work’s page · read it where answer relevance first appears

Abstract written by a model

Answer relevance is a RAG evaluation capability and criterion that measures whether a generated answer is relevant to the question, given the query and retrieved passage. In ARES, it is assessed by a dedicated binary judge trained on positive and generated negative examples. It is evaluated using mock query-passage-answer matches with known success rates and alongside context relevance. In experiments, ARES improves answer-relevance evaluation over existing automated approaches, averaging 0.132 higher Kendall’s tau than RAGAS in pseudo-system evaluation and 0.97 on existing RAG systems. It remains a component-level ARES score.

answer relevancy in RAGAS: Automated Evaluation of Retrieval Augmented Generation

Shahul Es, Jithin James, Luis Espinosa-Anke, Steven Schockaert · 2024 · the work’s page · read it where answer relevancy first appears

Abstract written by a model

Answer relevance is a quality dimension and relevance metric for judging how directly and appropriately a response addresses an initial question or instruction. It focuses on answer alignment, penalizing incomplete or redundant information while not considering factuality. It is computed as the average similarity, specifically average cosine similarity, between the original question and questions generated from the answer. Examples contrast a complete PSLV-C56 launch answer containing the requested date, time, and location with an incomplete, unrelated answer. It is used in evaluation, where subtle differences between candidate answers can lower agreement, and RAGAS predictions were closely aligned with human judgments.

Answer Relevance in Ragas: Automated Evaluation of Retrieval Augmented Generation

Shahul Es, Jithin James, Luis Espinosa-Anke, Steven Schockaert · 2025-04-28 · the work’s page · read it where Answer Relevance first appears

Abstract written by a model

Answer Relevance is a metric and quality aspect of retrieval augmented generation systems that measures whether a generated answer directly and appropriately addresses the actual question. It matters because it penalizes incomplete answers and redundant information, while not evaluating factuality. Its score is estimated by generating potential questions from the answer and averaging their cosine similarity to the original question. It can also be evaluated by human annotators through pairwise comparison of candidate answers. Across the records, it is presented as one of three central reference-free quality aspects, then defined and scored, with Ragas agreeing with human assessments on 0.78 of pairwise comparisons and predictions closely aligning with human predictions.
