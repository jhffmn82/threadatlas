Abstract written by a model

The tchung is the Chinese name for a bell. Its forms include the square copper-and-tin té-tchung or piao for marking time and musical divisions, sets called pien-tchung, and the clapperless oval hiuen-tchung, struck with a wooden mallet and popular in the Han dynasty.

CHAPTER V.

The tchung was the Chinese name for a bell. The té-tchung, or piao, was a somewhat square-shaped copper-and-tin bell used chiefly to indicate time and divisions in musical performances, while sets of such bells formed the pien-tchung and had the same scale as the king. The hiuen-tchung was an oval bell without a clapper, sounded with a small wooden mallet, and was associated with the Han dynasty.
