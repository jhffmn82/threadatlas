the Chorus of Corinthian Women in Medea

Euripides · -0430

The Chorus of Corinthian Women is a collective singing group of women, divided into speaking groups, that responds to Medea’s suffering and deeds. It first comes after hearing her cries, sympathizes with her, and promises secrecy, while fearing for the innocent children. It judges the hatred between Medea and Jason as terrible, laments women’s wrongs and abandonment, and declares that life, laws, marriage, and gender relations will be transformed. After celebrating Athens and Aegeus, it urges Medea not to kill the children, foresees the royal bride’s death, and mourns the children’s fate. After the deaths, it reacts with horror and pity, condemns Medea’s killing of her children, warns Jason of coming wrath, and some women try to save the children.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

MEDEA

The Chorus comes after hearing Medea's cries and expresses sympathy for her suffering. Its members promise not to betray her when she asks for help, but they also fear that her grief may move against the innocent children.

MEDEA.

The Chorus declares that life and its laws have changed and predicts that women will become terrible in story. It suggests that old accounts of women will be replaced and laments that men have long known and preserved stories of wrongs done to women.

_Enter from the right_ JASON.

The Chorus judges the hatred between Medea and Jason to be terrible and beyond healing. It sings against destructive passion and laments the fate of a woman cast out from her home and left without a city, brother, or protector.

CHORUS.

The Chorus celebrates Athens and Aegeus before Medea reveals her plans, then urges her not to kill the children. It later foresees the royal bride’s death from the gifts and laments the children’s impending fate.

[_Enter_ MESSENGER.

The Chorus reacts with horror and pity to the deaths and warns of the wrath that will fall on Jason. Some of the women try to enter the house and save Medea's children, while others invoke the story of Ino, who also killed her children.

the women of the Chorus in Medea

Euripides · -0430

A group of women sympathizes with Medea as displaced and landless, condemns men’s wrongs against women, and sings of her sea-crossing, broken oath, and loss of both paternal home and promised household.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

MEDEA.

The women sing of Medea's journey from her father's home across the sea and into a land where she has become an alien and landless ranger. They describe the weakening of the great oath and her loss of both her father's house and the household where she was once promised love.

Chorus in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

The Chorus is a group of Corinthian women who sympathize with Medea as a friend and fellow woman, recognize the injustice of Jason’s betrayal, and agree to keep silent about her plans. It worries that her grief and anger will become violence, condemns the conflict, and praises moderation in love. Although initially supportive of Medea’s response to Jason, it opposes the murder of her children on moral and emotional grounds. After the gifts are sent, it foresees disaster, pities Medea and her sons, hears their cries, and urges her to stop.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

MEDEA.

The Chorus of Corinthian women sympathizes with Medea and agrees to keep silent about her plans. It hears her cries, recognizes the justice of her anger at Jason, and worries that her grief will lead to violence.

JASON, MEDEA, CHORUS.

The Chorus condemns the anger between Jason and Medea and considers Jason unjust for betraying his wife. It praises moderation in love, mourns the suffering caused by exile, and later urges Medea not to kill her children.

JASON, MEDEA, CHORUS.

The Chorus first responds with hope that the quarrel between Medea and Jason has ended, but it soon foresees that Medea's gifts will destroy the bride and the children. It laments Medea's intentions, hears the sons' cries, and urges her to stop the murders.
