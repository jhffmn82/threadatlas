the ship in Iphigenia in Tauris

Euripides · -0413/-0411

The ship carries Orestes and Pylades from Argolis to Tauris, then waits moored near a purification channel with fifty oars ready. It later carries Orestes, Iphigenia, and Artemis’s image toward Greece, struggles against current and squall toward rocks, and is saved when Athena has Poseidon calm the waves.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

LEADER.

The ship lies moored near the channel where Iphigenia proposes to conduct the purification. Orestes says that fifty oars are waiting, making it possible for the group to flee by sea.

CHORUS. [STROPHE.]

The Greek ship carries Orestes, Iphigenia, and the image of Artemis away from Tauris. It fights the current and a squall, is driven toward the rocks, and is saved when Athena orders Poseidon to calm the waves.

ship in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

The ship is the vessel that brings Orestes and Pylades from Argos to Tauris after a dangerous voyage and appears near the Symplegades. It waits secretly at anchor, well-rowed and prepared for Iphigenia’s escape plan. Iphigenia, expected to end her slavery, boards it with Orestes, Pylades, and the sacred image of Artemis. As Thoas’s men attempt to stop the departure, the sailors resist. Strong currents first drive the ship back, but Minerva’s command makes it safe, and it voyages toward Greece and Athens under her protection.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

IPHIGENIA IN TAURIS.

Orestes and Pylades travel in a ship from Argos through the seas to Tauri. The herdsmen see the ship's arrival near the Symplegades.

CHOR. Thou hast told wondrous things concerning him who has appeared,

The Chorus wishes that a wind or ship from Greece had arrived carrying Helen and Menelaus so that Iphigenia could avenge herself. Orestes and Pylades have reached the land by ship after a dangerous sea voyage.

IPH. O dearest one, no more, for thou art dearest. I hold thee, Orestes,

The ship lies at anchor and is ready for sailing. Iphigenia plans to carry Diana’s image aboard it with Orestes and Pylades so that they can escape the barbarian land and return toward Greece.

The Grecian ship waits secretly near the shore with its sailors and receives Iphigenia, Orestes, and the sacred image. Men sent by Thoas try to stop it, but the ship's sailors resist them. The vessel is first driven back by strong currents and later is made safe by Minerva's command.
