Phaedra's secret love appears as a wasting illness: she lies weak, refuses food, changes restlessly, and speaks in fevered images. The Nurse says that caring for another person's pain is harder than bearing one's own, and the women cannot initially identify the cause.

Phædra's illness in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Phædra’s illness is an incurable, forbidden love for Hippolytus, shown through weakness, wasting, fasting, restlessness, longings, and disturbed speech; the Nurse discovers its cause, seeks a remedy, and Phædra chooses death.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

PHÆDRA, NURSE, CHORUS.

Phædra’s illness appears as weakness, wasting, fasting, restless changes of posture, unusual longings, and disturbed speech. The Nurse initially cannot identify its cause but eventually learns that love for Hippolytus lies behind it.

CHORUS, PHÆDRA.

Phædra's disease is her forbidden love for Hippolytus. The Nurse attempts to find a remedy, but Phædra says the suffering is incurable and chooses death.
