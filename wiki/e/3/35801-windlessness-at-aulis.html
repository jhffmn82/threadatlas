windlessness at Aulis

event · 2 works · 1 quoted fact · 2 connections
Also called: calm at Aulis, delay, favorable breeze, no power of sailing, stress of weather, was wind-bound, ἄπλοια κατεῖχε

Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the calm at Aulis prevented the Greeks from sailing, and the army demanded that the ships either be dismissed or continue seeking a way to depart. In The Library, the windlessness at Aulis prevented the fleet from sailing; Calchas said it would continue unless Iphigenia was presented as a sacrifice to Artemis.
