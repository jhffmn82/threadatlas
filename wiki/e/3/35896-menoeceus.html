Menoeceus in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Menoeceus is presented as Creon’s son, descended from the Spartoi, and as the required sacrifice to preserve Thebes. Though Creon urges him to flee, he brings Tiresias to the city, refuses to abandon his country, and fulfills the prophecy by stabbing himself in the dragon’s cave.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE PHŒNICIAN VIRGINS.

Menoeceus is the father of Jocasta and Creon.

ETEOCLES, CHORUS, MESSENGER.

Menoeceus, the son of Creon, brings Tiresias to the city and is identified as the descendant of the earth-born race who can fulfill the required sacrifice. Although Creon tells him to flee, Menoeceus declares that he will not abandon his country and plans to stab himself above the dragon's cave to save Thebes.

Menoeceus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Menoeceus, Creon’s son, hears Tiresias’s prophecy and kills himself before the gates as an offering to Ares, enabling Theban victory.

Menoeceus, son of Creon, kills himself before the gates after hearing Tiresias's prophecy. His self-sacrifice is presented as the condition for the Thebans' victory.

Menoeceus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Menoeceus, Creon’s son, voluntarily kills himself as Delphi’s oracle directs when Polynices and his Argive army approach Thebes; his tomb stands near gate Neistis.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXV.

Menœceus, the son of Creon, has a tomb near the gate Neistis at Thebes. He voluntarily killed himself in accordance with an oracle from Delphi when Polynices and his army came from Argos.
