Abstract written by a model

In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Philoctetes' exile is his abandonment on Lemnos by the Atreidae and Odysseus, where he survives alone for ten years before Heracles persuades him to leave. In The Library, the abandonment of Philoctetes on Lemnos occurs by Agamemnon's order because of his incurable, foul-smelling wound; Philoctetes survives in the wilderness by shooting birds with Heracles' bow. In The Fall of Troy, Philoctetes' abandonment leaves him for years on the desolate shore of Lemnos, suffering from his wound and living in a cave, until his anger toward Odysseus and Diomede melts away and he forgives them.

Philoctetes' exile in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · 449 to 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Philoctetes' exile first appears

Abstract written by a model

Philoctetes' exile is his abandonment on Lemnos by the Atreidae and Odysseus after his wound made his cries disruptive during rites and sacrifices. Left with scant rags and food, he awakens to the departed fleet and survives alone for ten years in physical and emotional suffering. Though he initially refuses to return to Troy, Heracles' command finally persuades him to leave.

MONODY.

Philoctetes' exile on Lemnos has lasted through severe physical and emotional suffering after the Achaeans cast him out because of his wound. He resists ending the exile by returning to Troy, but eventually agrees to leave under Heracles' command.
