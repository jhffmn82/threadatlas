Pepin in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

Pepin is an industrious general writer for general readers who, encouraged by early access to print, made publication a necessity before gaining sufficient knowledge or settled belief. He turns first impressions into forceful judgments and increasingly mistakes appearances for understanding, developing a peremptory, arrogant style that can misrepresent others. Though his writing harms better people, he is privately amiable and worthier than his professional work.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

XIV.

Pepin is an industrious general writer who gained an early hearing and made writing for publication a necessity before acquiring sufficient knowledge or settled belief. His youthful sense of unlimited ability has hardened into peremptory style, official arrogance, and a habit of seeming to know; he increasingly explains other people's writing through things he does not know about them. The narrator considers him privately amiable and worthier than his professional writing, though his work can insult or misrepresent better people.
