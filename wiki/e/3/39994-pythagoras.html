Pythagoras

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Pythagoras was an eloquent and virtuous teacher whose disciples practiced moderation, purity, and virtue, and whom the Crotonians revered as a god. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Pythagoras was a philosopher who settled at Crotona, taught the Pythagorean philosophy, and advocated abstinence from animal food and belief in the soul's transmigration. In Description of Greece, Pythagoras, surnamed the Wise, was identified as the great-grandson of Hippasus through Euphron and Mnesarchus. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Pythagoras was a Samian philosopher who founded a society at Crotona and taught doctrines concerning numbers, the soul, the Supreme Deity, and cosmic harmony.

Pythagoras in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Pythagoras first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pythagoras, said to have been born at Samos or Tyrrhenum, flourished at Athens in the sixty-first Olympiad, renowned for eloquence, gravity, sobriety, and rejecting luxury. He defended Salamis for Evagoras, cared for and buried Pherecydes, returned to Italy, and taught disciples oaths, moderation, purity, and virtue; Crotonians revered him as a god.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

3G. When Thericles was chief magistrate at Athens in the sixty^

Pythagoras flourished at Athens in the sixty-first Olympiad and was said to have been born at Samos, though some said Tyrrhenum. He was renowned for eloquence, gravity, sobriety, and persuading people to reject luxury; he cared for his former teacher Pherecydes on Delos, buried him, and returned to Italy. He taught his disciples to avoid unnecessary oaths, keep sworn promises, practice moderation, maintain bodily and spiritual purity, and cultivate virtue.

Pythagoras in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Pythagoras first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pythagoras is a Samian-born philosopher who voluntarily leaves Samos through hatred of tyranny, travels through Asia, and settles at Crotona in Italy. After studying the nature of things, he publicly teaches and promulgates Pythagorean philosophy, investigating the universe’s origin and nature and teaching gods, natural phenomena, the soul, abstinence from animal food, and continual change. He holds that nothing perishes, identifies himself with Euphorbus in a former incarnation, forbids animal sacrifice for food, and advocates vegetarianism because souls may inhabit animals and flesh pollutes bodies. His reputation is said to draw Numa Pompilius, though this timing is disputed; Livy places him under Servius Tullius.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

FABLES II. AND III. [XV.60-478]

Pythagoras is a Samian exile who fled his rulers because he hated tyranny. He studies the nature of the universe and teaches the public about the gods, natural phenomena, the soul, abstinence from animal food, and continual change.

“The story is, that in the far Northern Pallene[38] there are persons,

Pythagoras is presented as teaching that everything continually changes and that nothing perishes. He argues that human beings should not kill or eat animals because human souls may inhabit animal bodies, and he supports this teaching with examples of physical transformations in nature.

EXPLANATION.

Pythagoras is said by Ovid to have instructed Numa at Crotona, but this could not have happened because Pythagoras was born many years after Numa’s time. Livy places Pythagoras in the time of Servius Tullius, while modern authors place him more than two hundred years after Numa.

Pythagoras in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Pythagoras first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pythagoras was a philosopher native to Samos who spent the chief portion of his life at Crotona. Tradition places his study in Egypt and eastern visits to Chaldean Magi and Brahmins. He finally established himself at Crotona, led a society pursuing wisdom, and influenced sobriety and temperance. With no preserved writings, he taught numbers, the Monad, transmigrating souls, a Supreme Deity, and cosmic musical harmony.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXVII

Pythagoras was a native of Samos who spent much of his life at Crotona. He is said to have studied in Egypt and the East, taught that numbers were the essence of all things, and described the soul as immortal and capable of transmigration.
