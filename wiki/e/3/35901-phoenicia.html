Phoenicia in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Phoenicia, containing Tyre, is a region of cities whose coastal position, ships, grain, timber, and closeness to Egypt made it a repeated prize in war. It revolted from Persian control, with Sidon as the chief resistance center, but was recovered after Artaxerxes marched against it. When Alexander entered, its other cities submitted, while Tyre alone resisted. Later Ptolemy occupied its cities with garrisons; Eumenes sought ships there; and Antigonus built a fleet through its rulers, coastal arsenals, and wood from Mount Libanus. After Ptolemy destroyed or abandoned the principal cities he held, Antigonus recovered Phoenicia without a blow.

Phoenicia in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Phoenicia is the sea-washed homeland of the Chorus of Phoenician Virgins. The Chorus says that its members were sent from their city to serve Apollo and that Phoenicia shares kinship and potential misfortune with Thebes.
