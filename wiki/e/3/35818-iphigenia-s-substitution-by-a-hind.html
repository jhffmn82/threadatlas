Iphigenia's substitution by a hind in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Iphigenia's substitution by a hind is Artemis's exchange of a hind for Iphigenia at Diana's altar. At the sacrifice, Iphigenia vanishes; Agamemnon believes he strikes her, while a large stag or hind appears, is identified by Calchas as the Goddess's victim, and is sacrificed in her place.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

[_Enter a_ MESSENGER.[102]]

At the moment of the sacrifice, the girl vanishes and a large, beautiful stag is found panting on the ground. The stag's blood wets Diana's altar, and Calchas identifies it as the victim brought by the Goddess.

CHOR. I deplore thee now destined to the gory streams of the lustral

Iphigenia says that the Goddess saved her by giving a hind in exchange for her, while Agamemnon believed that he placed the sharp sword upon Iphigenia.
