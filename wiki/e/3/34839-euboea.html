Euboea in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Euboea, containing Chalcis, saw most chief cities except Acta join an association with Athens, prompting Athenian forces to secure allies and suppress opponents. Internal factions, backed by Boeotians and Athenians, then fought a devastating civil war and made peace. Later Ptolemy’s and Cassander’s armies confronted there; Cassander left Plistarchus guarding Chalcis and marched away.

Euboea was divided by internal quarrels, with one party seeking aid from the Boeotians and the other from the Athenians. The resulting civil war caused widespread devastation before the inhabitants agreed to peace.

Euboea in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Euboea is an island containing Artemisium, where the Grecian fleet assembled and was stationed against the enemy; it joined the Greek confederacy against Sparta, then suffered Persian capture of Istiaea, devastation, and destruction.

The Grecian fleet was stationed at Artemisium in Euboea. The Persian fleet later took Istiaea in Euboea by storm, razed it, and devastated the island and its surrounding country.
