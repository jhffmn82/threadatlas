Abstract written by a model

In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Euboea was the region where Heracles was reported to be besieging or conquering Oechalia and where he sacrificed to Cenaean Zeus after his victory. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Euboea contained Chalcis and saw most chief cities except Acta join an association with Athens; it later endured civil war and hosted the confrontation of Ptolemy’s and Cassander’s armies. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Euboea contained Artemisium, where the Grecian fleet assembled and was stationed against the enemy; it joined the Greek confederacy against Sparta, then suffered Persian capture of Istiaea, devastation, and destruction. In The Fall of Troy, Euboea was the region near which Athena’s storm overtook the Achaean fleet, with its surrounding sea and shores lashed by the tempest, especially near Caphereus.

Euboea in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Euboea first appears

Abstract written by a model

Euboea, containing Chalcis, saw most chief cities except Acta join an association with Athens, prompting Athenian forces to secure allies and suppress opponents. Internal factions, backed by Boeotians and Athenians, then fought a devastating civil war and made peace. Later Ptolemy’s and Cassander’s armies confronted there; Cassander left Plistarchus guarding Chalcis and marched away.
