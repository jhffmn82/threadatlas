the rites of Bacchus in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

The rites of Bacchus are Bacchic religious practices led by Bacchus and honored by the Bacchæ through initiation, purification, dancing, and sacrifice. They are practiced throughout barbarian lands, mostly at night, and their knowledge is unlawful for the uninitiated. Participants call on Bacchus, wear deerskins, ivy, garlands, and animal skins, carry thyrsi, drink wine, and make ecstatic cries while drums and cymbals accompany dancing, including in caves and on Cithaeron. Initiation is believed to confer prophetic power and the rites are associated with supernatural power. Pentheus condemns the practices, orders participants seized or driven away, and tries to observe and mock the revels; this attempt ends in his destruction.

Bacchus festivals in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Bacchus festivals are religious rites celebrated by Bacchus’s worshippers and prescribed by a priest. They spread through Greece, drawing matrons, maids, newly married women, men, and people of every rank. Participants wear skins or vine leaves, loosen and crown their hair, carry the god’s image and green thyrsi, offer frankincense, and use ivy and nebrides. Processions, ecstatic music, cries, shouts, tambourines, cymbals, pipes, Bacchanals, Satyrs, and Silenus accompany the rites. Greek additions include licentious ceremonies; opposition is met with reported miracles and punishments, while Pentheus’s intrusion ends in destruction.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

The Bacchic rites spread through Greece with festive howlings and attract matrons, newly married women, men, and people of every rank. They are celebrated on Mount Cithæron, where Pentheus’s intrusion leads to his destruction.

notes .. notes

Bacchus festivals included the Trieterica and rites held on Cithæron. Participants carried the god’s image, wore vine leaves, bore thyrsi, used musical instruments, and called Bacchus by names including Bromius, Lyæus, Evan, Lenæus, and Sabazius.

BOOK THE FOURTH.

The priest orders matrons, maids, and new-married women to wear skins, loosen their hair, crown themselves, carry green thyrsi, offer frankincense, and invoke Bacchus. The festival includes Bacchanals, Satyrs, Silenus, shouts, tambourines, cymbals, and pipes, while the Ismenian matrons celebrate the sacred rites.

notes .. notes

The rites of Bacchus were called orgia and were celebrated with cries such as ‘Evoë Bacche’ and ‘Iacche.’ They met with opposition, and stories of miracles and punishments were reportedly used to encourage acceptance of Bacchus’s worship.
