Benchmarking Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Medicine

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

This document presents a systematic evaluation of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) for biomedical and medical question answering. It introduces MIRAGE, a benchmark of 7,663 multiple-choice questions drawn from MMLU-Med, MedQA-US, MedMCQA, PubMedQA*, and BioASQ-Y/N, and MEDRAG, a toolkit for testing RAG systems. MIRAGE measures zero-shot, multi-choice, RAG, and question-only retrieval settings using answer accuracy, standard deviation, and average accuracy across tasks. MEDRAG organizes evaluations around five corpora, PubMed, StatPearls, Textbooks, Wikipedia, and MedCorp, four retrievers, BM25, Contriever, SPECTER, and MedCPT, and six LLMs: GPT-4, GPT-3.5, Mixtral, Llama-2, MEDITRON, and PMC-LLaMA. Across 41 combinations and more than 1.8 trillion prompt tokens, it generally improves results over chain-of-thought prompting without retrieval. GPT-4 has the highest reported MEDRAG average accuracy, 79.97%, while retrieval substantially improves GPT-3.5 and Mixtral, at times making them comparable with GPT-4 using chain-of-thought prompting. The document situates this work after RAG proposed by Lewis et al. (2020), arguing that earlier biomedical evaluations largely emphasized vanilla LLMs rather than systematic medical RAG evaluation. Its results show that performance depends on the task, corpus, retriever, number of retrieved snippets, and placement of evidence in the context. PubMed and MedCorp are broadly useful, while MedCPT, BM25, and reciprocal rank fusion are strong retrieval choices. Combining corpora or retrievers can help. The study identifies log-linear gains as retrieved-snippet counts increase, but also a “lost-in-the-middle” effect when ground-truth evidence appears in particular context positions. The conclusion offers observations and deployment recommendations for medical RAG. Limitations include its emphasis on vanilla RAG, incomplete corpus coverage and retrieval evaluation, reliance on examination-style multiple-choice QA, and unevaluated generated rationales; active RAG and other knowledge-intensive uses remain future directions. Guangzhi Xiong and Aidong Zhang acknowledge NIH and NSF support, while Qiao Jin and Zhiyong Lu acknowledge support from the NIH Intramural Research Program in the National Library of Medicine.

Abstract

The abstract concerns the evaluation and optimization of retrieval-augmented generation for medical question answering. It introduces the Medical Information Retrieval-Augmented Generation Evaluation (MIRAGE), a benchmark with 7,663 questions from five medical QA datasets, and the MEDRAG toolkit. Experiments evaluated 41 combinations of medical corpora, retrievers, and backbone LLMs using over 1.8 trillion prompt tokens. MEDRAG improved six LLMs by up to 18% over chain-of-thought prompting, while the study also identified log-linear scaling and “lost-in-the-middle” effects in medical RAG.

Introduction

The unit concerns Large Language Models (LLMs), Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), and their use in biomedical and medical question answering. It introduces MIRAGE, a benchmark with 7,663 questions from five biomedical QA datasets, and MEDRAG, a toolkit covering five corpora, four retrievers, and six LLMs. The evaluation finds that MEDRAG improves performance over chain-of-thought prompting, that corpus and retriever preferences vary by task, and that combining corpora or retrievers can be beneficial. The unit also reports log-linear scaling with the number of retrieved snippets and a “lost-in-the-middle” effect based on the position of the ground-truth snippet.

Related Work / Retrieval-augmented Generation .. Biomedical Question Answering

The unit reviews Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), biomedical question answering, and related evaluation settings. Lewis et al. (2020) proposed RAG to improve knowledge-intensive generation by incorporating retrieved information, and later studies developed improvements and biomedical applications. The text states that systematic biomedical evaluations have generally focused on vanilla large language models without RAG, while this study provides the first systematic evaluation of RAG systems in medicine. It also explains that large language models have surpassed BERT-based models on medical question answering and that the study evaluates RAG using medical QA datasets and both RAG and question-only retrieval settings.

The MIRAGE Benchmark / Evaluation Settings .. Component Datasets

The MIRAGE Benchmark evaluates retrieval-augmented generation systems for medical question answering under four settings: zero-shot learning, multi-choice evaluation, retrieval-augmented generation, and question-only retrieval. It contains 7,663 biomedical multi-choice questions from five datasets: MMLU-Med, MedQA-US, MedMCQA, PubMedQA*, and BioASQ-Y/N. The benchmark excludes ground-truth supporting contexts and evaluates systems by answer-choice accuracy, standard deviation, and the average accuracy across all five tasks.

The MEDRAG Toolkit

The MEDRAG Toolkit is introduced as a toolkit for systematically evaluating retrieval-augmented generation systems for medical question answering on the MIRAGE benchmark. It contains three components: Corpora, Retrievers, and LLMs, and includes PubMed, StatPearls, Textbooks, Wikipedia, and the combined MedCorp corpus. Its retrievers include BM25, Contriever, SPECTER, and MedCPT, while its language models include GPT-4, GPT-3.5, Mixtral, Llama-2, MEDITRON, and PMC-LLaMA. On the MIRAGE benchmark, MEDRAG generally improves average accuracy over chain-of-thought without retrieval, with GPT-4 achieving the highest MEDRAG average of 79.97%.

Results .. Practical Recommendations

The unit evaluates MEDRAG on the MIRAGE benchmark across backbone LLMs, corpora, retrievers, snippet counts, and context positions. GPT-4 performs best with chain-of-thought prompting, while MEDRAG substantially improves GPT-3.5 and Mixtral and can make them comparable to GPT-4 under chain-of-thought prompting. PubMed and MedCorp are the most broadly useful corpora, with MedCPT, BM25, and reciprocal rank fusion providing strong retrieval options. The results show that medical question answering depends on task-specific corpus selection, retrieval quality, snippet quantity, and snippet placement.

Conclusion

The conclusion concerns evaluating RAG systems in medicine. It introduces the MIRAGE benchmark and the MEDRAG toolkit for this purpose. Comprehensive evaluations produced novel observations and practical recommendations for research and real-world deployment of medical RAG systems.

Limitations

The study on MEDRAG systematically evaluates medical RAG systems and provides practical recommendations. It mainly evaluates the vanilla RAG architecture, in which retrieved documents are directly prepended to the LLM context, while leaving newer designs such as active RAG for future work. The study identifies limitations involving corpus coverage, retrieval evaluation, examination datasets, and the use of multiple-choice QA. It also notes that MEDRAG could support other knowledge-intensive tasks and that its generated rationales remain to be evaluated.
