Chorus in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Chorus is a group of sailors under Neoptolemus, whom it supports as captain. It portrays Philoctetes’ isolation and urges relief while weighing departure, then defers to Neoptolemus. Calling itself a seer, it says a night vision banishes fear and foretells vengeance. It then prays to Bacchus, asks of Haemon’s death, laments Creon’s ruin, and closes by praising wisdom and piety and condemning pride.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS.

The Chorus prays to Bacchus, questions the Messenger about Haemon’s death, and reacts to Creon’s ruin. It concludes that wise conduct and piety to Heaven bring happiness, while the proud are punished.

CHORUS.

The Chorus says that it would be an erring seer, deprived of sense and wisdom, if the prophetic Power did not soon overtake the offender. A vision of the night has freed it from fear, and it predicts vengeance for the blood-stained pair.

CHORUS.

The Chorus describes Philoctetes' long isolation, hunger, wound, and suffering on the island. It urges sleep to bring him relief and repeatedly asks Neoptolemus to consider whether to stay or sail. Later, it leaves the decision to its captain while acknowledging Philoctetes' bitterness and Neoptolemus' pity.

Chorus of Mariners in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

The Chorus of Mariners, an Achaean naval chorus serving aboard the fleet, supports Neoptolemus' mission and pities Philoctetes' isolation and suffering. It urges him to accept friendship and sail to Troy; after Heracles appears, it prepares to depart with Philoctetes and Neoptolemus, praying for protection.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

PHILOCTETES

The Chorus of Mariners enters after Odysseus withdraws and offers to support Neoptolemus' mission. It learns the location of Philoctetes' cave and expresses pity for his solitary life, disease, hunger, and suffering before hearing him draw near.

MONODY.

The Chorus first urges Philoctetes not to reject their friendship and tries to persuade him to go to Troy. After Heracles' appearance, it prepares to sail with Philoctetes and Neoptolemus and prays for the sea nymphs to protect their voyage.
