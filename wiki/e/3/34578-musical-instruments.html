musical instruments

topic · 1 work · 18 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: instruments, sound-producing means

Abstract written by a model

Musical instruments are objects made from wood, metal, animal hide, bamboo, gourds, horn, bone, glass, pottery, stone, plant fibers, silk, and other vibrating materials. They accompany songs and dances and differ in construction, decoration, shape, sound, and outward appearance. Usually classified as stringed instruments, wind instruments, or instruments of percussion, they can reveal musical intervals and inform musicians, ethnology, history, musical development, and national character. Medieval instruments are known through artistic representations, especially sculptures, though changing names and inconsistent Latin terminology complicate identification. From earlier distinctive and visually beautiful instruments suited to particular effects, construction greatly improved by the previous century, while some believed tonal variety had declined; new instruments and improvements also influenced compositions and musical effects.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER I.

Musical instruments are made from materials including wood, metal, animal hide, bamboo, gourds, horn, bone, glass, pottery, stone, plant fibers, silk, and metal. They vary widely in construction, decoration, shape, and sound, and their study provides information about musical development, national character, history, and ethnology.

CHAPTER VII.

Medieval musical instruments are studied through artistic representations, with sculptures considered more reliable than frescoes. Their names often changed over time, and older Latin writers frequently applied the same name to instruments differing in form, power, and compass.

CHAPTER X.

Musical instruments had greatly improved in construction since the previous century, but this progress was thought by some to have reduced variety in tone color. Older instruments were often more distinctive in sound and more beautiful in appearance, and each instrument's characteristics made it especially suitable for particular musical effects.
