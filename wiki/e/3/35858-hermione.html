Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Hermione is Helen and Menelaus’s daughter, seized and threatened by Orestes and Pylades, and destined by Apollo to marry her fiancé Orestes. In Hecuba and other plays, Hermione is Menelaus and Helen’s daughter, a Spartan princess and queen, and Achilles’ son’s wealthy wife; she confronts Andromache, seeks the death of Andromache and Molossus, then repents, attempts suicide, and flees with Orestes. In Heroides and Amores, Hermione is Menelaus’s daughter and Orestes’s established wife, forcibly held by Pyrrhus; she calls on Orestes, rejects Pyrrhus, and longs to be reunited with Orestes. In The Library, Hermione was Orestes’s wife and had previously been betrothed to Neoptolemus in Troy; when Orestes went mad, Neoptolemus took her.

Hermione in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Hermione first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hermione is the daughter of Helen and Menelaus, raised at Agamemnon’s palace after Menelaus sailed to Troy. She becomes a pledge for Orestes against Menelaus: after pouring libations at Clytemnestra’s tomb, Electra lures her inside, where Orestes and Pylades seize and threaten her. Orestes also asks Menelaus to preserve her life. Apollo declares that Hermione is destined to marry Orestes, to whom she is engaged.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

MENELAUS, ORESTES, CHORUS.

Hermione is Menelaus's daughter. Orestes says that he does not ask Menelaus to kill her and asks him to preserve her life along with his own and Electra's.

ELECTRA, ORESTES, PYLADES, CHORUS.

Hermione comes from Clytemnestra's tomb after pouring libations for her mother. Electra draws her into the house under the pretense of seeking her help and has her seized as a hostage. Orestes advances on her, and Electra orders that a sword be held to her neck.

PHRYGIAN, CHORUS.

Hermione arrives at the palace as Clytemnestra falls and is seized by Orestes and Pylades. Orestes threatens to kill her, but Apollo declares that she is destined to marry Orestes.

Hermione in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · about 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Hermione first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hermione is the daughter of Menelaus and Helen, a Spartan princess, queen, and wealthy wife of Achilles’ son, though she was formerly betrothed to Orestes. In her husband’s household, she regards Andromache as a rival and believes that Andromache has used drugs or magic to make her barren and hated. Defending what Menelaus calls her bridal rights, she confronts Andromache at Thetis’ temple, threatens her, and, with Menelaus’ help, seeks to kill both Andromache and her son Molossus; she is even given power to decide Molossus’ fate. Her actions reverse into extreme remorse when she fears punishment and exile from Phthia. Ashamed and terrified of her husband, she attempts suicide by noose or sword but is restrained. She asks Orestes to take her away and flees Peleus’ house, leaving behind a marriage Peleus curses.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

ANDROMACHE.

Hermione confronts Andromache and accuses her of trying to usurp her place, using drugs to make her barren, and making her hateful to her husband. She threatens to remove Andromache from Thetis’ temple by force and says that no temple or altar will save her.

MENELAUS, MOLOSSUS, ANDROMACHE, CHORUS.

Hermione is Menelaus’s daughter, and Menelaus says that he is defending her bridal rights. He gives her authority to decide whether Molossus will be killed or spared, and the Chorus describes her as coming furiously to destroy the victim.

THE NURSE OF HERMIONE, CHORUS.

Hermione, daughter of Menelaus and Helen, believes that Achilles’ son will punish her for attempting to kill Andromache and her son. Overcome with shame and fear, she tries to tear her hair, expose her breast, hang herself, or stab herself, but the Nurse and attendants restrain her. She begs Orestes to take her away from Phthia, and leaves with him.

PELEUS, CHORUS.

Hermione flees Peleus’s house because she fears that her lord will cast her out after she plotted to kill the boy. Peleus later curses the marriage that brought her into his house and calls her an execrable fiend.

Hermione in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · about 24 to 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where Hermione first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hermione, Menelaus’s daughter and Orestes’s established wife, is forcibly held by Pyrrhus despite a prior promise. After calling on Orestes for protection, she laments her captivity, rejects Pyrrhus, affirms Orestes’s worth, and longs to be reunited with him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

VIII

Hermione says that Pyrrhus holds her against her will and dragged her into his palace after she called upon Orestes for protection. She urges Orestes to act, defends his courage and lineage against Pyrrhus's insults, and describes her grief, her aversion to Pyrrhus, and her longing to be Orestes' wife.
