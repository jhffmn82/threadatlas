Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Thoas is the superstitious barbarian king who rules Tauris, orders pursuit of the escaped Greeks, and then permits their departure when commanded by Minerva. In Iphigenia in Tauris, Thoas is the king of Tauris who orders the strangers’ confinement, purification, and pursuit after their escape, but obeys Athena and releases the women.

Thoas in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Thoas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Thoas is the swift-footed, superstitious barbarian king who rules Tauris and Tauri. He receives two captured Greek strangers, orders them purified and guarded on Iphigenia’s account, and regards her actions as divinely guided. When Iphigenia and Orestes escape, he orders pursuit, but obeys Minerva’s command to stop and permits them and the Grecian women to depart.

IPHIGENIA IN TAURIS.

Thoas is identified as the barbarian ruler of Tauri and as the king to whom the two captured Greek strangers are brought.

After learning that Iphigenia and Orestes have escaped, he orders pursuit, but Minerva commands him to stop. He accepts the divine command and allows the fugitives and the Grecian women to depart.

Thoas in Iphigenia in Tauris

Euripides · 413 to 411 BC · the work’s page · read it where Thoas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Thoas is the king of Tauris who rules the Taurians and insists on slaughtering strangers, though he is described as a fine fellow. He questions Iphigenia, accepts her claim that strangers and the image have been defiled, and orders confinement, guards, restrictions, and cleansing. After Iphigenia and Orestes escape with the image, he orders pursuit, then obeys Athena, releases the women, halts the galleys, and wishes them well.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

PREFACE

Thoas is described as a fine fellow who nevertheless insists on slaughtering strangers. Iphigenia deceives him through desperate improvisation so that she can save Orestes and the other Greek, and the preface treats her lying as justified by the mortal danger.

CHARACTERS OF THE PLAY

Thoas is the King of Tauris, described as a savage country beyond the Symplegades.

LEADER.

King Thoas questions Iphigenia about removing the image and accepts her claim that the strangers and the image have been defiled. He orders bonds, dark coverings, guards, a citywide prohibition on movement, and the cleansing of the shrine while Iphigenia conducts the supposed purification.

CHORUS. [STROPHE.]

Thoas learns that Iphigenia and Orestes have escaped with the image of Artemis and orders men and boats to pursue them. After Athena intervenes, he submits to her command, releases the women, stops his galleys, and wishes Orestes and Iphigenia well.
