Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, divine punishment is inflicted by Bacchus on those who dishonor the gods and brings ruin upon Pentheus and his family. In The Bacchae of Euripides, the gods' punishment falls on Cadmus's house because Thebes and its rulers failed to recognize Dionysus as a god.

divine punishment in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where divine punishment first appears

Abstract written by a model

Divine punishment is inflicted by Bacchus on those who dishonor the gods. It punishes Pentheus for opposing Bacchus: the gods eventually pursue the impious, Pentheus is led to the Bacchae and dies by his mother’s hand, and his family suffers ruin, exile, and belated recognition of its error.
