writing and authorship

Also called: The act of writing, authorship, print, printing, the act of writing, the literary calling, write, writing

Abstract written by a model

Writing and authorship are a creative act that give the speaker a delightful illusion of an audience and turn personal reticence into freedom on paper. Yet, when pursued incessantly for publication rather than knowledge or veracity, they bring harmful internal consequences: premature judgment, vanity or income tied to error, dulled vision, and pleasure in others’ mistakes. Authorship therefore requires scruple.

The act of writing brings the speaker a vague and delightful illusion of an audience. It allows the speaker to write freely, including about personal matters, despite past experience.

XIV.

Writing and authorship impose internal consequences when they are pursued incessantly for printing rather than for knowledge or veracity. They can encourage writers to express judgments before becoming convinced, invest vanity or income in mistakes, lose accurate mental vision, and cultivate pleasure in others appearing wrong. Authorship is described as a vocation that ought to be among the best warranted, not a license for indiscriminate commentary.
