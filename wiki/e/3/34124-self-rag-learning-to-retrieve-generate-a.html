ABSTRACT

Large language models often produce factual inaccuracies because they rely solely on their parametric knowledge. Retrieval-Augmented Generation reduces these issues by adding retrieved knowledge, but fixed and indiscriminate retrieval can hurt versatility or produce unhelpful responses. The authors introduce Self-Reflective Retrieval-Augmented Generation (SELF-RAG), which trains a single language model to retrieve passages on demand and reflect on retrieved passages and its own generations using reflection tokens. SELF-RAG models with 7B and 13B parameters outperform ChatGPT and retrieval-augmented Llama2-chat on open-domain question answering, reasoning, fact verification, long-form factuality, and citation accuracy.

INTRODUCTION

The unit introduces Self-Reflective Retrieval-augmented Generation (SELF-RAG), a method designed to improve the factual accuracy and overall quality of large language model generation without reducing versatility. SELF-RAG learns to decide when retrieval is useful, evaluate retrieved passages, critique its own outputs, and select better generations using special reflection tokens. The method is trained end-to-end with reflection tokens inserted into text by a critic model and supports customizable decoding and citations. Experiments on six tasks reportedly show that SELF-RAG outperforms several larger language models and conventional retrieval-augmented approaches.

RELATED WORK

The unit reviews Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), which augments language-model inputs with retrieved passages and improves knowledge-intensive tasks. It contrasts prior retrieval and filtering approaches with SELF-RAG, which trains an arbitrary language model to retrieve on demand, use fine-grained self-reflection, and support controllable generation. Concurrent methods address retrieval, passage filtering, compression, or search, while SELF-RAG emphasizes robustness, attribution, and customizable inference. The unit also distinguishes SELF-RAG’s offline critic-augmented training and reflection tokens from RLHF and iterative refinement methods.

SELF-RAG: LEARNING TO RETRIEVE, GENERATE AND CRITIQUE / 3.1 / PROBLEM FORMALIZATION AND OVERVIEW .. 3.3 / SELF-RAG INFERENCE

SELF-RAG is a framework that improves an LLM's quality and factuality by combining retrieval, generation, and self-reflection through special reflection tokens. It trains a critic model C using GPT-4-generated judgments, then uses C and retriever R to augment a training corpus for training generator model M with a standard next-token objective. During inference, SELF-RAG decides whether retrieval is needed, evaluates passage relevance and output support, and assesses response utility. It can rank candidate segments with critique scores, apply thresholds for retrieval, or enforce hard constraints during decoding.

EXPERIMENTS / 4.1 / TASKS AND DATASETS .. 4.3 / EXPERIMENTAL SETTINGS

The experiments evaluate SELF-RAG and diverse baselines on closed-set, short-form generation, and long-form generation tasks using metrics for correctness, factuality, fluency, and citation quality. The datasets include PubHealth, ARC-Challenge, PopQA, TriviaQA-unfiltered, a biography generation task, and ALCE-ASQA. Baselines include models without retrieval, standard retrieval-augmented systems, and methods trained with retrieved passages or API calls. SELF-RAG is trained on 150k instruction-output pairs with Llama2 generator models, a Llama2 critic model, and Contriever-MS MARCO retrieval, and achieves strong results across the reported tasks.

RESULTS AND ANALYSIS / 5.1 / MAIN RESULTS .. 5.2 / ANALYSIS

This unit evaluates SELF-RAG against supervised, proprietary, and retrieval-augmented baselines across tasks including PubHealth, PopQA, and ASQA. SELF-RAG generally outperforms non-proprietary models and improves citation precision and recall, while sometimes surpassing ChatGPT. Ablations show that retrieval, critic training, adaptive retrieval, and ISSUP-guided search each contribute to performance. Further analyses examine inference-time customization, retrieval frequency, training-data scale, and the reliability of reflection tokens.

ETHICAL CONCERNS

The work aims to improve the factuality of LLM outputs, whose lack has contributed to real-world problems such as misinformation and incorrect or dangerous advice. Its method shows significant improvements in performance, factuality, and citation accuracy. However, the method can still generate outputs that are not fully supported by citations, and the work hopes that explicit self-reflection and fine-grained attribution will help users verify factual errors.
