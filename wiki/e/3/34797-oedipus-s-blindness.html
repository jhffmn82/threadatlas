Oedipus's blindness in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Oedipus's blindness is a self-inflicted condition of sightlessness. He blinds himself with Jocasta's clothing pins because he cannot bear to see his father, mother, children, or Theban people. In old age he depends on Antigone and is portrayed by Creon as helpless, yet he says he can “see by sound” and speak with sight.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS.

Oedipus blinds himself by driving Jocasta's clothing pins into his eyes. He says that sight could give him no pleasure and that he could not bear to see his father, mother, children, or Theban people.

OEDIPUS AT COLONOS

Oedipus is old and blind and depends on Antigone to guide him. He says that he can nevertheless 'see by sound,' and he claims that the words he speaks will be full of sight.

_Enter_ THESEUS.

Oedipus's blindness is repeatedly used by Creon to portray him as aged, helpless, and dependent on Antigone. Oedipus says that Creon, after he lost his eyes, is now taking away the people who made his darkness bearable.

Oedipus's blindness in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912

Oedipus's blindness is a future endless night foretold while his eyes still see clearly. It unfolds into sightlessness: he moves with a staff in beggar's clothing and relies on Antigone's guidance, yet claims his words retain sight and rejects contempt for his condition.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS.

Teiresias says that Oedipus's eyes currently see clearly but will thereafter be plunged into endless night. He also describes Oedipus as becoming blind of seeing and moving in beggar's clothing with a staff.

OEDIPUS AT COLONUS

Oedipus is blind and depends on Antigone to guide and support him. He says that although he lacks physical sight, his words will be instinct with sight, and he argues that his blindness should not make him an object of contempt.
