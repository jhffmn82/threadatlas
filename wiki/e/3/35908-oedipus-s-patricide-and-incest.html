Oedipus's patricide and incest

Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, it is described as Oedipus unknowingly killing his father Laius and marrying his mother Jocasta, with whom he has children. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, it is described as Oedipus fulfilling the fate of killing his father Laius and marrying his mother Jocasta. In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, it is treated as Oedipus's unknowingly killing his father and marrying his mother, and as the source of his incestuous family.

Sophocles · 1912 · the work’s page · read it where Oedipus's parricide and incest first appears

Abstract written by a model

Oedipus recognizes that he married Jocasta, the woman who gave birth to him, and fathered children by her. He describes the resulting family as a monstrous and incestuous brood. Creon calls Oedipus a godless parricide and reproaches him for incestuous marriage ties. Oedipus answers that he unknowingly killed his father and unknowingly married his mother, arguing that he should not be blamed for acts committed without knowledge and under the gods' compulsion.
