Jocasta in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Jocasta is queen of Thebes, daughter of Menoeceus, sibling of Creon, and former wife of Laius. She is mother of Oedipus without initially knowing his identity, later Oedipus’s wife, and mother of Antigone, Eteocles, and Polynices. Her central concern is her divided family: she recounts its suffering, welcomes Polynices after exile, questions him about his circumstances and expedition, and brings him into Thebes under truce to mediate between him and Eteocles. She urges her sons toward equality and peace, but fails to reconcile them. After learning they will fight, she goes with Antigone to stop them, finds them mortally wounded, laments them, and kills herself with a sword in grief.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

THE PHŒNICIAN VIRGINS.

Jocasta is the daughter of Menoeceus and the wife of Laius. She recounts the history of Oedipus and their children, and she attempts to reconcile Eteocles and Polynices by bringing Polynices into Thebes under a truce.

POLYNICES, CHORUS.

Jocasta welcomes Polynices after a long separation and laments the sufferings of her family. She questions him about exile, marriage, and the Argive expedition, then urges both sons to honor equality and abandon their conflict, but cannot reconcile them.

MESSENGER, JOCASTA, CHORUS.

Jocasta learns that her sons and city have survived the first battle, but then hears that Eteocles and Polynices will fight each other. She goes with Antigone to stop them, arrives after they are mortally wounded, laments over them, and kills herself with a sword.

ANTIGONE, CREON, CHORUS.

Jocasta finds Eteocles and Polynices fighting and unsuccessfully bares her breast in supplication to them. After seeing their wounds, she takes a sword from the dead and kills herself, falling among her children.

Jocasta in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Jocasta is Thebes's queen, Creon's sister, Laius's wife, and later Oedipus's wife. With Laius, she bears a son who is abandoned after his feet are bound; believing him dead, she rejects the oracle about Laius's death. She helps halt Oedipus and Creon's quarrel and recounts Laius's killing and the infant's abandonment, prompting Oedipus's suspicions. At first she treats Polybus's death as proof prophecy failed, but, fearing Oedipus's true parentage, begs him to stop the inquiry. When testimony reveals that Oedipus is her son, she grieves over her twofold marriage and hangs herself in the bridal room.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

KING OEDIPUS

Jocasta is identified as Oedipus's wife and Creon's sister. The opening account states that she was Laius's wife and that she later married Oedipus.

CHORUS.

Jocasta stops the quarrel between Oedipus and Creon and asks Oedipus to listen to Creon's appeal. She says that Laius was supposedly killed by foreign robbers at a crossroads and that Laius's infant son was abandoned after his feet were bound. Her account causes Oedipus to connect Laius's death with the man he killed, and she agrees to summon the surviving shepherd.

_Enter_ JOCASTA.

Jocasta initially argues that Polybus's death proves the prophecy concerning Oedipus's murder of his father false, and she urges Oedipus not to fear his mother's marriage-bed. When the shepherds' testimony begins to reveal Oedipus's parentage, she desperately begs him to stop investigating and then exits in grief.

CHORUS.

Jocasta enters the bridal room in distress, laments her twofold marriage, and hangs herself. Her death is discovered by Oedipus.

Jocasta in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912

Jocasta is queen of Thebes, widow and wife of Laius, and later Oedipus’s wife, consort, and mother. Seeking to prevent a prophecy, she orders the abandonment of her infant son, Oedipus. She later rejects the oracle concerning Laius and tries to reconcile Oedipus and Creon, recounting Laius’s prophecy, the infant’s exposure, and the reported murder. Distressed by ominous predictions, she brings offerings to Apollo and initially takes Polybus’s death as proof that prophecy has failed. As Oedipus investigates his birth, she becomes alarmed; when the Herdsman confirms she gave him away, she falls silent. Realizing she has married her son and borne his children, she dies by suicide in the bridal chamber.

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS.

Teiresias says that Oedipus's mother bore him a son who is also her husband. He further prophesies that Oedipus will be proven her son and husband, and the brother of her children.

CREON.

Jocasta tries to reconcile Oedipus and Creon and urges Oedipus to trust Creon’s oath. She recounts the prophecy given to Laius, the reported murder at a place where three roads meet, and the exposure of Laius’s infant son; after hearing Oedipus’s story, she agrees to summon the surviving herdsman.

[Enter JOCASTA.]

Jocasta brings offerings to Apollo because Oedipus is distressed by ominous predictions. She rejoices at Polybus’s death, believing it disproves the prophecy, but becomes alarmed when Oedipus investigates his birth. After the Herdsman confirms that she gave him the infant, she leaves, declaring that she will remain silent.

[Enter SECOND MESSENGER.]

Jocasta dies by her own hand after realizing that she married her son and bore children by him. She hangs herself in the bridal chamber, and Oedipus finds her body.

CHORUS.

Oedipus reveals that the woman who bore his daughters was also his wife and his mother. His marriage to her is described as incestuous and impious.
