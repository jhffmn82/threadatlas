public man in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

A public man, especially one like Touchwood, is vulnerable to partisanship when temper drives speech. An uncontrolled temper can make facts and questions serve impulsive attitudes, enslave honesty, and compel defense of insults or contradictory opinions; public life is therefore especially hazardous.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

VI.

A public man with an uncontrolled temper is in danger of using facts and questions only to justify an attitude adopted impulsively rather than to gain rectifying knowledge. His honesty may be dragged captive by his temper, forcing him to defend insulting words or maintain consistency with opinions expressed in a contradictory mood. The unit therefore regards public life as especially hazardous for someone like Touchwood.
