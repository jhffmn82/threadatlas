the Greeks in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

The Greeks retain ancient national memories that aid revived nationality and political restoration, are strong in the city, and are criticized for treachery, lying, and disreputable conduct among some modern Greeks.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

XVII.

The Greeks are described as having been aided by the memory of ancient Greece despite criticism of treachery, lying, and disreputable conduct among some modern Greeks. Their national past is treated as a source of revived nationality and political restoration.
