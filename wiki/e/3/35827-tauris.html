Abstract written by a model

In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Tauris is an inhospitable barbarian land ruled by Thoas, containing Artemis’s temple and sacred image and serving as the place where Greeks are sacrificed and from which Iphigenia, Orestes, the image, and the Grecian women flee. In Iphigenia in Tauris, the Taurian citadel contains Artemis’s temple and image and is the place from which Iphigenia leads the escape. In Iphigenia in Tauris, Tauris is a remote, desolate, savage country ruled by King Thoas, where the Tauri sacrifice foreign captives to Artemis and where Iphigenia serves as priestess until she escapes with Orestes, Pylades, and Artemis’s image. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Tauris is in Scythia, where strangers are sacrificed to Diana and where Iphigenia serves until she escapes with Orestes and Pylades and Diana's statue.

Tauris in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Tauris first appears

Abstract written by a model

Tauris is an inhospitable barbarian land ruled by Thoas, containing Artemis or Diana’s temple, its sacred image, and rites in which Greeks are sacrificed. Iphigenia lives there as the goddess’s priestess. Orestes and Pylades arrive seeking the image, and Orestes is threatened with sacrifice. Iphigenia first seeks to send Pylades away and asks Orestes to take her from the land; after recognizing and joining with Orestes, she plans an escape by ship with him and the image. Thoas orders pursuit when Iphigenia, Orestes, the image, and the Grecian women flee, but Minerva stops him and allows them to depart.

Tauris in Iphigenia in Tauris

Euripides · 413 to 411 BC · the work’s page · read it where Tauris first appears

Abstract written by a model

Tauris is a remote, desolate, savage country beyond the Symplegades, ruled by King Thoas. It is the land of the Tauri, whose custom is to slaughter foreign captives for Artemis at her temple and altar, where Artemis has placed Iphigenia as priestess. Iphigenia lives there as Orestes and Pylades are held for execution, but hopes to send Pylades away. In the end, Orestes, Iphigenia, and Artemis’s image escape; Thoas pursues them, then accepts Athena’s decision that they must leave.
