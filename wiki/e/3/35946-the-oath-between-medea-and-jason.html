Jason’s promised marriage to Medea in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · -0299/-0200

Jason’s promised marriage to Medea is an oath made in return for her aid. Before Zeus and Hera, with Hera witnessing as queen of marriage, Jason clasps Medea’s right hand and vows that, if he reaches Hellas, she will be his honored wedded wife and their love will last until death.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Then did night draw darkness over the earth; and on the sea sailors

Jason promises Medea that, if he survives and reaches Hellas, she will be honored there. He further promises that she will prepare their bridal chamber and that nothing will separate their love until death.

BOOK IV

Jason vows before Zeus and Hera that he will make Medea his wedded wife in his halls when they reach Hellas. He seals the oath by clasping her right hand.

the oath between Medea and Jason in Medea

Euripides · -0430

A sworn marriage-and-loyalty bond between Medea and Jason, recalled by Medea after Jason breaks faith; she invokes Zeus and Themis as witnesses and seeks its end.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

MEDEA

Medea recalls the oath, clasped hands, and vows that bound her and Jason together. She calls on Zeus and Themis to witness that Jason has broken faith and asks that the end of the oath be marked.

oaths in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Oaths bind Medea and Jason in mutual fidelity, but Jason violates them. Medea recalls their pledged hands, invokes Themis and Jove as witnesses, and treats the broken vows as justification for revenge as reverence for oaths disappears.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

MEDEA.

Medea recalls the oaths and pledged hands that bound her to Jason and invokes Themis and Jove as witnesses to the wrongs she has suffered. She regards the violation of these oaths as part of the justification for her revenge.
