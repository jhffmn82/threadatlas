juruparis in Musical Instruments

Carl Engel · 1875-08

Juruparis is a mysterious, greatly venerated trumpet of Indians on the Rio Haupés. Made from slips forming the inner tube of Paxiaba palm and usually four to five feet long, it is hidden in a forest stream, revealed at night for feasts, and blown outside houses. Women and uninitiated youths are forbidden to see it. Specimens are at Kew gardens.

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

The juruparis was a venerated trumpet of Indians on the Rio Haupés, a tributary of the Rio Negro. Women were forbidden to see it under threat of death, and youths could not see it until completing initiatory fastings and scourgings; it was hidden in a stream and brought out at night during feasts, when it was blown outside houses.
