the snake-poison murder scheme in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

The snake-poison murder scheme, devised by Dr. Grimesby Roylott, sends a trained swamp adder through a ventilator and dummy bell-rope to a bed’s clamped occupant. Roylott recalls it by whistle; its rapid, chemically undetectable poison kills the victim.

The snake-poison murder scheme uses the ventilator, dummy bell-rope, clamped bed, whistle, and a trained snake to attack the bedroom occupant. Dr. Grimesby Roylott sends the snake through the ventilator and later recalls it by whistle, while the poison causes rapid death and would be difficult to detect.
