Arthur Holder

Abstract written by a model

Arthur Holder, Alexander Holder’s only son and Mary Holder’s cousin and intended spouse, is portrayed as a wild, financially irresponsible habitual gambler with club and gambling debts, influenced by Sir George Burnwell despite his devotion to Mary. After Alexander refuses his request for £200, Arthur is found holding and apparently bending the coronet, leading Alexander to call him a thief. Sullen and silent, Arthur refuses to account for the three missing stones despite threats, is arrested, searched, and imprisoned. He withholds the truth because he saw Mary remove the coronet and pass it through a window to Sir George. Arthur pursued Sir George barefoot, fought him, and recovered the coronet, but protected Mary by accepting suspicion. Sherlock Holmes determines that Arthur did not steal the gems, and Arthur later confirms the explanation.

XI. THE ADVENTURE OF THE BERYL CORONET

Arthur Holder is Alexander Holder's only son, whom Alexander describes as wild, financially irresponsible, and heavily influenced by gambling and expensive companions. He asks Alexander for £200, is refused, and is later found beside the coronet holding and apparently wrenching or bending it. Arthur denies that any jewels are missing and refuses to explain further after being called a thief.

“By this time the whole house was astir, for I had raised my voice in

Arthur stands sullenly with his arms folded and asks whether the narrator intends to charge him with theft. He refuses to explain what he did with the three missing stones and remains silent despite persuasion and threats. He is arrested, searched, and removed to a cell.

“Do you receive much company?” he asked.

Arthur Holder is imprisoned after Mr. Holder sees him holding the coronet, but he remains silent about why he was in the dressing-room. Sherlock Holmes concludes that Arthur did not take the gems, and Arthur later confirms Holmes’s account after Holmes has explained the case to him.

“For Heaven’s sake, tell me, then, what is this extraordinary mystery!”

Arthur sees Mary take the coronet from Mr. Holder's dressing-room and pass it through the hall window. He pursues Sir George barefoot, struggles with him, recovers the coronet, and keeps Mary's involvement secret to protect her.
