Peruvian syrinx in Musical Instruments

Carl Engel · 1875-08

The Peruvian huayra-puhura is a syrinx made of cane or stone, sometimes ornamented. A tomb specimen from Arica in the British Museum has fourteen reed pipes in two rows, open and closed to produce octaves. Another greenish-stone specimen, procured by Paroissien from a Peruvian tomb, has eight pipes and lateral finger-holes that lower pitches by a semitone. Its intervals were purposely arranged to produce a pentatonic scale.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

The Peruvian huayra-puhura was a syrinx made of cane or stone, sometimes ornamented with embroidery or designs. A British Museum specimen from a tomb at Arica has fourteen reed pipes in two rows, one open and one closed so that octaves are produced, while another stone specimen has eight pipes and lateral finger-holes that lower certain pitches by a semitone.
