Abstract written by a model

Montreal is a place in Lower Canada central to escalating racial and political conflict from 1832 through the rebellion of 1837. A by-election riot in 1832 brought troops, who killed three and wounded two French Canadians, while the county was criticized over a missing election writ. During the 1836 agitation, Montreal hosted loyalist meetings, an attempted rifle corps, and the formation of the Fils de la Liberte. In November 1837, the Doric Club and Fils de la Liberte rioted there, and Papineau was urged to leave. As rebellion continued, rebels at St Eustache threatened to capture Montreal, prompting volunteers to guard the Bord a Plouffe bridge.

{33} CHAPTER V THE NINETY-TWO RESOLUTIONS After 1830 signs began to multiply that the racial feud in Lower Canada was growing in intensity.

Montreal was the scene of a by-election riot in 1832 in which troops fired on rioters, killing three and wounding two French Canadians. The county of Montreal was also criticized because the governor had failed to issue a writ for the election of a representative.

{57} CHAPTER VII THE RUSSELL RESOLUTIONS When the legislature of Lower Canada met in the autumn of 1836, Lord Gosford earnestly called its attention to the estimates of the current year and the accounts showing the arrears unpaid.

Montreal hosted loyalist meetings and an attempted loyalist rifle corps during the agitation. It was also the place where the Fils de la Liberte was formed in the autumn of 1837.

] {69} CHAPTER VIII THE DOGS OF WAR As the autumn of 1837 wore on, the situation in Lower Canada began to assume an aspect more and more threatening.

Montreal experienced rioting on November 6 between the Doric Club and the Fils de la Liberte. After the disturbances, a priest advised Louis-Joseph Papineau to leave the city because his presence had become a source of disorder.

{89} But the end of rebellion was not yet in sight.

Montreal was threatened by the rebels at St Eustache, who believed they could advance on and capture it. A detachment of volunteers was sent to guard the Bord a Plouffe bridge at the northern end of the island of Montreal.
