Governor of Mogador in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

Governor of Mogador is Sidi Abdallah Deleero, a Moorish Deputy-Governor commanding Mogador during a bombardment. He repeatedly prevents British and other European residents and detainees from leaving beforehand. He opposes abolition as the Sultan’s government’s position, says most Muslims in Morocco support slavery, and regrets he cannot aid anti-slavery work, believing argument will not persuade the Sultan.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VII.

The Governor of Mogador tells the narrator that he has heard nothing improper about his conduct and regrets being unable to assist his anti-slavery work. He says the Sultan will not be persuaded on slavery by argument, though he jokes about his own slaves and asks the narrator what he would pay for them.

APPENDIX.

Sidi Abdallah Deleero was the Moorish Deputy-Governor in command at Mogador. He refused repeated requests to allow the British and other Europeans to depart before the bombardment.
