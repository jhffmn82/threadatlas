Abd-el-Kader in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

Abd-el-Kader is a highly revered marabout warrior and an object of enthusiasm among Mussulmen across North Africa, especially Morocco. Though he combines princely and sacerdotal authority like the Shereefs, he is not of the Prophet’s family and therefore could not dethrone the Shereefian dynasty. After withdrawing from Algeria, he sought refuge among Rif tribes, resisted the Imperial government for months, and finally chose pitched battle with the Sultan.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

Abd-el-Kader is described as a highly revered marabout warrior and an object of enthusiasm among Mussulmen in North Africa, especially Morocco. The text argues that he could not have successfully dethroned the Shereefian emperor because Moroccans would not deliberately abandon the Prophet’s line for a ruler of different blood.

CHAPTER IV.

Abd-el-Kader sought refuge and support among the tribes of the Rif after withdrawing from Algeria. He resisted the Imperial government there for several months but ultimately chose to confront the Sultan in a pitched battle.

notes

Abd-el-Kader is described as uniting princely and sacerdotal authority like the Shereefs, although he is not descended from the Prophet. During a storm on his passage from Oran to Toulon, his companions threw handfuls of salt into the sea to appease its fury.
