North Africa

Also called: Africa, North African, North African world

Abstract written by a model

North Africa is a region including Morocco and distinguished from the regencies of Tunis and Tripoli and from Algeria, an integral part of France. Its peoples include Berbers, Arabs, Moors, Jews, and Blacks; Berbers are described as aboriginal and mountain inhabitants, while Moors form much of the town and city population through mixture. It was successively inhabited or conquered by Libyans, Getulians, Phoenicians, Romans, Vandals, and Arabs, and is portrayed as religiously homogeneous under Islam. Numerous plains bear salt and nitre efflorescence, said to support apparently inexhaustible fertility despite little manure.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

CHAPTER II.

North Africa is described as the region conquered by the Saracens and as the area where Abd-el-Kader was widely revered. Morocco is distinguished from Tunis and Tripoli, which are described as regencies, and from Algeria, which had become an integral part of France.

CHAPTER III.

North Africa is described as having been successively inhabited or conquered by Libyans, Getulians, Phoenicians, Romans, Vandals, and Arabs. Its population is portrayed as religiously homogeneous under Islam, while its peoples include Berbers, Arabs, Moors, Jews, and Blacks.
