African peoples in Vestiges of the Mayas, or, Facts tending to prove that Communications and Intimate Relations must have existed, in very remote times, between the inhabitants of Mayab and those of Asia and Africa

Augustus Le Plongeon · 1881

African peoples are described through customs and warfare: they file front teeth, drink honey-, water-, and bark-based beverages, make offerings and sacrifices at graves, abandon homes after repeated deaths, communicate with large drums, and wear charmed leopard skins believed to protect warriors from spears.

African peoples are described as filing their front teeth into sharp points, drinking a beverage made from honey, water, and tree bark, making offerings and sacrifices at graves, abandoning houses or village sites after repeated deaths, and using large drums for communication. Some African warriors are also said to wear a charmed leopard skin believed to make them immune to spears.
