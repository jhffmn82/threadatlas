Abstract written by a model

Jereed, also called Belad-el-Jereed and properly called the Tunisian Sahara, is a country of dates in the Tunisian portion of the Sahara near the Great Desert. Once occupied by Carthaginians and then Romans, as antiquities show, it contains Toser, its headquarters, Nefta and other date oases, and El-Sibhah, a vast salt lake or plain. It is largely flat, dry, sparsely inhabited, and marked by little water, ruins, Arab encampments, hunting plains, palm gardens, and warm watercourses. Date palms surround its towns; dates and palm products are its principal resources, with dates judged better than those of Ghabs. A single rainy day in the past year was linked to their ripeness. About twenty thousand people live entirely on dates for six months, while trade and tribute in dates, money, woollen goods, blankets, quilts, and animals support the Bey and Government. Sidi Mohammed, Captain Balfour, and Richard Reade traveled there, and tourists later carried away its baskets, dates, skins, woollen articles, and live animals.

CHAPTER VIII.

El-Jereed, or Belad-el-Jereed, is described as the country of dates and a part of the Sahara near the Great Desert. Its principal resources are dates and palm products, and about twenty thousand people live there for six months of the year entirely on dates. The region is an important part of the Tunisian territory and provides the Government with substantial revenue.

CHAPTER IX.

The Jereed was the destination of Sidi Mohammed's expedition with Captain Balfour and Richard Reade. It was portrayed as a largely flat, dry region with sparse inhabitants, little water, ruins, Arab encampments, and extensive plains suitable for hunting.

CHAPTER X.

The Jereed is a date-growing region containing Toser, Nefta, Wedyen, and other villages and oases. Its settlements rely on palm gardens, warm watercourses, local trade, and tribute collected for the Bey, while its inhabitants include Arabs, merchants, landed proprietors, craftsmen, saints, and slaves’ descendants.
