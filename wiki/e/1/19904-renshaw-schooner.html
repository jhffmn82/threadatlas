Renshaw schooner in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

The Renshaw schooner is a beautiful, fine-sailing, flat-bottomed shallow-draft vessel made for Barbary ports. After the narrator leaves the Governor at Mogador, it carries the narrator, a horse, young vulture, and an aoudad for Mr. Willshire and the Zoological Society through severe Atlantic weather.

The narrator embarked for the Renshaw schooner after taking leave of the Governor at Mogador. The vessel was a beautiful, flat-bottomed, shallow-draft ship built for Barbary ports and carried the narrator, a horse, a young vulture, and an aoudad through severe Atlantic weather.
