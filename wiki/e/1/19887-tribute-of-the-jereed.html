tribute of the Jereed

topic · 1 work · 3 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: tax imposed, tribute, tribute collected in the Jereed, tribute of the people

Abstract written by a model

The tribute is a yearly levy collected by a Sheikh, who gathers dates from the people and pays the Bey a fixed sum on arrival. At Toser it is assessed at two bunches for each date-tree; those unable to provide it face imprisonment and punishment. It includes 500 camel-loads of dates from Toser, Nefta, and surrounding districts, plus money and woollen goods, totaling 529 loads and an estimated £9,110, excluding smaller animal presents.

CHAPTER X.

The tribute is collected through a system in which the Sheikh gathers dates from the people and pays the Bey a fixed yearly sum when he arrives. At Toser, the tax is described as equivalent to two bunches of dates for each date-tree, and failure to have it ready leads to imprisonment and punishment.

On our march back to Tunis, we encamped for two days by the foot of a range of h

The tribute was estimated at forty camel-loads of money, six of burnouses, blankets, quilts, and other woollen goods, and 500 camel-loads of dates, for a total of 529 loads. The goods were valued at about £2,860 and the money was estimated at up to £6,250, producing a possible total of £9,110, excluding additional horses, camels, and other animals.
