Rif in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

Rif is Morocco’s mountainous northern coastal region, stretching along the Mediterranean from the Moulwia to Tangier and bordering Habat. It includes Hashbat, Gharet, and Aklaia; is fertile and mineral-rich; is inhabited by warlike Berbers under precarious Sultan authority; shelters Abd-el-Kader after his retirement from Algeria; and is exceptionally dangerous to travel.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IV.

The Rif is Morocco’s mountainous northern coastal region, extending along the Mediterranean from the Moulwia to Tangier. It contains the districts of Hashbat, Gharet, and Aklaia and is inhabited by warlike Berbers over whom the Sultan exercises precarious authority. The region is fertile and mineral-rich, with cork forests, grazing valleys, and exports of skins, but travel there is described as exceptionally dangerous.
