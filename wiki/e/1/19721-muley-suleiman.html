Muley Suleiman in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

Muley Suleiman was Sultan and Emperor of Morocco. Proclaimed Sultan at Fez in the north during the civil war after Yezeed’s death, he became Sultan of the whole empire following Muley Hisham’s retirement. Described as a good and patriotic prince, he maintained peace and tranquility, stayed neutral during European wars, and cultivated peace with Europe. His most beneficent act toward European nations was abolishing Christian slavery in his States; he also exempted Mogador Jewesses from removing their shoes at mosques. He chose and declared his nephew Muley Abd Errahman, previously excluded because of youth, as legitimate hereditary successor. He died on 28 March 1820, about age fifty, and Abd Errahman succeeded him.

CHAPTER II.

Muley Suleiman preferred his nephew Muley Abd Errahman as successor because Abd Errahman was considered more suitable to preserve and consolidate the empire. His designation of Abd Errahman was presented as an act of justice because Abd Errahman had been excluded from the succession earlier on account of his youth.

[5] In the Shereefian lineage of Muley Suleiman, copied for Ali Bey by the Emper

Muley Suleiman was proclaimed Sultan in the north at Fez during the civil war following Yezeed’s death, and later became Sultan of the whole empire after Muley Hisham’s retirement. He is described as a good and patriotic prince who maintained peace and tranquility, remained neutral during European wars, cultivated peace with Europe, and abolished Christian slavery in his States. He died on 28 March 1820 after declaring his nephew, Muley Abd Errahman, his legitimate and hereditary successor.
