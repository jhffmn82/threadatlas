population statistics of Morocco in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

Moroccan population statistics are presented as proximate, conjectural, uncertain, and often exaggerated because local resistance to counting and conflicting place estimates make reliable totals difficult. The records use discrepancies for towns and poorly known tribal populations to question estimates. Graeberg’s 8,500,000 is considered more plausible after a deduction of about two million, while Jackson’s nearly fifteen million is rejected as excessive.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IV.

Moroccan population statistics are described as approximate and conjectural because local people resist counting populations and regard such questions as ill-omened, intrusive, or suggestive of espionage. Conflicting estimates for Mazagran, Melilla, Gomera, and other places are offered as evidence of their unreliability. The text nevertheless attempts to provide figures to indicate Morocco’s approximate strength and resources.

CHAPTER VI.

The unit questions the reliability of Moroccan population statistics because the towns enumerated appear to contain scarcely one million inhabitants and the tribal population is poorly known. Graeberg estimates 8,500,000 people, while the author considers a total reduced by about two million more plausible and rejects Jackson's estimate of nearly fifteen million as excessive.
