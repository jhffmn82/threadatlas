Abstract written by a model

The date-palm is a male and female organism. The female requires communication with the male for good fruit. From shoots it bears in six or seven years; from stones in sixteen; reaches greatest vigour about thirty; and may bear for seventy years. At full vigour it produces fifteen or twenty date clusters annually and requires root water every four or five days. It yields leghma, branches, fibres, and hardened wood; tapped when fruit is scant. Fruitful palms may be destroyed in villages’ wars, though elsewhere in the Sahara they are spared.

Of the Maroquine Sahara, I have only space to mention the interesting cluster of

Date-palms are abundant in the gardens of Figheegh, and their dates supply the western province of Algeria. The villages' wars are unusually destructive because the inhabitants destroy the fruitful palms, unlike communities elsewhere in the Sahara that spare them during bloodshed.

CHAPTER VIII.

The date-palm is male and female, and the female requires communication with the male to produce good fruit. Trees propagated from shoots can bear in six or seven years, while those grown from stones require sixteen years; according to Tunisian accounts, they reach greatest vigour at about thirty years and may bear for seventy years. The palm also supplies sap, fruit, leaves, fibres, wood, baskets, ropes, and other useful materials.
