the hyaena in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

A wild animal abundant in the forests of the Morocco Atlas, the hyaena is described as generally stupid and not usually dangerous; it was flushed, shot by Si Smyle, and its skin and head were sent to Richard Reade.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IX.

A hyaena was flushed from brushwood and shot by the Mameluke Si Smyle. The Bey sent Richard Reade its skin and head, and the text records a Moorish belief that eating its brain causes madness, while describing the animal as generally stupid in the woods and not usually dangerous to people.
