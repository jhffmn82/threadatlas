Bey's camel seizure in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

After disorder caused heavy camel mortality and scarcity, parties for the Bey seized about two hundred of the finest camels, marked them taba as government property, despite former owners' cries and curses.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Rode to Kairwan.

Because camels had become scarce after a disorder caused heavy mortality, parties acting for the Bey drove in the finest camels they could find. About two hundred animals were marked with the Bey's mark and made government property despite the cries and curses of their former owners.
