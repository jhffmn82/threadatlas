Abstract written by a model

Tangier is a Moroccan port and a place in Barbary, strategically important to England as a former garrison and control point for the Herculean Straits. It marks the western extent of the Rif, with Tetouan southeast and the northern intermediate zone running toward Mogador. Its residents included Jews whom the Emperor forced to retain black turbans after some returned from Gibraltar in European dress. Tangier’s commerce was overwhelmingly tied to Gibraltar: in 1856 it imported British and foreign goods, exported to British and foreign ports, and supplied Gibraltar with cattle and provisions. England might seek it again in war.

Tangier in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860 · the work’s page · read it where Tangier first appears

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER I.

Tangier had Jewish residents who sometimes returned from Gibraltar wearing European dress. The Emperor ordered such Jews to be undressed and to resume wearing the black turban so they could not pass as Christians or Europeans.

CHAPTER IV.

Tangier marks the western extent of the Rif in the unit’s description of the northern coast. Tetouan lies roughly two-thirds of a day’s journey southeast of Tangier, and the northern intermediate zone extends from Tangier toward Mogador.

APPENDIX.

Tangier traded almost exclusively with Gibraltar and maintained active intercourse with that port. In 1856 it imported British goods valued at L101,773 6s. and foreign goods valued at L33,793, while exporting goods to British and foreign ports and sending thousands of cattle and other provisions to Gibraltar.

notes

Tangier is listed among the places in Barbary that have already been described. The notes also state that England might again need to possess its former garrison there if Morocco became an enemy in a European war.
