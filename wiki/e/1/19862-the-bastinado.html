the bastinado in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

The bastinado is a generally formal punishment intended as terror, inflicted on tied culprits’ soles. Against Arabs accused of stealing barley, inferior Mamelukes beat them, though many strokes were omitted or merely taps or scratches.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IX.

The bastinado was used against several Arabs accused of stealing barley, with the culprits tied and beaten on the soles of the feet by inferior Mamelukes. The text says it was usually a formal threat, with many strokes omitted and those delivered often amounting to taps or scratches.
