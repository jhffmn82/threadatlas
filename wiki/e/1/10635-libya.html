Abstract written by a model

In The Argonautica, Libya is a distant region reached by the Argonauts, whose shore strands them without water, paths, or herdsmen before they cross sandy wastes carrying Argo toward the Tritonian lake. In The Aeneid, Libya is a coastal region containing Carthage and the sheltered harbor where surviving Trojan ships land after Neptune calms the sea; Aeneas wanders its deserts before Venus leads him to Dido's city. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Libya is named as a region whose remote parts may contain wild animals unlike those found in Greece, and its asps are said to have the same color as the Egyptian asps.
