Romans in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Romans are a political and military people whose record centers on repeated war, expansion, alliances, public conduct, and changing character. It opens with conflict and diplomacy in Italy: they hold a league with the Samnites and a truce with the Praenestines, fight the Samnites, recover Sora, Harpina, and Serenia, and ultimately make peace after twenty-two years and six months. They then ally with the Marsi, Peligni, and Marrucini. Romans in Musical Instruments

Carl Engel · 1875-08

Romans derived or adopted many musical instruments from Etruscans, Greeks, Egypt, and western Asia, without essentially improving them. Their favorite stringed instrument was the lyre; their range included lyres, flutes, pipes, bagpipes, percussion, horns, trumpets, and hydraulic organ. They used cornu and tuba for war signals, while Nero favored tibia utricularis.

CHAPTER IV.

The Romans adopted many instruments from the Etruscans, Greeks, Egypt, and western Asia without essentially improving them.
