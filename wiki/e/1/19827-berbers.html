Berbers in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

Berbers are a group also called Moors or Barbarians in Roman usage, comprising many tribes and subdivisions, including Amayeegh, Kabyles, Aoures, Shelouh, Shouwiah, and Touarichs. Many live in Atlas and other mountains, while Arab tribes are said to occupy plains. They are portrayed as North Africa’s indigenous peoples or as descendants of the Getulians, resisting Roman and Arab conquerors and often retaining political independence. They protect certain Atlas Jewish communities and share a mountaineer way of life with them, but also join Arab tribes in plundering Mogador and fleeing Moors. In Rif and Garet they herd animals, use forests and other resources, and engage in market disputes under loose Sultan control. They founded Mequinez through Meknasab, built or inhabited several towns, and were feared by escaping Europeans during further plundering. Their present divisions differ from five ancient principal tribes.

Narrative, section by section

7 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER I.

Berbers protect certain Atlas Jewish communities, whose standards the Jews follow, and the Atlas Jews share their mountaineer way of life. During the destruction of Mogador, Berber and Arab tribes plundered the city and later pillaged Moors who had fled with booty.

CHAPTER III.

The Berbers are presented as descendants or representatives of North Africa’s indigenous peoples, especially the Getulians, and as persistent opponents of foreign domination. They inhabit Morocco’s mountains, while many Arab tribes occupy the plains, and their resistance continued against Roman and Arab conquerors.

CHAPTER IV.

Berbers inhabit the provinces of Rif and Garet and are portrayed as warlike, semi-barbarous, and only loosely controlled by the Sultan. They graze herds and flocks in the Rif’s valleys, exploit its forests and resources, and participate in fierce disputes at market gatherings.

CHAPTER V.

Berbers founded Mequinez through the tribe of Meknasab, a fraction of the Zenatah, in the middle of the tenth century. Berbers also inhabit the mountains near Mequinez and are among the peoples whose revolts or disaffection concern the Sultan.

CHAPTER VI.

Berbers built or inhabited many of the towns described, including Tefza, Pitideb, Meramer, Tednest, Aghmat, and Messa. The text portrays them as an aboriginal population whose mountain communities often retain substantial political independence.

APPENDIX.

The Berbers were feared by the escaping Europeans, who believed they might be murdered if discovered. Their attention was chiefly occupied with plundering the city while the fugitives remained near the water-port.

notes

The Berbers are described as comprising numerous tribes and subdivisions, including the Amayeegh, Shelouh, Kabyles, Aoures, Shouwiah, and Touarichs. The text gives traditions about their origins and notes that their present divisions do not correspond exactly to the five ancient principal tribes.
