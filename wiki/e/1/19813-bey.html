Sidi Mohammed in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860 · the work’s page · read it where Sidi Mohammed first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sidi Mohammed, Bey of the Camp, leads an expedition from the Bardo toward the Jereed, commanding its forces while honoring shrines, hunting, greeting divisions, receiving officers, and kindly hosting tourists; he dines alone, does not smoke tobacco, and is succeeded by Muley Yezeed.

CHAPTER IX.

Sidi Mohammed, Bey of the Camp, led the expedition from the Bardo across the plain of Manouba toward the Jereed. He regularly paid respects at saints' shrines, hunted daily, saluted the various divisions of the march, received local officers, and treated the tourists with familiarity and kindness.

Bey of Tunis in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860 · the work’s page · read it where Bey of Tunis first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Bey of Tunis is a Muslim ruler who supports the suppression of slavery through engagements with Christians. He exercises broad authority: administering justice morning and evening at Kairwan, directing plaintiffs, seizing camels when needed, ordering punishment, and controlling Arabs' water management for the camp. He possesses and receives many camels, including compensation for tribal manslaughter, and remits duty for a Sicilian nobleman's exported camels. During travel to Kairwan and onward toward Gilma and Ghafsa, he inspects arriving Arabs, exchanges gifts of horses and other presents, replaces a criticized grey horse, hunts and hawks, orders water released from Wady Tuckah, and keeps fifty Turks guarding the ruined Kasbah at Ghafsa.

Bey of the Camp in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860 · the work’s page · read it where Bey of the Camp first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Bey of the Camp is the heir-apparent to the Tunisian throne and sovereign in the Jereed during his annual flying-camp. Accompanied by officers, ministers, and mamelukes, he visits El-Jereed to administer affairs, collect tribute, and hold justice morning and evening from a palace at Toser. He receives gifts, shoots at targets, decorates horses, and punishes or imprisons people in disputes involving tribute and alleged theft. His exactions on tribes may have contributed to an attack on couriers, though tourists describe him as hospitable and kind. He owns Momakeeah and surrounding land. When the reigning Bey dies, he takes the hereditary beylick and nominates a successor.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VIII.

The Bey of the Camp visits El-Jereed each year with a flying camp to administer affairs and collect tribute. He is described as sovereign in the country and as heir-apparent to the Tunisian throne. On the death of the reigning Bey, he occupies the hereditary beylick and nominates his successor, generally according to seniority within the royal family.

CHAPTER X.

The Bey leaves his tent to occupy a palace at Toser and administers justice morning and evening while in the Jereed. He receives gifts, including a young ostrich, shoots at targets, decorates horses with henna and markings, and punishes or imprisons people over tribute and alleged theft.

On our march back to Tunis, we encamped for two days by the foot of a range of h

The Bey of the Camp’s exactions on the tribes were suggested as a possible reason for Arabs attacking two couriers. He ordered the party to continue marching after reaching Sidi-Mahammedeah, and he owned Momakeeah and much of the surrounding land. The tourists praised his hospitality and kindness, while the tribute collected during the expedition was associated with his camp.
