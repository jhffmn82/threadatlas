Islam

Also called: Islamism, Mahometan faith, Mahometan religion, Mussulmen, faith of Islam, orthodox religion

Abstract written by a model

Islam is presented as permitting slavery and the slave trade through the Koran and traditions, while barring sale or transfer of a Muslim, the sacred misshaf, or a young person to an unbeliever. It is credited with unifying North Africa through religious homogeneity and removing notions of human sacrifice. The Shereefian sultans claim especially strong leadership through descent from Ali and Fatima. In Morocco, Islam binds diverse populations and extends the Sultan’s influence, while non-Muslim enemies can unite hostile tribes.

Islam in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860 · the work’s page · read it where Islam first appears

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

The Shereefian sultans are portrayed as religious leaders whose claim to head Islam is stronger than that of the Ottoman sultans, according to the text. The Shereefs’ descent from Ali and Fatima is said to give them exceptional authority among Mussulmen.

[5] In the Shereefian lineage of Muley Suleiman, copied for Ali Bey by the Emper

The Shereefian correspondence presents Islam as authorizing slavery and the slave trade through the Koran and traditions of the Prophet. It also states that a Muslim, the sacred misshaf, and a young person may not be sold or given to an unbeliever.

CHAPTER III.

Islam provides the common religion that helps hold Morocco’s diverse populations together. The Sultan’s religious position allows him to claim influence across Western Barbary and Central Africa wherever Muslims are found, and a Christian or other non-Muslim enemy could unite otherwise hostile tribes.
