water management in the desert in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

Water management in the desert is controlled by the Bey through orders to district Arabs. At Wady Tuckah, water is released from the hills and directed to camp through tanks, cisterns, ducts, pools, streams, and springs. Though abundant enough to irrigate plains and support palms and olives, it is neglected.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Marched to a place called Gilma, near which the road passes through an ancient t

Water was directed to the camp at Wady Tuckah when the Bey ordered local Arabs to release it from the hills. The travelers used ancient and modern tanks, cisterns, stone ducts, pools, streams, and springs, including hot springs near Ghafsa and Dra-el-Hammah. The account argues that the abundant water could irrigate the neighboring plains and support thousands of palms and olive trees, but that people neglect this resource.
