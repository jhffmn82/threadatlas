Tafilett in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

Tafilett is a group of fortified towns and villages on the south-eastern side of the Atlas, always united to Morocco. Anciently called Sedjelmasa until 1530, it is the country and birthplace of the Shereefs, with places inhabited entirely by them and an area especially appropriated to the royal and Imperial family. The present Shereefian branch extended authority from Tafilett over the provinces of Morocco. It contains Ghourlan, is a date-country, and serves as a major market and Sahara caravan rendezvous. Its conjectural population is ten thousand; it also receives exiled troublesome Imperial-family members and is reached by the road from Aghmat.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

Tafilett is identified as the ancient country of the Sherfah, or Shereefs, and as a place especially appropriated for members of the royal family. The present Shereefian branch extended its authority from Tafilett over the provinces of Morocco.

CHAPTER V.

Tafilett consists of fortified towns and villages on the south-eastern side of the Atlas and is inhabited partly by the Imperial family. It is a major caravan rendezvous and market for products from the surrounding countries and the Sahara. The area is also used as a place of exile for troublesome members of the Imperial family.

CHAPTER VI.

Tafilett is identified as a southern destination reached by the road from Aghmat. The unit includes it among the distinguished places of Morocco but provides little further description.
