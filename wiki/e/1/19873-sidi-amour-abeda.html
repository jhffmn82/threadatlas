Sidi Amour Abeda in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

Sidi Amour Abeda is a Moorish saint treated as a man of exceeding sanctity. On a ride to Kairwan, he accompanies R.'s party to the Kaed's house, blesses sweetmeats, receives reverent hand-kissing, and is promised a burial house by order of the Bey.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Rode to Kairwan.

Sidi Amour Abeda was treated by Si Smyle, the Bash-Boab, and others as a man of exceeding sanctity. He accompanied R.'s party to the Kaed's house, blessed the sweetmeats while mumbling bismillas, and was to have a house built for the future deposit of his remains. The renegade doctor of the Bey said that he wished to see English infidels burnt alive.
