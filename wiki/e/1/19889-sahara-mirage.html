Abstract written by a model

Sahara mirage is the repeated appearance of water on salt-covered or sandy surfaces, causing travelers to see shores, bays, and islands before recognizing a salt plain with sand heaps, not a lake.

CHAPTER X.

The travelers repeatedly mistake the Sahara’s salt-covered or sandy surface for water, seeing apparent shores, inlets, bays, and islands. At Nefta and on the return from Toser, the seemingly liquid expanse is identified as the Palus Libya or a salt plain with sand heaps rather than an actual lake.
