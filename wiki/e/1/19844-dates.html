Abstract written by a model

Dates are the principal food and resource of El-Jereed, sustaining twenty thousand people in Jereed for six months each year. Harvested in considerable quantities, though fine kinds are not abundant, they are stored in suspended bunches, pressed into earthen jars for syrup, or dried and ground into durable meal for desert travellers. Large arrivals include sweet transparent Degalah, plum-like Trungah, and larger, drier, less-sweet Monachah. Rain harms the fruit, while the trees’ roots need abundant water.

CHAPTER VIII.

Dates are the principal food and resource of El-Jereed. They are stored in bunches suspended from poles and pressed into large earthen jars, where their juice collects as a thick syrup. They may also be dried and ground into meal that keeps indefinitely and serves as a valuable food for desert travellers.

The Sheikh Tahib's gardens were sold for 15,000 piastres, his wife also added to

Dates were coming in in large quantities and included several kinds, especially Degalah, Trungah, and Monachah. Degalah was described as very sweet and almost transparent; Trungah as plum-shaped and plum-like in taste; and Monachah as larger, drier, mealier, and less sweet. Rain was said to be bad for dates, although the roots of the date tree could not have too much water.
