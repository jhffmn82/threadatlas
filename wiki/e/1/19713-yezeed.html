Yezeed in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

Yezeed, son of Sidi Mohammed and a hereditary prince in the Shereefian line, was proclaimed Sultan at Salee three days after his father’s death. Partly descended through a renegade Irish maternal line, he was recognized by European consuls as a son of the Shereef. The records portray him as an exceptionally cruel tyrant whose principle was carnage, whose violent conduct and practical jokes helped cause civil war, and who made war on nearly the whole world except the English. After roughly 22 months of rule, he was mortally wounded by a poisoned arrow and died in February 1792. Though notorious for cruelty, he was buried with great solemnities at Kobah Sherfah; Muley Hisham succeeded him.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

[5] In the Shereefian lineage of Muley Suleiman, copied for Ali Bey by the Emper

Yezeed was the hereditary prince and succeeded his father, Sidi Mohammed, three days after his death in April 1790 or 1789. He is portrayed as a tyrant whose cruelties caused civil war; he was mortally wounded by a poisoned arrow and died in February 1792 during the 22nd month of his reign. Despite his reputation, he was buried with great solemnities at the mosque of Kobah Sherfah because he was a Shereef.

notes

Yezeed is described as the son of Sidi-Mohammed and the renegade Irish widow of a sergeant of Sappers and Miners. He is portrayed as a ferocious and cruel tyrant who conducted violent practical jokes, made war widely except on the English, and nevertheless received obeisance from European consuls.
