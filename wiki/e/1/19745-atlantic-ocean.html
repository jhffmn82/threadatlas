Atlantic Ocean in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

The Atlantic Ocean borders Morocco to the west and receives the country’s northwestern rivers. The Atlantic coast is characterized mainly by ridges of hills, and its ports offer poor protection from the ocean. The Atlantic Ocean borders the Moroccan towns described in this unit, including Arzila, El Araish, and the coastal settlements farther south. Its shores and roadsteads provide ports and anchorages, although many entrances are obstructed by sandbars, rocks, or shallow water.

the Atlantic Ocean in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

The Atlantic Ocean is a wide, illimitable ocean lying between America with no intervening land. Along the Cornish coast, it sends rollers and heavy ground-swells before storms, making boating and landing dangerous; yet on a divine day it reaches bathers in gentle ripples and invites swimming as far as prudence permits.

"Shall we get a boat?

The Atlantic Ocean sends large rollers and ground-swells along the Cornish coast, even when the wind is calm. Its waves make boating and landing dangerous and are associated with storms arriving from across the ocean.

DAY THE SIXTH And a day absolutely divine!

The Atlantic rolls to the feet of the bathers in gentle baby ripples, despite its immense distance and association with great ships going down. It tempts the narrator's two little mermaids to swim as far out as prudence permits.
