Abstract written by a model

Ceuta, or Sebta, is an ancient city and fortress, formerly regarded as the capital of Mauritania Tingitana and identified with Esilissa of Ptolemy. It occupies a peninsula near Gibraltar, includes Monte-del-Acho and Gibel Zaterit, and has a small insecure port. Portugal conquered it in 1415; it later became a Spanish presidio, retained by Spain after Portugal recovered independence in 1640, with a garrison and free population.

CHAPTER IV.

Ceuta, or Sebta, is an ancient city and fortress identified with Esilissa of Ptolemy and formerly regarded as the capital of Mauritania Tingitana. Portugal conquered it in 1415, and Spain retained it after Portugal recovered its independence in 1640. It contains a Spanish garrison of roughly 2,000 to 3,000 men, a free population of about 5,000 to 6,000, and a small insecure port.
