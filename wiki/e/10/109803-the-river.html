Abstract written by a model

The river is a waterway flowing irresistibly downstream toward the sea, at once dark, placid, mysterious, and at times broad, golden, painfully bright, and almost solid beneath savage sunlight. It is a route, a boundary, and a field of danger. At first it offers people cut off by land a possible way to reach the nunnery, while cries and activity rise along its bank. It carries jars, a funeral barge, pursuing and hostile boats, drifting fishing-boats, smoke, and the Hakka boat; Chok Chung is shot in its shallows. Though it can seem peaceful and safe, its landing is watched by concealed Chinese forces. The Hakka boat exploits the opposite shore, a crossing above, and the ebb to escape into midstream. As evening changes the water from copper to iron-gray, the boat drifts downstream through the night, until the river gives way to the sea.

The river appears broad, golden, painfully bright, and almost solid under the savage sunlight. It carries jars, a funeral barge, and the pursuing boat, while reflections move in the banyan's shade and Chok Chung is shot in its shallows.

CHAPTER XVI THE GUNWALE Rudolph's mission began quietly, with a glimpse which he afterward recalled as incredibly peaceful.

The river lies dark, placid, and seemingly safe beyond the burning bank. Chantel's boat departs along it while Rudolph remains ashore, and Wutzler later throws an empty bottle into the river.

Across the river, the Hakka boat waits between other vessels with a green flag and a rice-scoop displayed at its mast. The river landing is watched and covered by Chinese forces concealed in the marsh.

The river carries hostile boats, drifting fishing-boats, smoke, and the Hakka boat. The Hakka boat uses the opposite shore, a crossing above, and the ebb to approach unseen before carrying the fugitives into midstream.

The river changes from bright copper to vague iron-gray as evening falls, and the Hakka boat drifts downstream along its darkening banks. Shots are heard from inland, and the passengers continue through the night until the river gives way to the sea.
