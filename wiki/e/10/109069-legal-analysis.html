Legal analysis is a written legal work and central product of lawyers’ work. It uses preceding case context, factual findings, relevant case law, and citations to explain and support legal reasoning. CLERC is intended to retrieve supporting cases, find corresponding citations, compile cited text and context, and generate cogent analyses. Although models generate these analyses and are evaluated with citation retrieval metrics, those metrics do not ensure factual or analytical soundness.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Abstract

Legal analysis relies on citations to relevant precedents. CLERC supports finding corresponding citations for a given piece of legal analysis and compiling cited text and previous context into a cogent analysis.

Introduction

Legal analyses are a central product of lawyers' work and rely on relevant case law to support legal reasoning. CLERC is intended to help retrieve supporting cases and generate legal analyses.

Related Work .. 2.3

Long-form legal analysis is described as a core task for lawyers that requires substantial time and effort. The benchmark evaluates its generation together with citation retrieval.

3 / 3.1 .. Input Preparation

Legal analysis is represented by paragraphs that contain at least two case citations and are sampled from the later portion of case documents. The preparation process uses preceding case context and relevant external case passages to support the analysis.

4 .. 4.2.2

Legal analysis appears in later portions of a case document and explains legal reasoning using factual findings and citations. Models are asked to generate such analysis, but the reported citation metrics do not guarantee that the reasoning is factual or analytically sound.
