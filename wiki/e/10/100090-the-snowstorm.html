the snowstorm in From Sand Hill to Pine

Bret Harte · 1894-04-24

The snowstorm begins with fine, dry pellets driven by keen wind, becomes enormous wet flakes, buries and stops the coach, then leaves a crust and hidden depths that cause Mr. Masterton's horse to fall.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

A few hours later he was on the coach.

The snowstorm changes from fine, gritty pellets driven by a keen wind into enormous flakes that fall like an avalanche. It blankets the wagon, horses, passengers, and road, stops the vehicle, and later creates a crust and hidden depths that cause Mr. Masterton's horse to fall.
