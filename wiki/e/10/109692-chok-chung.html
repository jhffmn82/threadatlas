Chok Chung in Dragon's Blood

Henry Milner Rideout · 1909

Chok Chung is an extremely honest, mild Christian convert and merchant who owns a Chinese merchant’s hemp. In town, Au-yoeng kills him in his shop, alarming Dr. Earle because Chok Chung is one of his converts. In another account of his persecution, he is tried and questioned for following the Lord Jesus; after affirming his faith, his teeth are beaten out and he is thrown into prison, wearing soaked prison rags. Later he swims desperately toward a house while an armed boat pursues him, crying, “I am Jesus Christ!” He is shot in the shallows, sinks, and dies.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER V IN TOWN Night, in that maze of alleys, was but a more sinister day.

Chok Chung is identified as a Christian merchant who was extremely honest and was killed in his shop by Au-yoeng. Dr. Earle is troubled that the killing of one of his converts may lead to wider trouble.

" CHAPTER X THREE PORTALS Not till after dinner, that evening, did Rudolph rouse from his stupor.

Chok Chung, described as a Christian merchant, is questioned during his trial about following the foreign dogs and goats. After answering that he follows the Lord Jesus, his teeth are beaten out with a heavy shoe and he is thrown into prison.

" CHAPTER XIII THE SPARE MAN Beyond the scant greenery of Heywood's garden--a ropy little banyan, a low rank of glossy whampee leaves, and the dusty sage-green tops of stunted olives--glared the river.

Chok Chung, identified as the Christian, swims desperately toward the house while pursued by an armed boat and cries, "I am Jesus Christ!" He is shot as he reaches the shallows and sinks, becoming identified with the earlier drunken cormorant-fisher.
