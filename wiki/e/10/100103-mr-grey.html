Mr. Grey in From Sand Hill to Pine

Bret Harte · 1894-04-24

Mr. Grey, also called Don Pancho, is editor of the Mountain Clarion, working from its editorial office and opposing anti-Mexican prejudice personally and in print. He protects Colonel Starbottle’s secret and, after an accident and an assault involving Richards and Colonel Starbottle, questions Starbottle, resists private handling, and investigates the Ramierez family and the fonda. At the fonda, Cota challenges him with a mustang; his skilled riding and sharp American spurs keep him from being thrown. Later, concerned by Richards’s love for Cota, he hears Enriquez Saltillo’s lasso legend but abandons it as newspaper material when Richards identifies the assailant.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

WHAT HAPPENED AT THE FONDA PART I "Well!

Mr. Grey is the editor of the Mountain Clarion. He questions Colonel Starbottle about the accident, discourages Richards from handling the matter privately, and considers investigating the Ramierez family and the girl seen at the fonda.

PART II The next afternoon he went to the fonda.

Mr. Grey visits the fonda to investigate the assault on Richards and Colonel Starbottle. He meets Cota, accepts her challenge involving the mustang, and uses his riding skill and spurs to stop the animal from throwing him. He later escapes Cota's playful but unsettling behavior and returns home without useful information.

He was still more concerned a week later, on returning unexpectedly one afternoo

Mr. Grey discovers that Richards has developed a sincere passion for Cota Ramierez and becomes concerned about her apparent flirtation with him. He hears Enriquez Saltillo's lasso legend and plans to use it as newspaper material, but abandons the idea after Richards reveals the true identity of the assailant.
