Mrs. Forrester in Dragon's Blood

Henry Milner Rideout · 1909

Mrs. Forrester is a married Englishwoman who identifies herself as more French than English, wife of Gilbert Forrester, and an actress. Traveling alone in second class directly to Hongkong, she befriends Rudolph and encourages his conversation and romantic excitement, though he learns she is married when the ship’s doctor uses her title. She is distressed by Monsieur Jolivet’s death, rushes sobbing into the club for help, collapses into the protection of the dark-eyed girl, and remains shaken as singing resumes. Later, at a tiffin gathering at an ancient pagoda, she is lively and central to the audience: she tells stories, plays the Chinese lute, and sings several songs. She invites Rudolph up the tower, says she has thought of him, and touches his arm sympathetically before Maurice Heywood interrupts. On a morning ride with Rudolph, she challenges him to learn what is on the island and laughs at his pony in the mud. But when he returns with an abandoned infant girl, the child’s filth and suffering shock and disgust her. Saying she cannot bear suffering, she refuses to enter the hospital and leaves before Rudolph emerges.

Bertha Forrester in Dragon's Blood

Henry Milner Rideout · 1909

Bertha Forrester is a woman and Gilly Forrester’s wife, known to the men at the club. Gilly financially supports her travels to Japan, Kashmir, Ceylon, and Home. She is initially shaped by Dr. Chantel’s account of Rudolph Hackh: she resents Rudolph’s escort and accuses him of being like other men. Once Rudolph explains the confrontation, she admires his courage and honesty, likes that he is brave and different, and suggests she could be a better friend. Before an alarm, Bertha panics at her house, ransacks her treasure and belongings to decide what to save, clings to Rudolph, and urges him to leave with her by boat. When he refuses, Chantel pulls her aboard. She leaves the nunnery by boat rather than go to the besieged nunnery; Rudolph reports this choice to Gilly. During the attack, she is out of her wits.
