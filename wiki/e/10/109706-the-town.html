the town in Dragon's Blood

Henry Milner Rideout · 1909

The Chinese town is a hostile, crowded maze of alleys, galleries, dark corridors, and quarters, including a Jesus Religion Chapel, a rival lodge building, the dangerous West Quarter, and a gate left dark and open at nightfall. By night it remains active with labor, commerce, noise, smells, dim lanterns, rumors of plague, fear of supernatural forces, and dangerous unrest among its dregs. Heywood and Wutzler pass through it and escape by an unlighted gate; later Miss Drake and Heywood avoid its unfriendly streets, skirt its walls through an outlying hamlet, and reach the open road.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER V IN TOWN Night, in that maze of alleys, was but a more sinister day.

The Chinese town is portrayed as a maze of alleys and crowded galleries that remain active through the night, filled with labor, commerce, noise, smells, and dim lantern light. Its residents face social pressure, hostility, rumors of plague, and fear of supernatural forces.

CHAPTER XI WHITE LOTUS He was swarming up, quiet as a thief, when his fingers clawed the bare plaster.

Heywood and Wutzler move through the town's dark corridors, alleys, and galleries before escaping through an unlighted gate.

" CHAPTER XIV OFF DUTY The last of the sunlight streamed level through a gap in the western ridges.

Miss Drake and Heywood avoid the town's unfriendly streets and skirt it by the walls and through an outlying hamlet. Its twilight scenes include grotesque sights, a dentist's booth, shadow puppets, and a den marked 'Foreign Earth' and 'As You Like It'; they later reach the open road beyond it.
