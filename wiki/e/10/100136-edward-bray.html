Edward Bray

Abstract written by a model

Edward Bray is a poor, tall, mountain-bred young miner of the Eureka Mining Company, one of five camp partners and a partner in a mining claim. Level-headed, principled, honest-humored, and contemptuous of moneyed trading classes, he is skilled at carrying water without spilling it. When he volunteers to fetch water, Eugenia falls onto him at the spring; after being knocked unconscious, he rescues her from a ledge and guides her to the stage road. His attention to her makes him delay the water and the capitalist Neworth, for which he feels responsible, though he insists he is not in love. He returns to the spring hoping to see Eugenia and receives her note after Parkhurst finds gold there. He then goes to San Francisco to seek Eugenia, returns her note with an explanation, speaks with her in the Neworths' garden, and later visits properly and repeatedly. He purchases machinery for a new claim and has a new, fashionable outfit; ultimately, after leaving his old partners, he enters a new partnership with Eugenia.

'" A JACK AND JILL OF THE SIERRAS It was four o'clock in the afternoon, and the hottest hour of the day on that Sierran foothill.

Ned Bray volunteers to fetch water for the Eureka Mining Company when the tin pail is empty. At the spring, he is knocked unconscious when Eugenia falls onto him, then rescues her from the ledge and escorts her along the rocky trail to the stage road.

And with it came the dreadful reflection that as yet he had not carried the wate

Bray realizes that he has neglected to carry water to his expecting and thirsty comrades. He hurries to the spring, lingers while thinking about the girl, fills the pail, and returns up the trail tired but insisting that he is not in love.

Edward Bray feels responsible for ruining the capitalist's visit because his delay and dalliance with Eugenia held up Neworth. He returns to the spring hoping to see Eugenia once more, then becomes deeply agitated when Parkhurst discovers gold there and secretly gives him Eugenia's note.

That night Bray slept but little.

Edmund Bray goes to San Francisco intending to seek Eugenia Neworth and thank her for her note. After Eugenia initially passes him coldly, he sends her note back with an explanation, then climbs into the Neworths' garden to speak with her. He later calls properly at the Neworth residence, visits repeatedly, and eventually enters a new partnership with Eugenia.
