I.

This unit examines the use of large language models (LLMs) and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for Quranic studies, especially the retrieval of reliable and contextually relevant information. It compares 13 open-source LLMs using a dataset about the 114 surahs of the Qur’an rather than the Qur’an text itself. The study evaluates context relevance, response faithfulness, and answer relevance through human evaluation, with the goal of reducing hallucination and improving transparency through citations. It also considers the trade-offs among model size, performance, and computational efficiency in religious and culturally sensitive applications.

II.

The research study evaluates large language models in answering questions about Qur’anic studies using a Retrieval-Augmented Generation architecture and a dataset of descriptive information about 20 of the Qur’an’s 114 surahs. The study retrieves semantically relevant surah descriptions, generates responses with citations, and evaluates them for Context Relevance, Answer Faithfulness, and Answer Relevance. Human evaluators score the responses on a five-point Likert scale, while Fleiss’ Kappa measures Inter-Evaluator Agreement. The study compares Llama, Gemma, QwQ, and Phi models using specified hardware and software resources.

III.

The study evaluates 13 large language models (LLMs) answering questions related to Quranic studies through a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) architecture. Using a descriptive dataset of Quranic surahs, it compares the models on context relevance, answer faithfulness, and answer relevance. Llama3.2:3b performs exceptionally well across the evaluation metrics, while larger models generally demonstrate stronger performance than medium and small models. Intercoder agreement is assessed using Fleiss’ Kappa, with values ranging from 0.80 to 0.93 across the evaluated models.

IV.

The unit analyzes how large, medium, and small language models perform in a Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework for responses about Qur’anic studies. Large models generally achieve the strongest context relevance, answer faithfulness, and answer relevance, while medium models provide a balance between response quality and computational demands. Small models usually perform less well, although Llama3.2:3b achieves unexpectedly strong results, particularly in answer faithfulness and answer relevance. The findings indicate that the Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework and a descriptive Qur’anic dataset can improve response quality and reduce hallucinations, while model selection depends on accuracy, speed, and available computational resources.

V.

The study evaluated multiple large language models of different sizes on Quranic studies-related queries using a Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework. Llama3:70b, Llama3.1:70b, and Gemma2:27b achieved superior performance in context relevance, answer faithfulness, and answer relevance, but required substantial computational resources. Gemma2:9b and Llama3:8b balanced efficiency and performance, while Llama3.2:3b performed comparably to larger models in some aspects. The study found that the Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework improved response quality by grounding answers in external domain-specific knowledge and reducing hallucinations.
