Diomedes

person · 1 work · 10 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Διομήδην, Διομήδης

Abstract written by a model

Diomedes is described as both the son of Tydeus and Deipyle and a Thracian king, son of Ares and Cyrene, who rules the war-like Bistones and owns man-eating mares. He secretly comes from Argos, kills nearly all Agrius’s sons, gives Oeneus’s kingdom to Andraemon, and buries Oeneus at Oenoe. After the mares’ capture, Hercules kills him.

Diomedes is in one work on this site, The Library. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Diomedes was the son of Tydeus and Deipyle. He came secretly from Argos with Alcmaeon, killed nearly all the sons of Agrius, gave the kingdom to Andraemon because Oeneus was old, and took Oeneus to the Peloponnese. After Oeneus was killed, Diomedes carried his body to Argos and buried it at Oenoe.

THE LIBRARY, II. v. 4-5

Diomedes is a Thracian king, son of Ares and Cyrene, and ruler of the Bistones. He owns man-eating mares, and Hercules kills him during the fighting that follows the mares' capture.
