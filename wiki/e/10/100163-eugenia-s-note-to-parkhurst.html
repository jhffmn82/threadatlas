Eugenia's note to Parkhurst in From Sand Hill to Pine

Bret Harte · 1894-04-24

Eugenia Neworth’s kind note, addressed to Parkhurst as “Jack” and marked with a sprig of laurel, honors his “honest, manly calling” and predicts that Edmund Bray will strike it rich. It asks that he remember “Jill,” which Parkhurst mistakes for whiskey but Bray recognizes as Eugenia’s remembrance of him. Left at the spring for Bray, it is missed while he searches elsewhere; he later returns it to Eugenia with thanks and an explanation.

Eugenia's note is addressed on the back to "Jack" and says, "I want you to believe that I, at least, respect and honor your honest, manly calling, and when you strike it rich, as you surely will, I hope you will sometimes think of Jill." Parkhurst mistakes "Jill" for a reference to a gill of whiskey, while Bray recognizes it as Eugenia's remembrance of him and uses the note to explain the quartz discovery.

That night Bray slept but little.

Eugenia's note was intended for Bray and was left at the spring by a schoolboy instructed to wait until Bray arrived. Bray did not see it because he was searching elsewhere, and he later returns it to Eugenia with thanks and an explanation for his delayed acknowledgment.
