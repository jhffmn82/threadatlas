Related Work

The two-stage fine-tuning framework in SimRAG first fine-tunes a model on retrieval-related data. It then performs domain-adaptive fine-tuning with self-training using pseudo-labeled examples filtered by round-trip consistency.

SimRAG in SimRAG: Self-Improving Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Adapting Large Language Models to Specialized Domains

Ran Xu, Hui Liu, Sreyashi Nag, Zhenwei Dai, Yaochen Xie, Xianfeng Tang, Chen Luo, Yang Li, Joyce C. Ho, Carl Yang, Qi He · 2025

SimRAG is a self-improving retrieval-augmented generation and self-training approach for adapting large language models to specialized-domain question answering using unlabeled domain corpora. Its central idea is to jointly equip a model for question answering with context and question generation from context, then use the same model to create, filter, and learn from domain-specific synthetic data. The framework has two stages. In Stage-I, it applies retrieval-oriented fine-tuning on general-domain instruction-following, question-answering, and search-related data. In Stage-II, the Stage-I model extracts candidate answers from domain corpora, generates candidate questions and pseudo-labeled tuples, filters them through round-trip consistency to retain high-quality examples, and continuously fine-tunes on the resulting QA pairs for progressive self-improvement. Question filtering removes low-quality data and accelerates training; short-span question answering is among the generated question types. SimRAG supports multiple question-answering formats and is robust to DRAGON as an alternative retriever. It was evaluated on 11 datasets across biomedical, natural/social sciences, and computer science domains, with Llama3-8B-Instruct and Gemma2-27B-Instruct backbones. It consistently outperforms domain-specific LLMs, general-domain retrieval-augmented LMs, Llama3-8B-Instruct, and Gemma2-27B-Instruct in these domains, with reported gains of 1.2%–8.6%; using Gemma2-27B, it approaches GPT-4. Stage-II contributes materially: removing it lowers reported medical and scientific averages. SimRAG also has limitations involving pseudo-label refinement, added training time, and the choice of query generation models. The work was done during an internship at Amazon.

SimRAG: Self-Improving Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Adapting Large Language Models to Specialized Domains

SimRAG concerns adapting large language models to specialized domains through self-improving retrieval-augmented generation.

Abstract

SimRAG is a self-training approach for adapting large language models to specialized domains. It generates diverse questions from unlabeled corpora, filters the synthetic examples for quality, and uses them to improve performance on domain-specific retrieval-augmented generation tasks.

Introduction

SimRAG is a self-improving retrieval-augmented generation approach for specialized-domain question answering. It jointly trains question answering and question generation, creates and filters synthetic QA pairs from domain corpora, and continuously fine-tunes the model on those examples.

Related Work

SimRAG is presented as a method for adapting LLMs to domain-specific question answering and retrieval-augmented generation. It first applies retrieval-oriented fine-tuning, then generates and filters pseudo-labeled tuples through domain-adaptive fine-tuning with self-training, using the same LLM for question generation and answering.

Experimental Setup / Tasks and Datasets .. Implementation Details

SimRAG is the proposed method evaluated with Llama3-8B-Instruct and Gemma2-27B-Instruct backbones. SimRAG 8B reaches medical and scientific averages of 66.04 and 76.96, and SimRAG 27B reaches 65.17 and 81.64; removing Stage-II training reduces these averages to 64.30 and 75.44 for the 8B model and 62.33 and 79.08 for the 27B model.

Experimental Results / Main Results .. Case Studies

SimRAG consistently outperforms baseline models across the medical, scientific, and computer science domains and across multiple question-answering formats. It uses Stage-I retrieval-oriented fine-tuning and Stage-II fine-tuning on self-synthesized training tuples, with average gains over Llama variants of 8.01%, 6.37%, and 8.61% in the three domains, respectively. Its performance is robust to the choice of retriever, and its generated pseudo-labeled data is more relevant, self-contained, and challenging than data generated by the backbone model.

Limitation

SimRAG demonstrates notable improvements but has limitations involving pseudo-label refinement, additional training time, and the choice of query generation models.
