Gilbert Forrester in Dragon's Blood

Henry Milner Rideout · 1909

Gilbert Forrester, a senior member of the settlement and one of the white men, is a dinner guest and defender of the nunnery. During the war board, he proposes defending his house but admits his plans are vague and yields leadership to Heywood. He later survives gunfire, refuses to leave the water gate, and finally boards the Hakka boat.

" CHAPTER XII THE WAR BOARD "Rigmarole?

Gilbert Forrester initially argues that, as the senior man present except Dr. Earle, his house should be chosen for defense. He then admits that his plans are vague and resigns as acting head in favor of Heywood.

CHAPTER XX THE HAKKA BOAT "Pretty fair," Captain Kneebone said.

Gilbert Forrester survives the assaults with two bullets through his loose jacket and responds with a grave smile. He refuses to leave his post at the water gate, arguing that he is not sleepy, but later boards the Hakka boat with the others.

Gilly in Dragon's Blood

Henry Milner Rideout · 1909

Gilly is a European resident of the Stink-Chau colony, described by Heywood as its only gentleman. Married to Mrs. Gilly and identified as Bertha Forrester’s husband, he is a club member who welcomes Rudolph and discusses plague preparations, including fumigation, while wishing his wife were not returning. After her journey, he says she is resting and, despite initial embarrassment, joins the club concert by singing a French song about Tonkin. He is also linked to the hunting operation as a speaker of obvious conclusions. During the siege, Gilly holds the water-gate post, learns that Bertha Forrester has departed by boat, returns armed with Kempner’s rifle and belt, and volunteers for the first difficult position, including hazardous underground work.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II THE PIED PIPER A gray smudge trailing northward showed where the Fa-Hien--Scottish Oriental, sixteen hundred tons--was disappearing from the pale expanse of ocean.

Gilly is one of the European residents at Stink-Chau and is described by Heywood as the only gentleman among them. At the club, he welcomes Rudolph and discusses preparations for the plague, including possible fumigation. He says he wishes his wife were not coming back.

" CHAPTER III UNDER FIRE The desert was sometimes Gobi, sometimes Sahara, but always an infinite stretch of sand that floated up and up in a stifling layer, like the tide.

Gilly arrives at the club alone and explains that his wife is resting after her journey. He is drawn into the concert and begins singing, though he is embarrassed at first.

CHAPTER XVIII SIEGE He never believed that they could hold the northeast corner for a minute, so loud and unceasing was the uproar.

Gilly Forrester asks where his wife is and learns from Rudolph that Bertha Forrester has gone by boat. He returns to his post at the water gate with Kempner's rifle and belt and offers to take the first chance at any difficult position.

Sturgeon in Dragon's Blood

Henry Milner Rideout · 1909

Sturgeon, a battered convivialist, serves as a reluctant, sympathetic second in a duel, brings swords, tends Rudolph, and joins the riot response.

" CHAPTER IX PASSAGE AT ARMS "Boy.
