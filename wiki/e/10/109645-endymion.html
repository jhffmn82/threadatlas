Endymion

Abstract written by a model

In The Library, Endymion led the Aeolians from Thessaly, settled Elis, was loved by Selene, and chose eternal sleep while remaining immortal and ageless. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Endymion is a beautiful youth and shepherd on Mount Latmos whose story symbolizes the young poet, poetic love, dreams, and an early death.

Endymion in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Endymion first appears

Abstract written by a model

Endymion is a beautiful youth and shepherd on Mount Latmos. Diana finds him sleeping, kisses him, watches over him, and protects and increases his sheep and lambs. In another version, Jupiter grants perpetual youth and sleep. His story symbolizes the young poet, poetic love, dreams, and an early death.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XIX

Endymion was a beautiful youth who fed his flock on Mount Latmos. Diana found him sleeping, kissed him, and watched over him; another story says that Jupiter granted him perpetual youth and perpetual sleep. His flock increased and was protected from wild beasts, while his story came to symbolize poetic love, dreams, and an early death.
