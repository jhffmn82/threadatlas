Sir James Saunders in The Case-Book of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1921-10

Sir James Saunders, a great dermatologist, provides a second medical opinion on Godfrey at Tuxbury Old Park, diagnosing non-infectious pseudo-leprosy or ichthyosis rather than leprosy, an unsightly but possibly curable condition.

Sir James Saunders accompanies Holmes to Tuxbury Old Park and waits in the carriage until called. He examines Godfrey's condition and announces that it is not leprosy but a well-marked case of pseudo-leprosy or ichthyosis. He states that the condition is unsightly and obstinate but possibly curable and certainly non-infective.
