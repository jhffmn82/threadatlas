the Portuguese nunnery

place · 1 work · 11 quoted facts · 12 connections
Also called: A Portuguese nunnery, Nunnery, Portuguese Nunnery, Portuguese nunnery, his vacant nunnery, my nunnery, nunnery, the bare, white loft, the compound, the living-room, the long, white room, the nunnery, the nunnery gate

Abstract written by a model

The Portuguese nunnery is a desolate, lime-coated house and missionary institution, and the home Rudolph Hackh moves into. Its quiet courtyards, servants, sounds, and views give him work, reflection, and a feeling of nearly recovering a former life. As anti-foreign agitation grows, red placards appear at its gate, and it is judged the best defensive position: it has limited exposed sides, a courtyard well, open surrounding ground, and an alarm bell. Heywood proposes fortifying it and sheltering native converts there. The nunnery hosts a dinner and Captain Kneebone's banquet before coming under attack; its occupants are said able to hold it while women are removed. During the uprising, Rudolph sees it through smoke and flames and thinks it is burning, though Wutzler says the shooting continues. Across a narrow lane from Rudolph's go-down, it receives stores he had ordered moved. It then becomes the defenders' besieged compound, with guns in the first bedroom, defensive positions, a counter-mine, Kempner's grave, and a river-facing water gate. After days of intermittent attack, bombardment, and repeated assaults, its walls are heavily pitted, but the small company is mostly unharmed and evacuates by the water gate.

" CHAPTER VI THE PAGODA He was spared that inconvenience.

Rudolph moves into the Portuguese nunnery, whose desolate lime-coated spaces easily contain his few belongings. Its servants, quiet courtyards, sounds, and views give him occupation, reflection, and a sense of having almost recovered a former existence.

" CHAPTER XII THE WAR BOARD "Rigmarole?

The nunnery is considered the best defensive position because it has only two and a fraction sides exposed, a courtyard well, surrounding open ground, and a bell. Heywood proposes fortifying it and using it to shelter the native converts.

" CHAPTER XIV OFF DUTY The last of the sunlight streamed level through a gap in the western ridges.

The nunnery appears as a low black building with orange-lit windows beyond the town. Lantern-bearers gather near its landward wall, where red placards are pasted around both gate pillars.

CHAPTER XV KAU FAI At the top of the nunnery stairs, Rudolph met them with awkward ceremony, and with that smiling air of encouragement which a nurse might use in trying cheerfully to deceive a sick man.

The nunnery hosts the dinner and Captain Kneebone's banquet. It comes under attack from outside, but Heywood says that its occupants can hold it while the captain gets the women out.

CHAPTER XVI THE GUNWALE Rudolph's mission began quietly, with a glimpse which he afterward recalled as incredibly peaceful.

The nunnery is threatened by the uprising and appears to be burning to Rudolph through the smoke and flames. Wutzler corrects him, saying that the shooting is still continuing.

CHAPTER XVII LAMP OF HEAVEN The white men peered from among the oil-jars, like two of the Forty Thieves.

The nunnery lies across a narrow lane from Rudolph's go-down. Rudolph had ordered necessary stores moved there earlier that afternoon.

CHAPTER XVIII SIEGE He never believed that they could hold the northeast corner for a minute, so loud and unceasing was the uproar.

The nunnery serves as the defenders' besieged stronghold. It contains the compound, the defensive positions, and Kempner's grave, and remains under intermittent attack for several days.

CHAPTER XX THE HAKKA BOAT "Pretty fair," Captain Kneebone said.

The nunnery withstands prolonged bombardment and repeated assaults. Its walls become heavily pitted, but the small company inside remains mostly unharmed until it evacuates by the water gate.
