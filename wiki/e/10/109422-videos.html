videos in VideoRAG: Retrieval-Augmented Generation over Video Corpus

Soyeong Jeong · 2025

Videos are a rich, underutilized source of multimodal knowledge for RAG systems, representing contextual details through temporal dynamics, spatial details, and multimodal cues. As sequences of visual frames that may include subtitles, they can capture complex processes, context-dependent interactions, and non-verbal signals that static modalities may miss. Diverse, high-quality video data are available on YouTube, but videos can be long and redundant, making complete frame processing infeasible for LVLMs. VideoRAG addresses this by dynamically retrieving videos, selecting frames, and providing visual and textual information to a large video language model. This preserves multimodal richness more effectively than approaches that retrieve frames only from a preselected video or convert videos into text.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Abstract

Videos are presented as a rich source of multimodal knowledge that can represent contextual details more effectively than other modalities. VideoRAG retrieves them dynamically and uses their visual and textual information, while addressing the difficulty of processing all frames in extremely long videos.

Introduction

Videos are presented as an underutilized external knowledge source for RAG systems. They combine temporal dynamics, spatial details, and multimodal cues, enabling them to represent complex processes, context-dependent interactions, and non-verbal signals that static modalities may miss.

Method .. 2.4 / Auxiliary Text Generation

A video is represented as a sequence of visual frames and may include associated textual data such as subtitles. In VideoRAG, videos are retrieved, their frames are selected, and their visual and textual information is provided to a large video language model.

4 / Retrieval-Augmented Generation .. Large Video Language Models

Videos provide temporal dynamics, spatial details, and multimodal cues that are not matched by other modalities. Existing video-RAG studies either retrieve frames from a preselected video or convert videos into text, which can limit open-domain applicability or sacrifice multimodal richness.
