Jacky Ferguson in The Case-Book of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1921-10

Jacky Ferguson is the unnamed husband's fifteen-year-old son by his first wife, a pale, fair-haired, affectionate boy devoted to his father and his mother's memory. A childhood fall left him with a twisted, weak spine and shambling gait. Though the Peruvian wife repeatedly assaults him and says she hates him, his defining conflict is intense jealousy of Ferguson's baby. Holmes concludes that Jacky's cruel, possessive hatred led him to attempt to poison the child, and prescribes a year at sea.

THE ADVENTURE OF THE SUSSEX VAMPIRE

Jack is the unnamed husband's fifteen-year-old son from his first marriage, described as affectionate, devoted, and physically disabled from a childhood accident. The Peruvian wife has assaulted him twice, once with a stick and once with her hands, while repeatedly saying that she hates him.

It was evening of a dull, foggy November day when, having left our bags

Jacky Ferguson is Ferguson's pale, fair-haired older child, and he has a weak spine and a shambling gait. Holmes sees intense jealousy and cruel hatred on his face when Ferguson fondles the baby and concludes that Jacky attempted to poison the child out of distorted, possessive love. Holmes prescribes a year at sea for him.
