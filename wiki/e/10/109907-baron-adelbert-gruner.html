Baron Adelbert Gruner

person · 1 work · 34 quoted facts · 11 connections
Also called: Adelbert, Adelbert Gruner, Baron, Baron Gruner, him, my fiancé, the Austrian, the Austrian murderer, the Baron, the master of the house, the rascal, the victim, this devil, this man
  • In The Case-Book of Sherlock Holmes: Baron Adelbert Gruner is a wealthy Austrian man, forty-two, whose fortune came through shady speculations. He is remarkably handsome, with a swarthy, almost Oriental face, dark languorous eyes, raven-black hair and moustache, though his cruel, hard mouth is presented as a sign of murderous character. He is a horse fancier, a precise and tidy man, and a renowned collector and author on Chinese pottery; he owns an extensive Chinese collection. More importantly, he is portrayed as a highly dangerous antagonist: suspected of murdering his wife, connected with at least one or two further murders, and said to mistreat women by tempting, using, ruining, and discarding them. Kitty Winter says he collects ruined women as others collect moths or butterflies. Though he has a stormy past marked by bitter hatreds and accusations, he presents explanations that persuade Violet de Merville, who loves him and believes he has repented. Gruner resides at Vernon Lodge near Kingston, as well as being associated with 104 Berkeley Square, and protects his house with a burglar alarm and other precautions against Sherlock Holmes. He owns a locked brown leather book bearing his arms, written in his own hand, which records compromising romantic or sexual conduct. As he pursues and becomes engaged to Violet, intending an imminent marriage, he warns Holmes to withdraw and appears able to influence Violet against Holmes and his allies. The conflict turns when Gruner recognizes a visit by Dr. John Watson as a trap. Kitty Winter throws vitriol at him, severely burning his face, destroying one eye and badly damaging the other. While he is distracted, Holmes obtains the incriminating book despite Gruner's anti-burglary precautions. The planned marriage to Violet is canceled, and a journey to America had forced Holmes to act immediately.
