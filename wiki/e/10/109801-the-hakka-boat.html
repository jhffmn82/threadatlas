the Hakka boat in Dragon's Blood

Henry Milner Rideout · 1909

The Hakka boat is a shallow-draft river boat with a bamboo-thatched roof, manned by boatmen and used for escape. It is stocked and ready to take fugitives down the river and along the coast; Bertha boards it, while Rudolph refuses and is left behind when Chantel orders it to shove off. Across the river, it waits as a signal craft, displaying a green flag and a rice-measure at its masthead, with a coded promise to come if a fire is lit at the water gate. Hidden among masts, it seems to vanish, then secretly works upstream, crosses above the fugitives, and drifts down beneath the river-wall to take them aboard. It carries them toward midstream and downstream toward the coast, passes a war-junk, reaches the roadstead, survives an attack by armed men after the Pretty Lily drives them away, and is later followed by Heywood's fishing boat.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XVI THE GUNWALE Rudolph's mission began quietly, with a glimpse which he afterward recalled as incredibly peaceful.

Chantel has a boat ready and stocked for escape down the river and along the coast. Bertha is taken aboard, but Rudolph refuses to join them, and the boat leaves him behind after Chantel orders the boatman to shove off.

CHAPTER XVIII SIEGE He never believed that they could hold the northeast corner for a minute, so loud and unceasing was the uproar.

The Hakka boat waits across the river with a green rag and a rice-measure hanging from its mast. The coded message says it will come quickly if the defenders light a fire at the water gate, but Heywood sees that the route is covered by enemy forces.

CHAPTER XX THE HAKKA BOAT "Pretty fair," Captain Kneebone said.

The Hakka boat is initially hidden among the masts across the river and later appears to have vanished. It has secretly worked upstream, crossed above the fugitives, and drifted down close beneath the river-wall; it then takes the fugitives aboard and carries them toward midstream.

CHAPTER XXI THE DRAGON'S SHADOW The lowdah would have set his dirty sails without delay, for the fair wind was already drooping; but at the first motion he found himself deposed, and a usurper in command, at the big steering-paddle.

The Hakka boat travels down the darkening river, passes a war-junk without challenge, and reaches the roadstead. It is attacked by a smaller boat filled with armed men, but escapes after the Pretty Lily takes command of the other craft and drives it away. Later, the Hakka boat is followed by a fishing boat carrying Heywood.
