Miss Dunbar in The Case-Book of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1921-10

Miss Grace Dunbar is a young, beautiful governess employed under the millionaire's roof to educate his two children. Described as a tall brunette with a strong, sensitive face and innate nobility, she has a powerful beneficial influence over the Gold King and opposes wealth built on the ruin of thousands. Her position collapses into a murder accusation when Mrs. Gibson is shot. A note signed by Miss Dunbar arranged a meeting at Thor Bridge, she was seen near the bridge and admits writing the note and being there, and a revolver matching the fatal bullet is found in her wardrobe. She initially reserves her defence on her lawyer's advice. She later tells Holmes that Mrs. Gibson hated her, secretly summoned her to Thor Bridge, and angrily confronted her there. Miss Dunbar denies knowing of the pistol, while the millionaire insists she is innocent and Holmes suspects it was planted to incriminate her. Holmes works to vindicate her from the charge.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

THE PROBLEM OF THOR BRIDGE

Miss Dunbar, an attractive governess employed to educate the Gibson children, is accused of murdering Gibson's wife. A revolver matching the fatal bullet was found in her wardrobe, a note signed by her arranged a meeting at the scene, and she admitted being near Thor Bridge at the relevant time, though Neil Gibson insists that she is innocent.

"Then came Miss Grace Dunbar. She answered our advertisement and

Miss Grace Dunbar answered the millionaire's advertisement and became governess to his two children. The millionaire says that the whole world has proclaimed her a very beautiful woman and that she was under his protection.

To my surprise the Gold King took the reproof with equanimity.

Miss Dunbar exerted a powerful beneficial influence over the Gold King and believed that his excessive fortune should not be built on the ruin of thousands of men. Mr. Holmes intends to see her in Winchester.

There was some delay in the official pass, and instead of reaching

Miss Dunbar admitted writing the note arranging to meet Mrs. Gibson at Thor Bridge but, on her lawyer's advice, reserved her defence and said nothing further. A pistol found in her wardrobe appears to implicate her, but Holmes suspects that someone else placed it there to incriminate her.

We were compelled to spend the night at Winchester, as the formalities

Miss Dunbar tells Holmes that Mrs. Gibson hated her and summoned her secretly to Thor Bridge, where Mrs. Gibson angrily confronted her. She denies knowing about the pistol found in her wardrobe, and Holmes works to secure her vindication from the murder charge.
