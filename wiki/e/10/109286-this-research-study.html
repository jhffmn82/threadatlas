this research study in Investigating Retrieval-Augmented Generation in Quranic Studies: A Study of 13 Open-Source Large Language Models

Zahra Khalila, Arbi Haza Nasution, Winda Monika, Aytug Onan, Yohei Murakami, Yasir Bin Ismail Radi, Noor Mohammad Osmani · 2025

This research study is a comparative evaluation of 13 open-source large language models for answering Qur’anic-studies questions within a Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework. Using a curated Qur’anic surah descriptions dataset, it applies human evaluation and Inter-Evaluator Agreement to assess context relevance, response faithfulness, answer relevance, and intercoder agreement. Across models of different sizes, the study benchmarks accuracy and faithfulness while examining trade-offs among model size, response quality, performance, and computational efficiency. It concludes by suggesting optimization, fine-tuning, and greater dataset diversity as future work.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

I.

This research study benchmarks 13 open-source large language models within a Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework for Quranic studies. It evaluates context relevance, response faithfulness, and answer relevance through human evaluation, and investigates the balance among model size, response quality, and computational resources.

II.

The research study evaluates large language models’ ability to answer questions concerning Qur’anic studies. It uses a curated dataset, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, human evaluation, and Inter-Evaluator Agreement to assess response quality.

III.

The study evaluates the performance of 13 LLMs in answering questions related to Quranic studies within a RAG framework. It assesses context relevance, answer faithfulness, answer relevance, and intercoder agreement.

V.

The study evaluated multiple large language models of different sizes in responding to Quranic studies-related queries using a Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework. It identified trade-offs between model size, performance, and computational efficiency and suggested future work on optimization, fine-tuning, and dataset diversity.
