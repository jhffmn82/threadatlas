Abstract written by a model

Violet de Merville is the young, wealthy, beautiful, accomplished, strong-willed daughter of General de Merville and fiancée of Baron Adelbert Gruner. Completely and obsessively in love with Gruner, she plans to marry him despite knowing of his stormy past and despite warnings. During Holmes and Miss Winter's visit, she is cold, self-contained, and inflexible, insisting that Gruner has repented and dismissing testimony against him. Although Holmes expects Gruner's injuries to deepen her devotion, the contents of Gruner's incriminating book bring her back to reality and lead her to reject him.

Violet de Merville is in one work on this site, The Case-Book of Sherlock Holmes. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

THE ADVENTURE OF THE ILLUSTRIOUS CLIENT

Violet de Merville is described as young, wealthy, beautiful, accomplished, and strong-willed. She is completely in love with Baron Gruner, refuses to hear criticism of him, and plans to marry him despite his criminal past.

I did not see Holmes again until the following evening, when we dined

Violet de Merville receives Holmes and Miss Winter but remains cold, self-contained, and unmoved by their warnings. She says that she loves Baron Gruner, believes he has repented of the evil he has done, and dismisses Miss Winter's testimony as ravings.

Violet de Merville is expected to reject Baron Adelbert Gruner after learning the contents of his incriminating book. Holmes believes that Gruner's injuries alone would make her love him more as a disfigured martyr, but the book succeeds in bringing her back to reality.
