Document Segmentation Matters for Retrieval-Augmented Generation

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

This paper presents PIC (Pseudo-Instruction for document Chunking), a document-segmentation method for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). It addresses the problem that rule-based chunking can create oversized or semantically incomplete units, while semantic and generation-based approaches may be costly or insufficiently adaptive. PIC treats a document summary as a pseudo-instruction representing the document’s key theme and likely user needs. It encodes sentences, measures each sentence’s cosine similarity to the summary, and uses the mean similarity across the document as a dynamic threshold to group adjacent sentences into relevant or irrelevant chunks. The paper situates chunk quality as an important but underexplored part of RAG, alongside retriever and generation improvements. A Chinese New Year/Spring Festival example illustrates the aim of dividing a document into semantically related sections. The authors create PICWiki from Wikipedia documents and evaluate PIC using the 2024-12-01 English Wikipedia dump and six OpenQA benchmarks. They compare it with sentence, paragraph, fixed-size, semantic, proposition, rule-based, and generation-based chunking approaches. Experiments use GPT-4o-mini for summaries, bge-en-large-v1.5 for embeddings and retrieval, and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct for answer generation. PIC obtains the strongest average retrieval results, reaching 58.4 Top-5 and 69.5 Top-20 Hits@k, and it also yields the best end-to-end QA performance with both answer-generation models. It is weaker on SQuAD, which the authors associate with that benchmark’s relatively limited document set and reading-comprehension-like questions. Although QA accuracy remains below retrieval accuracy overall, PIC has the smallest decline, attributed to chunks containing less irrelevant information. Further analyses show that genuine document summaries outperform random summaries, random sentences, and mean sentence embeddings as pseudo-instructions; the mean-similarity threshold performs best; and real instructions achieve the strongest results on NaturalQuestions and Qasper. The authors find chunk structure more important than chunk length and report that PIC retrieves a majority of relevant chunks across datasets. They conclude that PIC improves retrieval and QA without retraining, while noting summary-generation overhead, general-domain-only evaluation, and cross-document reasoning as limitations and future work.

Abstract

The abstract concerns PIC, a document chunking method for retrieval-augmented generation that uses document summaries as pseudo-instructions. It addresses limitations of rule-based chunking methods, which can produce overly large or semantically incomplete chunks, and semantic-based approaches, which may require costly large language model calls or fail to group related sentences adaptively. PIC computes semantic similarity between sentences and a document summary to dynamically form chunks aligned with key themes and potential user instructions. Experiments on multiple open-domain question-answering benchmarks show improved retrieval accuracy and end-to-end QA performance without additional training.

1 / Introduction

The unit examines document segmentation as a foundational challenge in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), which combines large language models with textual knowledge to improve response quality and reduce hallucinations. It proposes PIC (Pseudo-Instruction for document Chunking), which uses document summaries as pseudo-instructions to group sentences by their semantic similarity to the summary. The authors create PICWiki from Wikipedia documents and evaluate the method on multiple open-domain question-answering benchmarks. PIC improves retrieval performance and end-to-end question-answering performance compared with existing chunking methods.

2 / Related Works

This unit reviews Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) methods, emphasizing improvements to retrievers, generation reliability, and retrieval-unit design. It states that most prior RAG work overlooks chunk quality, while newer approaches use semantic relationships and large language models to improve document segmentation. The unit also presents a proposed method, PIC, which dynamically segments documents according to the similarity between sentences and a pseudo-instruction. The Chinese New Year example illustrates how documents about the Spring Festival can be divided into semantically related chunks.

3 / Methodology .. 3.2 / Chunking Based on Pseudo-Instructions

This unit presents a methodology for chunking documents in the context of retrieval-augmented generation. It generates pseudo-instructions from a document summary and uses them to guide semantic chunking. Sentences are encoded and compared with the summary using cosine similarity, and adjacent sentences are grouped into relevant or irrelevant chunks according to a dynamic threshold. The threshold is set to the mean similarity between all sentences and the document summary.

4 / Experimental Settings .. 4.4 / Implementation Details

This unit evaluates document chunking methods for Retrieval-Augmented Generation using the 2024-12-01 English Wikipedia dump and six OpenQA benchmarks. It compares rule-based, semantic-based, generation-based, and PIC chunking methods through retrieval performance measured by Hits@k and end-to-end question-answering performance measured by Exact Match. PIC achieves the highest average retrieval performance, with 58.4 Top-5 and 69.5 Top-20 Hits@k, outperforming the listed baselines. The experiments use GPT-4o-mini for summarization, bge-en-large-v1.5 for embeddings and retrieval, and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct for answer generation.

5 / Main Results / 5.1 / Retrieval Performance .. 5.2 / End-to-end QA Performance

The unit evaluates PIC against sentence, paragraph, fixed-size, semantic, and proposition chunking methods on retrieval and end-to-end question-answering tasks across six datasets. PIC achieves the highest average retrieval performance and consistently produces the best QA results with both Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct. PIC performs less strongly on SQuAD, likely because its questions come from relatively few documents and resemble reading comprehension. QA accuracy is lower than retrieval accuracy overall, but PIC has the smallest performance drop, which the authors attribute to reduced irrelevant information in its chunks.

6 / Further Analysis / 6.1 / Impacts of the Pseudo-Instruction .. 6.4 / Chunk Analysis

This unit analyzes how pseudo-instruction design, chunking thresholds, and instruction-based chunking affect retrieval and question-answering performance. Using a document summary as the pseudo-instruction performs better than using a random summary, a random sentence, or the mean sentence embedding, while the mean similarity threshold gives the best results. Chunking with real instructions achieves the strongest results on NaturalQuestions and Qasper. Further analysis finds that chunk structure matters more than chunk length and that PIC retrieves a majority of relevant chunks across datasets.

7 / Conclusions / Limitations

The paper identifies document segmentation as a critical but underexplored challenge in retrieval-augmented generation systems. It proposes PIC, a document chunking method that uses document summaries as pseudo-instructions to guide semantic chunking and group sentences by relevance to the document topic. Across six QA benchmarks, PIC improves retrieval accuracy and end-to-end QA performance without additional model retraining. The paper notes computational overhead from summary generation and limits its evaluation to general-domain tasks, leaving cross-document reasoning for future work.
