Abstract written by a model

Chinese townspeople are human city residents who speak Chinese. They fill rain-darkened streets, bazaars, shops, and alleys, carrying baskets, working, serving food, operating chairs, and conducting business around Rudolph. Their voices, clothing, signs, and customs make the city feel overwhelming and unfamiliar to him. By night they continue amid smells, noise, and labor; in the chapel they listen and question Dr. Earle, then react to a killing with hostile cries and defenses against feared plague-goblins.

" CHAPTER III UNDER FIRE The desert was sometimes Gobi, sometimes Sahara, but always an infinite stretch of sand that floated up and up in a stifling layer, like the tide.

Chinese people fill the rain-darkened streets, carrying baskets, working in shops, and moving through the covered bazaar. They stare at Rudolph, operate the chairs, serve food, and conduct business around him. Their voices, clothing, signs, and customs contribute to his sense of an overwhelming and unfamiliar city.

CHAPTER V IN TOWN Night, in that maze of alleys, was but a more sinister day.

Chinese townspeople move through the alleys by day and night amid persistent smells, noises, shops, and labor. In the chapel they listen, smoke, fidget, and question Dr. Earle, while elsewhere they respond to the killing with hostile cries and use gongs, nets, and thorns against feared plague-goblins.
