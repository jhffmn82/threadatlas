Abstract written by a model

Tuxbury Old Hall is Colonel Emsworth's large, remote country house in Tuxbury Old Park, an old, inaccessible and rambling mansion capable of concealing many people. Its Elizabethan foundations, panelling, tapestry, and old pictures reflect several periods. It contains Emsworth's study and is served by the elderly Ralph and his wife. Dodd stays there with Emsworth and Godfrey's mother; food is carried toward a detached house, and Godfrey finally appears to Dodd there.
