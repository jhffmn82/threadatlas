PREFACE

Arthur Conan Doyle bids farewell to Sherlock Holmes, expressing hope that Holmes and Watson may find a place in an imagined Valhalla. He recounts Holmes's career from his debut in A Study in Scarlet and The Sign of Four through the short stories published in The Strand Magazine and the collections that followed. Doyle explains that he once ended Holmes's career after The Memoirs but later revived him in response to public demand. He thanks readers for their loyalty and for the distraction and change of thought provided by the stories.

During a visit to Tuxbury Old Hall, Dodd is rebuffed by the Colonel and hears the butler Ralph say that he wishes Godfrey were dead. Nathan Garrideb, a reclusive collector living in London, confirms John's account and eagerly hopes to obtain the money. Sherlock Holmes connects the deaths of Fitzroy McPherson and McPherson's dog, along with Ian Murdoch's sudden illness, to a deadly jellyfish in the bathing-pool. He identifies the creature as Cyanea capillata, whose radiating filaments produce the distinctive reticulated sting pattern and severe pain. The phrase “Lion's Mane,” spoken by McPherson before his death, is recognized as a warning describing the creature. Cyanea capillata is crushed beneath a boulder, McPherson's death is explained as an accidental envenomation, and Ian Murdoch is exonerated.
