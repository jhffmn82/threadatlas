Abstract written by a model

Miss Drake is a young woman on a hospital and nunnery staff, a pupil at the nunnery, and a medical attendant devoted to the hospital and its children. Wearing plain white clothing, she receives an abandoned infant girl without surprise and takes her away for compassionate medical care. She works with Heywood and refuses to leave on the next ship despite danger, because she must remain faithful to a guiding dream that has changed: she once believed in a dark continent of lost souls, but now questions what they can accomplish there. At a celebration, she accepts Captain Kneebone's two-thousand-rupee gift for the hospital and children after initial hesitation. During danger she tends wounded men and prepares medicines, declining rest because she is too busy. Her love for Heywood becomes explicit as more than ordinary liking. In the final record, she shares grief with Kneebone, speaks to Rudolph of Heywood as a Happy Warrior, and expresses a belief that invulnerability means being greater than anything that happens to a person, while saying she had always sensed Heywood's return was possible.

Miss Drake is in one work on this site, Dragon's Blood. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VII IPHIGENIA "Don't chop off a hen's head with a battle-axe.

Miss Drake receives the abandoned infant from Rudolph without haste or surprise. She says that the child may live and carries her away with a native woman to offer care.

" CHAPTER XIV OFF DUTY The last of the sunlight streamed level through a gap in the western ridges.

Miss Drake walks with Heywood through the village and around the town, discussing the stolen guns, their work, dreams, and the dangers facing them. She refuses to leave when the next ship arrives, saying that she must stand by her dream, even as it has changed. She admits that she once came believing in a dark continent of lost souls and now questions what they can do there.

CHAPTER XV KAU FAI At the top of the nunnery stairs, Rudolph met them with awkward ceremony, and with that smiling air of encouragement which a nurse might use in trying cheerfully to deceive a sick man.

Miss Drake attends the dinner and prompts Captain Kneebone to explain the source of the celebration. Kneebone gives her two thousand rupees for her hospital and children, which she initially hesitates to accept before thanking him.

CHAPTER XIX BROTHER MOLES This new danger, working below in the solid earth, had thrown Rudolph into a state of sullen resignation.

Miss Drake tends the wounded in the hospital room and prepares medicines during the brief lull. She declines Heywood's invitation to rest, saying that she is too busy.

CHAPTER XXI THE DRAGON'S SHADOW The lowdah would have set his dirty sails without delay, for the fair wind was already drooping; but at the first motion he found himself deposed, and a usurper in command, at the big steering-paddle.

Miss Drake tells Captain Kneebone that her feeling for Heywood was more than liking, and she sits with him in shared grief. At sea, she speaks with Rudolph about Heywood, calling men like him a kind of Happy Warrior and describing invulnerability as being greater than anything that happens to a person. She says that she knew all along, in some sense, that Heywood's return was possible.
