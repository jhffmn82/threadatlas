Lord Cantlemere

person · 1 work · 5 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: his lordship, the old courtier, the peer

Abstract written by a model

Lord Cantlemere, an old courtier and peer with fifty years of official experience, visits Holmes while doubting and opposing him. Holmes's Mazarin-stone trick briefly leaves Cantlemere in possession of it, showing how he could be arrested; bewildered, Cantlemere withdraws his criticism of Holmes's abilities.

Lord Cantlemere is in one work on this site, The Case-Book of Sherlock Holmes. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Lord Cantlemere visits Holmes to question the progress of the investigation and expresses skepticism about Holmes's abilities. Holmes places the Mazarin stone in his overcoat pocket and reveals it, demonstrating how possession could justify an arrest. Lord Cantlemere is bewildered but withdraws his criticism of Holmes's professional powers.
