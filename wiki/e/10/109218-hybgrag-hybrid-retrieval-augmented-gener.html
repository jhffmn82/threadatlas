Ioannidis, Huzefa Rangwala, and Christos Faloutsos of Carnegie Mellon University and Amazon, published in the Proceedings of the 63rd Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics. It addresses hybrid question answering over semi-structured knowledge bases that combine knowledge graphs with documents attached to entities. The paper argues that conventional Retrieval-Augmented Generation emphasizes text, while Graph RAG emphasizes relational structure, so either can miss questions requiring both sources. The paper identifies two central problems: useful information from textual and relational retrievers is largely non-overlapping, and initial retrieval often requires correction. It proposes HYBGRAG, an agentic framework with a retriever bank and a critic module. The bank includes text and hybrid retrieval modules; a router selects a module and retrieves top-K references. A validator assesses the results, and a commenter supplies corrective feedback when entity extraction, relation extraction, or routing is wrong. Through iterations, the router revises its retrieval choice, topic entities, and relations until the validator accepts the references. Experiments cover STARK-MAG, STARK-PRIME, and CRAG. HYBGRAG reports the best retrieval results on both STARK datasets and the best CRAG results among the compared graph, text, hybrid, and self-reflective baselines. On STARK, it achieves a 51% average relative Hit@1 improvement, including 47% on STARK-MAG and 55% on STARK-PRIME. The paper reports that corrective feedback raises subgraph hit rate from 0.6769 after one iteration to 0.9231 after two. Ablations attribute gains to the critic, iterative reflection, validation context, commenter, and multi-agent design; interpretability examples show switches from failed text retrieval to refined hybrid retrieval. The appendices detail benchmarks, prompts, infrastructure, implementation, API and token use, and additional ablations. The paper closes by noting limitations: simple retrieval modules, limited domain-adaptation advantage, random in-context examples, and weaker performance on STARK-PRIME than STARK-MAG. It suggests alternative rankers, PPR-based retrieval, and relevance-based experience selection as future directions.

Abstract

The unit concerns information retrieval for question answering over a semi-structured knowledge base (SKB) in which text documents are interconnected by relations. It explains that Retrieval-Augmented Generation and Graph RAG address textual and structured retrieval, respectively, but may struggle with hybrid questions requiring both types of information. The paper proposes HYBGRAG, which combines a retriever bank with a critic module to adaptively retrieve information and refine answers. On the STARK benchmark, HYBGRAG achieves an average relative improvement of 51% in Hit@1 over baseline methods.

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is introduced as a method that enables large language models to access information from an unstructured document database. The text states that RAG helps large language models address unknown facts. It also enables Open-Domain Question Answering with additional textual information.

opening

The unit introduces HYBGRAG, a framework for hybrid question answering over semi-structured knowledge bases that combine knowledge graphs with associated text documents. It argues that retrieval-augmented generation and graph retrieval-augmented generation each overlook one modality or confuse textual and relational aspects. HYBGRAG uses a retriever bank and self-reflection to refine question routing and retrieve both textual and relational information. On the STARK benchmark, it outperforms baselines, with reported relative Hit@1 improvements of 47% on STARK-MAG and 55% on STARK-PRIME.

2 / 2.1 .. 2.3

This unit defines hybrid question answering (HQA) over a semi-structured knowledge base containing a knowledge graph and entity-associated documents. It identifies the hybrid-sourcing question challenge, showing that textual and relational retrievers provide useful but largely non-overlapping information on STARK-MAG. It also identifies the refinement-required question challenge, finding that Claude 3 Sonnet often needs feedback and additional iterations to extract a subgraph containing the target entities. The experiments show that corrective feedback improves the subgraph hit rate from 0.6769 after one iteration to 0.9231 after two iterations.

3 / 3.1 .. 3.3

HYBGRAG is proposed to solve HQA through a retriever bank for Challenge 1 and a critic module for Challenge 2. The retriever bank contains text and hybrid retrieval modules plus a router that performs question routing and retrieves top-K references. The critic module uses an LLM validator to assess retrieved references and an LLM commenter to provide corrective feedback when routing is incorrect. Across iterations, the router refines its selected retrieval module, topic entities, and useful relations until the validator accepts the retrieval.

4 .. 4.1.2

The experiments evaluate HYBGRAG on STARK-MAG, STARK-PRIME, and CRAG for retrieval and end-to-end retrieval-augmented generation. HYBGRAG achieves the best reported retrieval results on both STARK datasets and the best CRAG results, outperforming graph, text, hybrid, and self-reflective baselines. Ablations show that its critic module, iterative self-reflection, validation context, commenter, and multi-agent design contribute to performance. Examples demonstrate that HYBGRAG refines incorrect or incomplete entity and relation extraction based on feedback.

5

The unit reviews Graph RAG (GRAG), agentic and self-reflective LLMs, and their limitations for HQA in SKB. Graph RAG methods have mainly addressed KBQA, ODQA, or question answering with a given subgraph, whereas this paper focuses on HQA in SKB. LLM agents and AVATAR support planning or iterative prompt optimization, but may not produce correct outputs initially. Self-reflection improves outputs through feedback, yet existing approaches lack suitable retrieval tools and effective guidance for refining retrieval in HQA.

6

The unit presents HYBGRAG as a proposed approach for solving hybrid question answering (HQA). It uses a critic module for self-reflective question routing and a retriever bank for textual, relational, and hybrid questions. The approach is described as agentic, adaptive, interpretable, and effective, outperforming all baselines on HQA benchmarks. Applied on STARK, HYBGRAG achieves a 51% average relative improvement in Hit@1.

Limitations

The unit describes limitations of HYBGRAG in comparison with existing RAG and GRAG methods on HQA. HYBGRAG uses only simple retrieval modules, has no significant advantage in domain adaptation, and selects random experiences for in-context learning. Its performance is worse on STARK-Prime than on STARK-MAG, and the text identifies alternative rankers, PPR-based retrieval, and relevance-based experience selection as future improvement directions.

A / A.1 .. A.2

The unit describes the STARK benchmark and the CRAG benchmark. STARK-MAG and STARK-PRIME each contain a knowledge graph and unstructured documents associated with some entity types, and their task is to retrieve documents satisfying question requirements. The STARK testing sets contain 2665 questions for STARK-MAG and 2801 for STARK-PRIME. CRAG provides 50 retrieved web pages and five domain-specific knowledge graphs for each question, requiring answer generation even when the answer may not appear in the provided references.

B / B.1 .. B.2

This unit describes interpretability experiments for HYBGRAG in STARK-Prime and an ablation study of its critic module. In one Fig. 6(a) example, HYBGRAG first uses text retrieval, rejects the retrieved document after validation, and then switches to hybrid retrieval based on commentor feedback. It refines the extraction of topic entities and useful relations over the next two iterations. The ablation results show that HYBGRAG performs best with all design choices and approaches an oracle’s performance.

C / C.1 .. CRAG

The appendix describes reproducibility details for experiments run on AWS EC2 P4 instances with NVIDIA A100 GPUs, using Amazon Bedrock for most LLMs and Ollama for Llama 3.1. HYBGRAG is implemented for STARK using training-set examples, default entity and relation types, ego-graphs of radius no more than two, and four self-reflection iterations. STARK entity-name ambiguities are resolved by selecting the entity with the answer in its one-hop neighborhood, affecting 3.83% of STARK-MAG questions and 0.07% of STARK-PRIME questions. For CRAG, web search and a VSS ranker support text retrieval, while graph retrieval prepares for relational questions using ego-graphs and reasoning paths.
