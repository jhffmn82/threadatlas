Milo

person · 1 work · 6 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Milo of Croton, Milo the son of Diotimus

Abstract written by a model

Milo of Croton, son of Diotimus, was a wrestling victor whose chief achievements were six Olympic prizes, including one as a boy, and six Pythian victories among men plus one as a boy. Seeking a seventh Olympic win, he lost to Timasitheus. He was associated with remarkable feats and a statue by Dameas, then died trapped in a split tree and killed by wild beasts.

Milo is in one work on this site, Description of Greece. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XIII.

Milo of Croton won six Olympic wrestling prizes, one among boys, and came to Olympia seeking a seventh victory but was defeated by Timasitheus. He was said to have carried his statue to the Altis and to have performed feats involving a pomegranate, an oiled quoit, a cord around his forehead, and the immobilization of a finger; he was later killed by wolves after becoming trapped in a split tree.
