the dead man’s killing of Chok Chung in Dragon's Blood

Henry Milner Rideout · 1909

The records describe the killing of Chok Chung in his Chinese merchant’s shop. Au-yoeng, drunk and carrying a knife, enters the shop and kills Chok Chung after poverty, theft and recovery of Fuh-kien hemp, neighborhood pressure, and his belief that Chok Chung and the Jesus religion caused his troubles. During the quarrel, a half-naked ruffian is also described as stabbing himself, collapsing outside, and prompting hostility toward the merchant.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IV THE SWORD-PEN "Wutzler was missing last night," said Heywood, lazily.

During a quarrel in a Chinese shop, a half-naked ruffian draws a blade and drives it into his own body. He stumbles outside and collapses, while the merchant appeals to Heywood and the surrounding crowd becomes hostile after Fang appears to provoke it.

CHAPTER V IN TOWN Night, in that maze of alleys, was but a more sinister day.

Au-yoeng enters Chok Chung’s shop while gloriously drunk and kills him with a knife. The killing follows Au-yoeng’s poverty, his theft of Fuh-kien hemp, the recovery of most of it, continued pressure from his neighbors, and his belief that Chok Chung and the Jesus religion caused his troubles.
