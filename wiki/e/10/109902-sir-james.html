Sir James in The Case-Book of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1921-10

Sir James is a diplomat and intermediary who serves an undisclosed, illustrious client and consults Sherlock Holmes to stop Baron Gruner's marriage to Violet de Merville. Loyal and chivalrous, he explains Gruner's history, interests, address, and private telephone contact. After Holmes summons him and recounts events, Sir James takes the incriminating book and blue saucer from Holmes's room, apparently helping prevent the marriage; Watson infers that Sir James himself is the client.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE ADVENTURE OF THE ILLUSTRIOUS CLIENT

Sir James Damery approaches Sherlock Holmes on behalf of an unnamed client who wants Baron Gruner's proposed marriage to Violet de Merville prevented. He explains Violet's devotion to Gruner, describes Gruner's criminal history and interests, and gives Holmes Gruner's address and a private telephone contact.

Sir James comes to Sherlock Holmes after being summoned and listens to Holmes's account of the events. He carries away the incriminating book and the blue saucer, apparently to help ensure that the marriage is prevented; Dr. Watson infers from his carriage's armorial bearings that he is the client.
