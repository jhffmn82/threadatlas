the redwood forest in From Sand Hill to Pine

Bret Harte · 1894-04-24

The redwood forest is a primeval, untrodden forest of lifeless, decaying redwood-tree matter. Thick interlaced branches obscure the sun, making it silent and gloomy and causing Fleming to lose his way. It contains a spring, outcrop, and secluded soil-testing area; Fleming reaches an opening toward the Jallinger cabin and meets Katinka there.

2 sections; each heading opens the text

" A TREASURE OF THE REDWOODS PART I Mr.

The redwood forest causes Fleming to lose his way because its thickly interlaced branches obscure the sun. Its decaying bark, dust, and primeval seclusion create a silent, gloomy environment. Fleming eventually reaches a rocky hillside and an opening leading toward the Jallinger cabin.

PART II Mr.

The redwood forest contains the spring, the outcrop, and the secluded area where Fleming tests the soil. Fleming and Katinka meet there and briefly mistake their reflections in the spring for a romantic omen.
