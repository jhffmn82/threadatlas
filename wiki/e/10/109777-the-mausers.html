Abstract written by a model

The Mausers are twelve old Argentine rifles, calibre .765, the settlement’s only real weapons. Left by Heywood’s predecessor with three cases of cartridges, they are kept oiled by Heywood, who considers them sound and takes them over for the settlement’s defense. They are first polished and orderly in a rack at Rudolph’s home, but Peng removes the side-bolts from all except two, crippling them. Six rifles are later recovered from the garden, mud, and shallow water. As an attack begins, the guns are located in the nunnery’s first bedroom.

" CHAPTER XII THE WAR BOARD "Rigmarole?

The twelve old Argentine Mausers, calibre .765, were left by Heywood's predecessor and have been kept oiled by Heywood. He says they are sound and has taken them over for the defense of the settlement.

" CHAPTER XIII THE SPARE MAN Beyond the scant greenery of Heywood's garden--a ropy little banyan, a low rank of glossy whampee leaves, and the dusty sage-green tops of stunted olives--glared the river.

The Mausers are polished and orderly in their rack at Rudolph's home, and Rudolph remembers that he was supposed to make an inventory of provisions. Heywood discovers that their side-bolts are missing from all but two rifles, leaving the group crippled.

CHAPTER XV KAU FAI At the top of the nunnery stairs, Rudolph met them with awkward ceremony, and with that smiling air of encouragement which a nurse might use in trying cheerfully to deceive a sick man.

Heywood says that the guns are in the first bedroom. He directs the men to their posts as the attack on the nunnery begins.
