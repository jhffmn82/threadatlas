Abstract written by a model

Chantel is a French-speaking French doctor, Julien’s brother, and a striking but bitter, mocking figure among the club’s white men. Early in the crisis of rats and plague, he speculates that an earthquake may explain the rats, supports fumigation with chloride of lime and charcoal, and notes bitterly that the danger does not attack Europeans because Julien remains alive. After attending Monsieur Jolivet, he is disinfected, announces Jolivet’s death from plague, forbids others to visit the dead man, and later sings with the group. Chantel’s conflict with Rudolph becomes central. Seen riding with a woman and later at the Flowery Pagoda, he immediately inspires Rudolph’s hatred. At the club he taunts Rudolph, heroes, and a lady; after Rudolph strikes him, he calmly arranges a duel. Known as a beautiful, highly skilled swordsman and expert fencer, he accepts Heywood as Rudolph’s substitute, dominates the encounter, and agrees to postpone it only after being struck in the face and breaking his sword. He continues to mock danger, defenses, converts, and the magistrate at the war board until Heywood silences him. Later, at dinner, Chantel appears melancholy and suspicious and slips away after Mrs. Forrester signals angrily. In the end, he struggles with Bertha and attempts to take her away by boat, offering a stocked escape vessel, carrying her belongings, pulling her aboard when Rudolph hesitates, and striking at Rudolph with a boatman’s pole.

Chantel is in one work on this site, Dragon's Blood. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Chantel in Dragon's Blood

Henry Milner Rideout · 1909 · the work’s page · read it where Chantel first appears

Narrative, section by section written by a model

8 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II THE PIED PIPER A gray smudge trailing northward showed where the Fa-Hien--Scottish Oriental, sixteen hundred tons--was disappearing from the pale expanse of ocean.

Doctor Chantel is a French-speaking doctor and a striking member of the club. He suggests that the rats' emergence may be connected to an earthquake and then agrees that chloride of lime and possibly charcoal should be used for fumigation. He bitterly notes that the phenomenon does not attack Europeans, since his brother Julien is still alive.

" CHAPTER III UNDER FIRE The desert was sometimes Gobi, sometimes Sahara, but always an infinite stretch of sand that floated up and up in a stifling layer, like the tide.

Dr. Chantel arrives at the club and interrupts the concert to announce Monsieur Jolivet's death from the plague. He says that he had been disinfected and insists that nobody go to Jolivet because the dead man needs no one. He later resumes singing with the group.

" CHAPTER VI THE PAGODA He was spared that inconvenience.

Chantel rides past Rudolph and Maurice Heywood with a woman who laughingly asks whether he understands her. Rudolph already hates Chantel at sight and refuses to join the party at the Flowery Pagoda, though he later encounters Chantel among the tiffin party at the ancient pagoda.

CHAPTER VIII THE HOT NIGHT Rudolph paced his long chamber like a wolf,--a wolf in summer, with too thick a coat.

Dr. Chantel plays cards at the club and repeatedly mocks Rudolph and the idea of heroes. He insults the lady, accepts Rudolph's blow with icy composure, and says he will ask Sturgeon to see Heywood's friend about the matter. Heywood says Chantel is a beautiful and exceptionally quick swordsman who will choose swords.

" CHAPTER IX PASSAGE AT ARMS "Boy.

Doctor Chantel accepts Heywood as Rudolph's substitute and displays superior fencing skill, prolonging the fight while repeatedly attacking. A thrown object strikes him in the face, and he later breaks his sword in the rubble; he says there are no more blades nearby and agrees to postpone the duel.

" CHAPTER XII THE WAR BOARD "Rigmarole?

Chantel mocks the threat, the proposed defenses, the native converts, and the magistrate. Heywood rebukes him sharply for mocking the magistrate, after which Chantel becomes quiet and takes his seat.

CHAPTER XV KAU FAI At the top of the nunnery stairs, Rudolph met them with awkward ceremony, and with that smiling air of encouragement which a nurse might use in trying cheerfully to deceive a sick man.

Chantel sits at the foot of the dinner table, melancholy and suspicious, while the others celebrate. After Mrs. Forrester signals angrily, Chantel slips out of the room; Rudolph is sent to call him back.

CHAPTER XVI THE GUNWALE Rudolph's mission began quietly, with a glimpse which he afterward recalled as incredibly peaceful.

Chantel struggles with Bertha at her house and tries to persuade her to escape by a stocked boat. He carries Bertha's selected possessions in a sarong bundle, pulls her aboard when Rudolph hesitates, and strikes at Rudolph with the boatman's pole.
