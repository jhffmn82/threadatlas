PIC

method · 1 work · 38 quoted facts · 12 connections
Also called: PIC-Mean, PIC-Random, PIC-Sent, Pseudo-Instruction for document Chunking, our PIC method, our approach, our method, proposed PIC method
  • In Document Segmentation Matters for Retrieval-Augmented Generation: PIC, or Pseudo-Instruction(s) for document Chunking, is a novel, simple document chunking method. It generates or uses a summary for each document as a pseudo-instruction, computes sentence similarity to that summary, and dynamically groups sentences according to whether their relevance is above or below an average threshold. This produces semantically coherent, complete chunks aligned with the document’s central topic and potential user instructions, while isolating irrelevant content and maintaining shorter average chunk length. PIC requires no additional training or model retraining. Its central aim is to improve retrieval relevance and downstream question answering by categorizing content through topic relevance, reducing irrelevant information, retrieval bias, and LLM hallucinations. In extensive experiments on six QA benchmarks, it outperforms Random and existing compared chunking approaches in retrieval accuracy and end-to-end QA performance. It achieves the highest average Top-5 and Top-20 Hits@k retrieval scores, reported as 58.4 and 69.5, and the highest average retrieval performance across the six datasets. It improves retrieval by 2 points on WebQ, though its retrieval performance is suboptimal on SQuAD. From retrieval to QA, PIC consistently obtains the best results with both evaluated LLMs, including a 5.7-point improvement on EQ with Qwen and nearly a 1-point average improvement with Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct. It has the smallest retrieval-to-QA drop, including 8.5 points under Top-5 with Qwen, attributed to reduced irrelevant information and fewer hallucinations. Analysis finds more than 50% relevant retrieved chunks in every evaluated dataset, and Appendix B provides a case study.

PIC in Document Segmentation Matters for Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Zhitong Wang, Cheng Gao, Chaojun Xiao, Yufei Huang, Shuzheng Si, Kangyang Luo, Yuzhuo Bai, Wenhao Li, Tangjian Duan, Chuancheng Lv, Guoshan Lu, Gang Chen, Fanchao Qi, Maosong Sun · 2025

PIC, or Pseudo-Instruction(s) for document Chunking, is a novel, simple document chunking method. It generates or uses a summary for each document as a pseudo-instruction, computes sentence similarity to that summary, and dynamically groups sentences according to whether their relevance is above or below an average threshold. This produces semantically coherent, complete chunks aligned with the document’s central topic and potential user instructions, while isolating irrelevant content and maintaining shorter average chunk length. PIC requires no additional training or model retraining. Its central aim is to improve retrieval relevance and downstream question answering by categorizing content through topic relevance, reducing irrelevant information, retrieval bias, and LLM hallucinations. In extensive experiments on six QA benchmarks, it outperforms Random and existing compared chunking approaches in retrieval accuracy and end-to-end QA performance. It achieves the highest average Top-5 and Top-20 Hits@k retrieval scores, reported as 58.4 and 69.5, and the highest average retrieval performance across the six datasets. It improves retrieval by 2 points on WebQ, though its retrieval performance is suboptimal on SQuAD. From retrieval to QA, PIC consistently obtains the best results with both evaluated LLMs, including a 5.7-point improvement on EQ with Qwen and nearly a 1-point average improvement with Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct. It has the smallest retrieval-to-QA drop, including 8.5 points under Top-5 with Qwen, attributed to reduced irrelevant information and fewer hallucinations. Abstract

PIC, or Pseudo-Instruction for document Chunking, is proposed as a simple method that uses document summaries to guide chunking. It dynamically groups sentences into semantically complete and relevant chunks without additional training.

1 / Introduction

PIC, or Pseudo-Instructions for document Chunking, generates a summary for each document and groups sentences according to whether their similarity to the summary is above or below an average threshold. The method improves retrieval accuracy and end-to-end question-answering performance in the reported experiments.

4 / Experimental Settings .. 4.4 / Implementation Details

PIC is evaluated alongside the baseline chunking methods and achieves the highest average retrieval performance in both Top-5 and Top-20 Hits@k. It records average scores of 58.4 Top-5 and 69.5 Top-20.

5 / Main Results / 5.1 / Retrieval Performance .. 5.2 / End-to-end QA Performance

PIC achieves the highest average retrieval performance across six datasets and consistently obtains the best end-to-end QA results with both evaluated LLMs. Its retrieval performance is suboptimal on SQuAD, but it has the smallest drop from retrieval to QA performance, which the authors attribute to reduced irrelevant information and fewer hallucinations.

6 / Further Analysis / 6.1 / Impacts of the Pseudo-Instruction .. 6.4 / Chunk Analysis

PIC uses summaries as pseudo-instructions to organize document sentences into chunks according to their relevance to the document's central theme. It achieves the best retrieval and question-answering performance among the tested pseudo-instruction implementations and retrieves more than 50% relevant chunks in all evaluated datasets.

7 / Conclusions / Limitations

PIC is a proposed document chunking method that uses document summaries as pseudo-instructions to guide semantic chunking. It improves retrieval accuracy and end-to-end QA performance across six QA benchmarks without additional model retraining.
