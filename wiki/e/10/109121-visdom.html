Abstract written by a model

This unit is a paper in Volume 1 of the Proceedings of the 2025 Conference of the Nations of the Americas Chapter of the Association for Computational Linguistics: Human Language Technologies, held April 29–May 4, 2025. Titled “VisDoM: Multi-Document QA with Visually Rich Elements Using Multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation,” it is by Manan Suri, Puneet Mathur, Franck Dernoncourt, Kanika Gowswami, Ryan A. Rossi, and Dinesh Manocha, affiliated with the University of Maryland, College Park, Adobe Research, and IGDTUW. The paper addresses question answering over multi-page PDF documents whose relevant evidence may be spread across documents and include text, tables, charts, and slides. It first positions Retrieval Augmented Generation against text-centric and single-document benchmarks, then introduces VisDoMBench, a multi-document multimodal benchmark built from PaperTab, FeTaTab, SciGraphQA, SPIQA, and SlideVQA. VisDoMBench contains 2,271 queries across 1,277 documents and uses sourcing, sampling, de-duplication, document augmentation, and query augmentation to retain grounded evidence. The paper then presents VisDoMRAG, which runs parallel textual and visual retrieval pipelines: the textual pipeline uses OCR-based text, while the visual pipeline retrieves pages with graphical information. Its modality fusion uses evidence curation, chain-of-thought reasoning, and consistency analysis to reconcile the two pipelines. Experiments compare text retrieval methods including BM25, MPNet, MiniLM, and BGE-1.5; visual retrieval methods ColPali and ColQwen2; and end-to-end Long Context, TextualRAG, VisualRAG, and VisDoMRAG systems using Qwen2-VL, Gemini-1.5-Flash, and ChatGPT-4o. ColQwen2 performs best among retrieval baselines, and VisDoMRAG generally exceeds the other QA approaches by 12–20%, with visual RAG particularly useful on visually rich data. Ablations favor late fusion and structured prompting. The paper concludes that the benchmark and system improve multimodal multi-document QA, while noting dependence on text extraction and parsing, three LLM calls per query, hallucination and safety risks, and difficulty isolating visual performance when document context contributes to answers. Future work targets end-to-end trained models and low-resource settings.

Abstract

The paper introduces VisDoMBench, a comprehensive benchmark for question answering over multiple visually rich documents containing tables, charts, and presentation slides. It also proposes VisDoMRAG, a multimodal Retrieval Augmented Generation approach that combines visual and textual retrieval with evidence curation, chain-of-thought reasoning, and consistency-constrained modality fusion. Experiments with open-source and proprietary large language models show that VisDoMRAG outperforms unimodal and long-context baselines for end-to-end multimodal document question answering by 12–20%.

Introduction

The unit concerns PDF documents and the challenges of answering questions over their mixed textual, visual, and tabular content. It introduces VisDoMBench, a multi-document, multimodal QA benchmark with annotated evidence for tables, charts, slides, and other visually rich content. It also introduces VisDoMRAG, which combines parallel textual and visual retrieval pipelines through evidence curation, chain-of-thought reasoning, and modality fusion. The authors report that VisDoMRAG outperforms long-context processing, textual RAG, and visual RAG baselines by 12%–20% across various open- and closed-source LLM settings.

Related Work

Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) addresses Large Language Models’ difficulties with integrating external knowledge and adapting to unseen data by incorporating external information. It has been applied to machine translation, dialogue generation, abstractive summarization, knowledge-intensive generation, and visual question answering, including systems that retrieve text, images, captions, or OCR-derived data. Long context document question-answer benchmarks vary in content types, domains, and support for multiple documents, but many remain text-centric or limited to single documents. VisDoMBench supports multi-document question answering across text, tables, charts, and slides in a multi-domain evaluation framework.

Problem Formulation

The unit concerns a framework for answering a query using a collection of multi-page documents. Each document may contain multiple pages, and relevant information may be distributed across pages and documents. The framework retrieves relevant evidence at the page, paragraph, or text-chunk level and uses that context to generate answer text. Its goal is to accurately answer each query.

VisDoMBench / 4.1

VisDoMBench is a multi-document question-answering benchmark combining text, tables, charts, and presentation slides from five existing datasets. Its data sourcing, sampling, de-duplication, document augmentation, and query augmentation procedures are designed to preserve grounded evidence and create realistic multi-document QA settings. The benchmark includes PaperTab, FeTaTab, SciGraphQA, SPIQA, and SlideVQA, totaling 2,271 queries across 1,277 documents. VisDoMRAG is a retrieval and reasoning system that combines visual and textual retrieval pipelines through modality fusion and consistency analysis.

VisDoMRAG / 5.1 .. 5.2

VisDoMRAG is a multimodal RAG approach for visually rich document question answering. It combines parallel textual and visual RAG pipelines with Modality Fusion to produce a final answer. The textual RAG pipeline extracts and retrieves OCR-based text, while the visual RAG pipeline retrieves document pages containing graphical information. Modality Fusion evaluates consistency between the two pipelines’ evidence and reasoning chains and reconciles discrepancies.

Experiments / 6.1 .. 6.2

The experiments evaluate retrieval and indexing models, followed by end-to-end question answering using selected retrieval models and several LLMs. Text retrieval compares BM25, MPNet, MiniLM, and BGE-1.5, while visual retrieval compares ColPali and ColQwen2 using extracted and segmented document content. Retrieval is evaluated through evidence extraction and source-document identification across PaperTab, FetaTab, SciGraphQA, SPIQA, and SlideVQA. End-to-end QA compares Long Context, TextualRAG, VisualRAG, and VisDoMRAG with Qwen2-VL, Gemini-1.5-Flash, and ChatGPT-4o on VisDoMBench.

Results / 7.1 .. 7.3

The unit evaluates VisDoMRAG and alternative retrieval and generation approaches on VisDoMBench across PaperTab, FetaTab, SciGraphQA, SPIQA, and SlideVQA. ColQwen2 outperforms other retrieval baselines, while VisDoMRAG generally surpasses Visual RAG, Textual RAG, and Long Context methods, with gains varying by dataset and model. Visual RAG is especially effective for visually rich data, whereas textual and visual performance differences are smaller on text-rich scientific datasets. Ablations show that late fusion and structured prompting with evidence curation, chain-of-thought, and reasoning consistency improve performance over early fusion and simplified prompts.

Conclusion and Future Work

The unit introduces VisDoMBench, a QA dataset for evaluating multimodal retrieval systems on multiple documents containing text, tables, charts, and slides. It presents VisDoMRAG, a multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation approach that combines visual and textual pipelines through consistency-constrained modality fusion. VisDoMRAG improves performance over traditional long-context, textual, and visual RAG by 12–20%. Future work will extend multimodal multi-document RAG to reasoning with end-to-end trained models, particularly in low-resource settings.

Ethics Statement

The research uses publicly available datasets. Human evaluators' identities remain confidential, and no personally identifiable information is used during the experiments. The work is intended solely for document QA applications. Users seeking a deeper understanding of risks and mitigation strategies in LLM safety are directed to works by Kumar et al. (2024), Cui et al. (2024), and Luu et al. (2024).

Limitations

The study presents an approach for visually rich document question answering and identifies several limitations. The study's approach retains text extraction and document parsing, uses three LLM calls per query, and remains vulnerable to hallucinations and AI safety risks. Unlike previous visual QA research, it incorporates document context, allowing textual information from other sections of a paper to contribute to query responses. This reliance on document context complicates the isolation of visual performance testing but reflects the interdependence of multimodal information in real-world systems.
