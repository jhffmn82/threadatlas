CHAPTER VIII THE HOT NIGHT Rudolph paced his long chamber like a wolf,--a wolf in summer, with too thick a coat.

The heat oppresses the entire night, leaving the air stagnant, sour, and breathless. It torments Rudolph, fills the club with bad temper, contributes to the men's reckless behavior, and is repeatedly blamed for their loss of self-control.
