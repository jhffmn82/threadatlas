Abstract written by a model

Cota Ramierez is Senora Ramierez’s daughter, a beautiful, dangerously pretty girl and fearless, skilled bareback rider. She owns and is especially fond of her pinto mustang, riding it at a gallop; she is also linked to a mustang lent to Colonel Starbottle. At the fonda, Richards describes her as a fair-skinned girl of about fourteen with blue eyes, dark reddish hair, a long braid, and a sing-song voice, before she is hurried away. Later, riding her pinto, she playfully confronts Mr. Grey, briefly traps him in a stable, and admires his handling of the horse. At Richards’s invitation she visits the composing-room, curious about printing and openly attentive to Grey. When Richards kills her horse, she becomes furious, leaves for San Jose, and ends her relationship with him.

Cota Ramierez is in one work on this site, From Sand Hill to Pine. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

WHAT HAPPENED AT THE FONDA PART I "Well!

The Ramierez daughter is described by Richards as a very pretty, fair-skinned girl of about fourteen, with blue eyes, dark reddish hair in a long braid, and a sing-song voice. She briefly appears at the fonda and is hurried out of the room.

PART II The next afternoon he went to the fonda.

Cota rides her pinto mustang bareback and approaches Mr. Grey on foot after stopping at a distance. She playfully challenges him, traps him briefly in the stable, and apologizes after claiming she feared the horse might have kicked him. She regards Grey as strong because he understands how to handle the mustang.

He was still more concerned a week later, on returning unexpectedly one afternoo

Cota visits the composing-room at Richards's invitation and shows simple, childish curiosity about printing while also directing meaningful looks and an audacious kiss toward Mr. Grey. She is furious when Richards kills her pinto horse, leaves for San Jose, and ends her relationship with Richards.
