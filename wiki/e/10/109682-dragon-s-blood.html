The nunnery endures a prolonged siege: attackers use rifles, arrows, ladders, a stink-pot, and underground mines. Kempner dies saving the defenders, while Rudolph and Heywood discover and destroy the enemy’s powder preparations. With ammunition dwindling and Wutzler apparently dead, the women and survivors escape by a Hakka boat. Heywood remains behind after attacking Fang near Wutzler’s displayed body and is presumed killed. Captain Kneebone guides the fugitives downriver; Mrs. Wu, the Pretty Lily, repels an attacking craft. At sea, Rudolph and Miss Drake reflect on Heywood’s influence, until Heywood unexpectedly returns alive in a fishing boat, bringing the survivors’ journey to a joyful close.

Frontispiece _Rudolph was aware of crowded bodies, of yellow faces grinning_ _He let the inverted cup dangle from his hands_ _He went leaping from sight over the crest_ CHAPTER I A LADY AND A GRIFFIN It was "about first-drink time," as the captain of the Tsuen-Chau, bound for Shanghai and Japan ports, observed to his friend Cesare Domenico, a good British subject born at Malta.

Rudolph Hackh travels from Port Said toward the Far East to take up a new position in China. During the voyage, he becomes romantically fascinated by Mrs. Forrester, a woman traveling to Hongkong, but is devastated when he learns she is married. After arriving, Rudolph receives a dispatch recalling Zimmerman and ordering him to take Zimmerman's station and proceed at once.

CHAPTER II THE PIED PIPER A gray smudge trailing northward showed where the Fa-Hien--Scottish Oriental, sixteen hundred tons--was disappearing from the pale expanse of ocean.

Rudolph Hackh arrives at Stink-Chau as the new agent for Fliegelman and Sons and is taken in by Heywood. Heywood introduces him to the isolated European community and brings him to the club, where Rudolph meets Gilly, Wutzler, and Doctor Chantel. That night, rats emerge in large numbers and leap as though struggling against an invisible force, alarming the Europeans. Heywood identifies the phenomenon as the first sign of the plague.

" CHAPTER III UNDER FIRE The desert was sometimes Gobi, sometimes Sahara, but always an infinite stretch of sand that floated up and up in a stifling layer, like the tide.

Rudolph Hackh begins work at the Fliegelman and Sons office in a Chinese city while struggling with fear, disorientation, and the oppressive rain. Heywood introduces him to the foreign community at the club, where Dr. Chantel announces that Monsieur Jolivet has died suddenly of the plague. Mrs. Forrester arrives in panic, and the group responds by singing together despite the danger. Rudolph joins them, recognizing that they are choosing solidarity and bravado while under threat.

CHAPTER IV THE SWORD-PEN "Wutzler was missing last night," said Heywood, lazily.

Heywood takes Rudolph on a holiday excursion by sampan to visit Captain Kneebone, whose steamer is carrying two plague cases among its coolies. After returning to shore, they discover that a property assigned to Rudolph includes a former Portuguese nunnery, but Heywood advises him to remain at Heywood's house for the time being. In a Chinese shop, a drunken or deranged man stabs himself during a confrontation, and the crowd turns hostile after someone calls Heywood a "Foreign Dog" and a "Jesus man." Heywood identifies the instigator as Fang, the Sword-Pen, and says that the Reverend James Earle is the other most dangerous man in the district.

CHAPTER V IN TOWN Night, in that maze of alleys, was but a more sinister day.

Rudolph and Heywood attend Dr. Earle’s Christian chapel service, where Rudolph begins to see the Chinese worshippers as human beings rather than devils or images. They tell Dr. Earle that Au-yoeng killed Chok Chung after hardship, theft, and persecution, and Dr. Earle fears trouble because Fang the Sword-Pen may exploit the incident. The two white men then visit Wutzler, who feeds them and laments his isolation and vulnerability amid rumors of plague and violence. As they leave, gongs and cries fill the town, and Heywood admits that he fears dying there.

" CHAPTER VI THE PAGODA He was spared that inconvenience.

The pestilence ends, and Rudolph Hackh settles into the Portuguese nunnery while studying Chinese and adapting to life in the town. A notice forbidding girls to drown in a pond unsettles him, especially after he sees Chantel riding with a woman who recalls his former attachment. Teppich and Sturgeon take Rudolph to tiffin at the ancient pagoda, where Mrs. Forrester performs, sings, and draws him into a private conversation on the pagoda tower. Maurice Heywood interrupts their moment and later mocks Rudolph's emotional response to Mrs. Forrester's sympathy.

CHAPTER VII IPHIGENIA "Don't chop off a hen's head with a battle-axe.

Rudolph and Mrs. Forrester take a morning ride and investigate a muddy island where abandoned infants have been left. Rudolph rescues a dying infant girl and carries her to the hospital, despite Mrs. Forrester's disgust and objections. Miss Drake receives the child and begins caring for her, while Rudolph learns that the hospital is crowded and poorly funded and that locals accuse it of stealing children.

CHAPTER VIII THE HOT NIGHT Rudolph paced his long chamber like a wolf,--a wolf in summer, with too thick a coat.

Rudolph, tormented by the heat and doubts about the lady, goes to the club, where Dr. Chantel insults her and Rudolph strikes him. Chantel declares that the matter will be settled by a duel, and Heywood explains that Chantel will choose swords, though Rudolph admits he does not know a sword's point from its handle. At dawn, the men witness the Christian merchant Chok Chung being taken to court amid signs of political unrest. Rudolph finally tells Heywood that he no longer cares about the lady, his escape from military service, or the likely consequences of the duel.

" CHAPTER IX PASSAGE AT ARMS "Boy.

Heywood, Rudolph, Nesbit, and Sturgeon go to a deserted garden where Rudolph is supposed to duel Doctor Chantel. While Heywood teaches Rudolph the guard, Heywood deliberately or accidentally wounds Rudolph, then takes his place as Chantel's opponent. Chantel dominates the fencing until a riot breaks out when men from the neighboring hamlet attack with cocoanuts and other missiles. The Europeans drive off the rioters, but Chantel's sword breaks, so the duel must be postponed.

" CHAPTER X THREE PORTALS Not till after dinner, that evening, did Rudolph rouse from his stupor.

Heywood learns from Otto Wutzler, disguised as a coolie, that riots are approaching and that Chok Chung has been brutalized and imprisoned after refusing to renounce Christianity. Wutzler says Fang the scholar, called the Sword-Pen, brought the case and leads a secret White Lotus lodge that is meeting that night to drink blood, recruit members, and set the date of an uprising. Heywood follows Wutzler into the lodge and passes three guarded portals by using passwords and ritual responses. He then descends into a foul crypt and climbs toward a chamber where voices are gathering.

CHAPTER XI WHITE LOTUS He was swarming up, quiet as a thief, when his fingers clawed the bare plaster.

Heywood secretly reaches the rival lodge building and witnesses the Fragrant Ones initiate a group of recruits through an oath, blood-drinking, and ritual ceremony. Fang, the Sword-Pen, asks when the foreign monsters will perish, and the Red Wand says the signal will be the barking of the Black Dog, but Heywood escapes before learning the day. A lodge member attacks him with a spear, but Heywood flees with help from Wutzler, who leads him out of the town. Back at Heywood's house, Rudolph Hackh reveals that he nearly died and discusses the danger facing them.

" CHAPTER XII THE WAR BOARD "Rigmarole?

The War Board debates the rumored threat to the settlement and plans to stock and fortify a defensible house. Heywood argues for using the nunnery as a refuge for the native converts, while Gilbert Forrester resigns as acting head in favor of Heywood. The magistrate visits Heywood, warns indirectly that the settlement must look after itself, and praises the padre's hospital work. The meeting ends with Dr. Earle distressed over the danger facing his flock and Chok Chung, just as Rudolph reports activity on the river.

" CHAPTER XIII THE SPARE MAN Beyond the scant greenery of Heywood's garden--a ropy little banyan, a low rank of glossy whampee leaves, and the dusty sage-green tops of stunted olives--glared the river.

Chok Chung, a Christian fugitive, is pursued down the river by a boat of armed men and shot as he reaches the shallows. Rudolph Hackh is sent to escort Mrs. Forrester home, and their quarrel gives way to mutual admiration after he explains his earlier fight with Chantel. Back at Rudolph's home, Maurice Heywood discovers that most of the Mausers have been disabled by missing side-bolts, apparently taken or thrown away by Peng.

" CHAPTER XIV OFF DUTY The last of the sunlight streamed level through a gap in the western ridges.

Miss Drake and Heywood walk from the hills through a peaceful village and around the town toward the nunnery, discussing their work, dreams, and growing danger. Heywood explains that six stolen guns have been recovered and warns Miss Drake to leave when the next ship arrives, but she says she cannot run away. In the town, they encounter warnings about hostile people and find red placards accusing foreigners of religious crimes and calling for them to be driven beyond the oceans. Miss Drake questions the purpose of their presence and admits that her earlier idealistic view of the land and its people has collapsed.

CHAPTER XV KAU FAI At the top of the nunnery stairs, Rudolph met them with awkward ceremony, and with that smiling air of encouragement which a nurse might use in trying cheerfully to deceive a sick man.

Captain Kneebone arrives at the nunnery with provisions and turns the planned dinner into a lavish banquet. He announces that he won the Derby Sweepstake Lottery, is rich and retired, and gives Miss Drake two thousand rupees while offering Maurice Heywood half of his fortune and a journey Home. During the celebration, Chantel leaves the room, and an attack begins with stones, shouting, gongs, conches, and the cry of "Kau fai." Heywood identifies the disturbance as the Black Dog's attack and organizes the occupants to defend the nunnery while the captain tries to get them to his ship, though Kneebone says he has no ship available.

CHAPTER XVI THE GUNWALE Rudolph's mission began quietly, with a glimpse which he afterward recalled as incredibly peaceful.

Rudolph learns that Bertha and Mrs. Forrester have left during an uprising and runs through fighting and flames to Bertha's house. He finds Bertha distraught and searching through her possessions while Chantel tries to take her by boat down the river. Rudolph follows them to the river but refuses to board, choosing to return toward the burning settlements and the people trapped there. After Wutzler rescues him from the mud, Rudolph hides with him among oil-jars while the attack continues around the nunnery.

CHAPTER XVII LAMP OF HEAVEN The white men peered from among the oil-jars, like two of the Forty Thieves.

Rudolph and Wutzler evade the battle and disturbance by disguising themselves and moving through smoke to Rudolph's go-down. Wutzler uses the clothing and weapons of a dead musketeer, then separates from Rudolph to confuse their pursuers. Inside the go-down, Rudolph witnesses rioters torture a Christian captive by threatening him with the Lamp of Heaven, but rifle fire disrupts the execution. Rudolph reaches Heywood at the pony-shed, rejoins his friends, and reports for duty at the northeast corner, recognizing that he has undergone a decisive transformation.

CHAPTER XVIII SIEGE He never believed that they could hold the northeast corner for a minute, so loud and unceasing was the uproar.

During the Chinese assault, Rudolph, Heywood, Captain Kneebone, and the other defenders hold the northeast corner of the nunnery against an attack involving gunfire, arrows, ladders, and a stink-pot cylinder. Kempner throws the burning cylinder back among the attackers, saving the defenders but dying in the effort, and Rudolph later helps carry his body to the nunnery. Gilly Forrester learns that Bertha has chosen to leave by boat and returns to his post at the water gate. During the continuing siege, Dr. Earle deciphers messages from Wutzler, while Rudolph and Heywood discover that attackers are digging in the clay-pit beneath the defenses.

CHAPTER XIX BROTHER MOLES This new danger, working below in the solid earth, had thrown Rudolph into a state of sullen resignation.

Rudolph and Heywood investigate an enemy tunnel beneath the defending position after learning that the beasts are preparing an underground attack. They discover a mine tunnel containing sacks of powder, capture a frightened coolie, and carry some of the powder away. At the clay-pit, Heywood finds the enemy's main powder supply arranged like a sandbag bulkhead, places a makeshift fuse in it, and retreats with Rudolph. The fuse appears to fail, but the powder later explodes, apparently destroying the enemy's preparations.

CHAPTER XX THE HAKKA BOAT "Pretty fair," Captain Kneebone said.

Captain Kneebone, Heywood, Rudolph, Gilbert Forrester, the padre, and the other fugitives endure repeated attacks on the nunnery while their ammunition runs low. After seeing what they believe to be Wutzler's head displayed above the wall, Heywood insists that the women be evacuated and helps prepare an escape by boat. The Hakka boat secretly approaches the water gate, and the fugitives board it after burying a dying wounded convert. When the boat passes below the wall, Heywood sees Wutzler's body displayed on a bier and Fang the Sword-Pen nearby; he attacks Fang with a halberd and disappears over the high field's crest as the boat escapes downriver.

CHAPTER XXI THE DRAGON'S SHADOW The lowdah would have set his dirty sails without delay, for the fair wind was already drooping; but at the first motion he found himself deposed, and a usurper in command, at the big steering-paddle.

Captain Kneebone takes control of the Hakka boat after Heywood is presumed dead, and the grieving passengers travel down the river toward the sea. The boat is attacked, but Mrs. Wu, called the Pretty Lily, drives off the hostile craft and is credited with saving them. At sea, Rudolph and Miss Drake speak about Heywood and the lessons he left behind, just before Heywood unexpectedly arrives alive in a small fishing boat. The travelers rejoice when Heywood's return awakens the sleeping Captain Kneebone and the others.
