Abstract written by a model

The attacking Chinese forces are a besieging force outside the nunnery, attacking from land, river, walls, fields, and underground. They use guns, arrows, copper cash, ladders, bamboos, a stink-pot cylinder, sniping, dummies, and attacks on both gates, while expecting reinforcements from up country. Their rush at the northeast corner is repelled when their cylinder explodes among them. They continue hiding around the nunnery, digging beneath its defenses, and storing powder near the clay-pit, but the powder is sabotaged and explodes. They later pursue Heywood but cannot stop the Hakka boat reaching midstream.

CHAPTER XVIII SIEGE He never believed that they could hold the northeast corner for a minute, so loud and unceasing was the uproar.

The attacking Chinese forces fire guns, arrows, and copper cash, then rush the northeast corner with ladders, bamboos, and a stink-pot cylinder. Their assault is repelled after the cylinder explodes among them, but they continue to hide around the nunnery and dig beneath the defenses.

CHAPTER XIX BROTHER MOLES This new danger, working below in the solid earth, had thrown Rudolph into a state of sullen resignation.

The enemy forces have been digging underground, sniping from outside, and storing large quantities of powder near the clay-pit. Their powder supply is sabotaged and subsequently explodes.

CHAPTER XX THE HAKKA BOAT "Pretty fair," Captain Kneebone said.

The hostile forces fire from the landward sides, boats in the river, a distant wall, and the high field. They batter both gates, use dummies to draw fire, display Wutzler's remains, and begin pursuing Heywood, but they fail to stop the Hakka boat from reaching midstream.
