Legal analysis generation is a new field and a CLERC task enabled by recent advances in LLMs and RAG, intended to help legal professionals co-write legal analyses. It tests whether models can continue a case document with a realistic next paragraph, using preceding context and salient passages or documents from cited cases, then comparing output with a reference paragraph. Preliminary work uses small datasets and small-scale models. Supplying cited case texts improves citation-related metrics, including Citation Recall, but does not ensure sound results: models may hallucinate, produce factually or analytically unsound analysis, or repeat a definition rather than make the claim a correct citation should support. High citation scores therefore do not establish generation quality.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Introduction

Retrieval-augmented analysis generation is a CLERC task that uses retrieved cases to aid the generation of legal analyses. The text presents it as a system that can help co-write legal analysis in legal documents.

3 / 3.1 .. Input Preparation

Legal analysis generation tests whether language models can produce realistic legal analysis paragraphs. The model receives preceding context and salient passages from cases cited by a target paragraph, and its output is compared with the reference paragraph.

4 .. 4.2.2

Legal analysis generation asks a model to continue a case document by generating its next paragraph using preceding paragraphs and relevant cited passages or documents. Providing the cited case texts improves citation-related metrics, but the resulting analyses can still be factually and analytically unsound.

5 / 5.1 .. 5.2

Analysis generation can fail even when the produced citation is correct. A generated passage may repeat a prior definition instead of stating the analytical claim that the citation supports.
