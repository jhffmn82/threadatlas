the club in Dragon's Blood

Henry Milner Rideout · 1909

The club is a social club in the settlement: a gloomy upper-story loft in Stink-Chau with billiard tables and little comfort, where European residents gather. It first serves as the setting for Rudolph’s introduction to Wutzler, Gilly, and Doctor Chantel, while rats appear outside. On Ladies’ Night it hosts foreign residents for billiards, piano, conversation, and singing, briefly sustaining normality before plague news interrupts. Later, oppressive heat and disorder dominate as men drink, play cards, and quarrel; Rudolph confronts Dr. Chantel there. Its poor facilities, improvised drinking vessels, dead cushions and billiard balls, and no veranda, underscore its decline.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II THE PIED PIPER A gray smudge trailing northward showed where the Fa-Hien--Scottish Oriental, sixteen hundred tons--was disappearing from the pale expanse of ocean.

The club is a gloomy upper-story room in Stink-Chau furnished with billiard tables, where the European residents gather. Rudolph is introduced there to Wutzler, Gilly, and Doctor Chantel, and the group witnesses the rats' appearance outside.

" CHAPTER III UNDER FIRE The desert was sometimes Gobi, sometimes Sahara, but always an infinite stretch of sand that floated up and up in a stifling layer, like the tide.

The club hosts a Ladies' Night gathering attended by foreign residents, including Heywood, Dr. Chantel, Gilly, Mrs. Forrester, and Rudolph. Its billiards, piano, conversation, and communal singing provide a brief appearance of normality before the news of the plague disrupts the evening.

CHAPTER VIII THE HOT NIGHT Rudolph paced his long chamber like a wolf,--a wolf in summer, with too thick a coat.

The club is a suffocating, disorderly place where men drink, play cards, quarrel, and endure the oppressive heat. It becomes the scene of Rudolph's confrontation with Dr. Chantel, and Heywood later disparages it as a poor establishment with jam-pots, dead cushions, dead billiard balls, and no veranda.
