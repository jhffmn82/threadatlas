Abstract written by a model

Brice, also called Ned and Edward Brice, is a young, inexperienced express company employee and stage passenger entrusted with protecting treasure. When the express box is robbed of greenbacks, he obeys Yuba Bill’s order to surrender it, discovers the lock forced and the money missing, and believes the theft occurred while he was at a settler’s cabin. Though unarmed, he doggedly pursues Snapshot Harry, seeking both the outlaw and Flora Tarbox/Flora Dimwood. He survives rifle fire and reaches Harry’s home, where he admits to Henry Dimwood that he concealed his early knowledge of the forced lock and took sole blame. He proposes a recovery plan and travels with Flora, whose presence shields him from the gang’s questions. Guided by Flora along a hidden trail, he finds a dead traveler and recovers the stolen greenbacks from the traveler’s belt. Although Flora urges him to flee with the money, he refuses to profit from the accidental death and carries the treasure back. In San Francisco he tells the company manager the full story, receives a check and promotion from the road to the San Francisco office, and learns Flora’s silence did not mean betrayal. He writes to Flora, gives her his handkerchief, and is her lover and intended husband.

Five minutes after Bill had gathered up the reins, they reached the scene of obs

Brice, the expressman, prepares to defend the express box but obeys Yuba Bill's command to hand it down. He discovers that the lock has been forced and the packet of greenbacks is already missing, then tells Bill that he believes the money was taken while he was at the settler's cabin. After hearing about Snapshot Harry, Brice decides to find him.

II The robbery furnished the usual amount of copy for the local press.

Mr. Ned Brice brings Hiram Tarbox a copy of the Red Dog Clarion containing praise for Hiram's assistance during the robbery, then asks for directions to Flora Tarbox. He follows the mountain trail toward Snapshot Harry's home, survives several deliberately aimed rifle shots, and continues toward the shooter. He tells the shooter that he wants to see Snapshot Harry.

When Brice had partaken of the whiskey, the stranger, who had kept silence, poin

Brice tells Henry Dimwood that he knew the coach's lock had been forced when he handed down the box, but snapped it together again and accepted sole blame. He proposes working with Henry to recover the greenbacks and later travels with Flora, whose company protects him from questioning by the gang.

"I am going to be a real guide to you now," she said suddenly.

Brice follows Flora Dimwood through a concealed trail and helps uncover the dead traveler. He removes the stolen greenbacks from the traveler's treasure belt, then accepts Flora's argument that he should escape with them rather than return immediately to uncle Harry.

III In twenty-four hours Edward Brice was in San Francisco.

Edward Brice reaches San Francisco with the recovered treasure and confides the full story to the manager, who advises him to keep it out of the newspapers. He receives a company check and a promotion to the San Francisco office, then finds Flora and learns that his suspicions about her silence were mistaken.
