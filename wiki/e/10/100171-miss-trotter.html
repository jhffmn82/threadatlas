3 sections; each heading opens the text

Mr. Bilson's Housekeeper

Miss Euphemia Trotter is a poor, hardworking woman who has been a teacher, hospital nurse, and reformatory assistant. As housekeeper, she supervises servants, examines bills and stores, and manages the Summit House with precision and authority. She nurses Chris Calton competently, adjusts his bandages, overlooks his interest in Frida Pauline Jansen, and receives a watch and chain from him in gratitude.

II One afternoon Miss Trotter received a message that Mr.

Miss Trotter tells Jim Calton that she has no right to interfere with a guest's matrimonial intentions and refuses to condemn Frida's conduct without evidence that Frida has harmed her reputation. She agrees to speak with Frida at Chris's request, then discovers Frida with Mr. Bilson in the Summit woods. Although Mr. Bilson's marriage announcement will promote her to full control of the hotel, she feels troubled that her material success is connected with Chris's disappointment.

III When the news of the marriage reached Buckeye Hill, it did not, however, make much scandal, owing, possibly, to the scant number of the sex who are apt to disseminate it, and to many the name of Miss Jansen was unknown.

Miss Trotter becomes an independent woman of means and assumes superior control of the Summit Hotel while Mr. Bilson is absent. She learns that Chris Calton’s apparent courtship of Frida was a deception intended to provoke her attention, and he confesses his love to her. She later changes her dress and becomes less severe socially, resigns from the hotel, and leaves for a foreign Spa, where she avoids meeting Chris.
