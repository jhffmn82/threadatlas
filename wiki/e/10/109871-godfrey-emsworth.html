Abstract written by a model

Godfrey Emsworth, Colonel Emsworth's only son, is a former lance-corporal of B Squadron and a soldier in the Imperial Yeomanry. He served with James M. Dodd in South Africa, where an elephant-gun bullet wounded his shoulder near Diamond Hill outside Pretoria. Afterward he slept in a leper's bed, developed conspicuous white skin patches, and feared leprosy. He stopped writing to Dodd; although the Colonel said he had taken a voyage around the world, he secretly concealed Godfrey in a detached house near the Emsworth home, where Mr. Kent cared for him. Dodd receives letters from Godfrey from Cape Town and Southampton, then sees him furtively near his window and suspects the detached lodge contains him. Godfrey finally appears before Dodd at Tuxbury Old Hall. Sir James Saunders diagnoses non-infective pseudo-leprosy, or ichthyosis, rather than leprosy.

THE ADVENTURE OF THE BLANCHED SOLDIER

Godfrey Emsworth served with James M. Dodd in South Africa and was wounded by an elephant-gun bullet near Diamond Hill outside Pretoria. He later ceased communicating with Dodd, and his father said he had gone on a voyage around the world. At the end of the unit, Godfrey unexpectedly appears before Dodd in his bedroom at Tuxbury Old Hall.

My client had paused as one in deep emotion.

Godfrey appears pale and furtive outside the client's window before fleeing into the darkness and closing a door. The client later sees a seated man in the detached lodge whom he believes is Godfrey, although the man's face is turned away.

Godfrey Emsworth is secretly living in a detached house under the care of Mr. Kent after developing conspicuous whitish patches on his skin. He tells Mr. James M. Dodd that he was wounded in South Africa and later slept in a bed at a leper hospital, after which he feared he had contracted leprosy. Sir James Saunders determines that his condition is pseudo-leprosy or ichthyosis, not infectious leprosy.
