Also called: Self-training, Self-training (or Pseudo-Labeling), self-training approach, self-training in LLM development

Abstract written by a model

Self-training, also called pseudo-labeling, is a semi-supervised learning method that refines large language models with synthetic examples from unlabeled corpora. A teacher model generates labels or pseudo-labeled query, context, and answer tuples; a student or fine-tuned LLM is then trained on the generated, filtered samples, progressively improving outputs. It is used for text classification, natural language understanding, ranking, instruction fine-tuning, reasoning, alignment, and domain-adaptive fine-tuning. Its major drawback is label noise, mitigated by sample selection and reweighting.

Introduction

Self-training inspires SimRAG's use of synthetic examples generated from unlabeled corpora to refine a model. In SimRAG, the model progressively improves its outputs by being fine-tuned on examples it generates and filters.

Related Work

Self-training, also called pseudo-labeling, uses a teacher model to generate new labels on which a student model is fitted. It has been applied to multiple NLP tasks and, in SimRAG, is used for domain-adaptive fine-tuning in a retrieval-augmented generation setting.

Methodology / Problem Setup .. Stage-II: Domain Adaptive Fine-tuning

Self-training uses the fine-tuned LLM to generate pseudo-labeled query, context, and answer tuples from specialized-domain text. The generated samples are filtered before being used for further training.
