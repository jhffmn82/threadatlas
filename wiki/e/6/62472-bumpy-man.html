Bumpy Man in The Scarecrow of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1915 · the work’s page · read it where Bumpy Man first appears

Abstract written by a model

Bumpy Man, known by reputation as a Mountain Ear, lives in a stone house on the mountain-top and has bumps covering him from birth; they predict weather. With kind, twinkling eyes, he serves the mountain-top and welcomes Trot and her companions, offering rain-collected lemonade and molasses candy. He warns of snow, shelters them by the fire, then shovels paths, helps uncover Button-Bright, and gives Cap'n Bill an old gray suit for swings.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Seven

The Bumpy Man welcomes the travelers into his stone house, prepares hot molasses candy, and gives them lemonade. He says his bumps predict the weather and warns that a snowstorm is coming, then keeps the fire burning while the travelers sleep.

Chapter Eight

The Bumpy Man explains that winter has arrived and that Mo snow is popcorn. He shovels a path, helps uncover Button-Bright, provides hospitality, and gives Cap'n Bill an old gray suit to use in making swings.

Mountain Ear in The Scarecrow of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1915 · the work’s page · read it where Mountain Ear first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Bumpy Man, known as the Mountain Ear, guards the mountain’s stability by listening to natural sounds and warning it of blizzards, rain, and snow, keeping it steady from uneasiness, quaking, or spouting.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter Seven

The Bumpy Man is known throughout the Land of Mo as the Mountain Ear. He listens to sounds made by nature and tells the mountain about blizzards, rain, and snow so that it will remain steady and not quake or spout.
