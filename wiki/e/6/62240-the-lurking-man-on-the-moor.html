the lurking man on the moor in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901/1902

The entity is a secret stranger, a tall, thin, apparently gentlemanly man lurking on the moor. He hides and lives in abandoned stone huts on the hillside, with Spartan habits and little concern for comfort. Others watch for and seek him after Selden reports seeing him among the huts. He is supplied with food by a boy who serves as his helper and messenger. While Watson first sees him watching from a tor and investigators try to locate him, the stranger has Watson observed through an agent. In the end, he comes to the hut and speaks to Watson.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 10.

The stranger on the moor is a tall, thin figure whom Watson saw watching from a tor. Barrymore reports that Selden had seen another apparently gentlemanly man hiding among the old stone huts, suggesting that this stranger is still watching and waiting.

Chapter 11.

The narrator has established that the lurking man on the moor is to be sought among the stone huts on the hillside. After questioning Mrs. Laura Lyons without reaching a conclusion, he turns back to investigating this man.

And that was a most vague direction. I realised it as I drove

The stranger lives in an abandoned hut on the moor and receives food brought by the boy. He has apparently arranged for Watson's movements to be observed and reported, and he eventually comes to the hut and speaks to Watson.
