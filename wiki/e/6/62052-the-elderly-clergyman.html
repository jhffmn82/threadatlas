Abstract written by a model

An elderly, expressive clergyman and father who values reasonable activity but despises foolhardy bravado. On the sands, he warns two young people away from a dangerous route near Devil's Throat, notes that his daughters are safely climbing Asparagus Rock, discusses his views with the narrator, and becomes a friendly companion for a time.

DAY THE SEVENTH John Curgenven had said last night, with his air of tender patronising, half regal, half paternal, which we declared always reminded us of King Arthur--"Ladies, whenever you settle to go to Kynance, I'll take you.

The elderly clergyman waits on the sands and calls out warnings to two young people attempting a dangerous route near Devil's Throat. He explains that his own daughters are safely climbing Asparagus Rock and says that he approves of reasonable activity but despises foolhardy bravado. He and the narrator discuss these views and become friendly companions for a time.
