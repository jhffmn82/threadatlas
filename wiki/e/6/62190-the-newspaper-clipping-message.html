the newspaper clipping message in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901/1902

A message assembled from words cut from The Times’s leading article on free trade and pasted onto foolscap. Holmes identifies its source by type, notes two phrases were cut together, and explains that “moor” was handwritten because unavailable in print.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4.

The newspaper clipping message was formed by cutting words from the Times’s leading article on free trade and pasting them onto a blank half-sheet of foolscap. Holmes identifies the source from the distinctive type, observes that “keep away” and “from the” were cut as single pieces, and concludes that “moor” was written in ink because it could not be found in print.
