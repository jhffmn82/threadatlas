Sophocles

person · 4 works · 9 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Sophocles', the poet

Abstract written by a model

In The Electra of Euripides, Sophocles deliberately sought an archaic Homeric style, reacting against Euripides through more classical convention and heroic spirits while avoiding matricide’s dark implications. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Sophocles, son of Theophilus, was a tragedian who won eighteen stage victories and died at about ninety. In The Library, Sophocles wrote Troilus, in which he referred to the marriage of Peleus and Thetis as voiceless or silent; a fragment attributed to him also says that Thetis deserted Peleus because she was reproached by him. In Description of Greece, Sophocles was a great light of Tragedy whose statue stood in the theatre; after his death, a dream reportedly honoured him and his plays as the new Siren.

Introduction[1]

Sophocles deliberately sought what he regarded as an archaic or Homeric style. His treatment is described as more classical and even more primitive than Aeschylus', preserving heroic good spirits while avoiding the dark moral and psychological implications of matricide.

CHAPTER XIX.

Sophocles was regarded as one of the great lights of Tragedy, and his statue stood in the theatre. After his death, a Lacedæmonian leader reportedly dreamed that Dionysus commanded him to honour the new Siren, which was understood to refer to Sophocles and his plays.
