Bossinney Cove in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

Bossinney Cove is a pleasant, shut-in cove, visited three times daily by an old sand-collecting man and his eleven named donkeys. During Arthurian days, the narrator and companions bathe there, remembering its steep dangerous path, secluded water, and patient man and animals.

DAYS FOURTEENTH, FIFTEENTH, AND SIXTEENTH-- And all Arthurian days, so I will condense them into one chapter, and not spin out the hours that were flying so fast.

The narrator and her companions visit Bossinney Cove for a sea bath and encounter an old man who collects sand there with eleven named donkeys. The cove is remembered for its steep, dangerous rock-path, its secluded water, and the patient old man and animals.
