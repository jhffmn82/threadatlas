Arthurian chivalry in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

Arthurian chivalry is an ideal that begins by enjoining courage, honour, and chivalrous love of woman, and unfolds as faith, chastity, worship of God, service to humanity, reverence for women, conscience, redressing wrongs, avoiding slander, and fidelity.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

DAYS FOURTEENTH, FIFTEENTH, AND SIXTEENTH-- And all Arthurian days, so I will condense them into one chapter, and not spin out the hours that were flying so fast.

Arthurian chivalry is described as an ideal of courage, honour, faith, chastity, worship of God, service to humanity, and reverence for women. The narrator associates it with an ideal knight who follows conscience, redresses wrongs, avoids slander, and remains faithful to one beloved.
