  • In The Scarecrow of Oz: Ork, personally named Flipper, is a strange, harmless, intelligent winged creature from Orkland. Neither clearly fish nor beast, it is birdlike and brilliantly plumaged, with four stork-like legs, tough-skinned inverted chopping-bowl wings, a parrot-shaped head and curved beak, scarlet crest, finger-like claws, and a mechanical, whirling propeller tail used for flight. It is about as heavy as Cap'n Bill, can carry several people, and flies when going somewhere, though walking painfully injures its feet on rocks. Water is necessary to it, yet it is a natural enemy of water and once escaped a whirlpool and cavern with mermaids' help. Flipper's central aim is to find and return to Orkland, where its family and friends live and where Orks rule living things. Restless and widely traveled, it searches the world for its home while becoming a loyal companion to Trot and Cap'n Bill. After emerging from a pool, it introduces itself, explores and guides them through a tunnel, and flies them out. It helps them cross an island and ocean, uses berries to shrink and transport them in Trot's sunbonnet, and lands with them on a mountain in an unfamiliar country. It then leads travelers, including enlarged birds, toward and beyond the sandy desert before leaving to seek Orkland, promising to return if unsuccessful. It returns from Orkland after a welcome and celebration, rescues Button-Bright, and chooses to aid the conquest of King Krewl before returning home again. Later, as an Ork leader among other Orks, it helps carry travelers over mountains into the Land of Oz, carrying Trot and departing with the other Orks after landing them.

Ork in The Scarecrow of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1915

Ork, personally named Flipper, is a strange, harmless, intelligent winged creature from Orkland. Neither clearly fish nor beast, it is birdlike and brilliantly plumaged, with four stork-like legs, tough-skinned inverted chopping-bowl wings, a parrot-shaped head and curved beak, scarlet crest, finger-like claws, and a mechanical, whirling propeller tail used for flight. It is about as heavy as Cap'n Bill, can carry several people, and flies when going somewhere, though walking painfully injures its feet on rocks. Water is necessary to it, yet it is a natural enemy of water and once escaped a whirlpool and cavern with mermaids' help. Flipper's central aim is to find and return to Orkland, where its family and friends live and where Orks rule living things. Restless and widely traveled, it searches the world for its home while becoming a loyal companion to Trot and Cap'n Bill. After emerging from a pool, it introduces itself, explores and guides them through a tunnel, and flies them out. It helps them cross an island and ocean, uses berries to shrink and transport them in Trot's sunbonnet, and lands with them on a mountain in an unfamiliar country. It then leads travelers, including enlarged birds, toward and beyond the sandy desert before leaving to seek Orkland, promising to return if unsuccessful. It returns from Orkland after a welcome and celebration, rescues Button-Bright, and chooses to aid the conquest of King Krewl before returning home again. Later, as an Ork leader among other Orks, it helps carry travelers over mountains into the Land of Oz, carrying Trot and departing with the other Orks after landing them.

Narrative, section by section

11 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Two

The strange winged creature emerges from the pool the morning after Trot and Cap'n Bill arrive. It is neither clearly a fish nor a beast, has four legs, tough-skinned wings, a parrot-like head and beak, and a scarlet crest of wavy plumes. It is as large as Cap'n Bill and causes both of them to stare in wonder and fear.

Chapter Three

The Ork introduces himself as a harmless, intelligent creature named Flipper. He says Orks rule all living things in Orkland, and he can fly using four small wings and a propeller-like tail. He explores the gulf below the tunnel, carries Cap'n Bill and Trot to a distant passage, and guides them through the tunnel, though walking causes severe pain in his feet.

Chapter Four

The Ork recognizes that the tunnel is nearing its end and uses its mechanical propeller tail to carry Trot and Cap'n Bill out of the shaft. After landing, it helps determine that the surrounding land may be an island, eats melon, and flies over the forest because its feet are sore and lumpy from walking on rocks.

Chapter Five

The Ork arrives in a tiny form after eating a lavender berry and tells Trot, Cap'n Bill, and Pessim how it was affected. It regains its natural size by eating a deep purple berry and then suggests using the same berries to shrink Trot and Cap'n Bill for transport. The Ork carries them away from the island in Trot's sunbonnet tied around its neck.

Chapter Six

The Ork carries Cap'n Bill and Trot in the sunbonnet over the ocean, flying steadily in a straight line while searching for land. It finds an unfamiliar country, lands on a mountain peak, and struggles to untie the sunbonnet because its claws cannot easily reach the strings.

Chapter Seven

The Ork flies to the top of the mountain to investigate and calls for Trot and Cap'n Bill to join him. He explains that he has traveled widely while searching for his home, eats some molasses candy, refuses the lemonade, and says that water is necessary for life.

Chapter Eight

The Ork is surprised that the birds are covered with feathers and later helps plan the travelers' escape from the mountain. It leads the three enlarged birds and the other travelers toward the sandy desert.

Chapter Nine

The Ork leads the flight into the country beyond the great sandy desert. It leaves the travelers to search for its home on the other side of the desert, promising to return in a week if it does not find Orkland.

Chapter Seventeen

The Ork returns from Orkland after receiving a grand welcome and celebration. It rescues Button-Bright by carrying him through the air to Pon's hut, then decides to help conquer King Krewl before flying home again.

Chapter Twenty

The Ork leader helps the Scarecrow and his companions cross the mountains into the Land of Oz. He carries Trot on his back and leaves with the other Orks immediately after landing the travelers.

Chapter Twenty-One

An Ork is a strange flying creature that carries Button-Bright, Trot, and Cap'n Bill into Jinxland. A flock of Orks later saves the Scarecrow from being nearly burned up, and four Orks carry the mortals toward the Land of Oz.
