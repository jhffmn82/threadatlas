  • In Rinkitink in Oz: King Rinkitink is a very fat, jolly, talkative, kind-hearted but careless king associated with Gilgad and the Kingdom or Dominion of Rinkitink. He wears rich purple robes and a jeweled cap or crown, dislikes walking and hard work, and usually rides Bilbil, his talking goat, longtime companion, servant, and friend. Though inexperienced at fighting, lacking magic, and inclined to run from danger, he is loyal to Prince Inga and tries to sustain others with jokes, songs, gifts, and tenderness. He keeps a parchment called "How to be Good," reads it often, and considers himself a shrewd philosopher. He secretly leaves his kingdom and arrives as King Kitticut's royal guest on Pingaree, bringing presents and spending a comfortable time eating, talking, sleeping, and reading. When invaders attack, he escapes captivity by falling into a well; Inga and Bilbil rescue him. He then joins Inga's effort to save Pingaree, helps where he can, and accompanies him by boat to Regos. Although fearful, he enters the conquered city with Inga, humorously claims credit for victory, and accidentally loses one of Inga's magic shoes by throwing it at a cat. Queen Cor captures him after seizing Inga, makes him her butler, and threatens him, but he sings defiant comic songs. After Inga regains magic, Rinkitink helps comfort the freed women and supports further efforts to rescue King Kitticut and Pingaree's people. He travels through Regos and then the difficult Nome Country, protected by the Pink Pearl while riding Bilbil, surviving Kaliko's traps and hazards and rejoining Inga. After reunion with Kitticut, he is treated comfortably by Kaliko, attends Ozma's banquet, and celebrates Bilbil's restoration as Prince Bobo. He returns with the party to Pingaree, reflects that life brings reversals of fortune, and remains a guest until Gilgad's fleet arrives. Though he fears being compelled to resume ruling, he accepts his return after three days of feasting, departs for Gilgad with Prince Bobo, and sings a farewell song.

Narrative, section by section

20 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Two

King Rinkitink arrives at Pingaree after secretly running away from his kingdom. He is a fat, cheerful, talkative king who brings gifts, rides Bilbil, and reads from the parchment entitled "How to be Good."

Chapter Three

King Rinkitink remains on Pingaree, spending his time eating, talking with King Kitticut, sleeping, and reading from his scroll. He follows King Kitticut and the others outside when the invasion begins, but he is not found among the captives.

Chapter Four

King Rinkitink falls into the well while fleeing the invaders and remains there with his head above the water. Prince Inga and Bilbil pull him out, after which he helps explore Pingaree and rides on Bilbil's back.

Chapter Five

King Rinkitink fears that he and his companions may be imprisoned on Pingaree and eventually starve, but he helps move a marble block by using his weight. He jokes frequently, asks Bilbil for stories, and tries to cheer Inga despite their misfortunes. Inga tells him only that they will leave the island in a boat that is expected to arrive.

Chapter Six

King Rinkitink wants to use the boat to return to Gilgad, but agrees to accompany Inga if Inga can row it to Regos. He cannot move the boat by rowing, enjoys singing during the voyage, and admits that he has never tried fighting and usually prefers to run away from danger.

Chapter Seven

King Rinkitink accompanies Prince Inga to Regos to demand restitution for Pingaree. He fears capture but remains unharmed while touching Bilbil, witnesses the defeat of the warriors, and crosses into the conquered city.

Chapter Eight

King Rinkitink enters conquered Regos with Inga and Bilbil, rejoicing in his safety and jokingly taking credit for the victory. He accidentally throws Inga's left shoe at a cat during the night, causing the loss of the Pink Pearl, and later helps search for both shoes.

Chapter Ten

King Rinkitink is with Inga when Queen Cor arrives and tries to rescue him after Cor seizes the Prince, but she kicks him and has him bound. In Coregos, Cor threatens to use him as a pincushion or tickle his feet, so he sings a song about a baby tiger and warns against fooling with tigers.

Chapter Twelve

King Rinkitink is identified as one of Bilbil's friends, although Bilbil can sometimes be harsh and disagreeable toward him. He is not otherwise present in the events of this unit.

Chapter Thirteen

King Rinkitink serves as Queen Cor's butler and is distressed when Inga is beaten. He accidentally spills coffee on Queen Cor and later collides with Bilbil while chasing the fleeing queen. After learning that Inga has recovered his magic, he helps comfort the freed women and gives Zella money for her honey.

Chapter Fourteen

Rinkitink agrees to help Inga rescue King Kitticut and the people of Pingaree. He travels to Regos with Inga, Bilbil, and Zella, sings a song aboard the boat, and suggests that King Gos may have carried King Kitticut to Regos.

Chapter Sixteen

King Rinkitink advises the others to make use of Nikobob's wisdom and observes that Nikobob may understand life better than they had thought. He says that he enjoys being a king, though a jeweled crown is heavy in hot weather.

Chapter Eighteen

King Rinkitink accompanies Inga to the Nome Country even though he dislikes the difficult mountain journey. He receives the Pink Pearl for protection, jokes with King Kaliko, and allows Bilbil to throw Kaliko from his back; later, Rinkitink is separated from Inga by Kaliko's trick.

King Rinkitink is seated astride Bilbil when Inga reaches the inhabited cavern. He appears pleased that Inga has rejoined him.

Chapter Nineteen

Rinkitink follows Klik to Kaliko's royal cavern and safely escapes falling rocks, a magical golden net, a Bottomless Gulf pit, and hundreds of flying knives. He rides Bilbil to remain protected by the Pink Pearl, chuckles at Kaliko's failures, and becomes alarmed when Kaliko reveals that Inga has been facing the Three Trick Caverns.

Chapter Twenty

King Rinkitink escapes with Inga and the talking goat after Pingaree is destroyed. He follows Inga into the Nome King's country and the caverns, where Dorothy sees him facing danger with Inga.

Chapter Twenty-One

Rinkitink is treated well by Kaliko and is in no hurry to leave because he has food and a comfortable bed. He joyfully reunites with King Kitticut and thanks him for supporting Inga.

Chapter Twenty Two

King Rinkitink is given a new costume and attends Ozma's banquet. He is pleased that Bilbil has become Prince Bobo, enjoys the people and animals of Oz, and composes and sings songs about Hank the mule and the party's victories.

Chapter Twenty Three

Rinkitink travels with the royal party from Oz back to Pingaree and remains there as a guest. He misses Bilbil but does not regret Bilbil’s disenchantment because he is fond of Prince Bobo. Rinkitink tells King Kitticut that life’s sudden changes bring both ups and downs, then sings his grandfather’s song about reversals of fortune.

Chapter Twenty-Four

Rinkitink recognizes the approaching fleet as belonging to his people of Gilgad and fears being forced to return to rule. He agrees to leave after three days of feasting with King Kitticut, then departs for Gilgad with Prince Bobo and sings a farewell song.
