Troaedes

Also called: _Troaedes_, the _Troaedes_

Abstract written by a model

Troaedes is an imperfect play, produced in spring of 416 B.C. after the fall and conquest of Melos, with little plot, construction, relief, or variety. Built around conquered women, it intensely studies a great situation after battle, when misery, waiting, and thought remain. Its only dramatic movement gradually extinguishes familiar lights of human life, through four heroic women and less fully characterized female figures. More than art, it is prophecy and bearing of witness, expressing pity for mankind.

INTRODUCTORY NOTE

The Troaedes is described as an imperfect play with little plot, construction, relief, or variety, whose movement consists largely of the extinguishing of the familiar lights of human life. It portrays conquest after the thrill of battle has ended, when misery, waiting, and thought remain, and it centers on four clearly lit and heroic conquered women alongside less fully characterized female figures. The play is also presented as a prophecy and a bearing of witness, expressing pity for mankind.
