Mrs. Laura Lyons in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901/1902

Mrs. Laura Lyons is a woman living in Coombe Tracey, the estranged daughter of Frankland, and the owner of a typewriting business established with local assistance. In an unhappy, persecuting marriage, she seeks freedom through a projected divorce and is largely supported by Sir Charles Baskerville’s kindness, as well as assistance from Stapleton. She corresponds with and has met Sir Charles, writing him a letter requesting help with divorce-related expenses. Her letter asks him to meet her at the gate on the day of his death, connecting her to the murder inquiry. Initially she denies writing it, then says she did not attend because help came from another source. She is involved intimately with Jack Stapleton and expects to marry him, believing him unmarried. Stapleton manipulates her: he dictates the letter, claims money is needed for her divorce, promises marriage, prevents her from keeping the appointment, conceals that he is married, and threatens her into silence. Once the truth is made clear, she admits Stapleton’s role and agrees to reveal what she knows, helping clarify the affair surrounding Sir Charles’s death.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 10.

Laura Lyons is identified as the woman whose initials match L. L. She lives in Coombe Tracey, is Frankland’s estranged daughter, and may possess information capable of clarifying an incident in the mystery surrounding Sir Charles’s death.

Chapter 11.

Mrs. Laura Lyons is questioned about her relationship with Sir Charles Baskerville and initially denies writing to him on the day of his death. She later admits that she wrote asking him to meet her, explaining that she wanted financial help with expenses connected to regaining her freedom from her husband. She insists that she never went to the appointment because she received help from another source, though the narrator suspects that she is withholding part of the truth.

Chapter 12.

Mrs. Laura Lyons is questioned by Dr. John Watson in Coombe Tracey, and her conversation helps clarify the case. She has an intimate relationship with Jack Stapleton and, while expecting to become his wife after a projected divorce, does not know that he is already married.

“We’re at close grips at last,” said Holmes as we walked together

Sherlock Holmes has great hopes that Mrs. Laura Lyons will help once the position of affairs is made clear to her.

Chapter 13.

Mrs. Laura Lyons admits that Mr. Stapleton dictated her letter asking Sir Charles to meet her at the gate and then persuaded her not to attend. She explains that Stapleton claimed the money was needed for her divorce, concealed his marriage, and threatened her into silence; she now agrees to hold nothing back.

Chapter 15.

Stapleton misrepresented himself as a single man to influence Mrs. Laura Lyons and promised to marry her if she obtained a divorce. He induced her to write to Sir Charles requesting an interview, then prevented her from attending it so that Sir Charles would be exposed.
