engine

Also called: engine or engines, engines, engines and boilers, steam engines

Abstract written by a model

An engine is chosen by first cost, fuel, operating hours, lubrication, repairs, depreciation, and boiler requirements. Multiple engines save large-diameter shafting expense and subdivide work. For large powers it is usually horizontal; an illustrative 100-horse-power case consumes five pounds of coal per hour per indicated horse-power.

ECONOMICAL STEAM POWER.

An engine should be selected by considering its first cost, fuel consumption, operating hours, lubrication, repairs, depreciation, and boiler requirements. Le Van states that engines driving manufactories generally cannot be too high a class because fuel savings usually outweigh the interest on their additional first cost.
