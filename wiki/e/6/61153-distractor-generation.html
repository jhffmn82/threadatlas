Abstract written by a model

Distractor Generation is an NLP task and research direction for producing distractors for multiple-choice questions, aiming to reduce educators’ manual creation time. It has two main research directions: a Generating and Ranking framework and Text2Text generation, with recent Text2Text work described as state of the art and Knowledge Graph integration relatively limited. The paper applies task-specific pretraining and knowledge augmentation to train or condition models for this downstream task. Evaluated on SciQ and MCQ datasets with F1, other automatic metrics, and human judgments of relevance, distractiveness, and utility, RAP, Knowledge Augmented Generation, and related methods improve generated distractor quality over baselines and reportedly outperform state-of-the-art results.

Abstract

The paper tackles distractor generation for multiple-choice questions. Its models are evaluated on benchmarking datasets and are reported to outperform state-of-the-art results.

Introduction

Automatic distractor generation is studied as a way to reduce the time and effort educators spend manually creating MCQs. The paper improves existing DG methods through task-specific pretraining and knowledge augmentation.

2 / 2.1 .. 2.3

Distractor Generation research is divided into the Generating and Ranking framework and the Text2Text generation architecture. Recent work using the Text2Text architecture represents the state of the art, while Knowledge Graph integration remains relatively limited.

3 / 3.1 .. 3.2.3

Distractor Generation is the downstream task for which the described pretraining and knowledge augmentation methods are used. The methods train or condition a model to generate distractors for a multiple-choice question.

4 / 4.1 / Sciq dataset .. 4.6

Distractor generation is evaluated using automatic metrics and human judgments of relevance, distractiveness, and utility. The experiments find that RAP, Knowledge Augmented Generation, and related methods can improve the quality of generated distractors over baseline systems.
