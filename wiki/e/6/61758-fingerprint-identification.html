fingerprint identification in Scientific American Supplement No. 360, November 25, 1882

Various · 1881

Fingerprint identification uses finger, thumb, and palm impressions as sign manuals or document attestations. Because finger rugae remain stable from cradle to grave, these marks resist personation and repudiation, including when a person cannot write in sales of children or women.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE CHINESE SIGN MANUAL.

Fingerprint identification is represented by the use of finger, thumb, and palm impressions as sign manuals. The text considers such marks difficult to forge or repudiate because the finger rugae remain substantially unchanged from the cradle to the grave.
