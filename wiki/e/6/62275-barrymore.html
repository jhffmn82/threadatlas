Barrymore in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901/1902

Barrymore is the butler and servant in charge of Baskerville Hall, where he lives with his wife, Mrs. or Eliza Barrymore. Tall and handsome with a full black beard, he comes from the former caretaker’s family and says that his family has served the Baskervilles for generations, even over a hundred years. He and his wife were attached to Sir Charles Baskerville, whose body Barrymore first discovered, and they consider leaving the hall because it is painful after Sir Charles’s death. When Sir Henry Baskerville and Dr. Watson arrive, Barrymore receives them, handles their luggage, offers dinner, and shows them their rooms and the dining room. Yet his black beard, the uncertain telegram delivery, his wife’s concealed crying, and his nighttime movements make him suspicious. Though he says he received the telegram, Mrs. Barrymore brought it to him and wrote his dictated reply. Watson sees him barefoot at night with a candle, watching the moor from a western window. Barrymore is eventually found using the candle as a signal. He admits that he and Eliza have been secretly feeding Selden, her brother and Barrymore’s brother-in-law, while concealing him from the police. He asks Sir Henry not to report Selden, fearing the effect on his wife and believing Selden will leave for South America. Sir Henry permits the couple to remain. Barrymore also reveals that Sir Charles received a letter from L. L. and went to the gate to meet her, and tells Watson that another man is hiding on the moor.

Narrative, section by section

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5.

Barrymore is the son of the former caretaker and has served the Baskervilles for generations with his wife. Holmes suspects that his appearance could match the black-bearded spy, but a telegram confirms that Barrymore is at Baskerville Hall.

Chapter 6.

Barrymore receives Sir Henry Baskerville and Dr. Watson at Baskerville Hall, helps with their luggage, and offers to serve dinner. He explains that he and his wife may leave when it is convenient because they were attached to Sir Charles Baskerville and find the house painful after his death. He shows Sir Henry and Dr. Watson to their rooms and the dining room.

Chapter 7.

Barrymore tells Sir Henry that the woman’s sob heard during the night could not have come from his wife, although Watson later sees that Mrs. Barrymore has clearly been crying. The postmaster confirms that the telegram was delivered to Mrs. Barrymore while Barrymore was supposedly in the loft, leaving no proof that Barrymore had not been in London.

Chapter 8.

Barrymore confirms that he received the test telegram, though his wife brought it to him and wrote the reply after he instructed her what to say. Watson later sees him walking barefoot through Baskerville Hall at night, holding a candle to a window and staring expectantly across the moor before extinguishing the light.

Chapter 9.

Barrymore is known to walk through the passages at night and is seen watching from the western window of Baskerville Hall. Sir Henry and Dr. Watson suspect that he is looking for someone or something on the moor.

So there is one of our small mysteries cleared up. It is

John Barrymore is discovered holding a candle at a second-floor window as a signal. He admits that he has been helping his wife feed Selden, and Sir Henry allows him and Eliza to remain in service after hearing the explanation.

Chapter 10.

Barrymore asks Sir Henry not to report Selden because he believes Selden will leave for South America and fears that his capture would harm Barrymore’s wife. He reveals that Sir Charles received a letter from a woman with the initials L. L. and had gone to the gate to meet her, and he later tells Watson that another man is hiding on the moor.
