Kynance Cove in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

Kynance Cove is a Cornwall place about two miles beyond Gull Rock and Asparagus Island. The party first considers rowing there but postpones the visit for daylight. On the seventh day, John Curgenven leads them by a path across the downs and a steep descent to its richly colored rocks, Drawing-room, Dining-room, and Kitchen caves, Asparagus Island, the Bellows, and Gull Rock; the advancing tide makes its attractions inaccessible.

Kynance in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

Kynance is a coastal place with a cove and rock chambers, accessible at low water, called a kitchen and drawing-room; it is valued for especially perfect natural bathing places. On the third day it cannot be visited because the tide will not suit until Wednesday or Thursday. On the fourth, it lies west along the cliff path from Lizard, visible in moonlight beyond smooth green stretches and steep, hazardous ones. By the eighth day, its waves are heard at midnight and its Devil's Throat is recalled.

Kynance lies west of the narrator's position along the cliff path from Lizard. Its cove is visible in the moonlight, and the path toward it is described as sometimes smooth and green but elsewhere steep and hazardous.

Kynance is visible from the moonlit Lizard coast, and the women recall the clerical friend's daughters visiting it without falling into the Devil's Throat. The narrator mentions hearing the waves at Kynance during her midnight walk.
