the coastguard in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

The coastguard is a group of picked service men, fearless, faithful, steady, and courageous. Accustomed to traveling the coast at all hours, they guide themselves by white-washed rocks on dark nights and help in wreck rescues. Their winter occupation is risky and tolerably well paid, though no money fully compensates for what they endure.

"Shall we get a boat?

The coast-guard men are described as steady, courageous, and accustomed to traveling the coast at all hours. They help with wreck rescues and use the white-washed coastal rocks as guides on dark nights.

The coastguard men are described as picked men of the service who lead a risky life during winter rescues. Their work is said to be tolerably well paid, but no money could fully compensate them for what they endure.
