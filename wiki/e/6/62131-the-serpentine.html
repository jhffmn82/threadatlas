the serpentine in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

Serpentine is a reddish and greenish stone found along the coast near the Lizard. It is used for cottage walls, fences, a stile, and polished ornaments and architectural objects, including candlesticks, brooches, mantelpieces, tombstones, and studs. It is the district’s principal commerce; at Poltesco it is fashioned into delicate, sometimes Pompeian forms, while Cadgwith rocks range from red and green to grey.
