Hank in Tik-Tok of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1914 · the work’s page · read it where Hank first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hank is Betsy Bobbin’s little, thin, sad-faced mule and donkey companion: shaggy, big-eared, long-tailed, harsh-voiced, short-tempered, but faithful, loyal, and protective. Thrown into the sea with Betsy, he swims to her raft, comforts her, promises to protect her, and remains awake while she sleeps. Ashore, his braying and temper bring conflict with the Royal Gardener and Roses; he eats an apple core, kicks the Gardener from the greenhouse, and leaves with Betsy, Shaggy Man, and Princess Ozga. He carries Betsy when she is tired, investigates a well, scatters Oogaboo’s officers with his heels, crosses the dry-water brook, and survives a rapid slide through the Hollow Tube with Betsy on his back. He later enters the Nome King’s cavern with Betsy and knocks down General Guph and the Nome King. After leaving the cavern, he reaches Oz, where he receives a cushioned stable stall. There he learns to speak animal speech, proudly defends Betsy, and is reconciled with the Sawhorse, Hungry Tiger, and Cowardly Lion.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

11 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Four

Hank, a thin, sad-faced mule, is thrown into the sea with Betsy. He swims to the raft, and Betsy helps him climb aboard. He comforts Betsy, promises to protect her, and stays awake through the night while she sleeps.

Chapter Five

Hank follows Betsy ashore and into the greenhouse. His loud braying startles the Roses and causes the Royal Gardener to throw a watering pot over his head; Hank becomes angry and backs toward the Gardener threateningly.

Chapter Six

Hank eats Shaggy Man's apple core and kicks the Royal Gardener out of the greenhouse. He is affectionate toward Shaggy Man when he sees the Love Magnet and helps resist the Gardener and the Roses during the expulsion. He leaves the Rose Kingdom with Shaggy Man, Betsy, and Princess Ozga.

Chapter Seven

Hank travels with the group and carries Betsy when she becomes tired. At the crossroads he investigates the old well by looking into it and braying, helping draw attention to something at its bottom.

Chapter Eight

Hank attacks the Army of Oogaboo by backing into the officers and kicking at them, scattering them and making them drop their swords. Betsy later holds him back by his forelock.

Chapter Nine

Hank tries to bounce across the brook but falls into the middle of it. He reaches the far bank and is found to be completely dry because the water is dry.

Chapter Ten

Hank pitches forward and slides rapidly down the Hollow Tube with Betsy clinging to his back. He cannot stop, but he lands on all four feet and is only slightly jarred; during the journey he gives a sympathetic 'Hee-haw!'

Chapter Sixteen

Hank accompanies Betsy Bobbin into the Nome King's cavern. His heels strike General Guph, sending him into the Nome King and knocking both of them down.

Chapter Seventeen

Hank is believed by Ruggedo to be in the Slimy Cave with Betsy Bobbin, but the cave is empty when the executioners search it.

Chapter Twenty-Four

Hank leaves the Nome King's cavern with Betsy Bobbin and the others. The Wizard transports him to the Land of Oz, where Ozma plans a golden stall for him and hopes he will become friends with the Cowardly Lion and the Hungry Tiger.

Chapter Twenty-Five

Hank is given a stall and cushions in Ozma's palace stable and learns that he can speak after entering the Land of Oz. He proudly defends Betsy Bobbin as the sweetest girl and is reconciled with the Sawhorse, Hungry Tiger, and Cowardly Lion.

Hank the Mule in The Lost Princess of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1917 · the work’s page · read it where Hank the Mule first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hank the Mule is a lean, bony, plain-looking but faithful and steady mule, a great fighter who never gets lost. Before entering the Land of Oz he lost his bray; afterward he can talk. Pricked by thistles, he retreats, then trembles across their field to safety and argues with the Woozy about beauty.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 8

Hank the Mule is pricked by the thistles and backs away from them. He later rides over the thistle field, trembling and nearly falling, but reaches the others safely.

CHAPTER 10

Hank recalls that he once lost his bray and found it uncomfortable because he could not make a noise. He argues with the Woozy about beauty and is described as a mule with large ears, a paintbrush-like tail, large hoofs, a long neck, and a skinny body.
