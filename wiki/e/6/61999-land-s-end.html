Land's End in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall / ] DAY THE FIRST I believe in holidays.

Land's End has been a recurring landscape-dream of the narrator's childhood, and she has long wanted to see it in reality. She plans to finish the coastal circuit there with her chickens.

] DAY THE TENTH I cannot advise Marazion as a bathing place.

The Land's End is described as the furthest point of the promontory near Castle-an-Dinas and as the travelers’ goal of pilgrimage. They see the coast toward it from the road and plan to visit it on the following Sunday, when they expect fewer tourists.

Land's End is reached by the narrator and traveling party on a Sunday after a long journey. They find it a grand, windy, solitary place where they eat at the First and Last Inn, explore the rocky coast, and look out over the Atlantic; the narrator feels that the reality is no disappointment.

DAY THE TWELFTH Monday morning.

The narrator and her companions take a second and final look at Land's End after leaving Whitesand Bay. They see its picturesque rocks, including the Armed Knight and the Irish Lady, and are glad to have previously visited it on a Sunday when it was less crowded.
