human resistance to heat

topic · 1 work · 2 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: resistance of our organs to the action of heat, resistance of the human organism to so high temperatures

Abstract written by a model

Human resistance to heat is greater in dry than vapor-saturated air, aided by perspiration evaporating to cool the body. As external heat rises, reduced carbonic-acid exhalation and internal combustion partly support it; moving dry air is tolerable, but hot liquids or solids are especially dangerous.

ON SOME APPARATUS THAT PERMIT OF ENTERING FLAMES.

The unit attributes human resistance to high temperatures partly to reduced carbonic-acid exhalation and reduced internal combustion as external heat rises. It also emphasizes that dry, moving air is more tolerable than humid air because evaporation of perspiration cools the body, while contact with hot liquids or solids is especially dangerous.
