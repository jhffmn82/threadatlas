the Cornish people in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

The Cornish people are portrayed as a distinct group reputed to be honest, simple, shrewd, kind, just, faithful, and brave. Though warned they seek favourable terms, the narrator rejects assumptions of cheating. They unfold as courteous, independent people whose rich and poor meet socially and treat strangers kindly.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall / ] DAY THE FIRST I believe in holidays.

Cornish folk are described by reputation as honest, simple, shrewd, and kind, with a strong sense of justice, fidelity, and bravery. A fellow-traveller warns that they are sharp and may try to obtain the best terms for themselves, but the narrator refuses to assume that they intend to cheat visitors.

DAY THE SIXTH And a day absolutely divine!

Cornish folk are presented as courteous and independent, with rich and poor meeting at the rectory tea for courtesy rather than charity. Their treatment of strangers is kind, and the narrator considers this a pleasant aspect of their human nature.
