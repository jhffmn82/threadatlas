Abstract written by a model

A planned boat excursion to the sea-caves is proposed after earlier boating plans with John Curgenven fail. With little time left at the Lizard, Charles suggests taking a boat from Church Cove before the party drives onward, and the women agree. The voyage unfolds in small open boats on rough, hazardous water, amid heavy waves, rocks, cliffs, and open sea. John rows confidently and takes the party into Raven's Ugo and Dolor Ugo, where they admire the caves and hear of wrecks and smugglers. In another rough-water passage from Porth Hern, two boatmen guide the party past cliffs and a slate quarry into a dark cave; fear and awe give way to daylight and sunshine on the return.

" DAY THE EIGHTH And seven days were all we could allow ourselves at the Lizard, if we meant to see the rest of Cornwall.

The women regret that earlier boating plans with John Curgenven have failed and that they may not see the sea-caves between the Lizard and Cadgwith. Charles proposes taking a boat from Church Cove before driving onward, and the women agree.

" DAY THE NINTH And our last at the Lizard, which a week ago had been to us a mere word or dot in a map; now we carried away from it a living human interest in everything and everybody.

The boat trip is hazardous because of the heavy waves, rough rocks, and open sea, but John rows confidently and guides the party into Raven's Ugo and Dolor Ugo. The travelers admire the caves, hear about wrecks and smugglers, and become accustomed to the boat's motion.

DAYS FOURTEENTH, FIFTEENTH, AND SIXTEENTH-- And all Arthurian days, so I will condense them into one chapter, and not spin out the hours that were flying so fast.

The sea voyage begins at Porth Hern in rough water, with two boatmen guiding the party along cliffs and past a slate quarry. The boat enters a dark cave where the passengers feel danger and awe before returning toward daylight and sunshine.
