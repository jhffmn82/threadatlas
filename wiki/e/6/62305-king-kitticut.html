Abstract written by a model

King Kitticut is the king and ruler of the island kingdom of Pingaree, husband of Queen Garee, and father of their only child, Prince Inga. A gentle king and friend of King Rinkitink, he governs with concern for his people and teaches Inga reading, writing, arithmetic, and just rule. Fearing renewed attack after an earlier battle with Regos and Coregos, he watches for strange boats and reveals Pingaree’s three hidden Magic Talismans to Inga so their secret will survive. When Rinkitink arrives, Kitticut courteously welcomes him, offering protection, hospitality, and an escort home. Soon the feared invaders from Regos and Coregos attack. Realizing Pingaree is lost, Kitticut tries to reach the hidden treasures but is seized, bound, and carried away. He and Pingaree’s men are enslaved in the mines of Regos, where he works in the lowest cavern. King Gos removes Kitticut from the mines while he is still chained and takes him with Queen Garee as a hostage. Gos and Queen Cor later bring him to the Nome King, Kaliko, forcing him to carry a bag of gold and jewels. Kitticut protests that Gos’s accusations are lies, but Kaliko confines him in a small cavern after removing his chains. Inga seeks to rescue him, and Dorothy ultimately compels Kaliko to release him. Kitticut joyfully reunites with Inga and thanks Rinkitink for supporting his son. After receiving a new costume from Ozma, Kitticut returns to a rebuilt Pingaree and its new, more magnificent palace. He appoints Nikobob Lord High Chamberlain over the pearl fisheries and kingdom business. Though saddened that Rinkitink must leave, he insists that Rinkitink must reign in Gilgad because fate made him a king.

King Kitticut in Rinkitink in Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1916 · the work’s page · read it where King Kitticut first appears

Narrative, section by section written by a model

13 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter One

King Kitticut rules Pingaree and lives there with Queen Garee and Prince Inga. He remembers the earlier battle against Regos and Coregos and fears another invasion, so he keeps watch for strange boats. He reveals the three Magic Talismans to Inga and entrusts their secret to him.

Chapter Two

King Kitticut goes to the beach to meet the approaching boat and courteously welcomes King Rinkitink to Pingaree. He offers Rinkitink protection, an escort home, and hospitality at his palace.

Chapter Three

King Kitticut recognizes the invaders as the men of Regos and Coregos and realizes that Pingaree is lost. He is seized while trying to reach the hidden treasures, bound, carried to a boat, and taken prisoner.

Chapter Six

Inga's father is identified as the King of Pingaree and is being held as a slave by the people of Regos and Coregos. Inga plans to rescue him.

Chapter Fourteen

King Kitticut had been working in the lowest cavern of the mines of Regos until King Gos took him away the previous evening while he was still in chains. He is later reported to have been taken away by boat with King Gos and Queen Cor.

Chapter Fifteen

King Kitticut is taken from the mines by King Gos and brought to Regos. He is carried away by boat with Queen Garee so that he and the Queen can be used as hostages.

Chapter Sixteen

King Kitticut is expected to return to Pingaree, where his people are rebuilding the island and a new palace for him. Inga sends furnishings from the palaces of Queen Cor and King Gos to equip his new residence.

Chapter Seventeen

King Kitticut is taken as a chained prisoner to the Nome King and forced to carry a bag of gold and jewels during the journey. He protests that Gos's accusations are lies and argues that Kaliko should not support Gos. Kaliko has him confined in a small cavern, where his chains are removed.

Chapter Eighteen

King Kitticut is held captive by King Kaliko after being taken by King Gos and Queen Cor. Inga continues his journey through the Nome Country in an effort to liberate him.

Chapter Twenty-One

King Kitticut is held captive by Kaliko until Dorothy forces Kaliko to release him. He joyfully embraces Inga and thanks Rinkitink for loyally supporting his son.

Chapter Twenty Two

King Kitticut receives a new costume from Ozma before attending the banquet. Rinkitink's song celebrates that he has found his own.

Chapter Twenty Three

King Kitticut returns to Pingaree and is amazed by the rebuilt island and the more magnificent new palace. He appoints Nikobob Lord High Chamberlain and places him in charge of the pearl fisheries and the kingdom’s business matters. Kitticut enjoys listening to Rinkitink’s songs and stories and considers him a shrewd philosopher.

Chapter Twenty-Four

King Kitticut listens to Rinkitink's protests and tells him that it is his duty to reign because fate made him a king. Although he will grieve their separation, he accepts that Rinkitink must return to Gilgad.
