Abstract written by a model

The narrator's companions are a group of female travellers who share a fifteen-day Cornwall journey with her. Practical and mutually supportive, they place themselves in Charles's care for a day's excursion, are prepared for emergencies, and make wet weather enjoyable with needlework, knitting, reading Idylls of the King and Morte d'Arthur, artwork, and shopping. Their travels include Kennack Sands, Poltesco, Cadgwith, Mullion Cove, Housel Cove, and the Lizard. They collect flowers, explore scenery and local life, help the narrator through a cave, bathe in the Atlantic, sketch near Kynance, and attend tea and church. In rough water, they join the narrator in hiring a boat for an evening row, abandon the plan to reach Kynance Cove, watch the sunset and Lizard Lights, and return safely with Charles and John Curgenven. More than a year later, the journey is remembered as an Unsentimental Journey taken by three companions during fifteen innocent days.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

"Shall we get a boat?

The narrator and companions hire a boat for an evening row from the Lizard. They struggle with the landing and rough Atlantic water, decide not to continue to Kynance Cove, observe the sunset and Lizard Lights, and return safely with help from Charles and John Curgenven.

] DAY THE THIRD "And a beautiful day it is, ladies, though it won't do for Kynance.

The women travelers place themselves entirely in Charles's hands and set out from Lizard Town for a full day's excursion. They visit Kennack Sands, Poltesco, and Cadgwith, collect flowers, observe local life, and admire the scenery, while regretting that they did not bring bathing dresses.

We had just time to dine, repose a little, and communicate our address to our af

The narrator's companions travel with her to Mullion Cove and safely pass through the near cave. They answer the narrator from the rocks, help her return through the cave, gather armfuls of corn-flowers, and later stay with her at the Old Inn.

DAY THE FIFTH "Hope for the best, and be prepared for the worst," had been the motto of our journey.

The travelers wake to an exceptionally wet morning and decide to remain prepared and comfortable indoors. They read the Idylls of the King, do needlework and knitting, make artwork, shop in Lizard Town, and conclude that the wet day has been one of their most amusing days in Cornwall.

DAY THE SIXTH And a day absolutely divine!

The narrator and companions visit Housel Cove, bathe in the Atlantic, and spend the morning attempting to catch a tiny fish in a rock pool. They sketch near Kynance, attend the rectory tea and evening church service, and flee from the village wind band when its music becomes unbearable.

] L'ENVOI Written more than a year after.

We three took an Unsentimental Journey together through Cornwall during the fifteen innocent days.
