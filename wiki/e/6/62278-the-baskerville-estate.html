the Baskerville estate in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901/1902

The Baskerville estate is a nearly million-pound property, including securities and a large residue, centred on Baskerville Hall amid lodge-gates, an avenue, and moorland. Entailed to the Desmond family if Sir Henry dies, it is meant by him to remain with the title and property. Beautiful to Sir Henry but melancholy to Dr. Watson, it becomes Stapleton’s target as he seeks to remove the two heirs before him and inherit it.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5.

The Baskerville estate is worth close to a million, including a residue of seven hundred and forty thousand pounds after the other provisions described by Dr. Mortimer. The estate is entailed to the Desmonds if Sir Henry dies, while Sir Henry wants the money to remain with the title and property.

Chapter 6.

The Baskerville estate includes the lodge-gates, a long tree-lined avenue, the moorland surroundings, and Baskerville Hall itself. The lodge is partly ruined, while a new building begun with Sir Charles Baskerville’s South African gold stands nearby. The estate appears beautiful to Sir Henry but dark and melancholy to Dr. Watson.

Chapter 15.

Stapleton learned that only two lives stood between him and the valuable Baskerville estate. His actions in Devonshire were directed toward removing those heirs and acquiring the estate.
