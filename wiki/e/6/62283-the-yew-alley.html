Abstract written by a model

The yew alley is a famous avenue on the grounds of Baskerville Hall, bounded by two impenetrable twelve-foot yew hedges, with a central walk, grass strips, a moor-gate, and a summer-house exit. Sir Charles Baskerville habitually walked it before bed; on the night of his death, his footprints changed beyond the moor-gate and ended where he was found after apparently running through the alley.

Chapter 2.

The yew alley at Baskerville Hall was the route Sir Charles Baskerville habitually walked before bed. On the night of his death, his footprints led down the alley, changed after he passed the moor-gate as though he had walked on his toes, and ended where his body was found.

Chapter 3.

The yew alley has two impenetrable yew hedges about twelve feet high, with a central walk and strips of grass on either side. It contains a wicket-gate leading to the moor and an exit through a summer-house; Sir Charles was found on the walk after apparently running through it.
