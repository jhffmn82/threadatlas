Association for Computational Linguistics · 2024

This is a paper in the Proceedings of the 62nd Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics, titled “Unsupervised Information Refinement Training of Large Language Models for Retrieval-Augmented Generation,” by Shicheng Xu, Liang Pang, Mo Yu, Fandong Meng, Huawei Shen, Xueqi Cheng, and Jie Zhou. It addresses a central weakness of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG): large language models can be misled or hindered by retrieved text that is noisy, incomplete, incorrect, or lacks a direct answer. The authors propose treating the model as an “Information Refiner” that combines retrieved knowledge with its parameterized knowledge to generate concise, accurate, and complete responses. The paper introduces INFO-RAG, an unsupervised, low-cost, task-general training method. It frames retrieval through three scenarios: texts that contain all needed knowledge; texts with incomplete or incorrect knowledge; and semantically relevant texts without a usable answer. Correspondingly, INFO-RAG trains models on Select and Copy, Correct and Complete, and Contextual Stimulation tasks. Training data are built from English Wikipedia using sentence prefixes, target continuations, and simulated retrieved texts; pretrained models learn through prefix language modeling and next-token prediction. The three tasks are mixed with LoRA training in proportions of 20%, 40%, and 40%. Experiments span 11 datasets and seven task types, including question answering, slot filling, multi-hop and long-form question answering, dialogue, language modeling, and code generation. Across LLaMA-2-7B, LLaMA-2-7B-chat, LLaMA-2-13B, and LLaMA-2-13B-chat, INFO-RAG improves zero-shot RAG performance by an average of 9.39% in reported results. It also improves in-context learning, robustness to changes in retrieval results, and SearChain in open retrieval. Analysis finds that Correct and Complete and Contextual Stimulation are especially beneficial, while Select and Copy is most useful in combination with them; added Wikipedia training alone does not improve the backbone. INFO-RAG also avoids substantial catastrophic forgetting on MMLU without RAG. The paper concludes that unsupervised information-refinement training can help RAG models use retrieval more effectively. Its main limitation is evaluation only at 7B and 13B parameter sizes because of computing constraints. The authors state compliance with the ACL Ethics Policy and acknowledge support from several Chinese research programs and organizations.

Abstract

The unit examines retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and the difficulty large language models (LLMs) have in effectively using retrieved information. It proposes viewing LLMs as an “Information Refiner” that integrates retrieved knowledge with model parameters to produce more concise, accurate, and complete text. The authors introduce INFO-RAG, an unsupervised, low-cost, and general information-refinement training method for RAG. Across 11 diverse datasets, INFO-RAG improves LLaMA2’s zero-shot performance by an average of 9.39% relative points and improves in-context learning and RAG robustness.

Introduction

The unit concerns retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and the difficulty large language models (LLMs) have in using retrieved texts of varying quality. It proposes viewing LLMs as an “Information Refiner” and training them with an Information Refinement objective to produce more concise, accurate, and complete text by combining retrieved information with model knowledge. The authors introduce INFO-RAG, an unsupervised method covering complete, incomplete or incorrect, and answerless retrieval scenarios. Experiments report that INFO-RAG improves zero-shot RAG for LLaMA2 across question answering, slot-filling, language modeling, dialogue, and code generation.

Related Work / Retrieval Augmented Generation .. Our INFO-RAG

Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) retrieves external information to improve the accuracy and credibility of text generated by language models and is used in tasks including open-domain question answering, dialogue, and code generation. Prior work observes that noise in retrieved texts can interfere with or mislead language models, but INFO-RAG argues that these studies do not clearly define the role of language models in RAG. INFO-RAG treats language models as “Information Refiners” that improve the information bottleneck of RAG and remain robust to retrieved texts. It is an unsupervised training method that constructs samples and training tasks based on three retrieved-text scenarios.

Positive Information Gain in RAG

The section presents Positive Information Gain in RAG as a perspective in which LLMs act as an Information Refiner. It defines three retrieved-text scenarios and describes how LLMs should extract, verify, correct, complete, or stimulate knowledge for a question. Scenario 1 concerns retrieved texts that already contain all the needed knowledge, Scenario 2 concerns texts with incomplete or incorrect knowledge, and Scenario 3 concerns texts without a usable answer but with semantic relevance. Unsupervised Learning .. Training Strategy

INFO-RAG constructs unsupervised training samples from English Wikipedia by pairing a sentence prefix and target continuation with simulated retrieved texts. It trains pretrained large language models through prefix language modeling to perform information refinement via next-token prediction. The three training tasks are Select and Copy, Correct and Complete, and Contextual Stimulation, which respectively teach extraction and copying, correction and completion, and generation from relevant context. These tasks are mixed in multi-task training with LoRA, using batch proportions of 20%, 40%, and 40%.

Experiments / Datasets and Evaluation Metrics .. Enhancing Previous SOTA in Open-Retrieval

The unit evaluates INFO-RAG on eleven datasets spanning seven tasks, including Open-domain Question Answering, Soft Filling, Multi-Hop Question Answering, Long-Form Question Answering, Dialogue, Language Modeling, and Code Generation. INFO-RAG improves the zero-shot retrieval-augmented generation performance of LLaMA-2-7B, LLaMA-2-7B-chat, LLaMA-2-13B, and LLaMA-2-13B-chat, with an average improvement of 9.39% across the reported results. The experiments use shared retrieved documents, ColBERTv2 with Wikipedia for several tasks, and task-specific or dataset-provided passages for others. INFO-RAG also improves in-context learning results and enhances SearChain in the open-retrieval setting.

Analysis / Fine-grained Analysis for Three Scenarios .. Avoid Catastrophic Forgetting Experiment on

The unit analyzes INFO-RAG across three retrieval-augmented generation scenarios involving incorrect, incomplete, and noisy retrieved texts. INFO-RAG improves performance over LLaMA-2 with RAG, while additional Wikipedia training does not improve the backbone. Ablations show that Correct and Complete and Contextual Stimulation provide gains, while Select and Copy works best when combined with the other tasks. INFO-RAG also improves robustness to retrieval-result changes and avoids substantial catastrophic forgetting on MMLU without RAG.

Conclusion

This paper proposes viewing LLMs in RAG as an “Information Refiner.” It introduces INFO-RAG, an unsupervised, low-cost, and task-general information refinement training method that integrates knowledge from model parameters and retrieved texts. Experiments across 11 datasets covering 7 tasks in a zero-shot setting show that INFO-RAG improves LLM performance for RAG, with additional advantages in ICL and robustness. INFO-RAG can also be combined with the SOTA RAG framework to further improve performance.

Limitations

This paper aims to enable large language models to perform information refinement in Retrieval-Augmented Generation through unsupervised training. The goal is to accurately extract correct information and avoid interference from incorrect information. Due to limited computing resources, the paper only evaluates models with 7B and 13B parameter sizes. It considers exploring larger models in the future with more computing resources.
