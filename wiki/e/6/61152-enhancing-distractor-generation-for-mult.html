Enhancing Distractor Generation for Multiple-Choice Questions with Retrieval Augmented Pretraining and Knowledge Graph Integration

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

This paper studies automatic distractor generation for multiple-choice questions, focusing on making incorrect options relevant, difficult, and useful. It develops two approaches: Retrieval Augmented Pretraining (RAP) and Knowledge Augmented Generation (KAG). RAP aligns pretraining with the downstream task by using an answer option to retrieve passages, masking the answer to form a pseudo question, and training a language model to generate the ground-truth distractors. It can use augmentation, cross-domain training, or ground-truth distractors during retrieval. KAG supplies a text-to-text generator with retrieved knowledge-graph triplets, selected using the question, answer, candidate distractors, and keywords, then ranked by sentence similarity or supervised classification. The work positions these methods against Generating-and-Ranking and Text2Text approaches, task-specific pretraining, and prior knowledge-graph use. It evaluates Text2Text, BART, T5, RAP, KAG, their combinations, and ChatGPT on SciQ, a 13,000-question science dataset, and MCQ, a cross-domain cloze-style dataset. Evaluation includes P@1, R@1, F1@3, MRR, NDCG@3, and human judgments of relevance, distractiveness, and utility. Results generally show gains from RAP and KAG. The best reported F1@3 rises from 14.80 to 16.47 on MCQ and from 15.92 to 16.50 on SciQ. However, combining RAP and KAG does not consistently add gains, potentially because triplets add noise and their ranker needs improvement. The paper also notes that token-based evaluation incompletely reflects output quality, while RAP may retrieve poor evidence for rare or specialized answers without a large knowledge corpus.

Abstract

The paper addresses distractor generation for multiple-choice questions. It introduces retrieval augmented pretraining to align language model pretraining with the downstream distractor generation task and explores integrating knowledge graphs. Experiments on benchmarking datasets show that the models outperform state-of-the-art results. The best-performing model improves the F1@3 score from 14.80 to 16.47 on the MCQ dataset and from 15.92 to 16.50 on the Sciq dataset.

Introduction

The paper investigates two ways to improve automatic distractor generation for multiple-choice questions: task-specific pretraining and knowledge augmentation. It proposes Retrieval Augmented Pretraining (RAP), which uses MCQ answers to retrieve passages, creates pseudo questions by masking the answers, and trains with the distractors. It also proposes knowledge-augmented generation (KAG), which provides knowledge-graph triplets as auxiliary information to a text-to-text distractor-generation model. The methods improve reported F1@3 scores from 14.80 to 16.47 on MCQ and from 15.92 to 16.50 on Sciq.

The unit reviews two main directions in Distractor Generation: the Generating and Ranking framework and the Text2Text generation architecture. It explains how task-specific pretraining incorporates relevant priors into pretraining to improve performance on low-resource downstream tasks, motivating RAP (Retrieval-Augmented Pretraining) for distractor generation. It also surveys Knowledge Graph use in NLP and proposes leveraging Knowledge Graphs as auxiliary tools to improve the relevance and difficulty of generated distractors.

3 / 3.1 .. 3.2.3

The methodology describes Retrieval Augmented Pretraining (RAP) and Knowledge Augmented Generation (KAG) for improving Distractor Generation (DG). RAP retrieves text using an answer option, masks that answer to create a pseudo question, and pretrains a language model to generate ground-truth distractors; it can use data augmentation, cross-domain training, or ground-truth distractors for retrieval. KAG retrieves knowledge triplets using the question, answer, generated candidate distractors, and extracted keywords, then ranks the triplets with either sentence-embedding similarity or supervised classification. A generation model is trained to produce distractors conditioned on the original question, answer, and retrieved knowledge triplets.

4 / 4.1 / Sciq dataset .. 4.6

This unit evaluates distractor generation methods on the SciQ and MCQ datasets, comparing Text2Text, BART, T5, RAP, Knowledge Augmented Generation, and ChatGPT. SciQ contains 13,000 science multiple-choice questions, while the MCQ dataset is a cross-domain cloze-style dataset with science, vocabulary, common sense, and trivia questions. The experiments use automatic metrics such as P@1, R@1, F1@3, MRR, and NDCG@3, as well as human ratings of relevance, distractiveness, and utility. The results show that RAP and Knowledge Augmented Generation generally improve distractor generation, although combining RAP with Knowledge Augmented Generation does not consistently produce additive gains.

5

The paper introduces the utilization of task-specific pretraining and a knowledge base. Its experimental results show significant performance improvement through their integration. The work presents a novel contribution to the field of distractor generation for MCQs. It demonstrates the potential of combining retrieval augmented pretraining and knowledge graphs to achieve superior results.

6

The unit reports limitations of the KAG method and its combination with RAP. Combining KAG with RAP did not produce additive effects, possibly because knowledge triplets introduce noise into Text2Text generation and because the triplet ranker needs improvement. The evaluation and training rely heavily on token scores, which do not fully represent generated output quality. The RAP framework may also fail to retrieve relevant sentences or passages for rare or specialized answers unless an abundant knowledge corpus is available.
