the legend of the Hound of the Baskervilles in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901 to 1902 · the work’s page · read it where the legend of the Hound of the Baskervilles first appears

Abstract written by a model

The legend of the Hound of the Baskervilles is a family curse story about a dreadful, demonic hound said to haunt the Baskervilles. It begins with Hugo Baskerville pursuing an abducted maiden across the moor and being killed by a monstrous hound; the legend then claims the creature caused sudden, bloody, mysterious deaths among family members. It deeply affects Sir Charles and creates fear that Sir Henry faces the same dark fate. Dr. Mortimer and witnesses connect marks near Sir Charles’s body and an apparition to the legend, while local discussion and Stapleton’s guarded comments sustain belief in an evil family influence. In the end, Stapleton exploits Sir Charles’s superstition and reports involving the hound to conceal human responsibility for Sir Charles’s death.

The legend of the Hound of the Baskervilles tells how Hugo Baskerville pursued an abducted maiden across the moor and was killed by a monstrous hound. It claims that the hound continued to bring sudden, bloody, and mysterious deaths to members of the Baskerville family.

The hell-hound legend is known to Dr. Mortimer and influenced his interpretation of the marks near Sir Charles Baskerville's body. The witnesses' descriptions of the apparition correspond closely to the hell-hound of the legend.

The Baskerville family legend concerns a fiend dog said to haunt the family. Jack Stapleton calls the peasants credulous but admits that the legend strongly affected Sir Charles.

the Baskerville curse in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901 to 1902 · the work’s page · read it where the Baskerville curse first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Baskerville curse is a family belief that a hound has plagued the Baskervilles since Hugo Baskerville's death, bringing evil fates to those who go to Baskerville Hall. Sir Charles and Dr. Mortimer take the threat seriously, especially for Sir Henry, while Holmes withholds belief in a supernatural cause and considers human danger.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 2.

The Baskerville curse is presented as the belief that the family has been plagued by a hound since Hugo Baskerville's death. Sir Charles Baskerville took this supposed curse seriously and believed that a dreadful fate threatened his family.

Chapter 3.

The Baskerville curse is represented by the belief that every Baskerville who goes to Baskerville Hall meets an evil fate. Dr. Mortimer fears it will claim Sir Henry, while Holmes refuses to accept a supernatural explanation before other hypotheses have been exhausted.

Chapter 4.

The unit discusses the family’s story of the hound and whether the events surrounding the inheritance and the uncle’s death are supernatural. Sir Henry refers to the possibility of a family fiend, while Holmes and Dr. Mortimer also consider danger from human beings.
