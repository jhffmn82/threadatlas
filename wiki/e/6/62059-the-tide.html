the tide in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

The tide comes in quickly at Kynance Cove, covering the sandy floor of Drawing-room Cave by noon and turning a visitor’s rock into an island. The narrator’s party leaves before the route is impassable, while newly arrived tourists are isolated and may need to wade or swim ashore.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

DAY THE SEVENTH John Curgenven had said last night, with his air of tender patronising, half regal, half paternal, which we declared always reminded us of King Arthur--"Ladies, whenever you settle to go to Kynance, I'll take you.

The tide comes in quickly at Kynance Cove, covering the sandy floors of the caves and turning one visitor's rock into an island. The narrator's party leaves hurriedly before the route becomes impassable, while a newly arrived group is left needing to wade and possibly swim ashore.
