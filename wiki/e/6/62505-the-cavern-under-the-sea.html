the cavern under the sea in The Scarecrow of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1915

The cavern under the sea is a warm, lonely, dome-like prison and the Ork’s former cavern. It holds rugged rocks, a sandy beach rising from a pool, seaweed, and a bluish-green glow, but no outside entrance; its only outlet is water. Trot and Cap’n Bill fall into it, dry their clothes, cook fish, and sleep, then are trapped there. The Ork arrives through submerged caverns, and a sloping passage leads to a black hole and precipice above a deep lake.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Two

The cavern is a warm, lonely, dome-like place with rugged rocks, a sandy beach, a pool of water, and a soft bluish-green glow. Trot and Cap'n Bill reach it after their underwater fall and remain there while their clothes dry, they cook a fish, and they sleep. The cavern has plentiful seaweed but no visible outside entrance, and staying there would eventually leave them without food or water.

Chapter Three

The cavern serves as a prison for Trot and Cap'n Bill after the whirlpool casts them there, and the Ork reaches it after swimming upward from another submerged cavern. It contains a sloping passage leading to a black hole and a precipice above a deep lake.
