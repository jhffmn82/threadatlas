oxide of carbon

substance · 1 work · 3 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: CO, carbonic oxide

Abstract written by a model

Oxide of carbon is a gas analyzed by rapid absorption in an absorbing liquid, replacing some liquid three or four times for the last fractions. It can also be measured by combustion: it forms carbonic acid in equal volume, and two-thirds of total contraction gives its volume.

BUNTE'S BURETTE FOR THE ANALYSIS OF FURNACE GASES. .. _Mode of Measuring the Gases and Absorption_.

The burette permits rapid absorption of oxide of carbon, although part of the absorbing liquid must be replaced three or four times for the last fractions. Oxide of carbon can also be quantitatively analyzed by combustion, with the total contraction from combustion and absorption multiplied by two-thirds giving its volume.
