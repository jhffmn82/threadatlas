caustic potassa

Also called: caustic potash, potash, pure caustic potash

Abstract written by a model

Caustic potassa is an absorbing liquid and caustic alkali, very soluble in water. In gas analysis it is sucked into a burette to absorb carbonic acid, used with pyrogallic acid and potash to determine oxygen, and admitted after combustion for oxide of carbon analysis. In soap making, pure caustic potash is dissolved, cooled, and mixed with melted tallow, grease, or oil to make neutral potash soap; it quickly melts and deteriorates in air.

BUNTE'S BURETTE FOR THE ANALYSIS OF FURNACE GASES. .. _Mode of Measuring the Gases and Absorption_.

Caustic potassa is sucked into the burette to absorb carbonic acid. It is also used with pyrogallic acid and potash to determine oxygen, and it is allowed to enter after combustion when analyzing oxide of carbon.

] SOAP AND ITS MANUFACTURE, FROM A CONSUMER'S POINT OF VIEW.

Pure caustic potash is used to make neutral potash soap. Twenty pounds are dissolved in two gallons of water, cooled to about 90 F, and mixed with eighty pounds of melted tallow, grease, or oil; the article warns that it melts and deteriorates quickly when exposed to air.
