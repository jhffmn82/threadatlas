tempering by compression in Scientific American Supplement No. 360, November 25, 1882

Various · 1881

Tempering by compression is a method for treating steel and other metals: heat to cherry red, compress strongly under prescribed pressure until completely cooled, producing tempering-like hardness, molecular contraction, and fine grain.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

TEMPERING BY COMPRESSION.

Tempering by compression consists of heating metal to a cherry red, compressing it strongly, and maintaining the pressure until the metal is completely cooled. The process produces effects resembling tempering, including great hardness, molecular contraction, and fine grain.
