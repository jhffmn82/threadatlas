Apple jelly is made in a factory from apples received in 500- to 1,000-bushel bins. Natural, ungrafted, crabbed, gnarled, or frozen apples may be used; frozen fruit must arrive solid. Later-ripening fruit yields more jelly, and crab apples yield the finest jelly.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE MANUFACTURE OF APPLE JELLY.

The factory receives apples in bins holding 500 to 1,000 bushels, and one and a half cubic feet is counted as a bushel. Natural, ungrafted, crabbed, gnarled, and even frozen apples can be used if frozen fruit is delivered while still solid. Later-ripening fruit produces more jelly than earlier varieties, and crab apples produce the finest jelly.
