Queen Cor

person · 1 work · 32 quoted facts · 15 connections
Also called: Cor, Queen, Queen Cor's, Queen of Coregos, Queen of Regos and Coregos, the Queen, the Queen of Coregos, the terrible Queen of Coregos, the wicked Queen, wicked Queen of Coregos

Abstract written by a model

Queen Cor is the cruel, cunning queen and ruler of Coregos, later described as queen of Regos and Coregos, and the wife and plotting partner of King Gos. A large woman with flashing black eyes, a dark complexion, and an evil expression often hidden by a sweet smile, she rules through forced labor and intimidation. She makes mostly women slaves, including captives from Pingaree, work the grain fields; punishes those too weak to work; and holds prominent captives, including the Prince of Pingaree, Prince Inga, King Rinkitink, Queen Garee, and King Kitticut. When angered by Gos's warriors consuming Coregos's provisions, she captures Inga and Rinkitink by deception and threatens them. She pays for honey with her own money and is fond of Zella's honey. After Inga's magic protects him from her attack and he frees the slaves, Cor flees toward Regos. With Gos, she takes Kitticut and Garee by boat, destroys the bridge of boats, allies with the Nome Kingdom, and proposes using the royal prisoners as hostages and forcing them to labor. King Kaliko refuses to let her control their treatment. Cor ultimately leaves with Gos and is drowned with him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

8 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Seven

Queen Cor rules Coregos and is wedded to King Gos. She is stern and cruel, makes mostly women slaves plow and harvest the grain, and receives the women and children captured from Pingaree to work in her fields.

Chapter Ten

Queen Cor is angry that King Gos and his warriors are crowding Coregos and consuming her provisions. She captures Prince Inga and King Rinkitink by deception, then makes Inga her page and threatens Rinkitink with cruel amusements.

Chapter Eleven

Queen Cor is expected to pay Zella’s family for the honey. She has made the conquering Prince of Pingaree a prisoner.

Chapter Twelve

Queen Cor is associated with the changed fortunes that leave King Gos and his people in control of the palace. Her palace is the destination Bilbil reaches after escaping.

Chapter Thirteen

Queen Cor orders the rebellious women from Pingaree to be whipped because they are too weak to work. She attacks Inga with her fists, a whip, and a dagger, but his recovered magic protects him. After Inga frees the slaves and threatens to put her in a dungeon, she flees toward Regos.

Chapter Fourteen

Queen Cor had taken the Queen of Pingaree away, but she later flees with King Gos. The two leave by boat with forty rowers and take King Kitticut and the Queen of Pingaree with them.

Chapter Fifteen

Queen Cor flees from Pingaree to Regos, dragging Queen Garee with her, and orders the bridge of boats destroyed. She persuades King Gos to hide King Kitticut and Queen Garee in the Nome Kingdom and use them as hostages against Prince Ingato.

Chapter Seventeen

Queen Cor accompanies King Gos in taking King Kitticut and Queen Garee to the Nome King's caverns. She suggests making the prisoners work so they will suffer, but King Kaliko refuses to let her decide their treatment. She leaves with Gos after celebrating their success with Kaliko.
