travel and rational tourism in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

Travel and rational tourism is a deliberate way of seeing places that rejects frantic sight-collecting. It begins with belief in holidays and unfolds by arguing that observing and enjoying matter because the traveller's mind creates travel's real interest.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall / ] DAY THE FIRST I believe in holidays.

The narrator argues that rational travel requires observing and enjoying rather than rushing from place to place merely to claim that a country has been seen. She maintains that the traveller's mind, not only the sights themselves, creates the real interest of travel.
