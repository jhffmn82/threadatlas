Mr. Frankland in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901/1902

Mr. Frankland is an elderly, red-faced, white-haired, choleric landowner, litigant, and amateur astronomer. He treats costly litigation as a habitual amusement, winning a right of way, closing Fernworthy, and suing Sir John Morland. From his telescope he watches the moor, sees a boy carrying food toward a stony hill, suspects aid to the escaped convict, tells Dr. Watson, and refuses police help.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

And that was a most vague direction. I realised it as I drove

Mr. Frankland tells Dr. Watson that he has seen a boy carrying a bundle toward a stony hill and believes the boy is taking food to the convict. He shows Watson the boy through his telescope but refuses to inform the police.
