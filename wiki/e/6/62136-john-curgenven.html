John Curgenven

person · 1 work · 18 quoted facts · 6 connections

Abstract written by a model

John Curgenven is an about-sixty-year-old Lizard fisherman and boatman, of Cornish ancestry, who owns and operates a boat for excursions. The elder boatman and stroke-oar, he knows the coast and safely handles his boat near dangerous rocks. He is portrayed as weather-beaten yet refined, with strong arms, a fearless honest face, self-control guided by conscience and common sense, and a bearing likened to King Arthur. Kind to young people and considerate of an old lady, he gives the party fish, offers to take them to Kynance Cove, leads them over the downs, assists them across rough ground, and explains caves, tides, and local dangers. He leaves before the Cove because of another engagement; later, his wife sells the party serpentine. On their final day at the Lizard, he rows them to the sea caves and helps them embark and disembark safely. He recounts rescuing women and children from the Brest wreck, during which a rope took half a finger. He also helps guide the narrator home along hedge walls.

John Curgenven in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884 · the work’s page · read it where John Curgenven first appears

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

"Shall we get a boat?

John Curgenven is the elder boatman and stroke-oar who knows the coast and handles the boat near the dangerous rocks. He is described as an exceptionally refined, kind, weather-beaten man from one of the oldest families in Cornwall, and he later helps guide the narrator home along the hedge walls.

DAY THE SEVENTH John Curgenven had said last night, with his air of tender patronising, half regal, half paternal, which we declared always reminded us of King Arthur--"Ladies, whenever you settle to go to Kynance, I'll take you.

John Curgenven comes to the narrator and the girls at breakfast with fish as a gift and offers to take them to Kynance Cove. He leads them across the downs, assists them over rough ground, and explains the route, the caves, the tide, and the dangers of Asparagus Island and Devil's Throat. He leaves before reaching the Cove because he has an engagement, then later sells the narrator's party serpentine through his wife.

" DAY THE NINTH And our last at the Lizard, which a week ago had been to us a mere word or dot in a map; now we carried away from it a living human interest in everything and everybody.

John rows the party to the sea caves and helps them board and leave the boat safely. He recounts rescuing women and children from the Brest wreck, showing a hand with half a finger missing from that rescue, and is described as strong, fearless, chivalrous, careful, honest, and kind.
