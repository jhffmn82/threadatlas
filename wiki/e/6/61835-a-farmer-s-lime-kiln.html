A FARMER'S LIME KILN

object · 1 work · 16 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: kiln, lime kiln, lime-kiln

Abstract written by a model

A FARMER'S LIME KILN is a limestone structure for burning lime, built at the foot of a steep bluff from stone quarried on the spot. It is 13 feet square and 25 feet high from the foundation, reinforced with iron rods, and includes an arch and a contracted well lined one course deep with fire-brick. The well is 7 feet wide at the bilge and has a 2-foot hole for drawing lime. Filled by hand-car on a railway track from the bluff, it uses coal and produces about 100 bushels in 24 hours.

A FARMER'S LIME KILN is in one work on this site, Scientific American Supplement No. 360, November 25, 1882. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

A FARMER'S LIME KILN.

A FARMER'S LIME KILN is described as a 13-foot-square structure 25 feet high, built from limestone quarried on the spot. It has iron rods and plates for reinforcement, an arched front with a plank floor, a fire-brick-lined well, movable iron bars for drawing the lime, and a railway track to facilitate filling. Its capacity is about 100 bushels in 24 hours.
