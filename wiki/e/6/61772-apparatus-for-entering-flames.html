apparatus for entering flames in Scientific American Supplement No. 360, November 25, 1882

Various · 1881

A protective envelope for entering flames, designed to let a person enter without being burned. It unfolds as metallic-gauze armor, helmet, cuirass, skirt, boots, and shield, worn over coarse cloth soaked in alum solution, with asbestos cloth covering head, hands, and feet.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ON SOME APPARATUS THAT PERMIT OF ENTERING FLAMES.

The apparatus was designed to let a person enter flames without being burned. It consisted mainly of metallic-gauze armor, including a helmet, cuirass, skirt, boots, and a shield, combined with coarse alum-soaked clothing and asbestos coverings for the head, hands, and feet.
