the danger on the moor

topic · 1 work · 3 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: a place of danger, danger, danger from human beings, danger from this family fiend, danger to Sir Henry, danger which he will run, place of danger

Abstract written by a model

The danger on the moor concerns Sir Henry’s planned journey to Baskerville Hall, where danger may be supernatural or human. Holmes thinks a warning either protects him or frightens him away for another purpose. Beryl says living there has been fatal to his family but cannot identify the danger. Miss Stapleton repeatedly urges him to leave, but Sir Henry stays after meeting her.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

Chapter 4.

Holmes believes there may be danger connected with Sir Henry’s going to Baskerville Hall. The warning may have been intended to protect Sir Henry, or it may have been sent to frighten him away for someone else’s purposes.

A short walk brought us to it, a bleak moorland house, once the

Beryl believes Sir Henry will run a danger by living at a place that has always been fatal to his family. She cannot identify the danger or provide Dr. Watson with definite information about it.

Chapter 9.

Miss Stapleton repeatedly warns Sir Henry that the moor is a place of danger and says she will not be happy until he has left it. Sir Henry does not wish to leave after meeting her, offering instead to take her with him through marriage.
