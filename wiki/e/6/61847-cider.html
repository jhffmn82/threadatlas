cider in Scientific American Supplement No. 360, November 25, 1882

Various · 1881

Cider is produced by grinding and pressing the apples, then is clarified and partially concentrated before becoming jelly. It may also be exchanged to farmers for apples, sold as boiled cider, or drawn from an upper evaporator tube at a desired consistency for pies and sauces. The account states that turning cider into jelly gives it twice the value for food purposes that it has as a beverage and intoxicant.

cider in Child's Health Primer for Primary Classes

Jane Andrews · 1885

Cider is a beverage made from crushed apples after apple-juice is pressed out. Soon, its sugar begins changing into alcohol and gas; it contains a little alcohol, though large amounts can provide much alcohol and may lead to drunkenness. It can often make drinkers ill-tempered and cross, may lead toward stronger liquors, and turns into vinegar if left warm long enough.

Cider is made from apples. Its sugar begins changing into alcohol and gas soon after the apple-juice is pressed out, and cider can make its drinkers ill-tempered and cross.

] CHAPTER VI. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Cider contains relatively little alcohol compared with distilled liquor. Distilled liquor can be made from cider, but drinking large amounts of cider can provide a great deal of alcohol and may lead to drunkenness.

CHAPTER VII. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Cider contains a little alcohol. Drinking cider can be a step toward drinking stronger liquors.
