  • In Scientific American Supplement No. 360, November 25, 1882: The Universal gas engine is a simple gas engine design, invented by J. A. Ewins and H. Newman and made by T. B. Barker. It uses a combustible gas-and-air explosive mixture, rotary ignition, and single-acting operation without compression. THE "UNIVERSAL" GAS ENGINE.

The Universal gas engine is a simple, single-acting engine invented by J. A. Ewins and H. Newman and made by T. B. Barker. It was constructed in sizes from one-eighth horse-power to one horse-power, with larger sizes being made, and was reported to work successfully at low gas cost.
