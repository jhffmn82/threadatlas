Abstract written by a model

In Scientific American Supplement No. 360, November 25, 1882, fats are described as non-nitrogenous constituents that contribute energy, including butter, lard, suet, and oil. In Child's Health Primer for Primary Classes, fat is described as a food that provides heat and forms body cushions and wrappings, but does not increase muscle strength or work joints.

Jane Andrews · 1885 · the work’s page · read it where fat first appears

Abstract written by a model

Fat is a food that provides heat and forms body cushions and wrappings for warmth and comfort; it does not increase muscle strength or work joints. Good fat comes from suitable foods, while alcohol makes it badly and wrongly placed.
