Abstract written by a model

A newspaper-clipping warning letter to Sir Henry tells him to keep away from the moor if he values his life or reason. Its pasted printed words, except “moor,” conceal the sender’s identity. Later it is evidence of human agency in London, possibly from either a protector or an enemy.

Chapter 4.

The warning message says, “As you value your life or your reason keep away from the moor.” All its words except “moor” are pasted onto the paper from printed material, and Holmes concludes that the sender took pains to hide identifying clues.

Chapter 10.

The warning letter to Sir Henry is cited as evidence of human agency in London. Watson notes that it may have been written by either a protecting friend or an enemy, and he wonders whether its writer is connected to the stranger on the moor.
