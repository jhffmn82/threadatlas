In Child's Health Primer for Primary Classes, the body is described as a living possession of every person whose organs work continuously, grows and repairs itself, and depends on food, water, air, exercise, and healthful functions.

body in Child's Health Primer for Primary Classes

Jane Andrews · 1885 · the work’s page · read it where body first appears

Abstract written by a model

The human body is a possession of every person: a living body with a brain, about two hundred bones, and joints better than those of dolls. Built during childhood, it grows and is repaired as parts wear out. It is a busy workshop whose organs work day and night, including during sleep. Food, carried through the blood, supplies its bones, muscles, nerves, brain, skin, hair, nails, and eyes, supporting growth and mending broken or worn parts. Made largely of water, it needs pure water from drinks and food, as well as salt and fat. Its health depends on pure blood, fresh air, exercise, and good, simple food. The lungs help purify blood; if they cannot take in enough air, the whole body suffers and life may be shortened. Skin covers it and, with the stomach, blood, heart, and lungs, helps prepare food, carry materials, use air, and remove waste. Internal work produces heat, while skin and breath continually lose it.

front matter

Child's Health Primer for Primary Classes .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

The human body is described as having about two hundred bones joined together in useful ways. It is also said to be built during childhood and later repaired as it wears out.

CHAPTER III. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

The body is a busy workshop in which many kinds of work continue day and night. Its organs keep working during sleep, and it should be kept pure, strong, and in good order.

CHAPTER X. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

The body receives prepared food for its bones, muscles, nerves, brain, skin, finger nails, hair, and eyes. Every part needs food to grow or to be mended when it wears out.

] CHAPTER XI. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

The body is made partly of water and needs pure water, food for its bones and muscles, salt, and fat for warmth and comfort. It receives water from drinks and from foods such as fruits, vegetables, beefsteak, and bread.

CHAPTER XII. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

The body receives food through the blood to make bone, muscle, skin, hair, eyes, and brain. Food can also help mend broken parts, such as a broken bone.

CHAPTER XIII. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

The body grows strong through open-air exercise, pure air, and good, simple food. A strong, healthy body with well-trained hands is described as a valuable possession.

CHAPTER XIV. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

The body receives food carried by the blood to every part. Exercise in fresh air warms the body better than a fire could do.

CHAPTER XV. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

The body depends on pure blood to remain well and strong. When the lungs cannot take in enough air to purify the blood, the whole body suffers and life may be shortened.

CHAPTER XVI. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

The body is covered with skin and lined with a more delicate kind of skin. It is kept healthy as the stomach, blood, heart, lungs, and skin perform their work of preparing food, carrying materials, using fresh air, and removing waste matter.

CHAPTER XVIII. / WHAT MAKES US WARM? .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

The body produces heat through its internal work but loses heat through the skin and breath. When the skin is chilled, perspiration tubes may close and prevent waste matter from leaving properly.
