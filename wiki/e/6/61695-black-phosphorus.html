Abstract written by a model

P. Thenard describes black phosphorus as perhaps not invariably a mixture of ordinary phosphorus and metallic phosphide traces. A specimen melted white, stayed white after cooling, then turned black after superfusion and contact with white or black phosphorus; it largely dissolved in carbon disulphide.

[Illustration] BLACK PHOSPHORUS.

Black phosphorus is described as possibly not always being a mixture of ordinary phosphorus and traces of a metallic phosphide. A dark gray specimen became white when melted and remained white after sudden cooling, but became black again after superfusion when brought into contact with white or black phosphorus. Most of the specimen dissolved in carbon disulphide, leaving only a trace of very pale yellow matter that seemed to be amorphous phosphorus.
