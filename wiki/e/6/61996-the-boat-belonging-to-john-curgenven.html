the boat belonging to John Curgenven in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

John Curgenven’s open boat, propelled by a single pair of oars, carries the narrator and companions among the rocks at the Lizard and later over very large waves to the sea caves. Boatmen steer near dangerous whirlpools before returning as darkness approaches. Landing is difficult because of waves and seaweed. After the sea-cave trip, an innocent leak leaves travelers soaked to the knees.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

"Shall we get a boat?

The boat carries the narrator and companions among the rocks near the Lizard. Its boatmen steer close to dangerous whirlpools without entering them, then turn back toward land as darkness approaches.

" DAY THE NINTH And our last at the Lizard, which a week ago had been to us a mere word or dot in a map; now we carried away from it a living human interest in everything and everybody.

John's boat carries the party over very large waves to the sea caves, though the voyage repeatedly rises and falls like a cork on the sea. It has an innocent leak, and the travelers' garments become soaked to the knees before they can be dried at a nearby cottage.
