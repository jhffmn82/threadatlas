St. Michael's Mount in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

St. Michael's Mount is a historic mount in the middle of Mount's Bay, resembling Mont St. Michel and formed by a battlemented rock, castle, chapel, mediaeval fortress, and modern residence. At its foot is a fishing village of about thirty cottages, while an underground tiny continuous-chain railway serves the Mount. By moonlight it appears grand and mysterious, linked to Cormoran as his legendary dwelling place. The St. Aubyn family bought it in 1660 and has inhabited it ever since. Visitors tour its chapel, vault, halls, drawing-room, and school-room, admiring the site despite its many steps, rough causeway, and difficulties of living there. It receives royal visitors, including an expected visit by Princess Frederica of Hanover and Baron Von Pawel-Rammingen, and shines in the setting sun as travelers pass nearby.

The travelers visit St. Michael’s Mount, finding there a medieval fortress and modern residence above a fishing village of about thirty cottages. They see the chapel, a vault containing the skeleton of a large man, the Chevy Chase Hall, the drawing-room, and the school-room, guided partly by a young St. Aubyn. They admire the site but note the many steps, rough causeway, and other difficulties of living there.
