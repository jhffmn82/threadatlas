Landewednack in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

Landewednack is a parish containing the comparatively modern Lizard Town. On the journey’s fourth day it is the narrator’s destination for an afternoon church visit; its name is said to mean the white-roofed church of St. Wednack in ancient Cornish. By the fifth day it prepares for a harvest festival, with ticketed tea in the rectory garden and an evening thanksgiving service. Its rectors reportedly included one who died aged 120 in 1683.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

DAY THE FOURTH Sunday, September 4th--and we had started on September 1st; was it possible we had only been travelling four days?

Landewednack is the parish containing the comparatively modern Lizard Town and is the destination of the narrator's afternoon church visit. Its name is said to mean the white-roofed church of St. Wednack in ancient Cornish.

DAY THE FIFTH "Hope for the best, and be prepared for the worst," had been the motto of our journey.

Landewednack is to hold a harvest festival the next day. The whole community and their wives are invited by shilling tickets to tea in the rectory garden and later to an evening thanksgiving service in the church.
