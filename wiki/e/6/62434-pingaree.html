  • In Rinkitink in Oz: Pingaree is a small, beautiful, green, heavily wooded island kingdom four days by boat north of Rinkitink and four miles from north to south. Ruled by King Kitticut, it is home to Prince Inga, Queen Garee, and the people of Pingaree. Its central grove, northern palace, well, trees, and sea sustain it; it has no animals, including no talking animals. Pingaree’s greatest wealth is its admired pearl fisheries and pearl trade with the Kingdom of Rinkitink, which bring prosperity and glory, though the rich kingdom is defenseless and vulnerable to invasion. At first peaceful and prosperous, Pingaree welcomes King Rinkitink. Then the men of Regos and Coregos invade by sea, destroy the palace and homes, seize boats, treasures, and valuables, and enslave or scatter the people, including Kitticut and Garee. The island is left barren, desolated, and deserted except for Inga, Rinkitink, and Bilbil, who find food and shelter among the ruins before departing. Inga’s later efforts focus on recovering Pingaree’s plundered goods, freeing its captive people, and reuniting his family. After the royal family is reunited and the people are freed, Pingaree is restored. Its inhabitants return, its homes and palace are rebuilt, its goods are recovered, and Nikobob is appointed to oversee rebuilding and the pearl fisheries. Prosperity returns. Finally, Kitticut, Garee, Inga, Rinkitink, and Prince Bobo come back to the renewed island kingdom, which receives the people of Gilgad for three days of feasting before giving Rinkitink a royal farewell from its shore.

Narrative, section by section

20 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter One

Pingaree is a small, beautiful, heavily wooded island ruled by King Kitticut. Its people harvest exceptionally large pearls, and the island is prosperous and peaceful despite Kitticut's fear of invasion.

Chapter Two

Pingaree receives King Rinkitink as a guest after his boat reaches the island. Its people carry Rinkitink's boxes to the palace and are astonished by his dispute with the talking goat.

Chapter Three

Pingaree is invaded by the men of Regos and Coregos. Its palace and houses are destroyed, its inhabitants are captured, and its treasures, boats, and other valuables are carried away.

Chapter Four

Pingaree is left deserted after the invaders carry away its people and valuables and destroy or burn its houses. Its remaining inhabitants are Prince Inga, King Rinkitink, and Bilbil.

Chapter Five

Pingaree remains devastated after the invasion, with the palace reduced to ruins. Inga, Rinkitink, and Bilbil find shelter and food there while preparing to leave the island.

Chapter Six

Pingaree is the island where Inga finds the boat and gathers food, water, oysters, and grass for the voyage. It gradually disappears from sight as the boat travels north across the Nonestic Ocean.

Chapter Seven

Pingaree is raided and despoiled by the forces of Regos and Coregos. Its houses are torn down, its boats are captured, and its people are enslaved, but Prince Inga later comes from the south to seek their freedom.

Chapter Eight

Pingaree is the homeland to which Inga hopes to return after rescuing his parents and its people. It had previously been despoiled, and its people remain captive or missing from Inga's perspective.

Chapter Nine

Pingaree is the far island from which the boy Prince came to conquer King Gos. Nikobob tells his family about the Prince of Pingaree.

Chapter Ten

Pingaree is the island from which King Gos has plundered goods and whose people he and Queen Cor have enslaved. Prince Inga comes to recover those goods and liberate its people.

Chapter Thirteen

Pingaree is the homeland of Inga, Queen Garee, and the enslaved people. Inga promises that the freed women and children will be restored to their homes there.

Chapter Fourteen

Pingaree is the home of Inga, King Kitticut, the Queen of Pingaree, and the people whom Inga is trying to rescue. The women and children are in Coregos, while the men have been enslaved in the mines of Regos.

Chapter Fifteen

Queen Cor believes Pingaree is as good as conquered by Prince Ingato. King Kitticut and Queen Garee are taken away from the island so they cannot be used to help liberate its people.

Chapter Sixteen

Pingaree is being restored after its homes and royal palace were destroyed and its valuables taken. Its people return with recovered goods, and Nikobob is appointed to manage the rebuilding and pearl fisheries.

Chapter Seventeen

Pingaree is described by King Gos as a small island north of Regos and Coregos. Its people have been scattered and the island desolated after King Kitticut and Queen Garee were captured.

Chapter Twenty

Pingaree is recorded as having been destroyed, while King Kitticut, Queen Garee, and the people of Pingaree were captured. Inga escapes and attempts to rescue them.

Chapter Twenty-One

Pingaree is represented by its royal family, King Kitticut and Queen Garee, who are reunited with Inga after being held captive. The family then travels to the Land of Oz.

Chapter Twenty Two

Pingaree is the home to which the party expects to sail after the festivities. Rinkitink's song celebrates their plan to return there together.

Chapter Twenty Three

Pingaree is the island kingdom to which King Kitticut, Queen Garee, Inga, Rinkitink, and Prince Bobo return. Its people have rebuilt their homes, the palace has been reconstructed, and the kingdom’s prosperity is renewed.

Chapter Twenty-Four

Pingaree receives the people of Gilgad and hosts three days of extraordinary feasting and merriment before Rinkitink leaves. Its people give Rinkitink a royal farewell from the shore.
