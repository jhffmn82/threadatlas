Tintagel in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

Tintagel is a natural-artificial fortress on a rocky sea promontory, identified as King Arthur’s legendary birthplace and as a castle associated with Ygrayne and the duke of Cornwall. It is also presented as the home of King Mark, Sir Tristram, and Iseult of Ireland. Approached through lonely country, its black rock and surrounding sea are seen at sunset. Travelers visit its decaying ruins, climb its rocks, and find a chapel and spring atop the rock, experiencing a place poised between legend and recorded history.

Tintagel Castle in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

Tintagel Castle is a landmark between legend and actual history, made of two castles, one in the sea and one on mainland heights, joined by a narrow causeway. In the travelers’ first view, its steep, dangerous appearance in fast-coming twilight keeps them on nearby cliffs and fields. Its ruins include a prehistoric-looking bastion and gateway, later remains, a spring, chapel, and small burial-ground. Its history is uncertain, possibly linked to Arthur, King Mark, or Castle Terrabil, while winds, waves, and the sea continually erode and divide it.
