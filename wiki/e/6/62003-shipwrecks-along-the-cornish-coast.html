Abstract written by a model

Shipwrecks are a recurring, often deadly danger along the Cornish coast, particularly at the Lizard and its Stags rocks. In winter, wild storms or dense fogs bring wrecks almost weekly: distress guns sound, lifeboats launch, ships break apart, and notices may report “all hands lost.” The records begin with the coast’s many marked sites of destruction and John’s account of a ship vanishing within ten minutes after striking. They continue with the lighthouse attendant’s repeated winter wrecks at the Lizard, then end with John’s interest in this history and accounts from Gunwalloe, where survivors, cargo, and other wreckage reach shore.

"Shall we get a boat?

Shipwrecks are described as frequent and often deadly along the picturesque Cornish coast. Every half-mile may mark a place where a ship was destroyed, sometimes with the words 'all hands lost,' and John has seen a ship disappear within ten minutes of striking.

" DAY THE EIGHTH And seven days were all we could allow ourselves at the Lizard, if we meant to see the rest of Cornwall.

The lighthouse attendant recounts numerous shipwrecks at the Lizard, especially near the rocks known collectively as the Stags. He says that almost every winter week brings storms or fog, distress guns, lifeboat launches, and ships breaking apart, including one that was destroyed in ten minutes.

" DAY THE NINTH And our last at the Lizard, which a week ago had been to us a mere word or dot in a map; now we carried away from it a living human interest in everything and everybody.

Shipwrecks are John's favorite subject and are a recurring feature of the Lizard coast's history. The narrator also hears of wrecks at Gunwalloe, where survivors have been saved from rocks and cargo and other remains have washed ashore.
