Abstract written by a model

Polaris is a circumpolar star near the north pole, apparently revolving around the celestial poles every 23 hours 56 minutes. Its specified orbit radius and polar distance support azimuth calculations. Selected to establish a true meridian, its corrected transit altitude gives latitude and its elongation azimuth marks the meridian.

HOW TO ESTABLISH A TRUE MERIDIAN. / INTRODUCTORY. .. ] APPROXIMATE EQUATION OF TIME.

Polaris is selected as the near-circumpolar star used to establish the meridian. Its altitude at meridian transit provides the latitude after correction for refraction and polar distance, while its position at elongation provides an observation from which the meridian can be marked using its azimuth.
