tunnel in The Scarecrow of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1915

The tunnel is an underground, winding passage with a large arched ceiling, though it is straight and smooth enough to walk. Beginning beyond a narrow cavern hole, it leads past a lake below a precipice and a dangerous gulf. Trot, Cap'n Bill, and the Ork travel it for hours by candlelight until it narrows, turns, and ends at a daylight-lit rocky shaft.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Three

The tunnel begins beyond a narrow hole in the cavern and eventually opens into a large passage beyond a dangerous underground gulf. Trot, Cap'n Bill, and the Ork walk through it for hours without finding a change in their surroundings, using candles while their food and light supplies diminish.

Chapter Four

The tunnel narrows and turns sharply before ending at a rocky shaft where daylight is visible from above. Cap'n Bill, Trot, and the Ork complete their passage through it after traveling for a long time.
