the village wind band in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

The village wind band, about six wind instruments played by village musicians, performs determinedly flat with a loud, irregular bass. Compared aspirationally with the Philharmonic and Crystal Palace orchestra, it plays at the rectory tea and near hotels, drives listeners away, and ends with “God save the Queen.”

1 section; each heading opens the text

DAY THE SIXTH And a day absolutely divine!

The village wind band consists of about half a dozen wind instruments that play determinedly flat, with the bass entering loudly without regard to time or tune. It plays at the rectory tea and later near the hotels, causing the narrator and companions to cover their ears and flee.
