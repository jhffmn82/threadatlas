CoV-RAG

method · 1 work · 46 quoted facts · 21 connections
Also called: CoV-RAG (green bars), CoV-RAG (w/ Chain), CoV-RAG Data, CoV-RAG M, CoV-RAG Model, CoV-RAG frame- work, CoV-RAG framework, CoV-RAG model, CoV-RAG-S, chain-of-verification (CoV-RAG), model CoV-RAG
  • In Retrieving, Rethinking and Revising: The Chain-of-Verification Can Improve Retrieval Augmented Generation: CoV-RAG (“Retrieving, Rethinking, and Revising”) is a novel retrieval-augmented generation method, model, and training approach built from a generator and a Chain-of-Verification mechanism. Its central purpose is to improve external retrieval correctness, internal generation consistency, factuality, and generation quality while mitigating hallucinations arising in both retrieval and generation. It integrates verification during both training and inference: it evaluates references and answers, identifies or mitigates generation and contextual-knowledge errors, and, when verification indicates a problem, refines the query for re-retrieval and produces an updated answer. Its full configuration uses multi-iteration retrieval, including end-stage query revision and a score-to-judge-to-revise chain. During training, CoV-RAG unifies question answering and verification through Chain-of-Thought reasoning. It generates chains of verification containing preferences and their rationales, and trains with retrieval-augmented generation and verification objectives using positive and negative RAG samples. This is intended to improve single-iteration QA as well as consistency and accuracy. For factual open-domain question answering, CoV-RAG uses two-stage retrieval: coarse-grained web search through Chrome followed by fine-grained LLM-augmented retrieval; its adaptability is also tested with Bing Search. It is evaluated across four open-domain question-answering datasets, including Mintaka, and across ChatGLM2-6b, Llama2-7b, Llama2-13b, Vicuna-7b, and Vicuna-13b. Reported results show it outperforming GPT-3, RRR, ChatGPT, Self-RAG, Perplexity.ai, and WebGLM; it achieves 72.2% accuracy with ChatGLM2-6b, and its multi-iteration version generally exceeds CoV-RAG-S. The framework nevertheless has limitations in training-data generation and verification, with multi-round validation and other improvements left for future work.

CoV-RAG in Retrieving, Rethinking and Revising: The Chain-of-Verification Can Improve Retrieval Augmented Generation

Bolei He, Nuo Chen, Xinran He, Lingyong Yan, Zhenkai Wei, Jinchang Luo, and Zhen-Hua Ling · 2024

CoV-RAG (“Retrieving, Rethinking, and Revising”) is a novel retrieval-augmented generation method, model, and training approach built from a generator and a Chain-of-Verification mechanism. Its central purpose is to improve external retrieval correctness, internal generation consistency, factuality, and generation quality while mitigating hallucinations arising in both retrieval and generation. It integrates verification during both training and inference: it evaluates references and answers, identifies or mitigates generation and contextual-knowledge errors, and, when verification indicates a problem, refines the query for re-retrieval and produces an updated answer. Its full configuration uses multi-iteration retrieval, including end-stage query revision and a score-to-judge-to-revise chain. During training, CoV-RAG unifies question answering and verification through Chain-of-Thought reasoning. It generates chains of verification containing preferences and their rationales, and trains with retrieval-augmented generation and verification objectives using positive and negative RAG samples. This is intended to improve single-iteration QA as well as consistency and accuracy. For factual open-domain question answering, CoV-RAG uses two-stage retrieval: coarse-grained web search through Chrome followed by fine-grained LLM-augmented retrieval; its adaptability is also tested with Bing Search. It is evaluated across four open-domain question-answering datasets, including Mintaka, and across ChatGLM2-6b, Llama2-7b, Llama2-13b, Vicuna-7b, and Vicuna-13b. Reported results show it outperforming GPT-3, RRR, ChatGPT, Self-RAG, Perplexity.ai, and WebGLM; it achieves 72.2% accuracy with ChatGLM2-6b, and its multi-iteration version generally exceeds CoV-RAG-S. The framework nevertheless has limitations in training-data generation and verification, with multi-round validation and other improvements left for future work.

Narrative, section by section

9 sections; each heading opens the text

Abstract

CoV-RAG is proposed to improve external retrieval correctness and internal generation consistency. It retrieves new knowledge using revised queries, unifies question answering and verification with Chain-of-Thought reasoning during training, and significantly surpasses state-of-the-art baselines across experiments with different Large Language Model backbones.

Introduction

CoV-RAG, or “Retrieving, Rethinking, and Revising,” adds verification to retrieval augmented generation during training and inference. It evaluates references and answers, revises queries for re-retrieval when needed, and trains models with verified and negative examples to improve factuality.

2 / Methods .. Algorithm 1 CoV-RAG Inference

CoV-RAG combines a generator with a Chain-of-Verification mechanism to enhance factuality and consistency in retrieval-augmented generation. During inference, it can verify an initial answer, trigger re-retrieval, and generate an updated answer.

2.3 / CoV-RAG Training

CoV-RAG enhances an LM in retrieval-augmented generation by generating answers with a chain of verification, including preferences and their rationale. It is trained with RAG and verification objectives using positive and negative RAG samples.

3 / Experiments / 3.1 / Datasets .. 3.3 / Metrics and Retrieval

CoV-RAG is evaluated on factual Open-Domain Question Answering, where it generates responses to factual queries using external knowledge. It uses two-stage retrieval consisting of coarse-grained web search through Chrome and fine-grained LLM-augmented retrieval, and its adaptability is also tested with Bing Search.

4 / Results and Analysis / 4.1 / Main Results .. 4.4 / Further Analysis on Retriever

CoV-RAG outperforms GPT-3, RRR, ChatGPT, Self-RAG, Perplexity.ai, and WebGLM across the reported open-domain question-answering results. Its multi-iteration version generally outperforms CoV-RAG-S, and its performance improves through end-stage revision, a score-to-judge-to-revise chain, and re-retrieval.

5 / Related Work

CoV-RAG enhances generation quality through chain of thought training and improves retrieval reliability through query revising. It is presented as addressing limitations of approaches that combine neither prompting with training nor questions unsuitable for retrieval.

6 / Conclusion

CoV-RAG is a novel retrieval-augmented generation method that mitigates hallucinations during internal generation and external retrieval. Experiments across different language model backbones show that it detects generation errors and improves generation quality.

Limitations

The CoV-RAG framework has limitations concerning its training-data generation and verification process. The text leaves multi-round validation and other improvements to the framework for future work.
