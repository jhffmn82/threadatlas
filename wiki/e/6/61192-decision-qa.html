Decision QA in PlanRAG: A Plan-then-Retrieval Augmented Generation for Generative Large Language Models as Decision Makers

Myeonghwa Lee, Seonho An, Min-Soo Kim · 2024

Decision QA is a proposed decision-making task and benchmark for answering the best decision, dbest, for a decision-making question using business rules and a structured database. It requires more than answering: a language model must plan, retrieve data through data-analysis queries because the database may be too large for its input, analyze the retrieved data, select a best decision, and possibly re-plan. The task concerns complex questions that require considering both business rules and business situations represented in the database. It supports relational and graph databases; its RDB settings include Building with RDB, averaging 579.0 rows, and Locating with RDB, averaging 2038.8 rows, and every question requires access to all tables. PlanRAG addresses the task through planning, retrieval, answering, and possible re-planning. Experiments evaluate decision-making language models in zero-shot, single-run settings, treating an answer as correct when it is semantically identical to the ground-truth best decision. The study focuses on relational and graph databases, leaving hybrid databases, vector databases, and low-level solving methods for future research.

Decision QA is defined as a question-answering task that takes a database, business rules, and a decision-making question as input and generates the best decision as output. The task requires planning and data analysis in addition to answering.

Decision QA is the task of answering the best decision given a decision-making question, business rules, and a structured database that follows a schema. The language model retrieves data from a database that is too large to fit in its input by posing data analysis queries.

Methodology: PlanRAG

Decision QA concerns answering the best decision, dbest, for a given question, schema, and rules. PlanRAG addresses it through planning, data retrieval, answering, and possible re-planning.

Experiments / Experimental Setup .. Results and Analysis

Decision QA is the task used to evaluate decision-making LMs on questions requiring database retrieval, analysis, and selection of a best decision. The experiments use zero-shot, single-run evaluation and count an answer as correct when it is semantically identical to the ground-truth best decision.

Related Work

Decision QA is the benchmark discussed in the unit. Its RDB-based settings average 579.0 rows for Building with RDB and 2038.8 rows for Locating with RDB, with every question requiring access to all tables.

Conclusions

Decision QA is a proposed task that answers the best decision for a given complex decision-making question. The task requires considering both business rules and business situations represented in a large database in either RDB or GDB.

Limitations

The study focuses on Decision QA using graph database or relational database. Decision QA based on hybrid databases or vector databases, as well as low-level methods for solving Decision QA, is left for future research.

DQA in PlanRAG: A Plan-then-Retrieval Augmented Generation for Generative Large Language Models as Decision Makers

Myeonghwa Lee, Seonho An, Min-Soo Kim · 2024

DQA is a benchmark for examining Decision QA, proposed because no existing benchmark can do so. It contains Locating and Building scenarios based on 301 specific situations from Europa Universalis IV and Victoria 3. Each set includes a database, a question, and an answer or ground truth, with decision results deterministically calculated and recorded by developed game simulators as annotations. DQA uses business rules as language-model input, and prompt-structure experiments sample questions from each database setting. In experiments, Llama 2 and Phi-2 versions of IterRAG-LM and PlanRAG-LM cannot solve any problems in this setting.

DQA benchmark in PlanRAG: A Plan-then-Retrieval Augmented Generation for Generative Large Language Models as Decision Makers

Myeonghwa Lee, Seonho An, Min-Soo Kim · 2024

DQA is a Decision QA benchmark built from Europa Universalis IV and Victoria 3 game data, using recorded decision results as annotations because real-world business data was difficult to use. It begins with two scenarios, Locating and Building, and contains 301 database-question pairs: 200 Locating and 101 Building. For each question it provides relational and graph database versions, totaling 602 databases, queried with SQL and Cypher Query Language. Nation names are anonymized as three-letter codes. Quality control omits low-impact countries and filters Building problems with multiple answers. It considers the EULA and is open-sourced under the MIT license.
