Solitaire

Abstract written by a model

The Solitaire is Jack Shedd's mine, apparently a contact vein in a porphyry-and-limestone ledge up to thirty feet wide. Its quartz carries sulphides and visible, free-milling horn silver with high reported assays, though a later unconfirmed claim called it only a blind lead.

Solitaire is in one work on this site, Scientific American Supplement No. 360, November 25, 1882. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

] A CHARACTERISTIC MINING "RUSH.

The Solitaire was Jack Shedd's location and showed what appeared to be a contact vein with porphyry and limestone walls, in places forming a thirty-foot-wide ledge. Its quartz vein carried sulphides and visible horn silver, with reported average assays of $350 to $500, but a later claim that it was only a blind lead lacked confirmation.
