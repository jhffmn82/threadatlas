the Chorus in The Electra of Euripides

Euripides · -0419/-0409

The Chorus is a group of Argive women, maids of Argos, who faithfully support Electra. They visit her at dawn for Hera’s festival and urge joy, but she remains mourning. They recount the golden lamb and Agamemnon’s murder, lament the family’s suffering, celebrate Orestes’s return and Aegisthus’s killing with song and dance, praise Justice, and end with a blessing against mischief.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

ELECTRA

The Argive women form the chorus and visit Electra at dawn in festive dress before Hera's festival. They urge her to accept joyful clothing and worship, but Electra rejects celebration because she remains absorbed in mourning and resentment.

CHORUS.

The Chorus tells the story of the golden lamb, sings of the change brought by its theft, and then celebrates Orestes's victory over Aegisthus. It praises Justice and joins Electra in rejoicing that the old king's son has returned.

LEADER.

The Chorus recounts Clytemnestra’s killing of Agamemnon, laments the murders, and describes Electra and Orestes as suffering under the returning consequences of wrong. It ends by blessing the person who can pass through life without stumbling into mischief.
