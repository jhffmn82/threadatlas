Orestes' exile

Abstract written by a model

Orestes' exile is a banishment caused by political danger that separates him from Argos and Electra. As a boy, an old servant takes him from Argos to King Strophios in Phocis to save him from Aegisthus. He wanders with enough bread but suffers humiliation, secretly returns while thought dead, and is then ordered to leave Argos for Athens and later Arcady.

Orestes' exile is in one work on this site, The Electra of Euripides. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

ELECTRA

Orestes was taken from Argos as a boy by an old servant and brought to King Strophios in Phocis to save him from Aegisthus. He has wandered in exile, possesses enough bread to live but suffers the weakness and humiliation of banishment, and now secretly returns.

[ORESTES _and_ PYLADES _go in, following the_ SERVANTS.

Orestes has been away in exile, and his name is regarded throughout Argos as belonging to someone overthrown and dead. His return is celebrated by the Chorus as a coming back to the house that did not know him.

LEADER.

Orestes must leave Argos and remain away from the earth where his mother lies. Electra also departs Argos to marry Pylades, while Orestes is directed toward Athens and later toward a dwelling in Arcady.
