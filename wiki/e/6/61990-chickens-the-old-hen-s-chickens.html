chickens (the old hen's chickens) in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

The chickens are two young female companions of the narrator, one in her teens and one in her twenties, described as young adventurers and bathers among the narrator's party. Expected to follow instructions, travel lightly, avoid needless risks, and support the group's health and happiness, they repeatedly visit sea-pools and search rocks and little pools for sea-treasures. They gather blackberries for pudding, collect flowers and heaths, and explore the Lizard's cove of sea-weeds and sea-anemones before the tide rises. Later they scramble over Penolver and return enthusiastic about its cliff walk. On the last day, they cross slippery rocks, board John's boat, assist at a difficult landing, gather apples, and travel to Helstone.

The narrator's chickens are two female companions, one just entering her teens and the other in her twenties. They are expected to obey the narrator's instructions, carry little luggage, avoid needless risks, and help preserve the group's health and happiness.

DAY THE SECOND Is there anything more delightful than to start on a smiling morning in a comfortable carriage, with all one's _impedimenta_ (happily not much!

The two young companions gather blackberries for the next day's pudding and later collect flowers and heaths. At the Lizard, they explore a cove filled with pools, sea-weeds, and sea-anemones, scrambling over the rocks before the tide covers it.

" DAY THE EIGHTH And seven days were all we could allow ourselves at the Lizard, if we meant to see the rest of Cornwall.

The young folks go walking and scrambling over Penolver while the narrator sits quietly by the sea. They return full of enthusiasm for the cliff walk and regret that the narrator could not join them.

" DAY THE NINTH And our last at the Lizard, which a week ago had been to us a mere word or dot in a map; now we carried away from it a living human interest in everything and everybody.

The narrator's children cross the slippery rocks and board John's boat, then help with the difficult landing. They later gather apples from a cottage tree and travel with the narrator to Helstone.
