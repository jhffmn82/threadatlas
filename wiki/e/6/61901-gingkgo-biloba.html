Various · 1881

Gingkgo biloba is a conifer, closely allied to the Yew, and presently the sole living representative of its genus. It existed during the coal period and tertiary epoch; related fossil forms were widespread in the northern hemisphere, while its truly wild state is unknown. Known to Kaempfer in 1690 and described in 1712, it reached France from England in the middle eighteenth century. Separate male and female plants bear catkins or flowers, then drupaceous fruit and seeds with embryos; germination produces fleshy cotyledons and leaves in threes.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE MAIDENHAIR TREE.

Gingkgo biloba is described as the sole living representative of its genus, though related fossil forms were widespread in the northern hemisphere. It was first known to Kaempfer in 1690, described by him in 1712, and introduced into the country in the middle of the eighteenth century.
