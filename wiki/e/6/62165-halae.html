Halae

place · 1 work · 2 quoted facts · 3 connections

Abstract written by a model

Halae, an Attic boundary place near Karystus’ Rock, receives Athena’s commanded temple for Artemis’ image and holds Artemis Tauropolos’ festival: a human victim reaches the altar, is sword-touched at the throat, then released.

Halae is in one work on this site, Iphigenia in Tauris. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Halae in Iphigenia in Tauris

Euripides · 413 to 411 BC · the work’s page · read it where Halae first appears

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

PREFACE

Halae in Attica held a festival at which a human victim was led to the altar of Artemis Tauropolos, touched on the throat with a sword, and then released.

CHORUS. [STROPHE.]

Halae is identified as a sacred place on the boundaries of Attica near Karystus’ Rock. Athena commands that a temple for the image of Artemis be built there.
