Abstract written by a model

The Baskerville mystery is the investigation of Sir Charles Baskerville’s murder and the threat to Sir Henry. Holmes first examines whether a crime occurred, how Sir Charles died, and clues including footprints, movements, the moor-gate, and fear-induced flight. As Stapleton’s family connection and Mrs. Laura Lyons’s testimony emerge, the case becomes connected. It ends with Holmes identifying Stapleton’s scheme to gain the estate by killing both men and disguising it through the hound legend.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3.

The investigation into Sir Charles's death has not yet formally begun, but Holmes identifies two initial questions: whether a crime was committed and what the crime was and how it occurred. He begins by analyzing the footprints, Sir Charles's movements, the moor-gate, and the possibility that someone or something frightened him into running.

Chapter 13.

The investigation advances through the discovery of Stapleton's connection to the Baskerville family and Mrs. Laura Lyons's testimony. Holmes says the case is becoming a connected narrative, but he still wants clearer evidence before proceeding against the 'very wily man.'

Chapter 15.

The Baskerville mystery concerned Stapleton’s effort to obtain the Baskerville estate through the deaths of Sir Charles and Sir Henry. Holmes explains that the mystery was made to appear supernatural through the family hound legend.
