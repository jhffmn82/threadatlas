Various · 1881

Slate, once imported mainly from Wales for colonial gravestones, roofing, and schools, became increasingly quarried in America during the nineteenth century, reaching an annual capacity of 20,000 cases valued at $10 each by 1860.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE BUILDING STONE SUPPLY.

Slate was used in colonial times for gravestones and to some extent for roofing and school purposes, though most supplies then came from Wales. American quarrying expanded during the nineteenth century, particularly in Pennsylvania, Maine, Vermont, New York, Maryland, and Michigan.
