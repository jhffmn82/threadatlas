the Baskerville family in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901/1902

The Baskerville family is an old, masterful line rooted in Baskerville Hall, where it has lived for five hundred years and which it has owned through its caretakers for four generations. Its identity is dominated by a hereditary legend: since the death of its ancestor Hugo Baskerville, a supernatural or demonic hound is said to have plagued the family, with many members suffering sudden, bloody, mysterious deaths. The hall’s portraits and coats of arms preserve the family’s long presence, while the legend remains a serious superstition for Sir Charles and a childhood story for Sir Henry. After Sir Charles’s death, Sir Henry inherits the estate as the last surviving representative, seeking to keep the title, house, land, and money together; the distant Desmond cousins would succeed if he died. Rodger Baskerville, the family’s black sheep, died in Central America in 1876, and the later account identifies Stapleton as connected to the family through Rodger.

The Hound of the Baskervilles

The Baskervilles are associated with a curse that is the subject of a listed chapter. The contents also refer to Sir Henry Baskerville and Baskerville Hall.

Chapter 2.

The Baskerville family is associated with a legend that a supernatural hound has plagued it since Hugo Baskerville's death. The manuscript states that many family members have suffered sudden, bloody, and mysterious deaths.

Chapter 3.

The Baskerville family is described as an old, masterful family whose members have suffered repeated evil fates when they went to Baskerville Hall. Sir Henry is the last surviving Baskerville, while Rodger Baskerville, the family's black sheep, died in Central America in 1876.

Chapter 4.

The Baskerville family is associated with a long-standing story about a hound, which Sir Henry has heard since childhood. Sir Henry is the last representative of the family and has inherited its estate after his uncle’s death.

Chapter 5.

The Baskerville family is represented in the succession by Sir Henry Baskerville and, if he dies, by the distant Desmond cousins. The family title, house, land, and money are discussed as assets that Sir Henry believes should remain together.

Chapter 6.

The Baskerville family has lived at Baskerville Hall for five hundred years and has left a deep mark on the area. Sir Henry Baskerville is described as a proud, strong, and fiery descendant of that line. The family’s ancestors are depicted in portraits and coats of arms throughout the hall.

Chapter 15.

The Baskerville family was connected to a legend about a demonic hound. Stapleton belonged to the family through Rodger Baskerville, the younger brother of Sir Charles.
