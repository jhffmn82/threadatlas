Air in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Air begins mixed with land and sea and void of light; once separated, it stands next to heaven, heavier than fire and lighter than water, contains clouds, transmits light, and belongs by nature to no one.

opening

The air is initially mixed with the land and sea and is void of light. Once separated from the other elements, it takes the place next to the heaven in both lightness and position.

atmospheric air in Scientific American Supplement No. 360, November 25, 1882

Various · 1881

Air is a gas mixed with gas before combustion. At 14.7 pounds per square inch and 62 degrees, 13.141 cubic feet weighs one pound, or about one ton per 30,000 cubic feet. In a fan, centrifugal force accelerates it outward; it tends tangentially, and blade pressure corresponds to a velocity-determined column weight.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE EFFICIENCY OF FANS.

Air possesses weight, with approximately 13.141 cubic feet weighing one pound at a pressure of 14.7 pounds per square inch and a temperature of 62 degrees. In a fan, air is accelerated outward by centrifugal force and tends to move tangentially to the blade path rather than directly radially. The pressure against the blades corresponds to the weight of a column of air whose height is determined by the air's velocity.
