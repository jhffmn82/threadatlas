the fishermen of the Lizard in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

The fishermen of the Lizard are generally unpaid or minimally paid volunteers who assist coast-guard men in rescue work when ships wreck, especially when the lifeboat is insufficient. They endure the community’s winter hardship and dangerous winter seas. In the Brest wreck, they rescue survivors, take women and children ashore, and shelter survivors in fishermen’s cottages and the parsonage.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

"Shall we get a boat?

The lifeboat volunteers are local volunteers who join rescue efforts when ships wreck. They may be needed because the lifeboat is not always sufficient, and the parson is often at the head of them.

" DAY THE EIGHTH And seven days were all we could allow ourselves at the Lizard, if we meant to see the rest of Cornwall.

The fishermen often volunteer in rescue work and receive little or nothing. They face the dangerous winter seas alongside the coastguard men.
