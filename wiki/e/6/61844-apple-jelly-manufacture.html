  • In Scientific American Supplement No. 360, November 25, 1882: uses apples [including natural or ungrafted, crabbed, gnarled, and frozen fruit] Apple jelly manufacture at the Mexico factory begins with apples being washed by water, grated, and pressed, after which the cider is clarified in a steam-heated defecator and concentrated in an evaporator. The process uses water power for the press and other machinery, steam at about twenty-five to thirty pounds per square inch for clarification and evaporation, and careful cleaning to prevent fermentation. The manufacture also recovers apple seeds from the pomace, and the seeds are valuable enough to pay the daily wages of all the workers.
