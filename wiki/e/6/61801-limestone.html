limestone in Scientific American Supplement No. 360, November 25, 1882

Various · 1881

Limestone is a building and road material, used where granite or sandstone is not readily available and sometimes hammer-dressed to resemble granite. It is especially suited to macadamizing roads and sidewalks. Quarried on the premises, it is burned in a kiln into lime for building; heat expansion requires the kiln well to contract toward the top for drawing out lime.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE BUILDING STONE SUPPLY.

Limestone was used for building where granite or sandstone was not readily available, and hammer-dressed limestone could resemble granite. It was also widely used for roads and sidewalks and was burned into lime, although the quality of lime varied by region.

A FARMER'S LIME KILN.

The kiln was constructed from limestone quarried on the premises. The limestone is burned in the kiln, and its expansion from heat makes a contraction toward the top of the well necessary for the lime to be drawn out.
