adult specimen of Rheinardius ocellatus in Scientific American Supplement No. 360, November 25, 1882

Various · 1881

A perfectly adult, magnificently preserved specimen called “THE OCELLATED PHEASANT” was captured in unexplored Tonkin and given to the Museum of Natural History of Paris by Mr. Maingonnat. Its tail feathers exactly matched museum feathers; it was neither peacock nor ordinary Argus of Malacca.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

|__________|____________| THE OCELLATED PHEASANT.

The adult specimen was described as being in a state of preservation that left nothing to be desired. Its tail feathers were absolutely identical with the previously known feathers preserved at the Museum of Natural History of Paris.
