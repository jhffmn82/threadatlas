Grimpen Mire in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901/1902

Grimpen Mire, also called the Great Grimpen Mire, is a huge, fog-covered moorland bog and morass, foul, deceptive, and dangerous to cross. Its quagmires, slime, hidden depths, and apparently safe green patches can swallow moor ponies or people; one pony disappears and dies, and another seems to be swallowed before Watson and Jack. The cry on the moor is believed to have come from its direction or borders. Jack says he can reach isolated places by following complex landmarks, but warns that entering without a guide is almost certainly fatal. Beyond its bog lies an island of refuge, containing an old tin mine that may be Stapleton’s refuge. Dense fog later conceals the mire and hinders pursuit. Stapleton uses his knowledge of its marked path, learned during insect hunts, to hide and kennel the hound; Anthony likely tends it. The mire may finally bury Stapleton permanently.
