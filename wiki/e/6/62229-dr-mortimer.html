Abstract written by a model

Dr. James Mortimer is a married medical doctor: a country practitioner based in Grimpen, medical officer for Thorsley and High Barrow, and formerly house surgeon at Charing Cross Hospital from 1882 to 1884. A presentation stick given by hospital friends at his marriage marked his departure from the hospital and from hopes of a consulting practice. He is described as a humble M.R.C.S., an unpractical but precise-minded scientific man, a dabbler in science, and learned in his own line. His scientific record includes the Jackson prize for Comparative Pathology, corresponding membership in the Swedish Pathological Society, articles in The Lancet and Journal of Psychology, comparative-anatomical interests in the Bushman and Hottentot, and amateur archaeology, including a prehistoric skull excavated at Long Down. He owns a curly-haired spaniel and is assessed by Sherlock Holmes as entirely honest. Mortimer’s central role concerns Sir Charles Baskerville. As Sir Charles’s personal friend, medical attendant, trustee, executor, and keeper of the Baskerville manuscript, he brings Holmes the manuscript and report of Sir Charles’s death. He recounts Sir Charles’s fear of the family legend, huge hound-like footprints near the body, and reports of a luminous spectral creature, and he entertains the possibility of a Baskerville demon. Concerned for Sir Henry Baskerville, he consults Holmes, brings Sir Henry to Baker Street, explains the inheritance and its succession, and helps frame the warning as possibly supernatural. He receives one thousand pounds under Sir Charles’s will, while also reporting the legacies to Barrymore and Barrymore’s wife. Mortimer travels with Sir Henry and Dr. Watson to Devonshire, delivers them to Baskerville Hall, and returns to his wife. He knows nearly all residents within driving distance, identifies Laura Lyons and the support she received, and reports information about Barrymore, Sir Charles’s weak heart, and Sir Henry’s arrival. He later visits Holmes and Watson with Sir Henry before their intended long voyage, which he advises to restore Sir Henry’s nerves. He is followed from Holmes’s house by a black-bearded spy and remains a companion of Baskerville throughout the affair.

Dr. Mortimer in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Narrative, section by section written by a model

9 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 1.

Dr. James Mortimer is identified as a young M.R.C.S. and country practitioner serving Grimpen, Thorsley, and High Barrow. He returns to recover his presentation stick and explains that friends at Charing Cross Hospital gave it to him on the occasion of his marriage, which led him to leave the hospital and abandon hopes of a consulting practice. He describes himself as an unpractical scientific man and asks Mr. Sherlock Holmes for help with a serious and extraordinary problem.

Chapter 2.

Dr. James Mortimer, Sir Charles Baskerville's personal friend and medical attendant, brings Sherlock Holmes the Baskerville manuscript and a newspaper account of Sir Charles's death. He explains Sir Charles's fear of the family legend, describes the circumstances surrounding the death, and states that he found footprints resembling those of a gigantic hound near the body.

Chapter 3.

Dr. Mortimer reports that he saw enormous marks near Sir Charles Baskerville's body and that several people had seen a luminous, spectral creature on the moor. Acting as trustee and executor of Sir Charles's will, he asks Holmes what to do with Sir Henry Baskerville. Holmes instructs him to meet Sir Henry at Waterloo Station and bring him to Baker Street the next morning.

Chapter 4.

Dr. Mortimer brings Sir Henry Baskerville to meet Sherlock Holmes and helps present the case concerning the Baskerville family. He argues that the warning letter may have come from someone convinced that the business was supernatural. He believes Sir Henry’s missing boot was merely mislaid and later tells Sir Henry the full account of his inheritance and his uncle’s death.

We heard the steps of our visitors descend the stair and the bang

Dr. Mortimer leaves with Baskerville and walks ahead of Holmes and Watson toward Oxford Street and Regent Street. He and Baskerville eventually vanish from Holmes and Watson's view.

Chapter 5.

Dr. Mortimer tells Holmes that Barrymore is a full-bearded man in charge of Baskerville Hall and that Barrymore and his wife each received five hundred pounds from Sir Charles Baskerville. He says that he received a thousand pounds and explains the succession of the Baskerville estate if Sir Henry dies.

Chapter 6.

Dr. Mortimer travels with Sir Henry Baskerville and Dr. Watson to Devonshire. He says that he and Sir Henry have not been shadowed during the previous two days, although they separated for parts of the previous day. He leaves Baskerville Hall after bringing Sir Henry and Dr. Watson there because his wife is expecting him.

Chapter 10.

Dr. Mortimer gives Watson a lift from the moor and identifies Laura Lyons as the local woman whose initials are L. L. He explains that she is Frankland’s daughter, lives in Coombe Tracey, and was helped by several people, including Sir Charles, to establish a typewriting business.

Chapter 15.

Dr. Mortimer informed Stapleton that Sir Charles had a weak heart and later provided details about Sir Henry’s arrival. He visited Holmes and Watson with Sir Henry before their planned long voyage.
