Abstract written by a model

This ACL 2024 long paper, “An Information Bottleneck Perspective for Effective Noise Filtering on Retrieval-Augmented Generation,” by Kun Zhu, Xiaocheng Feng, Xiyuan Du, Yuxuan Gu, Weijiang Yu, Haotian Wang, Qianglong Chen, Zheng Chu, Jingchang Chen, and Bing Qin, addresses noisy retrieved evidence in retrieval-augmented generation. It argues that large language models can suffer hallucination, knowledge gaps, weak retrieval, and irrelevant or harmful internet material; retrieved passages may therefore degrade question-answering performance. The work frames noise filtering through Information Bottleneck theory: a filter should retain information useful for the ground output while discarding unnecessary information from retrieved passages. Its conditional mutual-information objective balances correctness and conciseness, potentially preserving the useful overlap between evidence and output or reducing irrelevant passages to empty. The paper derives an Information Bottleneck formula to evaluate filters, choose supervised fine-tuning examples, and construct reinforcement-learning rewards. Its method proceeds in two stages. Candidate passage compressions are selected for supervised fine-tuning, then Direct Preference Optimization uses Information Bottleneck-based preferences. The approach is evaluated with LLaMA2, including LLaMA2-13B, on Natural Questions, TRIVIAQA, and HOTPOTQA, using Dense Passage Retriever passages. Across these question-answering settings, the proposed SFT and DPO filtering variants improve exact-match and F1 results, reduce false errors and True-Flip-Rate, and substantially shorten evidence. RANKGPT and LONGLLMLINGUA recover some performance but generally do not exceed the non-retrieval generator, whereas the proposed filter does. Ablations indicate that jointly considering correctness and conciseness, and selecting data with the Information Bottleneck criterion, yields better filtered data and outcomes. The reported compression rate is 2.5%. The conclusion presents the system as an approximation to oracle filtering that outperforms baselines while achieving strong compression. Limitations are that effectiveness depends on a capable, especially white-box, generator and that a predictive flag and additional training iterations involve trade-offs and added cost. The ethics statement notes that filtering can mitigate toxic retrieved material but cannot guarantee its removal. Kun Zhu and Xiaocheng Feng contributed equally, and Bing Qin is the corresponding author.

Abstract

The paper concerns improving retrieval-augmented generation when it must handle real-world noisy data. It introduces information bottleneck theory to filter noise by maximizing mutual information between compression and ground output while minimizing mutual information between compression and retrieved passage. The paper also derives an information bottleneck formula for comprehensive evaluations, supervised fine-tuning data selection, and reinforcement learning rewards. Experiments show improvements across question answering datasets in answer correctness and conciseness, with a 2.5% compression rate.

Introduction

The unit concerns retrieval-augmented generation and the use of information bottleneck theory to reduce noise in retrieved evidence for large language models. It identifies hallucination, domain-specific knowledge gaps, weak retrieval, and noisy or irrelevant internet data as key problems. The proposed method optimizes a noise filter by maximizing useful information and minimizing noise, allowing it to retain the intersection between retrieved passages and generated outputs or compress irrelevant passages to empty. Experiments with LLAMA2 on Natural Questions, TRIVIAQA, and HOTPOTQA report improved correctness and compression compared with RANKGPT, LONGLLMLINGUA, and LLLAMA2.

Related Work

This unit concerns the Information Bottleneck (IB), introduced by Tishby et al. (1999) and Fischer (2020). It characterizes learning as balancing data compression with information retention. Applied to specific tasks, it aims to extract essential task-relevant features while discarding redundant information. The approach has been applied to representation learning, deep learning, document clustering, speech recognition, and summarization.

Noise Filtering .. Reinforcement Learning

This unit concerns Noise Filtering for retrieval-augmented generation, using the Information Bottleneck to balance concise retrieval evidence with preservation of information relevant to ground outputs. It formulates a conditional mutual-information objective that minimizes retained information about retrieved passages while maximizing the likelihood of generating the correct output. The approach selects candidate compressions for supervised fine-tuning and uses Direct Preference Optimization with Information Bottleneck-based preferences for reinforcement learning. Experiments with LLaMA2-13B show that the SFT and DPO variants substantially reduce evidence length while improving open-domain question-answering results.

Experiments / Experimental Settings / Datasets and Retrieval Corpus .. Length of Summary and Conciseness

The experiments evaluate retrieval-augmented question answering on Natural Questions, TRIVIAQA, and HOTPOTQA using Dense Passage Retriever passages and LLAMA2 generators. Retrieved passages often introduce noise, lowering performance, while information-bottleneck filtering improves answer quality, reduces false errors, and achieves high compression. RANKGPT and LONGLLMLINGUA recover some performance but generally do not surpass the non-retrieval generator, whereas the proposed filtering approach improves EM and F1 and reduces the True-Flip-Rate. Additional ablations show that combining conciseness with correctness and using information bottleneck selection produces better filtered data and outcomes.

Conclusion

The unit applies the information bottleneck principle to noise filters in retrieval-augmented generation. It balances conciseness and correctness, using the principle to evaluate noise filtering, select supervised fine-tuning data, and provide rewards for reinforcement learning. A two-stage optimization approaches the oracle filtering objective, and experimental results show that the filter outperforms baseline methods and achieves an impressive compression rate.

Limitations

The method enhances the performance of the noise filtering task on retrieval-augmented generation. Its effectiveness relies on the generator, particularly a white-box generator with robust capabilities, to analyze mutual information involving filtered content ˜X, retrieval content X, and Y. The method introduces an additional predictive flag that decreases True-Flip-Rate (TFR) but may also decrease False-Flip-Rate (FFR). Training iterations mitigate this effect but increase training cost.

Ethics Statement

The ethics statement concerns text generation technology and its potential misuse to produce fake, toxic, or offensive content. It explains that harmful information in retrieved content can influence generated content. The approach aims to mitigate noise from retrieval, including toxic content, but cannot assure that all toxic material will be eliminated.
