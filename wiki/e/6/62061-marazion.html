Marazion in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

Marazion is a quiet town opposite St. Michael's Mount, reached from its shore, with a name linked to Mara-Zion, Jewish colonists, and Phoenician tin trading. Chosen as the women's next resting-place for its history, position, and promised comfort, it first appears after a gorgeous sunset as a picturesque-named but extremely commonplace little town. Its warm welcome and comfortable, home-like old-fashioned inn secure their stay. Though a mild little station, it proves unsuitable for bathing: the small beach is ugly, fishy-smelling, crowded, and covered by dirty shallow water. After visiting the Mount, the travelers leave for St. Ives. Later, the town gathers at beach and station for Princess Frederica of Hanover's expected arrival, watching boats head toward the Mount and speculating about the party.

Marazion is chosen as the women's next resting-place. They are attracted by its history, its position opposite St. Michael's Mount, and a hotel landlord's promise of comfort.

The travelers enter Marazion after a gorgeous sunset and find it, despite its picturesque name, an extremely commonplace little town. They decide to stay there because they receive a hearty welcome at a comfortable, home-like inn.

] DAY THE TENTH I cannot advise Marazion as a bathing place.

The travelers decide that Marazion is unsuitable as a bathing place because its small beach is ugly, fishy-smelling, crowded by the town, and covered by dirty shallow water. They return to their old-fashioned inn there after visiting St. Michael’s Mount and later set out for St. Ives.

DAY THE TWELFTH Monday morning.

Marazion gathers at the beach and station to witness the expected arrival of Princess Frederica of Hanover and her party. Boats follow the two boats carrying luggage and people toward St. Michael's Mount, while residents speculate about the visitors and their plans.
