Northumberland Hotel in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901/1902

The Northumberland Hotel is a London hotel chosen by Baskerville as his town lodging, where Sir Henry Baskerville stays when his warning letter arrives. Its register is examined by Holmes, who finds no interested parties staying there. The hotel’s importance is that Baskerville’s choice becomes known quickly, indicating he is closely followed by people watching from elsewhere.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 4.

Sir Henry is staying at the Northumberland Hotel when the warning letter addressed to him arrives. Holmes infers from the pen and ink that the address may have been written in a hotel and suggests that the sender might be found by examining hotel waste-paper baskets around Charing Cross.

We heard the steps of our visitors descend the stair and the bang

The Northumberland Hotel is the hotel Baskerville chose. Holmes reasons that the pursuer learned of this choice quickly, suggesting that Baskerville was being closely followed.

Chapter 5.

The Northumberland Hotel is where Sir Henry Baskerville is staying and where Holmes examines the register. Holmes learns that the people interested in Sir Henry are staying elsewhere, apparently watching him without wanting to be seen.
