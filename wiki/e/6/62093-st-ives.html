St. Ives in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

St. Ives is a town with dark, narrow, fish-smelling streets, a pilchard fishery, a Town Hall, and Tregenna, an ancient mansion made into an hotel. Travelers reach it by a beautiful but inconvenient road, dislike it and its meal, are shown around by a friendly elderly local man, and hurry back toward Penzance.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

] DAY THE TENTH I cannot advise Marazion as a bathing place.

The travelers visit St. Ives by the less convenient but more beautiful road and find its dark, narrow streets and fish-like smell unattractive. A friendly elderly local man shows them around, discusses the beginning pilchard fishery, and points out his shop and the Town Hall. After seeing the town and eating an untempting meal, they hurry back toward Penzance.
