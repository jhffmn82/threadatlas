Rheinardius ocellatus

animal · 1 work · 22 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: This Gallinacean, This bird, bird, magnificent bird, ocellated pheasant

Abstract written by a model

Rheinardius ocellatus is the type of a new genus in Phasianidae, connected after a manner with Argus and pheasants. More than two meters long, it is dominated by an extraordinary tail comprising about three-fourths of its length. Twelve broad, plane rectrices taper regularly and bear ocellated spots; the tail is held slightly elevated and its point reaches the soil. It has a small head with an erectable brown-and-white piliform occipital crest, silky brown and silvery gray head feathers, and ruddy brown neck feathers. Its blackish upper body and reddish brown underpart are spotted white or cafe-au-lait, with marked wings and remiges. The bird has a dark rose-colored argus-like beak, long spur-less legs, and delicate, partly webbed front toes.

|__________|____________| THE OCELLATED PHEASANT.

Rheinardius ocellatus is identified as the type of a new genus in the family Phasianidae. It has twelve broad, plane, horizontally spread rectrices, a piliform occipital crest, no leg spurs, and a body length of more than two meters, about three-fourths of which is tail.
