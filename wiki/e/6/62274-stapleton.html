Stapleton in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901/1902

Stapleton is a man who appears first as a black-bearded spy falsely claiming to be Sherlock Holmes while following Sir Henry Baskerville and Dr. Watson. In Devonshire he presents himself as Jack Stapleton, a naturalist, former schoolmaster, and entomological authority living at Merripit House near Baskerville Hall with Beryl, whom he calls his sister. He knows the moor and paths through Grimpen Mire, collects Lepidoptera, and cultivates Sir Charles Baskerville as a friend. His apparent concern over Beryl’s relationship with Sir Henry leads him to confront Sir Henry and later apologize. Holmes uncovers that this identity conceals a calculated criminal. Stapleton is Beryl’s husband, not her brother, and a Baskerville descendant, Rodger Baskerville’s son, whose real name was also Rodger Baskerville; he had also used Vandeleur. Seeking the Baskerville estate, he befriends Sir Charles, manipulates Mrs. Laura Lyons with a promise of marriage, dictates her letter to Sir Charles, and frightens her into silence. He causes Sir Charles’s death through terror and heart disease. Stapleton keeps a hound hidden in an old tin mine on an island in the Grimpen Mire, coats it with a luminous mixture to make it seem supernatural, and uses it to murder or frighten his targets. He plans Sir Henry’s death, using a missing boot to give the hound Sir Henry’s scent. When Selden is killed instead, Stapleton conceals his disappointment and continues the deception. He hosts Sir Henry at Merripit House, releases the hound, and flees after Holmes, Watson, and Lestrade intervene. Carrying Sir Henry’s boot, he attempts to cross the mire to his refuge and is believed to be swallowed by Grimpen Mire. His abandoned refuge exposes the hound, the luminous paste, and his plot.

Narrative, section by section

13 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 5.

The black-bearded spy watches Sherlock Holmes's house and follows Sir Henry Baskerville and Dr. Watson in a cab. He hires John Clayton, falsely claims to be a detective named Sherlock Holmes, and escapes through Waterloo Station after anticipating that Holmes would trace the cab.

Chapter 7.

Jack Stapleton introduces himself to Dr. Watson as a naturalist living at Merripit House. He discusses the moor, the Grimpen Mire, the Baskerville family legend, and Sir Charles Baskerville’s death, and he claims to know safe paths through the mire. He pursues a rare insect, then returns and watches Beryl and Watson closely.

A short walk brought us to it, a bleak moorland house, once the

Stapleton lives with Beryl in a repaired former farm on the moor. He says that he formerly ran a school, which was devastated by an epidemic, and that he now devotes himself to botany and zoology. He plans to visit Sir Henry and make his acquaintance.

Chapter 8.

Jack Stapleton shows guarded interest in the legend of the wicked Hugo and appears to share the popular view that an evil influence may affect families. He repeatedly prevents Sir Henry Baskerville and Miss Stapleton from being alone and appears strongly disapproving of their growing intimacy.

Chapter 9.

Stapleton sees Sir Henry with Miss Stapleton on the moor and runs toward them in great anger, interrupting their conversation and taking his sister away. He later apologizes to Sir Henry, explaining that he feared losing his sister, and the breach between them is healed.

Chapter 12.

Jack Stapleton is identified by Sherlock Holmes as the enemy behind the danger to Sir Henry Baskerville. Holmes learns that Stapleton is married to Miss Stapleton but presents her as his sister, and he suspects Stapleton of planning a cold-blooded murder.

A terrible scream—a prolonged yell of horror and anguish—burst

Stapleton approaches Holmes and Dr. John Watson after hearing the cries and seeing the body. He is visibly shocked and disappointed when he learns that the victim is Selden rather than Sir Henry Baskerville, but he conceals his reaction and supports the explanation that Selden died from exposure, madness, and a fall. He asks whether Holmes and Watson heard anything besides a cry, including evidence of the supposed phantom hound.

“We’re at close grips at last,” said Holmes as we walked together

Stapleton has seen Sherlock Holmes, and Holmes believes this may alter his plans. Stapleton would have Sir Henry believe that Selden’s death had the explanation he provided.

Chapter 13.

Mr. Stapleton is identified through the portrait of Hugo Baskerville as a Baskerville descendant who may be seeking the succession. Mrs. Laura Lyons states that he dictated her letter to Sir Charles, deceived her about his marriage, and frightened her into concealing her appointment with Sir Charles.

Chapter 14.

Stapleton entertains Sir Henry at Merripit House and secretly visits an out-house where the hound is kept. He follows the hound during the attack but is not found when Holmes, Watson, and Lestrade search the house; Mrs. Stapleton says he would likely flee to an old tin mine in the Grimpen Mire.

And now I come rapidly to the conclusion of this singular

Stapleton fled through the mire while clutching Sir Henry’s missing boot after using it to set the hound on the track. He apparently never reached the island of refuge and is believed to have been swallowed by the Grimpen Mire. Evidence on the island reveals that he confined the hound, used luminous paste on it, and created the appearance of a supernatural hell-hound.

Chapter 15.

Stapleton was actually a Baskerville descendant whose original name was the same as his father’s; he later used the names Vandeleur and Stapleton. He engineered Sir Charles’s death with a hound, plotted against Sir Henry to obtain the Baskerville estate, abused and imprisoned Mrs. Stapleton, and was driven to his destruction.

“No doubt. There only remains one difficulty. If Stapleton came

Stapleton had been living unannounced under another name close to the property. He had considered claiming the property from South America, disguising himself in London, or using an accomplice to present the proofs and papers of his identity.
