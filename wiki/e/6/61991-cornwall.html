Cornwall in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

Cornwall is a county and coastal region of strong Cornish character, ancestry, speech, and traditions: a respectable, sober-minded, religious-minded place whose inhabitants are portrayed as independent, dependable, generous, courageous, hospitable, and loyal. It contains fishing communities, working mining districts, tidy villages and cottages, old churches, quarries, sea-caves, hedge walls, distinctive rocks and plants, grey cows, deep-cut lanes shared with Devon, and a coast shaped by blue seas, cliffs, caves, shipwrecks, coast-guard courage, and danger from the Atlantic. In fine weather its atmosphere is fresh, dry, mild, balmy, and bracing, with clear skies and sunshine; elsewhere the sea, storms, darkness, and increasingly bare western landscape emphasize its wildness and risk. The records unfold as an Unsentimental Journey by three travelers who begin with a long-held wish to investigate Cornwall simply, avoiding fashionable towns where possible and going around the coast. Early days introduce its old, wild country, rough roads, granite, ancient associations, coastal hazards, and legends. At the Lizard, a place initially only a word or map-dot, the party spends seven days and comes to feel a living human interest in everything and everybody. They then continue toward Marazion by way of Helstone, encountering varied gardens, avenues, mining country, villages, broad views, and practical hospitality, with only one complaint of being charged double for milk. As the journey moves westward, Cornwall appears more barren but also peaceful, solitary, and sunlit. Later travel brings railway, wooded country, moorland, slate quarries, and grand sunsets. The final movement enters King Arthur's land, including Tintagel and Camelford: a sparsely inhabited world of ruins, caves, dramatic coast, and dangerous sea, where Arthurian loves, hatreds, virtues, and crimes seem fitted to the rugged landscape. More than a year afterward, the journey remains remembered as a shared passage through Cornwall.

Cornwall in His Last Bow

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1908-04-17

Cornwall is a peaceful setting of lonely dun-coloured moor, a lonely wood, ancient monuments, coastal cliffs, and villages including Tredannick Wollas. Holmes and Watson stay in a cottage there, investigate the Tregennis events and the Cornish Horror, while Leon Sterndale’s Cornish mother gives him family connections.
