Information Bottleneck

theory · 1 work · 33 quoted facts · 8 connections
Also called: IB, Information Bottleneck theory, information bottleneck, information bottleneck objectives, information bottleneck principle, information bottleneck theory, information bottlenecks, the information bottleneck theory
  • In An Information Bottleneck Perspective for Effective Noise Filtering on Retrieval-Augmented Generation: Information Bottleneck is a theory, concept, and principle for accomplishing a task with minimal information. It characterizes learning as a balance between data compression and information retention: it extracts features essential to a task, preserves information relevant to target signals or ground outputs, and discards redundant or unnecessary information. It has applications in representation learning, deep learning, document clustering, speech recognition, and summarization. In the work, it is introduced into retrieval-augmented generation as a principle and objective for filtering noise from retrieved passages, maximizing useful information while minimizing noise; it can also enable empty compression when retrieval is unnecessary or ineffective. The work provides an Information Bottleneck formula and uses its objective and IB score to balance conciseness or compression against correctness. From this basis, it supports comprehensive evaluation of noise filtering, selection of supervised fine-tuning training data, and construction of preferences or rewards for reinforcement learning. Experiments report improved retrieval filtering, fewer retrieval-induced errors, and filtered training data that outperforms tested exact-search and greedy-search candidates. The conclusion presents it as a way to balance conciseness and correctness across filtering, evaluation, training-data selection, and reinforcement learning.

Information Bottleneck in An Information Bottleneck Perspective for Effective Noise Filtering on Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Kun Zhu, Xiaocheng Feng, Xiyuan Du, Yuxuan Gu, Weijiang Yu, Haotian Wang, Qianglong Chen, Zheng Chu, Jingchang Chen, Bing Qin · 2024

Information Bottleneck is a theory, concept, and principle for accomplishing a task with minimal information. It characterizes learning as a balance between data compression and information retention: it extracts features essential to a task, preserves information relevant to target signals or ground outputs, and discards redundant or unnecessary information. It has applications in representation learning, deep learning, document clustering, speech recognition, and summarization. In the work, it is introduced into retrieval-augmented generation as a principle and objective for filtering noise from retrieved passages, maximizing useful information while minimizing noise; it can also enable empty compression when retrieval is unnecessary or ineffective. The work provides an Information Bottleneck formula and uses its objective and IB score to balance conciseness or compression against correctness. From this basis, it supports comprehensive evaluation of noise filtering, selection of supervised fine-tuning training data, and construction of preferences or rewards for reinforcement learning. Experiments report improved retrieval filtering, fewer retrieval-induced errors, and filtered training data that outperforms tested exact-search and greedy-search candidates. The conclusion presents it as a way to balance conciseness and correctness across filtering, evaluation, training-data selection, and reinforcement learning.
