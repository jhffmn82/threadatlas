Abstract written by a model

The apple jelly factory, largest in the county and near Mexico, New York, used Salmon Creek water power. Erected late in 1880, it had a main-floor saw mill, eight apple-storage bins, and equipment for washing, grating, pressing, defecating, evaporating, and seed recovery. About six men ordinarily produced 1,500–1,800 pounds daily; it made about forty-five tons of jelly in 1880, plus cider and boiled cider, with improvements expected to exceed a ton daily.

THE MANUFACTURE OF APPLE JELLY.

The apple jelly factory near Mexico used Salmon Creek for power and included a saw mill, apple bins, washing equipment, a grater, an iron press, a defecator, an evaporator, and seed-recovery troughs. It ordinarily employed about six men and produced 1,500 to 1,800 pounds of jelly during a ten-hour day; improvements were expected to raise production to more than a ton per day. The factory was erected late in 1880 and produced about forty-five tons of jelly that year, along with cider and boiled cider.
