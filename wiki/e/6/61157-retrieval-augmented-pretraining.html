Retrieval Augmented Pretraining in Enhancing Distractor Generation for Multiple-Choice Questions with Retrieval Augmented Pretraining and Knowledge Graph Integration

Han-Cheng Yu, Yu-An Shih, Kin-Man Law, Kai-Yu Hsieh, Yu-Chen Cheng, Hsin-Chih Ho, Zih-An Lin, Wen-Chuan Hsu, Yao-Chung Fan · 2024

Retrieval Augmented Pretraining (RAP) is a task-specific pretraining approach and data-augmentation design for distractor generation. It refines language-model pretraining by aligning it with the downstream Distractor Generation task and incorporating task-specific priors. RAP begins with MCQ options, using an answer or distractor as a starting point to retrieve a relevant sentence or short passage from an external, abundant corpus such as Wikipedia. It replaces the answer in the retrieved text with a mask token, producing a pseudo question. The pseudo question and ground-truth distractors then train a pre-trained language model to generate distractors during continuous pretraining, after which the model is fine-tuned for Distractor Generation. RAP can use the same dataset or separate datasets across domains. It improves distractor generation, with especially strong gains in some low-resource and cross-domain settings, and can outperform methods without RAP. Its retrieval may fail for rare or specialized answers, and combining it with knowledge graphs has potential for superior results.

Introduction

Retrieval Augmented Pretraining uses MCQ answers to retrieve relevant sentences or passages from a large corpus such as Wikipedia. It replaces the answer in a retrieved sentence with a mask token, then uses the resulting pseudo question and the distractors as training data.

RAP (Retrieval-Augmented Pretraining) is proposed to improve distractor generation by incorporating task-specific priors into pretraining. It aligns pretraining with the downstream distractor generation task and uses relevant task-specific information to improve generated distractors.

Retrieval Augmented Pretraining uses an answer option to retrieve a sentence or short passage, masks the answer in that text, and trains a pre-trained language model to generate the ground-truth distractors. It can be used as data augmentation on the same dataset as Distractor Generation or across domains using separate datasets.
