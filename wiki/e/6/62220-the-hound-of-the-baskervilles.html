Abstract written by a model

The Hound of the Baskervilles is a supposed family ghost in the Baskerville curse, feared by Beryl but dismissed by Dr. Watson. It is actually a strong, half-starved savage dog, hidden in a Grimpen Mire kennel and made terrifying with phosphorus paint by Stapleton. Stapleton uses it to pursue Sir Charles and plans it for Sir Henry, but Holmes and Watson kill it when it attacks Sir Henry.

the Hound of the Baskervilles in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901 to 1902 · the work’s page · read it where the Hound of the Baskervilles first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Hound of the Baskervilles is a gigantic, coal-black hound associated with the Baskerville family and its inherited curse. In the family narrative it is a huge, hellish, diabolical-seeming beast that kills Hugo Baskerville by tearing out his throat; local tradition later treats it as a supernatural creature or phantom that haunts the moor. Its alleged appearance, footprints near Sir Charles Baskerville’s body, and cries on the moor feed fear that the legend is real. Sir Henry, though once dismissive of the tale, becomes alarmed when he and Watson hear a strange cry. Watson rejects a supernatural explanation, while Holmes considers that a real, unusually large hound could account for the reports but lacks direct proof linking it to deaths. The hound is released, follows the scent of Sir Henry’s stolen clothing, and pursues Selden to his death. Holmes and Watson conclude it likely frightened Sir Charles to death as well, though it does not bite dead bodies. The truth is that Stapleton keeps this savage animal chained in an abandoned miners’ cottage on a bog-girt island, using it as his agent. The hound is a gaunt cross between a bloodhound and a mastiff, as large as a small lioness; Stapleton daubs it with a luminous mixture, giving it its phosphorescent, supernatural appearance. It attacks Sir Henry, but Holmes and Watson shoot it repeatedly with revolvers, killing it.

Chapter 2.

The Hound of the Baskervilles is described in the family narrative as a huge, black, hellish beast shaped like a hound. According to the manuscript, it killed Hugo Baskerville, and Dr. James Mortimer claims that footprints of a gigantic hound were found near Sir Charles Baskerville's body.

Chapter 3.

The original hound in the legend is said to have been material enough to tear out a man's throat while also being regarded as diabolical.

Chapter 4.

The hound is part of the Baskerville family’s traditional story and has been told as a pet story since Sir Henry was in the nursery. Sir Henry has not previously taken the story seriously, although it is considered in connection with his uncle’s death and the family’s affairs.

Chapter 7.

The Baskerville hound is described as a fiend dog believed by local peasants to haunt the family and the moor. Jack Stapleton says many peasants claim to have seen it, though he suggests that Sir Charles’s fear of such a creature may have contributed to his death.

The spectral hound remains an explanation considered for the reports of a strange creature and the repeated baying heard on the moor. Watson rejects the supernatural interpretation but admits that even the idea of a real large hound raises difficult questions.

The hound is heard as a deep, menacing rumble accompanying Selden’s screams. Holmes concludes that it was released and attracted to an article of Sir Henry Baskerville’s clothing, probably the boot taken from the hotel, and that it pursued Selden to his death.

The hound is believed by Holmes and Dr. Watson to have frightened Sir Charles to death and to have been involved in the other man’s death, but they have never seen it. They heard it, yet cannot prove that it was following the dead man’s trail, and Holmes says there are no visible marks or other direct evidence connecting it to the deaths.

Chapter 13.

Sir Henry recalls hearing the hound on the moor and insists that it is not merely superstition. Holmes says he intends to muzzle and chain it with Sir Henry's help.

Chapter 14.

The hound is an enormous coal-black animal resembling a combination of a bloodhound and a mastiff. It attacks Sir Henry but is shot several times by Holmes and Watson and dies after Holmes empties five revolver barrels into it.
