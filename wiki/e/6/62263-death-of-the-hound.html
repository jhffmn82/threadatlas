death of the hound in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901/1902

The death of the hound, caused by Holmes’s gunfire, shows that the apparently supernatural animal is mortal. Its glowing jaws and muzzle are traced to phosphorus. By the next morning, Holmes and the others search the mire and its hiding place, finding evidence of its confinement and use.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 14.

The hound’s death proves that it is mortal and exposes the supernatural appearance as a trick. Its jaws and muzzle still seem to glow afterward, and Watson identifies phosphorus on the animal.
