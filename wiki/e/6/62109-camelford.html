Camelford

place · 1 work · 2 quoted facts · 1 connection

Abstract written by a model

Camelford is a respectable, commonplace little town with shops, an inn, and private houses. It is chiefly associated in legend with King Arthur’s last battle, though it is not presented as Camelot. Travelers pass from the town to the River Camel and Slaughter Bridge, said to mark Arthur’s army’s defeat and his death-wound.

Camelford is in one work on this site, An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

DAY THE THIRTEENTH Into King Arthurs land--Tintagel his birth-place, and Camelford, where he fought his last battle--the legendary region of which one may believe as much or as little as one pleases--we were going to-day.

Camelford is described as the place where King Arthur fought his last battle, though the modern town is presented as a commonplace and respectable country town rather than King Arthur’s Camelot. The travelers walk from it to the River Camel and Slaughter Bridge, said to be where Arthur’s army was defeated and where he received his death-wound.
