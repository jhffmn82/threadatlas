Arthurian legends

Abstract written by a model

Arthurian legends are legends centered on King Arthur and associated with Cornwall, Avillion, Lyonesse, Tintagel, Camelford, and beliefs in Arthur’s return or survival in fairy land. They preserve Arthur’s existence despite time and change, while allowing competing accounts of his disappearance, burial, grave, coffin, and the Round Table. Their stories include Ygrayne, Uther Pendragon, King Mark, Tristram, and Iseult, alongside an idealized Arthur and noble knight. They unfold through a legendary region that readers may believe to differing degrees: from Arthur’s birth-place and last battle to traditions of his loss, return, and haunting of the coast. The legends portray a society where might was right and passions brought love and hate, yet sustain ideals of courage, honour, faith, chastity, devotion, service, and love of woman.

The King Arthur legend is connected with Cornwall through beliefs about Avillion, Lyonesse, Arthur's return, and his haunting the coast in the form of a Cornish chough. The narrator also wonders whether the Arthurian type of noble knight survives in modern Cornwall.

DAY THE THIRTEENTH Into King Arthurs land--Tintagel his birth-place, and Camelford, where he fought his last battle--the legendary region of which one may believe as much or as little as one pleases--we were going to-day.

The Arthurian legend is presented as a region of belief that one may accept as much or as little as one pleases. The text recounts competing traditions about Arthur’s grave, the preservation of his coffin and the Round Table at Winchester, and his possible survival in fairy land.

DAYS FOURTEENTH, FIFTEENTH, AND SIXTEENTH-- And all Arthurian days, so I will condense them into one chapter, and not spin out the hours that were flying so fast.

The narrator recounts legends involving Ygrayne and Arthur, King Mark, Tristram and Iseult, Uther Pendragon, and the idealized King Arthur. The legends are described as revealing a society of violence and force, while also preserving a vision of courage, honour, faith, chastity, religious devotion, service, and love of woman.
