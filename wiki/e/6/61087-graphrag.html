GRAG in GRAG: Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Yuntong Hu, Zhihan Lei, Zheng Zhang, Bo Pan, Chen Ling, Liang Zhao · 2025

GRAG, Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation, is a retrieval-augmented generation approach and computational framework for graph-based, graph context-aware generation. It extends traditional Retrieval-Augmented Generation by considering textual graphs in both retrieval and generation, aiming to retrieve an optimal query-relevant textual subgraph and use its textual and topological information to improve the relevance of LLM-generated content. Its central challenge is textual subgraph retrieval, where exhaustive search is NP-hard. GRAG addresses this with a divide-and-conquer, retrieval-then-pruning strategy: it retrieves important K-hop ego-graphs rather than individual entities, merges the top-N relevant ego-graphs, and applies soft pruning to reduce irrelevant graph information while preserving textual and topological information. This produces an approximately optimal subgraph, described as linear-time retrieval. In generation, GRAG supplies two complementary views of the retrieved graph: hierarchical text descriptions as hard prompts through text tokens, and graph-encoder outputs as soft prompts through graph tokens. For ExplaGraphs, whose graphs are small chains, it feeds the entire graph to the LLM and leverages topological information. Reported results show GRAG outperforming tested RAG retrievers, RAG-based LLM baselines, fine-tuned LLMs on all tasks, and LLM baselines in graph reasoning scenarios, especially multi-hop graph reasoning. It reports Hit@1 of 0.7236 on WebQSP, accuracy of 0.9223 on ExplaGraphs, and 79% valid-entity references in a small-scale human evaluation. Its retrieval may perform suboptimally when graph structure or node importance is difficult to estimate. Code and datasets are available at https://github.com/HuieL/GRAG.

Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation in GRAG: Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Yuntong Hu, Zhihan Lei, Zheng Zhang, Bo Pan, Chen Ling, Liang Zhao · 2025-07-12

Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation (GRAG) is a computational framework and Retrieval-Augmented Generation extension for networked documents and graph-based scenarios. It treats documents as textual graphs, considering graph context in both retrieval and generation to improve Large Language Models’ generation and multi-hop graph reasoning. Given a query, its objective is to retrieve an optimal relevant textual subgraph and incorporate that subgraph’s textual and topological information into an LLM. Rather than exhaustively searching subgraphs, an NP-hard challenge, GRAG uses a divide-and-conquer retrieval-then-pruning process: it directly retrieves important ego-graphs, merges the top-N relevant ego-graphs, and applies soft pruning to reduce redundant graph information while approximately preserving the relevant subgraph structure. The retrieved graph is represented through two complementary views: a text view, supplied with hard prompts, and a graph view, encoded by a graph encoder and supplied with soft prompts. It also uses hierarchical graph descriptions of retrieved textual subgraphs, enabling graph context-aware generation through joint textual and topological information. Across evaluated graph reasoning benchmarks, GRAG significantly outperforms tested RAG retrievers, RAG-based LLMs, LLM baselines, and a fine-tuned LLM when using a frozen LLM; reported improvements include WebQSP Hit@1 from 0.4148 to 0.7236 and ExplaGraphs accuracy from 0.3394 to 0.9223 without LLM fine-tuning. It can reduce training cost and has cross-dataset transferability of learned textual graph encoding capabilities. Its limitation is that retrieval may be suboptimal when graph structure or node importance is difficult to estimate.

GraphRAG in A Survey of Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Customized Large Language Models

Qinggang Zhang, Shengyuan Chen, Yuanchen Bei, Zheng Yuan, Huachi Zhou, Zijin Hong, Hao Chen, Yilin Xiao, Chuang Zhou, Junnan Dong, Yi Chang, Xiao Huang · 2025-09-29

GraphRAG, or Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation, is a graph-based subclass of retrieval-augmented generation and a paradigm for domain-specific LLM applications. It organizes external, structured and unstructured knowledge into graph-based resources, then retrieves and integrates relevant knowledge to enrich large language models with organized background knowledge and improved contextual reasoning. Its central value is support for complex and multi-hop reasoning: rather than relying only on conventional retrieval, it can retrieve subgraphs centered on topics or anchor entities, use connected paths, and prune irrelevant information to produce accurate, logically coherent generation. The approach unfolds through knowledge organization, retrieval, and integration. In planning and indexing, it draws concepts and element instances from source documents, summarizes elements, groups them into communities with community-detection algorithms such as Leiden, and creates community summaries. This yields a hierarchical, comprehensive knowledge base, reflecting inspiration from earlier tree-like indexing while using graph structure. For a query, retrieval can obtain node-, path-, or subgraph-level factual knowledge, including community summaries for global inquiries. It then combines community answers into a global answer; it is described as outperforming traditional retrieval on global questions in breadth and diversity of insights. Retrieved graph knowledge is integrated into LLMs through fine-tuning, in-context learning, graph-enhanced reasoning, or knowledge graph refinement. GraphRAG includes Knowledge-based, Index-based, and Hybrid GraphRAG, and is also presented through the paradigms Graphs for Knowledge Indexing, Graphs as Knowledge Carrier, and Hybrid GraphRAG. It supports applications including healthcare, finance, education, legislation, scientific research, and other natural language processing tasks. Its effectiveness depends on external-knowledge quality, while knowledge conflict, data privacy, and efficiency are critical limitations; reconciliation, consistency management, and alignment between external knowledge and generated outputs are required.
