people of Pingaree in Rinkitink in Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1916 · the work’s page · read it where people of Pingaree first appears

Abstract written by a model

The people of Pingaree are mild, peaceful pearl fishers who live on Pingaree, seldom quarrel, and have only oyster rakes as weapons. Though untrained for fighting, they once used their rakes to drive invading warriors back to sea. In a later invasion, they are terrified, captured, bound, and made slaves after failed escapes, leaving Prince Inga alone on the island. The invaders take them away: the men, including King Kitticut, are chained for mine work in Regos, while the women and children are forced into Queen Cor's grain fields in Coregos. Enslaved by King Gos and Queen Cor, they are the central people Inga seeks to free, along with his parents and Pingaree's plundered goods. Prince Ingato also intends to liberate them and return them to Pingaree before seeking his parents.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

8 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter One

The people of Pingaree are mild and peaceful pearl fishers who rarely quarrel and are not trained for fighting. In the earlier attack, they used their oyster rakes as weapons and drove the invading warriors back to the sea.

Chapter Three

The people of Pingaree are surprised and terrified by the invasion. They are captured and bound, and by nightfall all have become slaves of the conquerors; the pearl fishers' attempts to escape are unsuccessful.

Chapter Four

The people of Pingaree are taken away by the invaders to be slaves. No person besides Prince Inga remains on the island after the invasion.

Chapter Six

The people of Pingaree have been taken by the inhabitants of Regos and Coregos. Inga intends to free them and bring them back to Pingaree.

Chapter Seven

The men of Pingaree, including King Kitticut, are chained together and sent to work in the mines of Regos. The women and children are given to Queen Cor and forced to work in the grain fields of Coregos.

Chapter Eight

The people of Pingaree are among those Inga intends to find and rescue. Inga hopes that, once they are freed along with his parents, they can all return to Pingaree and be happy again.

Chapter Ten

Pingaree's people have been made slaves by Queen Cor and King Gos. Inga seeks to free them and recover the goods Gos plundered from Pingaree.

Chapter Fifteen

The people of Pingaree are intended to be liberated by Prince Ingato and taken back to their island. Ingato plans to arrange for them to return to Pingaree before pursuing his parents.
