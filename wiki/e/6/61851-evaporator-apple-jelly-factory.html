evaporator (apple jelly factory)

object · 1 work · 4 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: the evaporator, upper evaporator tube

Abstract written by a model

The evaporator is a system of six twelve-foot, three-inch copper tubes, each with an iron steam jacket. Powered by boiler-furnace steam at twenty-five to thirty pounds pressure, it receives cider from the defecator through a strainer. Sirup passes through for about three minutes, increasing from 20–23 to 30–32 degrees Baume to produce apple jelly; pressure controls reduction.

THE MANUFACTURE OF APPLE JELLY.

The evaporator contains six twelve-foot copper tubes, each surrounded by an iron steam jacket. Sirup passes through the tubes for about three minutes, entering at 20 to 23 degrees Baume and emerging at 30 to 32 degrees Baume, the proper range for jelly after cooling. Steam pressure can be adjusted to control the reduction, and boiled cider can be drawn from an upper tube or produced by lowering the steam pressure.
