Devonshire in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901/1902

Devonshire is the county containing Baskerville Hall and a poor countryside whose prosperity depends on the Hall having a tenant. Dr. Mortimer fears Sir Henry Baskerville may meet the Baskerville fate there. Sir Henry, Dr. Watson, and Dr. Mortimer leave London for Devonshire, where Holmes expects better investigative prospects despite danger. Arriving by train, they find greener, damper, rugged country of granite, wooded valleys, and distant moorland; Sir Henry warmly recognizes it.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 3.

Devonshire is the location of Baskerville Hall and the poor countryside whose prosperity depends on the Hall having a tenant. Dr. Mortimer fears that taking Sir Henry there will expose him to the fate associated with the Baskervilles.

Chapter 5.

Devonshire is the destination for Sir Henry Baskerville and Dr. Watson when they leave London. Holmes believes that the investigation may have better prospects there, although he warns Watson that the situation is dangerous.

Chapter 6.

Sir Henry Baskerville, Dr. Watson, and Dr. Mortimer travel to Devonshire by train. The countryside is described as greener, damper, and more rugged than the country they have left, with granite, wooded valleys, and moorland rising in the distance. Sir Henry recognizes its familiar features and expresses strong affection for the county.
