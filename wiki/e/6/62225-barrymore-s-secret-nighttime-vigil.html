Barrymore's secret nighttime vigil in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901/1902

Barrymore’s secret nighttime vigil is a nocturnal watch at Baskerville Hall. He leaves his room with a candle, enters an unoccupied room, and holds the light at a western window toward the moor with rigid expectation. After groaning, he extinguishes it and returns; Watson hears a key turn, while Sir Henry and Dr. Watson plan to follow him.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 8.

Barrymore’s secret nighttime vigil begins when he quietly leaves his room carrying a candle and enters an otherwise empty room. He holds the candle against the window, watches the darkness of the moor with rigid expectation, groans, extinguishes the light, and later returns; Watson also hears a key turn in a lock.

Chapter 9.

Barrymore’s nocturnal watch takes place from a western window of Baskerville Hall that overlooks the nearest part of the moor through an opening between two trees. Sir Henry and Dr. Watson plan to sit up and follow him to discover what he is watching.
