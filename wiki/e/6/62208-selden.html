Selden in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901/1902

Selden is an escaped convict from Princetown, identified as the Notting Hill murderer, whose ferocity and brutality make him feared by local farmers and pursued by warders. Desperate and believed capable of stopping at nothing, he hides on the moor, though residents later think he has left after no sightings. He is Eliza Barrymore’s younger brother, and Barrymore secretly supplies him with food and provisions. Watson and Sir Henry discover him through his signal light, but he escapes by throwing a rock and outrunning them. Selden says another man is also concealed on the moor and supplied by a boy. He plans to travel to South America once arrangements are made. During his escape, he wears Sir Henry’s clothing; the hound tracks him, and he falls over rocks, breaks his neck, and is found dead on the moor.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 6.

Selden is identified as the Notting Hill murderer, whose conduct was marked by peculiar ferocity and wanton brutality. He has escaped from Princetown and has been at large for three days while warders watch the roads and stations. The driver says that Selden would stop at nothing and may be hiding somewhere on the moor.

Chapter 8.

The escaped convict has not been seen or heard from for a fortnight, leading the local people to believe that he has left the moor. Watson considers it unlikely that he could have survived there so long without food, although the stone huts would provide hiding places.

So there is one of our small mysteries cleared up. It is

Selden is identified as Eliza Barrymore’s younger brother and an escaped convict hiding on the moor. Watson and Sir Henry find him by his signal light, but he escapes after throwing a rock and outrunning them.

Chapter 10.

Selden remains hidden on the moor and is being supplied with food and provisions by Barrymore. He told Barrymore that another, apparently non-convict man was also hiding there and had a boy bringing him supplies.

A terrible scream—a prolonged yell of horror and anguish—burst

Selden, the escaped convict, is the victim found on the moor. He had been wearing clothing formerly belonging to Sir Henry Baskerville and was apparently pursued by the hound before falling over rocks and breaking his neck.
