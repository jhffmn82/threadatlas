the church of Landewednack in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

The church of Landewednack, near Lizard Town and close to the sea, is described as England’s most southerly church. Picturesque and carefully maintained, it combines older features, a Norman doorway and curious hagioscope, with a modern serpentine memorial pulpit and neat churchyard. On the fourth day of travel it is noted for its history and notably good choir; by the sixth, the crowded evening service, beautiful music, and rich flowers and fruit make an especially impressive conclusion.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

DAY THE FOURTH Sunday, September 4th--and we had started on September 1st; was it possible we had only been travelling four days?

The church of Landewednack is a picturesque, most southerly church in England, with a Norman doorway, a hagioscope, a carefully tended churchyard, and preserved older features alongside modern additions. It is said to have hosted the last sermon in Cornish in 1678, and its choir sings notably well.

DAY THE SIXTH And a day absolutely divine!

The church is crowded for the evening service, with no seat vacant, and is decorated with exceptionally rich and tasteful flowers and fruit. Its organist, clergy, and congregation contribute to an impressive service.
