the Lizard in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

The Lizard is a Cornish coastal place and sightseeing point, recommended as possibly better than Land's End. It includes Lizard Town with shops, white-painted houses, two nearby hotels, a church, gardens and paths into quieter countryside. Its coast has cliffs, a sparkling sea, serpentine rocks, sea-pools, caves, and a cove reached by a difficult descent. The Lizard Lights, a huge white building and navigational installation, are a prominent feature. It is reputed for exceptionally dense fogs and is noted for longevity: register books record several deaths at more than one hundred years, while a rector is said to have died at 120. The travelers arrange lodgings by telegram, then arrive by carriage after crossing Goonhilly Down. During their stay they make excursions around the cliffs, shore, church connections, and lights; a severe nighttime storm keeps them there while Charles nevertheless brings a party through the weather. After a divine day, they bathe, explore the shore, and visit the lights before preparing for Marazion. What began as only a word or dot on a map becomes a place of human interest, memories, and affection, and they leave sadly after revisiting its town, church, coast, and sea caves.

the Lizard in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

The Lizard is a coastal district and region in Cornwall, marked by a sharp geological line of magnesian earth and serpentine blocks in turf, cottage walls, and fences. Its coast has rough seas, difficult landings, steep paths, dangerous rocks, and shipwrecks, with Pistol Meadow and the Lizard Lights. Though subject to dense fogs, it can unfold in clear blue sea, sunshine, bracing air, and villages such as Cadgwith.
