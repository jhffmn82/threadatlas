Bilan's machine

Also called: IMPROVED MACHINE FOR COMPRESSING REFUSE COAL INTO FUEL

Abstract written by a model

Bilan's machine is an agglomerating machine for manufacturing spherical conglomerates. Its cast-iron frame supports four vertical moulding wheels at right angles, with quarter-sphere cavities, plus a hopper, helices, shafts, screws, gears, and pinions. Plastic paste is progressively compressed from four quarters where the wheels meet; worn mould segments are replaceable. It produces about 40 tons of globular artificial fuel daily.

Bilan's machine is in one work on this site, Scientific American Supplement No. 360, November 25, 1882. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

MACHINE FOR COMPRESSING COAL REFUSE INTO FUEL.

Bilan's machine has a cast-iron frame supporting four vertical moulding wheels arranged at right angles, along with a hopper, vertical helices, shafts, screws, gears, and beveled pinions. The wheels progressively compress the paste from four quarters, and their mould-carrying segments can be removed and replaced when worn. The machine produces about 40 tons of globular artificial fuel per day.
