Abstract written by a model

In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Libya was a rich and fertile continent associated with Epaphos, destined to receive Euphemos’s descendants and Battos’s colony, and it received Kyrene as Apollo’s bride and queen. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Africa contained Carthage and Cyrene, was divided among Poeni, Libyan Poeni, Africans, and Numidians, supplied soldiers, and was the setting for Agathocles’s war against the Carthaginians and later conflicts involving mercenaries and Libyans. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Libya bordered Egypt and contained fertile Cyrene, the Syrtes, and broad southern and inland regions; its rulers governed farmers, herdsmen, and raiders, while Ammon, Bacchus, Dionysius, and Hercules successively shaped the land and its inhabitants. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Africa contained Carthage, Ethiopian peoples, and Libyan regions by the Nile, and was the setting for revolt among Carthage’s confederates after its defeat in Sicily. In The Library, Libya was ruled by Antaeus when Hercules traversed it while seeking the golden apples. In His Last Bow, Africa was Dr. Leon Sterndale’s intended destination and the place where he had left much of his property and unfinished work in central Africa.

Africa in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Africa first appears

Abstract written by a model

Africa contains Carthage and Cyrene with neighbouring cities. It is divided among four kinds of inhabitants: Poeni, Libyan Poeni, Africans, and Numidians. It supplied ten thousand soldiers to the Carthaginian expedition and became the main setting of Agathocles’s war against the Carthaginians. Crossing from Sicily, Agathocles landed with a mixed army in a fertile, wealthy Carthaginian region, captured a great city, moved to Tunis, defeated the Carthaginians, and blockaded Carthage. He gained towns and cities along the coast and in the interior, amid a Numidian revolt, Ophelas’s expedition, and Bomilcar’s coup at Carthage. His advances gave way to recovery by the Carthaginians: his forces were blocked, nearly starved, defeated, and shaken by mutiny and desertion. After his prospects were destroyed, he secretly departed by ship, and peace was made. Later Africa was also the setting for war against mercenaries and Libyans, after which Amilcar was briefly made general of all Libya.

Libya in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Libya first appears

Abstract written by a model

Libya borders Egypt and contains fertile Cyrene, the Syrtes, and broad southern and inland regions. Its farmers, herdsmen, and raiders live under rulers in riverside castles who require annual oaths, protect obedience, and execute resistance. Ammon ruled there; the first Bacchus and Dionysius performed exploits, won support, and improved land and inhabitants. Later Hercules slew Antaeus and wild beasts, making Libya safer, more productive, fertile, and purged of wicked men and tyrants.
