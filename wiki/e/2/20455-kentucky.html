Kentucky in Pen Pictures of Eventful Scenes and Struggles of Life

B. F. Craig · 1880

Kentucky, a new-born State admitted into the Union in 1792, lies between the Cumberland Mountains and the Ohio river, with fertile, black, undulating, wooded land. Once called “the dark and bloody ground,” it endured Indian incursions until 1795. During war, it sought peace and remained in the Union, but was invaded on both sides as Cliff Carlo joined Southern armies.

” SCENE THIRD--THE SEPARATED SISTERS.

Kentucky was admitted into the Union in 1792 and remained exposed to Indian incursions until Wayne’s treaty in 1795. The text describes its fertile, wooded landscape and explains that it was once called “the dark and bloody ground.” Tom Fairfield settled there and spent most of his adult life in the state.

SCENE SEVENTH--WAR BETWEEN THE STATES.

Kentucky initially claims to be the holy ground of peace and remains in the Union, a position Cousin Cæsar considers inadequate. In the second year of the war, Kentucky is invaded on both sides, and Cliff Carlo joins the Southern armies.
