Abstract written by a model

In The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies, Agamemnon's tomb is a royal grave-mound and place of invocation where Orestes, Electra, and the Chorus bring offerings and seek the dead's aid. In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Agamemnon's tomb holds the sisters' father, receives funerary offerings, and is where Orestes and Pylades plan to pour libations and retrieve a bronze vase. In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Agamemnon's tomb is where Orestes intends to supplicate his father for protection, with Pylades offering to guide him there. In The Electra of Euripides, Agamemnon's grave is a neglected barrow where Electra laments the lack of honors, Aegisthus insults the dead, and Orestes secretly leaves offerings.

Tomb of Agamemnon in The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies

Aeschylus · 457 BC · the work’s page · read it where Tomb of Agamemnon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Agamemnon’s tomb, a sepulchre and royal grave-mound in Mycenae, is a site of invocation. Orestes, Electra, and the Chorus gather there with libations, hair, prayers, and mourning; Electra finds a lock and footprints, then the siblings ask the dead for aid as Clytemnestra’s offerings arrive.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE LIBATION-BEARERS

The tomb is the place where Orestes, Electra, and the Chorus meet and where libations, hair, prayers, and mourning are offered. Electra finds a newly shorn lock of hair and matching footprints there.

ORESTES (_approaching suddenly_)

Agamemnon’s tomb is the place where Orestes, Electra, and the Chorus gather to invoke the dead and the powers below. Orestes and Electra ask Agamemnon to aid them, and Clytemnestra’s offerings are found to have been sent there after her dream.

Agamemnon's grave in The Electra of Euripides

Euripides · 419 to 409 BC · the work’s page · read it where Agamemnon's grave first appears

Abstract written by a model

Agamemnon's grave is a desert barrow without customary honors: Electra says it lacks tears, offerings, and myrtle, while Aegisthus insults it drunk. Orestes secretly brings tears, cut hair, and a sacrificed black lamb. Later the Old Man pours wine, lays myrtle, and discovers the ewe and hair, suspecting Orestes' visit.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ELECTRA

Orestes secretly visits Agamemnon's grave, pours tears and cut hair upon it, and sacrifices a black lamb. Electra says that the grave lacks ordinary honors, tears, offerings, and myrtle, and that Aegisthus insults it while drunk.

[ORESTES _and_ PYLADES _go in, following the_ SERVANTS.

The Old Man visits his master's grave and pours wine offerings there, laying myrtle sprays around the stone. He finds a freshly sacrificed black ewe and a lock of bright brown hair, which leads him to wonder whether Orestes has visited the grave.
