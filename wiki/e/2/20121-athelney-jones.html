Athelney Jones in The Sign of the Four

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1890-02

Athelney Jones is a fat, pompous yet well-known, energetic Scotland Yard police detective and inspector. Responsible for investigating the Upper Norwood tragedy and Bartholomew Sholto’s murder, he initially develops the mistaken theory that Thaddeus Sholto killed his brother for the treasure. He arrests Thaddeus, Mrs. Bernstone, Lal Rao, and McMurdo despite evidence of an outside intruder, and Holmes judges his conclusions flawed. After his case against Thaddeus fails, Jones admits the error and asks Sherlock Holmes for help. He accepts Holmes’s direction, supplies a police steam launch and officers, and joins Holmes and Watson in pursuing the Aurora. Though initially doubtful, he helps capture Jonathan Small and the fugitives, takes charge of Small, and ensures the iron treasure box is delivered to Watson. Dismayed to find the box empty, he reproaches Small for frustrating justice. He nevertheless insists on securing Small for trial and receives credit for the investigation, including catching Lal Rao. In relaxation, he is sociable.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter VI

Athelney Jones arrives with an inspector and develops a theory that Thaddeus Sholto killed his brother and took the treasure. He arrests Thaddeus despite the evidence of an outside intruder, while Holmes dismisses his conclusions as flawed.

Chapter IX

Athelney Jones admits that his theory against Thaddeus Sholto has failed and asks for Sherlock Holmes’s assistance. He agrees to place himself under Holmes’s direction, provide a police steam launch and officers, and allow Holmes an unofficial interview with Jonathan Small if Small is captured.

Chapter X

Athelney Jones joins Holmes and Watson in the launch and helps direct the pursuit of the Aurora. He doubts at first that their faster launch can catch it, but they eventually come alongside and capture the fugitives.

Chapter XI

Athelney Jones takes charge of Jonathan Small and joins Holmes and Dr. Watson after the capture. He arranges for Dr. Watson to land at Vauxhall with the treasure box and assigns an inspector to accompany him.

Chapter XII

Athelney Jones is angered and dismayed when he sees that the box is empty. He accuses Small of thwarting justice and warns that this may damage Small’s chances at trial.

Athelney Jones insists that duty requires him to place Jonathan Small safely under lock and key, despite having assisted Holmes and the narrator. He says that Holmes and the narrator will be wanted at the trial and claims the credit for catching one person in the Norwood affair.
