Furies in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

The Furies, also called the Eumenides or Erinnyes, are deities of vengeance associated with Potnia. They drive the house of the Atridae toward repeated bloodshed and are described as dark, winged, terrifying goddesses, snake-haired or Gorgon-visaged, who execute vengeance and slaughter. After Orestes kills Clytemnestra to avenge Agamemnon, they pursue, persecute, and torment him, appearing in his madness with serpents and fire and forcing him into wandering. Three Furies bring the charge against him at the hill of Mars, where the eldest oppose him in judgment. Some continue persecuting him afterward, until Phoebus’s command gives Orestes hope of release.

the Furies in The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies

Aeschylus · -0457

The Furies are an ancient chorus and company of hell: goddesses, children of Night, avenging powers of the slain, and ministers of justice in bloodshed. Older than Athena and the younger gods, they are bound to fate and claim an ancient right to pursue blood guilt, especially blood shed within a family. They inhabit the house of Atreus as avenging spirits, chant its inherited curse, hate its ancient crimes, and drink human blood for revelry. Invoked in Agamemnon’s sacrifice, their vengeance becomes central when Orestes is commanded to avenge Agamemnon and then kills his mother. They pursue him from home to home for both Agamemnon’s and Clytemnestra’s blood, appearing to him as dark-robed, snake-haired hell-hounds. After he escapes, they accuse Apollo of protecting a matricide and violating their ancient divine right. They track Orestes to Athens by the scent of his mother’s blood, demand his conviction, question him, and challenge Apollo’s defense before Athena’s judges. Fearing that their claim to justice has been defeated, they threaten Athens with blight, venom, pestilence, famine, and ruin. Athena’s promises change the outcome: they accept residence and honor in Athens, gain a sanctuary and worship from its citizens, and become resident goddesses who protect and bless the city while restraining civic violence.

The Furies are invoked as avenging powers of the slain. Apollo’s warning says that they will pursue Orestes for Agamemnon’s blood if he fails to carry out the commanded vengeance.

[_Enter Orestes and Pylades, in guise of travellers_.

The Furies are invoked as the avenging power of the house and are said to pursue Orestes after he kills his mother. Orestes sees them as dark-robed figures with snakes in their hair and calls them his mother’s hell-hounds.

THE FURIES

The Furies awaken after dreaming of their pursuit and discover that Orestes has escaped them. They accuse Apollo of violating ancient divine right by protecting a matricide, insist that their office is to chase him from home to home, and continue to demand vengeance for Clytemnestra’s blood.

_The scene changes to Athens. In the foreground, the Temple of Athena

The Furies track Orestes by the scent of his mother’s blood and demand that he be punished for matricide. They claim authority as ancient ministers of justice and threaten to bring ruin upon the land if their rights are overthrown.

[_Re-enter Athena, with twelve Athenian citizens_.

The Chorus questions Orestes about the killing and challenges Apollo’s defense of him. It threatens to punish Athens if its ancient claim to justice is defeated, but remains to hear the judges’ decision.

CHORUS

The Furies first threaten Athens with blight, venom, pestilence, and other forms of vengeance because they believe the younger gods have overruled their ancient rights. They accept Athena’s promises and agree to dwell in Athens, blessing its land and restraining civic violence.
