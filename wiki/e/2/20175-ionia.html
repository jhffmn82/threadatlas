Abstract written by a model

In Four Plays of Aeschylus, the Ionian lands are where Xerxes goes to war, according to Atossa’s account of her recurring visions. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Ionia is praised as a fertile region with healthy air, notable places, and many temples, baths, rivers, groves, caves, and wonders.

Ionia in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Ionia first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ionia is a praised region of fertile land and healthy air, rich in temples, baths, rivers, groves, caves, and wonders. Its notable places span Ephesus, Miletus, Colophon, Lebedus, Teos, Clazomenæ, Erythræ, Smyrna, Chios, and Samos, culminating in distinguished temples and the beautiful river Meles.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER V.

Ionia was praised for its fertile land, healthy air, temples, baths, rivers, groves, caves, and other wonders. Its notable places included sites at Ephesus, Miletus, Colophon, Lebedus, Teos, Clazomenæ, Erythræ, Smyrna, Chios, and Samos.
