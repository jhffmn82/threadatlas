people of Oz in Ozma of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1907

The people of Oz, including officers and a private transformed into green ornaments by the Nome King, are restored to their natural forms by Billina. After thanking her, they enter the palace with the people of Ev and confront the gathered warriors.

16. Purple, Green, and Gold

The people of Oz are represented among the twenty-six officers and the private, who have been changed into green ornaments. Billina restores them, and they thank her for freeing them from the Nome King’s magic.

17. The Scarecrow Wins the Fight

The people of Oz are restored during Billina’s mission. They enter with the people of Ev after the palace doors open and find the Nome King’s warriors gathered in the throne room.

Oz people in The Emerald City of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1910

The Oz people are good, happy fairy people who inhabit the Land of Oz under Princess Ozma’s rule. Every person divides life between half work and half play, and they do not eat chickens because Billina was the first hen in Oz. They are contented and prosperous, and they previously defeated the Nomes by rolling eggs at them. Later, King Roquat and invading forces plan revenge: to conquer their country, plunder or enslave them, and destroy their happiness. As the invaders threaten to break through the tunnel, Ozma refuses to abandon the people, while Dorothy and her companions choose to share their fate.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

4. How The Nome King Planned Revenge

The Oz people are described by King Roquat as good, happy, contented, and prosperous. They are the intended victims of the Nome King's plan to conquer, destroy, and enslave them.

11. How the General Met the First and Foremost

The Oz people are described by General Guph as happy, harmless fairy people whom the invaders plan to plunder and enslave. The Phanfasms intend to destroy their happiness and bring them sorrow and dismay.

13. How the General Talked to the King

The Oz people defeated the Nomes in an earlier attack by rolling eggs at them. The Growleywogs intend to take some of them as slaves, while the Phanfasms want to destroy them.

26. How Ozma Refused to Fight for Her Kingdom

The Oz people face enslavement and the destruction of their country when the invading forces break through the tunnel. Ozma refuses to abandon them, and Dorothy and her companions choose to share their fate.

the Oz people in The Magic of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1919

The Oz people are a people of the Land of Oz who live in Oz. They are generally happy, contented, and prosperous, love Ozma of Oz as their beautiful girl Ruler, and mix work and play so that both are delightful and satisfying. They took Ruggedo’s underground kingdom and magic tools, making him regard them as enemies. Ruggedo then falsely tells the forest animals that the Oz people intend to enslave them, urging conquest and transformation of the people into beasts. Some animals consider fighting, but representatives of the Oz side arrive as war threatens.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

3. Two Bad Ones

Ruggedo regards the Oz people as his enemies because they took his kingdom from him. He plans to conquer them and transform them into various beasts.

8. The Li-Mon-Eags Make Trouble

The people of Oz are falsely accused by Ruggedo of planning to make ropes, capture the forest beasts, and enslave them. Ruggedo proposes that the beasts conquer them and that Kiki Aru and he transform them into beasts.

11. The Beasts of the Forest of Gugu

Ruggedo claims that the Oz people plan to make the beasts prisoners, tie them with ropes, beat them with sticks, and force them to work. He says they have begun making ropes and proposes transforming them into beasts.

12. Kiki Uses His Magic

The Oz people are regarded by some of the forest animals as possible enemies, and some animals consider fighting them. The Cowardly Lion, Dorothy, and the Wizard arrive as representatives of the Oz side.
