vengeance in The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies

Aeschylus · -0457

Vengeance is embodied by the Furies as an ancient duty of blood repaid with blood. After Clytemnestra’s death, it drives them to pursue Orestes from place to place, renewed by her Ghost and disputed by Apollo in Athens.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE FURIES

Vengeance drives the Furies to pursue Orestes from place to place for Clytemnestra’s death. The Ghost of Clytemnestra calls them to renew that pursuit, and Apollo disputes whether their vengeance is rightly directed.

_The scene changes to Athens. In the foreground, the Temple of Athena

The Furies embody vengeance, pursuing Orestes relentlessly for the death of his mother. They describe their vengeance as an ancient duty in which blood is repaid with blood and pain with new pain.
