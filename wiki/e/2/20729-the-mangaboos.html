the Mangaboos in Dorothy and the Wizard in Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1908

The Mangaboos are a beautifully formed, attractively dressed vegetable people of the Land of the Mangaboos and the Glass City. Their handsome faces are expressionless: they have no hearts or warm blood and cannot smile or frown. They do not eat, because their bodies are solid inside. They grow on bushes in folk gardens; Princes and Rulers grow on the Royal Bush, and dead Sorcerers can be planted so other Sorcerers grow. When ripe and picked, they become fully alive and often live about five years if kept cool, moist, and free of accidents; sprouting is a misfortune requiring them to be planted again. Having never seen horses or cats, they gather to stare at Dorothy, Zeb, Jim, and Eureka, then watch the balloon and the contest between Oz and Gwig. Impressed by the Wizard's lanterns but frightened and scorched by fire, they become dangerous: wielding thorn branches, they drive the animals toward the mountain, wall up Jim and the piglets at the Black Pit, pursue the others, and attack while rebuilding the barrier.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

2. The Glass City

The people of the Glass City are beautifully formed, attractively dressed, and have handsome but expressionless faces. They have never seen a horse or a cat and gather to stare at Dorothy, Zeb, Jim, and Eureka. They follow the man with the star in a procession.

3. The Arrival Of The Wizard

The Mangaboos gather around the horse and buggy, watch the descending balloon, and observe the contest between Oz and Gwig. They are vegetable beings rather than flesh-and-blood people, and their dead Sorcerers can be planted so that other Sorcerers grow from their bushes.

4. The Vegetable Kingdom

The Mangaboos live without eating and grow on bushes in the folk gardens. They become fully alive when they are ripe and picked, usually live about five years if kept cool and moist, and must be planted again if they sprout.

6. The Mangaboos Prove Dangerous

The Mangaboos are described as cold, flabby, heartless vegetable people. They are impressed by the Wizard's lanterns but are frightened and scorched by the fire test; later, they use thorn branches to drive Jim, Eureka, and the piglets toward the mountain, though many are smashed or disabled when the animals resist.

7. Into the Black Pit and Out Again

The Mangaboos imprison Jim and the piglets by piling glass rocks at the Black Pit's entrance. They pursue Eureka, follow Dorothy, the Wizard, and Zeb to the mountain, and attack them with sharp thorns while rebuilding the barrier.
