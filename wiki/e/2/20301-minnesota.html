Minnesota in Novel-10146

Pioneer Press · about 1862 · the work’s page · read it where Minnesota first appears

Abstract written by a model

Minnesota began as a territory in 1849, a new place that received James Mills and drew young printers and editors; James M. Goodhue established a newspaper there, and Capt. E. Y. Shelly became the first printer to locate permanently. It held a 1857 election for delegates to a constitutional convention, held that convention in preparation for statehood, and entered the Union through a congressional bill; news of admission reached St. Paul by passenger boat in 1858 because telegraphic communication with Washington was lacking. Railroad aid authorized through the Five Million Loan amendment brought grading of nearly 250 miles of road, but company defaults compromised its credit until, under Gov. Pillsbury, it settled by paying half the securities' nominal value. During the Civil War it furnished regiments, including the Second Minnesota Infantry, whose volunteers exceeded the first regiment's quota and whose Mill Springs service was praised. While men were recruited, the Sioux outbreak of 1862 depopulated areas and left thousands homeless, prompting legislative relief and defense. It opposed troop removal, executed thirty-eight Dakota prisoners amid opposition to pardons, and later conducted a draft in counties behind quota.

News that Minnesota had been admitted into the Union reached St. Paul by passenger boat in 1858 because there was no telegraphic communication with Washington.

THE FIVE MILLION LOAN ELECTION.

Minnesota authorized railroad aid through the Five Million Loan amendment, but the railroad companies defaulted and the state's credit was compromised when the bonds could not be negotiated. Nearly 250 miles of road were graded, and during Gov. Pillsbury's administration the state compromised with bondholders by paying 50 percent of the securities' nominal value.

THE GREAT SIOUX OUTBREAK IN 1862.

Minnesota was attacked during the Sioux outbreak while many of its men were being recruited for the Union army. Large areas were depopulated, thousands of residents became homeless, and the legislature met in extra session to organize relief and defense.

As soon as Gen.

Minnesota's people opposed removing the troops after Gen. Pope declared the Indian war over. Public excitement over the condemned Indians became so intense that the governor urged citizens not to commit lawlessness.

PRINTERS AND EDITORS OF TERRITORIAL DAYS.

Minnesota is described as the new territory where James M. Goodhue established a newspaper in 1849 and where Capt. E.Y. Shelly became the first printer to permanently locate. Territorial printers from Minnesota served in numerous Civil War units, including the First, Second, Seventh, Eighth, and Tenth Minnesota regiments.

TERRITORIAL PRINTERS IN CIVIL LIFE.

Minnesota attracted many young printers and editors during its early territorial period.

THE DECISIVE BATTLE OF MILL SPRINGS.

Minnesota supplied the Second Minnesota Infantry Regiment after citizens volunteered in numbers exceeding the first regiment's quota. The state legislature praised the regiment's heroism at Mill Springs and contrasted its victory with the First Minnesota's service in defeat.
