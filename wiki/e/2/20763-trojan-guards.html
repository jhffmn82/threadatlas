Chorus of Trojan sentinels in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

The Chorus of Trojan sentinels guards the Trojan camp at night and reports activity in the Greek camp. It wakes Hector over fires and tumult, urges caution, supports sending a spy and Dolon, and celebrates Rhesus. It fails to detect Ulysses and Diomede, later challenges them, and accepts Ulysses after the watchword Apollo.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

RHESUS.

The Chorus of Trojan sentinels wakes Hector and reports the fires, torches, and tumult in the Greek camp. It urges caution, approves Aeneas’s plan to send a spy, encourages Dolon, and celebrates the arrival of Rhesus.

ULYSSES, DIOMEDE.

The Trojan guards fail to detect Ulysses and Diomede as they enter and leave the camp, and Hector threatens them with punishment or death. They later challenge the fugitives, but accept Ulysses after he gives the watchword Apollo.

the Guards in The Rhesus of Euripides

Euripides · -0454/-0439

The Guards are a Trojan picket responsible for maintaining watches at the Trojan camp. Near Hector's tent, they report Greek beacons and disorder, urge battle preparations, support Aeneas's spy plan and Dolon's departure, celebrate Rhesus's arrival, then rouse the Lycians, find casualties, confront Greek spies, and report disaster to Hector.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

RHESUS

The Trojan guards bring Hector news of the Greek beacons and apparent disorder during the night. They urge preparations for battle, support Aeneas's plan to send a spy, assist Dolon as he departs in disguise, and celebrate the reported arrival of Rhesus.

NOTES

The Guards watch near Hector's tent and discuss the night, Dolon's mission, and the order of the watches. They leave their post to rouse the Lycians, later discover wounded and dead men, struggle with the Greek spies, and report the disaster to Hector.
