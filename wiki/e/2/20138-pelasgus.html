Pelasgus in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

Pelasgus is the king and ruler of Argos and the Pelasgians, descended from Palaichthon. His central role is deciding whether Argos will protect the Danaids, a choice that could bring war with the sons of Aegyptus. When he appears, he questions the suppliants, recognizes their claim through Io, and delays a pledge until he can consult and persuade the Argive people. He later defends the sanctuary and the city’s will against the Herald, forbids the maidens’ removal by force, expels the Herald, and grants the Danaids shelter, protection, and hospitality.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

The Suppliant Maidens

The King of Argos has not yet appeared in this unit. Danaus expects that the chiefs or rulers of the land may be approaching to question the suppliants.

Enter the KING OF ARGOS.

Pelasgus identifies himself as ruler of the Pelasgians and king of the land, descended from Palaichthon. He questions the Danaids about their origin, recognizes their claim through Io, and refuses to promise protection until he has consulted the people of Argos. He orders Danaus to display the suppliant branches at the city’s shrines and promises to persuade the assembly.

Re-enter DANAUS.

The King of Argos confronts the Herald of Aegyptus for violating the sanctuary and the city’s will. He declares that no force may carry away the maidens, orders the Herald to leave, and offers the Danaids protected homes and the city’s hospitality.
