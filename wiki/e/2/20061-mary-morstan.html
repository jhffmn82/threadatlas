Miss Morstan in The Sign of the Four

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1890-02

Miss Mary Morstan is a young lady, governess, orphan, and daughter of Captain Arthur Morstan. She lives with and works for Mrs. Cecil Forrester in Lower Camberwell, having lived in Edinburgh during childhood. After her father disappears, she receives pearls annually and then an anonymous letter, leading her to seek Sherlock Holmes’s help. She brings a mysterious paper, her father’s preserved Agra plan, and asks Holmes and Dr. Watson to accompany her to a meeting. Though uneasy, she travels with them composedly and resolutely. Thaddeus Sholto reveals that her father died during a quarrel with Major John Sholto and that she is rightfully entitled to at least half of the Agra treasure. She continues to Pondicherry Lodge, comforts the frightened Mrs. Bernstone, and relies on Watson during the frightening events there. Afterward she is exhausted, faints and weeps, yet her chief concern is that Thaddeus be cleared of accusation because he has acted kindly and honourably. She may become an heiress, but the recovered iron treasure box is empty. Its loss removes Watson’s hesitation about declaring his love, and she accepts him as a husband in prospective. Holmes judges her charming and believes her preservation of the Agra plan showed a genius that could have made her useful in investigative work. She becomes a friend of Holmes and Watson and loves Watson reciprocally.

Narrative, section by section

10 sections; each heading opens the text

The Sign of the Four / Chapter I

Miss Mary Morstan is introduced when Mrs. Hudson announces her arrival at Baker Street. Sherlock Holmes does not remember her name and asks that she be shown upstairs.

Chapter II

Miss Mary Morstan visits Sherlock Holmes to seek help concerning her father’s disappearance and the mysterious pearls sent to her. She agrees to meet an unknown correspondent at the Lyceum Theatre and asks Holmes and Dr. John Watson to accompany her.

Chapter III

Miss Morstan accompanies Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson on the night’s expedition while maintaining a composed and resolute manner despite her uneasiness. She explains that Major Sholto was a close friend of her father and brings Holmes a mysterious paper found in Captain Morstan’s pocket-book. She gives her word that neither of her companions is a police-officer before the coachman takes them away.

Chapter IV

Miss Morstan meets Thaddeus Sholto to learn what he wishes to tell her about her father and the treasure. She learns that her father died during a quarrel with Major John Sholto and that she is entitled to at least half of the Agra treasure; the news makes her pale, but she continues with the others to Pondicherry Lodge.

Chapter V

Miss Morstan accompanies the others to Pondicherry Lodge and instinctively takes Watson’s hand during the frightening wait outside. She comforts the distressed housekeeper, Mrs. Bernstone, whose fear is eased by Miss Morstan’s presence.

Chapter VI

Miss Morstan is to be escorted home from the stricken house by Dr. Watson. She lives with Mrs. Cecil Forrester in Lower Camberwell.

Chapter VII

Miss Mary Morstan remains calm while supporting the frightened housekeeper but faints and weeps in the cab after the night’s events. Dr. John Watson restrains his declaration of love because she is distressed and may become an heiress through the Agra treasure.

Chapter IX

Miss Morstan is weary after the night’s adventures but eager to hear the news. She says that her main concern is clearing Thaddeus Sholto of the unfounded charge because he has behaved kindly and honourably. Holmes intends for her to be the first to open the recovered treasure.

Chapter XI

Mary Morstan is at Mrs. Cecil Forrester's house when Dr. Watson brings her the iron treasure box. She learns that it is empty, and she accepts Watson's declaration of love.
