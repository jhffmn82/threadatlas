Abstract written by a model

In The Rhesus of Euripides, Dolon’s wolf disguise conceals his body so he can crawl near the trenches and stand in open ground, and it is taken as a token after his death. In Hecuba and other plays, Dolon’s wolf disguise is the tawny hide and animal face covering he intends to use to approach the Greek trenches and ramparts undetected.

Dolon’s wolf-mask in The Rhesus of Euripides

Euripides · 454 to 439 BC · the work’s page · read it where Dolon’s wolf-mask first appears

Abstract written by a model

Dolon’s wolf disguise is a complete grey wolf’s hide covering his body, with a tooth-shaped head and fitted forepaw and hindquarter coverings. His distinctive concealment lets him crawl on four feet near trenches and stand on two in open ground. After his death, the hide is taken as a token.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

RHESUS

Dolon’s wolf disguise consists of a grey wolf's hide covering his body, a head shaped like a set of gleaming teeth, and sleeves and coverings fitted to resemble the wolf's forepaws and hindquarters. Dolon plans to crawl on four feet near the trenches and stand on two feet in open ground.

NOTES

The wolf-skin disguise is Dolon's distinctive method of concealment in the play. Unlike the Iliad's simpler wolf skin worn over a tunic, the play presents Dolon as covered in a complete wolf-skin and moving on all fours; the skin later serves as a token of his death.
