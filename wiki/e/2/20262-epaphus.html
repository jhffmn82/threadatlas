Epaphus in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Epaphus, sprung from Io and progeny of Jove, is invoked by the chorus to come to the land founded by his descendants.

Epaphus is described as sprung from the chorus's ancestor Io and as Jove's progeny. The chorus calls on him in a foreign tongue and with foreign prayers to come to this land, which his descendants founded.

Epaphus in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

Epaphus is the child of Zeus and Io, born to Io after Zeus’s restoring, life-giving touch and named for that touch. He becomes king of Argos and fathers Libya. Through Libya, the Danaids trace their descent, and his descendants inherit Nile-enriched land.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Enter the KING OF ARGOS.

Epaphus was born from Io after Zeus touched her and was named from that saving touch. He fathered Libya, through whom the Danaids trace their descent.

Enter IO, horned like a cow.

Epaphus will be born to Io at Canopus after Zeus restores her reason by touching her. His name will memorialize Zeus’s touch, and his descendants will inherit the land enriched by the Nile.

Epaphus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Epaphus, believed born to Io from Jupiter, reigns in Egypt and has temples throughout its cities. Equal to Phaëton in spirit and years, he mocks Phaëton’s claimed parentage; according to Herodotus, he is Io’s son and identified with Apis.

opening

Epaphus is believed to have been born to Io from the seed of great Jupiter. He possesses temples joined to those of his parent and mocks Phaëton for believing his mother and claiming an imaginary father.

Epaphus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Epaphus, son of Io, was born beside the Nile after Io regained her form, disappeared at Hera’s request and was found by Io; he reigned over the Egyptians, married Memphis, founded Memphis, and fathered Libya.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE LIBRARY, II. 1. 1-3

Epaphus was born to Io beside the river Nile after Io recovered her original form in Egypt. Hera asked the Curetes to make him disappear, but Zeus killed the Curetes after learning what they had done. Epaphus was later found by Io, reigned over the Egyptians, married Memphis, founded Memphis after her, and fathered Libya.
