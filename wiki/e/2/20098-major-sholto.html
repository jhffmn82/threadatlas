Major Sholto in The Sign of the Four

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1890-02

Major John Sholto was a British military officer and major of the 34th Bombay Infantry who served in India and at the Andaman Islands. There he commanded native troops with Captain Morstan and Lieutenant Bromley Brown, and he was Morstan’s close friend. After hearing Jonathan Small’s account of hidden treasure, Sholto and Morstan agreed to investigate in return for a share and help for the conspirators. They found the Agra treasure, but Sholto later took it and did not meet the agreed conditions, wronging Small. After retiring from the Indian army, Sholto settled at Pondicherry Lodge in Upper Norwood with his sons, wealth, curiosities, native servants, and the concealed treasure. He hid Morstan’s death and kept Morstan’s share, denying that he had heard Morstan was in London during Morstan’s disappearance. A frightening letter from India made him increasingly fearful, especially of a wooden-legged man, and he fortified and guarded his home with his sons, two prize-fighters, and a khitmutgar. Before dying, he confessed matters concerning Morstan to his sons. He died in bed at Pondicherry Lodge on April 28, 1882; afterward, the Sign of the Four was marked on him, and searches of his papers did not reveal the treasure’s location.

Chapter III

Major Sholto, of Upper Norwood and formerly of the 34th Bombay Infantry, was a particular friend of Captain Morstan and had served with him at the Andaman Islands. He denied having heard that Captain Morstan was in London and died on April 28, 1882.

Chapter IV

Major John Sholto was a former Indian army officer who lived at Pondicherry Lodge with his sons after returning from India with wealth and valuable curiosities. He concealed Captain Arthur Morstan’s death and kept Morstan’s share of the treasure, then confessed the matter to his sons before dying.

Chapter VII

Major Sholto possessed the Agra treasure and kept its hiding place secret except from one faithful servant who later died. After receiving a frightening letter from India, he guarded himself against a wooden-legged man, whom Sherlock Holmes identifies as Jonathan Small.
