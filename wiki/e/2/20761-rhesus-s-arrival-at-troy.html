Rhesus's arrival at Troy

event · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: A friend is come to Ilion, I am come, Rhesus, here on Trojan soil, Rhesus’s arrival at Troy, am come, he comes, he comes with help for Troy, my long absence, thy coming

Abstract written by a model

In The Rhesus of Euripides, Rhesus's arrival at Troy is a late Thracian military reinforcement during the Trojan War's tenth year; it is reported by a shepherd, welcomed despite Hector's resentment, and prompts Athena to send Odysseus and Diomede against the Thracian camp. In Hecuba and other plays, Rhesus's arrival at Troy follows a delayed journey caused by war with the Scythians near the Euxine; he says that he crossed the Hellespont and marched through various lands before reaching Troy.

Rhesus's arrival at Troy in The Rhesus of Euripides

Euripides · 454 to 439 BC · the work’s page · read it where Rhesus's arrival at Troy first appears

Abstract written by a model

Rhesus's arrival at Troy is a late Thracian military reinforcement during the Trojan War’s tenth year. A shepherd reports him leading a vast force from Mount Ida; despite Hector’s resentment, he is welcomed. Entering in dazzling white armour, Rhesus explains his delay, and his arrival prompts Athena to send Odysseus and Diomede against the Thracian camp.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

RHESUS

Rhesus's arrival at Troy is reported by a shepherd who sees him leading a vast Thracian force from the direction of Mount Ida. Hector initially resents this late aid but finally orders that Rhesus be welcomed as a war-friend.

_Enter_ RHESUS _in dazzling white armour, followed by his_ [vv. 379-395]

Rhesus's arrival at Troy is greeted by the Trojan soldiers as the arrival of a godlike warrior. Hector challenges him over his lateness, and Rhesus explains that the Scythian invasion delayed him. His arrival becomes the reason Athena sends Odysseus and Diomede to attack the Thracian camp before dawn.
