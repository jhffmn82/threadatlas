the dragonettes in Dorothy and the Wizard in Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1908

The dragonettes are saucy young dragons, about sixty-six years old, living in individual pockets around a vast cavern. Children of the mother dragon, they have a pedigree reaching back about twenty thousand years and eat people when possible. Their mother has tied their tails to mountain rocks, so they cannot reach the travelers while she hunts at the top of the earth.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

13. The Den of the Dragonettes

The dragonettes live in small pockets around the edge of a vast cavern and are young dragons about sixty-six years old. They eat people when they can, but their mother has tied their tails to the rocks, preventing them from reaching the travelers. They say their mother is about two thousand years old and has gone to the top of the earth to hunt.
