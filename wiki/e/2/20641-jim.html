Narrative, section by section

14 sections; each heading opens the text

1. The Earthquake

Jim is a large, thin, bony horse with damaged harness. He carries Dorothy and Zebediah in the buggy, becomes tense and frightened during the earthquake, and falls into the pit when the earth cracks open beneath him.

2. The Glass City

Jim is frightened and struck by falling stones during the descent, but he lands safely on the roof. He unexpectedly begins speaking and complains about the danger, his hunger, and his former work drawing a public cab in Chicago. After hesitating, he walks off the roof and floats safely down to the glass pavement.

4. The Vegetable Kingdom

Jim rides in the procession with the kitten on his back and eats the rainbow-colored grass. He refuses to leave the field because he is busy eating.

5. Dorothy Picks the Princess

Jim continues eating the pink grass while the others explore the gardens. After returning to the city, he is unharnessed, rolls over, and goes to sleep with Eureka beside him.

6. The Mangaboos Prove Dangerous

Jim protests being sent to the Black Pit and tries to protect the nine tiny piglets while the Mangaboos drive the animals away. He kicks and scatters many Mangaboos, but eventually becomes too tired to resist and is driven toward the mountain.

7. Into the Black Pit and Out Again

Jim is driven into the Black Pit with the piglets and later becomes trapped inside when the Mangaboos rebuild the barrier. He is harnessed to the buggy and cautiously pulls it through the dark, steep tunnel, stopping at times to rest before finally emerging into the new country.

9. They Fight the Invisible Bears

Jim is attacked by invisible bears and receives several deep cuts on his flanks. After the Wizard treats the wounds with sticking-plaster, Jim pulls the buggy across the river and races overland to Pyramid Mountain.

10. The Braided Man of Pyramid Mountain

Jim draws the buggy up the broad, winding stairs. He continues pulling it while the others walk.

11. They Meet the Wooden Gargoyles

Jim warns that the Country of the Gargoyles is dangerous and says that everything there is dead. He attacks the first swarm of Gargoyles by kicking them with his iron-shod hooves, scattering them and knocking splinters from some of their bodies.

12. A Wonderful Escape

Jim fights by kicking the Gargoyles, but they weigh him down by clinging to his legs and carry him to the prison house. Four Gargoyle wings are fastened to his harness, and he flies with the buggy toward the rock, though he wobbles, groans, and finds the flight frightening.

14. Ozma Uses the Magic Belt

Jim nearly gets caught by the moving rock, but he leaps into the farther passage before it stops. While trapped underground, he worries that their situation is unnatural and asks whether there are horses in Oz. He is transported with Zeb and the Wizard to the Emerald City, where he hears about the living Saw-Horse.

16. Jim, The Cab-Horse

Jim the Cab-horse is treated with great respect in the Land of Oz and becomes somewhat arrogant, demanding uncooked oatmeal for dinner and sleeping on rugs. He meets the Sawhorse and proudly explains the advantages of being a flesh-and-blood horse, including eating, breathing, bleeding, and feeling pain. He is frightened by the Cowardly Lion and Hungry Tiger but calms down when he learns they are friends, and he eats clover before meeting Dorothy.

17. The Nine Tiny Piglets

Jim rides in the procession while Zeb drives the buggy and the Wizard stands on its seat. Jim loses the race to the Sawhorse, kicks it in anger and damages it, then apologizes and recognizes that a flesh-and-blood horse cannot compete fairly with a tireless wooden animal.

20. Zeb Returns to the Ranch

Jim wants to return to the ranch and has been asking Zeb to go home since losing the race. He brings Zeb back to Hugson's Ranch, trotting along the familiar road.
