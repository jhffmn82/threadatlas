Caucasus

place · 3 works · 5 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Caucasian mountains, Caucasiis, Caucasus’ height, Mount Caucasus, mount Caucasus, peak, pinnacles, snowy Caucasus
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Caucasus is a mountain crossed by Alexander in sixteen days, near whose foot he founded Alexandria beside Prometheus's supposed cave and fetters.
  • In The Argonautica, Caucasus is a place whose mountains rise beside the route to Aea and Phasis, where a serpent guards the fleece and Prometheus, bound on rocks, suffers an eagle feeding on his liver.
  • In Four Plays of Aeschylus, the Caucasus is a world-topping mountain peak that Io must reach after crossing the Stream of Violence, with heights that overtop the world and a river descending from them in spray.

Caucasus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Caucasus, also called Paropamisus by some, is a mountain crossed by Alexander in sixteen days; near its foot he founded Alexandria beside Prometheus's supposed cave and fetters.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAP. IX.

Caucasus, also called Paropamisus by some, was crossed by Alexander in a sixteen-day march. Near its foot he founded Alexandria, and the text identifies a rock there with the supposed cave and fetters of Prometheus.
