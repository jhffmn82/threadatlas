  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Polynices demands Thebes’s government, flees to Argos, marries Adrastus’s daughter, helps arrange the expedition against Thebes, and dies fighting Eteocles.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Polynices is Oedipus’s younger son who leads an Argive army against Thebes, kills Eteocles while dying, and is denied burial by Creon.
  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Polynices is Oedipus’s elder son who returns from Argos with an army, dies fighting Eteocles, and is left unburied.
  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Polynices is Oedipus’s elder son who leads an army against Thebes, is cursed by Oedipus, accepts death, and refuses to withdraw.
  • In Four Plays of Aeschylus, Polynices is Oedipus’s exiled son who leads forces against Thebes, kills Eteocles in mutual combat, and is denied burial.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Polyneices is Oedipus’s heaven-born, golden-haired son who serves wine at Cadmus’s table before his fatal quarrel with Eteocles.
  • In The Library, Polynices is Oedipus’s son and Argia’s husband who makes an alternating-rule compact with Eteocles and kills him in single combat.
  • In The Library, Polynices is Oedipus’s son who is banished to Argos, fights Eteocles in single combat, and is left unburied until Antigone secretly buries him.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Polynices is Eteocles’s brother who returns from exile with an army, dies fighting Eteocles, and has his corpse brought to Eteocles’s funeral pile by Antigone.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Polynices is Eteocles’s brother who marries Adrastus’s daughter, leads the Seven against Thebes, dies fighting Eteocles, and is left unburied.
  • In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, Polyneices is Oedipus’s elder son who attacks Thebes, is slain by Eteocles, and is denied burial until Antigone buries him.
  • In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, Polyneices is a dead warrior whose exposed corpse is secretly buried, burned, and covered with earth despite a royal edict.

Polynices is the younger son of Jocasta and Oedipus, brother and eventual battlefield opponent of Eteocles. Polynices marries Argia, Adrastus's daughter, allies with Adrastus, and leads the Argive army against Thebes to claim his share of royal power and land. Polyneices in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912

Polyneices is Oedipus’s elder son, brother of Eteocles, Antigone, and Ismene. Driven from Thebes and denied the throne by Eteocles, he lives in exile in Argos, forms an alliance, and brings an Argive army against Thebes to recover sovereignty. Seeking victory, he asks Oedipus for forgiveness and blessing, but Oedipus rejects and curses him. Polyneices refuses to retreat and asks Antigone and Ismene to secure burial rites should he die. He is slain in the attack on Thebes after mutual fratricide. Creon calls him an outlaw and forbids burial or mourning because he attacked the city, but Antigone buries him as her brother.

[Enter ISMENE]

Polyneices, the elder son, has been kept from the throne and driven from the land by his younger brother. He has fled to Argos, formed a new alliance there, and vows either to establish Argos as ruler of the Cadmeian land or to exalt the victor.

[Enter ANTIGONE and ISMENE with THESEUS]

Polyneices comes from Argos as a suppliant after being expelled from his native land. He says that Eteocles deprived him of the sovereign throne and that he has brought an Argive army against Thebes to recover it. Oedipus rejects him and curses him, and Polyneices nevertheless refuses to turn back, asking Antigone and Ismene to ensure his burial if he dies.

ANTIGONE

Polyneices is described as an exile who attacked his father’s city and sought to destroy Thebes. Creon therefore declares that he must receive no burial or mourning, although Antigone insists that he remains her brother.

[Enter GUARD bringing ANTIGONE]

Antigone has buried her dead brother despite Creon’s prohibition. She says that the slain man was her brother and that she would have grieved with reason if she had left him unburied, even though Creon regarded him as an outlaw.

Polyneices in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912

Polyneices is a dead warrior whose corpse is left unburied, tombless, and exposed by royal edict, where animals gnaw and maul it. Though burial is required by ritual and divine law, the body is secretly dusted, then, after a warning, washed, burned, and covered with earth.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ANTIGONE

Polyneices’ corpse is left exposed under Creon’s order, intended to become food for dogs and carrion birds. The guards later find it covered with dust, with no visible trace of the person who performed the ritual.

[Enter TEIRESIAS and BOY]

Polyneices’s corpse has been gnawed and mauled by animals and remains exposed. After Teiresias’s warning, Creon orders that the body be washed, placed on branches, burned, and covered with a mound of earth.
