the planned murder of Clytemnestra and Aegisthus in The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies

Aeschylus · 457 BC · the work’s page · read it where the planned murder of Clytemnestra and Aegisthus first appears

Abstract written by a model

The entity is Orestes’ planned and carried-out vengeance killing of Aegisthus and Clytemnestra, ordered by Apollo and justified as retaliation for Agamemnon’s death. Orestes and Pylades first plan to enter the palace disguised as Phocian travelers. Orestes resolves to kill Aegisthus, if he rules in Agamemnon’s place, and Clytemnestra. After gaining entry, Orestes kills Aegisthus for adultery and then kills Clytemnestra, cutting her neck with a sword, for plotting Agamemnon’s murder. The killing of Clytemnestra becomes the central charge of matricide: the Furies demand punishment, while Orestes and Apollo defend it as commanded vengeance. It is judged in the trial of Orestes as blood guilt.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

ORESTES (_approaching suddenly_)

Orestes and Pylades plan to approach the palace as Phocian travelers and gain entry through deception. Orestes intends to kill Aegisthus first if he finds him ruling in Agamemnon’s place, and he also resolves to kill Clytemnestra.

[_Enter Orestes and Pylades, in guise of travellers_.

Orestes kills Aegisthus and then Clytemnestra in accordance with Apollo’s command and as vengeance for Agamemnon. He regards Aegisthus’ death as punishment for adultery and Clytemnestra’s death as punishment for plotting Agamemnon’s murder.

THE FURIES

Matricide is the charge against Orestes for killing his mother. The Furies call him a matricide and condemn Apollo for rescuing him, while Apollo maintains that he ordered the act in response to the death of Orestes’ father.

_The scene changes to Athens. In the foreground, the Temple of Athena

Orestes’ matricide is presented as the central issue in the dispute. He says it avenged Agamemnon and was commanded by Apollo, while the Furies insist that killing a mother demands punishment.

[_Re-enter Athena, with twelve Athenian citizens_.

Orestes killed his mother by cutting her neck with a sword. He says she had killed his father, and Apollo argues that the killing was justified by the oracle and by the greater importance of the father’s death.

Clytæmnestra's murder in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Clytæmnestra's murder first appears

Abstract written by a model

Clytæmnestra's murder is the killing of Clytemnestra by her son Orestes, aided by Pylades and urged by Electra. It follows Orestes' killing of Ægisthus and is presented as retaliation for Agamemnon's death, with Orestes saying Apollo commanded him. Orestes calls its motive just and avenging, yet also calls the act unholy. Accounts assign Pylades the direction of the sword and Electra the incitement that ended Orestes' delay. The killing leads not to good fortune but to Orestes' madness, exile, persecution by the Erinnyes, public condemnation, and threatened execution. Orestes and Electra are charged with the deed, while Apollo identifies Orestes as compelled to shed his mother's blood and sends him to trial. The Chorus treats it as a source of grief and continuing bloodshed.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

ORESTES.

Orestes committed the murder of Clytæmnestra because Apollo commanded it, while Electra and Pylades also took part. The deed has brought Orestes madness, public condemnation, and the threat of execution.

MENELAUS, ORESTES, CHORUS.

Clytæmnestra's murder was committed by Orestes with Pylades after Orestes had killed Ægisthus. Orestes calls the act unholy but maintains that it avenged Agamemnon and was commanded by Apollo.

PYLADES, ORESTES, CHORUS.

The murder of Clytemnestra is the charge brought against Orestes and Electra. Orestes defends it as revenge for Agamemnon, but the Chorus presents it as a deed that brings madness, grief, and continuing bloodshed.

ELECTRA, ORESTES, PYLADES, CHORUS.

Orestes says that he slew his mother, while Pylades says that he directed the sword and Electra says that she incited Orestes and freed him from delay. Their participation in the killing is presented as the reason for their present suffering and threatened punishment.

PHRYGIAN, CHORUS.

Orestes and Pylades kill Clytemnestra after accusing her of giving Agamemnon's offspring over to die at Argos. Apollo later identifies Orestes as the one compelled to shed his mother's blood and sends him to trial.

CHOR. Thou hast told wondrous things concerning him who has appeared,

Clytemnestra’s death is reported as the result of her son’s retaliation for Agamemnon’s murder. Orestes calls the act just in motive but says that it brought him no good fortune from the gods.

IPH. O dearest one, no more, for thou art dearest. I hold thee, Orestes,

Orestes refers to killing his mother in revenge for his father and declines to discuss the deed in detail. He says that the memory of it caused his exile and persecution by the Erinnyes.

the planned revenge against Clytemnestra and Aegisthus in The Electra of Euripides

Euripides · 419 to 409 BC · the work’s page · read it where the planned revenge against Clytemnestra and Aegisthus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Orestes, aided by Electra and accompanied by Pylades, undertakes vengeance for Agamemnon’s murder by planning to kill Clytemnestra and Aegisthus. Orestes targets Aegisthus at a feast, while Electra lures Clytemnestra to the house by claiming to have borne a son. They kill both, then face horror, remorse, blood-guilt, and its consequences.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

ELECTRA

Orestes returns with Pylades intending to kill Clytemnestra and Aegisthus in repayment for Agamemnon's murder. Electra openly agrees to participate, even saying that she would use the same axe that killed her father.

[ORESTES _and_ PYLADES _go in, following the_ SERVANTS.

Orestes plans to kill Aegisthus by meeting him at the feast outside the city, while Electra plans to bring Clytemnestra to the house by claiming that she has borne a son. Orestes and the Old Man depart to begin these actions, and Electra prepares to follow through with the killing if necessary.

LEADER.

Electra arranges for Clytemnestra to enter the house, where Electra and Orestes kill her and Aegisthus. The siblings then cover Clytemnestra’s body and confront the horror, remorse, and consequences of the deed.

CHARACTERS OF THE PLAY

The killing of Clytemnestra by Orestes causes Orestes to be pursued by Furies.

LEADER.

Orestes says that he and Pylades are guilty of having slain their mother. Iphigenia uses this bloodguilt as the basis for declaring them unclean and requiring their purification before the goddess.
