Abstract written by a model

Toby is a queer mongrel dog, ugly, long-haired, lop-eared, brown-and-white, and half spaniel and half lurcher. Owned or kept by Mr. Sherman at No. 7 Pinchin Lane, he has an extraordinary sense of smell and is used to track scents. Holmes obtains Toby to follow a creasote trail left by Jonathan Small and his associate. Toby tracks it from Pondicherry Lodge through London, eagerly leading Holmes and the narrator into a timber-yard. When crossed trails mislead him, he is returned to the confusion point, finds the true route toward the river, and is kept by Holmes for possible future use.

Toby is in one work on this site, The Sign of the Four. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Chapter VI

Toby is a queer mongrel owned or kept by Sherman, the bird-stuffer in Pinchin Lane. Holmes regards Toby’s extraordinary sense of smell as more useful than the entire London detective force for following the creosote trail.

Chapter VII

Toby is an ugly, long-haired, brown-and-white dog obtained from Mr. Sherman. After smelling the creasote handkerchief, Toby follows the scent from Pondicherry Lodge through the grounds and London streets toward the fugitives.

Toby becomes frantic with excitement and leads Sherlock Holmes and the narrator into Broderick and Nelson’s large timber-yard. He races through the sawdust, shavings, alleys, passages, and wood-piles before springing onto a large barrel and looking for appreciation.

Toby initially follows a false creasote trail but is not blamed because two trails had crossed. After being returned to the point of confusion, he picks up the true trail toward the river and is retained by Holmes as a possible future aid.
