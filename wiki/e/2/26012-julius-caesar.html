Virgil · -0028/-0018

Julius Caesar, a ruler of an empire bounded by ocean and heavens, impatiently awaits and grasps promised power among Roman and Julian lines, leading to Augustus's rise.

Julius Caesar in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Julius Caesar is an emperor and conqueror whose first acts are placed in the first year of the 180th olympiad. He subdues the warlike nations of the Gauls, enlarges the Roman empire to the British isles, conquers Britain, and compels the Britons to pay tribute. His achievements earn him the surname Divus and the title Divine.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE PREFACE.

Julius Caesar is identified as the emperor whose first acts fall in the first year of the 180th olympiad. After subduing the warlike nations of the Gauls, he enlarged the Roman empire as far as the British isles and was surnamed Divus because of his great achievements.

For over against the French shore^ opposite to the Hercynian

Julius Caesar is described as the first figure mentioned by an author to have conquered Britain and compelled the Britons to pay tribute. He is said to have lived in the period of the text and to have gained the title of Divine through his achievements.

Julius Cæsar in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

A Roman deity, father of Augustus and descendant of Venus through Æneas, is assassinated in the Senate-house; Venus transforms his liberated soul into a brilliant star.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE VIII. [XV.745-879]

Julius Cæsar is assassinated in the Senate-house after conspirators bring drawn swords into the temple. Venus carries his liberated soul among the stars, where it becomes a brilliant star with a flaming train.
