Hellespont bridge in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

A bridge constructed for Xerxes’s army to cross from Asia into Europe; after Xerxes crossed at Abydos, Themistocles destroyed it to hinder Persian land forces.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

LAST TEN BOOKS / BOOK XI.

The Hellespont bridge was constructed for Xerxes's army to pass from Asia into Europe. Xerxes crossed it at Abydos after the work had been completed.

bridge over the Hellespont in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

A bridge built by Xerxes across the Hellespont enables Persian land forces to pass from Asia into Hellas/Europe. Made with carpentry, cordage, and planking, it is vulnerable to sea and wind. The Chorus notes its weakness, and Darius condemns it as mortal defiance of divine power.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE PERSIANS

Xerxes has a bridge made over the Hellespont so that his land forces can pass from Asia into Hellas. The Chorus describes the bridge as dependent on cordage and planking that are weak against the force of the sea and wind.

The GHOST OF DARIUS rises.

The bridge across the Hellespont is built so Xerxes can move his large army from Asia into Europe. Darius condemns the project as an attempt by a mortal to restrain the sea and oppose divine power.
