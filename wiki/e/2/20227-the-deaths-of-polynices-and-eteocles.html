single combat between Eteocles and Polynices in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

A single combat for the royal palace: after a truce, Eteocles and Polynices fight between the armies, mortally wound each other, and leave no victor.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

MESSENGER, JOCASTA, CHORUS.

Eteocles and Polynices fight between the two armies after a truce and vows that the armies will abide by their decision. They exchange spear and sword attacks until each mortally wounds the other, leaving no victor.

the deaths of Polynices and Eteocles in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

The deaths of Polynices and Eteocles are their prophesied mutual killing in single combat during the war against Thebes. Though warned, Polynices proceeds; after conflict between Thebes and Argos, the brothers kill each other, ending their struggle.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ANTIGONE

Polynices and Eteocles kill each other in single combat after the conflict between Thebes and Argos. Their mutual death ends the struggle between the two brothers.

CHORUS.

Oedipus prophesies that Polynices and Eteocles will kill one another. Antigone recognizes the prophecy as a warning against the war, but Polynices dismisses the danger and proceeds.

the confrontation of Eteocles and Polynices in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

The confrontation of Eteocles and Polynices is a cursed brotherly combat at the seventh gate. Eteocles meets Polynices as ruler, rival, and brother despite the Chorus’s warning. They strike each other dead with swords, ending their conflict and saving the city, but leaving their house and the Chorus grieving.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Enter on one side THE SPY; on the other ETEOCLES and the SIX

The confrontation of Eteocles and Polynices is set at the seventh gate. Eteocles accepts the meeting as ruler against would-be ruler and brother against brother, while the Chorus warns that their mutual bloodshed will create a sin that time cannot expiate.

Enter THE SPY.

The mutual slaughter ends the brothers’ conflict, with each striking the other dead by the sword. Their deaths save the city from further conflict but leave the house and the Chorus in grief.

the fratricide in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912

The fratricide is the same-day mutual battle in which Eteocles and Polyneices kill each other, leaving neither brother's armor as a trophy.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ANTIGONE

The fratricide occurs when Eteocles and Polyneices kill each other in battle on the same day. The Chorus describes the brothers as the only two of the seven opposing champions who do not leave their armor behind as trophies.
