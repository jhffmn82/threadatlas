the wind in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

A wind carries Protesilaus from Laodamia and favors Danaans; later contrary winds delay their ships, a divine delay Laodamia sees as intentional.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

XIII

The wind carried Protesilaus away from Laodamia, although it was favorable to the sailors and unfavorable to her. Later, contrary winds delay the Danaan ships, and Laodamia interprets that delay as divine rather than accidental.

wind in Ozma of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1907

Wind, a mischievous natural force, grows from a strong breeze into violent storm-making power, creates huge waves, tears ropes, lifts the chicken-coop with Dorothy, then stops.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

1. The Girl in the Chicken Coop

The wind grows from a strong breeze into a violent force that creates huge waves and a terrible storm. It tears the ropes from the chicken-coop, lifts it into the air with Dorothy clinging to it, and later stops blowing.
