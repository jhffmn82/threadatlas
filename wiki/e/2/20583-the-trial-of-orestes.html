Abstract written by a model

In The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies, the trial of Orestes is the Athens judgment over whether Orestes must be punished for killing Clytemnestra and ends with Athena’s deciding vote acquitting him. In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Orestes' trial at the Areopagus concerns the charge that he shed Clytemnestra’s blood and ends with Orestes winning after Pallas supplies the equal deciding vote. In The Electra of Euripides, Orestes' trial establishes justice through equal judgment and mercy, with the judges dividing equally so that mercy prevails and he goes free.

the trial of Orestes in The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies

Aeschylus · 457 BC · the work’s page · read it where the trial of Orestes first appears

Abstract written by a model

The trial of Orestes is an Athens judgment over whether Orestes must be punished for killing Clytemnestra. Apollo sends him to Athena’s city to seek release from suffering. Athena appoints twelve Athenian judges and requires sworn witnesses and evidence. Orestes admits the killing, while Apollo argues that Zeus commanded it. The judges divide equally, and Athena’s deciding vote acquits Orestes, establishing his atonement. The balanced result preserves the Furies’ honor and supports their reconciliation with the younger gods.

THE FURIES

The judgment of Orestes has not yet occurred in this unit. Apollo sends Orestes to Athena’s city, where judges are expected to hear the case and determine whether he can be delivered from his suffering.

_The scene changes to Athens. In the foreground, the Temple of Athena

Athena orders that Orestes’ case be decided by judges chosen from the people of Athens. The parties are to present witnesses and evidence under oath, with the judgment carefully distinguishing the competing claims.

[_Re-enter Athena, with twelve Athenian citizens_.

The trial concerns whether Orestes should be punished for killing his mother. Orestes admits the act, Apollo defends it as commanded by Zeus, and the equal division of the judges’ votes leads to Orestes’ acquittal through Athena’s deciding vote.

CHORUS

The trial of Orestes ends with a balanced vote and no dishonor to the Furies. Athena cites the judgment that Orestes’s killing was atoned for, and the result becomes the basis for reconciling the Furies with the younger gods.

Orestes' trial at the Areopagus in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Orestes' trial at the Areopagus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Orestes’ trial at the council of the Areopagus concerns the charge that he shed Clytemnestra’s blood. Ordered by Apollo to face three Furies on the hill of Mars, Orestes and the eldest Erinnyes present their cases; Phoebus testifies for him, Pallas supplies the equal deciding vote, and Orestes wins.
