Pyramid Mountain in Dorothy and the Wizard in Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1908

Pyramid Mountain is a high, pyramid- or cone-shaped mountain beyond the Valley of Voe, with its point lost in clouds. Travelers reach its foot after crossing a river and going overland. An arched opening leads to stairs, including a spiral staircase, rising inside; halfway up are a cavern and the braided man's factory. Its interior also has rough tunnels, narrow galleries, cracks, loose rocks, and winding passages leading with difficulty to the dragonettes' cavern.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

9. They Fight the Invisible Bears

Pyramid Mountain is a high, pyramid-shaped mountain on the far side of the Valley of Voe. Its top is lost in the clouds, and the travelers reach its foot after crossing the river and hurrying overland.

10. The Braided Man of Pyramid Mountain

Pyramid Mountain is cone-shaped and so tall that its point is lost in the clouds. An arched opening leads into stairs that circle upward inside it, and the braided man has his factory halfway up the mountain.

13. The Den of the Dragonettes

The mountain contains rough, steep tunnels, narrow galleries, cracks, loose rocks, and winding passages rather than a regular flight of steps to the surface. Dorothy, the Wizard, Zeb, and the buggy travel through it with difficulty before reaching the dragonettes' cavern.
