the death of Rhesus in The Rhesus of Euripides

Euripides · -0454/-0439

The death of Rhesus occurs in Rhesus during a night attack on the sleeping Thracians. Odysseus, accompanied by Diomede, stabs Rhesus and wounds a charioteer. Though first blamed on Greek spies, Hector and the Muse identify Odysseus; the Muse says Athena directed it. Rhesus is mourned, then hidden beneath the earth.

_Enter_ PARIS.

Rhesus is stabbed while asleep, and the attack is initially attributed to Greek spies. Hector and the Muse identify Odysseus as responsible, while the Muse declares that Athena directed the deed.

NOTES

The death of Rhesus results from the night attack by Odysseus and Diomedes while the Thracians are sleeping. It brings grief and a lament from the Muse, but Rhesus is not given an ordinary grave; he is hidden beneath the earth for a possible future return.
