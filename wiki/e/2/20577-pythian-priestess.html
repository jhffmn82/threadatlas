THE FURIES

The Pythian Priestess recounts the prophetic succession of the shrine, naming Earth, Themis, Phoebe, and Apollo as its prophetic powers. When she enters the shrine, she sees Orestes at the central altar with a bloodied sword and the sleeping Furies, and she leaves in terror.

Pythian Priestess in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Pythian Priestess first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Pythian Priestess is at Delphi and is an authority of Apollo’s oracle there. She issues commands and oracles to communities and individuals: she forbids the Lacedæmonians to touch Arcadia; requires funeral rites for children stoned unjustly by the people of Caphyæ; gives the Phigalians terms for returning; and directs the Orchomenians to bring Hesiod’s bones from Naupactus to cure pestilence, with a crow to reveal them. She also orders rites for Actaeon, consultation of Trophonius for drought, and military actions by the Liparæans and the ruler of Haliartus. At Delphi she proclaims Pindar’s share of firstfruits and tells Themistocles to remove Persian spoils from Apollo’s temple.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIII.

The Pythian Priestess said that the children stoned by the people of Caphyæ had not been slain justly. She instructed the people to bury them and perform funeral rites for them annually.

CHAPTER XXXIX.

The Pythian Priestess told the Phigalians that they had no hope of returning by themselves. She said that their return would be possible if they took 100 picked men from Oresthasium and those men were slain in battle.

CHAPTER XXXVIII.

The Pythian Priestess instructed the Orchomenians to bring Hesiod’s bones from Naupactus to Orchomenus as a remedy for pestilence. When asked where the bones were, she said that a crow would show them; concerning Actaeon, she ordered them to bury his remains and fasten a brazen copy of the spectre to a stone.

CHAPTER XIV.

The Pythian Priestess told Themistocles not to place the beautiful spoils of the Persians in Apollo’s temple. She instructed him to remove them and send them home as quickly as possible.
