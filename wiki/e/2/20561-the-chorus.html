the Chorus in The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies

Aeschylus · -0457

The Chorus is a group of captive Trojan women serving the palace household. Ordered to bear libations to Agamemnon’s grave, it mourns him, reads Clytemnestra’s dream as the dead man’s anger, and urges Electra to seek an avenger. Loyal to Electra and Orestes, it supports their prayers, counsels secrecy, invokes Justice and blood-for-blood law, and helps bring Aegisthus unguarded. It celebrates the usurpers’ deaths, then witnesses Orestes’ distress at the Furies’ appearance.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

THE LIBATION-BEARERS

The Chorus accompanies Electra to Agamemnon’s tomb bearing libations and mourns the dead king. It interprets Clytemnestra’s dream as showing that the dead are angry with their slayers, advises Electra in her prayer, and calls for an avenger to deliver the house.

ORESTES (_approaching suddenly_)

The Chorus supports Orestes and Electra’s prayers while warning them to conceal their words from their masters. It invokes the dead, Justice, and the inherited law that whoever sheds blood will perish by bloodshed.

[_Enter Orestes and Pylades, in guise of travellers_.

The Chorus supports Orestes’ return and prays for his success in avenging Agamemnon. It helps ensure that Aegisthus comes without guards, celebrates the deaths of the usurpers, and then witnesses Orestes’ distress when the Furies appear.
