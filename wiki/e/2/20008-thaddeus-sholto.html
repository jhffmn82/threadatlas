Thaddeus Sholto in The Sign of the Four

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1890-02

Thaddeus Sholto is a small, thirty-year-old, wealthy man with a bald shining scalp fringed by red hair, a confirmed hypochondriac, and the twin son of Major John Sholto. He is Bartholomew Sholto’s brother, lives in the Sholto apartment after leaving Pondicherry Lodge, and uses a hookah as a sedative. As an heir entitled to half the Agra treasure, he seeks an inheritance claim for Miss Morstan and himself. He invites Miss Morstan, Sherlock Holmes, and Dr. Watson to hear the history of Major John Sholto, Captain Arthur Morstan, and the treasure, explains that he sent Miss Morstan pearls, and leads them to Pondicherry Lodge to claim her share. There he fears Bartholomew’s silence, admits helping lower the treasure, and reports the death. Suspected because of a quarrel and his earlier presence, he is arrested but later released and cleared by an unshaken alibi. A later record says he is killed by Tonga’s poisoned dart, though Jonathan Small says Sholto was already dead when he entered.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter IV

Thaddeus Sholto invites Miss Morstan, Sherlock Holmes, and Dr. Watson to his rooms and tells them about Major John Sholto, Captain Arthur Morstan, and the Agra treasure. He says he sent Miss Morstan pearls at intervals, argues that she should receive her share, and asks her and her companions to accompany him to Norwood to claim it from Bartholomew Sholto.

Chapter V

Thaddeus Sholto brings Holmes, Watson, and Miss Morstan to Pondicherry Lodge, but becomes terrified when Bartholomew does not answer. He says that he helped Bartholomew lower the treasure through an opening in the ceiling, fears being suspected of the murder, and follows Holmes’s advice to report the death to the police.

Chapter VI

Thaddeus Sholto is found in a distressed state after his brother’s death and is suspected by Athelney Jones because he had quarreled with Bartholomew and was with him beforehand. Jones arrests him for being concerned in his brother’s death, but Holmes says he can clear him.

Chapter IX

Thaddeus Sholto is released after proving an unshakable alibi showing that he was continually seen after leaving his brother’s room. The newspaper reports that it is impossible for him to have taken part in the Upper Norwood tragedy.

Chapter XI

Thaddeus Sholto is killed by a poisoned dart fired by Tonga. Jonathan Small says that Sholto was already dead when he climbed through the window, and Holmes believes the poison acted before Small reached the room.
