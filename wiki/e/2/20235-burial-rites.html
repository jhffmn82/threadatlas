Abstract written by a model

In Four Plays of Aeschylus, Eteocles is to receive burial with care, mourners, ritual, and honor, while Polynices is denied women-wailers, a tomb, a dirge, and attendance, though Antigone and part of the Chorus undertake to provide his burial. In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Creon grants Eteocles the full sacred rites appropriate to an honored dead man while denying them to Polynices, though the watchman reports that someone has performed the required rites for Polynices. In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, funeral rites are owed to Creon’s son Eteocles and Polynices; Creon washes and lays out his son, and the brothers are brought to the city for friends’ lamentation.
