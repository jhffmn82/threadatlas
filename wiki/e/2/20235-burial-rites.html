  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, funeral rites are owed to Creon’s son Eteocles and Polynices; Creon washes and lays out his son, and the brothers are brought to the city for friends’ lamentation.
  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Creon grants Eteocles the full sacred rites appropriate to an honored dead man while denying them to Polynices, though the watchman reports that someone has performed the required rites for Polynices.
  • In Four Plays of Aeschylus, Eteocles is to receive burial with care, mourners, ritual, and honor, while Polynices is denied women-wailers, a tomb, a dirge, and attendance, though Antigone and part of the Chorus undertake to provide his burial.

funeral rites in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Funeral rites are owed to Creon’s son, Eteocles, and Polynices; Creon washes and lays out his son, and the brothers are brought to the city for friends’ lamentation.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

MESSENGER, JOCASTA, CHORUS.

Creon comes to wash and lay out his dead son, saying that the living must honor the dead. The dead brothers are later brought toward the city so that their friends can lament over them.
