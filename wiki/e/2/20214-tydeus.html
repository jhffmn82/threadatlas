Tydeus

person · 4 works · 19 quoted facts · 10 connections
Also called: the bane of state and home, Τυδέα, Τυδεύς, Τυδεὺς, Τυδεῖ

Abstract written by a model

In Four Plays of Aeschylus, Tydeus is an Argive warrior who attacks the Proetides gate with violent eagerness despite Amphiaraus's opposition. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Tydeus is Oeneus's son, an exile who serves as Adrastus's envoy and kills fifty men in an ambush. In The Library, Tydeus is an Aetolian son of Oeneus who becomes one of the Seven Champions and dies during the attack on Thebes. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Tydeus is slain by Melanippus during the Argive siege of Thebes and reportedly buried by Mæon.

Tydeus in The Library

Apollodorus · about AD 70 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Tydeus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Tydeus is an Aetolian son of Oeneus, born to Periboea according to one account and Gorge according to another. After killing relatives or conspirators against Oeneus, he is banished from and flees Calydon. In Argos he fights Polynices until Adrastus separates them, marries Adrastus’s daughter Deipyle, and fathers Diomedes. He becomes one of the Seven Champions, wins boxing at Nemea, and serves as Adrastus’s envoy to Eteocles; ambushed by fifty men, he kills all but Maeon. In the attack on Thebes, stationed at Crenidian Gate, he is fatally wounded by Melanippus. After consuming Melanippus’s brains, he loses Athena’s intended medicine and dies in Thebes.

Tydeus was born to Oeneus by Periboea, though Pisander said that his mother was Gorge. After killing relatives or conspirators against Oeneus, according to differing accounts, he was exiled and fled to Argos, where he married Deipyle and fathered Diomedes. He later marched against Thebes with Adrastus and died from a wound inflicted by Melanippus.

VI. ᾿Ἑτεοκλῆς δὲ καὶ ἸΠολυνείκης περὶ τῆς

Tydeus, son of Oeneus, has fled Calydon and fights Polynices at Adrastus's palace before Adrastus separates them. He marries Deipyle, joins the champions, wins the boxing match at Nemea, and is sent to tell Eteocles to yield the kingdom to Polynices. When fifty men ambush him, he kills all of them except Maeon.

Οὗτος οὖν Θηβαίοις μαντενομένοις 3 εἶπε νική-

Tydeus is wounded in the belly by Melanippus and lies half dead. He splits open Melanippus's head and consumes the brains, causing Athena to withhold the medicine intended to make him immortal.

Tydeus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Tydeus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Tydeus is slain by Melanippus during the Argive siege of Thebes, then reportedly buried by Mæon beneath three rude stones near Melanippus’s tomb; Athenians claim Lysander bribed him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XVIII.

Tydeus was slain by Melanippus during the Argive siege of Thebes. Three rude stones near Melanippus’s tomb are said by Theban antiquarians to mark Tydeus’s burial, and Mæon is said to have buried him.
