the forced bridal of the Danaids in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

The forced bridal of the Danaids is a hated, unwanted wedlock decreed by Aegyptus between his sons and the daughters of Danaus. The sons seek the marriages, but the Danaids reject them as imposed, loveless unions with kinsmen that would destroy their maidenhood. Horror at this threat drives the daughters’ flight from Egypt to Argos, where the action turns on whether Argos will protect them from pursuing suitors who seek to abduct them for marriage. The women pray for escape, preferring exile or death to the bridegrooms’ beds.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

INTRODUCTION

The forced bridal of the Danaids is the threat that drives their flight from Egypt to Argos. The action of The Suppliant Maidens turns on whether Argos will allow the pursuing suitors to abduct the women for marriage.

THE SUPPLIANT MAIDENS / DEDICATION

Marriage between the sons of Aegyptus and the daughters of Danaus is desired by the sons but regarded with horror by the daughters. Their rejection of it leads to their flight to Argos.

The Suppliant Maidens

The forced marriages were decreed by Aegyptus for his sons and the daughters of Danaus. The daughters reject these unions because they are imposed rather than desired and because they would make unwilling brides of kinsmen.

Re-enter DANAUS.

The Danaids describe the threatened marriage to the sons of Aegyptus as loveless, hateful, and destructive of their maidenhood. They pray to escape it, even preferring death or exile to being forced into the bridegrooms’ beds.
