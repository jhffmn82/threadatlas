the poisoned thorn in The Sign of the Four

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1890-02

A long, dark, black thorn, not an English thorn, is driven or shot through a ceiling opening into Bartholomew Sholto’s scalp above his ear. Its pointed end bears dried gummy poison, apparently causing fatal poisoning. Holmes warns of it, and Watson removes it, leaving a tiny speck of blood.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter V

A long, dark thorn is found embedded in the skin just above Bartholomew Sholto’s ear. Holmes warns that it is poisoned, and Watson removes it, leaving only a tiny speck of blood.

Chapter VI

The poisoned thorn is found in Bartholomew Sholto’s scalp, apparently driven or shot through the ceiling opening. It is long, sharp, black, and coated near the point with a dried gummy substance; Holmes believes it caused the fatal poisoning.
