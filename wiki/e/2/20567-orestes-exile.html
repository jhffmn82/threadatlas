Abstract written by a model

In The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies, Orestes’ exile is his banishment after the murders, from which he returns secretly to his father’s tomb. In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Orestes’ exile is his wandering after killing Clytemnestra, as he travels with Pylades until arriving in Tauris. In Iphigenia in Tauris, Orestes’ exile is his arrival in Tauris, a banishment half forced and half sought after he killed his mother.

Orestes says that he has left his country unwillingly and willingly and is now in Tauris with Pylades. He describes himself as being nowhere and everywhere after the death of his mother.

notes

The wanderings of Orestes are explicitly noted and linked to his condition after his earlier crimes. The notes refer readers to an explanatory discussion of these wanderings and to Orestes’ eventual arrival in Tauris.
