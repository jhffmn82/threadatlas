  • In The Electra of Euripides, Electra is the central figure who mourns Agamemnon, seeks vengeance against Clytemnestra and Aegisthus, assists Orestes in their murders, and is promised to Pylades.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Electra watches over and assists Orestes, helps plan the murders of Clytemnestra and Helen, seizes Hermione, and is declared by Apollo to marry Pylades.
  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Electra mourns Agamemnon, resists Clytemnestra, seeks vengeance against Aegisthus, and joins Orestes in deceiving and killing him.
  • In The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies, Electra brings libations to Agamemnon’s grave, prays for Orestes’ return and vengeance, recognizes him, and urges the restoration of their house and the killing of Aegisthus.
  • In The Library, Electra appears as the daughter of Atlas and Zeus who bears Iasion and Dardanus, and as Agamemnon’s daughter who saves Orestes and is later married by him to Pylades.

Electra in The Electra of Euripides

Euripides · -0419/-0409

Electra is a woman, daughter of Agamemnon and Clytemnestra, sister of the exiled Orestes, wife of the Peasant yet still a virgin, and the central figure of a tragedy. Childhood suffering leaves her consumed by love for her dead father and brother and hatred for her mother and Aegisthus. Though born to luxury and state, she lives impoverished in a desolate mountain hut, mourning Agamemnon, doing household labor, and grieving her lost youth and status. She longs for Orestes’ return and seeks blood vengeance, ready to use a sword and declaring that she would help kill Clytemnestra with the axe used against Agamemnon. When Orestes returns and is recognized, she devises a summons for Clytemnestra by claiming to have given birth to a son. After Orestes kills Aegisthus, Electra crowns him and Pylades and condemns Aegisthus’s body. She confronts Clytemnestra with accusations, urges Orestes to kill her, gives the signal and word, and assists in the murder. Afterwards, she accepts blame and mourns her mother. By divine decree she is promised to Pylades and must leave Argos.

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Introduction[1]

Electra is the central figure of Euripides' tragedy. Shattered by childhood trauma, she is haunted by hatred for her mother and stepfather and love for her dead father and exiled brother; she also resents poverty, remembers former luxury and status, and feels her youth passing.

ELECTRA

Electra lives in mourning at a hut on the mountains of Argos, carrying water and performing household labor despite her royal birth. She mourns Agamemnon, condemns Clytemnestra and Aegisthus, longs for Orestes' return, and declares that she would help kill her mother with the same axe used against her father.

[ORESTES _and_ PYLADES _go in, following the_ SERVANTS.

Electra welcomes the Old Man, who brings food and gifts, and accepts his recognition of Orestes. She embraces Orestes after the scar confirms his identity, then devises the plan to summon Clytemnestra by claiming that she has been delivered of a son. She remains in the house with a sword across her knees, prepared to act if Orestes dies.

CHORUS.

Electra hears that Orestes has conquered and Aegisthus is dead, crowns Orestes and Pylades, and speaks a long condemnation over Aegisthus's body. When Clytemnestra approaches, Electra urges Orestes to kill her as she killed Agamemnon.

LEADER.

Electra confronts Clytemnestra with accusations concerning Iphigenia, Agamemnon, Orestes, and Clytemnestra’s relationship with Aegisthus. She summons Clytemnestra to perform rites for her newborn child as part of a plan to kill her, gives Orestes the signal and word for the murder, and helps kill Clytemnestra. Afterward she accepts blame, mourns her mother, and is told to marry Pylades and leave Argos.

Electra in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Electra is the unmarried daughter of Agamemnon and Clytæmnestra, also called a daughter of Tyndareus, and Orestes’ devoted sister. She remains beside Orestes as his constant watcher, guarding his sleep and tending his body, while condemning Apollo’s command that led to Clytæmnestra’s murder. She assists Orestes in that murder and hopes Menelaus will help them. After the Argive assembly condemns Electra and Orestes to death, she laments her ruined family and prepares for self-slaughter with him rather than abandon him. She helps plan the murder of Helen, conceals the attack, proposes seizing Hermione as a pledge against Menelaus, and orders Hermione held at sword point. Ordered to set the palace on fire, she is finally declared by Apollo to marry Pylades.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

ORESTES.

Electra remains unmarried and devotes herself to caring for Orestes, guarding his sleep and tending his body. She condemns Apollo for commanding the murder of Clytæmnestra and hopes that Menelaus will aid her and Orestes.

PYLADES, ORESTES, CHORUS.

Electra remains in the palace under guard and is condemned to die with Orestes for the murder of Clytemnestra. She learns of the assembly's decision from the Messenger and laments the ruin of her family and the approaching self-slaughter.

ELECTRA, ORESTES, PYLADES, CHORUS.

Electra mourns Orestes and refuses to abandon him, asking to die by his sword rather than be killed by the Argives. She proposes the plan to seize Hermione and use her as a pledge for the safety of herself, Orestes, and Pylades. She guards the house, directs the Chorus, helps conceal the attack on Helen, and orders Hermione to be held at sword point.

PHRYGIAN, CHORUS.

Electra is among those threatened with death if Menelaus brings aid against Orestes. Orestes orders her to set fire to the palace, and Apollo declares that she is to marry Pylades.

Electra in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Electra is Agamemnon’s daughter, sister and childhood nurse of Orestes, and sister of Chrysothemis. Unmarried, childless, and oppressed within her father’s palace, she lives under Clytemnestra’s rule among those she considers her father’s murderers. She publicly and continually mourns Agamemnon, follows divine law, hates Clytemnestra, and seeks vengeance, especially the killing of Aegisthus. She urges Chrysothemis to reject Clytemnestra’s offerings and pray for Orestes’ return. After a false report that Orestes is dead, she believes her sole hope is lost, grieves over an urn said to hold his remains, and proposes killing Aegisthus herself when Chrysothemis refuses to help. Orestes then reveals that he lives. Electra joins his deception, presses him to kill Aegisthus, and waits outside for the result.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

ELECTRA

Electra remains in Agamemnon's palace, mourning her father and living under the authority of his murderers. She urges Chrysothemis to reject Clytemnestra's offerings and pray at Agamemnon's tomb for Orestes to return and defeat their enemies.

CHORUS.

The Chorus addresses its daughter and tells her that the vision of the night has brought reassurance.

_Enter_ CLYTEMNESTRA.

Electra argues that Clytemnestra had no right to murder her father and condemns her for living with the blood-guilty Aegisthus. When the Old Man reports Orestes' death, Electra is devastated, believing that she has lost her only hope of avenging her father and her own suffering. She resolves to remain outside the palace and waste away among her enemies.

_Enter_ CHRYSOTHEMIS.

Electra rejects Chrysothemis's belief that Orestes has returned and tells her that he is dead. She proposes that they kill Aegisthus to avenge their father's death, insisting that she will undertake the plan alone when Chrysothemis refuses.

_Enter_ ORESTES _and_ PYLADES, _with the urn_.

Electra receives the urn and mourns intensely, believing it contains Orestes' remains. She recognizes Orestes when he reveals himself, helps maintain the deception, urges him to kill Aegisthus, and remains outside to watch for his arrival.

Electra in The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies

Aeschylus · -0457

Electra, daughter of Agamemnon and Clytemnestra and sister of Orestes, is an orphaned, homeless child whose surviving support is Orestes. At Agamemnon’s grave she brings libations, seeks guidance in prayer, and asks for Orestes’ return, vengeance on the slayers, and justice. Seeing signs of Orestes, she recognizes him, shares grief and hope, prays with him, and urges restoration of their house and the killing of Aegisthus.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE LIBATION-BEARERS

Electra comes to Agamemnon’s grave with libations and asks the Chorus of Captive Women how she should pray. She prays for Orestes to return, for the slayers to be slain, and for justice to guide the right to victory; she then notices a lock of hair and footprints that appear to indicate Orestes’ presence.

ORESTES (_approaching suddenly_)

Electra recognizes Orestes as her brother and expresses her love, grief, and hope for him. She joins him in praying at Agamemnon’s tomb and asks that Orestes restore their house and kill Aegisthus.
