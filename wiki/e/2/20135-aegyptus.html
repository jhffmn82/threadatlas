Aegyptus in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

Aegyptus, son of Belus and brother of Danaus, descends from Io through Epaphus and Libya. He fathers sons and decrees they marry Danaus’s daughters, prompting the daughters’ flight from Egypt while his sons pursue them.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE SUPPLIANT MAIDENS / DEDICATION

Aegyptus descends from Io through Epaphus, Libya, and Belus. His sons seek marriage with the daughters of Danaus.

The Suppliant Maidens

Aegyptus decreed that his sons should marry the daughters of his brother, Danaus. His decree is the cause of the daughters' flight from Egypt.

Egyptus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Egyptus, son of Belus and Anchinoe and twin of Danaus, was settled in Arabia, conquered the Melampods’ country, and fathered fifty sons, who later came to Argos seeking marriage with Danaus’s daughters.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE LIBRARY, II. 1. 1-3

Egyptus was the son of Belus and Anchinoe and the twin brother of Danaus. He was settled in Arabia, conquered the country of the Melampods, and had fifty sons by several women. His sons later came to Argos and sought to marry the daughters of Danaus.
