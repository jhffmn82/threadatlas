Aegyptus

person · 2 works · 9 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Aegyptus’, Egypt, Egyptus, the youthful stock of Belus’ line

Abstract written by a model

In Four Plays of Aeschylus, Aegyptus was the son of Belus and brother of Danaus, descended from Io through Epaphus and Libya; he fathered sons and decreed they marry Danaus’s daughters, prompting their flight from Egypt while his sons pursued them. In The Library, Egyptus was the son of Belus and Anchinoe and twin of Danaus; he settled in Arabia, conquered the Melampods’ country, fathered fifty sons, and had them seek marriage with Danaus’s daughters at Argos.

Egyptus in The Library

Apollodorus · about AD 70 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Egyptus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Egyptus, son of Belus and Anchinoe and twin of Danaus, was settled in Arabia, conquered the Melampods’ country, and fathered fifty sons, who later came to Argos seeking marriage with Danaus’s daughters.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE LIBRARY, II. 1. 1-3

Egyptus was the son of Belus and Anchinoe and the twin brother of Danaus. He was settled in Arabia, conquered the country of the Melampods, and had fifty sons by several women. His sons later came to Argos and sought to marry the daughters of Danaus.
