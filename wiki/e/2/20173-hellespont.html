Abstract written by a model

In Four Plays of Aeschylus, the Hellespont is the strait crossed by Xerxes’ forces on a fragile bridge, whose passage is endangered by storms and condemned by Darius. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, the Hellespont is a strategic passage contested by Eumenes, Antipater, Craterus, Polyperchon, and Clitus. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Hellespont is bridged and crossed by Xerxes, and is the setting for Dorieus’s sailing and naval operations near Dardanium, Abydos, and Cyzicum. In Heroides and Amores, the Hellespont separates Abydos from Sestos, keeps Hero and Leander apart, and is a stormy danger to lovers, ships, and swimmers. In The Library, the Hellespont is the sea where Helle drowns and which is thereafter named after her. In The Fall of Troy, the Hellespont is a fair-flowing waterway beside which Achaean ships lie and Achilles is buried, and across which the departing Argives travel. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Hellespont is the strait separating Asia and Europe, where Protesilaus died and Leander swam between Abydos and Sestos.

the Hellespont in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · about 24 to 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where the Hellespont first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Hellespont is a swollen water and dangerous place, named after Helle because she drowned in it. It separates Abydos from Sestos and, for Leander and Hero, keeps the lovers apart while Leander repeatedly must swim across it. Stormy waves and winds make it hostile to Hero, threaten ships and swimmers, and determine whether the speaker may embrace the addressee. Hero watches and protests its waves, invokes Helle’s death, and kisses Leander’s garments before he crosses. Only when tranquil is swimming trusted.
