Abstract written by a model

Times was a St. Paul daily, morning Republican paper and one of three competing papers. It competed with the Pioneer and Minnesotian for presidential messages, telegraphic news, and, after the 1859 election, state printer; it also printed and circulated bogus Minnesotian reports before learning of the deception. Managed by Thomas M. Newson, it traded insults with Dr. Foster of the Minnesotian, briefly merged as the Minnesotian and Times, then dissolved. Newson leased its office and material to W. R. Marshall and Thomas F. Slaughter, who started the St. Paul Daily Press. Its job department employed Richard Bradley and reporter Jebb.

The Times was a Republican paper managed by Thomas M. Newson, who exchanged frequent insults with Dr. Foster of the Minnesotian. It was temporarily merged with the Minnesotian as the Minnesotian and Times, but the partnership dissolved around the time the legislature adjourned. Newson later leased the office and its material to W. R. Marshall and Thomas F. Slaughter, who started the St. Paul Daily Press.

TERRITORIAL PRINTERS IN CIVIL LIFE.

The St. Paul Times employed Richard Bradley as foreman of its job department and Jebb as a reporter. Jebb's report about stolen chickens, which ended with a papal bull of excommunication against the thief, marked the end of his reporting career.
