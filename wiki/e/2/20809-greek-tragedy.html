Greek tragedy in The Rhesus of Euripides

Euripides · 454 to 439 BC · the work’s page · read it where Greek tragedy first appears

Abstract written by a model

Greek tragedy is presented as a form whose conventions were being challenged by Rhesus's wild, extravagant, martial, and partly fantastic scenes. The introduction places Rhesus near the outer limit of tragic convention, where it might belong to a pro-satyric mode like Alcestis, while still insisting that its incidents remain tragic rather than comic. Greek tragedy commonly assumes the audience knows the main outlines of heroic saga. The notes discuss its conventions of chorus, messenger speech, divine appearances, stage doubling, and the stylized treatment of battle and night action.
