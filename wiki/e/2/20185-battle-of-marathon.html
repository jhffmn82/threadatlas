Abstract written by a model

In Four Plays of Aeschylus, Marathon was the place where those who defeated Darius had previously defeated the Persians. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Battle of Marathon was where Datis had previously been defeated by the Athenians. In Description of Greece, the Battle of Marathon was fought at Marathon, where the Athenians defeated the Persians. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the Battle of Marathon yielded spoils shared with the Platæans and dedicated by the Athenians as firstfruits.

battle of Marathon in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where battle of Marathon first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Battle of Marathon was fought at Marathon, where the Athenians defeated the Persians, who lost ships while retreating. Its Persian spoil was used for Phidias's brazen statue of Athene. The Athenians buried their dead, the Platæans, and slaves there, honoured them as heroes, and reported Echetlæus fighting.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXVIII.

The Battle of Marathon is associated with the Persian spoil used for Phidias's brazen statue of Athene. The Athenians who fought there had tombs on the spot because of their valour, unlike the other Athenian war dead buried on the road to the Academy.

CHAPTER XXXI.

The battle was fought at Marathon, where the Athenians defeated the Persians. The Athenians buried their own dead and the dead of the Platæans and their slaves, honoured the fallen as heroes, and said that Echetlæus, a rustic figure who killed Persians with a ploughshare, appeared during the fighting.

Battle of Marathon in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Battle of Marathon first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Battle of Marathon yielded spoils shared with the Platæans and dedicated by the Athenians as firstfruits. Arimnestus commanded the Platæans; a tenth of the spoils funded Acropolis statues of Athene, Apollo, Miltiades, heroes, and other figures, made by Phidias.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IV.

The Battle of Marathon is associated with the spoil given by the Athenians to the Platæans and with the brazen statue that the Athenians dedicated in the Acropolis as its firstfruits. Arimnestus commanded the Platæans in this battle.

CHAPTER X.

The tenth of the spoils of Marathon was used for statues of Athene, Apollo, Miltiades, several heroes, and other figures associated with Athens. These statues were made by Phidias.
