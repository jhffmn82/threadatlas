Darius in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Darius is portrayed as Egypt’s sixth law-maker and a king of Asia who ruled the Persian kingdom for nineteen years. He rejected Cambyses’ impiety, learned theology and sacred records from priests, and was honored as a god. Called Xerxes’ father, he is later also called Xerxes’ eldest son and murdered by Artaxerxes after Artabanus’ false accusation.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

• And now it is necessary for us to speak of the legislators of Egypt^

Darius, the father of Xerxes, is called the sixth maker of laws for the government of Egypt. He rejected Cambyses' impiety, studied Egyptian theology and sacred records with the priests, imitated the ancient kings' kindness toward their subjects, and was honored by the Egyptians as a god during his lifetime.

CHAP. XIII.

Darius, the eldest son of Xerxes, was murdered by Artaxerxes after Artabanus falsely claimed that Darius had killed their father.

Darius in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

Darius is a deceased former Persian king, husband of Atossa and father of Xerxes, remembered as wise and restrained, ruling Persia without military harm. After Persia’s catastrophe, Atossa and the Chorus summon his royal phantom from the underworld through libations and cries. He recognizes disaster as mortal fate, laments the consequences of Xerxes’ failed invasion, foretells the coming slaughter of Plataea, instructs that Xerxes be received ceremoniously, and returns to the shades.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

INTRODUCTION

Darius appears as a royal phantom summoned by Atossa's libations and the Chorus's cries. He recognizes that disaster is the fate of mortal power, foretells the slaughter of Plataea, advises that Xerxes be received ceremoniously, and then returns to the shades.

THE PERSIANS

Darius was the former Persian king, the husband of Atossa and father of Xerxes. The invasion is undertaken to avenge his defeat at Marathon, and Atossa’s dream shows him lamenting over Xerxes after the king falls from the chariot. The Chorus urges Atossa to invoke Darius among the dead for help.

Enter a MESSENGER.

Darius is remembered as a wise and restrained ruler who did not waste Persia with reckless war. Atossa and the CHORUS perform rites and call upon the gods of the dead to bring him back so that he may advise them about the disaster.

Ghost of Darius in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

Ghost of Darius is a dead former Persian king, royal ancestor, father of Xerxes, sole ruler, and army leader. In The Persians, Atossa’s grief and the Chorus’s prayers and libations summon him after Xerxes’ defeat. Learning the army is destroyed, he condemns Xerxes’ recklessness, pride, and impiety, and warns Persia never again to attack Greek lands.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE PERSIANS

The Ghost of Darius is listed among the dramatis personae, and Atossa’s dream shows Darius appearing in grief over Xerxes’ fall. The Chorus intends to seek his aid through prayers and libations.

The GHOST OF DARIUS rises.

Darius rises from the dead in response to the mourning Persian elders and Atossa’s libation. He learns that Xerxes’ army has been destroyed and warns that Persia must never again attack the Greek lands. He attributes the disaster to Xerxes’ youthful recklessness, his attempt to control the Helle, and the gods’ punishment for Persian impiety and pride.
