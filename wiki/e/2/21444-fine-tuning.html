fine-tuning in A Survey of Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Customized Large Language Models

Qinggang Zhang, Shengyuan Chen, Yuanchen Bei, Zheng Yuan, Huachi Zhou, Zijin Hong, Hao Chen, Yilin Xiao, Chuang Zhou, Junnan Dong, Yi Chang, Xiao Huang · 2025-09-29

Fine-tuning is a strategy and knowledge integration method for adapting open-source large language models to specialized or private domains using specialized datasets while fixing most pre-trained parameters and adding limited parameters. It directly injects graph-retrieved information through LoRA-based and other data-efficient strategies, training with node-, path-, or subgraph-level knowledge and supporting GraphRAG alignment. It permanently integrates domain-specific knowledge, reducing reliance on extensive text retrieval and potentially improving speed and costs. However, distribution gaps and conflicting updates can harm existing understanding through catastrophic forgetting, and fine-tuning may require hundreds of gigabytes of memory.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

I. INTRODUCTION

Fine-tuning adapts large language models to specific or private domains by training them on specialized datasets while fixing most pre-trained parameters. Distribution gaps between specialized data and pre-training data can make it difficult to add new knowledge without harming existing understanding.

VI. KNOWLEDGE INTEGRATION .. C. Integration Enhancement Strategies.

Fine-tuning directly injects graph-retrieved information into open-source large language models, including through LoRA-based and other data-efficient strategies. It can train models with node-level, path-level, or subgraph-level knowledge.

C. Discussion on GraphRAG

Fine-tuning is presented as a way to permanently integrate domain-specific knowledge into large language models. It can reduce reliance on extensive external text retrieval, potentially improving response speed and reducing time and financial costs, although large language model fine-tuning may require hundreds of gigabytes of memory.
