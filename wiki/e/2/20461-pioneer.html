It employed editors, printers, and compositors including James Mills, Louis E. Fisher, George W. Armstrong, Marshall Robinson, and editor-in-chief Earle S. Goodrich. The Pioneer moved from quarters near Third and Cedar streets to the corner of Third and Robert on July 4, 1863, and therefore could not issue an extra about the Union victories. In 1858, it accidentally learned of Minnesota's admission into the Union and published the news before the Minnesotian and Times.

PRINTERS AND EDITORS OF TERRITORIAL DAYS.

The Pioneer was the first paper printed in St. Paul. Capt. E.Y. Shelly worked there for James M. Goodhue, and later the Pioneer consolidated with the Daily Democrat.

TERRITORIAL PRINTERS IN CIVIL LIFE.

The Pioneer employed James Mills in its editorial department, Louis E. Fisher as an editor, and George W. Armstrong as a printer. Marshall Robinson also worked as a compositor there, and Earle S. Goodrich was its editor-in-chief.
