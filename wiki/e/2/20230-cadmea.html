Abstract written by a model

In Four Plays of Aeschylus, the Cadmea is Cadmus’s citadel and the setting of The Seven against Thebes, defended by Eteocles and six others against Polynices’ forces. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Cadmea is Thebes’ citadel, repeatedly contested, seized and garrisoned by the Lacedaemonians and later held by a Macedonian garrison. In The Library, Cadmeia is ravaged by the Teumessian vixen, which Amphitryon must rid it of before Creon will assist the expedition against the Teleboans. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Cadmea is the citadel of lower Thebes, built by Cadmus and joined to the lower town by Amphion and Zethus.

Cadmea in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Cadmea first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cadmea is the citadel of Thebes. It is repeatedly contested as a decisive fortified place: Phoebidas and the Lacedaemonians seize and garrison it after defeating resistance and banishing prominent citizens; Thebans, aided by Athens and other Boeotians, besiege it until surrender. Its recovery by exiles is credited with freeing Thebes and enabling later Theban successes. Later a Macedonian garrison strengthens and holds it, sallies during Thebes’ fall, and helps defeat retreating Thebans.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. n.

The Cadmea, the citadel of Thebes, was seized by Phoebidas through a surprise attack. The Thebans resisted but were defeated, three hundred prominent citizens were banished, and a strong Lacedaemonian garrison was left behind.

CHAR III.

The Lacedaemonians unlawfully detained the Cadmea and held it with a garrison of at least fifteen hundred men. The Thebans, aided by Athens and other Boeotians, besieged it until the garrison surrendered under articles.

CHAP. IX,

Pelopidas was generally credited with freeing Thebes after the exiles recovered the citadel of Cadmea. The recovery of Cadmea was described as the chief cause of the later advantages and successes of the Thebans.

Alexander having quieted all the commotions in Greece, mardied

The Cadmea was held by a Macedonian garrison, whose governor strengthened its walls and supplied it with weapons. During the fall of Thebes, the garrison sallied against the retreating Thebans and helped defeat them.

BOOK XIV.

The Lacedaemonians seized the Cadmea at Thebes. The Cadmea was later retaken by the Thebans.
