Stanton, observes through a telescope, returns alone to ask why the moon appears inverted, visits Asaph Hall, and signs Hall's Navy commission.

Abraham Lincoln in Novel-10146

Pioneer Press · about 1862 · the work’s page · read it where Abraham Lincoln first appears

Abstract written by a model

Abraham Lincoln was President of the United States during the Civil War, described as the greatest of all presidents, a martyred president, and an ideal statesman. In 1862 he issued a 600,000-volunteer enlistment, responded to the Minnesota draft, and limited condemned Indians’ executions to thirty-eight cases. He spoke of firmness for the right, led the country through more than four years of war, and was assassinated by John Wilkes Booth as Union victory appeared near.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

If James M.

Abraham Lincoln issued a response concerning the Minnesota draft: "If the draft cannot take place, of course it cannot take place. Necessity knows no law. A. Lincoln." The statement was telegraphed after the provost marshal was not ready to conduct the draft on the designated day.

As soon as Gen.

After consulting military advisers and receiving remonstrances, the President of the United States authorized the execution of only thirty-eight of the condemned Indians. He directed that they be hanged on December 26, 1862.

THE DEATH OF PRESIDENT LINCOLN.

Abraham Lincoln is described as the martyred president and as an ideal statesman to Frederick A. Noble. He had patiently led the country through more than four years of war and was assassinated just as Union victory appeared near.

Abraham Lincoln in An Astronomer's Wife

Angelo Hall · 1908 · the work’s page · read it where Abraham Lincoln first appears

Abstract written by a model

Abraham Lincoln, a President of the United States and War President, attends a reception, visits the U.S. Naval Observatory with Edwin M. Stanton, observes through a telescope, returns alone to ask why the moon appears inverted, visits Asaph Hall, and signs Hall's Navy commission.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER XIII.

Abraham Lincoln attended a reception at which Angeline S. Hall took her little boy and later visited the Naval Observatory with Secretary Stanton. Hall showed him objects through the telescope, and Lincoln subsequently came alone through the dark streets to ask why the moon appeared inverted in the telescope.
