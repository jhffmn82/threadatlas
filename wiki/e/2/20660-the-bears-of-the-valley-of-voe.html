the bears of the Valley of Voe in Dorothy and the Wizard in Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1908

The bears of the Valley of Voe are large, fierce, and invisible from eating dama-fruit. They hunt and eat people of Voe when they catch them, though they usually cannot find them. They avoid water, attack travelers on land and slash Jim, but are driven away when the travelers reach the river; the Wizard kills one.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

8. The Valley of Voices

The bears of the Valley of Voe are large, fierce, and invisible because they eat the dama-fruit. They hunt and eat the people of Voe when they can catch them, although the bears usually cannot find them.

9. They Fight the Invisible Bears

The bears eat dama-fruit and are therefore invisible, but they cannot venture onto the water. They attack the travelers on land, slash Jim, and are driven away when the travelers escape onto the river; the Wizard kills one with his sword.
