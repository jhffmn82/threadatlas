Abstract written by a model

In The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies, blood guilt is the enduring pollution caused by murder, bringing punishment by the Furies and remaining contested after Orestes’s claimed purification. In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, blood pollution prevents Orestes from washing the blood from his hands and excludes him from every house, while earlier law required banishment as atonement for murder. In The Electra of Euripides, pollution from blood-guilt makes Orestes impure after his mother’s murder, while the Spirits pursue him and Athena is expected to protect him pending his trial in Athens. In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, blood-guiltiness defiles Thebes through Laius’s murder and must be purged by expelling or punishing the guilty person.

blood guilt in The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies

Aeschylus · 457 BC · the work’s page · read it where blood guilt first appears

Abstract written by a model

Blood guilt is the enduring pollution caused by murder. It cannot be cleansed or atoned for, leaves the dead angry, and brings increasing pain and punishment by the Furies in life and after death. It is associated with the murders and rule of Aegisthus and Clytemnestra; after final bloodshed, Orestes claims ritual purification has removed his guilt, but the Furies insist that a mother’s blood cannot be erased and must be paid for.

3 sections; each heading opens the text

[_The scene opens, disclosing Clytemnestra, who comes forward. The body

Pollution is associated with the murders and with the rule of Aegisthus and Clytemnestra. The Chorus accuses Aegisthus of feasting on pollution while his power lasts, and Clytemnestra asks that the avenger’s anger depart after the final bloodshed.

THE LIBATION-BEARERS

The Chorus declares that blood once shed cannot be cleansed or atoned for, even if every stream of Earth washed the stained hand. It says that the dead remain angry and that guilt eventually brings the sinner increasing pain.

_The scene changes to Athens. In the foreground, the Temple of Athena

Orestes claims that ritual purification, including cleansing at Apollo’s hearth and the killing of sacrificial beasts, has removed his blood guilt. The Furies maintain that a mother’s blood cannot be gathered up or erased and that the killer must pay for it.
