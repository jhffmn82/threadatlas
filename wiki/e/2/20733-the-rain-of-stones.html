the Rain of Stones in Dorothy and the Wizard in Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1908

The Rain of Stones is a damaging occurrence that strikes the Glass City and the Mangaboos' houses. It breaks roofs, steeples, and towers, cracks or chips buildings, and injures some Mangaboos. The man with the star blames Dorothy, Zeb, and their companions, while Dorothy says an earthquake caused it; Gwig describes a second fall as a Rain of People-and-Horse-and-Buggy with stones. The Glass City's damage heals only through natural regrowth over time.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

2. The Glass City

The Rain of Stones damages the Glass City by breaking roofs and steeples and cracking or chipping buildings. The man with the star accuses Dorothy, Zeb, and their companions of causing it.

3. The Arrival Of The Wizard

The Rain of Stones damages and cracks the Mangaboos' houses and injures some of their people. Gwig claims that a second fall was actually a Rain of People-and-Horse-and-Buggy, with some stones accompanying it. Dorothy states that the damage was caused by the earthquake rather than by her and Zeb.

4. The Vegetable Kingdom

The Rain of Stones breaks the towers and cracks the roofs of the glass city. The damaged glass eventually grows together again, but the Mangaboos must wait for this repair.
