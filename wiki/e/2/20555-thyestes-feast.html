Thyestes’ feast

event · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Thyestean banquet, feast of Thyestes, festal cheer, set before him flesh that was his children once, that eating, the entrails upon which their father fed

Abstract written by a model

In The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies, Thyestes’ feast is presented as a crime of inherited horror and kinship violence and is later invoked as an example the Chorus recognizes but cannot fully interpret. In The Library, the Thyestean banquet is described as a cannibalistic meal in which Atreus cooks and serves Thyestes’ sons to him, then reveals their extremities after Thyestes has eaten.

Thyestes’ feast in The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies

Aeschylus · 457 BC · the work’s page · read it where Thyestes’ feast first appears

Abstract written by a model

Thyestes’ feast is a crime of inherited horror and kinship violence: Atreus welcomes Thyestes, serves him the flesh of his own children, and Thyestes eats unknowingly before discovering the deed. It is later invoked as an example the Chorus recognizes but cannot fully interpret.
