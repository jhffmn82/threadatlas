Thyestes

person · 2 works · 8 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Thyestes’ loathly woe, my father, my sad father, Θυέστης, Θυέστου, Θυέστῃ, τὸν Θυέστην

Abstract written by a model

In The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies, Thyestes is Atreus’s rival brother and Aegisthus’s exiled father who unknowingly eats his children and curses Pleisthenes’s race. In The Library, Thyestes rules Mycenae, is banished after Atreus’s revenge, and is restored through Aegisthus’s killing of Atreus before being exiled by Agamemnon and Menelaus.

Thyestes in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Thyestes first appears

Abstract written by a model

Thyestes, a Pelopid and father of Aglaus, Callileon, and Orchomenus, initially rules Mycenae after using Aerope’s golden lamb, but the sun’s reversal confirms Atreus’s claim. Banished after swearing at Hera’s altar, he is fed his sons’ cooked bodies by Atreus. An oracle directs him to father Aegisthus with his daughter for revenge; Aegisthus kills Atreus, restoring Thyestes until Agamemnon and Menelaus exile him to Cytheria.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ιῖιο Ὅτι viol Πέλοπος Πιτθεὺς ᾿Ατρεὺς Θυέστης

Thyestes uses the golden lamb, which Aerope gives him, to claim the kingdom of Mycenae and becomes king until the sun’s reversal establishes Atreus’s claim. Atreus banishes him and later serves him the cooked bodies of his sons; Thyestes then receives a prophecy that a child born from his union with his daughter will avenge him.

Tz 15 «Τὸν δ᾽ ᾿Αγαμέμνονα ὃ τροφὸς μετὰ τοῦ Μενελάου

Thyestes learned from an oracle that he could obtain revenge by fathering a son with his own daughter, and Aegisthus was born from that union. After Aegisthus killed Atreus, Thyestes regained the kingdom, but Agamemnon and Menelaus later forced him to swear to dwell in Cytheria.
