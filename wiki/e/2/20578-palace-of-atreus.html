Abstract written by a model

In The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies, the Palace of Atreus is the palace at Mycenae and royal house where the household’s suffering unfolds and Agamemnon and Cassandra are killed. In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Pelopidae palace is the haunted home of the Atridae where Clytemnestra and Aegisthus rule and Orestes’s revenge culminates in their deaths. In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Agamemnon's palace is the setting at Argos, the bloodstained Atreid residence where Orestes’s crisis and the final standoff occur. In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the palace of Orestes is the guarded house where Orestes and Electra are confined, Helen stays, and Orestes and Pylades attack Helen and seize Hermione.

Palace of Atreus in The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies

Aeschylus · 457 BC · the work’s page · read it where Palace of Atreus first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Palace of Atreus is a palace at Mycenae and the royal house of the Atreid household. A Watchman observes stars and signal fires from its roof, while its household bears concealed suffering and past consequences. Agamemnon and Cassandra are killed inside it. Later, Aegisthus and Clytemnestra occupy Agamemnon’s former seat, and the excluded Orestes plans to enter disguised and kill them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

AGAMEMNON

The Palace of Atreus is the setting of the unit and the place from whose roof the Watchman observes the stars and signal fires. Its household is presented as burdened by concealed suffering and the consequences of past acts.

[_Exeunt all but Cassandra and the Chorus._

The palace is presented as the place where Agamemnon and Cassandra will be killed. Cassandra smells blood and charnel corruption within it, and Agamemnon’s fatal cries come from inside after she enters.

ORESTES (_approaching suddenly_)

The palace is the occupied royal house from which Orestes and Electra have been excluded. Orestes plans to enter it in disguise, find Aegisthus and Clytemnestra, and kill them.

Pelopidae palace in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · 449 to 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Pelopidae palace first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pelopidae palace is a haunted, troubled palace and home of the Atridae, ruled by Clytemnestra with Aegisthus after Agamemnon’s murder. Crime and misfortune continually increase there while Electra remains controlled. Orestes and Pylades enter with a deceptive revenge plan: Clytemnestra is killed inside, and Aegisthus is sent in to meet death.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

ELECTRA

The Pelopidae palace is the troubled house where Agamemnon was murdered and where Electra remains under the control of Clytemnestra and Aegisthus. Orestes and his companions prepare a deception and revenge against those ruling it.

CHORUS.

The house has continually accumulated crime and misfortune.

_Enter_ CLYTEMNESTRA.

Clytemnestra prays to remain secure while ruling the Atridae's home and kingdom with the people she now lives with. She later enters the palace with the Old Man, leaving Electra outside.

_Enter_ ORESTES _and_ PYLADES, _with the urn_.

The palace is the setting into which Orestes and Pylades enter to carry out their plan. Clytemnestra is killed inside it, and Aegisthus is ordered to enter it to meet his death.

Agamemnon's palace in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Agamemnon's palace first appears

Abstract written by a model

Agamemnon's palace at Argos, belonging to the bloodstained Atreidae, is the play's setting. It begins with sick Orestes watched by Electra, while Helen remains inside and Hermione leaves for Clytæmnestra's tomb. Marked by Agamemnon's and Clytemnestra's murders, it ends amid Helen's disappearance, fighting, a battlement standoff, and Orestes' threat to burn it.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

ORESTES.

The action begins before the palace of Agamemnon, where Orestes lies sick on a couch and Electra watches over him. Helen is inside the palace, and Hermione is sent from it to carry offerings to Clytæmnestra's tomb.

MENELAUS, ORESTES, CHORUS.

Agamemnon's palace is described as a house surrounded by lamentable woes. It was the place where Clytæmnestra killed Agamemnon and where Ægisthus became her secret husband.

PHRYGIAN, CHORUS.

The palace becomes the scene of Clytemnestra's murder, Helen's disappearance, the fighting between the Greek attackers and Phrygian attendants, and Orestes' threat to burn the building. Orestes and his companions occupy its battlements while Menelaus confronts them outside.

palace of Orestes in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where palace of Orestes first appears

Abstract written by a model

Orestes' palace is a guarded house where Orestes and Electra are confined and Helen stays. As an Argive decree nears, Pylades urges Orestes to leave. Orestes and Pylades then enter to attack Helen, bolt out attendants, threaten to burn the palace, hear Helen's cries, and seize Hermione.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

PYLADES, ORESTES, CHORUS.

The palace is where Orestes and Electra are confined under guard and where Helen is staying. Pylades urges Orestes to leave it before the Argive decree is carried out.

ELECTRA, ORESTES, PYLADES, CHORUS.

Helen is inside Orestes' house when Orestes and Pylades enter to attack her. The conspirators plan to bolt her attendants out of different parts of the house and threaten to burn the palace if the murder fails. The house becomes the scene of Helen's cries and the seizure of Hermione.
