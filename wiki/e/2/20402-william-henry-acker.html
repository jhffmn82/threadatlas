Abstract written by a model

William Henry Acker, born in New York State in 1833, came to St. Paul in 1854 and became a respected military leader. He was captain of the Pioneer Guards, commanded the Wide-Awakes in 1860, and, at the war’s outbreak, served as Minnesota’s adjutant general and organized a company for the First Minnesota Regiment. Wounded at Bull Run, he became captain in the Seventeenth United States Regulars. He was killed leading his company at Pittsburg Landing; his funeral was at St. Paul’s Church, and Acker Post was named for him.

William Henry Acker was a captain who was killed at Pittsburg Landing while leading his company and urging his men forward. He had served as adjutant general of Minnesota, organized a company for the First Minnesota regiment, fought at the first battle of Bull Run, and was wounded there. Born in New York State in 1833, he came to St. Paul in 1854, where he became a military leader and was widely respected; after his death, St. Paul's first Grand Army post was named Acker Post in his honor.
