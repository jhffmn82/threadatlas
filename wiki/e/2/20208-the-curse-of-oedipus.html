the curse of Œdipus in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

The curse of Œdipus is directed against Eteocles and Polynices after Œdipus discovers the incestuous marriage, blinds himself, and curses them to share the house with a sharpened sword. It hangs over their conflict, is feared as divinely fulfilled, and culminates in their mutual killing, causing both brothers’ deaths.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

THE PHŒNICIAN VIRGINS.

After discovering the incestuous marriage, Oedipus blinds himself and remains confined in the palace. He curses Eteocles and Polynices, wishing that they share the house with a sharpened sword, and the brothers fear that the gods will fulfill the curse.

POLYNICES, CHORUS.

The curse of Œdipus hangs over the brothers' conflict and is one reason Polynices says he originally avoided enmity and bloodshed. Jocasta asks whether the brothers will escape their father's curses, but Eteocles declares that the whole house may perish.

MESSENGER, JOCASTA, CHORUS.

The Chorus says that the deaths of Eteocles and Polynices fulfill the curse of Oedipus. The brothers' mutual killing is presented as the result of that curse.

Oedipus's curse in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Oedipus's curse begins against Laius's unknown killer but falls on Oedipus when his identity is revealed, prompting his request for exile. He later renews it against Polynices and Eteocles, invoking Zeus, Justice, and underworld powers: Polynices will not return to Argos, and the brothers will kill each other.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS.

Oedipus's curse is directed against the unknown killer of Laius, but it comes to rest on Oedipus himself once his identity is revealed. He fears the shock of his own curse upon his house and asks to be removed from the land.

CHORUS.

Oedipus renews his curse against Polynices and Eteocles, calling on Zeus, Justice, and the powers of the underworld. He declares that Polynices will fail to return to Argos, will die by a fratricidal hand, and will kill the brother who banished him.

the curse of Oedipus in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

The curse of Oedipus is a paternal imprecation and prophetic force over his sons Eteocles and Polynices. It declares that they will divide their kingdom or inheritance by iron and the sword rather than gold, governing their fate and driving them into mortal conflict. It remains effective until its fulfillment: the brothers fight, kill each other, and their mutual deaths realize the ancient family doom.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

THE SEVEN AGAINST THEBES

Oedipus’ curse falls upon his sons Eteocles and Polynices, who are said to have been destined to divide the kingdom by the sword. The curse is fulfilled when the two brothers fight and kill one another.

Enter on one side THE SPY; on the other ETEOCLES and the SIX

The curse of Oedipus declares that his children will divide their inheritance with iron rather than gold. It drives the brothers toward conflict and is described as a prophetic force seeking fulfillment in their bloodshed.

Enter THE SPY.

The curse of Oedipus is said to have remained effective and to have brought about the brothers’ mutual deaths. The Chorus presents the deaths as the fulfillment of the father’s imprecation and the ancient doom of the family.
