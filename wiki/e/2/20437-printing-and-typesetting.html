Abstract written by a model

Printing and typesetting was Capt. E.Y. Shelly’s profession before and after military service. Historically overlapping with theater, it joined technical and editorial work with politics, publishing, and civic service. Territorial offices and newspapers relied on mobile printers and notable compositors, but the iron printer diminished composing rooms’ former sociability and informal intellectual exchange.

PRINTERS AND EDITORS OF TERRITORIAL DAYS.

The unit describes printing offices, newspaper work, and the movement of printers among territorial papers. Charles R. Stuart is described as an exceptional compositor who reportedly set 26,000 ems of solid brevier in twenty-four hours and commonly set 2,000 ems of solid bourgeois per hour.

TERRITORIAL PRINTERS IN CIVIL LIFE.

The printing business included both technical and editorial work and was closely connected with politics, publishing, theater, and civic service. The introduction of the iron printer reduced the sociability and informal intellectual exchanges formerly associated with composing rooms.
