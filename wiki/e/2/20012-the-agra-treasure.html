Abstract written by a model

The Agra treasure is a valuable collection of Indian gems, estimated at nearly or at least half a million pounds. It includes diamonds, emeralds, rubies, carbuncles, sapphires, agates, beryls, onyxes, cats’-eyes, turquoises, pearls, the Great Mogul diamond, and a gold coronet set with twelve pearls. Four conspirators originally hide it beneath bricks in a preserved wall of the Agra fort. Major John Sholto and Captain Arthur Morstan acquire it in India, but Major Sholto betrays the agreement, takes it, and brings it to England, where he possesses it for years. At Pondicherry Lodge, Bartholomew Sholto discovers the treasure in a sealed garret or secret room. Soon after it is lowered through an opening in the ceiling, it vanishes from Bartholomew’s chamber. Its disappearance, followed by Bartholomew’s murder, leads Athelney Jones to suspect Thaddeus Sholto, while Sherlock Holmes attributes it to intruders and believes Jonathan Small came to regain what he considered his rights. The fugitives are thought to have carried away the box, and recovery matters especially because Miss Morstan is entitled to half, with the other half to go to Thaddeus Sholto. The recovered box proves empty. Jonathan Small says the treasure has brought murder, fear, guilt, and slavery, calls it cursed, and states that he placed it beyond recovery, possibly scattering it over several miles in the Thames. Thus the treasure ends lost despite its recovery being sought for six years.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

10 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter IV

The Agra treasure was a considerable treasure that Captain Arthur Morstan and Major John Sholto acquired in India and that Major Sholto brought to England. Bartholomew Sholto discovered it hidden in a sealed garret at Pondicherry Lodge, and its jewels are estimated to be worth at least half a million pounds.

Chapter V

The treasure that Bartholomew Sholto and Thaddeus had lowered through an opening in the ceiling is missing when the chamber is entered. Thaddeus declares that the treasure has been stolen.

Chapter VI

The treasure was found in the secret room and is later missing. Athelney Jones treats its disappearance as evidence against Thaddeus Sholto, while Holmes connects it to the intruders.

Chapter VII

The Agra treasure is rediscovered and immediately followed by the murder of Bartholomew Sholto. Sherlock Holmes believes Jonathan Small came to England to regain what he regarded as his rights and that the treasure-box was lowered from the garret and carried away.

Chapter VIII

The newspaper states that a valuable collection of Indian gems inherited by Bartholomew Sholto was carried off. Watson regards recovering the treasure as especially important because it belongs rightfully, at least in part, to Miss Morstan.

Chapter IX

The treasure is believed to be in the possession of the fugitives. Holmes expects it to be recovered when the men are captured and proposes that Dr. Watson take the box first to Miss Morstan, who is entitled to half of it.

Chapter XI

The Agra treasure is believed by Jonathan Small to be worth nearly half a million pounds and is to be divided between Mary Morstan and Thaddeus Sholto. The treasure is ultimately found to be missing from the box, and Small says it has brought murder, fear, guilt, and slavery to those connected with it.

Chapter XII

The Agra treasure is not found in the recovered box. Jonathan Small says he put it in a safe place, possibly scattering it over several miles, so that the authorities would have difficulty recovering it.
