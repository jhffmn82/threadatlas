Little Crow in Novel-10146

Pioneer Press · 1862~

Little Crow was born at Kaposia and influenced the 1858 sale of part of the Minnesota River reservation strip, drawing Sioux criticism. He became the acknowledged head of the war party and was described as chief instigator of the insurrection. After refusing surrender at Camp Release, he escaped, was killed by Lamson near Hutchinson, and his scalp was preserved by the Minnesota Historical Society.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE GREAT SIOUX OUTBREAK IN 1862.

Little Crow influenced the 1858 sale of the northern portion of the Minnesota River reservation strip. The sale caused dissatisfaction among the Sioux, and Little Crow was criticized for his role.

As soon as Gen.

Little Crow, described as the chief instigator of the insurrection, did not surrender at Camp Release. He escaped and was later killed by a farmer named Lamson near Hutchinson; his scalp was held by the state historical society.
