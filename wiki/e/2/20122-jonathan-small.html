  • In The Sign of the Four: Jonathan Small is a middle-aged, sunburned, bearded former convict with a wooden right leg, a scarred hand, black curly hair shot with grey, and a reckless, heavily lined appearance. Poorly educated but cunning, he is one of four associates connected by the Agra treasure: Mahomet Singh, Abdullah Khan, and Dost Akbar. He participated with them in Achmet’s murder, received a life sentence of penal servitude after a death sentence was commuted, and was transferred via Madras to the Andaman convict-barracks. There he served alongside the other three men, behaved well enough to gain privileges, learned some medicine while dispensing drugs, and came to regard the treasure as earned through twenty years of suffering, though useless if he could not possess it. Seeking freedom and vengeance, Small made a bargain with Major Sholto and Captain Morstan: a fifth share of the treasure in exchange for the conspirators’ release. The treasure was instead stolen from him, giving him reason to pursue the Sholtos and maintain watch over Pondicherry Lodge. He escaped the Andamans with Tonga after killing a convict-guard, using passages he arranged toward America or the Colonies. Holmes identifies him as the wooden-legged man linked to Captain Morstan’s chart, the removal of the treasure-box, and the search for revenge. Small says Tonga, not he, killed Thaddeus Sholto with a poisoned dart. While preparing to flee with the treasure, Small has Mordecai Smith hide the Aurora at Jacobson’s Yard and directs the launch during the Thames pursuit. The launch runs onto the southern bank; his wooden leg leaves him stuck in mud, and he is captured. In custody, he claims nearly half a million pounds from the treasure, says he acted for all four imprisoned men, and agrees to tell his story. He has hidden or discarded the treasure so that neither authorities nor the Sholto and Morstan families can recover it. After explaining that he brought the rope and that Tonga had only one dart left, he is taken away under guard by Athelney Jones.

Chapter VI

Jonathan Small is identified as one of the two people in the room. Holmes describes him as a poorly educated, small, active, sunburned, middle-aged former convict with his right leg missing, a worn wooden stump, a distinctive left boot, and skin missing from his palm.

Chapter VII

Jonathan Small is identified by Sherlock Holmes as the middle-aged, bearded, sunburned, wooden-legged white man connected with Captain Morstan’s chart. Holmes believes he sought revenge on Major Sholto, watched the search for the treasure, helped remove the treasure-box, and left the scene with his associate.

Chapter VIII

Holmes associates Small with the Andaman Islands and reasons that this may explain his having an Andaman islander as a companion. Holmes suggests that Small might regret employing such a dangerous companion.

Chapter IX

Jonathan Small is identified by Holmes as one of the men being pursued. Holmes wants to obtain detailed information about the case directly from him after he is captured.

Chapter X

Jonathan Small has Mordecai Smith conceal the Aurora at Jacobson's Yard while he prepares to escape with the treasure. He pilots or directs the launch during the chase, runs it onto the southern bank, and becomes stuck in the mud by his wooden leg before being hauled aboard and captured.

Chapter XI

Jonathan Small is held captive after being captured during the pursuit on the Thames. He insists that he did not raise a hand against Thaddeus Sholto and says that Tonga killed Sholto with a poisoned dart; he describes the Agra treasure as having brought him slavery and misfortune.

Chapter XII

Jonathan Small has hidden or discarded the Agra treasure so that neither the authorities nor the Sholto and Morstan families can recover it. He insists that he acted for the three men imprisoned in the Andaman convict-barracks as well as himself, and he describes the twenty years of hardship he endured there as the price by which he earned the treasure. He agrees to tell Holmes and the others his story.
