Abstract written by a model

In The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies, Helen’s departure with Paris causes the Trojan War and Troy’s ruin as she leaves the bride-bed and sails away while a pursuing force follows. In Hecuba and other plays, Paris carries Helen from hospitable shores to Priam’s realm, pursuing a marriage led by Venus, and his act causes the war and Troy’s ruin. In Heroides and Amores, Paris returns by ship with Helen instead of returning for Oenone, whose grief and self-wounding fill Ida with lamentation. In Heroides and Amores, Paris proposes that Helen leave with him willingly or be carried away, arguing that other women were taken without their lands being attacked and that her removal need not lead to war. In The Library, Alexander ignores Oenone’s warning not to sail for Helen and abducts Helen from Sparta. In The Library, Helen’s abduction includes Theseus and Pirithous taking her from Sparta, the Dioscuri carrying her away with Aethra, and Alexander persuading her to leave Sparta and taking her property; her rape marks the war’s chronology. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Paris abducts Helen from her husband, Menelaüs, causing the Greeks to unite and undertake the expedition against Troy. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Paris, aided by Venus, persuades Helen to elope and carries her from Menelaus’s home in Sparta to Troy, leading to the Trojan War.
