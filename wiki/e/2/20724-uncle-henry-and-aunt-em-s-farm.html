  • In Dorothy and the Wizard in Oz, the farm in Kansas is where Uncle Henry and Aunt Em have returned while mourning Dorothy, whom they believe was killed by the earthquake.
  • In The Emerald City of Oz, Uncle Henry and Aunt Em's farm is their home and livelihood, but its lack of profitability and unpaid mortgage put it at risk of being taken.

Uncle Henry and Aunt Em's farm in The Emerald City of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1910

Uncle Henry and Aunt Em's small Kansas farm is their home and livelihood. It produces only enough crops for food and was mortgaged to rebuild after a cyclone. Because it is insufficiently profitable and Uncle Henry cannot repay the loan, the farm is at risk of being taken, which would leave the family without a home.

2. How Uncle Henry Got Into Trouble

The farm is Uncle Henry and Aunt Em's home and means of earning a living. It produces only enough crops to buy food, and it has been mortgaged because Uncle Henry needed money to rebuild after a cyclone. The family must leave it if the mortgage is not paid.

3. How Ozma Granted Dorothy's Request

Uncle Henry and Aunt Em's farm in Kansas is insufficiently profitable, and Uncle Henry has pledged it as repayment for a loan. Since he cannot repay the money, the farm is going to be taken, leaving them without a place to live.
