Abstract written by a model

In Four Plays of Aeschylus, it is described as Prometheus’s secret aid to oppressed mortals, giving them fire and teaching them arts, science, letters, and handicrafts. In The Library, it is described as Prometheus’s theft of fire for human beings, for which Zeus has him bound on Mount Caucasus.

Aeschylus · 471 BC · the work’s page · read it where the gift of fire and civilization to humanity first appears

Abstract written by a model

The gift of fire and civilization to humanity is Prometheus’s secret aid to oppressed mortals, who had lived without arts, science, letters, or handicrafts. By carrying fire to them, he becomes their teacher and chief resource, prompting Zeus to imprison and torment him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

INTRODUCTION

The gift of fire and civilization to humanity is associated with Prometheus's aid to the human race. Before his intervention, humans live like ants or in caves without art, science, letters, or handicrafts; his benevolence leads Zeus to punish him.

PROMETHEUS BOUND

Prometheus’s theft and gift of fire is the act for which Zeus orders his imprisonment and torment. Prometheus says that he carried fire secretly to mortal men and thereby became their teacher and chief resource.
