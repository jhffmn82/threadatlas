LI in LI-RAGE: Late Interaction Retrieval Augmented Generation with Explicit Signals for Open-Domain Table Question Answering

Weizhe Lin · 2023

LI is a table-retrieval model used as the retrieval component in contrastive experiments. It is fine-tuned on E2E-WTQ before joint retriever-reader training, uses contrastive learning, and learns richer table representations, especially for the long and diverse E2E-WTQ tables. Training uses four negative samples per positive, Adam with learning rate 0.0001, batch size 6 per device, four gradient-accumulation steps, and eight GPUs; it runs 6,000 steps on NQ-TABLES and 600 on E2E-WTQ. LI produces table embeddings of lt × 128 and question embeddings of 32 × 128, padding shorter questions. It achieves the highest retrieval recall on both datasets, including 87.90% on NQ-TABLES with five retrieved tables, and outperforms BM25, DPR, RAG, and DPR-RAGE. Training is 1.75 iterations per second, with reported times of 60 minutes on NQ and 10 on WTQ; index building takes around five minutes.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Experimental Setup

LI is used as a table retrieval component in contrastive experiments. The text describes LI-RAGE as using ColBERT as its retriever, and reports that this configuration outperforms the DPR-based configuration on the main end-to-end results.

Results and Discussions / Main Results .. Remarks of Design Rationale

LI achieves the highest retrieval recall in both datasets, outperforming BM25 and DPR. Its models provide richer table representations, particularly for the long and diverse tables in E2E-WTQ.

Dataset Statistics

LI training uses four negative samples per positive sample, a learning rate of 0.0001, the Adam optimizer, a batch size of 6 per device, four gradient accumulation steps, and eight GPUs. Training runs for 6,000 steps on NQ-TABLES and 600 steps on E2E-WTQ.

CLTR and T-RAG Evaluation

LI has a reported training speed of 1.75 iterations per second, training batch size 6, and training times of 60 minutes on NQ and 10 minutes on WTQ.

Technical Details / Hyperparameters

LI extracts table embeddings with dimension lt × 128, where lt depends on the length of the input tables. It extracts question embeddings with fixed dimension 32 × 128 and pads questions with fewer than 32 tokens.

Computational Cost

LI does not significantly increase training time compared with DPR because both use contrastive learning. Its index building takes around five minutes.
