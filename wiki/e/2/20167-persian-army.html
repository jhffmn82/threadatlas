Persian army in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Persian army, commanded by Tithraustes and described as immensely numerous, crossed the Hellespont, marched through Thrace, and gained reinforcements. Despite cavalry, a fortified camp, and overwhelming numbers, it suffered repeated defeats at Thermopylae; after Mardonius was killed, it fled and endured immense losses.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

LAST TEN BOOKS / BOOK XI.

The Persian army crossed the Hellespont, marched through Thrace, and was reinforced by Thracians and other borderers. At Thermopylae it suffered repeated defeats despite its overwhelming numbers.

CHAP. HI.

The Persian army was described as numbering five hundred thousand men and was supported by cavalry and a fortified timber camp. It initially fought with courage, but after Mardonius was killed it lost heart, fled, and suffered immense losses.

Persian armament in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

Persian armament, a Persian host marshalled for war, includes fleet, cavalry, bowmen, and chariots as it crosses Asia toward Hellas; Chorus fears its loss and desolation in Persia.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE PERSIANS

The Persian armament crosses from Asia toward Hellas in a vast expedition of soldiers, horsemen, chariots, archers, and ships. The Chorus fears that its pride and flower may be lost and that Persia will receive news of desolation.

Persian army in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

The Persian army invades Hellas under Xerxes, crosses the bridge across the Hellespont, and suffers defeat at Salamis in a naval disaster. As it retreats through Greece and Thrace, hunger, thirst, pursuit, drought, and freezing reduce it to a small remnant. Xerxes leaves three hundred thousand men under Mardonius to fight by land; this remaining force is destroyed near Plataea by the Lacedaemonians, Athenians, and Tegeans.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

THE PERSIANS

The Persian army invades Hellas under Xerxes and later leaves three hundred thousand men under Mardonius to fight by land. That remaining army is destroyed near Plataea by the Lacedaemonians, Athenians, and Tegeans.

Enter a MESSENGER.

The Persian army is defeated, with countless men killed in the naval battle and many more dying during the retreat from Boeotia through Thrace. Hunger, thirst, pursuit, and the freezing of the Strymon reduce the returning force to a sorry few.

The GHOST OF DARIUS rises.

The Persian army suffers catastrophic losses in the sea disaster and in the fighting on land. Many contingents and commanders are dead, while Xerxes returns with no escort and only a small remnant is expected to survive. A rear-guard left in Boeotia is also foretold to suffer destruction.
