Pylades in Iphigenia in Tauris

Euripides · -0413/-0411

Pylades is a man, Prince of Phocis, son of King Strophios, Electra’s husband, and Orestes’ cousin, closest friend, devoted follower, and sworn companion. He accompanies Orestes from the mission to take Artemis’s image from Tauris through their attempted escape, sharing Orestes’ plan, madness, affliction, and guilt. Though Orestes is chief, Pylades is brave, unselfish, protective, and steadfast: he tends and shields Orestes during madness and attack, refuses to abandon him when they are bound, and is willing to die with him rather than return in shame. As bearer of Iphigenia’s tablet, he swears to carry her message to Argos, helps reveal Orestes’ identity, and urges Orestes to seize freedom.

PREFACE

Pylades accompanies Orestes on the mission to take Artemis’s image from Tauris. He is devoted, brave, and unselfish, and he serves as Orestes’ support even though Orestes is the chief.

Iphigenia in Tauris

LEADER.

Pylades urges Orestes to forget everything but Freedom and warns him not to lose their opportunity. He is identified as the son of King Strophios, Electra's husband, Orestes' cousin and closest friend, and a companion in the planned escape.

Pylades in The Electra of Euripides

Euripides · -0419/-0409

Pylades is Orestes’ loyal friend and battle ally. He accompanies Orestes’ secret return to Argos, brings armed servants, helps conceal his identity, assists in killing Aegisthus, and is honored as a partner in victory. Castor then commands him to marry Electra, leave Argos, and establish Orestes’ house on Phocis’ shore.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

ELECTRA

Pylades accompanies Orestes on his secret return to Argos and remains loyal to him during his exile. He brings armed servants and helps Orestes conceal his identity and approach Electra.

CHORUS.

Pylades accompanies Orestes in disguise and assists him during the sacrificial killing of Aegisthus. Electra crowns him as a partner in the victory, and he helps carry Aegisthus's body into the hut.

LEADER.

Pylades is appointed by Castor to marry Electra and lead her away from Argos. He is also to establish Orestes’ house on Phocis’ shore with golden wealth.

Pylades in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Pylades is the son of Strophius, from Phocian land, and Orestes’ steadfast friend, companion, and preserver. He voluntarily joins Orestes in hardship, is treated like a brother, and shares Orestes’ fate rather than abandon him. He helps Orestes carry out the vengeance and killing of Clytemnestra; because of this shared act, Strophius exiles him. Even then, he warns Orestes and Electra about the council against them, supports Orestes through sickness, madness, trial, capture, and danger, and insists he cannot live if Orestes dies. With Orestes and Electra, he plans the killing of Helen and participates in an attack on Helen, the palace, and the Phrygian attendants, while helping threaten Hermione. He is betrothed to Electra and later marries her, with Apollo approving the marriage. In another journey, he accompanies Orestes from Argos to Tauris, helps plan entry into Diana’s temple, and is captured with him. Chosen to carry Iphigenia’s letter to Argos, he swears the oath but refuses to survive by leaving Orestes behind. After learning that Orestes is Iphigenia’s brother, he urges them to focus on escape and is included in the ship’s plan. He is also engaged to Iphigenia through Orestes’ intended arrangement.

Narrative, section by section

9 sections; each heading opens the text

MENELAUS, ORESTES, CHORUS.

Pylades helped Orestes carry out the vengeance and death of Clytæmnestra. Orestes calls him a faithful friend in adversity, and he arrives from Phocis at the end of the unit.

PYLADES, ORESTES, CHORUS.

Pylades comes quickly to warn Orestes and Electra about the council assembled against them. Though Strophius has exiled him for sharing in Clytemnestra's murder, he remains devoted to Orestes, supports him through his illness, and conducts him from the assembly.

ELECTRA, ORESTES, PYLADES, CHORUS.

Pylades insists that he cannot live if Orestes dies, because he helped commit and plan the killing of Clytemnestra. He joins Orestes in planning Helen's murder and accepts Electra's plan involving Hermione. He enters the house with Orestes armed with a concealed sword.

PHRYGIAN, CHORUS.

Pylades assists Orestes in the attack on Clytemnestra and Helen and fights the Phrygian attendants. He helps Orestes threaten the palace and Hermione, and Apollo approves his marriage to Electra.

notes

Pylades is presented as Orestes’ friend who voluntarily attached himself to Orestes’ misfortunes. He is identified as the son of Strophius, who married Anaxibia, Agamemnon’s sister.

IPHIGENIA IN TAURIS.

Pylades accompanies Orestes from Argos and helps plan how they might hide and enter Diana's temple to seize the image. He tends Orestes during his madness and remains with him when the herdsmen capture them.

CHOR. Thou hast told wondrous things concerning him who has appeared,

Pylades accompanies Orestes from Greece to Tauris and is brought to the temple in chains. Iphigenia chooses him to carry her letter to Argos so that one of them may survive, and Orestes identifies him as his friend and fellow-sailor.

CHOR. I deplore thee now destined to the gory streams of the lustral

Pylades refuses to return alone to Argos while Orestes dies, fearing that people will accuse him of cowardice, desertion, or murdering Orestes for the sake of marrying his sister. He swears to carry Iphigenia's letter to her friends and later receives the message that Orestes is her brother.

IPH. O dearest one, no more, for thou art dearest. I hold thee, Orestes,

Pylades urges Iphigenia and Orestes to stop mourning and concentrate on securing their escape. He is included in the plan as another person polluted by the killing and is to accompany Orestes on the ship.
