Abstract written by a model

The Chorus of Persian Elders is a group of aged men of Susa, representing the Persian community and appointed as royal warders to guard Xerxes’ wealth and advise the household. It anxiously awaits news of the army, recounts its movement into Hellas, and interprets Atossa’s dream. After the messenger announces disaster, it laments Persia’s dead, widows, ruined cities, and broken kingship, summons Darius’ ghost, recalls his prosperous reign, mourns with Xerxes, and leads him back to the palace.

THE PERSIANS

The Chorus consists of Persian elders appointed to guard the wealth of Xerxes and advise the royal household. It anxiously awaits news of the army, describes the Persian forces and their passage into Hellas, and interprets Atossa’s dream as possibly requiring prayer and offerings. It explains that Athens has no single master and that its people fight with shields and spears.

Enter a MESSENGER.

The CHORUS responds with laments for the Persian dead, the widowed women, and the ruin of Susa, Ecbatana, and the Persian realm. It declares that the Persian yoke and kingship have been overthrown and calls for Darius to return from the realm of the dead.

The GHOST OF DARIUS rises.

The Chorus summons Darius, trembles before his ghost, and reports the disaster that has overtaken Persia. It recalls the former wealth and victories of Darius’ reign, then questions Xerxes about the dead and joins him in mourning. At the end, it agrees to lead the king back to the palace while lamenting the Persian losses.
