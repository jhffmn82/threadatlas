the house of Atreus in The Electra of Euripides

Euripides · -0419/-0409

The house of Atreus is a royal house linked to the golden lamb and ancient kingship. Its inherited calamity unfolds through recurring bloodshed among Agamemnon, Aegisthus, Clytemnestra, Electra, and Orestes, whose return restores the old line but leaves matricide threatened.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS.

The house of Atreus is associated with the golden lamb, the ancient kingship, and the cycle of crimes involving Agamemnon, Aegisthus, Electra, and Orestes. Orestes's return is celebrated as the restoration of the old king's line, though the house still faces the threatened matricide.

LEADER.

The House of Atreus is not named directly in this unit, but the continuing family bloodshed of Agamemnon, Clytemnestra, Orestes, and Electra is presented as an inherited calamity.

Argive family in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

The Argive family is the royal house of Argos/Mycenae, associated with Pelops, Atreus, Agamemnon, Menelaus, Iphigenia, and Orestes. It suffers murders, deaths, separation, and lost authority; Iphigenia mourns its destruction and believes Orestes lost.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

IPHIGENIA IN TAURIS.

The Argive royal house is associated with Pelops, Atreus, Agamemnon, Menelaus, and Iphigenia. Iphigenia and the Chorus mourn its suffering, murders, and loss of authority.

CHOR. Thou hast told wondrous things concerning him who has appeared,

Iphigenia mourns the destruction and separation of her Argive family. She learns that Agamemnon, Clytemnestra, and Electra are dead or believed dead, while she thinks Orestes is also lost.

the house of Atreus in The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies

Aeschylus · -0457

The house of Atreus is a cursed, bloodstained kingly stock and home, marked by ancestral crimes, kin-murder, inherited vengeance, grief, and threatened extinction. Cassandra foresees murder and retaliation continuing after Agamemnon’s death. Clytemnestra claims his death ends its frenzy, but the Chorus says the curse’s children remain. Orestes and Electra seek to regain its halls as its bloodshed demands repayment.

The house of Atreus is identified as a home cursed by the gods and stained by crimes among kin. Cassandra foretells that its cycle of murder and vengeance will continue through Agamemnon’s death and the retaliation that follows.
