Susa in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Susa is the royal city of Susiana, a king’s palace and treasure-holding place with a citadel. Governed by Abulites as lord-lieutenant, it was voluntarily surrendered to Alexander, who found vast uncoined and coined gold and silver, left Darius’s family there, and later stored Persian treasury there. At Susa he married Statira, arranged other marriages, received trained Persians, and faced the Macedonian mutiny. Eumenes then sought its treasury to support his army, received money there, and refreshed his forces. Its citadel, governed by Xenophilus, resisted Antigonus despite Seleucus’s appointment; finally Antigonus obtained Xenophilus’s submission, seized the castle’s valuables, and turned them into ready money.

CHAP. VU.

Susa, the royal city of Susiana, was voluntarily surrendered to Alexander by Abulites. Alexander found more than forty thousand talents of uncoined gold and silver and nine thousand talents in coined money there, left Darius's family at Susa, and later ordered part of the Persian treasury to be stored there.

At Susa, Alexander married Statira, the eldest daughter of Darius, and married Drypetis to Hephestion. He also arranged marriages between his leading friends and Persian noblewomen, received thirty thousand trained Persians, and later dealt with the Macedonian mutiny.

Eumenes intended to reach Susa to recruit forces from the higher provinces and use the king's treasures. After arriving there, he received money from the kings' treasury, paid the Macedonians in advance, and refreshed his army.

CHAP. n.

Susa contained the king's palace and a citadel governed by Xenophilus. Antigonus appointed Seleucus lord-lieutenant of the province and ordered him to besiege the citadel, but Xenophilus refused to obey Antigonus's commands. Eumenes remained a day's journey from Susa while defending the Tigris.

CHAP, m.

Antigonus traveled to Susa and took possession of its castle after receiving the submission of Xenophilus, who had been sent by Seleucus to guard the king’s treasure. He seized the valuables there and converted them into ready money.

Susa in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

Susa is a Persian city and seat of the royal palace, where the Chorus and royal household await news of Xerxes' army; as defeat becomes clear, it mourns the unprecedented loss of its warriors.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE PERSIANS

Susa is the Persian city from which the Chorus and royal household await news of Xerxes and his army. The Chorus imagines the city echoing with the lamentations of women if the expedition has been defeated.

The GHOST OF DARIUS rises.

Susa mourns the warriors who have been killed. Darius says that such extermination has never before fallen upon the city.
