the case in The Sign of the Four

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1890-02

The case begins with Captain Morstan’s unexplained disappearance, pearls sent annually to Mary, and a promise of justice to a wronged woman. It develops into the locked-room murder of Bartholomew Sholto, the theft of Indian gems, and an investigation of poison, intruders, and escape by the Aurora. Holmes follows physical traces and searches for the fugitives, while Jones suspects Thaddeus. Jonathan Small’s account concludes the case, and Holmes identifies Lal Rao as the confederate in the Norwood affair.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter II

The case concerns Captain Morstan’s unexplained disappearance, the annual delivery of valuable pearls to Mary, and an anonymous promise that a wronged woman will receive justice. Sherlock Holmes considers it a particularly strange mystery.

Chapter VI

The murder investigation focuses on the locked room, the route used by the intruders, the missing jewels, and the poison that killed Bartholomew Sholto. Holmes and Athelney Jones pursue competing explanations, with Holmes relying on physical traces and Jones accusing Thaddeus.

Chapter VIII

The case concerns Bartholomew Sholto’s murder, the theft of the Indian gems, and the escape of the suspected criminals by the Aurora. Holmes continues investigating independently while using the Irregulars to find the launch and studying the unusual evidence identifying the fugitives’ companions.
