The Sign of the Four in The Sign of the Four

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1890-02

The records recount Sherlock Holmes and Dr. John Watson’s investigation of Captain Morstan’s disappearance, the Agra treasure, and the murder at Pondicherry Lodge. Holmes begins in Baker Street, defending mental work and demonstrating deduction from Watson’s brother’s watch. Miss Mary Morstan then seeks help: her father vanished in 1878, anonymous pearls have arrived yearly since 1882, and a letter promises justice. Watson is immediately drawn to her, while Holmes warns against emotion in reasoning. A paper bearing a plan, a red cross, and “The sign of the four” leads Holmes, Watson, and Mary to Thaddeus Sholto. Thaddeus explains that Major John Sholto concealed Captain Morstan’s death and retained Morstan’s share of the Agra treasure. At Pondicherry Lodge they find Thaddeus’s brother, Bartholomew Sholto, murdered in a locked laboratory; the treasure is gone and another “sign of the four” is left behind. Holmes rejects Athelney Jones’s suspicion of Thaddeus. From a poisoned thorn, small bare footprints, creosote, and access through the roof and window, he identifies Jonathan Small and a small islander later named Tonga. With Toby the dog, Holmes and Watson trace the fugitives to the Thames. After delays, Holmes disguises himself, locates the Aurora, and joins Watson and Jones in a launch pursuit. Small is captured when the Aurora grounds; Tonga is shot into the river, and the treasure-chest is recovered. The chest proves empty. Small says he threw the treasure away rather than surrender it, and tells its history: he and Mahomet Singh, Abdullah Khan, and Dost Akbar killed Achmet during the Indian mutiny and hid his jewels. Imprisoned, they offered Major Sholto and Captain Morstan the treasure in return for help escaping, but Sholto took it and betrayed them. Small later escaped with Tonga, pursued the Sholtos, marked Major Sholto’s body, and stole the box after Tonga killed Bartholomew. Holmes identifies Lal Rao as the Norwood confederate. As Small is taken for trial and Jones receives credit for a capture, Watson declares his love to Mary; she accepts him, while Holmes returns to cocaine.

The Sign of the Four / Chapter I

Sherlock Holmes uses cocaine in Baker Street and explains his belief that mental work is necessary to prevent stagnation. Dr. John Watson challenges Holmes to analyze a watch, and Holmes correctly infers that it belonged to Watson’s elder brother, who was careless, sometimes poor, and died from drinking. Holmes demonstrates how observation and deduction lead from small physical details to broader conclusions. The visit of Miss Mary Morstan is announced at the end of the chapter.

Chapter II

Miss Mary Morstan asks Sherlock Holmes and Dr. John Watson to investigate her father Captain Morstan’s disappearance from London in 1878. She explains that an anonymous person has sent her a valuable pearl every year since 1882 and has now summoned her to the Lyceum Theatre, promising justice. Holmes identifies the anonymous letter and the pearl-box addresses as being written by the same person and agrees to accompany Mary with Watson. Watson becomes personally interested in Mary, while Holmes insists that emotional impressions must not influence his reasoning.

Chapter III

Sherlock Holmes connects Captain Morstan’s disappearance with Major Sholto’s death and the yearly presents sent to Miss Morstan, though he acknowledges unresolved difficulties. Miss Morstan brings a mysterious paper from her father’s pocket-book, bearing a building plan, a red cross, the note “3.37 from left,” and the names associated with the sign of the four. Holmes, Dr. Watson, and Miss Morstan travel by four-wheeler through foggy London to the Lyceum Theatre, where a coachman takes them to an unknown destination. They arrive at an unoccupied suburban house, where a Hindoo servant announces that the Sahib awaits them.

Chapter IV

Thaddeus Sholto brings Miss Morstan, Sherlock Holmes, and Dr. Watson to his richly furnished rooms and tells them about the Agra treasure and Captain Arthur Morstan’s death. He explains that his father, Major John Sholto, concealed Morstan’s death and kept Morstan’s share of the treasure, later confessing the matter to his sons before dying. A mysterious bearded intruder appeared at Major Sholto’s death, and a torn paper bearing “The sign of the four” was found afterward. Thaddeus says Bartholomew Sholto has discovered the treasure at Pondicherry Lodge, so they travel to Norwood to pursue Miss Morstan’s inheritance claim.

Chapter V

Watson, Sherlock Holmes, Miss Morstan, and Thaddeus Sholto arrive at Pondicherry Lodge and discover that Bartholomew Sholto has been murdered. Bartholomew is found dead in his locked chemical laboratory, with a strange grin, a poisoned thorn near his ear, and a note bearing “The sign of the four.” Thaddeus reports that the treasure is gone through an opening in the ceiling and fears that he will be suspected. Holmes advises Thaddeus to report the matter to the police while he, Watson, and Miss Morstan remain at the lodge.

Chapter VI

Sherlock Holmes examines the locked room where Bartholomew Sholto died and deduces that Jonathan Small entered through the window with a rope, while a mysterious ally entered through the roof trap-door. Holmes finds small naked footprints, traces of creosote, and evidence that Bartholomew was killed by a poisoned thorn that caused extreme muscular contraction. Athelney Jones arrests Thaddeus Sholto, but Holmes rejects Jones’s theory and identifies Jonathan Small from the physical evidence. Holmes sends Dr. Watson to bring back Toby so the scent of creosote can be tracked.

Chapter VII

Dr. John Watson escorts Miss Mary Morstan home, then retrieves Toby from Mr. Sherman and returns to Pondicherry Lodge. Sherlock Holmes examines the footprints, creasote scent, and a pouch of poison spines before using Toby to track the escaping perpetrators. Holmes concludes that the wooden-legged man is Jonathan Small and that an unnamed associate entered Bartholomew Sholto’s room, killed him with poison, and fled with the Agra treasure. Watson and Holmes follow Toby’s trail through the London suburbs toward the fugitives.

Sherlock Holmes and the narrator follow Toby through Nine Elms to Broderick and Nelson’s large timber-yard. Toby races through the yard and identifies a large barrel on a hand-trolley as the end of the trail. The barrel and trolley are smeared with a dark liquid, and the air smells strongly of creosote, causing Holmes and the narrator to laugh.

Chapter VIII

Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson recover Toby’s trail to the river, where they learn that Mordecai Smith’s steam launch, the Aurora, has been taken downstream by Smith and a wooden-legged man. Holmes questions Mrs. Smith and concludes that the fugitives have carefully covered their tracks, so he recruits Wiggins and the Baker Street Irregulars to search both banks of the river. A newspaper reports that Athelney Jones has arrested Thaddeus Sholto and several servants for Bartholomew Sholto’s murder, while Holmes privately investigates the stolen gems and the criminals’ escape. Holmes also identifies the second fugitive as probably an Andaman islander based on his small bare footprints, blow-pipe darts, and stone-headed weapon.

Chapter IX

Sherlock Holmes becomes frustrated when the search for Mordecai Smith’s missing steam launch produces no results, then disguises himself as an aged sailor to investigate further. After Holmes leaves, Dr. Watson learns from the newspaper that Thaddeus Sholto and Mrs. Bernstone have been released because Sholto’s alibi clears him of involvement in the Upper Norwood tragedy. Athelney Jones arrives seeking Holmes’s help, and Holmes returns in disguise with a plan to capture the fugitives and recover the treasure. Holmes arranges for a police steam launch and officers to meet at Westminster Stairs, while Dr. Watson is to deliver the treasure to Miss Morstan first.

Chapter X

Sherlock Holmes, Dr. Watson, and Athelney Jones pursue Jonathan Small and his companions down the Thames in a fast police launch. Holmes identifies Jacobson's Yard as the place where Mordecai Smith has concealed the Aurora, and the pursuers catch the launch after a prolonged chase. Small is captured after the Aurora runs aground, while the Islander is shot and falls into the river. The Sholtos' iron treasure-chest is recovered, but the Islander's body is not found.

Chapter XI

Jonathan Small, captured after the chase on the Thames, tells Sherlock Holmes that Tonga killed Thaddeus Sholto with a poisoned dart and that he himself did not attack Sholto. Small explains that he pursued the Agra treasure, which he believes would have made him nearly half a million pounds richer, but which has brought him imprisonment and misfortune instead. Dr. Watson takes the iron treasure box to Mary Morstan, only to find it completely empty. Believing the treasure lost, Watson declares his love for Mary, and she accepts him.

Chapter XII

Inspector Jones learns that the box believed to contain the Agra treasure is empty, and Athelney Jones is dismayed because the reward may be lost. Jonathan Small admits that he disposed of the treasure when the pursuing launch was about to overtake him, saying he acted for the three imprisoned men and himself. He argues that the treasure belongs to them because of the suffering he endured while imprisoned in the Andaman Islands. Holmes says that they cannot judge whether justice was originally on Small’s side until they hear his story, which Small agrees to tell.

The narrator recounts how he lost his right leg in India, became an indigo-plantation overseer for Abel White, and survived the Indian Mutiny by reaching Agra. At the Agra Fort, he joined a volunteer corps and was assigned to guard an isolated postern gate with the Sikh soldiers Mahomet Singh and Abdullah Khan. Abdullah and Mahomet threatened him into joining a conspiracy to murder Achmet, a merchant carrying the rajah’s jewels, and divide the treasure. Small recounts how he, Mahomet Singh, Abdullah Khan, and Dost Akbar murdered Achmet the merchant during the Indian mutiny and hid Achmet’s jewels in the Agra fort. After the four conspirators were arrested and imprisoned, Small persuaded Major Sholto and Captain Morstan to help them escape in exchange for knowledge of the treasure, but Sholto recovered the treasure and betrayed them. Small later escaped the Andamans with Tonga, killing a hostile convict-guard before they sailed away in Tonga’s canoe and were rescued by a trader.
