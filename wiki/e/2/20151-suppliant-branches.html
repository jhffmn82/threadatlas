suppliant branches in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

New-plucked olive boughs mark the Danaids’ appeal on gods’ shrines; Pelasgus directs Danaus to place them at city altars, identifying the Danaids as suppliants.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Enter the KING OF ARGOS.

The suppliant branches are new-plucked olive boughs laid upon the shrines as signs of the Danaids’ appeal. Pelasgus tells Danaus to carry them to the city’s many altars so that the people will recognize the Danaids as suppliants.
