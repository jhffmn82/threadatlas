Abstract written by a model

In Four Plays of Aeschylus, human arts and knowledge are the arts Prometheus gives mortals, including writing, number, memory, song, astronomy, crafts, technology, medicine, divination, sacrificial practice, and metalworking. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, human arts and inventions arose through necessity and experience, helping people use caves, preserve food, use fire, and develop other conveniences.

human arts and knowledge in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · 471 BC · the work’s page · read it where human arts and knowledge first appears

Abstract written by a model

Prometheus gives mortal men arts that aid their prosperity on earth. He connects these arts with the fire he secretly provides. Human arts and knowledge include writing, number, memory, song, astronomy, carpentry, animal training, chariot and seafaring technology, medicinal drugs, divination, interpretation of omens, sacrificial practice, and the discovery of copper, iron, silver, and gold. These arts were unknown or undeveloped among mortals until Prometheus taught or devised them.
