Aeschylus

person · 3 works · 9 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Aeschylus', the poet, Æschylus

Abstract written by a model

In Four Plays of Aeschylus, Aeschylus is described as a Greek dramatist who lived sixty-nine years, fought at Marathon and Salamis, and wrote nearly a hundred dramas, of which seven surviving plays remain major examples of early Greek drama. In The Electra of Euripides, Aeschylus faces the horror of the mother-murder rather than avoiding or disguising it, presenting it as both a sin requiring profound suffering and a duty that must be committed because it is commanded by a god. In Description of Greece, Æschylus is described as a great light of Tragedy who fought at Marathon, wrote tragedy after a dream command from Dionysus, represented Erinnyes with snakes, described seven warriors, and received a statue long after death.

INTRODUCTION

Aeschylus is said to have lived from 525 to 456 B.C. and to have written nearly a hundred dramas, of which seven survive. He is traditionally believed to have fought at Marathon and Salamis, and his surviving plays are presented as major examples of early Greek drama.

CHAPTER XIX.

Æschylus was regarded as one of the great lights of Tragedy, and his statue was completed long after his death. He said that Dionysus appeared to him in a dream and commanded him to write tragedy, after which he found the work easy.
