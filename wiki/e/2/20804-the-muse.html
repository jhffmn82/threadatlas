Muse of the Mountains in The Rhesus of Euripides

Euripides · 454 to 439 BC · the work’s page · read it where Muse of the Mountains first appears

Abstract written by a model

Muse of the Mountains is a Muse, goddess, and mother of Rhesus. Near the play’s end, she appears holding and mourning her slain son, curses Odysseus, Diomede, Helen, and Athena, and blames Athena while attributing his death to dead Thamyris’s vengeance. She asks that Rhesus escape ordinary death, remain her spirit, and stay hidden beneath earth until his people need him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

_Enter_ PARIS.

The Muse appears holding Rhesus's body and mourns him as her beloved son. She curses Odysseus, Diomede, Helen, and Athena, and asks that Rhesus be released from ordinary death and remain with her as a spirit.

NOTES

The Muse appears near the end of the play and delivers a beautiful lament for Rhesus. She connects his death with vengeance from the dead Thracian bard Thamyris and declares that Rhesus will remain hidden beneath the earth until his people need him.

the Muse in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · about 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where the Muse first appears

Abstract written by a model

One of nine sisters and Rhesus’s grieving mother, the Muse mourns his death, recounts his origins, blames Minerva, and prophesies his restoration to divine life.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

ULYSSES, DIOMEDE.

The Muse appears carrying Rhesus’s body, identifies herself as one of the nine sisters, and mourns her son. She explains Rhesus’s divine and royal origins, blames Minerva for his fate while acknowledging that Ulysses and Diomede gave the wound, and prophesies Rhesus’s restoration to divine life.
