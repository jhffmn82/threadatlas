LI-RAGE

  • In LI-RAGE: Late Interaction Retrieval Augmented Generation with Explicit Signals for Open-Domain Table Question Answering: LI-RAGE is a novel end-to-end, open-domain TableQA framework that combines a Latent Interaction retriever, RAGE loss, and binary relevance tokens for reader training. It uses ColBERT as its retriever and TaPEx as its answer generator. Its central design is late interaction retrieval, enabling finer-grained interaction between questions and tables; during training, it dynamically extracts question embeddings and retrieves the highest-scoring tables from a precomputed index. Joint training with RAGE loss and binary relevance tokens supports its results. LI-RAGE achieves the best reported performance across NQ-TABLES and E2E-WTQ: 54.17 Token F1 and 46.15 EM on NQ-TABLES, 62.10 accuracy on E2E-WTQ, and 87.90% recall@5 on NQ-TABLES. It has the highest recall on both datasets, outperforms BM25 on both, and exceeds DPR by about 3% on NQ-TABLES. Relative to previously best reported results, it improves Token F1 and EM by more than three points on NQ-TABLES and performance by 11.45 points on E2E-WTQ; these gains are attributed to strong LI retriever performance. Training uses AdamW, eight GPUs, batch size 1 per device, four gradient-accumulation steps, weight decay 0.01, and label smoothing 0.1. It trains for 20 epochs on NQ-TABLES and 15 on E2E-WTQ. Dynamic retrieval and larger multi-dimensional embeddings make end-to-end training longer, but deployment computation does not increase significantly; inference is 1.40 seconds per iteration with batch size 4.

Narrative, section by section

8 sections; each heading opens the text

Introduction

LI-RAGE is an end-to-end framework combining a Latent Interaction retriever, RAGE loss, and binary relevance tokens for reader training. It achieves state-of-the-art results on NQ-TABLES and E2E-WTQ.

Experimental Setup

LI-RAGE is the main system, using ColBERT as retriever, TaPEx as answer generator, RAGE loss for joint training, and a binary relevance token in the output. It achieves 54.17 Token F1 and 46.15 EM on NQ-TABLES, and 62.10 accuracy on E2E-WTQ.

Results and Discussions / Main Results .. Remarks of Design Rationale

LI-RAGE achieves the best performance across the reported results on NQ-TABLES and E2E-WTQ. It obtains 87.90% recall on NQ-TABLES when retrieving five tables and benefits from joint training and binary relevance tokens.

Conclusion

LI-RAGE is introduced as a novel open-domain TableQA framework. It leverages late interaction retrievers for finer-grained interaction between questions and tables and incorporates the RAGE loss and binary relevance tokens. These features enable significant improvements over the state of the art in two challenging TableQA tasks.

Dataset Statistics

LI-RAGE uses AdamW, linear learning-rate decay, a batch size of 1 per device, four gradient accumulation steps, eight GPUs, weight decay of 0.01, and label smoothing of 0.1. Its NQ-TABLES configuration has zero warmup steps, a retriever learning rate of 0.00001, and 20 epochs, while its E2E-WTQ configuration has no learning-rate decay, zero warmup steps, and 15 epochs.

CLTR and T-RAG Evaluation

LI-RAGE has a reported training speed of 0.74 iterations per second, batch size 1, and training times of 450 minutes on NQ and 50 minutes on WTQ. Its inference speed is 1.40 seconds per iteration with inference batch size 4.

Indexing and Dynamic Retrieval

During LI-RAGE training, question embeddings are dynamically extracted from the retriever, and tables with the highest scores are retrieved using the precomputed index.

Computational Cost

LI-RAGE has longer end-to-end training because dynamic retrieval is slightly slower and its retriever is refined using larger multi-dimensional embeddings. Its inference speed changes only modestly, from 1.22 to 1.40 seconds per iteration.
