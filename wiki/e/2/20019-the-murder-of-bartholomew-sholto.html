Abstract written by a model

The murder of Bartholomew Sholto occurred at Pondicherry Lodge in Upper Norwood during the theft of the treasure. Holmes identified the locked-room death as murder after finding a poisoned thorn, unusual hammer-like instrument, torn note, ceiling opening, and missing treasure. He attributed the extreme contraction and risus sardonicus to a powerful vegetable alkaloid, like strychnine, delivered by the thorn. Footprints, weapons, a card linked to Captain Morstan’s chart, and poison led Holmes to conclude that Jonathan Small’s unnamed associate killed Bartholomew while Small removed the treasure-box. A newspaper reported arrests and falsely credited Athelney Jones; later, Tonga was said to have killed him after entering through the roof.

Chapter V

The death of Bartholomew Sholto is identified by Sherlock Holmes as murder. The locked room contains a poisoned thorn, a peculiar hammer-like instrument, a torn note, and an opening in the ceiling, while the treasure is gone.

Chapter VI

The death of Bartholomew Sholto is attributed by Holmes to a powerful vegetable alkaloid delivered through a poisoned thorn. The body’s extreme contraction and risus sardonicus indicate a strychnine-like poison producing tetanus.

Chapter VII

The murder of Bartholomew Sholto is associated with footprints, unusual weapons, a card bearing words corresponding to Captain Morstan’s chart, and poison. Sherlock Holmes concludes that Jonathan Small’s unnamed associate committed the killing while Jonathan Small removed the treasure-box.

Chapter VIII

The newspaper reports that Bartholomew Sholto was found dead at Pondicherry Lodge under circumstances suggesting foul play. Athelney Jones has arrested Thaddeus Sholto, Mrs. Bernstone, Lal Rao, and McMurdo, and the report falsely credits Jones with proving that the criminals entered through the roof.
