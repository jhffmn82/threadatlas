The Seven against Thebes in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

The Seven against Thebes, the third part of the House of Laius trilogy, depicts defense of the Cadmea against Polynices and Argive allies, ending with Eteocles and Polynices dead, the male line extinct, and Antigone protesting Polynices' dishonorable treatment.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

INTRODUCTION

The Seven against Thebes was presented in 467 B.C. and depicts the defense of the Cadmea against Polynices and his Argive allies. It ends with the deaths of Eteocles and Polynices, the extinction of the male line, and Antigone's protest against the dishonorable treatment of Polynices.
