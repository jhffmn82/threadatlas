Vedas in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The Vedas are sacred books on which Hindu religion is professedly founded, containing rules of faith and religious ceremonies. Hindus attribute their composition to Brahma at creation and their arrangement to Vyasa; they portray Buddha as Vishnu's delusive incarnation and are entirely rejected by Buddhism.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXX

The Vedas are the sacred books on which Hindu religion is professedly founded, and Hindus attribute their composition to Brahma at the creation. The present arrangement is attributed to Vyasa, and the Vedas are said to contain the rules of faith and religious ceremonies.

BUDDHA

The Vedas represent Buddha as a delusive incarnation of Vishnu. Buddhists reject the authority of the Vedas and the religious observances prescribed in them.
