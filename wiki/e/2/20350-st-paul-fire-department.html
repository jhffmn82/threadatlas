The St. Paul Fire Department developed from volunteer companies organized in the 1850s, including Pioneer Hook and Ladder Company No. 1, Hope Engine Company No. 1, and Minnehaha Engine Company No. 2. It held its first annual parade in 1859. By March 1860 it had three fire engines, one fire truck, and one hundred men. After its first steamer arrived August 11, 1866, it expanded despite the city’s lack of waterworks in 1869, relying on cisterns and river water pumped through over two thousand feet of hose. At present it has 211 paid men, 15 steamers, four chemicals, eight hook and ladder companies, and 122 horses; chiefs include Wash M. Stees and Bartlett Presley.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

FIREMEN AND FIRES OF PIONEER DAYS.

The St. Paul Fire Department developed from volunteer companies organized in the 1850s, including Pioneer Hook and Ladder Company No. 1, Hope Engine Company No. 1, and Minnehaha Engine Company No. 2. Its first annual parade was held in 1859, and after the first steamer arrived on August 11, 1866, the department continued to expand.

HOTEL FIRES.

The fire department initially consisted of three engines and one truck, manned by one hundred men. In 1869 the city had no waterworks and relied on cisterns, with water sometimes pumped from the river through more than two thousand feet of hose.
