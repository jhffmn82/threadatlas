Achmet in The Sign of the Four

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1890-02

Achmet is a fat, trusted merchant of the rajah, traveling with Dost Akbar and carrying an iron box of jewels and pearls. Frightened, he seeks protection in the Agra fort. Although the narrator orders him taken to the main guard, Achmet runs through the corridors bloodied and pursued by a Sikh. The narrator trips him with a firelock, and the Sikh stabs him twice. Achmet is buried under loose bricks; his disappearance is later discovered when a spy reports his entry into the fort.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

“I am a Worcestershire man myself,—born near Pershore. I dare say you

Achmet was a frightened merchant traveling with an iron box containing valuable family possessions. He sought protection inside the Agra Fort, and the narrator ordered that he be taken to the main guard rather than killed by the Sikhs.

“I could hear the measured tramp of their footsteps sounding through

The fat man runs through the corridors with a smear of blood across his face while the Sikh pursues him. He is gaining on the Sikh and might have escaped into the open air, but the narrator trips him with a firelock, after which the Sikh stabs him twice.

He stopped, and held out his manacled hands for the whiskey-and-water

Achmet the merchant is carried into the Agra fort after being attacked and is buried beneath loose bricks in a natural grave. His murder is discovered after a spy reports that Achmet entered the fort but cannot be found.
