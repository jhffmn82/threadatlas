  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Ethiopia is described as a vast, hot African land of mountains, deserts, Nile regions, maritime territories, varied peoples, abundant natural resources, reported beasts, and a history involving Osiris, Egyptian rule, and resistance to foreign conquerors.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Æthiopia is described as an eastern region containing Cepheus’s land and possibly the place where Perseus finds Andromeda exposed.
  • In The Library, Ethiopia is described as ruled by Cepheus, where Perseus finds Andromeda exposed as food for a sea monster.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Aethiopia is described as the country ruled by Cepheus, whose coast is ravaged by a sea monster before Perseus rescues Andromeda.

Ethiopia in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Ethiopia is a vast, intensely hot place extending through remote mountains, deserts, lands beside the Nile, river islands, maritime parts, areas bordering Arabia, and territories in the heart of Africa. The Nile descends from its mountainous regions and passes through it before reaching Egypt; yearly rains are said to fall in some mountains from the summer solstice to the autumnal equinox, though heat and desert make the springs inaccessible. It includes Meroe, a capital city, fertile pasture and marshland, reeds, mines, ebony, precious stones, and a four-square vermilion lake with sweet water. It is higher than Egypt in discussion of the river’s course and lacks south winds. Its varied inhabitants include peoples living by fishing, whales, or roots, as well as Rizophages, Troglodytes, Hylogones, Elephantophages, Simoes, and Rhinoceros. Elephants descend toward Nile regions for pasture; sphinxes, Cynocephales, Cepus, wild bulls, Crocutas, and enormous serpents are also reported. The heat can kill people without drink, and water in bronze can boil in the sun. Osiris travels there, teaches husbandry and tillage, builds cities, and appoints governors and tribute collectors. Ethiopia later rules Egypt after Actisanes conquers Ammosis, and Egyptian soldiers migrate there to divide rich land. It resists Semiramis and Cambyses. Its maritime inhabitants maintain a six-hundred-year custom of sending two strangers away by sea.

Ethiopia is presented as the region through which the Nile flows before reaching Egypt. Its mountainous parts are said by Agartharchides of Cnidus to receive continual yearly rains from the summer solstice to the autumnal equinox.

CHAP. V.

Ethiopia ruled Egypt after Actisanes conquered Ammosis. More than two hundred thousand Egyptian soldiers later migrated to Ethiopia, where they settled in a rich and fruitful land and divided it among themselves.

The maritime parts of Ethiopia were the home of the inhabitants who carried Lambulus and his fellow-captive away for use in a ritual purification. Ethiopia had an ancient custom, attributed to oracles, of sending two strangers away by sea once every six hundred years.

Ethiopia is portrayed as a large region containing the capital city and Meroe, lands beside the Nile, river islands, areas bordering Arabia, and territories in the heart of Africa. Its inhabitants and resources vary greatly, and some parts are described as rich in pasture, marshes, reeds, and other food.

Ethiopia in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Æthiopia, regarded as a region of the East, contains Cepheus’s land and is proposed as the setting where Perseus finds Andromeda exposed and Cepheus and Cassiope rule, though Africa’s coast is considered more probable.
