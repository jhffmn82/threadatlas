Henry Hastings Sibley in Novel-10146

Pioneer Press · 1862~

Henry Hastings Sibley is a governor of Minnesota during the bond dispute and later a military commander. As Gov. Sibley, he initially refused to issue railroad bonds until the state's rights were protected, but, following the attorney general's advice and the supreme court's mandate, reluctantly signed them; he later failed to negotiate them in New York. Placed in command at St. Peter, he led poorly supplied troops, relieved Fort Ridgely, assembled about 2,000 men and a battery, reached Camp Release, secured surrender and captives' release, and formed a military tribunal for suspected offenders.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

THE FIVE MILLION LOAN ELECTION.

Henry H. Sibley, identified in the text as Gov. Sibley, initially refused to issue the railroad bonds until the state's rights were protected. On the advice of the attorney general and in compliance with the supreme court's mandate, he reluctantly signed them and later failed to negotiate them in New York.

THE GREAT SIOUX OUTBREAK IN 1862.

Henry Hastings Sibley was placed in command of the troops assembled at St. Peter because of his long familiarity with Indian character. He soon led partially uniformed and poorly supplied forces in offensive operations and relieved Fort Ridgely.

As soon as Gen.

Gen. Sibley collected about 2,000 men and moved against the Sioux after gathering a sufficient force. He led the command to Camp Release, where he proceeded cautiously until the Indian camp surrendered and the captives were released, then organized a military tribunal to try suspected offenders.
