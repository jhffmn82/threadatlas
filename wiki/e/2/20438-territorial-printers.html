territorial printers in Novel-10146

Pioneer Press · 1862~

Territorial printers were a group of printers, reporters, editors, publishers, foremen, and type founders. Closely affiliated with editors, they worked and often slept in printing offices because hotels were scarce and economy mattered. Their composing rooms fostered debate, literary quotation, and sociability. The record lists many members, including Capt. E.Y. Shelly, and identifies a smaller set then known to survive.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

PRINTERS AND EDITORS OF TERRITORIAL DAYS.

The unit lists numerous territorial printers, including E.Y. Shelly, George W. Moore, John C. Devereux, Martin Williams, H.O. Bassford, and many others. It states that only H.O. Bassford, George W. Benedict, David Brock, John C. Devereux, Barrett Smith, J.B.H. Mitchell, David Ramaley, M.R. Prendergast, Jacob T. McCoy, A.S. Diamond, R.V. Hesselgrave, H.P. Coates, J.R. Chaney, and M.J. Clum were then known survivors.

TERRITORIAL PRINTERS IN CIVIL LIFE.

Territorial printers held many roles, including printer, reporter, editor, publisher, foreman, and type founder. They often worked and slept in the same offices because of limited hotel accommodations, and their composing rooms were known for debate, literary quotations, and sociability.
