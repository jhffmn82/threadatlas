Persian kingship and power in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

Persian kingship and power is imperial authority marked by wealth, vast forces, royal command, and rule over many peoples. After defeat at Salamis, the loss of men and the king’s wrecked forces overturn that authority: the state cannot be preserved, subjects no longer crouch or obey Xerxes as before.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE PERSIANS

Persian imperial power is represented by wealth, vast numbers, royal authority, and the ability to bridge the Hellespont and command peoples from many lands. Atossa and the Chorus nevertheless fear that wealth and numbers cannot preserve the state if its men and king are lost.

Enter a MESSENGER.

The CHORUS says that the Persian kingship and power have fallen, that men will no longer crouch before the defeated authority, and that those ruled by Xerxes will no longer obey his word in the same way. Persian rule is portrayed as broken by the deaths and wreckage at Salamis.
