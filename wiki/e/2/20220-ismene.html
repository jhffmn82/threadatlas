Ismene in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Ismene is a young daughter of Oedipus and sister of Antigone, Polynices, and Eteocles. Cautious and obedient before stronger authority, she refuses to join Antigone’s plan to bury Polynices, saying that women cannot resist the citizens’ resolve; nevertheless, she offers secrecy, later wishes to share Antigone’s guilt and calamity, and fears life without her sister. She also serves Oedipus faithfully: she secretly informs him, brings news of the conflict between Eteocles and Polynices, reports that the Theban race will seek him because victory depends on him, and performs an offering on his behalf. Accompanied by her trusted slave, she joins Antigone in caring for and preparing Oedipus. After his disappearance, she grieves, longs for death with him, and seeks refuge with Antigone.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

ANTIGONE

Ismene refuses to join Antigone in burying Polynices, arguing that they are women who must submit to stronger authority and that they cannot withstand the citizens’ resolve. She agrees to keep Antigone’s plan secret, though Antigone rejects secrecy.

CHORUS.

Ismene says that she will share Antigone's guilt and calamity, but Antigone refuses to let her claim responsibility for an act she did not perform. Ismene argues that life without her sister has no value and asks whether Antigone's death will also destroy Haemon's marriage.

OEDIPUS AT COLONOS

Ismene arrives at Colonos on horseback and approaches Oedipus and Antigone. Antigone recognizes her and expects her voice to confirm her identity.

_Enter_ ISMENE.

Ismene comes to Oedipus with a trusted slave and reports the conflict between Eteocles and Polynices. She tells him that an oracle says the Theban race will seek him, alive or dead, because victory depends on him, and she goes to perform the required offering.

CHORUS.

Ismene is present with Antigone when Theseus returns them safely to Oedipus, but she has no individual speech in this unit.

CHORUS.

Ismene helps prepare Oedipus by bringing water with Antigone and shares in the farewell. She grieves after his disappearance, longs for death with her honored father, and asks where she and Antigone can find refuge before Theseus promises to aid them.

Ismene in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912

Ismene is Oedipus’s daughter and the sister of Antigone, Polyneices, and Eteocles, later orphaned by Oedipus’s death and kin to Creon. She appears first with Antigone before Oedipus, whose fears for her future reflect her vulnerable position. She is later restored safely to Oedipus and mourns his death, fearing that she and Antigone will be friendless and helpless. When Antigone plans to bury Polyneices, Ismene refuses to assist, believing that women must obey the ruler, though she keeps the plan secret and fears for her sister. After Antigone’s arrest, Ismene tries to claim shared guilt despite not participating, because life without Antigone seems worthless to her. Under compulsion, she obeys the royal edict.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

[Enter SECOND MESSENGER.]

Ismene is brought to Oedipus with Antigone. Oedipus mourns the difficult future and social disgrace that may await her because she is his daughter.

OEDIPUS AT COLONUS

Ismene is first seen approaching on horseback by Antigone, who recognizes her as her sister and Oedipus’s daughter.

[Enter ANTIGONE and ISMENE with THESEUS]

Ismene is brought back safely to Oedipus by Theseus and remains with Oedipus and Antigone as Polyneices speaks. She accompanies Antigone and Theseus when they follow Oedipus toward the place appointed for his death.

[Enter MESSENGER]

Ismene grieves Oedipus's death and wishes that she could share his fate rather than live desolate. She tells Antigone that Oedipus died without a tomb or anyone nearby, and she fears how they will continue friendless and helpless.

ANTIGONE

Ismene refuses to help bury Polyneices, arguing that they are weak women who must obey the ruler. She agrees to keep Antigone’s plan secret but fears that Antigone is doomed to fail.

[Enter GUARD bringing ANTIGONE]

Ismene appears grieving after Antigone’s arrest and claims that she took part in the deed so that she can share Antigone’s guilt. Antigone denies that Ismene helped her and refuses to let her die for an act she did not perform. Ismene says that life would have no profit without her sister and reveals that Antigone is Haemon’s plighted bride.
