Chorus in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

Chorus is a group of sea-nymphs, daughters of Oceanus, who worship Oceanus and appear as compassionate gods and supplicants seeking knowledge from Prometheus. In Prometheus Bound, they arrive in winged cars after hearing his punishment, pity and support him, and listen as he describes Zeus’s hidden, implacable rule. They mourn his suffering, recall his gifts to mortals, compare him with Atlas, and warn him not to offend Zeus. They question him about Io and his eventual release, horror-struck by Io’s fate. Though they urge him to yield, they reject Hermes’s charge of cowardice and remain loyally beside Prometheus. In the burial crisis after Eteocles and Polynices die, they grieve Oedipus’ cursed line and divide between Antigone’s burial of Polynices and Eteocles’ funeral.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Enter THE SPY.

The Chorus first reacts with fear and grief to the deaths of Eteocles and Polynices, lamenting the curse upon Oedipus’ line. After the Herald announces the burial decree, the Chorus divides: one group supports burying Polynices with Antigone, while the other carries Eteocles to his grave.

PROMETHEUS BOUND

The Chorus of Oceanides comes in winged cars to visit Prometheus after hearing the hammer blows. It expresses pity and fear at his suffering, listens to his account, and recognizes that Zeus rules with hidden and implacable power.

[_Exit OCEANUS._]

The Chorus mourns Prometheus’s punishment and compares his suffering with the burden borne by Atlas. It urges loyalty to Zeus and warns against offending him with words, while also recalling Prometheus’s love for mortals and the gifts he gave them.

Enter IO, horned like a cow.

The Chorus asks Prometheus to explain Io’s past and future and reacts with horror to her sufferings. It also asks to learn who will eventually release Prometheus.

[_Exit IO._]

The Chorus reflects that a person should marry within their own station and fears marriage to a god, citing Io’s suffering under Hera’s malice. It urges Prometheus to abandon his defiance, then rejects Hermes’s implied cowardice and chooses to remain at Prometheus’s side despite the danger.
