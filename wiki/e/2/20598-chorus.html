Abstract written by a model

Chorus is the collective of aged, physically weakened men of Mycenae and Argos: Agamemnon’s loyal lieges who remain behind while the army fights and who revere Clytemnestra’s authority. They recount the Greek expedition, its omens, the delay at Aulis, Iphigenia’s sacrifice, Troy’s fall, and the lesson that suffering may teach wisdom. Welcoming the Herald and Agamemnon, they temper celebration with unease over Helen, Paris, inherited guilt, and divine vengeance. They recognize Cassandra’s prophecy, fear Agamemnon’s murder, mourn and condemn Clytemnestra after it occurs, and oppose Aegisthus while debating resistance. Finally, they voice the Furies’ anger but accept Athena’s offer, blessing Athens with fertility, protection, and an end to civic bloodshed.

Chorus is in one work on this site, The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

AGAMEMNON

The Chorus recounts the Greek expedition, the omens involving the eagles, the delay at Aulis, and the sacrifice of Agamemnon's daughter. It celebrates the fall of Troy while mourning the Greek dead and declaring that suffering can teach wisdom.

_Enter_ HERALD

The Chorus welcomes the Herald and Agamemnon but expresses unease beneath its joy. It reflects on Helen, Paris, inherited guilt, divine vengeance, and the destruction brought upon Troy.

[_Enter Clytemnestra, followed by maidens bearing purple robes._

The Old men of Argos are addressed by Clytemnestra as witnesses to her account of the suffering she endured while Agamemnon was away.

[_Exeunt all but Cassandra and the Chorus._

The Chorus reacts with fear and pity to Cassandra’s prophecies, gradually recognizing that she speaks of Agamemnon’s imminent murder. After hearing Agamemnon cry out from within the palace, its members debate whether to call for help, enter the palace, or resist the apparent tyranny.

[_The scene opens, disclosing Clytemnestra, who comes forward. The body

The Chorus condemns Clytemnestra’s boastfulness and mourns Agamemnon’s death. It recalls Iphigenia’s sacrifice, invokes the inherited curse and blood vengeance, threatens Aegisthus, and calls for armed resistance before Clytemnestra ends the confrontation.

CHORUS

The Chorus voices the Furies’ anger at being displaced, then accepts Athena’s offer and proclaims blessings upon Athens. It promises fertility, protection from famine and untimely death, and an end to civic bloodshed.
