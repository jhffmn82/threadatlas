Abstract written by a model

In Four Plays of Aeschylus, the sons of Aegyptus are the Danaids’ suitors and armed pursuers whose violent claim to marriage drives the women and Danaus to flee Egypt for Argos. In Heroides and Amores, the sons of Aegyptus are the Danaids’ bridegrooms who are massacred in their bridal chambers, with Lynceus as the sole survivor. In The Library, the sons of Egyptus marry the daughters of Danaus by lot and are killed by their brides while asleep, except for Lynceus.

the sons of Aegyptus in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · 471 BC · the work’s page · read it where the sons of Aegyptus first appears

Abstract written by a model

The sons of Aegyptus are a group of Aegyptus’s sons, first cousins and suitors of the daughters of Danaus. They desire marriage with the Danaids and claim a kinship-based right to them, but the women reject the union. Their wanton, violent pursuit drives the daughters and Danaus to flee from Egypt to Argos. The sons arrive or are expected by ship as armed adversaries to reclaim the Danaids for forced wedlock, creating a threat of conflict with Argos that may be settled through war and many deaths.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

INTRODUCTION

The sons of Aegyptus are the Danaids' first cousins and suitors. They pursue the Danaids from Egypt and arrive by ship to reclaim them for forced wedlock.

THE SUPPLIANT MAIDENS / DEDICATION

The sons of Aegyptus wish to marry the daughters of Danaus. Their pursuit causes the daughters to flee with their father to Argos.

The Suppliant Maidens

The sons of Aegyptus pursue the daughters of Danaus in order to force them into marriage. The Chorus describes them as a wanton and lustful crowd seeking an embrace and bridal union that the daughters reject.

Enter the KING OF ARGOS.

The sons of Aegyptus are pursuing the Danaids and claim a right to hold them as next of kin. Their threatened arrival creates the danger of conflict between them and Argos.

Re-enter DANAUS.

The sons of Aegyptus pursue the Danaids by sea and are expected to arrive as armed adversaries. The Herald says that the matter will be decided in the court of Ares, with war and many deaths if necessary.
