the future fall of Zeus in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

The future fall of Zeus is a foretold, threatened event in which Zeus loses pride, sway, and kingship. Prometheus predicts it will result from Zeus’s mindless counsels and a marriage, threatened according to Prometheus, that produces a child destined to overcome his sire and fulfill Cronos’s curse. Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

PROMETHEUS BOUND

The future fall of Zeus is foretold by Prometheus as a time when Zeus will lose pride and sway. Prometheus says that Zeus will need his knowledge of a destined stratagem, but he will not reveal it until Zeus abandons his purpose and makes amends.

Enter IO, horned like a cow.

Zeus’s overthrow will occur because of his own mindless counsels and a marriage that produces a child destined to overcome him. Prometheus says this fall will happen, though he does not reveal every detail.

[_Exit IO._]

Prometheus predicts that Zeus will be brought low and lose his kingship through a marriage that fulfills the curse spoken by Cronos. He claims that a mighty being will arise who can overcome Zeus’s lightning and Poseidon’s trident, though Zeus’s fall remains a threatened future event in this unit.
