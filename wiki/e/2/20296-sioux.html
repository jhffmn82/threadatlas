Abstract written by a model

The Sioux occupied a strip along the Minnesota River and were hereditary enemies of the Chippewas. Divided between a government-favored farming party and an opposing Indian party, they had grievances over land, trader accounts, annuity deductions, and delayed payments, and communicated with Hole-in-the-Day. Before and during the 1862 outbreak, they attacked settlers, Company B, and fought at Fort Ridgely, New Ulm, Birch Coulie, and Wood Lake. Routed at Wood Lake, many at Camp Release were implicated in massacres, and the Sioux forfeited their Minnesota lands.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

THE FIVE MILLION LOAN ELECTION.

The Sioux were attacked near Shakopee by Chippewa warriors commanded by Chief Hole-in-the-day. Fifteen or twenty Sioux were killed or wounded, and the text identifies the Sioux and Chippewas as hereditary enemies.

THE GREAT SIOUX OUTBREAK IN 1862.

The Sioux were divided between a farming party favored by the government and an Indian party opposed to adopting white agricultural practices. After disputes over land, trade debts, and delayed annuity payments, Sioux forces attacked settlers and fought at Fort Ridgely, New Ulm, and Birch Coulie.

As soon as Gen.

The Sioux opposed Gen. Sibley's forces at Wood Lake and were routed by Col. Marshall's charge. Many Sioux at Camp Release had been involved in the massacres, and the outbreak caused them to forfeit their Minnesota lands to the government.
