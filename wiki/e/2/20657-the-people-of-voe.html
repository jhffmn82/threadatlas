the people of Voe in Dorothy and the Wizard in Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1908

The people of Voe are invisible inhabitants of the Valley of Voe, made unseen by eating dama-fruit to avoid bears. They still see nature and prefer invisibility, freeing them from concerns such as clothing and dirty faces. They welcome Dorothy, Zeb, and the Wizard, feed and warn them, describe Pyramid Mountain, the staircase and Gargoyles, and provide a water-walking plant.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

8. The Valley of Voices

The people of Voe are invisible because they eat the dama-fruit. They welcome Dorothy, Zeb, and the Wizard, explain the valley's dangers, and offer the travelers food.

9. They Fight the Invisible Bears

The unseen family explains that the Valley's people prefer invisibility because it protects them from the bears and lets them ignore ordinary concerns such as clothing and dirty faces. They tell the travelers about Pyramid Mountain, the spiral staircase, the Gargoyles, and the danger of the bears; an unseen girl also gives the travelers the water-walking plant.
