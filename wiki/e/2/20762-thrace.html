  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Thrace is a kingdom and region bordering the Macedonians, valued for Amphipolis; Philip conquered it, it suffered conflicts and invasions, Antipater campaigned there, and Diegylis and Seuthes ruled it.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Thrace is where Brasidas commanded the Lacedaemonian army and the Athenians campaigned; Cleon advanced along its coasts toward the Strymon and Amphipolis.
  • In The Argonautica, Thrace supplies the captive maids taken by the Lemnian men, becomes the place where those men live and plough fields, and lies beside the Argonauts’ route and their later harbour.
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Thrace is the realm of the Thracian king, including Thracian Chersonesus, where Priam sends Polydore to be raised and where the king’s household becomes the setting of his murder.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Thrace is ruled by Polymestor and is where Polydore was concealed and murdered; Polymestor claims he killed him to protect Thrace and neighboring lands.
  • In The Rhesus of Euripides, Thrace is a land ruled by Rhesus and represented by him and his army; Scythians attack its borders, Rhesus defeats them, and the Muse mourns its hills, kingship, and warlike life.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Thrace is a foreign land opposite Phrygia, inhabited by Bistonians, home to Mænades, and containing Polymnestor’s palace; Orpheus withdraws to its mountains, and its women later attack him and Hecuba.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Thrace, formerly called Scython, contains the Ciconians’ walls, and Boreas carries Orithyïa there despite Tereus and the Thracians’ opposition.
  • In The Library, Thrace contains Salmydessus and is traversed by Dionysus as he hastens against the Indians and later reaches Thebes.
  • In Description of Greece, Thrace contains Nestus, includes the part adjoining Macedonia ruled by Lysimachus after Alexander the Great’s death, and is described as densely populated before becoming subject to the Romans.

Thrace in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Thrace is a kingdom and region bordering the Macedonians, valued for places such as advantageously situated Amphipolis. Philip conquered it before an alliance formed. It suffered Triballian incursions, fighting between Triballians and Abderites, Memnon’s rebellion, and an insurrection; Antipater campaigned there. Diegylis ruled violently, then Seuthes ruled during Lysimachus’s invasion.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. IV.

Thrace was the region in which the Triballians and the Abderites fought. Its territories were affected by the Triballians' incursions and need for provisions.

CHAP. VI.

Thrace was the scene of Memnon's rebellion and of Antipater's initial campaign against him. Antipater settled matters there as well as he could before marching into Greece.
