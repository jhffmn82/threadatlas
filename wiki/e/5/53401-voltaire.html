Voltaire in Scientific American Supplement No. 360, November 25, 1882

Various · 1881

Voltaire is presented as France’s principal eighteenth-century agent of lucidity, opposing absurdity and benefiting cultivated classes through that quality, though he harmed France through want of seriousness and reverence rather than lucidity.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

LUCIDITY.

Voltaire is identified as the principal agent of France's eighteenth-century travail for lucidity. Although Arnold says that Voltaire harmed France through his lack of seriousness and reverence, he credits Voltaire's lucidity with doing good and compares him to the Luther of the eighteenth-century cultivated classes.
