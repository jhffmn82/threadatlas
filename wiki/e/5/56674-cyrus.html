Cyrus

Also called: Cyrus, son of Cambyses

Abstract written by a model

Cyrus was a Persian king and emperor, son of Cambyses and Mandane, who transferred empire from the Medes to the Persians. He excelled in courage, prudence, and other virtues, was formidable to enemies and mild toward subjects, who called him father. He honored Croesus as a councilor, fathered Atossa, and later Cappadocian kings claimed descent through her.

Cyrus is in one work on this site, The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

BOOK XXXI.

The kings of Cappadocia claimed descent from Cyrus through Atossa, whom they identified as Cyrus's daughter. Their royal genealogy was traced down from Cyrus through several generations to the Cappadocian kings.

*BOOKVI.

Cyrus, the son of Cambyses and Mandane and nephew of Astyages, excelled others of his age in courage, prudence, and other virtues. He was courageous against enemies and mild and gentle toward his subjects, for which the Persians called him their father; he also treated Croesus with honor and respect and made him one of his privy council.
