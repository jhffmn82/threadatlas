Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Battle of Thermopylae was a battle between Leonidas's Grecian forces and Xerxes's Persians. Greek defenders used close formation, large shields, and narrow ground to defeat several Persian forces, but a Trachinian led twenty thousand Persians through a mountain pass against Leonidas's rear. Xerxes gained the passes and the defenders were slaughtered, though their unequal stand and honourable death were remembered by historians and poets.

At the Battle of Thermopylae, the Greek defenders used their close formation, large shields, and the narrow ground to defeat the Medes, Cissians, Sacians, and Persian troops called immortal. The battle turned when a Trachinian led twenty thousand Persians through a mountain pass to threaten Leonidas's rear.

The battle of Thermopylae is represented through the defenders' unequal stand against the Persians and their honourable death. Its memory was preserved by historians and poets, including Simonides.
