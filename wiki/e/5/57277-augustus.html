Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Octavius Augustus helps fight Brutus and Cassius, defeats Antonius for supreme power, and holds the empire throughout his life. In The Aeneid, Augustus is presented as a divinely promised Caesar who will restore the golden age and establish worldwide dominion. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Augustus is the emperor addressed by Ovid who orders Ovid’s exile from Rome to Tomi around 8 A.D. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Augustus Cæsar is the Roman ruler residing in Palatium whose life defines the author’s times. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Augustus is Julius Cæsar’s illustrious heir and successor, a conquering ruler who establishes peace, regulates manners, enacts laws, is deified, and rules into old age. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Augustus reshapes cities, populations, political representation, and sacred objects after defeating Antony and his allies.

Augustus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Augustus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Augustus is a Roman emperor whose actions reshape cities, populations, political representation, and sacred objects after victory over Antony and his allies. Near Actium, he populates Nicopolis with Ætolians, relocates people to fill it, transfers statues from Ætolia and Acarnania there, and secures it a place in the Amphictyonic Council by moving votes from joined peoples and the extinct Dolopes. In Achæa, he enlarges Patræ by incorporating people from various towns, razes Rhypæ, and grants Patræ freedom and privileges. After his victory, he carries off the statue of Athene Alea and the Calydonian boar’s tusks, following an established custom, and displays the statue in his Forum at Rome.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XVIII.

Augustus populated Nicopolis near Actium with people from Ætolia and transferred many statues from Ætolia and Acarnania there. He enlarged Patræ's population with people from various towns, destroyed Rhypæ and incorporated its Achæans into Patræ, and granted Patræ freedom and other privileges.

CHAPTER XLVI.

Emperor Augustus carried away the statue of Athene Alea and the tusks of the Calydonian boar after his victory over Antony and his allies. He was said to have followed a long-established custom of carrying off votive offerings and statues from conquered nations, and he displayed the statue of Athene Alea in his Forum at Rome.

CHAPTER VIII.

Augustus wished the inhabitants of Nicopolis near Actium to belong to the Amphictyonic Council. He joined the Magnetes, Malienses, Ænianes, and Phthiotes to the Thessalians and transferred their votes, along with those of the extinct Dolopes, to Nicopolis.
