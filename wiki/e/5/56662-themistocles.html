Themistocles in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Themistocles is an Athenian statesman, general, and famous military commander, esteemed for prudence, experience, and faithfulness to his country. He commanded forces toward Tempe, sought soldiers from Greek cities, directed the Greek fleet, and urged battle at Salamis, where he arranged the fleet and deceived Xerxes into withdrawing. He strengthened Athens by delaying and deceiving the Lacedaemonians while its fortifications continued, enlarging Piraeus, expanding the fleet, and supporting foreign residents and artisans. Accused of conspiring with Pausanias, he was acquitted but later ostracized. He fled through Admetus's territory to Asia, was received by Xerxes, gained several cities, and died at Magnesia. The records praise him for saving Greece and advancing Athens.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

LAST TEN BOOKS / BOOK XI.

Themistocles commanded the Athenian contingent sent toward Tempe. He and Synetus sent messengers to the Greek cities to raise soldiers at the common expense.

Having now said enough of these gallant men, we shall return

Themistocles directed matters concerning the Greek fleet and advised attacking the Persians before they could assemble. He argued for fighting at Salamis because the narrow straits favored the Grecians, and he helped arrange the fleet for battle. He later sent a false message to Xerxes claiming that the Grecians intended to destroy the bridge, causing Xerxes to withdraw.

I.

Themistocles advised the Athenians to continue fortifying Athens while he delayed and deceived the Lacedaemonians. He later planned the enlargement of the harbor at Piraeus, persuaded the people to expand the fleet, and promoted the admission of foreign residents and artisans.

CHAP. VII.

Themistocles was accused by the Lacedaemonians of conspiring with Pausanias, but was acquitted at Athens before later being ostracized. He fled through Admetus's territory to Asia, was received by Xerxes, acquired Magnesia, Myus, and Lampsacus, and died at Magnesia; the text praises him as the foremost of the Greeks for saving Greece and advancing Athens.

Themistocles in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Themistocles made Piraeus Athens’ port for its convenience and three harbours, fought Xerxes and the Medes in a great sea-fight, and built walls after the Persian defeat. The walls were later pulled down; his tomb stood at the largest harbour, and his sons returned from exile to dedicate his painting.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Description of Greece / BOOK I.--ATTICA.

Themistocles made Piraeus the port of Athens because it seemed more convenient and provided three harbours instead of one. The walls he built after the Persian defeat were later pulled down by the Thirty Tyrants, and a tomb of Themistocles stood at the largest harbour; his sons later returned from exile and dedicated a painting of him in the Parthenon.

Themistocles in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Themistocles, child of Neocles, is honored at Olympia after expelling Xerxes from Greece by sea-fights. He brings Median spoils to Apollo, but the priestess rejects them, a refusal linked to his later supplication to the Persians.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIV.

Themistocles brought spoils of the Medes to Delphi and asked whether he should offer them inside Apollo’s temple. The Pythian Priestess ordered him to remove them, and some interpreted the refusal as connected with his later status as a suppliant of the Persians.
