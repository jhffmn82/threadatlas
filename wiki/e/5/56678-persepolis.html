Persepolis in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Persepolis was the fruitful, wholesome metropolis and royal seat of the Persian kingdom, called the richest city in Asia, with a rich citadel and pleasant surrounding gardens, valleys, walks, and springs. Eumenes reached it from Pasitigris, and Peucestes held a magnificent sacrifice and public feast at the king's palace. Alexander later gave the city to his soldiers and the Macedonians to plunder; its men were killed, property seized, and palace and city burned.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. VU.

Persepolis was the metropolis of the Persian kingdom and was described as the richest city in Asia. Alexander gave it to the Macedonians to plunder, after which its men were killed, its property was seized, and its palace was burned.

CHAP. n.

Persepolis was the royal seat of the Persian kingdom, reached by Eumenes after a twenty-four-day march from Pasitigris. Peucestes governed the province and held a magnificent sacrifice and public feast there for the gods, Alexander, Philip, and the army. The surrounding country included pleasant gardens, shaded valleys, natural walks, and refreshing springs.

BOOK XVII.

Alexander took Persepolis and gave it to his soldiers to plunder. Its citadel contained great riches, and the city was burned at the instigation of Phais.
