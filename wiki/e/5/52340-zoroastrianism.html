Zoroastrianism in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Zoroastrianism is an ancient Persian religion, founded or reformed by Zoroaster from an earlier religion. Based on the Zendavesta, it centers on Ormuzd, source of good, and Ahriman, author of evil. Dominant in Western Asia from Cyrus to Alexander's conquest, it survives as Parsees in India.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXX

Zoroastrianism was founded or reformed by Zoroaster and became the dominant religion of Western Asia from the time of Cyrus until the conquest of Persia by Alexander the Great. Its central opposition is between Ormuzd, the source of good, and Ahriman, the author of evil.
