It begins by situating LLM use in disease prediction, clinical decision-making, outbreak surveillance, and infoveillance, while emphasizing that inaccurate outputs can mislead clinical guidance and policy. After reviewing biomedical knowledge-graph and RAG approaches including KG-RAG, KRAGEN, Zebra-Llama, and MedRAG, the article introduces MEGA-RAG’s multi-stage design. The system retrieves evidence from PubMed, WHO IRIS, and the CPubMed-KG knowledge graph; reranks and evaluates evidence and candidate answers using tools including FAISS, BM25, BERTScore, cosine similarity, clustering, and majority voting; then resolves disagreements through a DISC module that asks clarification questions, obtains additional evidence, and refines answers within bounded iterations before final fusion. The authors evaluate five methods on HealthQuestDB, an 890-pair benchmark drawn from PubMed and WHO IRIS and reserved for evaluation rather than training. MEGA-RAG outperforms PubMedBERT, PubMedGPT, LLM + RAG, and a generic LLM, reporting accuracy of 0.7913, precision of 0.7541, recall of 0.8304, and F1 of 0.7904; the article also reports over 40% hallucination reduction and a better false-positive/false-negative balance. A case concerning silver ion water shows the framework producing a response consistent with expert public-health advisories and cautioning against colloidal or ionic silver as an antiviral treatment. The conclusion emphasizes traceable evidence provenance and alignment with public-health best practices, while acknowledging computational overhead from repeated retrieval and LLM calls, no detailed efficiency benchmark, and future needs in scalability and optimization. Proposed future work includes additional benchmarks, dynamic guidelines, multilingual deployment, clinician feedback, real-time epidemiological data, and efficiency improvements. The unit concerns the increasing adoption of large language models in public health and the risks posed by factually inaccurate or misleading outputs. It presents MEGA-RAG, a retrieval-augmented generation framework designed to mitigate hallucinations in public health applications through multi-source evidence retrieval, reranking, and discrepancy-aware refinement. 1 Introduction

AI is widely used in public health for disease prediction, clinical decision-making, outbreak surveillance, and large-scale infoveillance. Public health activities include disease management, improving health outcomes, and emergency response. Large language models face challenges from hallucinations, which can produce misleading clinical guidance and policy. Existing mitigation strategies offer only partial solutions, motivating the development of MEGA-RAG.

2 Related work

The unit reviews work integrating knowledge graphs into retrieval-augmented generation, particularly in biomedicine. KG-RAG embeds biomedical knowledge graphs using a token-optimized algorithm, while KRAGEN applies a biomedical knowledge graph framework to problem-solving and clinical contexts. Zebra-Llama combines context-aware large language models with external knowledge bases to improve access to rare-disease knowledge, and MedRAG uses knowledge graph-elicited reasoning to refine retrieval-augmented generation. These approaches aim to address hallucinations and strengthen clinical decision-making systems in public health.

3 Methods / 3.1 MEGA-RAG framework .. 3.6 Final answer fusion module

The unit describes MEGA-RAG, a retrieval-augmented question answering framework developed by Xu et al. for biomedical and public health domains. It combines multi-source retrieval from PubMed, the WHO IRIS database, and the CPubMed-KG knowledge graph with diverse answer generation, semantic-evidential evaluation, discrepancy resolution, and final answer fusion. The framework uses FAISS, BM25, knowledge graph retrieval, BERTScore, cosine similarity, clustering, and majority voting to improve evidence grounding and consistency. When candidate answers conflict, the DISC module generates clarification questions, retrieves additional evidence, and refines the answer under bounded iteration rules.

4 Experiment / 4.1 Datasets .. 4.3 Experimental setting

Xu et al. evaluate methods for answering public health queries using HealthQuestDB, a benchmark of 890 question-answer pairs curated from PubMed and WHO IRIS sources. HealthQuestDB covers epidemiology, disease prevention, treatment guidelines, healthcare policy, and related topics, and is used exclusively for evaluation rather than model training. The study separates the evaluation test set from the external knowledge base to avoid direct answer overlap and evaluates five methods using accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. MEGA-RAG achieves the highest reported accuracy and F1-score among the compared methods.

5 Results .. 5.3 Evaluating hallucinations in diﬀerent

Xu et al. evaluate MEGA-RAG against PubMedBERT, PubMedGPT, LLM + RAG, and a generic LLM on public health tasks, focusing on accuracy and hallucination reduction. MEGA-RAG achieves the highest reported accuracy (0.7913) and F1 Score (0.7904), outperforming PubMedGPT and standard LLM + RAG. Confusion matrix analysis shows that MEGA-RAG achieves a stronger false-positive and false-negative balance than LLM and LLM + RAG. In a case study about whether silver ion water can prevent or treat viral infections, MEGA-RAG produces a factual response aligned with expert public health advisories and warns against using colloidal or ionic silver for antiviral purposes.

6 Conclusion

Xu et al. evaluate MEGA-RAG for public health question answering on HealthQuestDB, covering epidemiological, preventive, treatment, and policy-related queries. MEGA-RAG integrates multi-source biomedical evidence retrieval with hallucination mitigation and outperforms generic and domain-adapted baselines on accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score. Its modular workflow supports traceable evidence provenance and produces responses aligned with public health best practices. Future work includes evaluation on MedQA and PubMedQA, dynamic guideline ingestion, multilingual deployment, clinician feedback, real-time epidemiological data, and more efficient deployment.

7 Limitations

MEGA-RAG uses a multi-stage design with multiple retrieval and large language model calls, creating additional computational overhead compared with vanilla RAG. The design aims to improve accuracy, evidence alignment, and robustness in high-stakes biomedical/public-health QA, where latency increases of typically seconds are considered acceptable. The overhead mainly comes from multi-source retrieval and reranking, which are readily parallelizable and do not introduce orders-of-magnitude delays. The unit notes that a detailed efficiency benchmark was not provided and identifies scalability and system-level optimization as areas for future work.
