Abstract

The unit concerns OG-RAG, a method for improving large language models on specialized, knowledge-driven tasks. It uses domain-specific ontologies to organize document knowledge into a hypergraph and retrieves a minimal set of factual hyperedges for each query. Compared with baseline methods, OG-RAG reportedly improves accurate-fact recall, response correctness, attribution speed, and fact-based reasoning across four large language models.

Introduction

The introduction describes how large language models struggle with precise, domain-specific data and fact-based adaptation in specialized workflows. It presents Ontology-Grounded Retrieval Augmented Generation (OG-RAG), which uses domain-specific ontologies and hypergraph representations to improve retrieval, factual deduction, and response attribution. OG-RAG retrieves a minimal set of related hyperedges to provide compact context to an LLM. Evaluations in agriculture and news report gains in fact recall, response correctness, attribution speed and quality, and fact-based reasoning.

Related Work

This unit reviews retrieval methods, domain adaptation for RAG, attribution, and deductive reasoning. It contrasts traditional retrieval augmented generation with graph-based approaches such as GraphRAG and RAPTOR, noting their reliance on ad-hoc extraction or complete knowledge graphs. It presents OG-RAG as an approach that uses domain experts-defined semantic schema and domain ontologies to ground unstructured documents for domain-specific retrieval without retraining. It also describes how OG-RAG supports interpretable attribution and combines fact-based reasoning with the flexibility of LLMs.

Background

The unit defines an ontology as a set of triples that represents entities, attributes, and values, where values may be other entities or unspecified domain values. It explains how ontology-mapped data is extracted from domain-specific documents and organized into self-contained factual-blocks that preserve contextual differences. The OG-RAG approach maps documents to an ontology, generates JSON-LD with GPT-4o, and uses a flattened hypergraph to answer queries such as which Soybean seeds are grown in Madhya Pradesh. The example returns JS335 and JS 95-60 as the relevant seed varieties.

OG-RAG .. Relevant Hyeredges as Context

Ontology-Grounded Retrieval Augmented Generation (OG-RAG) integrates ontologies into retrieval to adapt large language models to structured knowledge bases and domain-specific queries. It maps documents onto an ontology, converts the information into flattened factual blocks, and represents them as a hypergraph of hypernodes and hyperedges. For a query, OG-RAG selects relevant hypernodes using similarity between the query and node keys or values, then greedily retrieves hyperedges that cover those nodes. The resulting retrieved context is a compact collection of ontology-grounded facts for supporting accurate responses.

Experimental Setup

The experimental setup evaluates OG-RAG on specialized industrial workflows in the agriculture domain and knowledge work in the news domain. The agriculture evaluation uses 85 expert-prepared documents about Soybean and Wheat cultivation in India, while the news evaluation uses a Multi-hop RAG dataset to create a hard subset of RAGAS reasoning questions. OG-RAG is compared with RAG, RAPTOR, and GraphRAG using GPT-4o-mini, GPT-4o, Llama-3.1-8B, and Llama-3.1-70B for zero-shot question answering. Retrieval quality and generated responses are assessed with RAGAS metrics, including Context Recall, Context Entity Recall, Answer Similarity, Answer Correctness, and Answer Relevance.

Experiments / Query answering .. Ontology sensitivity

The unit evaluates OG-RAG for query answering, context attribution, factual deduction, efficiency, and ontology sensitivity across Soybean, Wheat, and News datasets. Using questions generated with RAGAS, it reports that OG-RAG generally improves context recall, answer correctness, answer relevance, and human verification support compared with RAG, RAPTOR, and GraphRAG, while adding limited query time. A human study found that participants verified OG-RAG responses faster and attributed greater factual support to their contexts. Additional experiments show that OG-RAG supports deductive reasoning from predefined agricultural rules and remains effective until approximately half of the ontology information is dropped.

Conclusion

The conclusion presents OG-RAG as a novel hypergraph-based retrieval method for domain adaptation of LLMs using ontology-grounded retrieval-augmented generation. Experiments on agriculture and news datasets indicate that OG-RAG improves factual accuracy, accelerates answer attribution, and strengthens rule-based reasoning. The method is described as applicable to healthcare, law, agriculture, journalism, and research, while offering greater control over context retrieval and use. Future work may investigate automated or semi-automated ontology construction for end-to-end frameworks.

Limitations

The unit describes limitations concerning ontology creation and a human experiment. Effective domain adaptation and retrieval require domains to have well-defined ontologies, which may be unavailable in newer and fast-developing domains such as technology. The hypergraph-based retrieval strategy benefits from existing domain ontologies and is reported to answer domain-specific questions accurately and succinctly when such ontologies are available. The human experiment involved 16 university-educated participants aged 18–34 who were familiar with LLMs, providing targeted evidence about context attribution.
