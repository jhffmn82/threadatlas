Abstract written by a model

Published in PLOS Digital Health on June 11, 2025, this systematic review assesses retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) for large language models in healthcare. Using PRISMA, it reviews English-language full-text articles and eligible arXiv or medRxiv archives published from January 2020 through February 2025, while excluding reviews and short conference papers. It synthesizes 70 studies of approaches that ground LLMs in medical information, examining datasets, retrieval strategies, augmentation and generation, medical question answering, information retrieval, and evaluation benchmarks. The review finds that 69 studies assessed pipeline or output accuracy, but that standardized evaluation remains limited. Its central concern is the gap between accuracy-focused assessment and ethical evaluation: relatively few studies address privacy, safety, or bias, leaving substantial room for improvement. Abstract

Retrieval augmented generation for large language models in healthcare: A review assesses datasets, RAG methodologies, and evaluation frameworks for RAG-based approaches used by Large Language Models (LLMs) in healthcare. It identifies strengths, limitations, and gaps in the literature, including limited standardized evaluation and insufficient attention to ethical considerations.

Methods .. Exclusion criteria

The RAG in healthcare review investigates the state of the art in retrieval-augmented generation for large language models in healthcare. It includes English-language articles published between January 2020 and February 2025, including archives with an arXiv ID or medRxiv ID, and excludes review articles, papers without full text, and short conference papers.

Results / Included studies .. Datasets

The review concerns innovative RAG-based approaches that ground large language models in medical information. It covers 70 studies from 2020–2025 and discusses datasets, retrieval strategies, medical QA, information retrieval, and evaluation benchmarks.

Ethical considerations

The review included 70 studies, of which 69 assessed the accuracy of their RAG pipelines and generated outputs. It found that relatively few studies addressed privacy, safety, or bias, and it identified substantial room for improvement in the ethical evaluation of RAG-based healthcare LLMs.
