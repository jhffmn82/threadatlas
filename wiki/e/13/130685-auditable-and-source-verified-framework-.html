  • In An auditable and source-verified framework for clinical AI decision support: integrating retrieval-augmented generation with data provenance: This is a conceptual, future-oriented framework for auditable and source-verified AI-based clinical decision support. Grounded in evidence-based medicine, data provenance, trustworthy AI, and verified sources for factual claims, it synthesizes existing technical approaches rather than presenting a new algorithm or prototype. Its central aim is to improve traceability, clinician trust, and regulatory readiness while promoting transparency, reliability, user control, source verification, auditability, and safety monitoring. The design begins with a Data and Knowledge Layer: a curated, comprehensive, up-to-date medical knowledge base with explicit provenance metadata. A Retrieval and Reasoning Engine then constrains reasoning to verified retrieved sources, anchoring probabilistic model behavior to a deterministic evidence base. It attaches reference IDs to answer points and addresses citation fidelity. Finally, a User Interface with Audit Logging presents clinician-centered annotated clinical guidelines and records retrieval, reasoning, and decision pathways in tamper-evident, inspectable logs for accountability, investigation, and monitoring. The framework’s main unresolved issue is knowledge-base governance and updating; it also identifies bias, latency, usability, privacy, and regulatory alignment as concerns. It has not been implemented, tested, or empirically validated, so its practical effectiveness is unknown. Its planned path forward includes implementation, clinical integration, staged simulation studies, clinician-centered user research, broader evaluations, and continuous monitoring.

This is a conceptual, future-oriented framework for auditable and source-verified AI-based clinical decision support. Grounded in evidence-based medicine, data provenance, trustworthy AI, and verified sources for factual claims, it synthesizes existing technical approaches rather than presenting a new algorithm or prototype. Its central aim is to improve traceability, clinician trust, and regulatory readiness while promoting transparency, reliability, user control, source verification, auditability, and safety monitoring. The design begins with a Data and Knowledge Layer: a curated, comprehensive, up-to-date medical knowledge base with explicit provenance metadata. A Retrieval and Reasoning Engine then constrains reasoning to verified retrieved sources, anchoring probabilistic model behavior to a deterministic evidence base. It attaches reference IDs to answer points and addresses citation fidelity. Finally, a User Interface with Audit Logging presents clinician-centered annotated clinical guidelines and records retrieval, reasoning, and decision pathways in tamper-evident, inspectable logs for accountability, investigation, and monitoring. The framework’s main unresolved issue is knowledge-base governance and updating; it also identifies bias, latency, usability, privacy, and regulatory alignment as concerns. It has not been implemented, tested, or empirically validated, so its practical effectiveness is unknown. Its planned path forward includes implementation, clinical integration, staged simulation studies, clinician-centered user research, broader evaluations, and continuous monitoring.
