Clinical decision-support systems for chronic low back pain have traditionally used static decision trees or rules, emphasizing biomedical factors and offering limited integration of multi-source data, expert-like reasoning, and personalized advice. Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

1 Introduction

Decision-supporting systems for chronic low back pain have explored drug therapy, physical therapy, psychological intervention, and other treatment strategies. Existing systems have limited ability to integrate multi-source data, simulate expert decision-making, and provide personalized advice, while patients report dissatisfaction with contradictory and biased information from medical professionals.

5 Discussion

Clinical decision-support systems are described as traditionally relying on static decision trees or rule-based methods that have limited ability to handle complicated CLBP cases. The unit argues that systems incorporating broader patient variables, dynamic evidence retrieval, and auditable reasoning can provide more comprehensive and personalized recommendations.
