AI-CDS in An auditable and source-verified framework for clinical AI decision support: integrating retrieval-augmented generation with data provenance

Fidelis Fidelis Alu and Sunkanmi Oluwadare · 2026-02-04 · the work’s page · read it where AI-CDS first appears

Abstract written by a model

AI-CDS is a proposed theoretical design for clinical decision support, not an implemented prototype or new algorithm. It aims to improve diagnostic accuracy, tailor treatment recommendations, and help clinicians quickly find relevant medical information. Its central features are source verification, provenance metadata, retrieval, explicit links between recommendations and medical evidence, and logging of all reasoning steps for auditability. The account begins with expected clinical benefits, contrasts earlier inspectable rule-based systems with current risks such as probabilistic reasoning, hallucination, citation instability, and transparency needs, and concludes with an auditable, source-verified framework.

AI-powered clinical decision support is presented as promising for diagnosis, treatment customization, and clinical information retrieval. The proposed framework seeks to make such support source-verified and auditable.

2 Background and related work

Earlier clinical decision support systems used curated, deterministic rules that could be manually inspected and reconstructed. Current systems must additionally address probabilistic reasoning, hallucination, citation instability, transparency, validation, and auditability.

AI-CDSS tool in An auditable and source-verified framework for clinical AI decision support: integrating retrieval-augmented generation with data provenance

Fidelis Fidelis Alu and Sunkanmi Oluwadare · 2026-02-04 · the work’s page · read it where AI-CDSS tool first appears

Abstract written by a model

AI-CDSS is an envisioned, not yet implemented or tested, trustworthy clinical decision support tool. It provides patient-specific recommendations from curated evidence, with identifiable supporting sources, claim tracing, and retrospective decision examination. For each recommendation workflow, it records every processing stage and presents linked references in a user-friendly format, aiming to earn clinicians’ trust.

The AI-CDSS is designed to provide patient-specific recommendations backed by identifiable sources, record each processing stage, and present outputs in a user-friendly format. Its outputs are generated from curated evidence and linked to supporting references.

5 Conclusion

The AI-CDSS tool is envisioned as a system that clinicians might genuinely trust because every claim can be traced to its origin and every decision can be retrospectively examined. The proposed design has not yet been implemented or tested.
