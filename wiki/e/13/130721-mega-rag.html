MEGA-RAG in MEGA-RAG: a retrieval-augmented generation framework with multi-evidence guided answer refinement for mitigating hallucinations of LLMs in public health

Shan Xu, Zhaokun Yan, Chengxiao Dai and Fan Wu · 2025-10-08

MEGA-RAG is a retrieval-augmented generation framework and proposed method for public health applications, designed to mitigate hallucinations by large language models. It was developed in response to a lack of comprehensive hallucination-mitigation methods specifically for public health. Its central feature is multi-evidence guided answer refinement: it brings together dense retrieval through FAISS, keyword-based retrieval through BM25, biomedical knowledge graphs for multi-source evidence retrieval, cross-encoder reranking, and discrepancy-aware answer refinement. Implemented with LLaMA3-70B, the framework unfolds as a multi-stage design that retrieves and organizes evidence from these sources, reranks it, and refines answers using detected discrepancies. The intended result is factually reliable, medically accurate responses, improving accuracy, evidence alignment, and robustness for high-stakes biomedical and public-health question answering. In the reported evaluation, MEGA-RAG outperformed PubMedBERT, PubMedGPT, and a standard retrieval-augmented generation baseline, reduced LLM hallucinations by over 40%, and had the highest reported accuracy (0.7913), precision (0.7541), recall (0.8304), and F1 score (0.7904) among evaluated models. It may support clinical communication, health education, and evidence-based policy development. The framework does not provide a detailed efficiency benchmark and does not make computational efficiency its core focus; future work is identified to improve responsiveness through system-level optimizations. It is the subject of an open-access Original Research article in Frontiers in Public Health, published on 08 October 2025 under CC BY.

MEGA-RAG is a retrieval-augmented generation framework designed for public health applications. It combines dense retrieval via FAISS, keyword-based retrieval via BM25, biomedical knowledge graphs, cross-encoder reranking, and discrepancy-aware answer refinement. It outperformed four baseline models and achieved the highest reported accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score.

1 Introduction

MEGA-RAG was developed in response to the lack of comprehensive methods specifically targeting hallucination mitigation in public health.

4 Experiment / 4.1 Datasets .. 4.3 Experimental setting

MEGA-RAG is included as the study's proposed method and is evaluated under the same conditions as the other methods. It achieves the highest reported accuracy at 0.7913 and an F1-score of 0.7904, with recall of 0.8304, and it is implemented using LLaMA3-70B.

7 Limitations

MEGA-RAG demonstrates strong methodological performance through a multi-stage design involving multiple retrieval and large language model calls. Its design aims to improve accuracy, evidence alignment, and robustness in high-stakes biomedical/public-health QA, while future work should improve its responsiveness through system-level optimizations.

MEGA-RAG in MEGA-RAG: a retrieval-augmented generation framework with multi-evidence guided answer refinement for mitigating hallucinations of LLMs in public health

Shan Xu, Zhaokun Yan, Chengxiao Dai and Fan Wu · 2025-10-08

MEGA-RAG is a modular retrieval-augmented generation and question-answering framework for biomedical and public health domains, designed to mitigate hallucinations, improve decision accuracy, and provide traceable evidence provenance. Its central features are structured multi-source evidence retrieval, precision-focused hallucination mitigation, targeted knowledge refinement, and transparency through retrieval and editing stages. It unfolds in four stages: Multi-Source Evidence Retrieval, using vector embedding search, lexical ranking, knowledge-graph extraction, cross-encoder re-ranking, and weighted entailment scoring; Diverse Prompted Answer Generation; Semantic-Evidential Alignment Evaluation; and Discrepancy-Identified Self-Clarification with dynamic knowledge editing, followed by final answer fusion. Evaluated on HealthQuestDB, it outperforms generic and domain-adapted baselines across accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score, achieving 0.7913 accuracy and 0.7904 F1, with 114 false positives and 71 false negatives. It maintains recall comparable to human annotators and human-level recall on disease-related queries while enhancing precision to reduce unsupported claims. It can support outbreak surveillance, rapid clinical decision support, and health misinformation countermeasures. Future work includes MedQA and PubMedQA evaluation, health-agency guideline ingestion, multilingual and cross-regional infoveillance, clinician-in-the-loop feedback, real-time epidemiological data, and efficient deployment in field clinics and mobile health units.
