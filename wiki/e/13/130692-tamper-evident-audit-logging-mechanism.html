The tamper-evident audit logging mechanism is a secure audit trail within the proposed framework. It records system inputs, patient-data elements as cryptographic hashes, retrieved evidence and document identifiers, inference-chain information, final-output identifiers, timestamps, and user and session identifiers. By preserving the retrieval and decision pathway, it enables retrospective reconstruction of what information the AI used and how a recommendation or final suggestion was derived. It supports transparency, integrity, traceability, accountability, auditing, and review by clinicians, hospitals, regulators, or auditors, and can help track performance and investigate adverse events. Extensive logging may also create latency, usability, privacy, and security concerns.

front matter .. An auditable and source-verified framework for clinical AI decision support: integrating retrieval-augmented generation

The tamper-evident audit logging mechanism records system inputs, retrieved evidence, and inference steps for retrospective review.

1 Introduction

A secure audit trail is proposed to record which patient data and references were used and how the final suggestion was derived. This record supports transparency, accountability, and retrospective review.

2 Background and related work

Audit logs record system events such as data reads and writes, inferences, and user actions with timestamps and user IDs. In the proposed framework, they document the retrieval and decision pathway and can support review by clinicians, hospitals, regulators, or auditors.

3 Methods (framework design) .. 3.3.4 Step 4: Forensic audit logging

The audit log records timestamps, user and session identifiers, the original query, accessed patient-data elements represented by cryptographic hashes, retrieved document identifiers, inference-chain information, and final-output identifiers. It is designed to support retrospective reconstruction of what information the AI used and how a recommendation was produced.

4 Discussion .. 4.2 Bias mitigation and evidence equity

Audit logging is proposed as a mechanism for tracking system performance, investigating adverse events, and showing which information influenced a decision. The unit notes that extensive logging may introduce latency, usability burdens, and privacy and security concerns.
