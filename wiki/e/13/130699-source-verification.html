source verification in An auditable and source-verified framework for clinical AI decision support: integrating retrieval-augmented generation with data provenance

Fidelis Fidelis Alu and Sunkanmi Oluwadare · 2026-02-04

Source verification is a foundational Evidence-Based Medicine principle and a core requirement for trustworthy clinical decision support. It requires each AI-generated claim to trace to an authoritative medical source, with exact citations to retrieved references. It unfolds by attaching explicit references to recommendations, letting clinicians inspect guideline excerpts or abstracts, and checking claims against cited passages. It connects recommendations to evidence and aims to reduce unsupported outputs, but cannot guarantee accuracy if the underlying evidence is flawed, outdated, or biased.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

1 Introduction

Source verification requires every claim in an AI output to trace back to an authoritative medical source. In the proposed framework, generated answers include exact citations to retrieved references.

2 Background and related work

Source verification has been a foundational principle of Evidence-Based Medicine since the 1990s. The unit presents it as a core architectural requirement for trustworthy clinical decision support rather than merely a user-interface feature.

3 Methods (framework design) .. 3.3.4 Step 4: Forensic audit logging

Source verification is supported by attaching explicit references to recommendations and allowing clinicians to access the underlying guideline excerpt or abstract. The framework also uses retrieved-passage verification to check whether generated claims are supported by cited text.

4 Discussion .. 4.2 Bias mitigation and evidence equity

Source verification is intended to connect recommendations to supporting evidence and reduce unsupported outputs. The unit cautions that it cannot ensure accuracy when the underlying evidence is flawed, outdated, or biased.
