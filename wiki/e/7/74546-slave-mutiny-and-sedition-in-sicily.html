slave mutiny and sedition in Sicily

Also called: an opportunity to rebel, another rebellion of the slaics, mutiny and sedition of the slaves in Sicily, rebel, slave revolt in Sicily

Abstract written by a model

A slave rebellion in Sicily, sparked by masters' growing cruelty and slaves' hatred, became a vast mutiny. It ransacked cities, caused robbery, murder, insecurity, and the collapse of law and justice, while seeking masters' destruction. With no Roman praetor, lawlessness spread across the island, nearly placing Sicily under slave control.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XXXIV.

A vast and unexpected slave mutiny broke out in Sicily, with many cities ransacked, countless people suffering, and the whole island nearly falling into the hands of the slaves. The revolt arose after growing cruelty by masters and hatred among the slaves.

BOOK XXXVI.

The slave revolt in Sicily expanded into widespread devastation, robbery, murder, and insecurity affecting both slaves and free people. The absence of a Roman praetor allowed lawlessness to spread through the cities, towns, countryside, and highways.
