Amilcar in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Amilcar first appears

Abstract written by a model

Amilcar is a Carthaginian commander and general whose records center on campaigns in Sicily and Spain and on the enlargement of Carthaginian power. Against Agathocles, he rebuilt his army in Sicily, defeated him at the river Himera, won several Sicilian cities, and marched on Syracuse. He tried to seize its walls, later besieged it by cutting supplies, but a night defeat led to his capture, torture, and killing by Syracusans; his head was sent to Agathocles. Other records place him in Sicily after Hanno the elder, defeating Romans at Thermæ, gaining places, fortifying Drepanum, and destroying Eryx except its temple area. He then advanced toward the Pillars of Hercules and Gades, subdued Iberians, Tartessians, and Celts, founded Acra Leuca, and gained many Spanish cities. His career ends in another account with drowning while fleeing across a river after raising the siege of Helice. He is regarded as among the greatest captains, with victories that greatly enlarged Carthaginian dominions.

Amilcar received orders to hasten from Sicily to assist the Carthaginians and used the beaks of Agathocles's ships to spread a false report that Agathocles's army and fleet had been destroyed. He marched against Syracuse, attempted to seize its walls, and withdrew after his soldiers were discovered and defeated; he then sent five thousand men to relieve Carthage.

CHAP. IL

As general of the Carthaginian forces in Sicily, he reduced much of the island and besieged Syracuse by cutting off its supplies. The Syracusans defeated his army at night, captured him, and delivered him to people who tortured and killed him; his head was then sent to Agathocles.

BOOK XXIIL

Amilcar was sent to Sicily as general in place of Hanno the elder. He defeated the Romans at Thermæ, captured or received several places through betrayal, strengthened Drepanum, and destroyed Eryx except for the part surrounding its temple.

vooKxxiy.

Amilcar expanded Carthaginian power toward the Pillars of Hercules and Gades, subdued Iberians, Tartessians, and Celts, and gained many cities in Spain. He founded Acra Leuca and was drowned while fleeing across a river after raising the siege of Helice.

BOOK XXIII.

Amilcar, called Barcas, is described as one of the greatest generals to have appeared before or after his time. His victories greatly enlarged the dominions of the Carthaginians.

Amilcar, a Carthaginian general, was known before appointment for magnanimity. As general he pursued fame, despised danger, and showed prudence, courage, and industry, becoming considered the best king and most valiant general before being imprisoned with Bostar's corpse.
