marriage customs of the Cathari

topic · 1 work · 3 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Indian widow-burning law, another law, the common law of the land, the law, this law

Abstract written by a model

The records describe an Indian widow-burning law: after wives allegedly poisoned husbands, it required a widow’s self-immolation with her deceased husband. Pregnant widows and those who had borne children were excepted. A widow who refused remained a widow and lost sacred rites and legal privileges. The law is characterized as cruel and inhuman.

The Indian widow-burning law required a wife to be burned with her deceased husband, except when she was pregnant or had borne children. A woman who refused was to remain a widow and be excluded from sacred rites and other legal privileges. The law was described as having been introduced after wives had poisoned husbands, though the text also calls it cruel and inhuman.
