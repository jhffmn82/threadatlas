Agis in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Agis, king and general of the Lacedaemonians and their confederates, fought with valor, suffered many wounds, ordered his soldiers to save themselves, and died in battle after a dart shot through his body.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAP. VI.

Agis was made general of the Lacedaemonians and their confederates. He fought with great valor and resolution, received many wounds, ordered his soldiers to leave him and preserve themselves for future service, and died fighting after being shot through the body with a dart.

Agis in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Agis, king of Sparta and son of Eudamidas, attacked Megalopolis with a larger, better-equipped army, commanded the Lacedæmonians, then was surrounded and fell in battle.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER X.

Agis commanded the Lacedæmonians and occupied the centre with his staff. He pressed hard against Aratus’s division but was surrounded with his army and fell in the battle.
