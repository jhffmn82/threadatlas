Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Agis, king and general of the Lacedaemonians and their confederates, fought with valor, suffered many wounds, ordered his soldiers to save themselves, and died in battle after a dart shot through his body. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Agis, king of Sparta and son of Eudamidas, attacked Megalopolis with a larger, better-equipped army, commanded the Lacedæmonians, and fell in battle after being surrounded.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

CHAP. VI.

Agis was made general of the Lacedaemonians and their confederates. He fought with great valor and resolution, received many wounds, ordered his soldiers to leave him and preserve themselves for future service, and died fighting after being shot through the body with a dart.
