the Peloponnesian War in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Peloponnesian War was a war between Athens and Lacedaemon, their allies, and opposing confederates. It began after disputes involving Megara and wider rivalry between Athens and Sparta. Its course included invasions of Attica and Peloponnesus; the siege of Potidea; naval fighting at Rhion; Athenian campaigns in Sicily and Lesbos; the destruction of Plataea; plague and famine at Athens; fighting across western Greece, Boeotia, Megara, and Thrace; the fortification of Pylos; capture of the Sphacterian prisoners; Brasidas’s expansion around Amphipolis; and the Boeotian victory at Delium. After ten years, Athens and Lacedaemon made a fifty-year peace requiring prisoners’ release and restoration of captured cities, but conflict later renewed amid shifting alliances, with Athens preparing war against the Syracusans in Sicily. The final phase brought political change at Athens, naval battle between Sestos and Abydos, and weakening on both sides. It ended when the Athenian fleet was defeated, Athens was blockaded, and the city surrendered. The records variously give durations of twenty-two and twenty-seven years. It caused many murders and lamentable bloodshed, and left Lacedaemon sovereign by sea and land, able to organize governments in Greek cities.
