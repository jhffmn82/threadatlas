Mamertines in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Mamertines were foreign mercenary soldiers who, after being received by the Messenians as friends and confederates, murdered their hosts, took their wives, seized Messana, and named it Mamertina after Mamers. They hired Decius, allied with the Carthaginians against Pyrrhus, were defeated by Hiero near Lotanus, and later made a league with Hannibal, who reinforced Messana.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XXI,

The foreign soldiers who seized Messana called the city Mamertina, taking the name from Mars, whom they called Mamers in their own country language.

BOOK XXII.

The Mamertines had treacherously murdered the inhabitants of Messana and later allied themselves with the Carthaginians against Pyrrhus. Hiero defeated their army near the river Lotanus, killing nearly all of them and capturing their general Cion. Hannibal then made a league with them and reinforced Messana with soldiers.

FROM THE SAME..

The Mamertines were mercenary soldiers who, after agreeing with the Syracusans, were received by the Messenians as friends and confederates. They then murdered their hosts, took their wives, and seized the city.
