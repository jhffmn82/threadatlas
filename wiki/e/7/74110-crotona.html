Crotona in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Crotona is a place home to Pythagoreans during Pythagoras’s lifetime, whose Crotonians revered him as a god; Cylo’s rejection by them bred hostility, and Agathocles later deceived, besieged, breached, plundered, and killed its men.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XXI,

Agathocles deceived Crotona by claiming that he was sending his daughter Lanassa to Epirus to marry. He besieged the city, breached it through mines and batteries, and after its gates were opened his soldiers plundered the houses and killed the men.

3G. When Thericles was chief magistrate at Athens in the sixty^

The Crotonians revered Pythagoras as if he were a god. One wealthy citizen of Crotona, Cylo, became hostile to the Pythagoreans after they rejected him.

Crotona in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Crotona, a populous city, sheltered Sybarite exiles at its altars; urged by Pythagoras, its hesitant people fought and defeated Sybaris, then expelled the Thessalians who rebuilt it.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

agreed on upon certain conditions. Whilst things were thus acting

Crotona received the Sybarite exiles who sought refuge at its altars. Although its people initially hesitated to protect them against Sybaris, they decided to fight after Pythagoras advised them to defend the suppliants. Crotona defeated Sybaris and later expelled the Thessalians who had rebuilt the city.

Crotona in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Crotona is a place in Italy, founded by Myscelos during Numa Pompilius’s fourth year near Æsar’s destined mouth, and named for Croton, whose sacred bones lie beneath a mound.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK THE FIFTEENTH.

Crotona is founded by Myscelos in Italy at the place where the sacred bones of Croton are buried. The city receives the name of the person entombed beneath the nearby mound.
