the Wall of Solid Air in The Tin Woodman of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1918

The Wall of Solid Air is an invisible, circular barrier around Nimmie Amee's house, six feet thick and perhaps a mile high. It keeps strangers and people larger than the Blue Rabbit from reaching the house, though the rabbit's burrow passes beneath it. After the storm, when Polychrome departs, it would imprison Scarecrow, Tin Woodman, and Tin Soldier if they remained inside. The travelers escape by using the Blue Rabbit's burrow to reach the outside.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Twenty-One

The Wall of Solid Air is invisible, about six feet thick, and perhaps a mile high. It forms a circle around Nimmie Amee's house and prevents people larger than the Blue Rabbit from reaching it, although the rabbit's burrow passes underneath.

Chapter Twenty-Three

The Wall of Solid Air threatens to trap the travelers if they remain inside it until after the storm and Polychrome leaves. The group reaches the outside of the wall by traveling through the Blue Rabbit's burrow.
