Lysimachus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Lysimachus is a king and military ruler whose career centers on Thrace, repeated wars, shifting alliances, and expansion into Asia. He begins as a fighter close to Alexander: he killed a lion in Syria with his own hand, suffered a left shoulder torn to the bone, and was later restrained from attacking another lion with Alexander’s hunting spear. In Thrace he invaded, fought Seuthes while badly outnumbered, withdrew after heavy losses nearly victorious, and later campaigned against rebels, taking Odessus and the Istrian city, defeating Scythians, besieging Callantinians, fighting through Seuthes’s force at Mount Haemus, and killing Pausanias. He founded Lysimachia in Chersonesus and held chief command in Thrace under a peace with Cassander, Ptolemy, and Antigonus. His position remained contested: he faced revolts, lost the Callantinian garrison, was defeated while trying to relieve Antipater, and was captured by Dromichaetes, who treated him courteously and sought the return of Thracian forts and castles. Lysimachus repeatedly joined Cassander, Ptolemy, and Seleucus against Antigonus, though he also made peace with Antigonus. He entered Asia, freed Lampsacus and Paros, captured Sigeum and territory in the Hellespont and Phrygia, sent Prepelaus against Aeolia and Ionia, and avoided battle before wintering. Near Heraclea he joined Seleucus for a future campaign despite desertions to Antigonus. He also supported the Rhodians with wheat and barley and was related to Stratonice through Antiochus.

Lysimachus invaded parts of Thrace and fought Seuthes despite being greatly outnumbered. After heavy losses on both sides, he withdrew to his camp nearly victorious and prepared for another engagement.

Lysimachus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Lysimachus, a Macedonian and Alexander the Great’s armour-bearer, ruled Thrace after Alexander’s death, fought several rivals, and eventually ruled Macedonia. Though father of Agathocles and husband of Arsinoe, he permitted Agathocles’ execution, then was defeated and killed by Seleucus I.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VII.

Lysimachus ruled Thracian territory after Alexander the Great’s death, fought the Odrysæ, Dromichetes, Demetrius I Poliorcetes, and Pyrrhus of Epirus, and eventually ruled Macedonia. He married Arsinoe, permitted Agathocles’ execution, was defeated by Seleucus I, and was killed in the battle.
