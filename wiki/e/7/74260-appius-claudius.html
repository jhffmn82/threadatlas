Appius Claudius in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Appius Claudius is a censor at Rome whose tenure with Caius Plautius reshapes laws, civic arrangements, and public works. He abrogates ancient laws, disregards the senate to favor the people, admits libertines to the senate, enables citizen choices, supports Caius Flavius, brings in the Appia water supply, and builds much of the Appian Way before leaving office under claimed blindness.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Having now gone through the affairs of Africa and Sicily, we

Appius Claudius, serving as censor with Caius Plautius, abrogated many ancient laws, disregarded the senate to gratify the people, brought the Appia water supply into Rome, and constructed much of the Appian Way. He admitted libertines to the senate, allowed citizens to choose their tribes and tax ranks, and supported Caius Flavius's advancement; after leaving office, he kept his house under the pretense of blindness.
