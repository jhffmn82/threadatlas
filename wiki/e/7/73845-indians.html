Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Indians possessed and drove elephants into battle, defended Aornos, fought under Porus, resisted in towns and from rooftops, and wounded Alexander before his rescue. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Indians are said to report that tigers have three rows of teeth and tails with hurled stings, though the speaker considers them mistaken about the tiger's color.

Indians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Indians first appears

Abstract written by a model

Indians possessed and drove elephants into battle. They defended Aornos but fled at night; others killed Aphrices and entered Alexander’s protection. Fighting under Porus, they suffered over twelve thousand dead and over nine thousand captured. They later resisted in towns and from rooftops, and at the Oxydracee and Mallii capital wounded Alexander before his troops rescued him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. IX.

The Indians defending Aornos were frightened by the Macedonian assault and abandoned the rock during the night. Elsewhere, Indians killed Aphrices, brought his head to Alexander, and were received into his protection.

CHAP. X.

The Indians fought Alexander under Porus and lost more than twelve thousand men, including two sons of Porus, army generals, and leading commanders. More than nine thousand Indians were taken prisoner.

After the king had moved from thence, he was informed that

Indians resisted Alexander in several towns and fought from rooftops during the capture of a city. During the siege of the Oxydracee and Mallii capital, they surrounded and wounded Alexander before his troops rescued him.

Indians in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Indians first appears

Abstract written by a model

Indians are said to report that the tiger has three rows of teeth in each jaw and a tail with stings that it can hurl at a distance. The speaker believes they accept this account because of their excessive terror of the animal and says they are also mistaken about its color.
