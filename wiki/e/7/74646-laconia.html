Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Laconia is an ancient Spartan country invaded and devastated by an allied army. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Laconia was invaded and ravaged by Tolmides and the Athenians. In Description of Greece, Laconia is a land and coastal region whose history, settlements, landmarks, and conflicts are described. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Laconia was overrun and plundered throughout by the Theban allies.

Laconia in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Laconia first appears

Abstract written by a model

Laconia is a land and coastal region west of the Hermæ, formerly called Lelegia under Lelex. Its earliest rulers included Lelex, Myles, Eurotas, and Lacedæmon, followed later by Sparta’s kings. Lacedæmon first named the whole district, then the city originally called Sparta, whose government, market-place, temples, tombs, roads, and settlements are described. The region’s bays, towns, promontories, temples, and rivers are traced along its coast. Its border with Messenia was fixed at Gerenia by Augustus. Laconia supplies an invading army, suffers raids and plundering in conflict with Messenia, and is later connected with Lycurgus and war with Elis.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK III.--LACONIA.

Laconia is described as lying west of the Hermæ. Its early rulers included Lelex, Myles, Eurotas, Lacedæmon, and later the kings of Sparta.

CHAPTER X.

Lacedæmon was originally the name of the whole district and later became the name of the city originally called Sparta. The account of Lacedæmon includes its government, market-place, temples, royal tombs, roads, and surrounding settlements.

CHAPTER XXIII.

Laconia is the region whose coastal places and traditions are described throughout the unit. Its bays, towns, promontories, temples, and rivers are repeatedly identified in relation to the surrounding coastline.

BOOK IV.--MESSENIA.

Laconia was formerly called Lelegia under Lelex, and its border with Messenia was fixed at Gerenia by Augustus. Polycaon and Messene gathered an army from Laconia and Argos to invade the country that became Messenia.

CHAPTER XVI.

Aristomenes raids Laconia, including Pharis, and carries off booty before defeating Lacedæmonian troops on the return journey. The Messenians later plunder Laconia from Eira, prompting the Lacedæmonians to prohibit cultivation in Messenia and neighboring parts of Laconia.

BOOK V.--ELIS.

Lacedæmon is associated with Lycurgus, whose legislation was contemporary with Iphitus. The unit also refers to a later war between Elis and the Lacedæmonians, whose settlement had been described earlier.
