Greek common liberty in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Greek common liberty first appears

Abstract written by a model

Greek common liberty is the political right of cities to govern by their ancient laws. After the Lacedaemonians lost sovereignty and the Thebans became chief commanders of Greece, Athens presented this liberty as its cause against the Macedonians, pledging navies, lives, and fortunes. Antigonus and Ptolemy proclaimed city freedom to win Greek goodwill; Antigonus ordered garrisons withdrawn, cities freed, and ancient laws restored across several regions, though the policy also served his conflict with Cassander.

The Lacedaemonians lost their sovereignty of Greece after their defeats at Leuctra and Mantinea. The Thebans then became chief commanders of Greece, while the Lacedaemonians failed to recover their former glory and dignity.

The Greek common liberty was presented by Athens as the cause for opposing the Macedonians. Athens claimed that it would defend this liberty with its navies, lives, and fortunes.

CHAP. IV,

Antigonus proclaimed that the Greek cities should be free and ordered Cassander to withdraw his garrisons from them. Ptolemy made a similar declaration, since both rulers believed that gaining the goodwill of the Greeks was important to their affairs.

THE affiiirs of this year being brought to this conclusion^ Theo->

Antigonus repeatedly presented the restoration of Greek city liberties as his policy and ordered commanders to free cities and restore their ancient laws. His forces freed cities in Peloponnesus, Asia, Euboea, Boeotia, and Phocis, though the policy also served his struggle against Cassander.
