Rhegium in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Rhegium is a city in Sicily, first held by Dionysius the younger’s garrison and governed by Decius. It favors Timoleon against the Carthaginians, is occupied by Campanian soldiers who murder inhabitants, then sees Decius expelled and blinded. Nearly surprised and besieged by Dionysius, it surrenders; Philo and his son suffer cruelty.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. XI.

Rhegium's people favored Timoleon and promised to join him against the Carthaginians. They prolonged an assembly with speeches while Timoleon secretly escaped from the port.

FROM THE SAME..

Rhegium was occupied by Campanian soldiers who murdered its inhabitants and seized their property. Decius was later expelled from the city, and a physician from Rhegium took revenge on him by blinding him.

BOOK XIV.

Rhegium was nearly surprised and later besieged by Dionysius. It eventually surrendered, and Philo, its governor, and his son were treated cruelly.

Rhegium in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Rhegium was first the name of the place where the narrow neck connecting Sicily to the continent was said to have been severed; many years later a city was built there. A key of Italy, it supported Syracusan exiles and repeatedly opposed Dionysius, rejecting his marriage proposal, receiving his enemies, resisting an attack, and aiding against his fleet. After a truce, Dionysius imposed tribute, hostages, and the surrender of seventy ships, then besieged the city. Rhegium endured eleven months of siege and extreme famine before surrendering; many citizens were imprisoned, sold as slaves, or punished.

Rhegium was the name given to the place where the narrow neck connecting Sicily to the continent was said to have been severed. Many years later, a city called Rhegium was built there.

CHAP. VII.

Rhegium supported the Syracusan exiles when they attempted to campaign against Dionysius. Later, its council rejected Dionysius's proposal that it provide him with a woman of the city as a wife, despite his offer of land and assistance.

CHAP. IX,

Rhegium quarrelled with Dionysius because he fortified Messana and seemed to threaten the city. It received Dionysius's enemies, resisted his night attack, and later entered a one-year truce with him.

CHAP. XU.

Rhegium sent ships to assist the Thurians' allies and helped drive Dionysius's fleet from the shore, contributing to his loss of ships and men. Dionysius later forced Rhegium to agree to a tribute of three hundred talents, surrender seventy ships, and provide one hundred hostages, then besieged the city after seeking a pretext to break the agreement.

CHAP. XIII.

Rhegium endured an eleven-month siege by Dionysius and suffered extreme famine, eating horses, beasts of burden, boiled skins, leather, and grass. The citizens surrendered, after which many were taken prisoner, sold as slaves, or otherwise punished.
