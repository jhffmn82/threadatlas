the Golden Dragon in Toto's Merry Winter

Laura E. Richards · 1887

The Golden Dragon is the most sacred idol in China, a Fiery Idol worshipped in the Temple of the Saki-Pan. About ten feet long, it stands fifteen feet above the floor on its altar, made of diamond-tipped gold scales, with ruby eyes and an emerald crown. Its faithful servant Ly-Chee brings melons weekly. Chop-Chin steals it from the altar in a crimson sash, then uses it to terrify Emperor Wah-Song into pardoning and rewarding Ly-Chee. Found outside the temple, it is returned by priests, who say it went abroad for the city's welfare.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

" And without further preface, she began as follows:-- CHAPTER II.

The Golden Dragon is a magnificent idol about ten feet long, made of delicate gold scales tipped with diamonds, with ruby eyes and an emerald crown. Chop-Chin lifts it from its golden altar and carries it away wrapped in his crimson sash.

Ten o'clock of a dark night.

The Golden Dragon appears to Emperor Wah-Song in a terrifying vision, accusing him of condemning Ly-Chee and commanding him to reverse the punishment. After Chop-Chin's deception, it is found outside the Temple of the Saki-Pan and is carried back by the priests, who proclaim that it went abroad for the welfare of the city.
