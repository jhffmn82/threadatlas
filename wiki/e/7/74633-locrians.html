Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, the Locrians opposed Philomelus, suffered defeat, invasion, losses, and prisoners, sought Theban aid, and joined the Boeotians against the Phocians. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the inhabitants chose Zaleuchus as their law-maker and were required to believe in and worship the gods, keep their minds pure, and preserve reconciliation and friendship with their adversaries. In The Library, the Locrians returned to Locris after regaining their country, received an oracle to propitiate Athena at Ilium after a plague, and sent two maidens as suppliants for a thousand years.

Locrians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Locrians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Locrians, a Greek people including the Epicnemidii, opposed Philomelus after he seized the temple. Defeated near Delphos and at the Rocks of Phaedra, they suffered invasion, losses, and prisoners, then sought Theban aid and joined the Boeotians against the Phocians.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. VI.

The Locrians marched against Philomelus after he seized the temple but were defeated near Delphos. Philomelus later invaded and wasted their country, fought them again, and forced them to exchange the bodies of the dead.

CHAP. VII.

The Locrians fought Philomelus at the Rocks of Phaedra and were routed, with many killed and many taken prisoner. They later sought assistance from Thebes against the Phocians and joined the Boeotians in opposing them.
