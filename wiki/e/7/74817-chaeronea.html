Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Chaeronea was taken by Philip during his expansion in Greece. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Chaeronea was captured by Tolmides, where the Boeotians surprised the Athenian forces and won a bloody battle. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Chæronea is a town formerly called Arne and traditionally named for Chæron, son of Apollo and Thero; it is remembered for battles, makes flower-boiled unguents, lies below Petrachos, and begins routes into Phocis.

Chæronea in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Chæronea first appears

Abstract written by a model

Chæronea is a town, formerly called Arne and, by local tradition, named for Chæron, son of Apollo and Thero. It is remembered for battles: the Thebans fell against Philip nearby, Sulla and the Romans raised two trophies there, and Metellus and the Romans cut down returning Arcadians. It makes flower-boiled unguents, lies below the crag Petrachos, and begins routes into Phocis, including roads toward Delphi and Stiris.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XV.

Chæronea was the place where Metellus and the Romans met the Arcadians returning toward the Peloponnese. The Arcadians were cut to pieces there.

CHAPTER XL.

Chæronea was said in ancient times to have been called Arne and to have received its later name from Chæron, son of Apollo and Thero. It had two trophies erected by Sulla and the Romans, and near it stood the common sepulchre of the Thebans who fell against Philip.

CHAPTER XLI.

At Chæronea, unguents are made by boiling together lilies, roses, narcissuses, and irises. The town is also associated with Petrachos, the crag above it.

CHAPTER XXXV.

Chæronea was the starting point for routes into Phocis, including a direct road to Delphi through Panopeus and Daulis and a mountainous road to Stiris measuring 120 stades.
