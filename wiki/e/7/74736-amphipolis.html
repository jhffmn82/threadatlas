Amphipolis in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Abstract written by a model

Amphipolis was the principal object of Athenian concern in the conflict with Philip. Philip secured peace with Athens by agreeing to abandon all his claim to the city. Amphipolis was attacked and captured by Philip after he was provoked by its injuries. Philip battered its walls, entered through the ruins, killed many opponents, executed his chief enemies, and spared the rest.

Amphipolis in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Amphipolis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Amphipolis is an Athenian colony and city on the Strymon, settled by ten thousand Athenians and confederates who divided its surrounding country by lot. It underwent repeated changes of population and control and was frequently contested in war. It surrendered to Brasidas, who made it a base; later Brasidas and Cleon met there in battle, both died, and the Lacedaemonians won.

CHAP. XIX.

Amphipolis received a colony of ten thousand men sent by the Athenians, drawn partly from citizens and partly from confederates. The colonists divided the surrounding country by lot.
