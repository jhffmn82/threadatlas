This is a paper in the Proceedings of the 2024 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, titled “Towards Cross-Cultural Machine Translation with Retrieval-Augmented Generation from Multilingual Knowledge Graphs.” Simone Conia, Daniel Lee, Min Li, Umar Farooq Minhas, Saloni Potdar, and Yunyao Li present work from Sapienza University of Rome, Adobe, and Apple on translating text whose entity names, such as movies, books, foods, locations, and sometimes people, have culturally variable target-language forms. The paper argues that advances in multilingual models and data have not resolved this entity-name problem. It introduces XC-Translate, a manually curated benchmark of over 58,000 instances and more than 100,000 human-produced references across 10 English-to-X language pairs. Built from short English knowledge-seeking questions, its translations were created and verified by qualified annotators. The authors also introduce M-ETA, a manually based metric for checking whether valid target-language entity names occur in a translation. To address the task, the paper proposes KG-MT, an end-to-end retrieval-augmented translation method using multilingual knowledge graphs, with Wikidata as its reference source. A retriever identifies relevant entities, and a knowledge-enhanced translator uses their source- and target-language names. Explicit integration provides entity translations in the input, while implicit integration combines entity embeddings with encoder representations; the two approaches are complementary. Experiments compare GPT-3, GPT-3.5, GPT-4, multilingual MT systems, and KG-MT on XC-Translate and WMT benchmarks. Existing systems, including NLLB-200 and GPT-4, perform relatively well on conventional metrics but struggle on entity-aware M-ETA evaluation. KG-MTNLLB achieves the best average M-ETA, and KG-MT is reported to improve over NLLB-200 and GPT-4 by 129% and 62% relatively. Its WMT BLEU and COMET results remain competitive or slightly improved, suggesting no loss of general translation quality. Retrieval reaches 85.9% hits@1 and 92.1% hits@3, although the translator does not always use even gold knowledge effectively. The paper concludes that retrieval-augmented translation can improve cross-cultural entity translation and may extend to idioms and metaphors. It notes limitations in language and entity coverage, Wikidata-related bias, incomplete evaluation metrics, retrieval capped at three entities, and comparison-system coverage. The work was carried out mainly while Conia and Lee were at Apple.

Towards Cross-Cultural Machine Translation with Retrieval-Augmented Generation from Multilingual Knowledge Graphs

The unit presents a work titled “Towards Cross-Cultural Machine Translation with Retrieval-Augmented Generation from Multilingual Knowledge Graphs.” It concerns Cross-Cultural Machine Translation using Retrieval-Augmented Generation from Multilingual Knowledge Graphs. The work is authored by Simone Conia, Daniel Lee, Min Li, Umar Farooq Minhas, Saloni Potdar, and Yunyao Li, affiliated with Sapienza University of Rome, Adobe, and Apple.

Abstract

The paper addresses cross-cultural translation of text containing potentially culturally nuanced entity names. It introduces XC-Translate, a large-scale manually created benchmark for machine translation, and KG-MT, an end-to-end method that integrates multilingual knowledge-graph information into neural machine translation through dense retrieval. Experiments show that current machine translation systems and large language models struggle with entity names, while KG-MT outperforms NLLB-200 and GPT-4 by relative improvements of 129% and 62%, respectively.

Introduction

The introduction describes how multilingual large language models and massive multilingual datasets have advanced Machine Translation, including support for low-resource languages. It focuses on the unresolved challenge of translating text containing culturally variable entities, such as movie, book, food, location, and sometimes people names. The authors introduce XC-Translate, a manually created benchmark covering 10 language pairs, and propose KG-MT, an MT system using retrieval-augmented generation from multilingual knowledge graphs. They report that XC-Translate reveals limitations in current MT systems and LLMs, while KG-MT outperforms state-of-the-art approaches with minimal supervision and computational resources.

Related Work

This unit reviews recent advances in machine translation, external knowledge integration, and the handling of entity names. It describes prior retrieval-augmentation and constrained-generation methods, while noting that retrieval from large knowledge sources such as Wikidata remains understudied. It identifies limitations in existing entity-name research, including its emphasis on transliteration, limited large-scale benchmarks, and reliance on synthetic data augmentation. The authors introduce XC-Translate, a manually created benchmark for cross-cultural translation involving entity names, and KG-MT, which retrieves and integrates entity information during translation.

3 .. 3.3

The unit presents KG-MT, a method for enhancing Machine Translation with Multilingual Knowledge Graphs. KG-MT uses a knowledge retriever to find relevant entities from a knowledge graph and a knowledge-enhanced translator to incorporate their source- and target-language names into translation. Its explicit knowledge integration supplies entity-name translations in the input, while its implicit knowledge integration fuses entity embeddings with the translator’s encoder representations. The method uses Wikidata as its reference knowledge graph but can also be extended to other multilingual knowledge graphs.

4 .. 4.3

XC-Translate is introduced as a large-scale, manually curated benchmark for cross-cultural translation of texts containing entity names. It contains parallel texts in 10 English-to-X language pairs, more than 58,000 instances, and over 100,000 human-produced references. The benchmark was designed around short English knowledge-seeking questions involving entity names, which were translated and verified by qualified human annotators. The unit also introduces M-ETA, a manually based metric that evaluates whether valid target-language entity names appear in a translation.

5

The unit evaluates GPT-3, GPT-3.5, GPT-4, multilingual MT systems, and KG-MT on XC-Translate and WMT benchmarks. KG-MT uses multilingual knowledge graphs for retrieval-augmented translation and substantially improves entity-aware translation, with KG-MTNLLB achieving the best average M-ETA on XC-Translate. NLLB-200 and GPT-4 outperform other baselines on several conventional metrics but obtain much lower M-ETA scores than KG-MT. On WMT benchmarks, KG-MT achieves competitive or slightly improved BLEU and COMET scores, indicating that it does not degrade general-purpose translation quality.

6

This section analyzes KG-MT and discusses possible directions for future improvement. It compares explicit and implicit knowledge integration, finding that both methods are effective and complementary, with their combination producing the best results. The knowledge retriever achieves 85.9% hits@1 and 92.1% hits@3 on XC-Translate, while hard negative mining improves retrieval over mContriever. Evaluation with gold entities shows that the knowledge-enhanced translator remains a major bottleneck because it does not always use the gold knowledge effectively.

7

The paper addresses cross-cultural translation of texts containing entity names. It introduces KG-MT, a retrieval-augmented translation approach that uses multilingual knowledge graphs, and XC-Translate, a large-scale manually curated benchmark for this task. Experiments show that KG-MT significantly outperforms the state of the art on XC-Translate while achieving comparable results on general-purpose machine translation benchmarks. The authors suggest that retrieval-augmented translation could also help with cultural references such as idioms and metaphors.

Limitations

The limitations concern XC-Translate’s language, entity, evaluation, retrieval, and comparison-system coverage. XC-Translate omits many linguistic families, languages, and dialectal or regional varieties, while its Wikidata-based entity selection may exclude culturally relevant entities and inherit biases from Wikipedia. KG-MT evaluation relies partly on M-ETA, BLEU, and COMET, none of which fully measures translation quality. The knowledge retriever returns at most three entities per source text, and the comparison systems may not represent the best systems for cross-cultural translation.
