Tyre in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Tyre is an island city separated from the continent. It denied Alexander entry and, relying on its position, supplies, fleet, and hoped-for support, endured a seven-month siege before being taken, with mass killing, enslavement, and loss of its ruler. Later, during wars among Alexander’s successors, Antigonus blockaded it for thirteen months; it surrendered and received his garrison.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. IV.

Tyre denied Alexander entry and endured a seven-month siege because it trusted its island position, supplies, fleet, and hoped-for Carthaginian support. Alexander eventually captured it, and the city suffered mass killing, enslavement, and the loss of its former ruler.

CHAP. IV,

Tyre was besieged by Antigonus after he encamped nearby and began building ships. After being blockaded for thirteen months and deprived of supplies, the city surrendered on terms and received an Antigonus garrison.

BOOK XVII.

It was besieged and taken by Alexander. The Tyrians used various inventions to defend themselves, and later Tyre was besieged and taken again during the wars among Alexander’s successors.
