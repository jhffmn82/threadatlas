Iphicrates

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Iphicrates, an Athenian general commanding Greek mercenaries, led forces in Persian and Egyptian expeditions; after urging an unsuccessful attack on Memphis, he secretly returned to Athens and became admiral, later refusing battle in a storm as equal commander against rebellious confederates, for which he was accused of treason, fined, and dismissed. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Iphicrates, an Athenian commander at Corinth, commanded forces defending the Corinthian faction and defeated the exiles near the Lechaeum, also defeating Lacedaemonians near Corinth and fighting successfully at Phlius and Sicyon before giving up his intended control of Corinthian territory. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Iphicrates commanded Athenians and targeteers attacking Epaminondas near Lechæum and, after their rout and pursuit toward Athens, prevented the Athenians from going out to fight.

Abstract written by a model

Iphicrates, an Athenian general commanding Greek mercenaries, led forces in Persian and Egyptian expeditions. After urging an unsuccessful attack on Memphis, he secretly returned to Athens and became admiral. Later, equal commander against rebellious confederates, he refused battle in a storm, was accused of treason, fined, and dismissed.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. V.

Iphicrates commanded twenty thousand Greek mercenaries in the Egyptian expedition and urged an immediate attack on Memphis, but Pharnabazus rejected his proposal. He secretly sailed to Athens after the expedition failed and was nevertheless made admiral of the Athenian fleet.

CHAP. V.

Iphicrates was sent with Timotheus to reinforce Chares and held equal command in the war against the rebellious confederates. During the storm in the Hellespont, he refused to fight; after Chares accused him of treason, the Athenians fined him and revoked his commission.
