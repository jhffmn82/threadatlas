Medea's magic in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Medea’s magic comprises incantations, enchanted herbs, songs, rites, curses, and powers over natural things. It is said to win and control Jason, protects him from fire-breathing bulls and armed warriors, and puts the sleepless dragon to sleep so he can take the Golden Fleece. Later, Medea says these arts no longer help her and prepares poison for Creusa.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

VI

Medea’s magic is said to include incantations, enchanted herbs, the power to draw down the moon, stop waters, move forests and rocks, gather bones from funeral pyres, curse absent people, and pierce wax images. Hypsipyle claims that Medea used such arts to win and control Jason.

XII

Medea's magic protects Jason from the fire-breathing bulls, enables him to survive the armed warriors born from the seeds, and puts the sleepless dragon to sleep. Later, Medea says that her songs, herbs, arts, and rites to Hecate no longer help her, though she prepares poison for Creusa.

Medea's enchantments in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Medea’s enchantments are magic used first to preserve Jason through his appointed labors, protecting him from bulls, Earth-born warriors, and a dragon. Her practices use invocations, sacrifices, purification, herbs, waters, flames, sulphur, and a complex potion. The magic restores Æson’s youth, then transforms an old ram into a lamb, providing a convincing model for deceiving Pelias’s daughters.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK THE SEVENTH.

Medea’s enchantments preserve Jason from the dangers of his labors. They protect him from the bulls, aid him when the Earth-born warriors attack, and help bring sleep to the dragon.

opening

Medea’s magic includes invocations, sacrifices, herbs, enchanted waters, flames, sulphur, and a potion made from many strange ingredients. It restores Æson’s youth and creates the convincing transformation of an old ram into a lamb, enabling her deception of Pelias’s daughters.

notes .. notes

The magic described includes invocations of Hecate and the king and queen of the shades, the use of a brazen sickle, bare feet, repeated purification with flame, water, and sulphur, and the boiling of a potent mixture. The numbers three and nine are said to have been considered especially powerful in incantations.

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Witchcraft and sorcery are associated with Medea's invocations of Hecate, the moon, the earth, and other powers. Medea uses these arts to gather magical plants, rejuvenate Aeson, cast spells, deceive Pelias's daughters, and pursue revenge.
