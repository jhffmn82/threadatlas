Artaxerxes, surnamed Ochus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Artaxerxes, surnamed Ochus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Artaxerxes, surnamed Ochus, is a Persian king who initially avoided arms, preferring ease and pleasure while Egyptian rebellions persisted and his generals failed. Wider revolts by Phoenicians and Cypriot kings roused him to prepare a vast force. He recovered Phoenicia and Cyprus, sought Greek auxiliaries, then invaded Egypt after Sidon’s ruin. Following Nectanebus’s flight, he conquered Egypt, demolished city walls, plundered temples, appointed Pherendates to govern, returned to Babylon with spoil, and rewarded Mentor, whose influence also won pardons for Artabazus and Memnon.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

In Asia, the Persian king having invaded ligypt some years before

Artaxerxes, surnamed Ochus, had previously remained inactive despite Egyptian rebellions, because he preferred ease and pleasure and had suffered failures through his generals. When the Phoenicians and kings of Cyprus rebelled, he personally prepared for war, raised 300,000 foot soldiers and 30,000 horsemen, and equipped 300 galleys and 600 ships of burden. He marched against Phoenicia, recovered it and Cyprus, and sought Greek auxiliaries for his renewed war against Egypt.

CHAP. IX.

Artaxerxes marched against Egypt with a large army after the ruin of Sidon. He took possession of Egypt after Nectanebus fled, demolished the walls of many cities, plundered the temples, and returned to Babylon with great spoil. He rewarded Mentor with a hundred talents of silver, rich household furniture, and authority over the Asiatic shore.
