Hannibal in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Hannibal is a Carthaginian general, the eldest son of Amilcar Barcas, regarded with his father among the greatest generals and himself as the most expert of the Carthaginians. After the Mamertines’ defeat, he went first to Hiero under a deceptive pretext, then to Messana, persuaded the Mamertines not to surrender, allied with them, and reinforced the city. After Asdrubal was slain, the Carthaginian army and people unanimously chose him general. He besieged and captured Saguntum, leading to the Hannibalic War. Through victories he enlarged Carthaginian dominions, led disciplined forces, devastated much of Italy, and used policy and stratagems to overcome stronger enemies. His Campanian army was weakened by luxury; he punished men unwilling to leave Italy, yet honored Sempronius Gracchus after death. Though said not to be conquerable by arms, he was compelled by P. Scipio to leave Italy, crossed into Africa, and was defeated by Scipio in a great battle. Later, a peace settlement required Antiochus to deliver him to the Romans.

BOOK XXII.

Hannibal, the Carthaginian general, went to Hiero after the defeat of the Mamertines, apparently to congratulate him but actually to deceive him. He then went to Messana, persuaded the Mamertines not to surrender, made a league with them, and reinforced the city with soldiers.

vooKxxiy.

Hannibal, the eldest son of Amilcar, was unanimously chosen general of the Carthaginians after Asdrubal was slain. He besieged and captured Saguntum, an event that led to the Hannibalic War.

BOOK XXIII.

Hannibal is identified as the son of Amilcar Barcas and is described, with his father, as one of the greatest generals to have appeared before or after them. Their victories greatly enlarged the dominions of the Carthaginians.

BOOK XXVI.

Hannibal is described as naturally warlike and highly experienced through long military service. His army in Campania became weakened by luxury, and he later forced those who wished to remain in Italy to surrender captives and put twenty thousand infantry and three thousand cavalry to death. He showed respect for Sempronius Gracchus after his death, sent his bones and ashes to the Roman camp, and later crossed into Africa.

Hannibal the Carthaginian in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Hannibal the Carthaginian received Ammon’s oracle that he would die and be buried in Libyssa, misread it as Libya, then died of fever after wounding his finger near Nicomedia at Libyssa.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XI.

Hannibal the Carthaginian received an oracle from Ammon saying that he would die and be buried in Libyssa. He expected this to refer to Libya, but he died from a fever after wounding his finger, at a place near Nicomedia that was called Libyssa.
