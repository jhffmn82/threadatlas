Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Helice is described as one of Achaia's principal cities and the chief city affected by the disaster. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Helice is described as an important Achaean city destroyed by an earthquake and the sea.

Helice in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Helice first appears

Abstract written by a model

Helice was an Achaean town and formerly important city near Ægium and the sea. Ion founded it, naming it for his wife Helice, daughter of Selinus; defeated Ionians later took refuge there. Its most important feature was the most holy temple of Poseidon of Helice, which received gifts. An earthquake and the sea overwhelmed and totally destroyed Helice; none of its people remained, Ægium occupied its territory, and its ruins became hard to find.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Description of Greece, Volume II / BOOK VII.--ACHAIA. / CHAPTER I.

Helice was the only child of Selinus and became Ion's wife. Ion founded the city of Helice and named it after her; it later served as the place where the defeated Ionians took refuge.

CHAPTER XXIV.

Helice was once an important city about 40 stades from Ægium and contained the most holy temple of Poseidon of Helice. An earthquake and simultaneous encroachment of the sea overwhelmed the city, and its ruins later became difficult to find and less clearly visible.

CHAPTER XXV.

Helice was totally destroyed by the god, and none of its people remained afterward. Its territory was occupied by the people of Ægium. The unit also says that Helice had formerly received gifts for Poseidon.
