Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Messenia is the land successively associated with Neleus and Nestor, Orestes and Cresphontes's descendants, Lacedaemonian rule, revolt, exile, and restoration. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Messenia is the country against which Sparta prepared for war and whose inhabitants were expelled from Greece. In Description of Greece, Messenia is the country whose history includes conflict with Lacedæmonia, subjection to Spartan rule, and eventual restoration of its people. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Messenia is the district bordering the sea, Megalopolis, and Lacedæmonia, later ravaged in conflicts with the Achæans.

Messenia in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Messenia first appears

Abstract written by a model

Messenia is a country, named after Messene, wife of Polycaon, and said originally to have been uninhabited. It contains Ithome and has a border with Laconia, fixed by Augustus at Gerenia. Its early tradition includes sheltering Tyndareus, while the Heraclidæ claimed it because Hercules had entrusted it to Nestor and expelled Nestor’s descendants. Its central history is conflict with the Lacedæmonians: raids passed in both directions, then Ithome was evacuated and captured. Messenia was conquered, divided, and subjected to Spartan rule; its people surrendered half their produce, mourned Spartan kings and officials, and were largely expelled from the Peloponnese or reduced to servitude. After a general rising and nearly 300 years of exile, the Messenians were restored to Messenia.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XVIII.

The Heraclidæ claimed Messenia by arguing that Hercules had entrusted it to Nestor when he took Pylos. They expelled the descendants of Nestor, including Alcmæon, Pisistratus, the sons of Pæon, and Melanthus.

BOOK III.--LACONIA.

Messenia sheltered Tyndareus according to its own tradition and later became the center of conflict with Lacedæmon. After defeat, its inhabitants were largely expelled from the Peloponnese or reduced to servitude on Lacedæmonian soil.

BOOK IV.--MESSENIA.

Messenia was originally described as uninhabited and was named after Messene, the wife of Polycaon. Its rulers, cities, religious institutions, and conflicts with the Lacedæmonians are traced from its earliest traditions through the capture of Amphea.

CHAPTER VI.

Messenia is raided by the Lacedæmonians, who initially treat it as their own country and carry away its produce and livestock. The Messenians also raid the maritime parts of Laconia and farms near Mount Taygetus.

CHAPTER XI.

Messenia was conquered by the Lacedæmonians after the evacuation and capture of Ithome. Its inhabitants were required to surrender half the produce of the land to Sparta and to mourn Spartan kings and officials, but the people later organized a general rising.

CHAPTER XXIV.

Messenia was conquered and divided among the Lacedæmonians, while the Asinæi retained their territory and Mothone was given to the people of Nauplia. The Messenians were eventually restored to Messenia after nearly 300 years of exile.

Messenia in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Messenia first appears

Abstract written by a model

Messenia is a district touching the sea at Mothone, Pylos, and near Cyparissiæ, and bordering Megalopolis at Hermæum. Restored by Olæas and the Heraclidæ from Lacedæmon and Argos, it was supported by Arcadians in war with Lacedæmonians. Later it disputed with Achæans, was ravaged by Lycortas, and saw Philopœmen captured and taken to Messene.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER V.

Æpytus was restored to Messenia by Olæas and the Heraclidæ from Lacedæmon and Argos. The Arcadians supported the Messenians during their war with the Lacedæmonians.

CHAPTER XXXIV.

Messenia is one of the districts whose boundary with Megalopolis is marked by Hermæum. Hermæum contains a Hermes on a pillar.

CHAPTER XXXV.

Messenia borders the district of Megalopolis at Hermæum, near the temple of Despœna. The road from Megalopolis to Carnasium in Messenia follows the Malus and reaches the place where the Alpheus rises.

CHAPTER LI.

Messenia was the scene of a dispute with the Achæans and was ravaged by Lycortas’s army. Philopœmen was captured there and taken to Messene, and Lycortas later marched against Messenia after Philopœmen’s death.
