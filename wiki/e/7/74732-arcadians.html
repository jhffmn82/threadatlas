Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, the Arcadians formed a confederacy, fought alongside and against neighboring Greek powers, founded Megalopolis, campaigned in Laconia and Elis, helped rebuild Messene, and held the temple and dedications at Olympia. In Description of Greece, the Arcadians were the indigenous inhabitants of Arcadia, persistent allies of the Messenians in their wars against Sparta, supporters and resettlers of the Messenians, and participants in conflicts with Elis.

Arcadians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Arcadians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Arcadians were a confederacy allied with the Argives and Elians, formed amid struggle with the Lacedemonians. After defeat by the Lacedemonians and fear of them, they founded and populated Megalopolis for security. They defeated the Lacedaemonians at Orchomenus, joined the Boeotian invasion, devastated Laconia, helped rebuild Messene, and captured Pellene. With Athenian support they recovered Lassia from the Elians, then invaded Elis and occupied Marganus, Cropium, Cyparissia, and Coryphasium. They also celebrated the Olympic games with the Pisates and possessed the temple and dedicated things at Olympia. Later, Tegean and Mantinean factions divided them; after Mantinea they made peace, resumed conflict, and became involved in Megalopolis's dispute with neighboring towns.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. vn.

They defeated the Lacedaemonians at Orchomenus and sought alliances with the Argives and Elians after declining to rely on their own strength. They joined the Boeotian invasion, devastated Laconia, helped rebuild Messene, and later captured Pellene.

CHAP. VIIL

The Arcadians were defeated by the Lacedemonians in a battle in which more than ten thousand Arcadians were said to have been slain. Fearing the Lacedemonians afterward, they founded Megalopolis and settled people from several Arcadian communities there.

CHAP. IX,

The Arcadians recovered Lassia after the Elians had dispossessed them, with Athenian support in the war. After victory near Lassia, they invaded Elis and captured Marganus, Cropium, Cyparissia, and Coryphasium.

CHAP. X.

The Arcadians divided into factions led by the Tegeans and Mantineans. They fought among themselves and participated in the conflict surrounding Mantinea.

CHAR XL

The Arcadians made a general peace after the battle of Mantinea but soon resumed conflict. The Arcadians were drawn into the dispute between Megalopolis and neighboring towns.

Arcadians in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Arcadians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Arcadians are an indigenous people who inhabit Arcadia and have remained there continuously. Their central role is as persistent allies of the Messenians: they aided them in full force in the first Messenian War, rejected Spartan overtures, and supported them again in the second war, including at Boar’s Memorial and the Great Trench. At the Great Trench, however, their king Aristocrates, bribed by the Lacedæmonians, ordered a retreat; other Arcadians later exposed and stoned him. They welcomed surviving Messenians at Mount Lycæus, gave food, clothing, and offers of residence, helped restore Messene, and later fought beside them against the Lacedæmonians. After the capture of Megalopolis, fleeing Arcadians were received by the Messenians with equal rights. They also aided Elis in Hercules’s campaign, but later invaded Elis and were defeated after fleeing from a dragon-like portent.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XI.

The Arcadians came to the aid of the Messenians in full force during the first Messenian War and fought alongside them. They rejected Spartan attempts to win them over, and during the second Messenian War they again supported the Messenians, including at the battle at Boar’s Memorial.

CHAPTER XVI.

The Arcadians come from their cities to aid the Messenians at the Great Trench. Their king and general Aristocrates, after accepting Lacedæmonian money, orders them to flee at the start of the battle, leaving the Messenian centre and left wing exposed.

CHAPTER XXI.

The Arcadians welcomed the surviving Messenians at Mount Lycæus, supplied them with food and clothing, and invited them to live in their cities. About 300 Arcadians wished to join Aristomenes’ planned attack on Sparta, while other Arcadians exposed Aristocrates’ betrayal and stoned him.

CHAPTER XXIV.

The Arcadians supplied victims for the sacrifices at the restoration of Messene and sacrificed to Dionysus and Apollo Ismenius. They also assisted the Messenians in their later war against the Lacedæmonians.

CHAPTER XXIX.

The Arcadians had anciently shown friendliness toward the Messenians and had helped in rebuilding Messene. After Cleomenes captured Megalopolis, the escaping Arcadians were received by the Messenians, who granted them equal rights.

BOOK V.--ELIS.

The Arcadians are identified as an indigenous people of the Peloponnese who remained in Arcadia. They assisted the people of Elis during Hercules’s campaign against Augeas, and later some joined conflicts involving Elis.

CHAPTER XVII.

The Arcadians invaded Elis with an army but fled after the child offered by a woman was transformed into a dragon. Those who were killed had a monument on the hill toward the west after crossing the Cladeus.
