Abstract written by a model

Staunton’s telegram, sent by Godfrey Staunton from London to Dr. Leslie Armstrong at six-fifteen the previous evening, was urgently addressed without a name and asked for help against danger: “Stand by us for God’s sake!” Sent within hours of Staunton’s disappearance, it suggests that he and another person sought protection; Armstrong denies receiving it.

Staunton's telegram is in one work on this site, The Return of Sherlock Holmes. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

THE ADVENTURE OF THE MISSING THREE-QUARTER

Staunton’s telegram was sent within hours of his disappearance and was addressed without a name. Its surviving words, “Stand by us for God’s sake!”, lead Holmes to infer that Staunton and another person faced a serious danger and sought protection.

It was already dark when we reached the old university city.

The urgent telegram was sent by Godfrey Staunton from London to Dr. Leslie Armstrong at six-fifteen the previous evening. Holmes says it is undoubtedly associated with Staunton's disappearance, but Armstrong claims not to have received it.
