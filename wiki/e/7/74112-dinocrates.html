Dinocrates in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Dinocrates is a Syracusan leader who serves as captain and general of exiles opposing Agathocles. He revives the Agrigentines’ cause, declares himself Protector of the common liberty, seeks Carthaginian aid, recovers expelled exiles, captures Gabu, and commands large forces and important cities with king-like authority. He repeatedly challenges Agathocles and gains advantages, rejecting an offer because he values his authority and fears becoming a private citizen at Syracuse. Though he prevails in Sicily and Agathocles is willing to resign government, he refuses an arrangement. Desertions weaken his army, which Agathocles routs. Dinocrates is then received into favor, becomes general of part of Agathocles’s army, seizes Pasiphilus at Gela, delivers Gela’s castles and cities to Agathocles, and betrays the confederates.

Dinocrates, captain of the exiles, revived the cause of the Agrigentines, declared himself Protector of the common liberty, and gathered an army of twenty thousand foot and fifteen hundred horse. He challenged Agathocles, whose smaller army made a running fight while Dinocrates repeatedly gained advantages.

CHAR IV,

Dinocrates led the exiles and controlled more than twenty thousand foot soldiers, three thousand horsemen, and several important cities. He rejected Agathocles’s offer because he preferred his existing authority and feared becoming a private citizen at Syracuse.

In the mean time the Rhodians sent ambassadors to him, entreat*

Dinocrates commanded the exiles against Agathocles with a much larger army, but his forces were defeated after about 2,000 men deserted. He was later received into Agathocles's favour, made general of part of the army, and seized Pasiphilus at Gela before delivering castles and cities to Agathocles.

BOOK XX.

Dinocrates prevails in Sicily while Agathocles is willing to resign his government, but he does not accept the arrangement. Agathocles later routs his great army with few men, then takes Dinocrates into favor, and Dinocrates betrays the confederates.
