Onomarchus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Onomarchus, colleague of Philomelus, succeeded him as general and commander of the Phocians. He renewed the war, recruited and commanded mercenaries, used coined money and pressure to gain support, captured cities, and twice defeated Philip of Macedon. Later routed in Thessaly with Phocians and mercenaries, he was taken and crucified; a dream marked him a sacrilegious offender.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. VII.

Onomarchus succeeded Philomelus as general of the Phocians and persuaded them to renew the war. He recruited mercenaries, distributed coined money, bribed or intimidated allies and opponents, and captured several cities. He defeated Philip of Macedon in two battles, but was later defeated and killed in Thessaly.

Onomarchus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Onomarchus, brother of Phayllus, became Phocian commander after Philomelus’s death, was defeated by Philip allied with the Thebans, fled, and was killed by his own soldiers.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

After Philomelus's death, the Phocians appointed Onomarchus to command them. After Philip the son of Amyntas joined the Thebans and defeated him, Onomarchus fled toward the sea and was shot by his own soldiers, who blamed the defeat on his cowardice and inexperience.
