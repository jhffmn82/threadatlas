Pelusium in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Pelusium was a strongly fortified Egyptian frontier city toward Syria at the first mouth of the Nile, defended by trench, walls, blocked waterways, obstacles, and five thousand men under Philophron. Lacrates besieged it, drained the trench, breached the walls, and it surrendered after Nectanebus left for Memphis, receiving a Persian garrison.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. V.

Pelusium was the strongest fortified Egyptian frontier city toward Syria, protected by a trench, walls, blocked waterways, and obstacles placed across possible routes of invasion.

CHAP. IX.

Pelusium, the first mouth of the Nile, was strongly fortified and held by five thousand men under Philophron. Lacrates besieged it, drained its trench, and battered down part of its walls. The city surrendered after its defenders heard that Nectanebus had departed for Memphis, and it was placed under a Persian garrison.
