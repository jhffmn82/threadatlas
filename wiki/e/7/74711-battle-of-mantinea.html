Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, the battle of Mantinea involved cavalry and infantry from both sides; the Boeotians routed a Lacedaemonian division, Epaminondas was mortally wounded, both sides claimed victory, and peace among the Arcadians soon broke down. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the battle at Mantinea was a fight between Mantinean and Athenian cavalry and Bœotian cavalry in the Pelagos oak plantation, during which Epaminondas was mortally wounded.

battle of Mantinea in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where battle of Mantinea first appears

Abstract written by a model

The battle of Mantinea involved cavalry and infantry on both sides: Mantineans, Lacedaemonians, Elians, Athenians, and allies fought Tegeans, Boeotians, Argives, and confederates. Boeotians routed a Lacedaemonian division, but Epaminondas was mortally wounded. Both sides claimed victory and erected trophies; peace among Arcadians followed, then quickly broke down.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. X.

The battle involved the Mantineans, Lacedaemonians, Elians, Athenians, and allies against the Tegeans, Boeotians, Argives, and their confederates. The Boeotians gained the advantage after Epaminondas killed the Lacedaemonian general and routed their division, but Epaminondas was mortally wounded; both sides initially claimed victory and erected trophies before the dead were buried.

CHAR XL

After the battle of Mantinea, a general peace was made among the Arcadians. The peace quickly broke down when neighboring towns returned to their former homes and Megalopolis attempted to force them back.

battle at Mantinea in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where battle at Mantinea first appears

Abstract written by a model

At Mantinea, Mantinean and Athenian cavalry fought Bœotian cavalry in the Pelagos oak plantation; during the battle Epaminondas was mortally wounded, with accounts differing over whether Machærion or Gryllus wounded him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

CHAPTER XI.

At the battle at Mantinea, the cavalry of the Mantineans and Athenians fought the Bœotian cavalry. Epaminondas was mortally wounded during the battle, and differing accounts name Machærion or Gryllus as the person who wounded him.
