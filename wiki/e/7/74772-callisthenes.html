Callisthenes in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Callisthenes is a philosopher, described in the opening context as a sophist, who writes a ten-book history of Greece and Sicily. He opposes adoration of Alexander, insisting that divine worship and human honors remain distinct, which earns Alexander's hatred. His intimacy with Hermolaus contributes to accusations that he instigated the pages' conspiracy. Imprisoned in chains, he dies in captivity; accounts disagree whether he dies while carried with the army or after torture and hanging.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

SUR IX.

Callisthenes opposes the adoration of Alexander, distinguishing divine worship from human honors and warning that such worship would dishonor both Alexander and the gods. Alexander comes to hate him for his speech, and Callisthenes's intimacy with Hermolaus helps lead to accusations that he encouraged the pages' conspiracy. The accounts disagree about his fate: Aristobulus says he died while being carried in chains with the army, while Ptolemy says he was racked and then hanged.
