Apsyrtus

person · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Aeetes’ son, her brother, his son Apsyrtus, their prince

Abstract written by a model

In The Argonautica, Apsyrtus is a Colchian prince who leads the Colchians to stop the heroes’ escape, is lured to a sacred island by Medea, and is killed by Jason. In The Library, Apsyrtus accompanies Medea and Jason aboard the Argo, is killed and dismembered by Medea, and his murder leads Zeus to send a storm and require the Argonauts’ purification.

Apsyrtus in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · 299 to 200 BC · the work’s page · read it where Apsyrtus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Apsyrtus is a Colchian prince, son of Aeetes and brother of Medea. He leads Colchians through river channels to stop the heroes’ escape, but Medea’s promises deceive him into coming alone at night to a sacred island. Jason kills and buries him; the murder angers Zeus and requires purification.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Now some of the Colchians, in a vain search, passed out from Pontus

Apsyrtus leads the Colchians through the river channels and attempts to cut off the heroes’ escape. Deceived by Medea’s promises, he comes alone by night to the sacred island, where Jason kills him; his body is hidden in the ground, and the nearby islands thereafter bear a name derived from Apsyrtus.

When Apsyrtus had fallen in mighty overthrow Zeus himself, king of

Apsyrtus is slain by Jason, whose sword remains with him when he comes before Circe as a suppliant. His murder brings the wrath of Zeus and requires purification.
