Flaminius

person · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Flamininds, Flamininus, Flaminius the Roman, Titus Flaminius, Titus Quinctius Flamininus

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Titus Quinctius Flamininus promoted freedom for the Grecians and presented Antiochus with Rome's alternatives concerning his claims in Europe and its support for Rome's allies. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Flaminius is described as a Roman general who sought to free Greece, fought the Macedonians, plundered Eretria, besieged Corinth, urged the Achaeans to join Rome, and pursued Hannibal.

Flaminius in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Flaminius first appears

Abstract written by a model

Titus Flaminius, a Roman general sent by Rome to free all Greece, urged Elatea to revolt and promised its ancient polity, defeated Macedonians at Eretria and Cynoscephalae, plundered Eretria, besieged Corinth, urged Achaeans to join Romans, and pursued Hannibal alive.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VIII.

Flaminius defeated the Macedonian garrison at Eretria, plundered the town, and then besieged Corinth. He urged the Achæans to join the Romans and later defeated Philip and the Macedonians at Cynoscephalæ.

CHAPTER XXXIV.

Titus Flaminius, the Roman General sent from Rome to free all Greece, promised Elatea its ancient polity and invited it to revolt from the Macedonians.
