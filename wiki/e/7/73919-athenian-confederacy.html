Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, the Athenian confederacy was a union of cities joined with Athens against the Lacedaemonians that grew as cities defected, preserved members’ own laws, placed them under Athenian administration, and ultimately included seventy equally represented cities. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Athenian confederates began consulting about general defection, while some places independently decided to revolt without waiting for a general assembly.

Athenian confederacy in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Abstract written by a model

The Athenian confederacy was a union of cities joined with Athens against the Lacedaemonians. It grew as cities defected, preserved members’ own laws, placed them under Athenian administration, and ultimately included seventy equally represented cities, strengthening Athens and attracting others.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAR III.

The Athenian confederacy grew when Chios, Byzantium, Rhodes, Mitylene, and other cities defected from the Lacedaemonians. Its members retained their own laws while coming under the conduct and administration of the Athenians.

Whilst these things were acting in Greece, Acoris^king of Egypt^

The Athenians formed a confederacy that included seventy cities, each with a voice in the senate on equal terms. The arrangement strengthened Athens and encouraged other Greek cities to join.
