KG-MT in Towards Cross-Cultural Machine Translation with Retrieval-Augmented Generation from Multilingual Knowledge Graphs

Simone Conia · 2024 · the work’s page · read it where KG-MT first appears

Abstract written by a model

KG-MT is a novel end-to-end, knowledge-graph-based machine translation system and retrieval-augmented translation approach. It improves translation of texts containing entity names by using dense retrieval to obtain relevant entity names from a multilingual knowledge graph on the fly at inference time, rather than requiring memorization of every entity-name translation through synthetic data augmentation. KG-MT unfolds through two main components: a knowledge retriever, which retrieves knowledge, and a knowledge-enhanced translator, which translates with that knowledge. Retrieved information is integrated explicitly by adding entity names to the source text, implicitly by fusing entity embeddings with encoder hidden states, or through both methods; the components' representations are also fused to enable closer interaction. The two integration methods are complementary, and their combination gives the best results. Across MT backbones and language pairs, KG-MT and its variants substantially outperform baselines on XC-Translate in M-ETA, including reported relative improvements of 129% over NLLB-200 and 62% over GPT-4. It requires minimal supervision and computational resources compared with data augmentation approaches, while maintaining competitive or comparable general-purpose translation quality on WMT and other general-purpose MT benchmarks. With gold entities, average M-ETA improves by 11.6%, showing that the knowledge-enhanced translator does not always use gold knowledge effectively.

KG-MT in Towards Cross-Cultural Machine Translation with Retrieval-Augmented Generation from Multilingual Knowledge Graphs

Simone Conia · 2024 · the work’s page · read it where KG-MT first appears

Abstract written by a model

KG-MT is a knowledge-enhanced translator trained on gold translations from Mintaka. It generates target text from source text augmented with retrieved entities’ source- and target-language names, using a standard sequence-to-sequence Machine Translation model. It combines explicit attention to translated entity names with implicit attention to retriever entity embeddings, integrating up to three retrieved entities per source text. It must handle noisy or irrelevant retrieval by relying on parametric memory. Although evaluated mainly with M-ETA, with BLEU and COMET also reported on XC-Translate and WMT, gold-entity evaluation shows that effective knowledge integration remains a bottleneck.

3 sections; each heading opens the text

3 .. 3.3

The knowledge-enhanced translator generates target text from a source text augmented with source- and target-language names of retrieved entities. It is implemented using a standard sequence-to-sequence Machine Translation model that learns to attend to the provided entity-name translations.

6

The knowledge-enhanced translator integrates retrieved entities into the translation process. It must be robust to noisy or irrelevant knowledge and fall back on parametric memory when necessary. Evaluation with gold entities shows that it is not always capable of using the gold knowledge effectively, making knowledge integration a likely bottleneck.

Limitations

KG-MT is evaluated mainly with M-ETA, while BLEU and COMET are also reported on XC-Translate and WMT benchmarks. Its knowledge retriever retrieves at most three entities for each source text, and its backbone systems include mBART-50, M2M-100, and NLLB-200.
