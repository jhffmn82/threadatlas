Talos

person · 2 works · 12 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: the man of bronze, the warder of Crete, Τάλω, Τάλως

Abstract written by a model

In The Argonautica, Talos is a bronze man and warder of Crete who is killed after Medea bewilders him and he wounds his life-vein. In The Library, Talos is a bronze man or bull who guards Crete, prevents the Argonauts from landing, and is killed through Medea’s intervention.

Talos in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · 299 to 200 BC · the work’s page · read it where Talos first appears

Abstract written by a model

Talos is a bronze man, last survivor among the men sprung from ash-trees and a son of the gods. Nearly invulnerable except for a blood-red life-vein beneath his ankle sinew, he guards Crete for Europa by circling it three times daily. Medea bewilders him; he wounds the vein on a pointed crag and dies.

Talos, the bronze man and last of the men sprung from ash-trees, guards Crete for Europa by striding around the island three times a day. His body is invulnerable except for a blood-red vein beneath the sinew by his ankle, and Medea bewilders him so that he grazes this place on a pointed crag and dies.

Talos in The Library

Apollodorus · about AD 70 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Talos first appears

Abstract written by a model

Talos is a bronze man, though some call him a bull, who guards Crete by circling it three times daily. With a single vein sealed by a bronze nail, he prevents the Argonauts from landing and pelts the Argo with stones. Medea causes his death through drugs, removal of the nail, or an ankle shot.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

τῶν ταύρων οὐκ ἐδίδου τὸ δέρας Αἰήτης, ἐβούλετο

Talos prevents the Argonauts from landing in Crete and throws stones at the approaching Argo. He is described as a brazen man or bull with one vein running from his neck to his ankles and a bronze nail at its end. Medea causes his death, according to differing accounts, by drugs, by removing the nail after promising immortality, or by inducing him to be shot in the ankle by Poeas.
