The Argonautica

Abstract written by a model

The Argonautica follows Jason’s quest for the golden fleece and the long, dangerous return of the Argonauts. Pelias, fearing an oracle that a one-sandaled man will cause his death, sends Jason to seek the fleece from Aeetes. With Athena’s help Argo is built, and heroes including Heracles, Orpheus, Polydeuces, and others join Jason. They elect Jason leader, sacrifice to Apollo, and sail from Iolcos. Their outward voyage brings both welcome and loss. On Lemnos they are received by Hypsipyle and the women who killed their husbands; Heracles eventually forces the companions to leave. A mistaken night battle with the Doliones kills their king Cyzicus, while Hylas is taken by a water-nymph and Heracles is left behind searching for him. Polydeuces kills the boxer-king Amycus, and the blind Phineus, freed from the Harpies by Zetes and Calais, gives the heroes crucial directions. Athena helps Argo pass the Cyanean rocks. Idmon and the helmsman Tiphys die before the company reaches the island of Ares, meets the shipwrecked sons of Phrixus, and arrives in Colchis. In Aea, Aeetes demands that Jason yoke fire-breathing bulls, plough Ares’ field, sow dragon’s teeth, and defeat the armed Earthborn men. Hera and Athena enlist Cypris and Eros so that Medea, Aeetes’ daughter, loves Jason. Torn between family and desire, Medea gives him a powerful charm and instructions; Jason completes the trials by making the Earthborn kill one another. When Aeetes plans resistance, Medea flees with Jason. She puts the guardian serpent to sleep, and Jason takes the fleece. The return is marked by pursuit, guilt, divine aid, and wandering. Medea lures her pursuing brother Apsyrtus into Jason’s trap, and Jason kills him; Zeus therefore requires their purification by Circe and imposes further hardships. Hera, Thetis, and the Nereids aid Argo through storms, the Wandering rocks, the Sirens, Scylla, and Charybdis. At Drepane, Alcinous allows Medea to remain with Jason after their marriage. Blown into Libya, the heroes carry Argo across the desert, lose Canthus and Mopsus, and regain the sea through Triton’s guidance. Medea defeats Talos in Crete; after further divine help and stops at Anaphe and Aegina, Jason and the surviving Argonauts finally return safely to Pagasae.

The Argonautica / BOOK I

Jason comes to Pelias after an oracle foretells that a man with one sandal will bring about the king’s death. Pelias sends Jason on a dangerous voyage through the mouth of Pontus and between the Cyanean rocks to seek the golden fleece. Athena guides the building of Argo, and Argus helps construct the ship. Many heroes assemble for the quest, including Heracles, and they are commonly called Minyae because many claim descent from Minyas’s daughters.

Heracles declines leadership and persuades the heroes to choose Jason, who organizes the launch of Argo, assigns Tiphys to the helm, and leads a sacrifice to Apollo. Idmon prophesies that the heroes will return but that he will die in Asia, while Orpheus calms a quarrel by singing. After departing Pagasae, the Argonauts sail along the coast, pass Mount Pelion and other landmarks, and reach Lemnos.

When Argo arrives, Hypsipyle and the Lemnian women’s assembly decide to receive the Minyae with gifts and invite them into the city, and Jason is welcomed by Hypsipyle. The Argonauts remain on Lemnos until Heracles reproaches them and urges departure, after which they sail onward toward the Hellespont while Jason and Hypsipyle part with promises concerning a possible child. The unit also describes Jason’s splendid mantle, the Argonauts’ rites at the island of Electra, and their continued voyage past Thracian and other shores.

The Argo reaches the Mount of Bears in the Propontis, where the Argonauts and the Doliones initially welcome one another, but a nighttime misunderstanding leads to a battle in which Cyzicus and many others are killed. The Minyan heroes mourn Cyzicus and his wife Cleite, who takes her own life, while the Doliones endure profound grief. A twelve-day storm delays the Argo until Mopsus interprets a halcyon’s cry as a command to sacrifice to the Mother of the gods on Dindymum. Jason and the heroes perform the sacrifice to Rhea, the winds cease, and the Argo resumes its voyage, though Heracles later breaks his oar.

Heracles and his companions reach the land of the Cianians, where the Mysians welcome them and provide food, wine, and lodging. While Heracles cuts a pine for an oar, Hylas goes to the spring called Pegae, where a water-nymph abducts him. Polyphemus son of Eilatus hears Hylas’s cry, but Heracles’s search fails, and the Argonaut ship sails away with Heracles and Hylas left behind. Glaucus explains that their separation is ordained by Zeus, after which Jason and Telamon reconcile and the remaining heroes continue their voyage.

BOOK II

Amycus, king of the Bebrycians, forces the Argonauts to accept his boxing challenge, and Polydeuces defeats and kills him. When the Bebrycians attack in retaliation, the Argonaut heroes repel them while Lycus and the Mariandyni ravage Bebrycian lands. The Argonauts tend their wounded, sacrifice and feast, then sail through the Bosporus, where Tiphys safely steers the ship past a terrifying storm.

Phineus, son of Agenor, is tormented by blindness, old age, and the Harpies, which continually steal and foul his food. Zetes and Calais, the sons of Boreas, drive the Harpies away, but Iris prevents them from killing the creatures and swears that they will not return. Phineus then foretells the Argonauts’ route through the Cyanean rocks and Pontus to Aea, describing the dangers and landmarks leading to the Golden Fleece. After receiving his guidance, Jason and the Argonauts prepare to continue their voyage when the Etesian winds begin to blow.

The Argonauts, guided by Phineus's counsel, escape the Cyanean rocks with Athena's aid and continue along the Bithynian coast. After Apollo appears on Thynia, they sacrifice to him, establish an altar, sing and dance, and swear an oath of mutual aid. They then reach the Acherusian headland and the land of the Mariandyni, where Lycus and his people welcome them because Polydeuces slew Amycus and defeated the Bebrycians. Lycus hears Jason recount the expedition, mourns the loss of Heracles, gives the Argonauts gifts, and sends his son Dascylus with them.

Idmon, son of Abas, is killed by a white-tusked boar, and Tiphys, son of Hagnias, dies soon afterward from a short sickness. The Argonauts mourn and bury both men with honours, then Ancaeus takes up the steering of the Argo after Hera gives him courage. During the voyage they pass many lands and peoples, including the Amazons and the Chalybes, and reach the island of Ares, where the birds of Ares attack them. Following Amphidamas’ advice, they defend themselves with locked shields, spears, helmets, and loud noise, causing the birds to flee.

The sons of Phrixus are shipwrecked near the island of Ares and meet Jason and the heroes after a violent storm. Argus explains their lineage from Phrixus and their journey toward Orchomenus, while Jason recognizes them as relatives and asks them to guide the heroes to Aeetes and the golden fleece. After sacrificing at the temple of Ares, the combined company sails past Philyra, the Caucasian coast, and Prometheus until reaching the river Phasis and the land of Colchis. Jason makes offerings and, following Argus’s advice, anchors the ship in a shaded backwater to plan how to approach Aeetes.

BOOK III

Hera and Athena ask Cypris to persuade Eros to make Medea, the daughter of Aeetes, fall in love with Jason so that she will help him obtain the golden fleece. Hera explains that she favors Jason because he once carried her, disguised as an old woman, across a flood, and she wants Pelias punished. Cypris finds Eros playing dice with Ganymedes and promises him a golden ball if he shoots Medea with his love-inspiring arrow. Eros takes his bow and quiver and travels down from Olympus toward the earth, where Jason and his companions are seeking the fleece.

Jason and the Argonauts enter Aea and visit Aeetes’ palace to request the golden fleece, while Jason proposes winning it through speech rather than force. Aeetes angrily demands that Jason yoke his bronze-footed bulls, plough the field of Ares, sow serpent’s teeth, and kill the earthborn armed men that arise. Medea falls in love with Jason after Eros shoots her with an arrow, and she fears that Aeetes or the bulls will kill him. Argus suggests seeking Medea’s help because Hecate taught her the use of powerful magic herbs.

The gods send a sign when a dove fleeing a hawk falls into Jason’s lap and the hawk is impaled on the ship’s stern-ornament. Mopsus interprets the sign as a command to seek the maiden with the aid of the Cyprian goddess and to follow Argus’s counsel. Idas objects to relying on women, doves, hawks, and supplication instead of warfare, but Jason orders Argus to go to the city and directs the Argonauts to bring the ship openly to shore.

Aeetes calls an assembly of the Colchians and threatens the Minyae and their ship’s crew with destruction if the contest of the oxen is attempted. He explains that Phrixus was welcomed because Zeus sent Hermes to provide him a friendly host, but he suspects the sons of Phrixus of plotting against his throne. Medea, troubled by love for the stranger, dreams that she chooses him over her parents and then seeks help from Chalciope. Chalciope asks Medea to save her sons, and Medea promises magical aid for the stranger’s contest by bringing charms to Hecate’s temple.

Medea spends the night torn between helping Jason and ending her own life, but fear of Hades and love for him lead her to prepare the charm of Prometheus. She travels to the shrine of Hecate, where she secretly arranges to meet Jason and gives him instructions for surviving the contest with the fire-breathing bulls and the earthborn men. At their meeting, Medea gives Jason the charm and explains the rites, while Jason promises to remember her, honor her in Hellas, and make her his wife. Medea and Jason part in grief, as Jason prepares to face Aeetes’ deadly contest and continue the quest for the fleece.

Medea parts from Jason in distress, regretting the evil deed she has helped through her counsels, while Jason returns joyfully to his comrades and tells them about her advice and the dread charm. Aeetes gives Telamon and Aethalides the dragon’s teeth for the contest, believing Jason will fail. That night Jason performs a sacrifice to Hecate Brimo, whose terrifying arrival frightens him as he withdraws without looking back.

Aeetes arms himself and travels from the city to watch Jason undertake the contest on the plain of Ares. Prompted by Medea, Jason uses the charm to protect himself and his weapons, then yokes and ploughs with the fire-breathing bronze bulls while sowing the dragon’s teeth. When the Earthborn men rise from the furrows, Jason throws the boulder of Ares Enyalius among them, causing them to attack one another, and then kills the survivors with his sword. Aeetes returns to the city in bitter grief, pondering how to oppose the heroes, while Jason’s contest ends with the day.

BOOK IV

Medea flees Colchis and joins Jason and the heroes after Aeetes learns of her aid. She helps Jason lull the guardian serpent to sleep, allowing him to take the golden fleece from the sacred grove. Aeetes and the Colchians pursue them, but Hera aids the escape, and the ship reaches the Paphlagonian coast before following a route toward Ister.

The Colchians, led by Apsyrtus, pursue the heroes through the river channels and islands near the Cronian sea, while the heroes seek escape with Medea and the golden fleece. Jason and Medea arrange a covenant and then devise a plot in which Medea lures Apsyrtus to a sacred island, where Jason kills him. After the slaying, the heroes destroy the Colchian force, escape by ship, and continue their return voyage as Hera prevents the remaining Colchians from pursuing them. The unit also recounts the settlement of some Colchians and the heroes’ passage through the lands and islands beyond Ister, ending by asking why Argo later reached the seas near Ausonia and the Ligystian isles.

After Apsyrtus is slain, Zeus decrees that Jason and Medea must be purified by Circe and endure many hardships before returning. Hera drives the Argo through storms and strange waterways, guiding the Minyan heroes past Eridanus, Rhodanus, the Celtic lands, and the Stoechades to Aeaea. Circe performs a purification ritual for Jason and Medea, recognizes their guilt, and then sends them away without approving Medea’s flight. The Argonauts’ return voyage continues after they leave Circe’s halls.

Hera sends Iris to summon Thetis, stop Hephaestus’s fire and Aeolus’s winds, and secure the Argonauts’ passage toward the Phaeacian isle of Alcinous. Thetis and the Nereids guide Argo through the dangerous Wandering rocks, while the west wind carries the ship onward. The Sirens try to lure the heroes, but Orpheus’s lyre overcomes their song; only Butes leaps into the sea and is rescued by Cypris. The Argonauts then pass Scylla, Charybdis, the Wandering rocks, and the mead of Thrinacia.

The Argonauts and Medea reach Drepane, where the Phaeacians, led by Alcinous and Arete, welcome them while the Colchians demand Medea’s return to Aeetes. Medea pleads for protection, and Arete urges Alcinous to judge the matter according to whether Medea is still unmarried or has joined Jason in marriage. Jason and Medea are married in Macris's sacred cave, and Alcinous rules that Medea may remain with Jason because they have become husband and wife. The Argonauts then leave Drepane with gifts, but their journey is destined to continue to Libya before they reach Achaea.

A north wind drives Jason and the Argonauts into Syrtis, where Argo is stranded on the Libyan shore and the heroes despair of returning. Libyan warder nymphs direct them to carry their mother, the ship, across the desert, and the Argonauts bear Argo for twelve days and nights to the Tritonian lake. They find water at the spring made by Heracles, but Canthus is killed by Caphaurus and Mopsus dies from a serpent’s bite. Triton then guides Argo from the lake into the sea after receiving Apollo’s tripod and a sacrificial offering.

The Argo and the Argonauts reach Crete, where Medea overcomes Talos, the bronze guardian, allowing them to land. After escaping the Pall of Darkness with help from Phoebus, they discover Anaphe and establish a shrine there. Euphemus casts a clod of earth into the sea, from which Calliste rises, destined to shelter his descendants. The Argonauts then race for water at Aegina and return safely to Pagasae.
