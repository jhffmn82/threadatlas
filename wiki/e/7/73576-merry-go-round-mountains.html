Merry-Go-Round Mountains in The Lost Princess of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1917

The Merry-Go-Round Mountains are a close-set cluster of low, cone-shaped mountains, silently and steadily whirling in opposing directions. Deep gulfs surround them and their continuous outer gulf makes passage seem impossible; they block access to the Thistle-Eaters and Herkus beyond. Yet several rows of spinning peaks toss travelers from mountain to mountain, carrying them across in dizzying rapid flights. Each mountain stands on a rocky column extending into the pit. The Lion infers they are rubber because travelers bounce without injury, though the Wizard does not confirm it.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 6

The Merry-Go-Round Mountains are said to be close together and surrounded by deep gulfs, making them impossible to pass. The shepherd claims that the Thistle-Eaters and the Herkus live beyond them, though no one knows what those peoples are actually like.

CHAPTER 7

The Merry-Go-Round Mountains are cone-shaped peaks that whirl continuously, with some turning in one direction and others in the opposite direction. Several rows of them block the travelers' advance, but their spinning surfaces toss the travelers from peak to peak and ultimately carry them across.

CHAPTER 8

The Merry-Go-Round Mountains cause the travelers to make dizzying, rapid flights from one mountain to another. The Lion guesses that they are made of rubber because the travelers bounce across them without injury, but the Wizard says this is unconfirmed.
