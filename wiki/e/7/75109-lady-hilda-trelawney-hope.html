Lady Hilda Trelawney Hope in The Return of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1903-09-26

Lady Hilda Trelawney Hope, the Duke of Belminster’s youngest daughter and Trelawney Hope’s wife, lives at the Hope residence and fears damage to her husband’s political career. Before marriage she wrote an indiscreet letter, which Eduardo Lucas uses to blackmail her. Secretly visiting Sherlock Holmes, she seeks information about a stolen political paper while concealing her motive. She steals the document from her husband’s despatch-box with a duplicate key to recover the letter, initially denies it, then confesses to Holmes, gives him the document, and returns it, fearing her husband’s discovery.

Lady Hilda Trelawney Hope visits Holmes secretly and asks him to explain the stolen political paper, its consequences, and the possible effect on her husband's political career. Holmes refuses to disclose the confidential facts, and afterward suspects that she was concealing her true expression and motive.

Lady Hilda initially denies taking the political document, but finally admits that she stole it to recover her indiscreet letter from Eduardo Lucas. She gives the document to Holmes, explains the circumstances of her actions, and remains concerned that her husband might learn of her past and her theft.
