oracle at Delphi in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The oracle at Delphi, delivered by the Pythian Priestess, gives warnings and directions on war, succession, legitimacy, leadership, and religious restoration. It warns against a lame king and tells the Lacedæmonians about Aristodemus’ sons, Orestes’ bones, Messenian land, and Spartan ambushes; its answer on Demaratus’ legitimacy is compromised by bribery. It directs the Dorians to choose Oxylus and Iphitus to restore the Olympian games, while an earlier oracle protects Pisa.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK III.--LACONIA.

The Delphic oracle directed the Lacedæmonians in matters concerning the succession of Aristodemus’ sons and the recovery of Orestes’ bones. It also answered the question of Demaratus’ legitimacy, though the priestess had been bribed by Cleomenes.

CHAPTER XI.

The Pythian oracle told the Lacedæmonians that the Messenian land had been obtained by cunning and would be taken by the same means, but their attempted use of supposed deserters and efforts to corrupt allies failed. It later told the Messenians that the god granted them glory in war but warned them about treacherous Spartan ambushes, hidden things, fate, and the tripods of Zeus of Ithome.

BOOK V.--ELIS.

The oracle of Delphi told the Dorians to make a man with three eyes their leader, which led them to accept Oxylus. It also directed Iphitus to join the people of Elis in restoring the Olympian games, and an earlier oracle protected Pisa from Hercules’s attack.

oracle at Delphi in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The oracle at Delphi, located at Delphi and delivered by the Pythian Priestess in at least one consultation, gives warnings, commands, predictions, and guidance. It directs Eurypylus concerning a chest and a strange sacrifice; its wider prophecy ends the annual sacrifice ordered for Melanippus and Comætho when a foreign king brings a foreign god. It predicts victory connected with Codrus’s self-devotion, guides Antinoe by a serpent, warns Epaminondas about Pelagos, misunderstood as the sea rather than an oak plantation, and tells Alcmæon where vengeance cannot follow him. It also directs settlements, the removal of Arcas’s remains, mourning for Scephrus during famine, and is consulted by the people of Phigalia during famine.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIX.

The oracle at Delphi blamed Melanippus and Comætho for the sufferings of the country and ordered annual sacrifices of the most handsome maiden and lad to Artemis Triclaria. It also foretold that a foreign king would bring a foreign god and stop the sacrifice, and it directed Eurypylus to dedicate his chest wherever he found people offering a strange sacrifice.

CHAPTER XXV.

The oracle at Delphi was followed by Codrus, who devoted himself in accordance with its message. His death caused the Peloponnesians to withdraw from Attica.

CHAPTER XI.

The oracle at Delphi warned Epaminondas to beware of Pelagos. He understood Pelagos to mean the sea and avoided traveling on a trireme or transport, but Apollo evidently meant the oak plantation near Mantinea.

CHAPTER XXIV.

The oracle at Delphi told Alcmæon that the Avenger of his mother Eriphyle would follow him everywhere except onto land most recently created by the sea. It also informed Aglaus that he would experience both good and bad fortune.

CHAPTER XLII.

The Pythian Priestess served as the oracle consulted by the people of Phigalia when famine came upon the land.

the oracle of Apollo in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The oracle of Apollo answers consultations by declaring Psyche’s monstrous, nonmortal marriage, predicting Acrisius’s death by Danae’s child, and directing Cepheus to expose Andromeda to a sea-monster.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CUPID AND PSYCHE

The oracle of Apollo tells Psyche's parents that she is destined to marry no mortal lover and that a monster awaits her on the top of a mountain.

PERSEUS AND MEDUSA

An oracle tells Acrisius that he will be slain by the child of his daughter Danae. An oracle also directs Cepheus to expose Andromeda to the sea-monster.
