beasts of Oz in The Magic of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1919

The beasts of Oz live in its forests, do not die, and follow laws they made to restrain fighting, though conflicts still occur. Some savage beasts in remote places may follow a leader. Ruggedo seeks to recruit them as an army by promising transformation into men and women. In Gugu Forest, the animals argue over fighting the Oz people, transformation, or inaction; after Kiki's transformations, they panic and flee.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

3. Two Bad Ones

Ruggedo says that the forests of Oz contain savage and cruel beasts that might follow a leader. He plans to recruit them into an army and reward them by transforming them into men and women.

7. The Forest of Gugu

The beasts of Oz live under laws made to prevent them from fighting and tearing one another to pieces. Many wild beasts inhabit Gugu Forest, and Ruggedo hopes to win their favor and recruit them.

12. Kiki Uses His Magic

The animals argue about whether to fight the Oz people, undergo transformation, or do nothing. After seeing Kiki's transformations, they panic and flee the clearing.
