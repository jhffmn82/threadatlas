Triton in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · -0299/-0200

Triton is a sea god, son of Poseidon, who rules the Libyan shore and is also called Phoreys and Nereus. Appearing as a youth, he accepts earth from Euphemus, shows Argo the lake’s outlet, then reveals his true form and guides her into the sea.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Now had they left behind the gulf named after the Ambracians, now with

Triton appears in the form of a youth, accepts a clod of earth from Euphemus in exchange for guidance, and points out the outlet from the Tritonian lake. After Jason sacrifices a sheep, Triton rises in his true form, seizes Argo’s keel, and guides her into the sea.

Triton in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Triton, a minor sea god and son of Neptune and Amphitrite, is depicted with a human upper body and fish tail. Standing above the deep, he precedes and announces Neptune: at command, his shell’s sound makes the waters stop and retreat.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

opening

Triton stands above the deep with his shoulders covered by native purple shells. At Neptune's command, he blows his resounding trumpet, whose signal is heard by the waters and causes them to stop and retreat.
