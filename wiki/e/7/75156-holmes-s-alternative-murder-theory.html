Holmes's alternative murder theory in The Return of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1903-09-26

Holmes's alternative murder theory challenges the conclusion that John McFarlane killed Oldacre. Holmes proposes that a passing tramp entered after McFarlane left, seized McFarlane's walking stick, killed Oldacre, and burned the body to conceal the murder, taking no documents because they could not be negotiated. The theory ultimately protects McFarlane when Oldacre is found alive in a hidden room, proving the scene and evidence were staged.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

It was a clang of the bell, followed instantly by heavy steps

Holmes proposes that a passing tramp saw valuable-looking documents through the half-closed blind, entered through the French window after McFarlane left, killed Oldacre with the walking stick, and burned the body. He suggests that the tramp took nothing because the papers could not be negotiated.

When the detective departed, my friend rose and made his

Holmes seeks an alternative theory to the police conclusion that McFarlane murdered Oldacre. His theory is confirmed when the apparent victim is found alive in a hidden room, showing that the crime scene and evidence were staged.
