Amazons in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Amazons are a warrior nation of women and virgins, centered at Themiscyra on the Thermodon, with martial government, training, and religious festivals and sacrifices for Mars and Diana. They conquer neighboring peoples and large parts of Europe and Asia, and are celebrated as valiant warriors. They ally with Bacchus against the Titans and, in another account, are commanded by Minerva. After refusing Hercules’s demand for Hippolyta’s belt, they are attacked near Themiscyra: many leaders are killed, queen Melanippe is defeated, and the nation is said to be destroyed. The survivors assemble at Thermodon, ally with the Scythians, march through the Cimmerian Bosphorus into Attica, encamp at Amazonian Field, and are defeated by Theseus; those remaining go into Scythia as the nation gradually becomes extinct.

Amazons in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

The Amazons, companions of Hippolyte, fought with Hercules against Theseus, who carried off Antiope or Melanippe. They later marched on Athens and were defeated. At Phaedra’s wedding, they appeared armed and threatened the guests; battle followed, killing Hippolyte and, in another account, an Amazon previously married to Theseus.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ES 16

The Amazons fought alongside Hercules against Theseus, and Theseus carried off Antiope, or alternatively Melanippe. They later marched against Athens, encamped near the Areopagus, and were defeated by Theseus and the Athenians. The Amazon previously married to Theseus appeared armed with other Amazons during Phaedra’s wedding and was killed, according to one account, after the guests shut the doors.

Amazons in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The Amazons are a warlike nation and band of female warriors with flourishing cities, led by Queen Antiope and later associated with Penthesilea. They raise only girls. After Hercules attacks, Theseus carries off Antiope; they invade Athens but are defeated by Theseus in a final city battle.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter XII

The Amazons were a warlike nation of women with several flourishing cities. They raised only female children, while boys were sent away or put to death. Juno incited them to attack Hercules by claiming that the strangers were carrying off their queen.

Chapter XIII

The Amazons are attacked by Theseus, who carries off their queen, Antiope, after they have not yet recovered from Hercules's attack. They later invade Athens and fight Theseus in a final battle within the city.
