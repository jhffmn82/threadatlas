Elatea in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Elatea, captured and taken by Philip, became a base for his intended invasion of Attica, causing great consternation in Athens.

Elatea was captured by Philip, who then brought his forces there as a base for an intended invasion of Attica.

Elatea was taken by Philip. Its capture caused great consternation in Athens because of fear of Philip.

Elatea in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Elatea is a town in Phocis, opposite Amphiclea and 180 stades away by road. Obscure before the Phocian War, it claims Arcadian ancestry and, according to the Elateans, was founded by Elatus. It was burned by Mede and shared the Phocians’ disasters, yet became next to Delphi the greatest town in Phocis. Its handsome market-place, temple of Asclepius, theatre with an ancient bronze Athene, and nearby temple of Athene Cranæa mark it. Elateans repulsed Cassander, resisted Taxilus and barbarians from Pontus, received Roman freedom and immunity, remained faithful to Philip, and were later reduced by Roman blockade.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXIV.

Elatea is next to Delphi the greatest town in Phocis, opposite Amphiclea and 180 stades away by road. Its people repulsed Cassander and the Macedonians, resisted Taxilus and the barbarians from Pontus, and received freedom and immunity from taxation from the Romans. Elatea contains a handsome market-place, a figure of Elatus, a temple of Æsculapius, a theatre, and an ancient bronze statue of Athene.
