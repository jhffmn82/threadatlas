luxury and moral corruption in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Luxury and moral corruption arise from excessive plenty: wealthy Sicily’s inhabitants become comfortable, indulgent, proud, and insolent, amid harsher masters and conditions for slave revolt. After conquest and peace, Rome likewise abandons military virtue for extravagance, wealth, and indulgence as happiness.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XXXIV.

The inhabitants of wealthy Sicily became excessively comfortable and indulgent through abundant prosperity, then fell into pride and insolence. This moral decline accompanied the worsening cruelty of masters and helped create the conditions for the slave revolt.

BOOK XXXVI.

After Rome had conquered many nations and enjoyed prolonged peace, its people declined from military virtue into luxury and moral corruption. Extravagant feasts, costly furnishings, fine wines, luxurious clothing, expensive cooks, and enslaved boys were widely desired, while wealth and indulgence were treated as signs of happiness.
