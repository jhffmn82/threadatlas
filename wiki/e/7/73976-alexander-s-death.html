Alexander's death in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Alexander’s death, foretold by prodigies, followed a violent illness after he drank at a banquet from Hercules’s cup. Dying without issue, he predicted contention among commanders over his funeral; his death left government leaderless, triggered succession disputes and revolt, and ended proposed projects.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. XII. /

Alexander died after a violent illness that followed a banquet at which he drank from the great bowl called Hercules’s cup. His final words foretold that his chief friends and commanders would fight and contend over his funeral.

BOOK XVIII.

Alexander's death occurred without issue and left the government without a head, producing disputes and divisions over the succession. It also prompted the revolt of the Greeks in the upper provinces and led to the abandonment of his costly proposed projects.
