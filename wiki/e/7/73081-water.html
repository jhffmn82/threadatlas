water

substance · 5 works · 17 quoted facts · 14 connections
Also called: Pure water, Water, a draught of water, a drop, every drop of water, moisture, precious fluid, the cool water, the ocean, the overflow, the running stream, the sea, the water, waters

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, water or moisture is called Oceanus by the ancients, with the Egyptians identifying it with the Nile as the birthplace of the gods. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, water is denied and muddied as Latona seeks relief, prompting her curse. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, water is a heavy element that can evaporate into air, become dense and form earth, and change in various ways. In Child's Health Primer for Primary Classes, water is a substance needed by the body and used in preparing food, with various sources, uses, and potential health dangers. In From Sand Hill to Pine, water from a spring cools Bray, is offered to Eugenia, is needed by his comrades, and is scarce and valuable at the mining camp, where it appears to have exposed quartz and gold.

water in Child's Health Primer for Primary Classes

Jane Andrews · 1885 · the work’s page · read it where water first appears

Abstract written by a model

Water is a substance needed for the body and used in preparing food. It may come from clouds, cisterns, springs, wells, and food, and is carried to houses through pipes. When boiled, it becomes steam; steam touching a cold plate or tin cover becomes drops of water again, allowing distilling. It does not burn, but water from lead pipes or wells near drains or stables may be poisonous or make people sick.

Abstract written by a model

Water is a clear, cold liquid from the spring. After Bray’s hot journey, it cools his wrists, head, shoulders, shoes, and ankles; he offers it to Eugenia and holds a filled pail to her lips. Needed by his expecting, thirsty comrades, it is initially forgotten, then carried back. Scarce and valuable at the mining camp, its action appears to have caused erosion and exposed quartz and gold.
