Pigeon Pretty in Toto's Merry Winter

Laura E. Richards · 1887

Pigeon Pretty is a sober-minded wood-pigeon and the grandmother’s companion, perching on her shoulder during a dancing lesson. She affectionately reassures Bruin about his size. Later, anticipating Baldhead’s fear of Bruin, she brings reviving dried leaves, restores the fainted hermit, and explains Bruin’s kindness so he can welcome the visitors.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER V.

Pigeon Pretty perches on the grandmother's shoulder so that they can keep each other company during the dancing lesson. She affectionately tells Bruin that his large size is a great good fortune and that she would not have him smaller.

CHAPTER VIII.

Pigeon Pretty anticipates that Bruin's visit might frighten Baldhead and brings pungent dried leaves from her old nest. She uses the leaves to revive Baldhead after he faints and explains Bruin's kindness, enabling the hermit to welcome his visitors.

Pigeon Pretty in Toto's Merry Winter

Laura E. Richards · 1887

Pigeon Pretty, a gentle wood-pigeon, perches in Toto's cottage, is chased by a large hawk, collapses at Bruin's feet, asks mercy for starving Ger-Falcon, and recovers on the hermit's knee.

Pigeon Pretty is pursued by a large hawk and collapses exhausted at Bruin's feet. She asks Bruin to spare the starving hawk, recovers while sitting on the hermit's knee, and is described as gentle and compassionate.
