source reliability in Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Estimation of Source Reliability

Jeongyeon Hwang, Junyoung Park, Hyejin Park, Dongwoo Kim, Sangdon Park, Jungseul Ok · 2025

Source reliability is an estimated, time-varying probability that a source or its retrieved documents provide correct factual information. It matters because differing reliability can make unweighted aggregation vulnerable to misinformation, causing retrieval of incorrect documents and misleading outputs. Reliability-Aware RAG addresses this by cross-checking information among multiple sources, estimating reliability from the proportion of non-IDK fact-checking responses that agree with estimated answers, and using those estimates to select documents and prioritize trustworthy sources. The estimates are rescaled into inference weights, with higher weights for reliable sources and lower weights for unreliable ones. Reliability is estimated during database construction, shows strong agreement with oracle reliability for some real-world sources, and may require periodic updating because it changes over time.
