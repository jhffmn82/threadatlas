Pericles in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Pericles first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pericles, son of Xanthes, was an eminent Athenian orator, statesman, admiral, and general, renowned for nobility, wealth, eloquence, arts, and military stratagems. He led forces into Peloponnesus, Acarnania, Chersonesus, and against Naxians; defeated Sicyonians; ravaged Oeniadae; and distributed conquered land to Athenian citizens. As commander against Samos, he defeated its fleet, besieged and captured the city with allied ships and siege engines, restored democracy, fined it, and took hostages. He served as treasurer, guarded Alcibiades, faced accusations of embezzlement, and opposed ending the embargo against Megara, urging resistance to Lacedaemonians. Though deprived of office and fined, he was recalled, commanded naval operations, and died at Athens while general.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. XXIV.

Pericles led Athenian expeditions into Peloponnesus, Acarnania, Chersonesus, and against the Naxians. He defeated the Sicyonians, besieged Sicyon until Lacedaemonian aid arrived, ravaged the country of the Oeniadae, and divided conquered lands among Athenian citizens.

CHAP. V.

Pericles captured Samos, established a democracy there, imposed a fine of eighty talents, and took youths as hostages. After the Samians revolted again, he defeated them at sea, besieged Samos, confronted the Phoenician fleet, recovered from the defeat of his detached ships, and finally captured the city, punished the revolt's leaders, restored democracy, and returned to Athens.

CHAP. Vlf. /

Pericles argued against rescinding the decree against Megara and persuaded the Athenians to resist the Lacedaemonians. He directed naval operations against Peloponnesus, was praised for his command, was later deprived of the magistracy and fined thirty talents, was recalled, and eventually died at Athens.

Pericles son of Xanthippus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Pericles son of Xanthippus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pericles son of Xanthippus, an Athenian statesman, was commemorated by an Acropolis statue and an Academy-road tomb; later comparison said Lycurgus added more treasury wealth than he collected.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXVIII.

A statue of Pericles son of Xanthippus stood among the votive offerings on the Acropolis. He also had a tomb on the road to the Academy, near the tombs of Thrasybulus, Chabrias, and Phormio. Lycurgus was said to have put 6,500 talents more into the public treasury than Pericles had collected.
