Egyptian war

Also called: the war against the Egyptians, war, war against Egypt

Abstract written by a model

The Egyptian war was Artaxerxes's renewed campaign against Egypt after an earlier failed invasion. Treating conquest as especially important, he sought Greek auxiliaries, entered Phoenicia, crossed difficult terrain, defeated Egyptian troops, captured Pelusium, and secured the cities' submission, aided especially by Mentor's leadership and stratagems.

In Asia, the Persian king having invaded ligypt some years before

Artaxerxes renewed the war against Egypt after an earlier failed invasion. He considered the conquest of Egypt especially important and solicited auxiliary forces from Greece before entering Phoenicia on the way to the campaign.

CHAP. IX.

In the Egyptian war, Artaxerxes's forces crossed difficult terrain, defeated Egyptian troops, captured Pelusium, and gained the submission of the cities. Mentor's leadership and stratagems were especially important to the Persian victory.
