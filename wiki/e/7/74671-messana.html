Messana in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Messana first resisted Agathocles and sheltered Syracusan exiles, but expelled them, admitted him, and served his purges. Foreign soldiers later murdered their hosts, married their wives, and seized it as the Mamertines. After Hiero’s victory nearly forced surrender, Hannibal allied with and reinforced them. Besieged by Hiero and Carthaginians after Roman intervention, it was relieved when Appius Claudius arrived and Hiero withdrew.

Messana initially stands out against Agathocles and becomes a refuge for the Syracusan exiles. Its inhabitants later expel the exiles and admit Agathocles, who then uses the city as a place for purging opponents.

BOOK XXI,

Foreign soldiers expelled from Syracuse went to Messana, where they were received as friends and confederates. They massacred their hosts at night, married their wives, and seized the city.

BOOK XXII.

Messana was held by the Mamertines, who were nearly forced to surrender after Hiero defeated their army. Hannibal prevented the surrender, made a league with the Mamertines, and reinforced the city.

BOOK XXIIL

Messana was besieged jointly by Hiero and the Carthaginians after the Roman intervention there. Appius Claudius arrived from Rhegium, and the Roman presence caused Hiero to withdraw to Syracuse.

Messana in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Messana is a city, anciently called Zancle and afterwards Messana; its earlier name was linked to Zanclus and the harbour Acte. Himilco captured it after a Carthaginian fleet entered its harbour, with inhabitants killed, captured, or driven away, then demolished it almost completely. Dionysius later fortified it, and its townsmen and his mercenaries defeated Heloris amid further campaigning.

Zancle in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Zancle, a fortified pirate base in fertile Sicily, was conquered by Anaxilas and the Messenians; its people were spared, incorporated, and the place renamed Messene.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXI.

Zancle had originally been occupied by pirates and served as a fortified base for expeditions by land and sea. Anaxilas and the Messenians conquered it, and its people took refuge at altars and temples before being spared and incorporated into the new community.
