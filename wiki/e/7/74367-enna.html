Enna

place · 4 works · 7 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Ennians, Henna, city Enna, fair field of Enna, the Grove of Enna, the vale of Enna
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Enna is the city where Eunus was enslaved and where the slave rebellion began; the rebels seized it, massacred its inhabitants, held it against Rupilius, and lost it after Cleon was killed and the city was betrayed and captured.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Enna lies near the center of Sicily beside a fragrant meadow filled with violets and other flowers, where Proserpine was carried away through a cave and underground passage associated with Pluto’s departure.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Henna, also called Enna, is a city in central Sicily near Pergus, its lake, and a flower-filled grove, marked by worship of Ceres and by springs, lakes, groves, flowers, and a cavern linked to Proserpina’s abduction.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Enna is a wooded, flower-filled vale containing River Cyane and a lake, where Proserpine gathers flowers before Pluto abducts her.

Henna in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Henna, also called Enna, is a city in central Sicily, near Pergus, its lake, and a flower-filled grove. Regarded as Sicily’s navel, it is chiefly marked by prominent worship of Ceres and by springs, lakes, groves, flowers, and a cavern linked to Proserpina’s abduction while gathering flowers.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Henna is the place near which the lake of Pergus and its flower-filled grove are located. Proserpina is abducted there while gathering flowers.

notes .. notes

Henna, also called Enna, is described as lying almost exactly at the center of Sicily and therefore as the island’s navel. Its worship of Ceres was so prominent that ancient writers compared the whole city to a temple and its inhabitants to priests; Cicero described nearby springs, lakes, groves, flowers, and a cavern associated with the abduction of Proserpine.

Enna in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Enna is a wooded, flower-filled vale containing River Cyane and a lake; its beauty frames Proserpine gathering flowers before Pluto abducts her.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

PROSERPINE

Enna contains a secluded, flower-filled vale with a lake where Proserpine gathers lilies and violets before Pluto abducts her. The story presents this place as an especially beautiful land that Milton later compares with Paradise.
