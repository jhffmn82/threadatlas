sacrilege at Delphi in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The sacrilege at Delphi was the Phocians’ violation and seizure of Apollo’s temple and oracle, taking and spending sacred treasures to finance an eleven-year Sacred War and pay mercenaries. The Amphictyons condemned them; Philomelus, his followers, Phocian generals, and associates were treated as sacrilegious. Athenians and Lacedaemonians were also called partners for accepting payments. The account ends with defeats, deaths, enslavements, and other misfortunes presented as punishment.

CHAP. VII.

The Phocian sacrilege consisted of violating Apollo's temple and using its sacred treasures to finance the war. The Amphictyons condemned the Phocians for it, and Philomelus and many of his followers were treated as sacrilegious persons.

CHAP. X.

Phocian generals and others took and spent the sacred treasures of Delphi to support the war and pay mercenaries. The Phocians were blamed for the sacrilege, while the Athenians and Lacedaemonians were also described as partners because they accepted disproportionate payments.

Phocian seizure of Delphi in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The Phocian seizure of Delphi occurred during the reign of Archidamus. It prompted competing offers of aid to the Thebans and public support for the Phocians from the Lacedæmonians and Athenians. The Phocians planned extreme violence against the inhabitants of Delphi, but Archidamus prevented the massacre, enslavement, and destruction.
