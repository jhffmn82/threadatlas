Abstract written by a model

Pietro Venucci is the murder victim found outside 131 Pitt Street in connection with the Kensington incident. Tall, sunburned, powerful, poorly dressed, apparently under thirty, and unidentified at first, he is found with his throat cut. A horn-handled clasp knife lies beside him, while his pocket holds a photograph of the dead man; an apple, string, and a shilling map are also found. He is later identified as Lucretia Venucci’s brother, a notorious Naples cut-throat allegedly connected to the Mafia. Lestrade and Holmes link his murder to Beppo and the pearl, but the exact relationship remains uncertain.

THE ADVENTURE OF THE SIX NAPOLEONS

The dead man found outside 131 Pitt Street is tall, sunburned, powerful, and apparently under thirty, but poorly dressed and unidentified. His throat has been cut, and a horn-handled clasp knife, an apple, string, a shilling map of London, and a photograph are found near or on him.

Sherlock Holmes and I walked together to the High Street, where

Pietro Venucci is identified as the dead man found in connection with the Kensington incident. Inspector Lestrade says he was from Naples, was a notorious cut-throat connected with the Mafia, and may have been pursuing the man in Beppo’s photograph.

When we met again next evening, Lestrade was furnished with much

Pietro was Lucretia Venucci’s brother and was murdered two nights before this meeting. Holmes believes he was connected with Beppo and the pearl, though he cannot determine whether Beppo stole it from him, acted as his confederate, or served as a go-between.
