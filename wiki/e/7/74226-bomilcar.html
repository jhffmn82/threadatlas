Bomilcar in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Bomilcar was a Carthaginian general in Africa who sought monarchy and sovereign power over Carthage. Appointed alongside Hanno, he ordered a retreat after Hanno’s death, fearing victory would block his ambition, and the retreat became a rout. He later attacked Carthage with citizens and mercenaries, declared himself sole monarch, was defeated, and was executed.

Bomilcar was appointed a Carthaginian general alongside Hanno. Ambitious to gain the monarchy, he ordered a retreat after Hanno's death because he believed a Carthaginian victory would prevent him from seizing power; the retreat became a rout.

Bomilcar plotted to gain sovereign power at Carthage. He led 500 citizens and about 1,000 mercenaries in an attack on the city and declared himself sole monarch, but the Carthaginians resisted, forced his followers into the New City, and later executed him despite the terms by which the revolt was settled.
