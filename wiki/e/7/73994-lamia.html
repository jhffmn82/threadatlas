Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Lamia was Antipater’s refuge, which he fortified and supplied while it was under siege. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Lamia was the site of a Greek reverse where the Athenians became slaves of the Macedonians.

Lamia in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Lamia first appears

Abstract written by a model

Lamia became Antipater’s refuge after his defeat. He strengthened its walls and accumulated arms, grain, and other provisions there while the Macedonian army waited for reinforcements. Lamia was under siege when the Greeks withdrew to meet Leonatus. Antipater had previously been shut up there, and Athens later surrendered after the Macedonian victory.
