Brennus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Brennus, king of the Gauls, led a large Gallic army into Macedonia and Greece, seeking especially to plunder Delphos and its temple. After heavy losses and three serious wounds, he appointed Cichorius successor, ordered retreat, and killed himself.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK XXII.

Brennus led the Gauls into Macedonia and Greece and suffered many losses and three serious wounds during the fighting. Near death, he advised the Gauls to kill the wounded, burn the carriages, return home, and make Cichorius king; he then killed himself after drinking wine.

Brennus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Brennus is a Celtic leader and strategist who instigates and commands the Galatian invasion of Greece alongside Acichorius. He persuades Galatian assemblies and chieftains to invade by stressing Greek weakness and the wealth of communities and temples, then is chosen commander. He directs a large barbarian army with a bodyguard of its finest and boldest men, showing tactical skill by sending 10,000 tall, able-swimming troops across the Sperchius at a weakly guarded marsh, ordering bridges built, and advancing toward Heraclea and Thermopylæ. Seeking to isolate the Ætolians, he appoints Orestorius and Combutis to lead an expedition into Ætolia, uses Ænianes and Heracleans as guides through Mount Œta, and leaves Acichorius with the main army at Thermopylæ while he marches on Delphi. Wounded in the battle at Delphi and carried away unconscious, he later kills himself by drinking neat wine, from fear of or shame before his comrades.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIX.

Brennus urged the assemblies and individual chieftains of the Galati to invade Greece, emphasizing its weakness and the wealth held in its communities and temples. He was chosen as a commander alongside Acichorius for the resulting campaign.

CHAPTER XX.

Brennus is described as intelligent and, for a barbarian, a capable strategist. He responds to the Greek defenses at the Sperchius by sending 10,000 troops to cross the river at a less-guarded marshy section.

CHAPTER XXI.

Brennus ordered the inhabitants near the Maliac Bay to build bridges over the Sperchius, then led his army across them toward Heraclea. He advanced toward Thermopylæ after learning that a strong Greek force was gathered there, and he ordered an attack at daybreak despite having no Greek seer or, apparently, priests of his own country with him.

CHAPTER XXII.

Brennus believed that forcing the Ætolians back into Ætolia would make war against the other Greeks easier. He sent Orestorius and Combutis into Ætolia, then arranged with the Ænianes and people of Heraclea to guide him through a pass over Mount Œta, while Acichorius attacked the Greek force from the front.

CHAPTER XXIII.

Brennus waited for Acichorius’s troops and then marched toward Delphi with the Galati. He was wounded in the battle, was carried away in a fainting condition, and later committed suicide by drinking neat wine, either from fear of or shame before his comrades.
