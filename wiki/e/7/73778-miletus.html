In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Miletus was an Ionian city called Anactoria for two generations before Nileus and the Ionians conquered it.

Miletus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Miletus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Miletus, a city and former home of the Branchidae, was defended with soldiers and supplies until Alexander breached it by land and sea. It surrendered; he treated the Milesians mercifully, restored Greek city liberties after the citadel fell, and sold other captured defenders into slavery.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. It.

Miletus was defended by numerous soldiers and ample supplies, but Alexander breached its walls after attacking by land and sea. The Milesians surrendered themselves and the city; Alexander treated the Milesians mercifully but sold the other captured defenders into slavery.

SUP. I.

Miletus was the former home of the Branchidae, who were removed from it by Xerxes. The Milesians in Alexander’s army retained an old grudge against the Branchidae because their ancestors had been betrayed.

Miletus (city) in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Miletus (city) first appears

Abstract written by a model

Miletus, an Ionian city, was called Anactoria for two generations under Anax and Asterius before Nileus and the Ionians conquered it, killing men and marrying women.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

Miletus was formerly called Anactoria for two generations, during the reigns of Anax the Autochthon and his son Asterius. Nileus and the Ionians conquered it, killed its male inhabitants except those who escaped, and married their wives and daughters.
