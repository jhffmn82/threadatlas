Minotaur

creature · 6 works · 14 quoted facts · 7 connections
Also called: a monster, belua, dUnotaur, double figure of a bull and of a youth, him that was man in part, and in part bull, monster, the MiDotaur, the Minotaur, Μινώταυρον
  • In Heroides and Amores, the Minotaur is described as part man and part bull; Theseus kills it with a club, and Ariadne’s thread shows him the way out.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Minotaur lived in Crete and devoured the boys and girls sent by the Athenians as tribute; Theseus killed it with Ariadne’s advice and assistance, then learned the way out of the labyrinth.
  • In The Library, Minotaur is a bull-headed man, offspring of Pasiphae and a bull, whom Minos confines in the Labyrinth; it receives seven Athenian youths and seven maidens each year, and Theseus kills it in the third tribute.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the Minotaur is a two-formed bull-and-youth monster, born from Minos’s mother’s adultery, enclosed in the Labyrinth, fed Athenian blood twice, and killed by Theseus.
  • In The Library, the Minotaur is born to Pasiphae after she joins Minos’s bull with Daedalus’s assistance, has a bull’s face and human body, and is confined by Minos in the Labyrinth in accordance with oracles.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Minotaur is a strong, fierce monster with a bull’s body and human head, confined in Daedalus’s labyrinth and fed Athenian youths and maidens until Theseus kills it.
