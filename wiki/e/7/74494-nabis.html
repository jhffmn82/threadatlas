Nabis

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Nabis sends ambassadors with Titus Quinctius Flamininus to Rome concerning the terms of peace. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Nabis is described as the tyrant of Lacedæmon who attacked the Messenians, fought the Romans, and was assassinated by a Calydonian.

Nabis in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Nabis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nabis was a tyrant of Lacedæmon, described as a man of extreme, unrelenting cruelty. He first attacked the Messenians, took all Messene but its citadel, and withdrew when Philopœmen arrived. He later fought the Romans and gained a temporary truce. A Calydonian, suborned by the Ætolians, assassinated him; his power at Sparta ended.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VIII.

Nabis is described as a man of extreme cruelty whose power at Sparta was overthrown. After his power ended, the Lacedæmonians became their own masters again before being brought into the Achæan League.

CHAPTER L.

Nabis became tyrant of Lacedæmon and attacked the Messenians, taking all Messene except its citadel until Philopœmen arrived and compelled his withdrawal under conditions of war. He later fought the Romans, obtained a temporary truce from them, and was assassinated by a man who came ostensibly to negotiate an alliance but had been suborned by the Ætolians.
