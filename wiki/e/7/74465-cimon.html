Cimon in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Cimon was an Athenian admiral and son of Miltiades. He led Athenian and allied fleets in Asia to aid confederate cities and free cities held by Persian garrisons, capturing Eion and Scyros and conquering Persian-held cities in Caria and Lycia. He won at Eurymedon through a stratagem and night assault, and at Cyprus captured Citium and Malus and defeated Persian forces by sea and land. After peace with Persia, he died of illness in Cyprus.

CHAP. XIII.

Cimon captured Eion and Scyros, conquered Persian-held cities in Caria and Lycia, and commanded victories at Cyprus and Eurymedon. He used captured Persian ships disguised with Persian ornaments to surprise the Persian land army.

DIODORUS SICULUS. / BOOK XIL

Cimon, the son of Miltiades, commanded the Athenian expedition against Cyprus. He captured Citium and Malus, defeated Persian forces by sea and land, helped secure peace with Persia, and died of illness in Cyprus.
