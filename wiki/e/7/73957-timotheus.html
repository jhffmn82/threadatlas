Timotheus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Timotheus, son of Clearchus, is portrayed as a commander appointed for an expedition against the Lacedaemonians. As admiral of the Athenian fleet, he blockades a city in Cephallenia, seeks Acarnanian support, allies with Acetas, king of the Molussians, and defeats the Spartans at Leucades. He receives Zacynthian exiles, helps them seize Arcadia, is redirected between Corcyra and Thrace, and is restored after Athens removes him. Later, sharing command against rebellious confederates, he refuses battle in a storm; Chares charges treason, and Athens fines and dismisses him. He is also accused of devising a killing scheme and concealing further deaths.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. IV.

Timotheus became admiral of the Athenian fleet, blockaded a city in Cephallenia, and worked to bring the cities of Acarnania to the Athenian side. He allied with Acetas, king of the Molussians, and defeated the Spartans in a sea-fight at Leucades.

CHAP. V.

Timotheus received the Zacynthian exiles and helped them seize Arcadia. He was sent toward Corcyra with a fleet, but diverted to Thrace, later returned with additional ships and ambassadors, and was restored to command after Athens had removed him.

CHAP. V.

Timotheus was sent with Iphicrates to reinforce Chares and held equal command against the rebellious confederates. He refused to fight during the storm, after which Chares accused him of treason and the Athenians fined him and revoked his commission.

Timotheus is accused by Antipater of contriving the whole scheme and persuading the young man to kill his brother. When the great men and commanders begin to discover the design, he becomes frightened, releases the remaining condemned people from torment, and later has them secretly put to death.
