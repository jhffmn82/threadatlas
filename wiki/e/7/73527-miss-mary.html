Miss Mary in Toto's Merry Winter

Laura E. Richards · 1887

Miss Mary is a parrot living in Central Africa and perched at a comfortable window perch in Toto's cottage. The family considers her sober-minded, though she is not specially affectionate; she is fond of Pigeon Pretty and is married to Miss Mary's husband. Her father, Polacko, is reputed to have a notable beak and, she says, escaped China after a mandarin's grandson tried to make him eat with chopsticks. After two tail feathers are bitten off while she sleeps, Miss Mary silently lets two brown mice eat the squash pie. She disdains dancing until Cracker seizes and whirls her, whereupon she threatens to peck his eyes out and returns to her perch. She then confronts a robber, helps defend the cottage, and thanks Bruin after he saves her life, changing from bad terms to friendship. Finally, she tells The Three Remarks.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER III.

Miss Mary tells the family that her father, Polacko, lived for twenty years with a mandarin in China. She says he escaped after the mandarin's grandson tried to make him eat with chopsticks.

CHAPTER IV.

Miss Mary says she saw two brown mice enter the cupboard through the crack under its door and eat the squash pie. She did not give the alarm because someone had bitten two fine feathers from her tail while she slept, and she exchanges a knowing look with <DW53>.

CHAPTER V.

Miss Mary stands on one leg and watches the dancing with silent disdain before Cracker grabs her and whirls her around. She gasps, struggles to escape, threatens to peck Cracker's eyes out, and finally flies back to her perch.

CHAPTER IX.

Miss Mary watches a robber enter the kitchen and ransack the cottage. When he takes the silver teaspoons and the porringer, she cries "Stop thief!" and faces his threat to wring her neck. After the raccoon drives him away, she gives the raccoon her thanks and offers him feathers for his future needs.

CHAPTER X.

Miss Mary helps defend the cottage against the attempted robber and is praised by Toto for her bravery. She is initially suspicious of Jim Crow, but becomes pleased when he formally addresses her as "Lady Parrot" and escorts her inside.

" CHAPTER XI.

Miss Mary tells the company the story of The Three Remarks after everyone agrees that they would like another story.
