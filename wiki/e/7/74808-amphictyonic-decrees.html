Amphictyonic decrees in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Amphictyonic decrees, ratified and approved by all the Greeks, imposed severe measures on Phocis: demolition, disarmament, exclusion from Delphi, tribute, and expulsion of Phocian exiles as accursed. They condemned Phocians and Lacedaemonians, threatened confiscation and dedication of Phocian land, and ordered war. After defeating the Locrians, Philomelus destroyed the decrees’ pillar texts and records. Later provisions barred Phocians from the temple and Council, required restitution, annual payments, destruction of arms, surrender of horses, and reduction of cities to villages.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. VI.

The Amphictyonic decrees condemned the Phocians and Lacedaemonians and threatened the Phocian land with confiscation and dedication to the deity. After defeating the Locrians, Philomelus cut the decrees from the pillars and destroyed the records concerning those condemned.

CHAP. VII.

The Amphictyonic decrees condemned the Phocians and ordered war against them. They also provided the basis for condemning captured mercenaries to death and for imposing heavy fines on the Lacedaemonians.

CHAP. X.

The decrees barred the Phocians from the temple and the Amphictyonic Council, required restitution of the stolen money before they could possess horses or arms, and ordered the destruction or reduction of their cities into small villages. They also condemned the exiles and other participants in the sacrilege, required an annual payment of sixty talents to the oracle, and ordered the destruction and burning of Phocian arms and the surrender of their horses.
