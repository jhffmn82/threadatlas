the blood thumbprint

object · 1 work · 4 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: The thumb-mark, a stain of blood, that mark, that print, the mark, the well-marked print of a thumb, thumb-mark

Abstract written by a model

The blood thumbprint is evidence against McFarlane, presented by Lestrade as conclusive because it matches his right thumb. Holmes notes it was absent when he examined Oldacre’s hall the previous day, so it was added during the night. He concludes that Oldacre copied McFarlane’s seal impression in wax, moistened it with blood, and transferred it to the wall.

A blood thumbprint on the hall wall is presented by Lestrade as conclusive evidence against McFarlane. Holmes confirms that it matches McFarlane's thumb but points out that the mark was not present when he examined the hall the previous day.

Holmes smiled, and clapped Lestrade upon the shoulder.

The thumb-mark on the wall had not been present the previous day, so Holmes concluded that it had been added during the night. He inferred that Oldacre took a wax impression of McFarlane’s thumb-mark from a seal, moistened it with blood, and transferred it to the wall.
