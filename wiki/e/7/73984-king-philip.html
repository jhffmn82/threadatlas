king Philip in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Philip III, the name given to Aridaeus, was made king after Alexander’s death and reigned six years and four months. He summoned commanders while Perdiccas held executive power, was husband of Eurydice, and later joined other kings under Eumenes. After Macedonians abandoned Eurydice, Olympias imprisoned Philip and had Thracians stab him to death.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XVIII.

Philip III was the name given to Aridaeus when he was made king. He summoned a general council of the chief commanders, while Perdiccas exercised the executive power.

Eurydice being queen-regent in Macedonia, as soon as she heard

Philip III Arrhidaeus, called Philip in the text, was captured with his servants after the Macedonians abandoned Eurydice. Olympias imprisoned him, then delivered him to Thracians who stabbed him with poniards after he had reigned six years and four months.

BOOK XVII.

He was made king after Alexander’s death. The provinces were divided among the chief commanders, and he later joined the other kings under Eumenes.
