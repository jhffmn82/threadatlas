Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, the Carthaginian army was a military force that besieged Tunis, was put to flight after Hanno was killed and Bomilcar ordered retreat, suffered routs and setbacks, and was divided in a night attack on Syracuse. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Carthaginian army was a force drawn from Africa and Spain that was routed near Himera after Amilcar’s death and the fleet’s burning, captured Messana, advanced on Syracuse, ravaged its surroundings, and suffered disease.

the Carthaginian army in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where the Carthaginian army first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Carthaginian army was a military force listed at 40,000 foot, 1,000 horse, and 2,000 chariots. It besieged Tunis with battering rams, then, after Hanno was killed and Bomilcar ordered retreat, was put to flight. Routed in Africa despite Amilcar’s report, it assaulted Syracuse and Tunis but suffered setbacks. In a night attack on Syracuse with 120,000 foot and 5,000 horse, terrain, darkness, and disorder caused rout and division.

Carthaginian army in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Carthaginian army first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Carthaginian army was a force drawn from Africa and Spain, including confederates and mercenaries, reported at over three hundred thousand foot, four thousand horse, and chariots. Divided near Himera, it was routed after Amilcar’s death and the fleet’s burning. It also captured Messana, advanced on Syracuse, ravaged its surroundings, and suffered disease.

The Carthaginian army numbered no less than three hundred thousand and was divided between camps near Himera. It was routed after Amilcar was killed and the fleet was burned; roughly one hundred and fifty thousand were slain, and the remainder either reached a naturally fortified place or surrendered.

The Carthaginian army was assembled from forces in Africa and Spain, including confederates and mercenaries, and was reported to number more than three hundred thousand foot and four thousand horse, with chariots. It captured Messana, marched against Syracuse, ravaged the surrounding country, and camped near the city. Disease later struck the army.
