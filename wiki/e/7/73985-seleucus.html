Seleucus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Seleucus is a Macedonian commander, governor, prince of the higher provinces, and later king whose career centers on Babylon, conflict with Antigonus, and alliances with other rulers. While governor of provinces centered on Babylon and lord-lieutenant of Susa, he refused to obey Eumenes, attempted to win Antigenes and the Argyraspides, flooded Eumenes’s camp by diverting the Tigris, made a truce for Eumenes’s crossing, and informed Antigonus. When Antigonus came to Babylon and demanded an accounting of its revenues, Seleucus claimed the province had been granted for his service under Alexander. Fearful of Antigonus, he fled with fifty horsemen to Egypt, where Ptolemy received and supported him. From this exile he opposed Antigonus through Ptolemy. He commanded operations in Cyprus through Polyclitus, with Menelaus and allied forces, taking Cyrinia and Lapithus, gaining Stasiecus, demanding hostages, and besieging Citium. He persuaded Ptolemy to invade Coele-Syria, joined Ptolemy against Demetrius, and presented their struggle as a response to Antigonus’s seizure of conquered provinces and deprivation of Babylon. After Demetrius’s defeat at Gaza, Seleucus returned toward Babylon with only eight hundred infantry and two hundred cavalry, trusting the inhabitants’ affection. With their support he recovered Babylon, defeated Nicanor near the Tigris, secured Media, Susa, and neighboring provinces, and later prospered in the upper provinces. He appointed Patrocles over Babylon and responded when Demetrius again threatened it. Seleucus then joined Cassander, Ptolemy, and Lysimachus against Antigonus, raised a numerous army, and marched from Babylon with a great force. In Cappadocia he wintered with 20,000 foot, 12,000 horse, 480 elephants, and 100 iron chariots before joining Lysimachus for war. He also rejected a request to kill the imprisoned Demetrius, sought his son Antiochus Soter’s advice, and intended to release Demetrius honorably. He is recorded as Antiochus’s father and as bound through his father’s league with Rome.

Seleucus refused to obey Eumenes, though he offered to supply the kings with what they needed. With Python, he tried to win over Antigenes and the Argyraspides, diverted the Tigris to flood Eumenes's camp, and later made a truce allowing Eumenes to cross before sending word to Antigonus.

CHAP. IV,

Seleucus, governor of the provinces, received Antigonus at Babylon but refused to account for the revenues of Babylon, claiming that the Macedonians had granted him the province for his service under Alexander. Fearing Antigonus, he fled with fifty horsemen to Egypt, where Ptolemy received him kindly. He later commanded operations in Cyprus, taking Cyrinia and Lapithus, gaining Stasiecus as an ally, demanding hostages from the prince of the Amathusians, and besieging Citium.

Seleucus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Seleucus, son of Antiochus and a king described as upright, pious, and religious, was exiled by Antigonus and welcomed by Ptolemy. Ptolemy rallied Cassander and Lysimachus against Antigonus. Seleucus restored a brazen Apollo and made conditions with Ptolemy; later refugees were received after Antiochus violated them.

CHAPTER VI.

Seleucus, the son of Antiochus, was driven into exile by Antigonus and was welcomed by Ptolemy. Ptolemy persuaded Cassander and Lysimachus to join him in opposing the exile of Seleucus and the growing power of Antigonus.

CHAPTER VII.

Seleucus I had made conditions with Ptolemy I Soter that Antiochus I was persuaded to violate. He later received Lysandra, her sons and brothers, and Alexander, the son of Lysimachus, when they fled from Lysimachus.
