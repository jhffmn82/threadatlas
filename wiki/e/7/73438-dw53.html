<DW53> in Toto's Merry Winter

Laura E. Richards · 1887

<DW53> is a handsome, dark-furred raccoon and Toto’s enduring animal companion, household helper, and friend. Bright-eyed and usually good-hearted despite a love of jokes, he wears a red cravat or scarlet neck-ribbon and later a scarlet feather given by Miss Mary. He fears Toto’s tea-kettle as if it were alive after burning his paw on it, yet performs cottage chores: sweeping the hearth or using his tail for sweeping, setting the table, catching rats, and polishing the kettle. Saving and unable to bear waste, he is deeply upset when his saved squash pie disappears; after learning that brown mice ate it, he plans to send them from the cellar to the forest. He refuses to sweep red-hot coals, choosing to tell the story of the Useful Coal instead, then joins dancing lessons. With Toto and Cracker, he attends the woodmouse wedding in Toto’s cravat, enjoys the attention, treats the couple graciously, and waltzes with Toto. He questions and sarcastically challenges Toto’s explanation of a comet, listens to stories, and is praised for helping defend the cottage during an attempted robbery. Near the end, he helps decorate the cottage, recounts falling through a hollow tree while gathering honey, and joins the games. He also enjoys chasing cats and curls his beautiful tail about his toes or over his back.

Narrative, section by section

8 sections; each heading opens the text

Toto's Merry Winter / CHAPTER I.

The raccoon is afraid of the tea-kettle because he believes it is alive, especially after burning his paw on it. He has swept the hearth, helps set the table, and performs various chores and playful tasks around the cottage. He sits on a hassock by the fire while the group gathers for the grandmother's story.

CHAPTER IV.

<DW53> is very melancholy after discovering that the piece of squash pie he saved in the cupboard is gone. He suspects the squirrel because the cupboard was entered through a crack made by small, sharp teeth, but Miss Mary explains that two brown mice ate the pie. He plans to send the brown mice from the cottage cellar to the forest and agrees to attend the woodmouse family wedding with Toto.

CHAPTER V.

The raccoon refuses to sweep the red-hot coals from the hearth and tells the story of the Useful Coal instead. He then joins the dancing lesson and is instructed by Toto to hold his head up and stop looking at his tail.

" CHAPTER VI.

The raccoon accompanies Toto and Cracker to the wedding wearing Toto's red cravat. He is proud of the sensation he creates, is welcomed by Mr. Woodmouse, Senior, and behaves graciously toward the bride and groom. He dances a waltz with Toto.

CHAPTER VII.

<DW53> asks Toto what a comet is and sarcastically criticizes Toto's uncertain explanation. He listens to Toto's story of The Naughty Comet.

CHAPTER IX.

Bruin polished the kettle earlier that morning, making it shine like gold.

CHAPTER X.

<DW53> listens to Toto's account of the attempted robbery and receives Toto's praise for helping defend the cottage. He greets Jim Crow graciously, listens to Cracker's story, and comments humorously on the meaning of the word "low."

CHAPTER XIV.

The raccoon helps Toto decorate the cottage with ground-pine, fir-boughs, and alder-twigs. He tells how he fell through a hollow tree while collecting honey and was pulled out by Bruin, and he joins the games after helping arrange the decorations.
