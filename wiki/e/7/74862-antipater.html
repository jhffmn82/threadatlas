Antipater in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Antipater is a Macedonian commander, Alexander’s viceroy in Europe, and father of Cassander. He begins by gathering forces, marching through Macedonia into Thrace against Memnon, settling the Thracian war as he could, and entering Greece to defeat the confederates. He routs the Lacedemonians in a great battle in which Agis is killed, postpones their appeal to a Corinthian assembly, and takes leading Spartan nobles as hostages. As viceroy, he quarrels with Olympias; one account says he ordered his son, Alexander’s cup-bearer, to poison Alexander’s wine. When Greek defection occurs, he initially underestimates Leosthenes, is defeated, and retreats to Lamia. There he repairs the walls, gathers supplies, and holds out for reinforcements. After Craterus joins him, he defeats the Greeks, makes separate peace with Greek cities, compels Athens to surrender, and establishes an Athenian democracy limited by a property qualification. His government is later associated with oligarchies influenced by his friends and favorites, and with opponents of Athens’s democracy and ancient laws after the Lamian war. Antipater’s alliances and commands change amid wider conflict. He had allied with Perdiccas and arranged Perdiccas’s marriage to Nicaea, but later joins Ptolemy against Perdiccas after learning of plans against Ptolemy. With Craterus he campaigns against the Aetolians, temporarily makes peace with them, and prepares for Asia. After crossing into Asia, he receives Neoptolemus, divides the army with Craterus, and marches toward Cilicia against Perdiccas while Craterus faces Eumenes. Following Craterus’s defeat, he hastens to Cilicia. His general Polycles is then defeated and killed by the Aetolians. At Triparadisus, the Macedonians choose Antipater as protector with absolute authority. He suppresses a mutiny stirred by Eurydice and makes a second division of the provinces. Near his death, he appoints Polyperchon protector of the kings with chief authority and Cassander chiliarch, orders Demeas executed over letters linking him to opposition, and repeats his warning against Olympias governing. He dies amid cruel torments, accusing Timotheus of arranging a scheme to have a young man kill his brother. Polyperchon succeeds him in Macedonia.

CHAP. II.

Antipater allied with Ptolemy when he learned that Perdiccas intended to dispossess Ptolemy of Egypt. With Craterus, he defeated the Greeks, made separate peace with the Greek cities, compelled Athens to surrender, and imposed a property qualification on Athenian political participation.

Antipater in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Antipater, after the Greek reverse at Lamia, initially makes speedy peace with Athens and leaves Greece free to pursue war in Asia Minor. Persuaded by Demades and other Athenian traitors, he changes course; he also kills his mother Thessalonice for favoring Alexander.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER X.

After the Greek reverse at Lamia, Antipater wished to proceed quickly to the war in Asia Minor. He was initially content to arrange a speedy peace, since he did not regard it as important whether Athens or all Greece remained free, but Demades and other Athenian traitors persuaded him to act otherwise.
