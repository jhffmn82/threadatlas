Arabia in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Arabia is a country between Syria and Egypt, bordering Syria to the north and Egypt to the west or northwest. Partly desert and parched, it is divided into several nations and is constantly freedom-loving; its strong inhabitants resisted foreign rulers, while Ninus allied with Arieus and later returned him with spoils and gifts. Accounts then describe fertile, populous, pastoral, and coastal regions rich in aromatic plants, balsam, spices, gold, stones, fruits, cattle, and trade, but also serpents and coastal hazards. They end by naming peoples including Nabathcans, Sabeans, and others.

DIODORUS SICULUS. / BOOK IL

Arabia was described as inhabited by strong and freedom-loving people who resisted foreign rulers. Ninus allied with Arieus, king of Arabia, and later sent the Arabian king back with rich spoils and gifts.

CHAP. IV.

Arabia is described as lying between Syria and Egypt and as being divided into several nations. It contains desert and fertile regions, produces aromatic plants, balsam, gold, precious stones, cattle, unusual beasts, and valuable fruits, and supports farmers, shepherds, merchants, and traders.

Arabia in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Arabia is a place associated with dangerous wildlife and balsam: it may be the origin of the savage, people-killing Stymphelides, and it contains vipers near balsam trees whose sap-fed venom is said to be milder.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXII.

Arabia is said to contain savage birds called Stymphelides, which attack and kill people and can pierce coats of mail and thick robes of mat. The text suggests that birds formerly associated with Stymphelus may have flown from Arabia, though their appearance and original Arabian name are uncertain.

CHAPTER XXVIII.

Arabia is described as a place where vipers live near balsam trees. The vipers there feed on balsam sap, and their venom is said to have milder properties.
