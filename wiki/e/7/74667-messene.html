Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Messene was a Grecian city rebuilt and repopulated by Epaminondas and his allies. In The Library, Messene was the place from which Castor and Pollux carried off and married Leucippus’s daughters. In The Library, Messene was the third territory assigned to Cresphontes through his stratagem in the drawing of lots. In Description of Greece, Messene was the new capital of Messenia, rebuilt by the returning Messenians after Epaminondas selected its site. In Description of Greece, Messene was the homeland of the Messenians, later resisting Macedonians and Nabis and rejoining the Achaean League. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Messene was a destination of roads from Megalopolis with an unconquered citadel, restored by Epaminondas.

Messene in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Messene first appears

Abstract written by a model

Messene was the Messenians’ homeland, under Ithome and about 80 stades from the mouth of the Pamisus. During the siege of Eira it was defeated at the Great Trench; Theoclus read the oracle as ending its safety, and Aristomenes hid its sacred records for a hoped-for recovery. After Eira fell, it could not be recovered, and its name was given to former Zancle in Sicily. Later Messene repelled Demetrius’ Macedonians, received Arcadians from Megalopolis with equal rights, resisted Nabis, and rejoined the Achæan League. It had a stone circular wall, towers and battlements, and a nearby temple of Serapis and Isis.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XVI.

Messene suffers defeat at the Great Trench and faces the loss of its territory during the siege of Eira. Theoclus interprets the Pythian oracle as declaring that its period of safety has ended. Aristomenes hides sacred records so that Messene may one day have a chance to recover its country.

CHAPTER XXI.

Messene was the homeland the Messenians fought to preserve, but Theoclus and Aristomenes believed its destruction had been foretold. After Eira fell, the Messenians could no longer recover it, and the name was later given to the former Zancle in Sicily.

CHAPTER XXIX.

Messene repelled the Macedonian attack led by Demetrius, son of Philip. It later received the Arcadians who escaped from Megalopolis, granted them equal rights, resisted the Spartan tyrant Nabis, and eventually rejoined the Achæan League. The city is described as standing under Mount Ithome and surrounded by a strong stone wall with towers and battlements.

CHAPTER XXXIV.

Messene is about 80 stades from the mouth of the Pamisus.
