Eurydice in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Eurydice is a queen-regent in Macedonia and wife of Aridaeus and Philip III Arrhidaeus. She intervenes in government, seeks to control protectors, stirs Macedonians against Antipater, and claims greater fitness to rule than Olympias. She later seeks Cassander’s aid and wins supporters with bribes and promises. After Macedonians defect to Olympias, she is captured and imprisoned with Philip, tends his wounds, and kills herself with her garter when ordered to choose her death.

Eurydice interfered in matters of government and tried to control the protectors, causing Python and his friends to become disgusted. At Triparadisus she stirred the Macedonians to mutiny against Antipater, but he calmed the army and brought her to a more moderate temper.

Eurydice being queen-regent in Macedonia, as soon as she heard

As queen-regent in Macedonia, Eurydice sought Cassander's aid and used bribes and promises to win active Macedonians to her side. After the Macedonians defected to Olympias, Eurydice was captured at Amphipolis, imprisoned with Philip, and later ordered to choose between a sword, a rope, and poison; she bound Philip's wounds and strangled herself with her garter.
