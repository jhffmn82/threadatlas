Abstract written by a model

Granny is Toto’s blind, old and beloved grandmother, a family member who lives with Toto and the animals in a little cottage. She has snowy curls, an armchair in the warm chimney-corner, and, on special occasions, wears gold-bowed spectacles and uses an ebony stick. Her evenings center on knitting by the blazing hearth, working at a spinning-wheel, and telling the grandmother’s story, which she herself learned from her grandmother. At first she comes from her room with a squirrel on her shoulder, joins supper, and begins a story while enjoying the easier life the animals’ help has given her. She guides the household: she explains a Chinese pig-tail, sends everyone to bed, has Bruin cover the fire and lock the house, worries over disturbances and possible injuries, and later helps settle Toto and Cracker’s dispute. She welcomes Toto home, joins the group around the fire, and helps complete a poem. For a supper with guests, she prepares pastries, oversees the meal, and dresses carefully. By the end, she thanks the animals for their winter comfort and help, mourns Bruin’s apparent departure, then joyfully welcomes him back to live with her and Toto.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Toto's Merry Winter / CHAPTER I.

The blind grandmother comes from her room with the little squirrel on her shoulder and joins the others for supper. She has been enjoying a much easier life because the animals help with the work. She agrees to tell a story and begins one that was told to her by her own grandmother.

CHAPTER III.

The grandmother explains to Bruin that a Chinese pig-tail is a hairstyle, not a pig's tail. She sends everyone to bed at nine, asks Bruin to cover the fire and lock up the house, and takes Cracker to her room.

CHAPTER V.

The grandmother watches the dancing from her armchair in the chimney-corner and repeatedly worries that someone has been hurt. She has a custard pie that Bruin accidentally spoils and a platter that he breaks.

CHAPTER IX.

The grandmother naps in her room with the squirrel beside her. She wakes after hearing the disturbance, asks what happened, and returns to her room after Miss Mary assures her that the noise came from an excited acquaintance.

CHAPTER X.

The grandmother welcomes Toto home and joins the household around the fire after supper. She asks who will tell a story, supplies the missing line of a poem about the lowing herd, and helps settle the dispute between Toto and Cracker.

CHAPTER XIV.

The grandmother prepares pastries and oversees the supper while listening happily to the sounds of her guests eating. She dresses for the occasion in her snowy curls, cap, kerchief, gold-bowed spectacles, ebony stick, and a large breastpin made from her grandfather’s hair.

CHAPTER XV.

The blind grandmother warmly thanks the animals for comforting and helping her during the winter. She is deeply saddened when she thinks Bruin has left without saying good-by, but is delighted when Toto brings him back. She welcomes Bruin to remain with her and Toto in the cottage.
