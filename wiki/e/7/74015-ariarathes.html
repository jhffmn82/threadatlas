Abstract written by a model

Ariarathes is a Cappadocian prince and king, though the records combine several rulers of that name. One resisted the Macedonian empire, was defeated, captured, scourged, and crucified by Perdiccas. Another, adopted as lawful heir, fled to Armenia, recovered Cappadocia, married Stratonice, maintained goodwill and alliance with Rome, and opposed ties with Demetrius.

Ariarathes is in one work on this site, The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. II.

Ariarathes, prince of Cappadocia, resisted the Macedonian empire with an army of thirty thousand foot and fifteen thousand horse. Perdiccas defeated him, captured him, and had him and his kindred scourged and crucified.

BOOK XXXI.

Several rulers named Ariarathes appear in the Cappadocian genealogy. One was killed by Eumenes, while the adopted Ariarathes fled to Armenia and later recovered Cappadocia; a later Ariarathes became co-ruler with his father and then ruled alone, and another succeeded to the kingdom as a young child.
