Rango the Gray Ape in The Magic of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1919

Rango the Gray Ape is a wise, experienced gray ape, counselor to Gugu the Leopard, and chief of all the forest’s monkey tribes. He rejects the Li-Mon-Eags as mischief-makers, summons the forest beasts, recognizes a magician’s transformations, and seeks rescue for six monkeys. Though initially refusing to lend monkeys for Ozma’s birthday, he consents after assurances of their safe return.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

8. The Li-Mon-Eags Make Trouble

Rango rejects the Li-Mon-Eags as mischief-makers and advises Gugu to send them away. At Gugu's command, he goes to tell his monkey tribe to order the forest beasts to assemble in the Great Clearing at sunrise.

16. The Glass Cat Finds the Black Bag

Rango, the Chief of all the monkey tribes of the forest, recognizes that the strange magician caused the transformations and asks the Wizard to save the six monkeys. He initially refuses to lend monkeys for Ozma's birthday, but changes his mind after the Wizard promises to treat them well and return them safely.
