Abstract written by a model

Enterprise chatbots are generative AI applications and productivity tools in an enterprise context, intended to enhance employee productivity. They extend company search tools and use enterprise data to answer HR benefits, IT help, sales, engineering, financial earnings, and general enterprise-content questions. Earlier chatbots used dialog flows, intent training, information retrieval, and question answering; newer versions use Large Language Models, Retrieval Augmented Generation, and Langchain/Llamaindex orchestration frameworks for broader conversation. Their successful construction depends on meticulously engineered RAG pipelines and secure, flexible architectures that can support domain-specific, enterprise-wide, and copilot variations. The central challenges are preventing inaccurate or irrelevant responses, maintaining freshness, security, accuracy, and reliability, protecting sensitive data, managing latency and LLM costs, and demonstrating return on investment. Because responses require validation, RAG-based chatbots need lengthy security, regression, automated, ground-truth, and human-oversight testing. Future work targets agentic architectures, updated-data summarization, automated RAG optimization, and stronger evaluation frameworks.

ABSTRACT

Enterprise chatbots powered by generative AI are emerging as widely explored applications for improving employee productivity. NVIDIA built chatbots for IT and HR benefits, company financial earnings, and general enterprise content, and the unit presents a framework for building secure, enterprise-grade RAG-based chatbots.

INTRODUCTION

Enterprise chatbots are increasingly used as extensions of company search tools for HR benefits, IT help, sales queries, and engineering issues. Earlier systems relied on dialog flows, intent training, information retrieval, and question answering, while newer systems use LLMs and retrieval augmented generation to provide broader conversational capabilities.

ENSURING FRESHNESS OF ENTERPRISE .. 3.1

LLM-powered chatbots can provide inaccurate or irrelevant responses when their retrieval and generation processes are not carefully controlled. The unit discusses methods for improving their freshness, security, accuracy, and reliability.

BUILDING FLEXIBLE ARCHITECTURES FOR

Chatbots are being built by multiple departments and can be developed as domain-specific, enterprise-wide, or copilot variations. NVBot supports their coexistence and orchestration within an enterprise.

COST ECONOMICS OF CHATBOT

Generative AI-based chatbots can incur significant costs when they rely on major or commercial LLMs across multiple use cases. Their deployments also require attention to sensitive-data protection, latency, and return on investment.

TESTING RAG-BASED CHATBOTS (T) .. 6.1

RAG-based chatbots require lengthy testing because their responses need validation. They should be tested through security testing, prompt-change regression testing, representative ground truth datasets, automated evaluations, and human oversight.

CONCLUSIONS

RAG-based chatbots are presented as requiring attention to content freshness, architecture, LLM cost, testing, and security. Future work includes developing agentic architectures, improving summarization of frequently updated enterprise data, automating RAG optimization, and strengthening evaluation frameworks.

Abstract written by a model

Enterprise generative AI-based chatbots are retrieval-augmented generation chatbots examined in a practitioner-focused investigation of building and deployment. They require careful design, thorough evaluation, guardrails, and flexible, adaptive architectures as departments develop them amid a rapidly evolving AI landscape. The central concerns are RAG engineering, knowledge relevance, access permissions, concise referenced responses, privacy, user engagement, speed, cost, and enterprise content security. They remain vulnerable to jailbreaks, adversarial attacks, security issues, and derivative risks despite improving foundational-model guardrails. During response generation, they must comply with document Access Control Lists to protect sensitive enterprise data. The work positions secure enterprise-grade chatbots within the FACTS framework and presents a reference architecture.
