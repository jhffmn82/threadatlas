Sicyon in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Sicyon, ruled by Epopeus, passed from Cratesipolis to Ptolemy, who garrisoned it. Demetrius then captured it by night; its garrison withdrew to the castle and surrendered. He moved inhabitants there, leveled the harbor-side city, aided rebuilding, and renamed it Demetriades. It executes innovators only after full conviction.

Sicyon was captured by Demetrius in a night attack, and its garrison withdrew into the castle before surrendering. Demetrius persuaded the inhabitants to move into the castle, leveled the part of the city near the harbor, and helped them rebuild; the city was renamed Demetriades in his honor before later circumstances ended the associated ceremonies.

Sicyon (city) in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Sicyon, first built in the plain by Ægialeus, was later razed by Demetrius, who built the present city on the citadel, with temples, tombs, theaters, a market-place, gymnasium, and sanctuaries.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

Sicyon was first built by Ægialeus in the plain, while Demetrius the son of Antigonus later destroyed that settlement and built the present city on the former citadel. The city contained numerous temples, tombs, theaters, a market-place, a gymnasium, and sanctuaries dedicated to several gods and heroes.

Sicyon in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Sicyon is a place near Corinth, connected by road to Phlius and serving as the starting point for roads to Phlius and Titane. Its people preserve traditions of Ægialeus founding Ægialea and of a burnt temple of Apollo on the citadel. Nearby temples and groves honor Hera, Demeter, Proserpine, and the Eumenides.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

And as you go from Corinth along the coast in the direction of Sicyon

Sicyon is near Corinth, and its people preserve traditions about their region and early rulers. They say that Ægialeus founded Ægialea in the plain and that the site of the burnt temple of Apollo was the city’s citadel.

CHAPTER XI.

Sicyon was the starting point for roads to Phlius and Titane. Near Sicyon were temples and groves dedicated to Demeter, Proserpine, and the Eumenides, whom the Sicyonians called the Eumenides and worshipped with sacrifices, libations, and flowers.
