Olynthians

group · 1 work · 3 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: OlynthU ans, Olyuthians, the Olyn- thians

Abstract written by a model

The Olynthians, defended by Olynthus, retained Amyntas’s grant, refused its return, and defeated Eudamidas, Amyntas, and Teleutias while mobilizing confederate aid. Later defeated by Pelopidas and subjected to the Lacedaemonians, they became rich, powerful allies of Philip, who promised Potidea and gave Pydna. Philip then defeated them twice, besieged and captured betrayed Olynthus, and sold them as slaves.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. n.

The Olynthians retained land previously granted by Amyntas and refused to return it when he recovered his power. They defeated forces led by Eudamidas and Amyntas and later defeated Teleutias, while preparing provisions and aid from their confederates for a prolonged war.

CHAR III.

The Olynthians were defeated by Pelopidas, driven within their walls, and forced to submit to the Lacedaemonians as sovereign lords. They were then enrolled among the Spartan confederates.

CHAP. 11.

The Olynthians were considered rich, powerful, and populous, making them valuable allies. Philip made a league with them and promised them Potidea and later gave them Pydna with its grounds and territories.

CHAP. X.

The Olynthians were defeated by Philip in two battles before he besieged Olynthus. After the city's betrayal and capture, its citizens were sold into slavery.
