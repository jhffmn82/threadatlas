Antigonus's army in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Antigonus's army first appears

Abstract written by a model

Antigonus's army was a large, mixed military force of infantry, cavalry, elephants, and troops from several regions. It approached Eumenes's army but was delayed by a river, trench, and bad ground, skirmishing and foraging before pursuit; it later numbered over 28,000 foot, 8,500 horse, and 65 elephants. Though its left wing and phalanx were routed, remaining forces counterattacked, rallied, buried the dead, and marched to provisioned Gamarga. It then marched via Babylon, captured Gaza and Joppa, besieged Tyre, built a fleet, and finally turned back toward Syria after hardship in the desert toward Egypt.

Antigonus's army included cavalry from Media, Armenia, Phrygia, Lydia, and other regions; Tarentines; Macedonian-style infantry; Macedonians; elephants; and other troops. Its left wing and phalanx were routed, but Antigonus's remaining forces counterattacked, rallied, buried their dead, and marched to Gamarga in Media, where provisions were plentiful.

CHAP. IV,

Antigonus's army marched from Susiana to Babylon and then toward Cilicia. It captured Gaza and Joppa, maintained the siege of Tyre, and supported the construction of a large fleet in Phoenicia.

CHAR IV,

Antigonus’s army crossed the desert toward Egypt but suffered from difficult ground, scarcity of food and fodder, and desertions encouraged by Ptolemy’s offers of payment. Its soldiers and commanders ultimately favored returning to Syria.
