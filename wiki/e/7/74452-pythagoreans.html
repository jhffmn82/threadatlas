Pythagoreans

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, the Pythagoreans avoided oaths as much as possible, supported members who fell into poverty, sometimes risked their lives for one another, practiced memory exercises, abstinence, moderation, and moral purity, and were highly esteemed by the Crotonians. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Pythagoreans formed a society pursuing wisdom, sharing property, practicing purity and simplicity, requiring novices to remain silent and accept Pythagoras's authority without proof, and killing no animals because they believed in transmigration of souls.

Pythagoreans in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Pythagoreans first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Pythagoreans formed a society pursuing wisdom, sharing property and practicing purity and simplicity. Novices first remained silent, accepted Pythagoras’s authority without proof, and killed no animals because they believed in transmigration of souls.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXVII

The Pythagoreans formed a society devoted to the pursuit of wisdom, shared their property, and practiced purity and simplicity. They were initially required to remain silent and accept the authority of Pythagoras, and they killed no animals because of their belief in the transmigration of souls.
