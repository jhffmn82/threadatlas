Abe Slaney

person · 1 work · 10 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Mr. Abe Slaney, Slaney, the American, the criminal, the most dangerous crook in Chicago, the prisoner, the rascal, the writer

Abstract written by a model

Abe Slaney is an American criminal, formerly linked to a seven-member Chicago gang and described by Wilson Hargreave as Chicago’s most dangerous crook. He loves Mrs. Hilton Cubitt, to whom he had been engaged before her marriage, but threatens her after she rejects him. Living at Elrige’s Farm, he writes the dancing men messages and communicates with Elsie Cubitt. Holmes identifies him, summons him with a coded note, and has him arrested. Slaney says Hilton Cubitt fired first and that he then shot him, while denying harm to Mrs. Hilton Cubitt. Though condemned to death, he receives penal servitude after mitigation.

Abe Slaney is in one work on this site, The Return of Sherlock Holmes. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Abe Slaney is identified as the writer of the dancing men messages and as the person who had been communicating with Elsie Cubitt. Holmes learns from Wilson Hargreave that Slaney is known as the most dangerous crook in Chicago, and he sends a note summoning him at Elrige’s Farm.

Abe Slaney comes to the house in response to Holmes's coded note and is arrested by Holmes and Inspector Martin. He says that Hilton Cubitt fired first during a confrontation and that Slaney then shot him, while denying that he harmed Mrs. Hilton Cubitt. He is later condemned to death, but his penalty is changed to penal servitude because of mitigating circumstances and the certainty that Hilton Cubitt fired first.
