Magic Flower in The Magic of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1919

Magic Flower is a curious, beautiful magic plant and flower that grows in a large, heavy golden flower-pot. It begins on a small island in a river running through a forest in the northern Munchkin Country, on the Magic Isle. Continuously, day and night, it changes from one kind of blossom to another, showing strikingly bright, glowing colors, unusual shapes, and successive varieties including peonies, lilies, chrysanthemums, daffodils, tulips, gladioli, yellow roses, and violets. Its flowers alternate with fruit: when Trot wishes for fruit, it produces many kinds for only a while; picked fruit remains good, but fruit left on the plant disappears, and the plant returns to flowers. Trot, Cap'n Bill, and the Glass Cat seek it as Ozma's birthday present, but their effort brings them into the magic of the queer island. Cap'n Bill eventually retrieves it. Because the pot is too heavy to carry, it is transported on a fitted board tied to the Cowardly Lion's back. Moved to Trot's room in the Royal Palace, it keeps its magic. At Ozma's birthday banquet it produces fragrant mammoth blossoms, fills the room with delicate fragrance, and is given to Ozma, who plans to place it in her boudoir.

The Magic Flower grows in a golden flower-pot and produces different kinds of flowers one after another, continuously, day and night. It is hidden on a small island in a river running through a forest in the northern Munchkin Country.

9. The Isle of the Magic Flower

The Magic Flower is in a gold flower-pot at the center of the island and is described as curious and beautiful. It changes from one kind of flower to another, producing glimpses of glowing colors.

10. Stuck Fast

The Magic Flower grows on the island and displays flowers of strikingly bright colors and varied, unusual shapes. Trot, Cap'n Bill, and the Glass Cat reach it intending to take it as a birthday present for Ozma.

15. The Lonesome Duck

The Magic Flower continually changes its blossoms, producing peonies, lilies, chrysanthemums, and other flowers. When Trot wishes that it would bear fruit for a while, it produces many kinds of fruit, which remain good after being picked but disappear if left on the plant. After a variety of fruits appears, the plant returns to producing flowers.

16. The Glass Cat Finds the Black Bag

The Magic Flower is being sought by Trot and Cap'n Bill as a gift for Ozma's birthday. Their attempt to obtain it leads to their being trapped by the magic of a queer island.

19. Dorothy and the Bumble Bees

The Magic Flower grows different flowers in succession, including daffodils, tulips, gladioli, yellow roses, and violets. Cap'n Bill retrieves it as a birthday present for Ozma.

20. The Monkeys Have Trouble

The Magic Flower is too heavy for Cap'n Bill to carry all the way, so it is placed in its flower-pot on a specially fitted board tied to the Cowardly Lion's back. Cap'n Bill walks beside the Lion and holds the flower-pot to prevent accidents.

21. The College of Athletic Arts

The Magic Flower was moved from its island to Trot's room in the Royal Palace. It did not seem to lose any of its magic after being moved, and Trot believed Ozma would prize it as a delightful treasure.

22. Ozma's Birthday Party

The Magic Flower blooms gloriously during the banquet, producing mammoth blossoms that fill the room with delicate fragrance. Ozma receives it as a birthday gift and says she will place it in her boudoir.

the Magic Plant in The Magic of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1919

The Magic Plant, called the world's most beautiful by the Cowardly Lion, grows in a golden flower-pot on the Magic Isle. It rapidly produces hundreds of varied flowers: picked blooms remain perfect a long time, while unpicked ones soon disappear and are replaced.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

17. A Remarkable Journey

The Magic Plant grows in a golden flower-pot on the Magic Isle and is said to produce hundreds of flowers of different kinds and sizes in quick succession. Flowers picked while blooming remain perfect for a long time, while unpicked flowers soon disappear and are replaced.
