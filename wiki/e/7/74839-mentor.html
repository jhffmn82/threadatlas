Mentor in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Mentor is a commander whose shifting service shapes the campaigns of Egypt and Asia. After aiding Sidon against Persian commanders with Greek mercenaries, he secretly offers to betray Sidon to Artaxerxes and help conquer Egypt. He commands Persian forces, recovers Bubastis and other cities by stratagem, gains authority over Asia, allies with Bagoas, suppresses rebellions, and captures Hermias to recover Atarnea’s revolted cities and forts.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

In Asia, the Persian king having invaded ligypt some years before

Mentor the Rhodian commanded four thousand Greek mercenaries supplied by the Egyptians and helped Sidon defeat the Persian commanders in an earlier engagement. Later, he secretly offered to betray Sidon to Artaxerxes and promised to help him conquer Egypt because he knew its places and Nile crossings.

CHAP. IX.

Mentor commanded the third brigade in the Persian invasion of Egypt and recovered Bubastis and many other cities through stratagem. Artaxerxes rewarded him with high authority over the Asiatic shore and made him general with power to suppress rebellions. Mentor later captured Hermias by deception and recovered the revolted cities and forts of Atarnea.
