In Sicily, the Syracusans, labouring under intestine seditions, and / ^

Timoleon, son of Timodemus, was regarded as Corinth's most valiant and experienced commander. He opposed monarchy, advised Timophanes to abandon his designs, killed him in the market-place, and was then sent to Sicily; there he managed affairs with honour and advantage to the Sicilians, subdued the Carthaginians, rebuilt destroyed Grecian cities, and restored Sicily to liberty.

CHAP. XI.

Timoleon sailed from Corinth to Sicily with four galleys, seven hundred mercenaries, and three skiffs, later joined by three vessels from the Leucadians and Corcyrians. He escaped the Carthaginians at Rhegium, defeated Hicetas near Adranum with a much smaller force, entered Syracuse, and eventually gained control of the city. He secured Dionysius's surrender, destroyed the tyrant's strongholds, freed the towns from garrisons, and organized democratic laws.

CHAP. XII.

Timoleon campaigned against the Leontines and Hicetas, besieged the new city and Engyum, and forced Leptines into exile. He received Apolloniadcs and Engyum into his protection, restored them to liberty, raided Carthaginian territory to obtain money for his soldiers, and captured Entella. His reputation grew until Greek cities throughout Sicily submitted to him, after which he allowed them to govern by their own laws.

CHAP, XIII.

Timoleon settled his conflict with Hicetas, led about twelve thousand men against the Carthaginians, overcame a mutiny, and won a major victory over their army. He later expelled deserters, killed the pirate Posthumous, made peace with the Carthaginians, subdued Hicetas and other tyrants, freed Sicilian cities, revised Syracuse's laws, and promoted colonization and prosperity.

CHAP. XIV.

Timoleon settled affairs in Syracuse and died after governing there for eight years. The Syracusans honored him with an elaborate funeral and decreed annual commemorations with music, horse-racing, and gymnastic contests.

BOOK XIV.

Timoleon went to Sicily, defeated Hicetas, and gained Syracuse. He expelled Dionysius, made good laws, defeated the Carthaginians in a remarkable victory, and died after his actions in Sicily.
