Macedonian kings in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Macedonian kings are a royal group whose authority is exercised through commanders and protectors during campaigns and conflicts. Perdiccas plans to take them on the expedition against Ptolemy in Egypt. They later travel from the Nile to Triparadisus with Aridaeus, Python, and the army, then toward Macedonia with Antipater. After Antipater’s death, Polyperchon protects them while Antigonus seeks to disregard their authority. Acting through the kings, Polyperchon promotes peace, restored Greek governments, recalled exiles, and settlements. The kings empower Eumenes with money, troops, and full command in Asia, and remain with Polyperchon in Phocis as he seeks support in Attica and the Peloponnesus.

The kings came with Aridaeus, Python, and the army from the Nile to Triparadisus. Antipater later marched with them toward Macedonia to conduct them back into their own country, while Antigonus was appointed commander of the royal army.

CHAP. IV.

The kings were placed under Polyperchon’s protection after Antipater’s death. Antigonus intended to disregard their authority, while Polyperchon and Eumenes acted to defend their interests against those seeking independent power.

The kings were presented as granting peace, restoring Greek governments, recalling exiles, and directing the promised settlement. Polyperchon acted as their protector and was ordered to manage these matters.

CHAP. V.

The kings gave Eumenes five hundred talents, ordered the governors and treasurers of Cilicia to fund his needs, and placed a thousand Macedonian Argyraspides under his authority. They appointed him general with full power over Asia and commanded the Macedonians to obey him.

The kings remained with Polyperchon in Phocis and were supported by him in his efforts to control Attica and the Peloponnesus. Cities that followed Polyperchon were ordered to restore their ancient laws and support the kings.
