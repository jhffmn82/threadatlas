Diodorus Siculus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Diodorus Siculus is presented as a historian and author of historical fragments, including lost books later recovered as fragments and eclogues. His writings cover Asia over approximately 244 years and include accounts of Evemerus’s theology, the Corinthian kingdom, and Roman origins. He is named among historians and learned men, quoted by Eusebius Pamphilus through a Book VI fragment, and identified in chapter and book headings. His surviving fragments were later organized under historical subjects, including Of Virtues and Vices, under Constantine the seventh and published by Valesius.

Diodorus Siculus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Diodorus Siculus is a Greek historian who lived in the time of Julius Caesar and Augustus and wrote a forty-book general history from the beginning of the world to his own time. Fifteen books survive, while five books immediately before Book XI are lost. His work addresses the gods, the origin of mankind, mythology, ancient heroes and famous men, antiquities of the Grecians, Egypt, Ethiopians, islands, and the affairs of the known world. He acknowledges uncertainty over the first kings and most ancient nation, and stresses the difficulty of reconciling conflicting accounts, genealogies, and chronologies. He defends historical truth, urges charitable judgment of earlier historians, and criticizes careless writers. Having visited Egypt under Ptolemy Dionysius the younger and later Ptolemy Lagus, he reports Egyptian priests and other writers on Egypt's customs, rulers, temples, and sepulchres, and consulted Ethiopian priests. The extant sequence begins with gods and humanity's origins, proceeds through Egypt and Greek antiquities, including Dionysius, then Book V's islands beginning with Sicily, and Book XI's account from Xerxes's expedition into Greece to the year before Cymon's invasion of Cyprus. It also includes Lambulus's adventures and discussion of Ethiopians in Book III.

Narrative, section by section

front matter

Diodorus Siculus is described as a Greek historian who lived about sixty years before the birth of Christ, during the time of Julius Caesar and the reign of Augustus. He wrote a forty-book general history, but only fifteen books remain; the unit also characterizes him as an author attentive to divine providence and to the periods in which notable historians, philosophers, and poets flourished.

DIODORUS SICULUS. / BOOK L

Diodorus Siculus states that he will discuss the gods, the origin of mankind, and the affairs of the known world from the most ancient times. He says that he cannot determine with certainty who the first kings were or which nation was the most ancient.

DIODORUS SICULUS. / BOOK I.

Diodorus Siculus reports what the Egyptian priests and other writers say about Egypt's ancient customs, rulers, temples, and sepulchres. He states that he himself came into Egypt during the reign of Ptolemy, who took the name of Dionysius the younger, and later during the reign of Ptolemy Lagus.

DIODORUS SICULUS- / BOOK IV.

Diodorus Siculus states that ancient history is difficult to compile because accounts, genealogies, and chronologies often differ. He resolves to relate the antiquities of the Grecians and begins with Dionysius because he considers him the most ancient and the greatest benefactor to mankind.

DIODORUS SICULUS. .. BOOK. V.

Diodorus Siculus presents Book V as a description of the islands and begins with Sicily, emphasizing orderly historical arrangement and the usefulness of historical writing.

LAST TEN BOOKS / BOOK XI.

Diodorus Siculus presents Book XI as a continuation of his history, beginning with Xerxes's expedition into Greece and ending the year before the Athenians under Cymon invaded Cyprus.
