Chares in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Chares was an Athenian general and fleet commander. He replaced Leosthenes and led a fleet against Alexander, frightening enemies but oppressing allies at Corcyra, causing sedition, slaughter, plunder, and harm to Athens’s reputation. During the siege of Chios he commanded land forces, assaulted the walls, and fought the garrison. Sharing fleet command with Iphicrates and Timotheus, he urged battle in a storm, accused them of treason, later gained sole command, and joined Pharnabazus against Persian troops; the Persian king’s complaint helped end the Social War.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAR XL

Chares replaced Leosthenes as Athenian commander and was sent with a fleet against Alexander. He spent his time frightening the enemy and oppressing allies, especially at Corcyra, where his actions caused sedition, slaughter, plunder, and damage to Athenian reputation.

CHAP. 11.

Chares commanded the Athenian land forces during the siege of Chios. He assaulted the walls and fought the garrison in the open field.

CHAP. V.

Chares commanded an Athenian fleet of sixty ships and held equal authority with Iphicrates and Timotheus. He wanted to fight despite the storm, accused his colleagues of treason when they refused, and later joined Pharnabazus to defeat the Persian king's troops. Pharnabazus gave him enough money to pay all his soldiers, but the Persian king's complaint helped lead the Athenians to end the Social War.
