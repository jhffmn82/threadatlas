Agesipolis in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Agesipolis, king of Lacedaemon, opposed oppressing Greeks and urged peace; later he commanded Lacedaemonians against Olynthians, dying in his fourteenth regnal year.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAP. n.

Agesipolis opposed the oppression of the Greeks and argued that Sparta would become infamous if it enslaved Greek cities. Later, he was appointed general of the Lacedaemonian army sent against the Olynthians.

Agesipolis in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Agesipolis, king of Sparta and son of Pausanias’ royal successor, came of age, invaded Argos but withdrew after disasters, then campaigned successfully in Chalcidice and died suddenly near Olynthus.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK III.--LACONIA.

Agesipolis succeeded to the kingship after coming of age and first invaded Argos, where earthquakes and lightning-struck soldiers forced his withdrawal. He later campaigned successfully against the Olynthians and other cities in Chalcidice but died suddenly while hoping to capture Olynthus.

Agesipolis in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Agesipolis, son of Pausanias and king of Sparta, invaded and defeated Mantinea, capturing it by diverting the Ophis through its walls so unbaked brick dissolved.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VIII.

Agesipolis, son of Pausanias and king of Sparta, invaded the territory of Mantinea, defeated the Mantineans, and captured their city. He did so by turning the river Ophis through the city walls, causing the unbaked brick to dissolve.
