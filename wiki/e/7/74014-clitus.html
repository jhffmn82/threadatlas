Clitus

person · 1 work · 8 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Cli- tus, Clitos, Clitus the lord-lieutenant

Abstract written by a model

Clitus, a Macedonian raised by Lanice, saved Alexander at the Granicus and credited Macedonians for many of Alexander’s achievements. He opposed Alexander’s barbarian customs, hated flatterers, and, while intoxicated, defended Philip and ancient heroes, disparaged Alexander, and was killed by him. Later, as Polyperchon’s admiral, Clitus commanded the Macedonian fleet and the Hellespont, won sea fights at the Echinades and near Byzantium, then was surprised by Antigonus, fled his ship, and was killed by Lysimachus’s soldiers.

Clitus is in one work on this site, The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Clitus commanded Polyperchon’s fleet near the Hellespont and strengthened his army with the forces of Aridaeus. He defeated the opposing fleet near Byzantium, but was later surprised by Antigonus’s land and naval attacks, abandoned his ship, and was killed after encountering soldiers of Lysimachus.

SUP. VIIL

Clitus opposed Alexander's imitation of barbarian customs and the flatteries directed toward him. While intoxicated, he defended the ancient heroes and Philip, disparaged Alexander's achievements, and claimed that his own hand had saved Alexander at the Granicus before Alexander killed him.
