Ozma’s loss in The Lost Princess of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1917 · the work’s page · read it where Ozma’s loss first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ozma’s loss is the sudden, unexplained disappearance of Oz’s ruler from the Emerald City. It is discovered when Dorothy finds Ozma’s royal suite empty; Ozma has not been seen since the previous evening, and her Magic Picture is missing. The loss leaves the people of the Emerald City grieved and anxious, while even Ozma’s closest friends do not know what became of her. The Wizard seeks Glinda’s help, but the Great Book of Records is also missing and the available magic cannot locate Ozma. Her friends conclude she was likely abducted by someone with magic powerful enough to prevent her escape. Glinda and several parties search Oz, while Dorothy and the Wizard continue despite uncertainty. Suspicion gradually turns toward Ugu the Shoemaker, whose ambition and possible theft of magical things link him to Ozma’s disappearance and the stolen magic of the Emerald City.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 1

Ozma’s disappearance is discovered when Dorothy finds her bedchamber and every other room in her royal suite vacant. No one has seen Ozma since she went to her rooms the previous evening, and the Magic Picture that could have located her is also missing.

CHAPTER 2

Ozma suddenly disappears from the Emerald City, and no one there knows what has become of her. The Wizard of Oz seeks Glinda's assistance, but the missing Great Book of Records and Ozma's Magic Picture cannot be used to locate her.

CHAPTER 5

Ozma’s friends cannot explain her disappearance except by supposing that she was abducted against her will. They reason that whoever took her must have been more powerful in magic than Ozma or otherwise able to prevent her escape.

CHAPTER 6

Ozma’s loss leads Glinda and several groups of companions to begin searches across different regions of Oz. The people of the Emerald City are grieved and anxious for her recovery.

CHAPTER 11

Ozma's disappearance remains unexplained in this unit, and the travelers continue trying to find her. Dorothy suggests that she could be held in a dungeon, while the Wizard avoids discouraging the group despite lacking his magic tools.

CHAPTER 12

Ozma’s suspected theft is linked by the travelers to Ugu the Shoemaker, who is ambitious to become the most powerful magician in the world. Vig cannot confirm that Ugu stole Ozma but believes he might steal magical things if he could.

CHAPTER 17

Ugu is believed to have stolen Ozma and all the magic in the Emerald City. The theft is connected with the other stolen magic in Oz and motivates Dorothy's party's search for Ugu.
