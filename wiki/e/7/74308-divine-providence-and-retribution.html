punishes Agathocles [for murdering Ophelas] Divine providence and retribution are presented as governing the reversals in Agathocles's fortunes. The text treats the loss of his sons and army on the anniversary of Ophelas's murder, and the destruction of his sons by men associated with Ophelas, as a double punishment for his crime.
