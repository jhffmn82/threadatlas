Abstract written by a model

Agathocles’s tyranny in Syracuse and Sicily is presented as a remarkable overthrow of democracy toward monarchy. Though he sometimes appeared among ordinary people without royal insignia, fear of opposition drove him to rely on mercenaries, massacre suspected enemies, oppress others, and kill relatives.

The preface states that powerful men who rise above others may overturn democracies and seek monarchy. It presents Agathocles’s tyranny at Syracuse as an especially remarkable example, marked by massacres, oppression, and the killing of his relatives.
