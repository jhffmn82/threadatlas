siege of Rhodes in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where siege of Rhodes first appears

Abstract written by a model

The siege of Rhodes began when Demetrius landed, encamped, plundered the island, destroyed nearby towns and trees, fortified his camp, and enlarged the harbor. He attacked by land and sea with engines, ships, towers, ladders, mines, and the Helepolis. Rhodian resistance, counterattacks, damaged engines and ships, storms, and reinforcements prevented capture, ending in negotiated peace.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAR IV,

The siege of Rhodes began when Demetrius landed and encamped near the city. He sent forces to plunder the island, destroyed nearby towns and trees, fortified his camp with trenches and ramparts, and enlarged the harbor for his ships.

In the mean time the Rhodians sent ambassadors to him, entreat*

The siege involved attacks from both land and sea, including battering engines, stone-throwing machines, towers, scaling ladders, firebrands, and fighting ships. Demetrius assaulted Rhodes for eight days and later renewed his attacks, but Rhodian counterattacks, damaged engines and ships, storms, and reinforcements prevented him from taking the city.

CFIAP. V.

Demetrius intensified the siege by land and sea with the Helepolis, battering rams, Testudoes, galleries, mines, and other engines. The Rhodians repeatedly resisted his attacks, and the siege ended with a negotiated peace.
