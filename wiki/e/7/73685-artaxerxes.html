Artaxerxes in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Artaxerxes is king of Persia, ruling and commanding Persians. He fought Cyrus for the Persian throne, organized a large army and fleet against Evagoras, and later faced revolts by governors, commanders, and coastal peoples. Seeking mercenaries for war with Egypt, he promoted and negotiated peace among Greek cities. He ordered expeditions against the Egyptians, appointing Pharnabazus commander-in-chief and Iphicrates over Greek mercenaries; he also appointed Tachos as general, later accepted Tachos after his flight from Egypt, and used him against the Egyptians. He ordered Orontas to seize Teribazus, tried and then restored Teribazus, and arranged Datames's death by ambush. After forty-three years of reign, he died following the Egyptian and Greek conflicts.

Artaxerxes, king of Persia, organized the expedition against Evagoras, providing a large army and fleet. He later ordered Orontas to seize Teribazus, tried Teribazus after the war with the Cadusians, and restored him to high honor after his acquittal.

The Persian king appointed Pharnabazus commander-in-chief for the war against Egypt. The Persian king was reportedly alienated from Athens by Chabrias's service under Acoris.

CHAP. IV.

Artaxerxes sought to quiet Greece so that he could recruit more mercenaries for his war against the Egyptians. He sent ambassadors to negotiate peace among the Greek cities.

CHAP. V.

The king of Persia ordered an expedition against the Egyptians, placing Pharnabazus over the barbarians and Iphicrates over twenty thousand Greek mercenaries.

CHAR XL

Artaxerxes faced revolts by governors, commanders, and coastal peoples, as well as war with Tachos and the Lacedaemonians. He accepted Tachos after Tachos fled Egypt, used him against the Egyptians, and later arranged Datames's death by ambush. He died after reigning forty-three years.

BOOK XIV.

Artaxerxes fought Cyrus for the Persian throne and later sought repeatedly to establish peace among the Greeks. He conducted expeditions against Evagoras and Egypt and died after the Egyptian and Greek conflicts described in the text.

Artaxerxes in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Artaxerxes, son of Darius and Persian king, responded to Agesilaus’s victories by executing Tissaphernes and funding Timocrates to provoke war against the Lacedæmonians.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

Artaxerxes, son of Darius, was the Persian king whom the Lacedæmonians intended to attack in Asia. After learning of Agesilaus’ victories, he had Tissaphernes condemned to death and sent Timocrates with money to stir up war against the Lacedæmonians in Greece.
