Nicanor in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Nicanor is a Stagira native and messenger who proclaimed Alexander’s restoration order. He held Munychia for Cassander as governor of its garrison, commanding troops and later a fleet. When Polyperchon was expected, he urged Athens to stay allied with Cassander, delayed it with promises, reinforced Munychia, seized the Piraeus walls and harbor entrance, and said he lacked authority to negotiate independently; he also postponed surrendering these places to Olympias. He received Cassander at the fort and retained it with a defensive force. Elsewhere he governed Alexandria for Alexander and Media for Antigonus, then marched from Media against Seleucus with ten thousand infantry and seven thousand cavalry. After surprise and desertion near the Tigris, he fled; later, suspected of revolt, he was trapped and killed by Cassander.

While these things were acting, Nicanor (who held Muayohis)

Nicanor, who held Munychia for Cassander, urged the Athenians to remain allied with Cassander after hearing that Cassander had left Macedonia and that Polyperchon was expected in Attica. He delayed them with promises, secretly reinforced Munychia, and then seized the walls of the Piraeus and the harbor entrance. He claimed that he had no authority to negotiate independently and later repeatedly postponed obeying Olympias’s order to surrender Munychia and the Piraeus.

After this, Cassander having got five*and-thirty long ships and

Nicanor, governor of the fort, received Cassander and retained Munychia with enough troops to defend it. He later commanded a fleet against Clitus, joined with Antigonus’s navy, and was eventually trapped and killed by Cassander after Cassander suspected him of intending to revolt.

In Asia, Sclcucus after the routing of Demetrius at Gaza in Syria,

Nicanor marched against Seleucus from Media with ten thousand foot and seven thousand horse. After Seleucus's surprise attack near the Tigris, many of Nicanor's soldiers deserted, and Nicanor fled through the desert with some companions.
