Cleonymus

person · 2 works · 7 quoted facts · 3 connections

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Cleonymus was a Lacedæmonian general supplied to the Tarentines who commanded forces in Italy, seized Corcyra, and returned to Italy after revolts. In Description of Greece, Cleonymus was a Lacedæmonian of the royal house who disputed Sparta’s kingship with Areus, persuaded Pyrrhus to invade the Peloponnese, and harmed his country.

Cleonymus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Cleonymus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cleonymus, supplied by the Lacedæmonians as general to the Tarentines, commanded over twenty thousand infantry and two thousand cavalry, joined Messapians in Italy, seized Corcyra, rejected alliances, and returned to Italy after revolts.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

In Sicily, Agathocles on a sudden, and without any provocation,

Cleonymus was sent by the Spartans to command the Tarentines and raised more than twenty thousand infantry and two thousand cavalry. He seized Corcyra, rejected separate offers of alliance from Demetrius and Cassander, and later returned to Italy after learning that Tarentines and others had revolted.

Cleonymus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Cleonymus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cleonymus, a Lacedæmonian of the royal house and son of Cleomenes, disputed Sparta’s kingship with Areus but was excluded when the Senate chose Areus. Resentful, he persuaded Pyrrhus to enter the Peloponnese and Sparta to seek the kingdom, harming his country and razing Zarax.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIII.

Cleonymus, a Lacedæmonian excluded from the kingship after a dispute with Areus, called Pyrrhus into the country in an attempt to obtain the kingdom. He had earlier persuaded Pyrrhus to leave Macedonia and come to the Peloponnese.

CHAPTER VI.

Cleonymus, son of Cleomenes, disputed the succession with Areus, son of Acrotatus, but the Senate awarded the hereditary kingship to Areus. Resentful of his exclusion, he eventually harmed his country and invited Pyrrhus, grandson of Æacus, into Sparta.
