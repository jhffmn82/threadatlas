Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, the inhabitants of the Lipari islands are described as having been attacked by Agathocles while at peace and security, with their sacred treasury plundered and eleven ships carrying the extorted money destroyed by a storm. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Aeolian Islands are described as seven islands in the Tyrrhenian seas between Sicily and Italy, about one hundred and fifty furlongs from Sicily, marked by visible volcanic openings and eruptions and linked by tradition with Liparus and Aeolus.

DIODORUS SICULUS. .. BOOK. V.

The Aeolian Islands are said to number seven: Strongyle, Euonymos, Didyme, Phoenicusa, Hiera, Ericusa, and Lipara. They lie between Sicily and Italy, are associated with volcanic eruptions, and are connected by tradition with Liparus and Aeolus.
