muscles in Child's Health Primer for Primary Classes

Jane Andrews · 1885

Muscles are lean meat and strong parts of the body. Fastened to bones by tendons, they move joints by contracting, shortening when a joint bends, and returning to their first shape when it straightens; each joint has two or more muscles. Brain messages and nerves keep muscles working, including quiet, steady breathing muscles. Muscle also makes up the heart and enables it to pump blood. Muscles grow through daily use, exercise, brisk play, work, and food carried by blood, while idleness weakens them. Alcohol may change part of muscles to fat, take away strength, harm muscles, and leave a fatty heart less able to work. Swallows have large, strong breast muscles.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Muscles move the joints by contracting and then returning to their original shape, and tendons fasten them to bones. Muscles become strong through use, while idleness weakens them. Alcohol may change part of the muscles to fat, taking away some of their strength.

CHAPTER III. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Muscles move when the brain sends them messages. Some muscles move quietly and steadily without conscious thought, including the muscles that move the chest during breathing.

CHAPTER XIII. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Muscles grow through plenty of exercise and are supported by food carried to them by the blood. Alcohol cannot make good muscles.

CHAPTER XIV. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Muscle makes up the heart and enables it to pump blood. Alcohol can harm muscles, and a fatty heart cannot work as well as a muscular heart.
