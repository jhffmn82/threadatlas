knowledge retriever

mechanism · 1 work · 12 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Knowledge Retriever, Knowledge retrieval, a dense retrieval mechanism, dense retrieval mechanism, knowledge retrieval, retrieval model, retriever

Abstract written by a model

The knowledge retriever is a dense retrieval mechanism for KG-MT that collects the most relevant knowledge-graph entities for a source text. It represents entities with primary names and descriptions, defines relevance with cosine similarity, and contrastively trains an encoder on Mintaka. Its fine-tuning uses hard negative mining, including homonymous entities sharing a relevant entity’s name. For each source text, it returns the top relevant entities but at most three, balancing relevance against computational cost. On XC-Translate, it reaches 85.9% hits@1 and 92.1% hits@3; hard negative mining improves these by 5.6% and 4.2% versus mContriever. Whether the three-entity limit suits longer documents is unclear.

3 .. 3.3

The knowledge retriever retrieves the top-k entities relevant to a source text from a knowledge graph. It represents entities using their primary names and descriptions, trains an encoder contrastively, and uses homonymous entities as hard negative examples.

6

The knowledge retriever plays a fundamental role in KG-MT by collecting entities for the knowledge-enhanced translator. On XC-Translate, it achieves 85.9% hits@1 and 92.1% hits@3, and hard negative mining improves these results by 5.6% and 4.2%, respectively, compared with mContriever. KG-MT retrieves at most three entities per source text as a trade-off between relevance and computational cost.

Limitations

The knowledge retriever uses a retrieval model that returns at most three entities for each source text. This limit reflects a trade-off between retrieving more relevant entities and increasing computational cost, but its suitability for longer documents is unclear.
