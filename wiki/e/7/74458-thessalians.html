Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, the Thessalians are a military and political group whose cavalry is central to their actions, fighting in conflicts involving Jason, Alexander of Pherae, the Phocians, Philip, and the Lamian War. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Thessalians initially assist the Athenians, desert to the Spartans during the Battle of Tanagra, and later attack Athenian provisions before being routed by the Athenians. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the Thessalians live near Mount Pelion, perfect horsemanship, use horses instead of chariots, excel among Greeks, and become associated with horsemen and bull-killing Centaurs. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the Hippocentaurs are Thessalians named for their skilled horsemanship and hunting and killing of wild bulls. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the Thessalians are represented in the general Council of the Greeks and the Amphictyonic Council, consult Delphi, and repeatedly fight the Phocians as persistent neighbors and enemies.

Thessalians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Thessalians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Thessalians are a military and political group whose cavalry is repeatedly central to their actions. Jealous of Jason of Pherae's growing power, they nevertheless appoint him general of all their forces and entrust him with the war. They fight Alexander of Pherae, suffer losses, seek Theban aid under Pelopidas, and regain towns after Alexander's defeat. As Amphictyonic confederates, they first support Apollo's temple against the Phocians, are defeated at Argola, briefly remain neutral after Onomarchus's bribe, then join Philip, help defeat the Phocians, and take part in arrangements for the Pythian games. In the Lamian War, their cavalry defeats Leonatus's cavalry but cannot prevent defeat after Antipater attacks the infantry.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. VL

The Thessalians became jealous of Jason's increasing power and ambition. They later appointed him general of all their forces and entrusted him with the management of the war after he urged them to seek the sovereignty of Greece.

CHAP. IX,

The Thessalians made war upon Alexander of Pherae but were often beaten and lost many men. They solicited Theban assistance under Pelopidas, and after Alexander's defeat received the return of the towns he had taken.

CHAP. VII.

The Thessalians joined the forces supporting Apollo's temple and fought the Phocians at Argola, where they were defeated. Onomarchus later bribed them to remain neutral, but they subsequently joined Philip of Macedon and helped him defeat the Phocians in Thessaly.

CHAP. X.

The Thessalians joined Philip and the Boeotians after the Boeotians requested assistance. They participated in the senate that referred the settlement of the war to the Amphictyonic Council and were to join Philip in conducting the Pythian games.

CHAP. II.

The Thessalians provided the principal cavalry strength of the Greeks and defeated Leonatus's cavalry. In the later battle, their horse initially had the advantage, but the Greek army was defeated after Antipater attacked the infantry.

Thessalians in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Thessalians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Thessalians, some living near Mount Pelion, train and use horses instead of chariots, perfect horsemanship, excel among Greeks, and become associated with horsemen and bull-killing Hippocentaurs or Centaurs.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

EXPLANATION.

The Thessalians were reputed to have excelled all other Greeks in horsemanship, so that the terms for a horseman and a Thessalian became synonymous. Their skill in killing wild bulls was also associated with the name Hippocentaurs or Centaurs.

Thessalians in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Thessalians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Thessalians are a Greek group represented in the general Council of the Greeks and by six Amphictyonic Council members. They consult Delphi about Ceressus and make offerings there. As persistent neighbors and enemies of the Phocians, they attack at Hyampolis, invade with forces from all cities, kill Gelon and 300 men, but suffer repeated defeats, including against Phocian cavalry and a night attack.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK X.--PHOCIS. / CHAPTER I.

The Thessalians attacked the Phocians at Hyampolis and suffered losses when their horses encountered buried earthenware pots. They later invaded Phocis with forces from all their cities, killed Gelon and 300 Phocian men, and were subsequently defeated in battle and in a later night attack.

CHAPTER XIII.

The Thessalians were described as constant enemies and neighbors of the Phocians, except where the Epicnemidian Locrians intervened. Thessalians of Pharsalus made votive offerings at Delphi, including Achilles on horseback with Patroclus running beside him, while their cavalry was defeated by the Phocians in the second encounter.
