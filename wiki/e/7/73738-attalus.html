Attalus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

He was Cleopatra’s uncle, commanded forces and fleets, and was sent with Parmenio to free Greek cities from slavery; he was intended to lead the army sent into Asia. Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP, XV.

Attalus was sent with Parmenio into Asia to free the Greek cities from slavery and was intended to command the army sent before into Asia. He had earlier been consulted by Pausanias about the latter's grievance and later arranged for Pausanias to be abused at a feast. He pursued the assassin after Philip was stabbed.

BOOK XVII.

Attalus, Cleopatra's uncle and a commander in Asia, was suspected of seeking the kingdom and of conspiring against Alexander. He later attempted to clear himself by sending Alexander a flattering letter, but Hecateus killed him on Alexander's orders.

98. Ptolemy Physcon, the brother of Philometor, began hb

Attalus took a course contrary to Diegylis by releasing Thracian prisoners and receiving Thracian nobles who fled from Diegylis. His generosity and clemency became widely known.

BOOK XXXIV.

Attalus, newly come to the throne in Asia, ruled differently from his predecessors and was cruel and bloodthirsty. Suspecting powerful relatives and associates of plotting against him, he arranged their murders along with those of their wives and children, causing his subjects and neighboring nations to seek his overthrow.

BOOK XXXVIL

Attalus was associated with Asian princes who received an honorable reception at Rome because of their friendship and services to the Romans. Ambassadors who came with Attalus answered accusations successfully and returned home with honor.
