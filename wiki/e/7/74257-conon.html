Conon in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Conon is an Athenian admiral and commander whose career moves from leading Athens’s fleet to commanding Persia’s navy in ways intended to restore Athens’s power at sea. Chosen among ten Athenian commanders, he received the fleet from Alcibiades, assembled seventy ships, aided Methymna, and fought near Mitylene before withdrawing into the city with forty ships. Sharing command with Philocles, he escaped the defeat at Aegos Potamos and fled to Evagoras in Cyprus. While serving there, he was appointed Persian admiral at Pharnabazus’s recommendation. He commanded fleets at Caunus and elsewhere, gained Rhodes’s support, promised Artaxerxes to ruin the Lacedaemonian fleet, and jointly commanded with Pharnabazus. Their victory at Cnidus enabled him to win over cities and islands, sail to the Piraeus, help rebuild Athens’s walls, make a league with Corinth, and return to Asia.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. IX.

Conon was chosen from among ten newly appointed Athenian commanders to be admiral. He was sent to receive the fleet from Alcibiades and took command of it.

CHAP. X.

Conon assembled a fleet of seventy ships and went to the aid of Methymna. He lured part of the Peloponnesian fleet into pursuit, attacked it near Mitylene, and later withdrew with forty ships into the city after the larger enemy fleet arrived.

CHAP. XV.

Conon commanded the Athenian fleet jointly with Philocles. He escaped the defeat at Aegos Potamos with a few other commanders and, fearing to return to Athens, fled to Evagoras, prince of Cyprus.

CHAP. VI.

Conon, an Athenian soldier serving with Evagoras in Cyprus, was appointed admiral of the Persian fleet at Pharnabazus's recommendation. He accepted in hopes of recovering the sea's sovereignty for Athens and sailed with forty ships to Cilicia while the fleet was being completed.

CHAP. vm.

Conon, the Persian admiral, commanded a fleet at Caunus and later gathered eighty galleys and sailed to Chersonesus. He received the support of Rhodes, went to Babylon, promised Artaxerxes that he would ruin the Lacedaemonian fleet, chose Pharnabazus as his assistant, and returned toward the sea.

CHAP. IX,

Conon commanded the Persian fleet with Pharnabazus and defeated the Lacedaemonian fleet at Cnidus. He then brought many cities and islands over to his side, sailed to the Piraeus, helped rebuild Athens's walls, and later returned to Asia after making a league with Corinth.
