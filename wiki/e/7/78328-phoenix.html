Abstract written by a model

In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Phoenix is a bird that renews and reproduces itself, dying after five ages and giving rise to a young Phoenix that carries its nest and remains to Hyperion's temple. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Phoenix is an imaginary bird that lives for five hundred years, dies on a fragrant nest, and gives rise to a young bird that carries its body or ashes to the temple of the Sun at Heliopolis.

Phoenix in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Phoenix first appears

Abstract written by a model

Phoenix is an imaginary being and modern monster, a bird resembling an eagle in outline and bulk, with gold-colored and crimson plumage. It lives for five hundred years, eats frankincense and odoriferous gums, and builds a nest of cinnamon, spikenard, and myrrh in an oak or palm-tree. It dies on the nest, and a young bird is born from its body and lives as long as its predecessor. The young bird deposits the nest or carries the parent’s body or ashes to the temple of the Sun at Heliopolis in Egypt. Its existence was denied by Sir Thomas Browne.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXIX

The Phoenix is said to live for five hundred years, build a nest of aromatic materials, die upon it, and produce a successor from its remains. Ovid, Tacitus, and other writers describe the young bird carrying the parent’s body or ashes to the temple of the Sun, while Herodotus describes it as eagle-like with gold-colored and crimson plumage. Sir Thomas Browne was the first writer named who denied that the Phoenix existed.
