In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, impiety and contempt of the gods are shown in Epopeus, Salmoneus, and Romulus Sylvius, whose conduct brings destruction or divine punishment. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Erisicthon’s impiety toward the gods is shown by his refusal to honor them at their altars and his violation of Ceres’ sacred grove, bringing divine punishment in the form of Famine.

Abstract written by a model

Impiety and contempt of the gods are shown in figures such as Epopeus, Salmoneus, and Romulus Sylvius, who challenged, dishonored, or mocked the gods. Their conduct is presented as bringing destruction or divine punishment: Epopeus destroyed temples and altars, Salmoneus rejected sacrifices and imitated Jupiter's thunder, and Romulus Sylvius was struck dead by a thunderbolt.
