Cambyses

person · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Cambyscs, Cambyses the Persian, Camhyscs

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Cambyses was described as a furious, mad, fierce, and insolent Persian king and lord of Egypt who took Memphis and Pelusium, desecrated Amasis's body, and ordered attacks on Jupiter Ammon's temple. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Cambyses conquered Egypt after Amasis's reign, then burned Egyptian temples with the Persians, carried away treasures, and stripped gold from Osymandyas's monument.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

3G. When Thericles was chief magistrate at Athens in the sixty^

Cambyses was described as furious, mad, fierce, and insolent. After taking Memphis and Pelusium, he desecrated and ordered the burning of the embalmed body of Amasis, and he ordered an army to plunder and burn the temple of Jupiter Ammon.

Cambyses in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Cambyses first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cambyses conquers Egypt after Amasis's reign, then, with the Persians, burns Egyptian temples, carries away treasures, and strips gold from Osymandyas's monument.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

DIODORUS SICULUS. / BOOK I.

Cambyses and the Persians are said to have burned the temples of Egypt and carried away their silver, gold, ivory, and precious stones. Cambyses also carried away the gold border from the top of Osymandyas's monument.
