Alexander, son of Polyperchon in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Alexander, son of Polyperchon, is a military commander active in Attica and the Peloponnesus. He came to Attica with an army, seized Munychia and Piraeus for war rather than restoring them, dealt secretly with Nicanor, and received Phocion’s fleeing followers. After allying with Antigonus in Asia, he opposed Cassander, campaigned against Cassander’s garrisons with Aristodemus, then deserted Antigonus for a league with Cassander and command in the Peloponnesus. He took Dyme and was assassinated near Sicyon.

2 sections; each heading opens the text

While these things were acting, Nicanor (who held Muayohis)

Alexander came into Attica with an army, but instead of restoring Munychia and the Piraeus, he seized and retained them for the war. He refused to let the Athenians negotiate with Nicanor, while conducting separate and secret transactions with Nicanor, and he received Phocion and his followers after they fled Athens.

And now, having related the things done throughout all Asia, we

Alexander communicated with opponents of Cassander at Argos and later campaigned with Aristodemus against Cassander's garrisons in the Peloponnesus. He formed a league with Cassander after being promised command of all the forces in the Peloponnesus. He took Dyme, but was later assassinated near Sicyon by Alexion and others pretending to be his friends.
