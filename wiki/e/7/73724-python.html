Python in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Python was a former squire of Alexander, an able and ambitious commander, and an orator described as inferior to Demosthenes in eloquence. Chosen to lead a Macedonian force against revolting Greeks, he planned to win their troops for his own power, but obeyed Perdiccas’s order to kill them after their capitulation. He later served as protector of the kings with sovereign authority, but resigned with his friends because of dissatisfaction with Eurydice’s interference; the Macedonians then chose Antipater. Appointed governor of Media and general of the higher satrapies, Python was defeated by other governors, driven from Parthia, and joined Seleucus in trying to detach Antigenes and the Argyraspides from Eumenes. In Antigonus’s army he commanded the left-wing horse, whose initial flank attack was reversed by Eumenes. Finally, while plotting to win Media’s soldiers through bribes and promises, he trusted Antigonus’s assurances, was convicted before a council of war, and was beheaded.

BOOK XVIII.

Python, a former squire of Alexander and a skilled commander, was chosen general against the revolting Greeks. He intended to win them over and use their forces to establish his own power, but Perdiccas ordered him to put them all to the sword; after the Macedonians killed the Greeks despite their capitulation, Python returned to Perdiccas.

Python was appointed lord-lieutenant of Media and was also general of the higher satrapies. After being defeated by the other provincial governors and driven from Parthia, he joined Seleucus and attempted to persuade Antigenes and the Argyraspides to abandon Eumenes.

Python commanded the horse on Antigonus's left wing, including archers and lancers who attacked by repeated charges. His forces avoided the elephants, attacked Eumenes's troops from the flank, and initially inflicted serious damage, but Eumenes's counterattack put the Pythonians to flight.

CHAP, m.

Python plotted to draw soldiers in Media to his side through bribes and promises and had already won over many supporters. Deceived by Antigonus’s assurances, he went to him, was convicted before a council of war, and had his head cut off.
