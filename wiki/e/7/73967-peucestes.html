Peucestes in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Peucestes is a Persian governor and general, formerly Alexander's bodyguard, who governs most of Persia and is greatly beloved by its natives. Chosen unanimously to command a Macedonian army assembled from provincial forces, he brings large numbers of Persian darters, slingers, archers, infantry, and cavalry. After initially withholding archers because he lacks chief command, he supplies ten thousand, distributes booty, and holds a feast at Persepolis, arousing Eumenes's suspicion; Eumenes first weakens and then reconciles with him. Fearing Antigonus, Peucestes flees a battle and refuses to fight again, contributing to Eumenes's defeat. Antigonus later removes him from Persia through false hopes of higher preferment despite native opposition.

Eurydice being queen-regent in Macedonia, as soon as she heard

Peucestes was chosen general by unanimous agreement before Eumenes's arrival, having brought together forces from the provinces. He had served as Alexander's bodyguard, governed much of Persia, was esteemed by the natives, and commanded substantial infantry and cavalry forces.

CHAP. n.

Peucestes initially refused to send more Persian archers because he had been denied the chief command, but he later supplied ten thousand archers. At Persepolis, he distributed booty and held a great feast for the army, which made Eumenes suspect that he sought the supreme command. Eumenes then diminished his influence through a forged letter and later won him back with promises and conciliatory conduct.

Peucestes feared that Antigonus would attack before Eumenes could assemble the scattered forces. During the battle he fled with his horse and fifteen hundred others, later refused Eumenes's proposal to fight again, and thereby contributed to Eumenes's defeat.

CHAP, m.

Peucestes was much beloved in Persia, and the natives opposed Antigonus’s attempt to remove him. Antigonus held him out with vain hopes of higher preferment until he had drawn him out of the country.
