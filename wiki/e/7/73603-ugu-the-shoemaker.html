  • In The Lost Princess of Oz: Ugu the Shoemaker is a former shoemaker from Herku who becomes a powerful, wicked magician. Descended through his great-grandfather from a great wizard and sorcerer, he grows dissatisfied with shoemaking, studies inherited magical books and instruments for a year, and builds or inhabits a wickerwork castle on a mountain west of Herku. Ambitious to become the most powerful magician and ruler of Oz, he steals Cayke’s Magic Dishpan, Ozma, magical possessions from Ozma, Glinda, and the Wizard, including the Great Book of Records and the Magic Picture. His power increases greatly, and he uses the stolen tools to watch and follow the travelers while imprisoning Ozma, ultimately hidden inside a golden peach pit. Ugu defends his castle with magical barriers and an apparent army. From a golden cage in its central hall, surrounded by stolen magic, he refuses to return Ozma or the magic and declares his plan to make everyone obey him. He traps and taunts the group seeking Ozma, then attempts to destroy Dorothy. Dorothy instead transforms him into a dove. Though still knowledgeable in magic, he escapes in the jeweled dishpan to Quadling Country. After Ozma is recovered, Ozma removes Ugu’s magical materials, and Dorothy keeps him a gray dove until he reforms. In the end, Ugu asks forgiveness, expresses deep sorrow for stealing Ozma and the magic, values honest contentment, and prefers a simple life as a bird, hoping to become a good and respected dove.

Ugu the Shoemaker in The Lost Princess of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1917

Ugu the Shoemaker is a former shoemaker from Herku who becomes a powerful, wicked magician. Descended through his great-grandfather from a great wizard and sorcerer, he grows dissatisfied with shoemaking, studies inherited magical books and instruments for a year, and builds or inhabits a wickerwork castle on a mountain west of Herku. Ambitious to become the most powerful magician and ruler of Oz, he steals Cayke’s Magic Dishpan, Ozma, magical possessions from Ozma, Glinda, and the Wizard, including the Great Book of Records and the Magic Picture. His power increases greatly, and he uses the stolen tools to watch and follow the travelers while imprisoning Ozma, ultimately hidden inside a golden peach pit. Ugu defends his castle with magical barriers and an apparent army. From a golden cage in its central hall, surrounded by stolen magic, he refuses to return Ozma or the magic and declares his plan to make everyone obey him. He traps and taunts the group seeking Ozma, then attempts to destroy Dorothy. Dorothy instead transforms him into a dove. Though still knowledgeable in magic, he escapes in the jeweled dishpan to Quadling Country. After Ozma is recovered, Ozma removes Ugu’s magical materials, and Dorothy keeps him a gray dove until he reforms. In the end, Ugu asks forgiveness, expresses deep sorrow for stealing Ozma and the magic, values honest contentment, and prefers a simple life as a bird, hoping to become a good and respected dove.

Narrative, section by section

12 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 11

Ugu the Shoemaker is identified by a Bluefinch as the person who enchanted the peach and placed it in the center of the Great Orchard. The bird and the White Rabbit say that Button-Bright must explain to Ugu why he ate it.

CHAPTER 12

Ugu was formerly a citizen of Herku who made shoes, but he became a powerful magician after discovering the magical books and recipes of his great-grandfather. He lives in a wickerwork castle in the mountains west of Herku, and Vig suggests that he may have stolen Ozma and the other magical objects.

CHAPTER 16

Ugu the Shoemaker is identified as a magician who lives in a mountain castle made of wickerwork. The Bear King’s image shows him as a thin, old, unpleasant-looking man reading a Great Book, and he is said to possess Cayke’s jeweled dishpan.

CHAPTER 17

Ugu is identified as the thief of Ozma, the magic in the Emerald City, and Cayke's dishpan. He watches the travelers through Ozma's Magic Picture, but when the vision is reversed he can no longer see them.

CHAPTER 18

Ugu is described as a powerful and wicked magician who stole Ozma and several magical objects. He knows the travelers are coming because he has seen them in the Magic Picture and read about their actions in the Great Book of Records.

CHAPTER 19

Ugu the Shoemaker becomes dissatisfied with making shoes and studies the magical books and instruments of his ancestors. He builds the Wicker Castle, steals the Magic Dishpan and the magical possessions of Ozma of Oz, Glinda the Good, and the Wizard of Oz, and captures Ozma. He learns that two expeditions are traveling to his castle to recover the stolen property.

CHAPTER 20

Ugu the Shoemaker is described as a wicked and dangerous magician who stole Ozma and possibly Toto’s growl. Dorothy, the Wizard, and their companions decide to question him about Ozma and continue toward his castle.

CHAPTER 21

Ugu the Shoemaker uses magical barriers and an apparent army to stop the group from reaching his wicker castle. The Wizard says Ugu knows all of their tricks as well as some of his own.

CHAPTER 22

Ugu sits inside a golden cage in the central hall, surrounded by stolen magic and Cayke's dishpan. He refuses to reveal where he has hidden Ozma or restore the captured magic, and declares that he intends to become Ruler of Oz. He turns the hall upside down, traps the group, and leaves through a trap door while taunting them.

CHAPTER 23

Ugu tries to destroy Dorothy with magic, but she transforms him into a dove. He retains his knowledge of magic, escapes in Cayke's dishpan to Quadling Country, and is later seen perched in a tree.

CHAPTER 25

Ugu had hidden Ozma inside a golden peach pit and is described as a bold and clever magician. Ozma takes away his magical materials, and Dorothy keeps him transformed into a dove until he reforms.

CHAPTER 26

Ugu the Shoemaker remains in the form of a gray dove and comes to Dorothy to ask forgiveness for his misdeeds. He says he is very sorry, has learned from quiet thought, and hopes to become a good and respected dove.
