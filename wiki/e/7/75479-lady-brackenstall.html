Lady Brackenstall in The Return of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1903-09-26

Lady Brackenstall is a woman from South Australia, married for about a year to Sir Eustace Brackenstall in an unhappy and abusive marriage. After his death at Abbey Grange, she is injured over one eye and on her arm. She tells Sherlock Holmes and Inspector Stanley Hopkins that three burglars attacked her, tied and gagged her, killed Sir Eustace, drank wine, stole silver, and escaped through a French window. Holmes concludes that this burglary account was fabricated, that she was tied to the chair after Sir Eustace’s death, and that she is protecting the real criminal. The false story, corroborated by the maid, is intended to shield that person from prosecution, while Captain Crocker seeks to protect her if possible.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

THE ADVENTURE OF THE ABBEY GRANGE

Lady Brackenstall tells Sherlock Holmes and Inspector Stanley Hopkins that three burglars attacked her, tied her to a chair with the bell-rope, gagged her, and killed Sir Eustace Brackenstall. She has a severe injury over one eye and two other red marks on her arm, and she says that the burglars took silver, drank wine, and escaped through the French window.

A change had come over Holmes’s manner. He had lost his listless

Lady Brackenstall maintains that three burglars entered the Abbey Grange, drank wine, murdered her husband, tied her to a chair, and escaped with silver. Holmes confronts her with his belief that the story is an absolute fabrication, but she refuses to change her account. He concludes that she was placed in the chair after her husband’s death and that she is protecting the real criminal.

Mary Fraser is described as having been mistreated by her husband and as loving Captain Crocker. After her husband attacks her, she helps Crocker and Theresa Wright make the killing appear to have been committed by burglars, and Holmes seeks to keep her out of court.
