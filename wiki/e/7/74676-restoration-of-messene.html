Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, the rebuilding of Messene was promoted by Epaminondas, who persuaded the Arcadians and other allies to rebuild and repopulate the strategically placed city, distribute its land by lot, and rebuild its surrounding country. In Description of Greece, the restoration of Messene was the Messenian return to their ancestral country after dispersion; after the victory at Leuctra, Epaminondas, Epiteles, the Thebans, and their allies examined the site through omens, sacrificed to gods and heroes, built the city, and restored other towns. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the colonization of Messene was undertaken by Epaminondas after he marched against Sparta and Agesilaus declined a combat.

restoration of Messene in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where restoration of Messene first appears

Abstract written by a model

The restoration of Messene was the Messenian return to their ancestral country after dispersion. After the victory at Leuctra, Epaminondas, Epiteles, the Thebans, and allies examined the site through omens, sacrificed to gods and heroes, and built the city and restored other towns. Later the Messenians faced conflicts, aided Arcadians, captured Sparta, and rejoined the Achæan League.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIV.

The restoration of Messene was undertaken by Epaminondas, Epiteles, the Thebans, and their allies after the victory at Leuctra. The site was examined through omens, sacrifices were made to numerous gods and heroes, and the city’s walls, houses, streets, and temples were built while other Messenian towns were restored.

CHAPTER XXIX.

The Messenians were described as having suffered dispersion to remote parts of the world before being restored to their own country long afterward. Their later history included renewed conflicts, refuge given to the Arcadians, participation in the capture of Sparta, the capture of Philopœmen, punishment of those responsible for his death, and renewed membership in the Achæan League.
