Rhodians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Rhodians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Rhodians are the defenders and citizens of Rhodes, acting collectively in war, diplomacy, and civic recovery. They first joined Chians, Byzantines, and Coians in defecting from Athens during the Social War, helped raise the siege of Samos, prepared to relieve Byzantium, and later made peace. Maintaining alliances and peace with princes while secretly favoring Ptolemy, they refused Antigonus and prepared for Demetrius. During the ensuing year-long siege, they armed inhabitants and foreigners, freed deserving servants, supported families of the fallen, strengthened and defended walls and harbour, countermined enemy works, raided by sea, and used ship beaks against vessels carrying engines. Six thousand citizens were in arms, and aid in provisions and troops came from Ptolemy, Cassander, Lysimachus, and others. They made peace with Demetrius while retaining their laws, estates, and revenues, though giving one hundred hostages. Afterwards they rewarded faithful defenders, freed and enrolled brave slave fighters as citizens, honored allies, and repaired the city. Later, after favoring Perseus and breaking their league with the Romans, they sent ambassadors to Rome, were rebuked and fined, and eventually received Caria and Lycia from Roman commissioners.

The Rhodians appealed to Ptolemy, Lysimachus, and Cassander for assistance, admitted inhabitants and foreigners to bear arms, freed deserving servants, and promised public support for the families of those who died. They organized extensive preparations, defended the walls and harbour, burned or damaged enemy ships and engines, and forced many Demetrians to withdraw or surrender. They later received 150 Gnossians and 500 men sent by Ptolemy.

CFIAP. V.

The Rhodians built additional walls, countermined Demetrius’s works, conducted naval raids, defended the city in repeated assaults, and accepted provisions and troops from their allies. They ultimately made peace with Demetrius while preserving their laws, estates, and revenues, and providing one hundred hostages.

The Rhodians ended a year-long siege and war, rewarded those who had proved honest and faithful, and freed and enrolled as citizens the slaves who had fought bravely. They honored Ptolemy, Cassander, Lysimachus, and others with statues, dedicated a grove and gallery to Ptolemy, and repaired and beautified their damaged buildings.

BOOK XXXI.

The Rhodians sent ambassadors to Rome to beg pardon for appearing to favor Perseus and for breaking their league with the Romans during the war. Their embassy failed to obtain everything it sought, but the senate's answer relieved their fears; they were sharply reproved and fined.

BOOK XXXVIL

The Rhodians received Caria and Lycia from the Roman commissioners. Later, their ambassadors came to Rome to answer accusations that Rhodes had secretly favored Perseus and weakened its friendship with the Romans; the Senate relieved their greatest fears but sharply rebuked some of their conduct.

Rhodians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Rhodians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Rhodians were regarded as Rhodes’s original inhabitants and, after plague swept away earlier inhabitants, settled colonists there. They held Rhodes consecrated to Sol, worshipped him above other gods as their ancestor, and honored Phorbas and Althasmenes as demi-gods. Later they rejected the Lacedaemonian fleet, revolted, received Conon and his navy, and helped seize Egyptian grain ships.

The Rhodians regarded Rhodes as consecrated to Sol and worshipped the sun above all other gods, believing him to be the ancestor from whom they sprang. They later received Phorbas as a proprietor, and after his death honored him as a demi-god; they also honored Althasmenes as a demi-god.

CHAP. vm.

The Rhodians refused the Lacedaemonian fleet entry to their harbor, revolted from the Lacedaemonians, and received Conon. They helped him capture the Egyptian grain ships and bring their cargo into the port.
