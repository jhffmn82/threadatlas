Helepolis in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Helepolis was an enormous nine-story siege engine built by Demetrius, about ninety cubits high and forty-five cubits broad on each side, with a square base, eight wheels, iron plates on three exterior sides, and artillery throughout. Its lowest story held stone-throwers and its middle story dart-and-arrow machines. Operated by more than two hundred men for assaults, it was threatened by fire attacks: defenders burned it with other engines, while another account says it was moved out of range and repaired.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. ni.

The Helepolis was an enormous siege engine built by Demetrius for the assault on Salamis. It was forty-five cubits broad on every side and ninety cubits high, carried weapons and stone shot on several levels, and was operated by more than two hundred men. Menelaus's defenders burned it with other principal engines.

CFIAP. V.

Demetrius built the Helepolis as a nine-story siege engine nearly one hundred cubits high, protected with iron plates and equipped with artillery. Rhodian fire attacks damaged and endangered it, forcing Demetrius’s men to move it out of range and later repair it.
