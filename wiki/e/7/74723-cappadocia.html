Cappadocia in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Cappadocia is a kingdom and region, claimed through its royal genealogy to descend from Cyrus. After Ariarathes’ defeat, Perdiccas conquered it and assigned its government to Eumenes of Cardia. It became a central site of Eumenes’ struggles: he defeated Craterus and Neoptolemus there, was later defeated by Antigonus and fled to Nora, briefly returned after his release, and left again as Menander approached. Antigonus also marched from Cappadocia against Alcetas. Later, Eumenes’ killing of Ariarathes brought it under Macedonian control, but Ariarathes’ adopted heir recovered it with Armenian aid. Under a later Ariarathes, it welcomed learned Greeks and renewed friendship with the Romans.

Eumenes was in Cappadocia when Perdiccas deserted him. Antigonus later fought Eumenes there, defeated him, and forced him to take refuge in Nora.

CHAP. IV.

Cappadocia was the region from which Antigonus marched against Alcetas. Eumenes had previously ruled Cappadocia prosperously and later remained there briefly after his release from Nora.

CHAP. V.

Eumenes left Cappadocia because Antigonus's army under Menander was approaching and it was no longer safe for him to remain there. Menander's army arrived too late to catch him and returned to Cappadocia.

BOOK XXXI.

Cappadocia was brought under Macedonian control after Eumenes defeated and killed Ariarathes, but Ariarathes, the adopted heir, later recovered the kingdom with Armenian assistance. Under a later Ariarathes, Cappadocia became attractive to learned Greeks, and its ruler renewed peace and friendship with the Romans.
