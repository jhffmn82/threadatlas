Sidonians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Sidonians, a Phoenician people inhabiting Sidon, gained great wealth through trade and had 100 galleys. They resisted Persian authority, destroyed Persian supplies, and fortified the city, but betrayal admitted Persians; many burned themselves, families, and houses, and over 40,000 reportedly perished.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

In Asia, the Persian king having invaded ligypt some years before

The Sidonians resisted Persian authority, destroyed the king's garden and Persian supplies, and prepared arms, provisions, walls, trenches, and ships. After betrayal opened the city to the Persians, many Sidonians burned themselves, their families, and their houses rather than be captured; more than 40,000 people reportedly perished.
