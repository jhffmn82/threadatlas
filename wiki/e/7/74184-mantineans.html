Mantineans

group · 2 works · 21 quoted facts · 12 connections
Also called: Mantincans, Mantinæans, citizens, the Mantineans, them of Mantinea, those inhabitants

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, the Mantineans are a wealthy and militarily capable Arcadian community who fought the Elians and Boeotians, resisted Lacedaemonian demands to demolish their walls, and ultimately surrendered and returned to their former villages. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the Mantineans maintain temples and shrines, preserve sacred fires, honor Antinous, commemorate local heroes, and build the temple of Aphrodite Symmachia after fighting the Romans off Actium.

Mantineans in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Mantineans first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Mantineans were a wealthy, militarily capable Arcadian community, reputed among the most valiant Arcadians and formerly esteemed by the Lacedaemonians. In faction with the Tegeans, they profaned sacred objects, then promoted war against the Elians to avoid accountability. Seeking help from Athens and Lacedaemon, they fought at Mantinea but were routed by Boeotian and Thessalian horsemen. Later they rejected a Lacedaemonian demand to demolish their walls; after defending through summer, flooding forced their surrender and return to their former villages.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian / BOOK XV.

The Mantineans rejected the Lacedaemonians' demand to demolish their walls and return to five villages. They defended Mantinea with their own forces after the Athenians refused to aid them.

CHAP. n.

The Mantineans bravely defended their city throughout the summer but surrendered when flooding caused their houses to fall. They were forced to leave the city and settle with their families in their former villages.

CHAP. X.

The Mantineans promoted war against the Elians because they feared being called to account for sacred objects they had taken and profaned. They sought Athenian and Lacedaemonian assistance, fought at Mantinea, and were routed by the Boeotian and Thessalian horsemen.

Mantineans in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Mantineans first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Mantineans maintain many temples and shrines, including those of Zeus, Castor and Pollux, Demeter and Proserpine, Athene Alea, Aphrodite Symmachia, and Antinous. They carefully keep a continual fire in the temple of Demeter and Proserpine, honor Antinous with rites and fifth-year games, and preserve images and honors for Diomenea, Arcas, and Podares. After fighting with the Romans off Actium, they built Aphrodite Symmachia’s temple. They also recount Gryllus, Cephisodorus, and Podares as the bravest against Epaminondas and the Thebans, while Arcas was removed by Delphi’s oracle.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IX.

The Mantineans maintain numerous temples and shrines, including those of Zeus, Castor and Pollux, Demeter and Proserpine, Hera, Aphrodite Symmachia, Athene Alea, and Antinous. They keep a fire continually burning in the temple of Demeter and Proserpine, honor Antinous with annual rites and games every fifth year, and erected a temple to Aphrodite Symmachia to commemorate their participation with the Romans in the seafight off Actium. They also honor Arcas and Podares, and say that Gryllus the son of Xenophon was the bravest man in the battle against Epaminondas and the Thebans, followed by Cephisodorus and Podares.
