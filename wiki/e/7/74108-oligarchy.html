oligarchy in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Oligarchy is a form of government favored by the Lacedaemonians and imposed in cities hostile to them. Athenians adopted it in desperation, but the government of the four hundred failed and was abolished; elsewhere, it was removed and democracy restored.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. 111.

The Athenians preferred oligarchy to their former democracy when their situation appeared desperate. The oligarchic government of the four hundred was unsuccessful and was subsequently abolished.

CHAP. V.

Oligarchy was favored by the Lacedaemonians and by the leading men at Corcyra who sought to bring the city under Lacedaemonian control. Theramenes removed an oligarchy at Paros and restored the democracy.

CHAP. III.

The Lacedaemonians ordered an oligarchy to be established in every city because democrats were hostile to them. Some cities were governed by a decemvirate and others by an oligarchy.
