Aridaeus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Aridaeus, Philip’s son, was described as weak-spirited and naturally infirm. After Alexander’s death he was made king, later named Philip III. He spent two years preparing Alexander’s funeral chariot and transported Alexander’s body from Babylon toward Egypt. He held sovereign authority as protector of the kings, came from the Nile to Triparadisus with the kings and army, and was Eurydice’s husband. As governor of Phrygia at the Hellespont, he fortified cities and unsuccessfully besieged Cyzicum. Defying Antigonus, he later fled to a city of the Ganians, joined Clitus, and strengthened his army.

Aridaeus was one of the protectors of the kings and came from the Nile with the kings and the army to Triparadisus. He is also identified as the husband of Eurydice, one of the kings of Macedon.

CHAP. IV.

Aridaeus, governor of Phrygia at the Hellespont, fortified his principal cities and besieged Cyzicum. After the siege failed, he refused Antigonus’s demand that he leave the province and declared that he would settle the dispute by force.
