Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Antigonus's war against the four kings is the conflict that ends with Antigonus's death in battle and Demetrius and Stratonice fleeing to Cyprus. In Description of Greece, the war against Antigonus is the conflict Ptolemy, Cassander, and Lysimachus wage against Antigonus, ending with his defeat and death.

Antigonus's war against the four kings in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Antigonus's war against the four kings first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ptolemy, Cassander, and Lysimachus prepared a war against Antigonus after he refused their demands. Their forces operated through Egypt, Cyprus, Caria, Cappadocia, and the Peloponnesus, while Antigonus sought additional allies and built a fleet. Antigonus's war against Ptolemy Lagus, Seleucus, Lysimachus, and Cassander ended with Antigonus's death in battle. Demetrius and Stratonice then fled with their wealth to Cyprus.

war against Antigonus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where war against Antigonus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ptolemy persuaded Cassander and Lysimachus to join him in war against Antigonus, presenting the exile of Seleucus and Antigonus’s aggrandizement as a common danger. The conflict included fighting in Syria, Phœnicia, Cyprus, Egypt, and Rhodes, and ended with Antigonus losing most of his forces and dying.
