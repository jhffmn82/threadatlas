Cracker in Toto's Merry Winter

Laura E. Richards · 1887

Cracker is a little squirrel, also described as a boy, who lives in a cottage with Toto and Bruin. He has sharp teeth, access to nuts in the barn-chamber, enjoys waltzing, attends Toto’s grandmother when idle, and tells household stories. After angrily denying that he took squash pie and threatening to bite ears and throw cheese, he is rebuked and ashamed. At dancing school he learns jumps and foot changes, then whirls Miss Mary while waltzing until they stumble against Bruin’s paw. At a wedding he greets woodland guests, defends Mrs. Cow and Toto’s food, and dances with a young red squirrel. He wonders about a comet, urges help for Baldhead after hearing of his broken leg and hunger, helps after a robbery, and recounts Mrs. Cow’s moon story despite Toto’s interruption.

Narrative, section by section

7 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IV.

Cracker angrily denies taking the squash pie and threatens to bite <DW53>’s ears and throw cheese. The grandmother gently rebukes him for forgetting his manners, and he hangs his head in shame.

CHAPTER V.

Cracker takes part in the dancing-school lesson and learns to jump and change his feet. He later grabs Miss Mary and whirls her around the room while waltzing, until she escapes after he and she stumble against Bruin's paw.

" CHAPTER VI.

Cracker attends the wedding and enjoys greeting the woodland guests, especially a young red squirrel. He defends Mrs. Cow and the food at Toto's home when Uncle Munkle questions his way of living, then dances with the red squirrel.

CHAPTER VII.

Cracker notices the comet among the stars and asks whether comets have legs and what they live on. He listens to Toto's explanation and story.

CHAPTER VIII.

Cracker tells Bruin that Chipper learned Baldhead had broken his leg and had no food. He urges that the matter be attended to immediately.

CHAPTER IX.

Cracker is in the kitchen during the robbery and later questions the raccoon about what happened. He helps put the distracted-looking room in order and learns that the kettle may have acted on its own to attack the robber.

CHAPTER X.

Cracker welcomes Toto home and later tells the family Mrs. Cow's version of how a cow supposedly jumped over the moon. He explains the young cow's mistaken belief about moon-calves and becomes briefly annoyed when Toto interrupts him, but soon continues his story.
