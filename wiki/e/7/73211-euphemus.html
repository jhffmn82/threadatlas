Euphemus

person · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Euphemos, Euphemos', swift-footed Euphemus

Abstract written by a model

In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Euphemos is an Argonaut associated with the cave at Tainaros; he receives a clod of earth from Eurypylos but accidentally drops it into the sea, determining his descendants’ settlement in Kalliste or Thera and their founding of Kyrene in Libya. In The Argonautica, Euphemus, a son of Maia, dreams that a clod of earth becomes a woman who identifies herself as a daughter of Triton and Libya and asks to be returned to the sea; after Jason interprets the dream, he casts the clod into the depths, producing Calliste. In The Argonautica, Euphemus carries and releases a trembling dove to test passage between the Cyanean rocks; when the rocks clip its tail-feathers, he urges the rowers to strain at their oars.

Euphemos in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · 497 BC · the work’s page · read it where Euphemos first appears

Abstract written by a model

Euphemos is an Argonaut associated with the cave at Tainaros. He receives a clod of earth from Eurypylos but accidentally drops it into the sea rather than bringing it home. This loss determines his descendants’ settlement in Kalliste or Thera and their founding of Kyrene in Libya.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

IV.

Euphemos is an Argonaut who receives a clod of earth from the god Eurypylos, though it is accidentally lost into the sea. His descendants eventually settle in Kalliste and become the founders of the line that rules Kyrene.

[Footnote 1: Libya. Epaphos was son of Zeus by Io.]

Euphemos dropped a clod into the sea instead of carrying it safely home to Tainaros. Because of this, his descendants were destined to colonize Thera first and later found Kyrene in Libya.
