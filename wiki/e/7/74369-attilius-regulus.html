Abstract written by a model

Attilius Regulus, a Roman general marked by pride, rejects an honourable advantageous peace and humiliates distressed Carthaginians with unreasonable terms, prolonging war and causing miseries for his country; he ends defeated, dishonoured, and scorned.

BOOK XXIII.

Attilius Regulus is condemned for pride, folly, madness, and insolence. Although he could have concluded an honourable and advantageous peace for the Romans, he imposed unreasonable conditions on the Carthaginians, provoking them to continue the war; he later suffered defeat, dishonour, and the scorn of those whom he had previously mocked.
