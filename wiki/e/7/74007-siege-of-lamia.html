siege of Lamia

event · 1 work · 2 quoted facts · 0 connections
Also called: besieged, blocked it up, penned them up

Abstract written by a model

The siege of Lamia was a blockade conducted by Leosthenes against Antipater's Macedonian army. Leosthenes enclosed Lamia with fortifications to cut off supplies, but Macedonian resistance and Antipater's sally fatally injured him. The Greeks then abandoned the siege when Leonatus approached to relieve Antipater.

Leosthenes surrounded Lamia with a fortified camp, trench, rampart, and later a wall intended to prevent supplies from reaching the city. The Macedonians resisted attacks from the besiegers, and the siege was disrupted when Antipater made a sally that led to Leosthenes’s fatal injury.

CHAP. II.

The Greeks abandoned the siege of Lamia when they learned that Leonatus was approaching to relieve Antipater. Lamia had previously held Antipater and his forces inside it.
