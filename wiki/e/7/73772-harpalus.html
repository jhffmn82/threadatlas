Abstract written by a model

Harpalus, Alexander's treasurer and governor of Babylon, abused authority during Alexander's Indian expedition through luxury, sexual misconduct, extravagant spending, and gifts to Pythonice and Glycera. After corrupt governors were punished, he fled Asia with five thousand talents and six thousand mercenaries, seeking Athenian protection. He later reached Crete, where his friend Thimbron murdered him and seized his money and soldiers. His treasure supported Leosthenes and the war, and bribery accusations linked him to Demosthenes' condemnation and flight.
