Abstract written by a model

Emperor Wah-Song, the great and mighty Emperor of China, has a celestial train held up by seven boys and is capricious in going out unannounced. He enters the Imperial court-yard, has Ly-Chee threatened after his clothes are soaked, then mistreats attendants before dreaming of the Golden Dragon. Terrified, he pardons, rewards, and appoints Ly-Chee Chief Sweeper, and becomes meek after his ordeal.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

" And without further preface, she began as follows:-- CHAPTER II.

The Emperor unexpectedly enters the Imperial court-yard while Ly-Chee is working. After his clothing and shoes are soaked, he orders Ly-Chee's head removed unless the Golden Dragon itself comes down and begs for Ly-Chee's life.

Ten o'clock of a dark night.

Emperor Wah-Song prepares for bed after mistreating several attendants, then dreams that the Golden Dragon has come to confront him. Terrified by the Dragon's commands, he orders Ly-Chee's pardon, rewards, and appointment as Chief Sweeper, and afterward lies in bed for two weeks before becoming as meek as a six-year-old child.
