Abstract written by a model

Counter-misinformation responses are produced by users, fact-checkers, and automated methods to prevent misinformation’s continued propagation. Many existing, including social-correction, responses lack politeness, direct refutation, supporting facts, or evidence. RARG is designed to generate polite, factual responses that clearly refute claims, using an actor model based on the input claim and retrieved evidence. During optimization, responses are evaluated for refutation, factuality, politeness, and relevance. Results report improved claim and evidence relevance and stronger quality for in-domain and cross-domain real-world scenarios. However, unreliable evidence sources can introduce inaccuracies and reduce validity.

Abstract

Many responses to misinformation lack politeness and supporting facts. RARG is designed to generate polite, factual responses that clearly refute misinformation.

Introduction

Counter-responses are produced by users, fact-checkers, and automated generation methods to prevent the continued propagation of misinformation. Existing responses may lack politeness, direct refutation, or supporting evidence.

Related Work / Detecting and Countering Misinformation

Responses to misinformation are often produced through social correction by users or experts. Many such responses lack politeness and supporting facts.

opening

The response is generated by the actor model based on the input claim and retrieved evidence. It is evaluated for refutation, factuality, politeness, and relevance during optimization.

Experiments / Experiment Design .. Response Generation Results

Counter-misinformation responses are generated by RARG for both in-domain and cross-domain data. The reported results indicate improved claim and evidence relevance and stronger generation quality across real-world scenarios.

Limitations

Generated responses may contain inaccuracies and have reduced validity when the reliability of evidence sources cannot be guaranteed.
