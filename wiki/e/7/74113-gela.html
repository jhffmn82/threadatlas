Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Gela recovered political liberty with Xenodicus’s assistance, but Agathocles suspected it of intending to revolt, killed about four thousand inhabitants, and left a strong garrison there. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Gela sheltered Agrigentum’s evacuees, sent five hundred soldiers to aid Agrigentum, and was captured and plundered by the Carthaginians, under whom it became tributary.

Gela in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Gela first appears

Abstract written by a model

Gela sheltered Agrigentum’s evacuees, its roads and countryside filling with refugees, while Dexippus, honored there, sent five hundred soldiers to aid Agrigentum. After surrounding devastation, Amilcar besieged Gela; weak confederate aid and Dionysius’s failed relief led to its capture and plunder by Carthaginians, under whom it was tributary.

Gela was where Dexippus lived in high esteem and from which he brought five hundred soldiers to aid Agrigentum. The Agrigentines who evacuated their city were escorted to Gela, and its roads and surrounding country filled with the refugees.

CHAP. XVI.

Gela was besieged by Amilcar after the surrounding country was devastated. Its inhabitants resisted without effective aid from their confederates, but after Dionysius's failed relief attempt and nighttime withdrawal, the Carthaginians entered the city and plundered it.
