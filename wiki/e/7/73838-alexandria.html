Alexandria in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Alexandria first appears

Abstract written by a model

Alexandria is a city associated with Alexander: one account places it between marshes and the sea, near Pharos and its haven, with a long broad street, a large palace, and more than three hundred thousand freemen. Another calls it near the foot of Caucasus, at a pass toward Media. Alexander founded Alexandria in Egypt, named it after himself, planned its streets, walls, defenses, palace, and harbor access; succeeding kings enlarged it. Ptolemy deposited Alexander’s body there and built him a temple. It later received Roman ambassadors, and another Alexandria in Paropamisus was repopulated and given new governors.

Alexander founded Alexandria in Egypt between the marshes and the sea and named it after himself. He planned its streets, walls, defenses, palace, and harbor access, and the city was later enlarged by succeeding kings.

CHAP. IIL

Alexandria, the city Alexander had built, became the place where Ptolemy deposited Alexander's body. Ptolemy built a temple there in Alexander's honor.

23. Ambassadors came to Rome both from the yoonger and the

Alexandria receives Scipio Africanus and the other Roman ambassadors. Ptolemy shows them the palace, treasury, city, and the Pharos, while the delegation studies the city's situation and capacity.

SUP. XII.

Alexandria, which Alexander had built in Paropamisus during his first expedition into Bactria, was repopulated with inhabitants from neighboring countries and Macedonians no longer fit for war. Alexander removed its governor for bad behavior, appointed Nicanor as governor, and appointed Tyriaspes over the wider region.
