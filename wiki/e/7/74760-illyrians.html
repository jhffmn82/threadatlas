Illyrians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Illyrians were a kingdom bordering the Macedonians. Allied through Alcetas with Dionysius, they received arms, invaded Epirus, defeated the Molossians, attacked Paros, and lost their fleet. They subdued Amyntas, held Philip hostage, then fought Philip under Bardyllis, surrendered Macedonian cities, and made peace. Later, with Thracian kings and Paeones, they yielded to Philip’s kingdom; the Roman Senate eventually liberated them and allowed their own laws.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. n.

The Illyrians allied with Dionysius through Alcetas and received soldiers and arms from him. They invaded Epirus to restore Alcetas, defeated the Molossians, and later attacked the Greek settlement of Paros before their fleet was defeated by Dionysius's governor of Lissus.

BOOK XVI.

The Illyrians had subdued Amyntas and forced him to pay tribute, taking Philip as a hostage. They later invaded Macedon, fought Philip under Bardyllis, were defeated, and surrendered all the cities belonging to Macedon before making peace.

CHAP. V.

The Illyrians joined the kings of Thrace and the Paeones against Philip. Philip surprised the allied forces before their troops were formed and forced them to yield to the Macedonian kingdom.
