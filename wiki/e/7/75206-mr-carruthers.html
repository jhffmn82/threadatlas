Mr. Carruthers in The Return of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1903-09-26

Mr. Carruthers is a rich South African widower, deeply interested in gold shares, who employs Violet Smith as music teacher and domestic employee at Chiltern Grange, where his ten-year-old daughter and housekeeper Mrs. Dixon live. An older, polite, musical man, he proposes to Violet; she refuses, and his deep, honourable but unrequited love makes matters strained. He had joined a South African scheme with Jack Woodley to obtain Violet’s inheritance, initially as a prospective conspirator, but rejects forced marriage because he will not use violence. He secretly follows Violet in a beard and dog-cart to protect her, intervening after Woodley assaults her. At the forced wedding he shoots Woodley and admits his love, having tried to stop the marriage. Holmes judges that he has made efforts to amend his role in the evil plot, though he faces a lenient punishment for the assault.

THE ADVENTURE OF THE SOLITARY CYCLIST

Mr. Carruthers is a South African acquaintance of Ralph Smith who hires Violet Smith to teach his ten-year-old daughter at Chiltern Grange. He is described as an older, polite, musical widower, and he intervenes when Mr. Woodley assaults Violet; Violet also suspects that Carruthers takes a personal interest in her.

We had ascertained from the lady that she went down upon the

Miss Smith’s employer proposes marriage to her and takes her refusal seriously but gently. His feelings are described as deep and honourable, though the refusal leaves the situation strained.

The Thursday brought us another letter from our client.

Bob Carruthers follows Violet Smith by bicycle while disguised with a dark beard, intending to protect her without revealing himself. He reaches the forced wedding, shoots Jack Woodley, and admits that he loves Violet. He explains that he and Woodley came from South Africa to pursue Violet’s inheritance, but that he rejected the use of violence and later tried to prevent Woodley from marrying her.

Holmes rose and tossed the end of his cigarette into the grate.

Mr. Carruthers is told that he has done what he could to make amends for his share in an evil plot. Holmes gives him his card and offers evidence for his trial; Carruthers’s assault is apparently treated leniently, with a few months considered sufficient punishment.
