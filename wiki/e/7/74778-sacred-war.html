Sacred War in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Sacred War was a war concerning the oracle, begun when Philomelus seized the temple at Delphos. It pitted the Phocians and allies including Athenians and Lacedaemonians against Boeotians, Locrians, and other defenders of the oracle. Onomarchus was an instigator, and his son Phalaecus later commanded the Phocians. The conflict, marked by the consequences of seizing and robbing the oracle, lasted nine or ten years. It ended when Phalaecus negotiated terms with Philip and withdrew; the Phocians surrendered, and the Amphictyonic Council set their penalties.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. VI.

The Sacred War began after Philomelus seized the temple at Delphos and continued for nine years. The conflict opposed the Phocians and their allies, including the Athenians and Lacedaemonians, to the Boeotians, Locrians, and others who defended the oracle.

CHAP. Vlll.

The sacred war is identified in the account with the conflict in which Onomarchus was an instigator. Phalaecus, the son of Onomarchus, later became general of the Phocians during the continuing conflict.

CHAP. X.

The Sacred War continued for ten years between the Phocians and their opponents. It ended when Phalaecus made terms with Philip and withdrew, after which the Phocians surrendered and the Amphictyonic Council determined their penalties.

Phocian war in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Phocian war began when Philomelus seized the temple at Delphi, after the Amphictyons decreed war against the Phocians for sacrilege and Philomelus defeated the Locrians. It involved Phocians, Boeotians, Locrians, Thessalians, and allies; battles exhausted Thebans and their treasury. Philomelus and then Onomarchus died, and Philip's intervention ended it with punishment of the Phocians.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. VII.

The Phocian war began after the Amphictyons decreed war against the Phocians for sacrilege. It involved the Phocians, Boeotians, Locrians, Thessalians, and other Greek allies, and continued through the defeats and deaths of Philomelus and Onomarchus.

CHAP. Vlll.

The Phocian war continued through battles between the Phocians and Boeotians, including engagements at Orchomenum, the river Cephisus, Coronea, and Chaeronea. The war exhausted the Thebans and depleted their treasure, leading them to request financial aid from the king of Persia.

BOOK XIV.

The Phocian War began when Philomelus seized the temple at Delphi after defeating the Locrians. Philomelus died in battle, Onomarchus succeeded him, and the war ended after Philip's intervention and the punishment of the Phocians.

Sacred War in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The Sacred War was a war between the Thebans and Phocians, beginning after the Phocians seized and plundered the temple. During it, both sides made dedications at Delphi. It ended ten years later, in the first year of the 108th Olympiad, when Philip brought it to a close, destroying many Phocian towns, politically punishing the Phocians, and changing the Amphictyonic Council.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER III.

The Phocian or Sacred War ended when Philip brought it to a close. It had followed the plunder of the temple and resulted in the destruction of many Phocian towns and the political punishment of the Phocians.

CHAPTER VIII.

The Sacred War ended ten years after the Phocians seized the temple. Its conclusion was followed by changes to the Amphictyonic Council, including the admission of the Macedonians and the exclusion of the Phocians and Lacedæmonians.

CHAPTER XIII.

The Sacred War was the conflict in which the Thebans dedicated a statue of Hercules at Delphi while fighting the Phocians. The Phocians also dedicated a group depicting Hercules and Apollo in connection with their campaign against the Thessalians led by Tellias of Elis.
