Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Salamis is described as the greatest Cypriot city, ruled by Evagoras under Persian suzerainty and later held by Menelaus for Ptolemy before Demetrius besieged it. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Salamis is described as an island and metropolis of Cyprus, a refuge and fleet station for the Greeks, the site of their victory over the Persians, and a strongly garrisoned city later taken by Evagoras, who became king.

Salamis in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Salamis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Salamis, the greatest Cypriot city, was ruled by Evagoras under Persian suzerainty and besieged by Persians, yet remained his after tribute and recognition of Artaxerxes. Held by Menelaus for Ptolemy, it was later besieged by Demetrius after Menelaus’s defeat.

The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian / BOOK XV.

Salamis was ruled by Evagoras and became the main object of the Persian siege. It ultimately remained under Evagoras, who agreed to pay tribute and recognize Artaxerxes as a king above him.

CHAP. ni.

Salamis was held by Menelaus for Ptolemy. Demetrius defeated Menelaus outside the city, besieged Salamis by land and sea, and fought Ptolemy's relieving fleet near it.

Salamis in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Salamis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Salamis is an island and a metropolis of Cyprus, described as the island’s greatest and richest city. It became refuge for Athenians and station for the Grecian fleet after Artemisium; in its straits the Grecians defeated the Persians, after which the Persian fleet withdrew. Athenians later fled there during Mardonius’s invasion. Salamis held a strong, well-supplied Persian garrison that resisted Cimon’s siege, and it was eventually taken by Evagoras, who became king after expelling Abdemon the Tyrian.

Salamis became the refuge of the Athenians and the station of the Grecian fleet after the fleet withdrew from Artemisium. The Grecians fought the Persians in its straits and won a major naval victory there.

CHAP. HI.

After the battle at Salamis, the Persian fleet withdrew to Cuma and later Samos. The Athenians also fled to Salamis when Mardonius invaded Attica.

DIODORUS SICULUS. / BOOK XIL

Salamis was the metropolis of Cyprus and contained a strong Persian garrison with abundant arms, ammunition, and provisions. Cimon considered its capture essential to securing the whole island, but the garrison successfully defended the city during the siege.

CHAP. XL

Salamis was taken by Evagoras, who became its king after expelling Abdemon the Tyrian. It was described as the greatest and richest city of Cyprus.
