the forest in Toto's Merry Winter

Laura E. Richards · 1887

The forest is a wild home and beloved woodland place for Toto, the party of friends, brown mice, and many small creatures. It contains Chucky's aunt's burrow, a hollow tree, and a fairy pool. In fresh snow it shows creatures' tracks and homes and offers the brown mice space for a new home. In spring it calls wild creatures back; Toto, Bruin, and the grandmother return to enjoy its pool, greenery, water, birds, and insects.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IV.

The forest is covered with fresh snow and contains tracks and homes of many small creatures. The woodmouse says the brown mice can choose a new home there within a range of ten miles.

CHAPTER XV.

The forest calls the woodchuck and the other wild creatures back in spring, with wet earth, running sap, swelling buds, and young reeds signaling the season. Toto and the animals return there after their winter at the cottage, and Toto, Bruin, and the grandmother later visit the forest to enjoy its pool, greenery, leaves, water, birds, and insects.
