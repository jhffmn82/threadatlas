Sicilians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Sicilians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Sicilians are a people said to have moved from Italy into Sicily with their families after the Sicanians withdrew from parts of the island. They spread into neighboring lands, fought the Sicanians, and gave the island its name. Once frightened by barbarian forces, they regained courage after Gelon’s victory and honored his modest and humane rule. Under Dionysius, they first supported him, but hatred led many to side with the Carthaginians, defend Messana, or retreat to forts. After the Carthaginian rout, those allies escaped to their cities. At Taurominium, they resisted Dionysius, expelled his forces, and killed more than six hundred men.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

DIODORUS SICULUS. .. BOOK. V.

The Sicilians are said to have transported themselves from Italy into Sicily after the Sicanians had withdrawn from parts of the island. They expanded into neighboring territories, fought the Sicanians, and later gave their name to the island.

CHAP. U.

The Sicilians were initially terrified by the size of the barbarian forces, but Gelon's victory restored their courage. They honored Gelon, who later behaved with modesty and humanity toward them.

The Sicilians initially supported Dionysius, but many deserted to the Carthaginians because of their hatred of him. Some joined the defense of Messana, while others withdrew to castles and forts; Dionysius later gathered forces and fought with them near Catana.

The Carthaginians being thus routed both by sea and knd, seat

Sicilians who had sided with the Carthaginians fled through the countryside and escaped to their own cities before the Syracusans could reach them. After the Carthaginian defeat, former inhabitants of cities taken by Carthage returned to their countries, while Dionysius continued campaigns and settlements throughout Sicily.

CHAP. IX,

The Sicilians at Taurominium defended the place against Dionysius, believing they had a right to protect their country and avenge their ancestors. They drove Dionysius's forces from the city and castle and killed more than six hundred of his men.
