Megalopolitans in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Megalopolitans were residents of Megalopolis who struggled against the Lacedaemonians, seeking and receiving aid from Argives, Sicyonians, Messenians, and Thebans before making a truce. Later, loyal to Cassander because they favored oligarchy, they prepared fifteen thousand citizens, strangers, and servants for siege, repelled a Macedonian assault, rebuilt defenses, and defeated Polyperchon’s elephants.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. Vlll.

The Megalopolitans came into conflict with the Lacedaemonians and sought aid from their confederates because they could not resist the Lacedaemonians by their own strength. The Argives, Sicyonians, Messenians, and Thebans assisted them, and they eventually entered into a truce with the Lacedaemonians.

The Megalopolitans remained loyal to Cassander and prepared Megalopolis for siege by gathering supplies and organizing fifteen thousand people able to bear arms. They repelled the Macedonian assault, rebuilt their defenses, and defeated Polyperchon’s elephants with concealed spiked planks.
