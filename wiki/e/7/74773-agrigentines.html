Agrigentines in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Agrigentines joined the Sicilian liberty movement against Agathocles, appointing and supplying Xenodicus as general. They captured or liberated cities including Gela and Enna to restore ancient laws, but defeats by Agathocles’s captains led them to summon Xenodochus for trial; he fled to Gela.

Agrigentines in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Agrigentines are a populous, wealthy civic group inhabiting Agrigentum, with more than 20,000 citizens and at least 200,000 people including resident strangers. Their prosperity came from vineyards, olive production, and trade with the Libyans, and was expressed in luxury. They aided Motya, were defeated by Deucetius, later retook Motya, and encamped with the Syracusans. Angered when the Syracusans spared Deucetius without their consent, they fought them, lost at the river Himera, and made peace. They received Selinus's refugees hospitably, hired Dexippus for relief soldiers, and joined Dionysius. Rejecting Carthaginian alliance and neutrality, they mobilized, endured siege and famine, evacuated toward Gela, and received Leontium from the Syracusans.

The Agrigentines became angry with the Syracusans because they had spared Deucetius without their consent and because of envy of the Syracusans. They made war upon the Syracusans, were defeated at the river Himera, and afterward sent ambassadors to Syracuse to make peace.

CHAP. XII.

The Agrigentines were prosperous through vineyards, olive production, and trade with the Libyans, and they displayed their wealth in buildings, entertainments, clothing, and precious household objects. During the siege they were so accustomed to luxury that a decree limiting guards to a bed, tent, woollen mantle, and two pillows was considered harsh.

The Agrigentines rejected the Carthaginians' proposed alliance and neutrality, mobilized their able-bodied population, and defended their city. After prolonged resistance and the loss of outside support, they suffered severe famine, evacuated toward Gela, and were later given Leontium by the Syracusans for habitation.
