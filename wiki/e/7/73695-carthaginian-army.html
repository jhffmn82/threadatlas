Abstract written by a model

The Carthaginian army, commanded by Hanno in Sicily, was a large force of men, foot soldiers, horse, chariots, wagons, siege equipment, supplies, and 150 warships. It entered Sicily, besieged Entella, camped near Syracuse, and withdrew after Timoleon’s reinforcement. Later, near a river, Timoleon defeated part of it during a storm, causing at least 10,000 deaths and over 15,000 prisoners.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. XI.

The Carthaginian army brought fifty thousand men, three hundred chariots, two thousand carts, siege engines, weapons, and provisions into Sicily, supported by one hundred and fifty warships. It besieged Entella and later camped near Syracuse, but withdrew to Carthaginian territory after Timoleon was reinforced.

CHAP, XIII.

The Carthaginian army numbered about seventy thousand foot soldiers, with horsemen, chariots, and wagons, and was supported by a large fleet. It was defeated when Timoleon attacked part of it near a river and a violent storm hindered the Carthaginians; many were killed, drowned, or captured.
