Nereids in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

Sea goddesses dwelling in unfathomed depths, Nereids are Thetis’ sisters; hearing Achaean mourning, they rise in dark mantles to lament her son and anoint Achilles’ bones.

So the kings drew him from that stricken field

The Daughters of Nereus hear the Achaeans' mourning and rise from the sea in dark mantles to join the lament for their sister Thetis' son. They shed ambrosia and precious nards over Achilles' bones after the funeral fire.

Nereids in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The Nereids are immortal sea-nymphs, including Amphitrite, Thetis, and Galatea. Offended when Cassiopeia compares her beauty to theirs, they send a sea-monster against Aethiopia and later secure her head-downward starry placement near the pole.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

PERSEUS AND MEDUSA

The Sea-Nymphs become indignant when Cassiopeia compares her beauty with theirs. They cause a sea-monster to ravage the coast of Aethiopia and later ensure that Cassiopeia's starry form is placed near the pole, where she is held head downward for part of each night.

Chapter XV

The Nereids are sea-nymphs and are described as immortal. Amphitrite, Thetis, and Galatea are among the most celebrated Nereids.
