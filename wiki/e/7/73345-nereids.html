Abstract written by a model

In The Argonautica, the Nereids gather at Thetis’s call, travel to the Ausonian sea, and surround and lift the Argo over the waves through the Wandering rocks. In The Library, the Nereids are sea nymphs who inhabit a cave, carry a young man there for his music, and reject a sea-maiden after motherhood. In The Library, the Nereids become angry with Cassiopeia over her claim of superior beauty, and Poseidon shares their anger by sending a flood and sea monster against the land. In The Fall of Troy, the Nereids are sea goddesses and Thetis’s sisters who rise to lament Achilles and anoint his bones. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Nereids are immortal sea-nymphs who send a sea-monster against Aethiopia and secure Cassiopeia’s head-downward starry placement after she compares her beauty to theirs.

Nereids in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Nereids first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Nereids are immortal sea-nymphs, including Amphitrite, Thetis, and Galatea. Offended when Cassiopeia compares her beauty to theirs, they send a sea-monster against Aethiopia and later secure her head-downward starry placement near the pole.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

PERSEUS AND MEDUSA

The Sea-Nymphs become indignant when Cassiopeia compares her beauty with theirs. They cause a sea-monster to ravage the coast of Aethiopia and later ensure that Cassiopeia's starry form is placed near the pole, where she is held head downward for part of each night.

Chapter XV

The Nereids are sea-nymphs and are described as immortal. Amphitrite, Thetis, and Galatea are among the most celebrated Nereids.
