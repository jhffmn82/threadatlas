Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Pharnabazus is the Persian king’s commander-in-chief, preparing funds and war plans, requesting Iphicrates, commanding the expedition against Egypt, disputing with Iphicrates, and later revolting, routing the king’s troops with Chares, and funding Chares’s soldiers. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Pharnabazus is a Persian lieutenant, satrap, commander, and naval leader who supports Lacedaemonian operations, deals with Alcibiades, raises forces, helps obtain and commands a fleet with Conon, relieves Caunus, and incites Persian-allied cities and islands to revolt.

Pharnabazus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Pharnabazus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pharnabazus is a commander-in-chief appointed by the Persian king. He prepares money and war plans, requests Iphicrates from Athens, and commands the Persian army in the expedition against Egypt. After delaying the expedition for years, he disputes with Iphicrates over attacking Memphis and quarrels with him again after the army returns to Asia. Later, having revolted from the Persian king, he and Chares rout the king's troops despite a small force, and he funds Chares's soldiers.

Whilst these things were acting in Greece, Acoris^king of Egypt^

Pharnabazus was appointed commander-in-chief by the Persian king and prepared money for the war. He complained to Athens that Chabrias's service under Acoris had alienated the Persian king and requested Iphicrates.

CHAP. V.

Pharnabazus commanded the Persian forces in Egypt and delayed the expedition for many years. He disagreed with Iphicrates over attacking Memphis, and later renewed his quarrel with him after the army returned to Asia.

CHAP. V.

Pharnabazus had revolted from the Persian king and was preparing to fight the Persian lord-lieutenants with a small force. Chares joined him, and together they routed the king's troops; Pharnabazus then gave Chares enough money to pay all his soldiers.

Pharnabazus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Pharnabazus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pharnabazus is a Persian lieutenant, lord-lieutenant, satrap, commander, and naval leader serving the Persian king. He receives Miletan democratic refugees at Clauda, prepares money and three hundred Phoenician ships for the Lacedaemonians, but sends the ships back after Alcibiades argues that their success would harm Persian interests. He supports Lacedaemonian operations at Dardanium, Cyzicum, and Claros, where his army is defeated and withdraws. After exposing Alcibiades’s planned disclosure to Artaxerxes, he denies him a passport and has him killed. He makes truces with Dercyllidas, raises forces, helps obtain a fleet under Conon, relieves Caunus, and commands the Persian fleet with Conon. After Cnidus, they invade Lacedaemonian allies and incite cities and islands to revolt.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. 111.

Pharnabazus, the Persian lieutenant on the sea-coasts, was prepared to supply the Lacedaemonians with money and three hundred Phoenician ships. Alcibiades persuaded him to return the fleet to Phoenicia because strengthening the Lacedaemonians would not serve Persian interests.

CHAP. V.

Pharnabazus supported the Lacedaemonian fleet with land forces at Dardanium and assisted Mindarus in capturing Cyzicum. At Claros his army fought from the shore, but it was eventually broken and withdrew with the Peloponnesians.

CHAP. III.

Pharnabazus learned of Alcibiades's intended disclosure to Artaxerxes and sent a messenger to reveal the conspiracy first. He later refused Alcibiades a passport and arranged for men to ambush and kill him.

CHAP. VI.

Pharnabazus agreed to an eight-month truce with Dercyllidas and later persuaded the Persian king to equip a fleet and appoint Conon as admiral. He raised forces with Tissaphernes and marched toward Ephesus, where a further truce was arranged.

CHAP. vm.

Pharnabazus joined Artaphemes in bringing a large army to relieve Caunus. Conon later chose Pharnabazus as his Persian colleague and assistant in command.

CHAP. IX,

Pharnabazus commanded the Persian fleet with Conon. After the battle of Cnidus, he and Conon invaded the Lacedaemonians' confederates and brought several cities and islands into revolt.
