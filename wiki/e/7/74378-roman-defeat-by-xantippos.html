Roman defeat by Xantippos in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Roman defeat by Xantippos was a sudden reversal in the war: after Attilius's Roman army defeated Carthaginians and captured African towns and castles, Xanthippus took command, totally routed and destroyed the Romans, inflicted prodigious slaughter, confined survivors to a small town, and raised Carthaginians to prosperity.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XXIIL

A Roman army under the consul Attilius initially defeated the Carthaginians and captured many towns and castles in Africa. After Xanthippus the Spartan took command of the Carthaginians, they totally routed the Romans and destroyed the army.

BOOK XXIII.

Xantippos's defeat of the Romans produced a sudden reversal of the war. The Romans, who had recently been conquerors and masters of sea and land, were routed after a prodigious slaughter and confined to a small town.
