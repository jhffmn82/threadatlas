  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Uranus is the first king of the Atlantides, a civilizing ruler whose prognostications and astronomical skill lead to his worship as a god.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Uranus is Vesta’s spouse, father of Saturn, Rhea, and Ceres, and the first king, a just astrologer who first worshipped the heavenly gods.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Heaven is the primordial sky and god born from Earth, father of the Titans, Cyclopes, and hundred-handed sons, later mutilated by Cronos, and a place nine days’ fall from Earth.
  • In The Library, Heaven is the upper sky and source of fire, reached or entered by people and creatures through various means so they can obtain fire.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Heaven is the lofty, weightless, fiery realm of æther covering the universe, distinguished from the atmosphere and associated with Atlas, the stars, and the Giants’ assault on Olympus.
  • In The Library, Sky is the first ruler of the whole world and universe, husband of Earth, father of many divine races, binder of the Cyclopes in Tartarus, and victim of Cronus’s overthrow.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Uranus or Ouranos is Heaven, the all-covering region separated from earth and sea whose fiery portion became the skies and which was the source of Kronos or Saturn.

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Uranus is the first king of the Atlantides, ruling much of the world, especially westward and northward. He settles wandering people in towns and cities, teaches useful practices and the use and storage of earth’s fruits, and measures time by the sun and moon. After death, his prognostications and astronomical skill lead to his honor as a god.

Uranus is described as the first king of the Atlantides, who settled wandering people in towns and cities and taught them to use and store the fruits of the earth. He practiced astrology, measured years and months by the sun and moon, divided days into hours, and was honored as a god after his death; the starry heaven was named after him.

Heaven in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Heaven, a starry primordial sky and god, was born from Earth as her equal, though one account calls him Aether’s son. With Earth he fathered Titans, three Cyclopes, and three hundred-handed sons, then hid the Cyclopes and his children in a secret place of Earth. Cronos mutilated him. Later, with Earth, he told Rhea what was destined for Cronos and Zeus; he is also a place nine days’ fall from Earth.

Heaven in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Heaven is the sky, an upper world and source of fire. It matters most as a remote place people, animals, birds, insects, and Prometheus reach or enter to obtain fire. Earth people make arrows into a ladder or chain, or tear a hole in it; a spider’s thread reaches it, and the woodpecker and man descend after gaining fire.

The sky is the destination and battleground of the Earth people. Arrows stick into it to form a ladder or chain, and in one version the Earth people tear a hole in it to enter the upper world.

Heaven in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Heaven is a lofty, weightless fiery realm of æther in the highest region, covering the universe and resting on Atlas's mountain form with many stars. At first it covers all things, mixed with earth, sea, and air; once distinguished from gross atmosphere, it rises highest. Giants seek sovereignty by stacking Olympus, Pelion, and Ossa, while Calisto and Arcas become neighboring constellations.

Narrative, section by section

opening

opening

Uranus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Uranus, or Ouranos, is Heaven embracing all things, an all-covering region before creation was ordered; separated from earth and sea, its fiery portion became skies, and it was source of Kronos or Saturn.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter I

Uranus, or Ouranos, represented Heaven embracing all things. He was described as the source from which Kronos or Saturn was born.

CHAPTER II

Heaven was separated from the earth and sea, with its fiery portion rising to form the skies. The gods' palaces were said to stand along the Milky Way in heaven.
