Megalopolis in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Megalopolis is a city built and founded by the Arcadians after defeat by the Lacedemonians, in a secure location and populated from twenty villages. Its forced settlers later left, but Pammenes made them return. Later, supporting Cassander, it withstood Polyperchon’s siege despite damaged towers and walls and an elephant assault.

CHAP. VIIL

Megalopolis was founded by the Arcadians after their defeat by the Lacedemonians. It was placed in a location suited for security and populated with Menalians and Parrhasians from twenty villages.

Megalopolis faced a conflict with neighboring towns that had been forced to settle there and later returned to their former homes. The Megalopolitans sought Athenian aid, and Pammenes compelled the towns to return to Megalopolis.

Megalopolis in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Megalopolis is a city and large town in Arcadia, Greece, created as one large settlement for Arcadians gathered from many towns after Epaminondas the Theban persuaded them to leave insecure small towns. Epaminondas is regarded as its founder, alongside founders from Mantinea, Tegea, Clitor, Mænalus, and Parrhasium. Peopled zealously by Arcadians, it received Greece’s best wishes and became strong through alliance with Thebes, whose arms crowned it. It resisted Lacedæmonian attacks, defeated Acrotatus and, under Aristodemus, the Lacedæmonians; its Myropolis portico was built from Lacedæmonian spoils. It later joined the Achæan League. Cleomenes eventually captured and destroyed it by fire, defeating and expelling its people; a nearby monument to those killed in that battle was called the Treaty Violation because he violated the treaty. During this crisis Philopœmen, a native, evacuated two-thirds of the adult citizens and all women and children to Messene, then brought exiles back armed without accepting conditions. Pylades, a Pythian-winning harper, was also native to the city. The Helisson divides Megalopolis into two parts; the southern side contains a theatre. It had a market-place, temples, precincts, porticoes, public offices, and a council chamber, as well as the Philip’s portico and a statue honoring Polybius. It borders Heræa at the sources of the Buphagus, and the Alpheus reemerges at Pegæ in its territory. Roads lead from it toward Messene, Lacedæmon, Methydrium, Pallantium, Tegea, and other inland places, including through its suburb Ladocea. Once prestigious and fortunate, it had, in the author’s day, lost that standing and become mostly ruins.

Narrative, section by section

11 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXVI.

Megalopolis received an Arcadian colony at the time when many inhabitants abandoned Aliphera. Buphagium and the sources of the Buphagus lie on the boundary between the districts of Megalopolis and Heræa.

CHAPTER XXVII.

Megalopolis was founded as a recent city of Arcadia by gathering inhabitants from many Arcadian towns, and it became strong through its alliance with Thebes. Its people resisted Lacedæmonian attacks, defeated Acrotatus, later joined the Achæan League, and were eventually defeated and expelled when Cleomenes captured and destroyed the city.

CHAPTER XXVIII.

Megalopolis lies along the road from Gortys, near a monument to those who fell in the battle against Cleomenes. Its people call the monument the Treaty Violation because Cleomenes violated the treaty.

CHAPTER XXX.

Megalopolis is divided by the river Helisson and contains a market-place, temples, precincts, porticoes, public offices, and a council chamber. Its people named the Philip’s portico after Philip the son of Amyntas to gratify him, and they erected the Myropolis portico from spoils taken from the Lacedæmonians. Megalopolis also honored Polybius with a statue on a pillar.

CHAPTER XXXIII.

Megalopolis was peopled with zeal by the Arcadians and received the best wishes of Greece. It has lost its ancient prestige and felicity and is now mostly ruins.

CHAPTER XXXIV.

Megalopolis is the starting point of the route described toward Messene. About 7 stades from it lies the temple of Maniæ, and Hermæum later marks the boundary between its district and Messenia.

CHAPTER XXXV.

Megalopolis is the starting point for roads to Messene, Lacedæmon, Methydrium, and other inland places. Roads from it pass through the Alpheus valley and connect with sites such as Phalæsiæ, Tricoloni, and the territory near Hermæum.

CHAPTER XLIV.

Megalopolis is connected by road with Pallantium and Tegea, and the road passes through its suburb Ladocea. The Alpheus reemerges at Pegæ in Megalopolis after flowing underground with the Eurotas.

CHAPTER XLIX.

Megalopolis was the home of Philopœmen and was captured by Cleomenes. Philopœmen evacuated two-thirds of its adult citizens and all its women and children to Messene, and later persuaded the exiles to return armed and without accepting conditions.

CHAPTER L.

Megalopolis was the home of Pylades, a noted harper who had won at the Pythian games. Philip sent men to Megalopolis to kill Philopœmen, but the attempt failed.

CHAPTER XIV.

Megalopolis was established by Epaminondas for the Arcadians after he persuaded them to evacuate their insecure small towns. It was one large town and was still called Megalopolis.
