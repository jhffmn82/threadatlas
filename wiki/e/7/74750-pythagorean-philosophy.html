Pythagorean philosophy in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Pythagorean philosophy centers on metempsychosis and transformation: souls are not destroyed but continually pass into new bodies, while physical forms change without permanent destruction. Presented through Pythagoras, it connects these teachings to abstinence from animal food and the principle that nothing in the universe remains permanently one form.

FABLES II. AND III. [XV.60-478]

Pythagorean philosophy, as presented through Pythagoras, teaches that souls are not destroyed, that they pass into new bodies, that animal food should be avoided, and that nothing in the universe remains permanently in one form.

Pythagorean philosophy is described as having two main features: metempsychosis, or the continual transmigration of souls, and an account of changes in the physical world. Its teachings are associated with abstinence from animal flesh and with the principle that everything changes without being destroyed.
