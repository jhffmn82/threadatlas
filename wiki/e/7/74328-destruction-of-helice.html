destruction of Helice

Also called: an earthquake, earthquake that destroyed Helice, earthquakes, earthquakes and inundations in Peloponnesus, the destruction of Helice, the earthquake, this grievous calamity, this kind of earthquake

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, it is described as part of a grievous calamity in which earthquakes destroyed Helice and Bura, followed by an inundation that swept away people and houses. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, it is described as a deadly earthquake sent by Poseidon that destroyed Helice and caused the sea to cover its ruins.

Abstract written by a model

Nighttime earthquakes destroyed Helice and Bura as buildings collapsed; the sea later swept away people and houses, attributed to nature or divine anger.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAP. V.

The earthquakes occurred at night, causing buildings to collapse on their inhabitants, and the sea later swept away people and houses. The destruction was attributed either to natural causes or to divine anger over religious impiety.

destruction of Helice in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where destruction of Helice first appears

Abstract written by a model

The destruction of Helice was a most deadly earthquake, sent by Poseidon as vengeance for the killing of suppliants. During Asteus's archonship, in the fourth year of the 101st Olympiad, it rapidly overthrew buildings, undermined foundations, and rased Helice to the ground. The sea's advance obscured its ruins; no inhabitants remained, and Ægium occupied the territory.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIV.

The earthquake that destroyed Helice was described as the most deadly kind, undermining the foundations of buildings and making them rock to and fro. It left no clear trace of previous habitation and, together with the sea's advance, rased Helice to the ground.

CHAPTER XXV.

The destruction of Helice occurred when Asteus was Archon at Athens, in the fourth year of the 101st Olympiad, when Damon of Thuria was victor. No inhabitants remained afterward, and the people of Ægium occupied the territory.
