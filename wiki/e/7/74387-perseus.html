In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Perseus was king of Macedonia and father of Andriscus, whose attacks on Roman allies violated the peace and led Macedonia to defeat.

Perseus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Perseus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Perseus was the last king of Macedonia, also called king of Macedon. Suspected of stirring disturbances in Thessaly and among the Aetolians, driving Abrypolis from his kingdom, and cooperating against Rome, he faced a Senate declaration of war and the expulsion of his ambassadors and Macedonians. He repeatedly opposed the Romans with great armies but was marked by negligence, covetousness, and poor judgment, including alienating Gaulish helpers and destroying treasure and ships. Defeated and captured by Aemilius, he lost Macedonia, was imprisoned, later moved to milder confinement, and died in the prison of Albinius after deprivation of sleep.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XXXI.

Perseus, the last king of Macedonia, was captured by Aemilius after repeatedly opposing the Romans in the field, and Aemilius celebrated a triumph after the victory. Perseus was confined in the prison of Alba, later moved to a milder confinement through the intervention of Marcus Aemilius, and eventually died after his keeper deprived him of sleep.

37. P. Scipio, when he was but very young, performed great and

Perseus repeatedly acted with negligence, covetousness, and poor judgment. He refused to pay the Gauls who had come to assist him, lost their support, ordered treasure and ships destroyed, removed the inhabitants of Dion to Pydna, and was eventually conquered by the Romans, confined with a keeper, and brought to a miserable end.

BOOK XXXVIL

Perseus was suspected of causing disturbances in Thessaly and among the Aetolians and of driving Abrypolis, king of Thrace, from his kingdom. The Senate declared war against him, expelled his ambassadors and the Macedonians from Rome and Italy, and later investigated alleged cooperation between Perseus and Eumenes.

Perseus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Perseus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Perseus, king of Macedonia and father of Andriscus, began at peace with the Romans under Philip's agreement, but attacked their ally Abrupolis and dispossessed the Sapæans, violating the peace and leading Macedonia to defeat in war.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER X.

Perseus was initially at peace with the Romans under the agreement made with his father, Philip, but violated it by leading an army against Abrupolis, king of the Sapæans, and dispossessing them. He and the Macedonians were defeated in war because of this action.
