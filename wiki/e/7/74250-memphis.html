Memphis in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Memphis is the most famous city of Egypt, situated at the Nile’s branching head in the Delta, where it commands river shipping. Founded by Uchoreus, it had a circuit of one hundred and fifty furlongs and was protected by a rampart and trench against inundation and enemies; the Nile flowed around it and inundated its southern side. Most later kings preferred it to Thebes as a royal residence. It was linked to canal works toward the sea, and Sesostris placed statues of himself, his wife, and his sons in its temple of Vulcan. After Sabacos departed, twelve nobles called a senate there; Psammetichus built at the temple, and Amasis moved mercenary soldiers there. Apis was worshipped, kept, and later died there. Memphis also stands near Acherusia and its funerary landscape, with Vulcan’s stately porch and the field called Golden Venus.
