Demosthenes in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Demosthenes is an eminent Greek orator and political speaker who urged Athens to defend Greece against Philip and Macedonian rule. He advised seeking Boeotian aid, led an embassy to Thebes, and persuaded the Boeotians to join Athens for their common liberty. He later encouraged resistance to Alexander, supplied the Thebans with arms, and was among the orators Alexander demanded, though later pardoned after an Athenian embassy. The records also report Persian money for opposing Macedonia and that he fled after condemnation over Harpalus's bribes.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. X.

Demosthenes, described as the most eminent Greek in politics and eloquence at the time, chiefly incited the Athenians to defend all Greece against Philip.

CHAP. XIV.

Demosthenes urged the terrified Athenians to send an embassy to Thebes and persuade the Boeotians to defend their common liberty. He led the embassy himself and prevailed upon the Boeotians to join Athens.

BOOK XVII.

Demosthenes encouraged Athens to resist Macedonian rule and was associated with opposition to Alexander. He refused to accompany the Athenian ambassadors to Alexander, and the text reports that he had received Persian money to oppose the Macedonians.

Demosthenes solicited the Boeotians to join the Athenians against Philip.

Demosthenes in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Demosthenes was twice exiled by the Athenians, first to Calauria and again after the defeat at Lamia. Returning to Calauria, he died by poison, avoiding surrender by Archias to Antipater and the Macedonians. He was honored with a tomb in Poseidon’s precinct and elsewhere in Greece, and Philoxenus’ letters omitted him from recipients of Harpalus’ money.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VII.

Demosthenes was twice driven into exile by the Athenians, first to Calauria and later again after the defeat at Lamia. He returned to Calauria and died there by taking poison; he was the only exile whom Archias did not hand over to Antipater and the Macedonians.

CHAPTER XXXII.

Demosthenes had a tomb within the precincts of Poseidon at Calaurea and received honors from the people of Calaurea and elsewhere in Greece. The account states that he had no share in the money brought from Asia by Harpalus. Although Philoxenus obtained information about those who received Harpalus’s money and sent their names and sums to Athens, he did not mention Demosthenes, despite Alexander’s hatred of him and Philoxenus’s private enmity.
