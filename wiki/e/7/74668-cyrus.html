Cyrus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Cyrus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cyrus is presented as a Persian ruler and prince whose records combine accounts of conquest, rule, rebellion, and death. As king of Persia and the most powerful prince of his age, he conquered Astyages and transferred the empire from the Medes to the Persians. Another account says that, after leading a mighty army into Scythia, he was defeated, captured, and crucified by its queen. He is also described as Darius's son and Artaxerxes's brother. Summoned into Persia, he entrusted provincial affairs to Lysander; he commanded maritime provinces, appointed kinsmen as governors and Tanios of Memphis over Ionia and Aeolia, and prepared secretly to wage war against Artaxerxes. With Lacedaemonian aid, Greek mercenaries, and Asian troops, he assembled at Sardis, crossed the Euphrates toward Babylon, and concealed his purpose until gradually revealing it and raising pay to prevent desertion. In battle he struck Artaxerxes with a javelin but was mortally wounded by a common Persian soldier. His death frightened his army, prompted the Greeks to retreat, and ended the expedition in defeat.

Cyrus, king of Persia, led a mighty army into Scythia. The queen of Scythia defeated his army, captured him in battle, and afterward crucified him.

CHAP. III.

Cyrus secretly prepared with the Lacedaemonians to make war against his brother Artaxerxes. After Clearchus fled to Ionia, Cyrus became familiar with him, gave him money, and ordered him to raise an army of foreign soldiers.

CHAP. V-

Cyrus secretly prepared an expedition against his brother Artaxerxes, gathering about seventy thousand men from Asia and thirteen thousand Greek mercenaries. He marched through Cilicia and across the Euphrates toward Babylon, disclosed his design to the army only gradually, and promised rewards to secure its continued support. In the battle, he struck Artaxerxes down with a javelin but was mortally wounded by a common Persian soldier and died.
