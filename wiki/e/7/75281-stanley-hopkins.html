Stanley Hopkins in The Return of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1903-09-26

Stanley Hopkins is a young, promising Scotland Yard police inspector and detective who admires Sherlock Holmes as a pupil and repeatedly seeks his scientific help in difficult murder investigations. In the Peter Carey case, Hopkins reports failure, presents the scene and evidence, leads Holmes and Watson to it, joins a stakeout, and captures John Hopley Neligan. Convinced that Neligan’s bloodstained notebook proves guilt, he is corrected when Holmes identifies Patrick Cairns as the killer; Hopkins accepts Holmes’s superior reasoning and is told to release Neligan. At Yoxley Old Place, Hopkins investigates Willoughby Smith’s murder but cannot identify either motive or murderer. He brings Holmes the golden pince-nez, guides him through the grounds, follows Holmes’s lead concerning a strange woman, finds confirming witnesses, and arrests Anna after Holmes reveals the solution. At Abbey Grange, Hopkins summons Holmes after Sir Eustace Brackenstall’s killing, initially supports the theory that the Randall gang committed it, and supplies Lady Brackenstall’s account and the physical evidence. When the gang is arrested in New York and the stolen silver is recovered, Hopkins remains without a clear explanation. Across these cases, he is an active investigator and officer whose evidence-based but sometimes mistaken conclusions are refined by Holmes.

Stanley Hopkins reports absolute failure in the investigation into Peter Carey's death and asks Sherlock Holmes for help. He describes the crime scene and its evidence, including the harpoon, tobacco pouch, notebook, sheath-knife, rum bottle, and glasses.

Stanley Hopkins leads Holmes and Watson to the scene, identifies the signs of forced entry, and joins the stakeout. He captures John Hopley Neligan, questions him, produces Neligan's bloodstained notebook, and takes him toward the police station.

Inspector Stanley Hopkins believes that young Neligan’s notebook provides sufficient evidence that Neligan killed Peter Carey. Holmes disproves this theory, captures Patrick Cairns, and tells Hopkins to obtain Neligan’s release; Hopkins acknowledges Holmes’s superior reasoning.

Stanley Hopkins investigates the death of Willoughby Smith at Yoxley Old Place but cannot determine the motive or identify the murderer. He brings the case details and the golden pince-nez to Sherlock Holmes and asks Holmes to assist him.

THE ADVENTURE OF THE ABBEY GRANGE

Inspector Stanley Hopkins summons Sherlock Holmes to Abbey Grange after Sir Eustace Brackenstall is killed. He believes the crime was committed by the three members of the Lewisham gang and presents Lady Brackenstall’s account and the physical scene to Holmes.
