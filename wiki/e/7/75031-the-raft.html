the raft in The Magic of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1919

The raft is a small log raft built by Cap'n Bill with sharp-ended stakes to cross the river to the Isle of the Magic Flower, since he and Trot cannot travel beneath the water. Equipped with a long pole and a flat paddle he makes, it carries Trot, Cap'n Bill, and the Glass Cat to the Magic Isle. Left on its beach, it is later brought across the river by the Cowardly Lion and used by the Wizard to carry himself and Dorothy to the island.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

9. The Isle of the Magic Flower

Cap'n Bill constructs a raft from logs cut from the trees and binds them together with sharp-ended stakes. He plans to use it to float across the river to the Isle of the Magic Flower because Trot and he cannot walk beneath the water as the Glass Cat did.

10. Stuck Fast

Cap'n Bill builds a small raft from which he cuts a pole and whittles a flat paddle. It carries Trot, Cap'n Bill, and the Glass Cat across the river to the Magic Flower's island, but the Glass Cat leaves without taking it back because it cannot manage the raft alone.

18. The Magic of the Wizard

The raft used by Trot and Cap'n Bill remains on the beach of the Magic Isle. The Cowardly Lion brings it across the river, and the Wizard uses it to take himself and Dorothy to the island without stepping onto the island.
