  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian: Polyperchon is a Macedonian military and political leader whose career centers on commanding armies, protecting the kings, and struggling for control of Macedonia and Greece. He first enters Thessaly with a strong army, defeats Menon’s forces, kills Menon, and recovers Thessaly. Succeeding Antipater as protector of the kings, he receives chief and absolute authority in Macedonia, invites Olympias from Epirus to act as queen-regent, and becomes Cassander’s opponent. He issues an edict restoring Greek liberties, abolishes oligarchies, promotes democratic governments, and supports Eumenes against Antigonus. During the war with Cassander, Polyperchon commands Macedonian forces, marches into Attica, and besieges Cassander at the Piraeus, while leaving his son Alexander in command of remaining troops. He tries to balance his proclaimed liberties with his wish to retain the Piraeus, and has Phocion and his followers seized. He goes into the Peloponnesus, where he restores governments in many cities but fails at Megalopolis after an elephant assault. He also assembles forces with Aeacidas, brings Olympias and Alexander’s son back into Macedonia, and employs commanders including Eumenes and Callas. His position then weakens: Callas bribes away most of his soldiers, and, after being besieged in Naxos and learning of Olympias’s death, Polyperchon flees through Thessaly with Aeacidas to Aetolia. He later holds Messenia with a garrison and remains active in the Peloponnesus. Seeking to challenge Cassander again, he supports Hercules, gathers a large army with Aetolian help, and attempts to restore Hercules to Alexander’s kingdom. Cassander persuades him to murder Hercules secretly. Polyperchon then openly joins Cassander, receives troops, withdraws into Locris after opposition, and with Cassander harasses Greece during Demetrius’s absence.

Polyperchon was protector of the kings and prepared for a major war with Cassander. He secured a decree restoring Greek liberties, directed cities to punish supporters of Antipater, invited Olympias back to Macedonia, and offered Eumenes support against Antigonus.

Polyperchon sent Eumenes letters confirming the kings' financial support and appointment of him as general over Asia. Eumenes intended to obtain shipping so that Polyperchon could later be transported from Macedonia into Asia against Antigonus.
