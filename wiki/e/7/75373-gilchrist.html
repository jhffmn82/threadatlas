Gilchrist in The Return of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1903-09-26

Gilchrist is a student, fine scholar, and athlete who plays rugby and cricket, excels at hurdling, the long jump, and long-distance jumping, and is tall, lithe, and agile. Poor after his father Sir Jabez Gilchrist’s ruin, he works hard and occupies the lower student room. After seeing examination proofs through Soames’s window, he enters, copies them sheet by sheet, and hides in the bedroom. He confesses, refuses to compete in the scholarship examination, accepts a Rhodesian Police commission, and leaves for South Africa.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE ADVENTURE OF THE THREE STUDENTS

Gilchrist occupies the lower student room. He is described as a fine scholar and athlete who plays rugby and cricket, has earned his Blue for hurdling and the long jump, and works hard despite being poor after his father’s ruin. He welcomes Holmes and the others into his room.

Holmes made no further allusion to the matter that day, though he

Gilchrist confesses that he saw the examination proofs through Soames’s window, entered the room, and began copying them before hiding in the bedroom. He says he will not sit the examination and plans to leave for South Africa after accepting a commission in the Rhodesian Police.
