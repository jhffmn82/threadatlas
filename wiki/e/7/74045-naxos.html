Naxos

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Naxos was besieged by Chabrias and the Athenian fleet, and its assisting force was defeated in a naval battle. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Naxus was an island formerly called Strongyle, first settled by Thracians and later possessed by Carians, with King Naxus renaming it after himself. In The Library, Naxos was the place Theseus reached at night with Ariadne and the rescued children, from which Dionysus carried Ariadne away. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Naxos was the most famous island of the Cyclades and sacred to Bacchus, near which shipwrecked mariners were transformed. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Naxos, also called Dia, was the island where Theseus abandoned Ariadne and Bacchus found and aided her. In The Library, Naxos was the destination to which Dionysus wished to be transported, although the Tyrrhenian pirates carried him past it while attempting to sell him in Asia. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Naxos was Bacchus’s home and favorite island, where Ariadne was abandoned, found, and married by Bacchus.

Naxos is Bacchus’s home and favorite island. The Tyrrhenian mariners promise to take him there but instead sail in the opposite direction, while Acetes later obeys Bacchus’s command to steer toward it. Ariadne is abandoned on Naxos, where Bacchus finds and marries her.
