Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAP. n.

Peucestes's feast was arranged in four concentric rounds for the army and its leaders, with the largest extending ten furlongs in circumference. The circles contained mercenaries and allies, Macedonian silver targeteers, officers and horsemen, and finally the generals, Persian nobility, and altars dedicated to the gods, Alexander, and Philip. The feast won Peucestes the favor of the soldiers but caused Eumenes to suspect that he was seeking supreme command.
