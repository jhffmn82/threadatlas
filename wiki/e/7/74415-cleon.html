Cleon

Abstract written by a model

Cleon, a Cilician and Sicilian near Mount Taurus, had practiced robbery since boyhood. Inspired by Eunus's success, he led neighboring slaves in revolt, plundered Agrigentum, joined Eunus as general of five thousand, fought Romans, and was killed in a sortie from Enna; his body was exposed.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XXXIV.

Cleon, a Cilician, led another slave uprising but joined forces with Eunus and served as his general with five thousand soldiers. He fought against the Romans and was killed while making a sortie from Enna; Rupilius then exposed his body and soon captured the city.

BOOK XXXIV.

Cleon was a Sicilian near Mount Taurus who had practiced robbery from boyhood while tending horses. After hearing of Eunus's success, he persuaded neighboring slaves to join him, revolted, and plundered Agrigentum and the surrounding country.
