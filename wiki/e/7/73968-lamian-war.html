Lamian War in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Lamian War was a war between the Athenians and Antipater, begun after Alexander’s death amid the gathering of discharged mercenaries under Leosthenes. Greek forces initially defeated the Macedonians and besieged Lamia, but Antipater and Craterus later defeated them, broke their confederacy, forced separate negotiations, and ended the war with Athenian submission.

CHAP. XI.

The Lamian War broke out after Alexander ordered the provincial governors to disband all mercenary soldiers. Discharged foreigners gathered at Tenarus, where they joined with Persian forces and made Leosthenes their general.

The Lamian War was the war begun by the Athenians against Antipater after Alexander’s death. The Greek forces initially defeated the Macedonians, but the war continued through the defense and siege of Lamia.

CHAP. II.

The Lamian War ended after Antipater and Craterus defeated the Greeks, broke their confederacy, and compelled the cities to negotiate separately. Athens and the Aetolians remained the principal opponents, but Athens eventually surrendered.

Lamian War in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

After Alexander’s death, the Athenians and several allied Greek cities took up arms against Macedonian control. Leosthenes led the coalition and initially achieved notable successes, but his death caused widespread discouragement and contributed to the failure of the effort.
