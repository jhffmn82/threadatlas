In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Pelopidas was a general and Boeotarch who commanded campaigns, helped free Thebes, extended Theban power, secured Philip as a hostage, and died of wounds. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Pelopidas was imprisoned by Alexander while visiting him as a personal friend and friend of the Theban people, and was released after Epaminondas took command of the Theban force.

Pelopidas in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Pelopidas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pelopidas was a general and Boeotarch, closely associated with Ismenias and often sharing command with Epaminondas. Appointed by the Lacedaemonians against the Olynthians, he bound his soldiers by oath, won victories, confined the Olynthians within their walls, and compelled their submission. He later returned with exiles to free Thebes and recover Cadmea, remained Boeotarch until his death, commanded the sacred band at Leuctra, and helped extend Theban power through campaigns in the Peloponnesus and Thessaly. In Thessaly he took Larissa, negotiated with Alexander of Pherae, and secured Philip as hostage, but Alexander imprisoned him and Ismenias. Freed with Epaminondas's help, he again aided the Thessalians, defeated Alexander's forces, and died of wounds. He was also credited with helping obtain Messenia for Thebes.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAR III.

The Lacedaemonians appointed Pelopidas general and sent him against the Olynthians. He swore his soldiers to be faithful to him, won many victories, drove the Olynthians within their walls, and forced them to submit to Lacedaemonian rule.

CHAP. vn.

He shared command with Epaminondas during the Boeotian invasion of the Peloponnesus. Later, he led a Boeotian army into Thessaly, took the castle at Larissa, made an agreement with Alexander, and secured Philip as a hostage.

CHAP. VIIL

Pelopidas went to Thessaly with Ismenias to enlarge Theban power beyond the Peloponnesus. Alexander, tyrant of Pherae, unexpectedly seized and imprisoned him, but Epaminondas later helped free him.

CHAP. IX,

Pelopidas led seven thousand men to assist the Thessalians against Alexander of Pherae. He won a victory after personally charging Alexander's forces but died from numerous wounds; the text praises his services in freeing Thebes, defeating the Lacedaemonians, helping rebuild Messenia, and extending Theban power.

BOOK XIV.

Pelopidas was sent against the Olynthians and later led forces in the Peloponnesus. Alexander of Pherae imprisoned him and Ismenias, but Pelopidas was freed and later killed in the war against Alexander; the text praises him.
