Also called: consuls, consuls at Rome, the Roman cpusuls, the consuls
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Roman consuls are chosen yearly, with one from the senators and one from the common people, though both may be common people; under the settlement, Marcus Horatius and Lucius Valerius Potitus later served and completed the laws.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, the Roman consuls entered the country of the Samnites with an army, defeated them at Talius, and later benefited from renewed fighting in which many Samnites were killed and more than 2,200 were captured.

Roman consuls in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The consuls are chosen yearly: one from senators and one from common people, though both may be common people. Under the settlement, Marcus Horatius and Lucius Valerius Potitus later served and completed the laws.

The consuls were required by the settlement to include one person chosen from the senators and one from the common people, with both permitted to come from the common people if desired. Marcus Horatius and Lucius Valerius Potitus later served as consuls and completed the laws.
