Hesperides

mythological entity · 4 works · 7 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: daughters of Hesperis, daughters three, nymphs, Ἑσπερίδων
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Hesperides are daughters of Atlas, also called Atlantides after their father, and are associated with a garden or country called Hesperis; some mythologists said they possessed golden apples guarded by a dragon, while other accounts interpreted the apples as sheep or golden-colored fleeces.
  • In The Argonautica, the Hesperides are nymphs who lament Ladon after Heracles strikes him; when heroes approach, they turn to dust and earth, with Aegle, Hespere, and Eretheis becoming trees.
  • In The Library, the Hesperides are named Aegle, Erytheia, and Arethusa; after eight labours, they guarded the golden apples with an immortal dragon.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Hesperides are the daughters of Hesperis entrusted with guarding Juno's golden apples, assisted by a watchful dragon; Atlas is their father, and Milton described them as the daughters of Hesperus.
