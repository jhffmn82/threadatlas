Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Epirus is entered by Atarchias to block Aeacidas, then banishes Aeacidas and allies with Cassander after the Epirotes revolt. In The Aeneid, Epirus’s rocky coast is sailed along by Aeneas’s fleet after it leaves Leucate, before the fleet turns toward Chaonia and Buthrotus. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Epirus lies by the Ambracian Gulf, with the Molossi on its eastern side, and is compared figuratively to the Infernal Regions as a proposed kingdom of Aidoneus. In Description of Greece, Epirus is Pyrrhus’s homeland, ruled by his descendants amid succession struggles, ravaged by Lysimachus, and brought to the end of its glory by Pyrrhus’s death.

Epirus is proposed by Le Clerc as the kingdom of Aidoneus, who may have been the historical figure behind Pluto. Because its entrance was reached over the river Acheron and the region lay low, it could have been described figuratively as the Infernal Regions.

Epirus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Epirus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Epirus, Pyrrhus’s homeland, was ruled by his descendants amid succession struggles involving Æacides, Alcetas, and Pyrrhus; Lysimachus ravaged it, and Pyrrhus’s death, after he entered the Peloponnese and did not return, ended its glory.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VII.

Epirus was ruled by the descendants of Pyrrhus and was involved in the succession struggles of Æacides, Alcetas, and Pyrrhus of Epirus. Lysimachus ravaged Epirus and was accused, though the account doubts it, of disturbing the tombs of its kings.

CHAPTER XIII.

Epirus was Pyrrhus’s homeland and the place to which he did not return after entering the Peloponnese. The death of Pyrrhus is presented as the end of the glory of Epirus.
