Dr. Leslie Armstrong

person · 1 work · 22 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Armstrong, Dr. Armstrong, Leslie Armstrong, our friend, Armstrong, the doctor, the grim doctor, the worthy doctor

Abstract written by a model

Dr. Leslie Armstrong is a formidable, grim, ascetic, self-contained doctor: a medical-school head, lecturer, consultant, and European-reputation thinker. He avoids general practice, writes a treatise rather than converse with Holmes, owns a house and a carriage, and makes long daily journeys while guarding against followers. Though he dislikes Holmes’s profession, he knows and loves his intimate friend Godfrey Staunton and helps conceal Staunton’s secret marriage to protect his inheritance. Initially Armstrong denies knowledge of Staunton’s disappearance, angrily expels Holmes and Watson, detects their surveillance, and attempts to mislead them with a circuitous route. He ultimately returns to the cottage, explains the marriage and the wife’s illness, and accepts Holmes’s promise of confidentiality.

Dr. Leslie Armstrong is in one work on this site, The Return of Sherlock Holmes. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

It was already dark when we reached the old university city.

Dr. Leslie Armstrong is a prominent medical lecturer, consultant, and thinker who strongly disapproves of Sherlock Holmes's profession. He denies knowing anything about Godfrey Staunton's disappearance, becomes furious when confronted with a receipted bill and an urgent telegram, and orders Holmes and Watson from his house. He makes long daily carriage journeys and detects Holmes following him.

Dr. Armstrong warns Holmes that following him is futile and tries to mislead him by taking a circuitous route through the countryside. He returns to the cottage, explains Staunton’s secret marriage and his wife’s illness, and is reassured by Holmes that the matter will be kept confidential.
