Scipio in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Scipio is a Roman commander and tribune, later surnamed Africanus, renowned for clemency, compassion, faith, and integrity. While very young in Spain, he achieved unexpected successes against the Carthaginians, delivered his country from danger, and compelled Hannibal to leave Italy without fighting. He later commanded the Romans in an African campaign, defeated Hannibal in a great battle, and brought Carthage under Roman rule. He treated Syphax humanely, refused retaliation against Carthaginian ambassadors, acted against Pleminius after sacrilege, honored promises to besieged enemies, and sought burial for Romans killed in a skirmish. His humane conduct made enemies trust Roman agreements when he signed them.

BOOK XXVI.

Scipio treated Syphax humanely, freeing him from chains, giving him his tent, and allowing him considerable liberty. He refused to retaliate against Carthaginian ambassadors for the attempted murder of Roman ambassadors. He also imprisoned Pleminius after the sacrilege at the temple of Proserpina and was ordered to return to Rome if he had directed or contrived the offense.

37. P. Scipio, when he was but very young, performed great and

While still very young, P. Scipio performed great things in Spain beyond general expectation. He defeated the Carthaginians, delivered his country from imminent dangers, compelled Hannibal to leave Italy without fighting, and later overcame him in battle, bringing Carthage under Roman rule.

77* After the Romans had conquered Perseus, (orratherCbaropea),

Publius Scipio, later surnamed Africanus, kept his promises to besieged enemies when other Romans disregarded their sworn agreements and treated those who fell into his hands with humanity and clemency. His reputation for faith and integrity became so great in Africa that people surrendered to the Romans only when he joined in signing the articles. He also requested that Asdrubal bury three Romans killed in a skirmish, and Asdrubal complied.

Scipio Africanus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Scipio Africanus is a Roman leader described as the most eminent of Romans before him. He conquered Hannibal and the Carthaginians, became adoptive grandfather of Publius Scipio, and later traveled as an ambassador through Alexandria, Egypt, Cyprus, Syria, and other regions, helping reconcile and judge disputes.

Scipio Africanus, who conquered Hannibal and the Carthaginians, was the adoptive grandfather of Publius Scipio. He is described as the most eminent of all the Romans who had lived before him.

Scipio Africanus travels with other Roman ambassadors to Alexandria and through Egypt, Cyprus, Syria, and other regions. The delegation's conduct earns honor, and it reconciles disputants, urges wrongdoers to act justly, restrains the obstinate, and refers difficult cases to the senate.
