Dion in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Dion is a Syracusan soldier and philosopher, brother of Hipparinus and Megacles and brother-in-law of Dionysius the younger. Suspected by Dionysius because of his noble birth and spirit, he fled Sicily to Peloponnesus and Corinth, then sought help to recover Syracuse's liberty. Sailing from Zacynthus to Minoa with two ships and a thousand mercenaries, he gained Sicilian support, armed Syracusans, and was chosen general with Megacles. He entered Syracuse, fought at the wall, was wounded in the right arm, and helped restore the ancient liberties of the Syracusans and Sicilians. After conflict with the Syracusans, he led foreign soldiers to the Leontines, later rescued Syracuse, and received absolute governorship by unanimous vote. He pardoned his opponents but was ultimately killed by mercenaries of Zacynthus.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. 11.

Dion, brother of Hipparinus and brother-in-law of Dionysius the younger, was skilled in philosophy and regarded as the most expert soldier among the Syracusans. Fearing that Dionysius intended to kill him, he fled Sicily to Peloponnesus and Corinth, gathered forces, and returned to seek the liberty of Syracuse.

CHAP. III.

Dion sailed from Zacynthus to Minoa in Sicily with two ships and a thousand mercenaries, then gathered support from several Sicilian communities and amassed an army. He distributed arms to the Syracusans, was chosen general with Megacles, entered Syracuse, and was credited with restoring liberty. During the battle at the wall, he broke the mercenaries, was wounded in the right arm, and was rescued by the Syracusans before defeating the enemy.

CHAP. IV.

Dion joined Heraclides in managing the war against Dionysius. After the Syracusans rejected his advice and turned against him, he led three thousand foreign soldiers to the Leontines, defeated the Syracusans, and later returned to rescue Syracuse. The people then made him chief governor with full and absolute power, and he pardoned those who had injured him.

BOOK XIV.

Dion fled to Corinth and later returned to Sicily. He entered Syracuse, defeated Dionysius's forces, and eventually left the Syracusans amid their troubled condition.
