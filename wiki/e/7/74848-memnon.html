Memnon in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Memnon was a Rhodian and Persian commander praised for valour, courage, and discipline, serving Darius against Macedonia. He marched with five thousand men toward Cyzicus, failed to take it, ravaged its territory, and helped force the Macedonians to abandon Pitane’s siege. He urged Persian commanders to devastate the country before Alexander and move the war to Macedonia, but they refused. As defender of Halicarnassus, he directed sallies against siege engines and withdrew with citizens and wealth. Made general of all Darius’s forces, he raised troops, equipped three hundred ships, won places and alliances in Chios, Lesbos, and the Cyclade islands, then died before finishing his plans. Later records place him in revolt in Thrace with a great army.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XVII.

Memnon of Rhodes was chosen as one of Darius's leading commanders and was praised for courage and discipline. With five thousand men he marched toward Cyzicus, attacked it unsuccessfully, ravaged the surrounding country, and later caused the Macedonians to abandon their siege of Pitane.

CHAP. It.

Memnon the Rhodian advised the Persian commanders to destroy the country before Alexander and transfer the war to Macedonia, but they rejected his plan. He later commanded the defense of Halicarnassus, directed sallies against Alexander’s siege engines, and eventually left the city with the citizens and much of their wealth.

CHAP. III.

Darius sent Memnon money and appointed him general of all his forces. Memnon raised troops, equipped three hundred ships, took several places in Chios and Lesbos, and gained alliances among the Cyclade islands, but he died of a mortal illness before completing his plans.

CHAP. VI.

Sent as general into Thrace, Memnon rebelled at the instigation of the barbarians and appeared in open war with a great army.
