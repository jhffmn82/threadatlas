Athenian navy in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Athenian navy first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Athenian navy was a great Athenian fleet, described as containing two hundred galleys and as the foundation of greater Athenian power. After many sea victories, Themistocles urged annual expansion. A large force sent to Egypt was trapped on Prosopitis and burned. Later, under Cimon, it defeated the Persian fleet near Cyprus and controlled its sea; under Pericles and Phormio, its hundreds of ships raided, attacked, and won engagements.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

I.

The Athenian fleet had already won many victories at sea and was regarded by Themistocles as the foundation of greater Athenian power. He advised the Athenians to add twenty galleys to it every year.

CHAP. XIX.

The Athenians prepared a fleet of three hundred ships for Egypt, though two hundred ships are stated to have arrived there. The fleet was later trapped on Prosopitis after the Persians diverted the river and was burned by the Athenians.

DIODORUS SICULUS. / BOOK XIL

The Athenians equipped a navy of two hundred galleys for the campaign against Cyprus. Under Cimon, it defeated the Persian fleet, sank many vessels, captured one hundred ships, and controlled the sea around Cyprus.

CHAP. Vlf. /

The Athenian navy included hundreds of ships and was presented by Pericles as a major advantage over the Lacedaemonians. Athenian fleets raided Peloponnesus, attacked coastal towns and castles, and won important engagements under Phormio.

Abstract written by a model

The Athenian fleet, more skilled in naval affairs and containing most Athenian men, gathered at Samos, trained, pursued and defeated the Lacedaemonian fleet, captured ships, and operated at Cyzicus and Samos.

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAP. 111.

The Athenian fleet gathered at Samos under Thrasyllus and Thrasybulus, where its soldiers practiced naval warfare. It pursued the Lacedaemonian fleet to the Hellespont, won the battle between Sestos and Abydos, captured ships, and later operated at Cyzicus and Samos.
