Potidea

Also called: Fotidea, Poiidea, Potideni, Potldeans, colony at I'otidea, them of Potidea
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Potidea, a Corinthian colony, was the first city to defect from Athens after Corinthian and Perdiccas’s urging; Athens defeated forces near Pallene and besieged it, and after its surrender its inhabitants settled among the Chalcidicans in Thrace while Athens colonized the city and surrounding country.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Philip promised to give Potidea to the Olynthians, took the city, removed the Athenian garrison, and allowed its members to return to Athens.

Potidea, a Corinthian colony, was the first city to defect from Athens after Corinthian and Perdiccas’s urging. Athens defeated forces near Pallene and besieged it. After surrendering, its inhabitants settled among the Chalcidicans in Thrace, and Athens colonized the city and surrounding country.

CHAP. VI.

Potidea revolted from Athens after the Corinthians stirred up the city, while Perdiccas persuaded other cities of Chalcidica to revolt. Athens sent forces against Potidea and laid siege to it after defeating the opposing forces near Pallene.

CHAP. Vlf. /

Potidea was the first city to make a defection from the Athenians and was besieged by an Athenian army. After its surrender, its inhabitants left with severely limited possessions and settled among the Chalcidicans in Thrace, while Athens sent a colony to occupy the city and surrounding country.
