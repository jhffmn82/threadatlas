the monkeys in The Magic of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1919

The monkeys are forest-dwelling people and animals led by Rango the Gray Ape as chief of the forest monkey tribes. After fleeing giant soldiers, they return, recognize the giants as transformed friends and relatives, and gather. Twelve intelligent, good-tempered monkeys volunteer to accompany the Wizard of Oz to the Emerald City for Ozma's birthday. Temporarily three inches high and confined in a golden cage, they learn and perform the Wizard's tricks.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

16. The Glass Cat Finds the Black Bag

The unenchanted monkeys first flee from the giant soldiers, then return and discover that the giants are their transformed friends and relatives. They gather in large numbers, and twelve intelligent, good-tempered monkeys volunteer to go with the Wizard to the Emerald City for Ozma's birthday.

21. The College of Athletic Arts

The twelve tiny monkeys were kept in a cage in the Wizard of Oz's tower room. They were intelligent, learned every trick the Wizard taught them, and promised to do their best for Ozma's birthday.
