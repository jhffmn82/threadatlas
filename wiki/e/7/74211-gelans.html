Gelans

group · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Gelians, people of Gela, the Gelans, the Gelians, they of Gela

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, the Gelans were accused of planning to revolt, had about four thousand of their number killed, suffered confiscation of their estates, and saw the survivors expelled from Gela and settled at Phintia under threat of death unless they surrendered their money. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Gelians dedicated Apollo’s brazen statue, joined Dionysius as an allied Sicilian city, fiercely defended Gela against siege, briefly aided the Italians, and, angered by Dionysius, departed for Leontium.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAP. XVI.

The Gelians resisted the siege by defending their walls, repairing breaches at night, and attacking Carthaginian stragglers. They briefly sallied to aid the Italians during the battle before Gela but returned to protect their walls. Afterward, they became incensed against Dionysius and turned off to Leontium.
