Phalaecus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Phalaecus, son of Onomarchus, became general of the Phocians after Phayllus died, with Mnaseas as tutor and governor. He commanded mercenaries, was defeated in cavalry at Chaeronea, took the city, and abandoned it when the Thebans relieved it. Removed for converting sacred treasure, he was later restored. At the Sacred War’s end he made peace with Philip and withdrew with about eight thousand men. After robbing the temple, he wandered, took Lyctus, was defeated by Archidamus, and died besieging Sidon, by heavenly fire or a soldier.

CHAP. Vlll.

Phalaecus, the son of Onomarchus, became general of the Phocians after Phayllus died, with Mnaseas appointed as his tutor and governor. He was defeated in a cavalry engagement at Chaeronea and later took Chaeronea, but abandoned it when the Thebans came to relieve the city.

CHAP. X.

Phalaecus was removed from command after being convicted of converting sacred treasure to his own use, though he was later restored. At the end of the Sacred War he commanded mercenaries at Nicaea, negotiated peace with Philip, and withdrew to the Peloponnese with about eight thousand men.
