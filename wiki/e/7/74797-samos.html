Samos in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Samos, an island, is governed by Athenians, restored to Samians by Perdiccas, then attacked and besieged until confederates depart to relieve Byzantium.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAP. V.

Samos was attacked by the Chians, Rhodians, and Byzantines, who harassed its country and besieged its city by sea and land. The confederates later raised their siege to prepare to relieve Byzantium.

Samos in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Samos is an island whose history centers on shifting alliances, conflict, and democracy. It sent ambassadors seeking aid for Asia’s inhabitants; Greek commanders returned there after battle and accepted Ionians and Eolians as confederates. Pericles first reduced Samos and established democracy. A later opposing faction, aided by Persians, seized it, but Pericles besieged and captured it, demolished its walls, removed its ships, and restored democracy. Later, forces assembled there and received Samian ships before sailing to Arginusae.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. IV.

Samos sent ambassadors to the Greek commanders asking them to assist the inhabitants of Asia. The Persian fleet withdrew from Samos to Mycale, and after the battle the Greek commanders returned to Samos, where they received the Ionians and Eolians as confederates.

CHAP. V.

Samos was first reduced by Pericles and placed under a democracy. During a later sedition, the opposing faction seized the city with Persian assistance, but Pericles eventually captured it after a siege, demolished its walls, removed its ships, and restored its democracy.

CHAP. XIV.

The Athenians sailed to Samos, where they joined other commanders who had assembled eighty additional galleys from other islands. They obtained ten more ships from the Samians before sailing to Arginusae.

Samos in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

An island near Mycale, Samos was inhabited by reluctantly received Ionian colonists; expelled Samians settled elsewhere, then recovered it from Ephesians, with Leogorus recovering it ten years later.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IV.

Samos was inhabited by Ionian colonists who were received by its inhabitants more from necessity than goodwill. After the Samians were expelled, some settled at Samothrace and others at Anæa, and the Samians later recovered the island from the Ephesians.
