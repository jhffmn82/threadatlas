Ephesus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Ephesus is a military base in Asia. It repeatedly serves as a gathering, command, departure, and retreat point: Lysander assembles and sails a fleet, Thimbron and Dercyllidas station forces, and Agesilaus gathers troops, begins his campaign, and returns with his army.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. IX.

Ephesus received Lysander, who assembled a fleet there and obtained further ships and supplies from neighboring cities. Lysander later sailed from Ephesus with seventy ships.

CHAP. VI.

Ephesus served as Thimbron's base after he crossed from Corinth and later as his place of retreat when Tissaphernes approached. Dercyllidas also commanded an army there when the Persian forces advanced.

CHAP. vm.

Ephesus was the place where Agesilaus assembled additional troops and began his Asian campaign. Agesilaus returned there with his army after campaigning.

Ephesus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Ephesus is a place associated with Ephesus, thought to be son of the river Cayster. Before the Ionian settlement, Leleges and Lydians inhabited it; Androclus expelled those in the upper city while temple-district inhabitants stayed peaceful. Later it received Colophon and Lebedus colonists, who resisted Lysimachus and Macedonians.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

Ephesus was associated with Ephesus, thought to be the son of the river Cayster, and was inhabited by Leleges and most of the Lydians before the Ionian settlement. Androclus drove the Leleges and Lydians from the upper city, while those living near the temple remained on peaceful terms with the Ionians.

CHAPTER III.

Ephesus received inhabitants from Colophon and Lebedus after Lysimachus dispeopled those cities. The colonists from Colophon were the only colonists there who fought against Lysimachus and the Macedonians.
