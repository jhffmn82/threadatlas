Agathocles is a potter by early trade who rises to be a military officer, commander, prince, tyrant, and king ruling Syracuse and Sicily. Born at Thermae after his father exposed him because of an oracle, he becomes known for strength, courage, military skill, stratagems, valour, and policy. He gains power at Syracuse through an expedition, troop raising, massacre, and a claim to defend democracy, then rules with absolute authority while seeking popular support. His rule unfolds through expansion, war, and extreme violence. He breaks promises, seizes cities, suppresses dissent, executes opponents, confiscates property, builds revenues, garrisons cities, strengthens the fleet, and employs mercenaries. He uses stratagems involving owls and imitation shields, sends news and spoils to Syracuse, and kills Ophelas despite friendship and hospitality to take his army. Setbacks follow: deserters abandon him in Africa, his own men put him in chains, and he escapes back to Sicily. He became a soldier and commander noted for strength, courage, military skill, and stratagems, then used an expedition to raise troops and seized power at Syracuse. Agathocles receives Messana and later executes about 600 opponents from Taurominium and Messana. He suppresses suspected dissent at Centuripians, defeats forces associated with the Syracusan exiles, attacks Carthaginian holdings, and returns to Syracuse with spoils.

CHAP. VH.

Agathocles expanded his power in Sicily, murdered about four thousand Gelans, and confiscated their property. He was defeated by Amilcar at the river Himera, withdrew to Syracuse, and prepared to transfer the war to Africa.

Agathocles left Archagathus at Tunis with part of the army and pursued the Carthaginians with 8,000 infantry, 800 cavalry, and 50 African carriages. He defeated the Carthaginians after forcing a passage across a difficult river, but the Numidians raided his camp while he was attacking the enemy camp. He later invited Ophelas to assist him, killed Ophelas after accusing him of treachery, absorbed the surviving army, and sent spoils and some Cyrenians toward Syracuse.

He escaped from Syracuse with seventeen warships, unexpectedly defeated the Carthaginians at sea, defeated the Agrigentines under Leptines, and later had suspected opponents massacred.

Agathocles attacked the Carthaginians in Africa but was forced to retreat after losing about three thousand men. After the African deserters abandoned him, he planned to escape secretly with Heraclides, was captured and bound by his soldiers, and later escaped by ship. In Sicily he extorted wealth from Aegesta, ordered severe tortures and killings there, and commanded the slaughter of the families and associates of those who had gone over to the Carthaginians.

Agathocles fortified and taxed the Sicilian cities under his command because he feared that they would regain their liberty. After suffering military and political setbacks, he offered to surrender his sovereignty and later made peace with the Carthaginians.

Agathocles marched against Dinocrates and the exiles with 5,000 foot and 800 horse, despite being greatly outnumbered. After some of Dinocrates's men deserted, Agathocles defeated the exiles, offered them freedom to return home, and then disarmed and killed those who accepted his guarantee. He later received the remaining exiles and made Dinocrates a general.

Agathocles suddenly invaded the peaceful Lipari islands and exacted fifty talents of silver. He forced the Liparians to surrender sacred treasury money, but a storm destroyed eleven ships carrying it; the text later says that Vulcan punished him by burning him alive with hot coals.

FROM THE SAME..

Agathocles massacred nearly two thousand Ligurians and Tyrrhenians after they mutinously attacked his son Archagathus over their pay. Timaeus accused him of many invented crimes and minimized his successes, although the text says Agathocles was a prudent and expert general who rose from humble circumstances to rule Sicily and conquer parts of Italy and Africa. Callias, having been enriched and advanced by Agathocles, praised him excessively despite his violations of divine and human laws.

BOOK XX.

Agathocles invades Africa, burns his ships, defeats Hanno and Bomilcar, and carries on campaigns in Africa and Sicily. He decoys and kills Ophelas, defeats Dinocrates's army, massacres seven thousand who submit on terms, and displays further cruelty at Aegesta and Syracuse. His army suffers reverses in Africa; his own men put him in chains, he escapes to Africa, and his two sons are killed before peace is made with the Carthaginians.
