Hicetas in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Hicetas, a Syracusan ruler, gained Syracuse during struggles involving Dionysius and Dion, defeating Dionysius and holding all but the Island. He ruled as prince for nine years, defeated Phintias at Hibleum, and was allied with the Carthaginians, though they later defeated him at the river Terias. He joined the Leontines, marched from Leontium to besiege Syracuse, lost much of his army, and returned. Initially at war with Timoleon, he later joined him, but Timoleon defeated and subdued him, took Syracuse, and ordered Hicetas an honourable burial. Thynion also deprived him of Syracuse.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. XI.

Hicetas led an army against Dionysius at Syracuse and defeated him after Dionysius pursued his retreating forces. He entered Syracuse and gained the whole city except the Island, but Timoleon later defeated his forces near Adranum and took Syracuse from him.

CHAP. XII.

Hicetas joined the Leontines with a great army. He later marched from Leontium against Syracuse, lost a large part of his army there, and hastened back to Leontium.

CHAP, XIII.

Hicetas was initially at war with Timoleon but later joined him, increasing Timoleon's forces. Timoleon eventually subdued him and ordered him an honourable burial.

BOOK XXII.

Hicetas ruled Syracuse for nine years and defeated Phintias at Hibleum. He later suffered defeat by the Carthaginians at the river Terias and was deprived of Syracuse by Thynion.

BOOK XIV.

Hicetas gained control of Syracuse during the struggles involving Dionysius and Dion. He was defeated by Timoleon, who then gained Syracuse.
