Eunus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Eunus is a Syrian slave from Apamea, servant of Antigenes of Enna, who presents himself as inspired by goddess Syria and as a magician and prophet, even using fire-breathing tricks. He leads the initial slave revolt, is declared king as Antiochus, and rules Enna with a large rebel army called Syrians. He kills many but spares former admirers and benefactors. Defeated by Rupilius, he flees, is captured, and dies imprisoned at Morgantium.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XXXIV.

Eunus, a Syrian slave of Antigenes of Enna, claimed prophetic powers and used tricks to breathe fire from his mouth. He led the initial slave revolt, was made king under the name Antiochus, and ruled with an army that grew to many thousands. After Rupilius defeated the rebellion, Eunus fled into caves, was captured with four attendants, and died in prison at Morgantium, eaten by lice.

BOOK XXXIV.

Eunus was declared king during the Sicilian slave revolt. He put many people to death but spared those who had previously praised his prophecies at feasts or had shown him kindness by sharing food with him.
