Hiero in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Hiero, king of Syracuse, expanded his power by capturing Myle and Amesalus, razing Amesalus, taking Alese, and gaining Abaceni and Tyndaridae. He defeated the Mamertines at the river Lotanus, but Hannibal’s intervention led him to abandon a siege of Messana and return to Syracuse. He ruled Syracuse and several cities under his jurisdiction. He joined the Carthaginians against the Romans in besieging Messana, then made a fourteen-year peace with Rome while retaining his cities. He supplied Romans with corn, siege engines, and other war instruments, and aided shipwreck survivors near Camarina.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XXII.

Hiero captured Myle and Amesalus, destroyed Amesalus, and then took Alese while gaining the submission of Abaceni and Tyndaridae. He defeated the Mamertines near the river Lotanus, but Hannibal's intervention discouraged him from besieging Messana, so he returned to Syracuse. He later entered a confederacy with the Carthaginians to besiege Messana.

BOOK XXIIL

Hiero allied with the Carthaginians against the Romans and joined in the siege of Messana. He argued that the Mamertines deserved to be besieged because of their violence and seizure of Messana, but he later made a fourteen-year peace with Rome, retaining Syracuse and the cities under his jurisdiction. He also supplied Roman forces with siege engines and other instruments of war and courteously aided Roman survivors of the shipwreck near Camarina.

Hiero the son of Hierocles in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Hiero, son of Hierocles, was tyrant of Syracuse, rising after Agathocles’s death in the second year of the 126th Olympiad. He initially aided the Carthaginians in Sicily, then joined the Romans, befriended Pyrrhus, and was assassinated by Dinomenes; his sons dedicated Olympia statues.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XII.

He was also tyrant of Syracuse and rose to power after the death of Agathocles in the second year of the 126th Olympiad. He first supported the Carthaginians in the war for Sicily but soon joined the Romans, believing their power stronger and more lasting. He was assassinated by Dinomenes, and his sons dedicated statues of him at Olympia.
