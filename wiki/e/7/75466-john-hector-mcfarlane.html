John Hector McFarlane in The Return of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1903-09-26

John Hector McFarlane is a young London solicitor, bachelor, Freemason, and asthmatic who lives with his parents at Torrington Lodge and is junior partner in Graham and McFarlane. Jonas Oldacre unexpectedly makes him beneficiary of a will and takes him to Norwood to examine documents, later showing him out through a French window. After staying at a Norwood hotel, McFarlane learns on his morning train that he is accused of Oldacre's murder and seeks Sherlock Holmes in fear of arrest. Police cite his bloody walking-stick, footprints, and an apparent right-thumb bloodprint; Lestrade arrests him. Holmes establishes that Oldacre is alive and that the evidence was manufactured: McFarlane had unknowingly left a thumb impression in soft wax, which Oldacre stained with blood to frame him.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

THE ADVENTURE OF THE NORWOOD BUILDER

John Hector McFarlane is a young London solicitor and the suspected murderer of Jonas Oldacre. He comes to Mr. Sherlock Holmes in a frantic state, says he fears imminent arrest, and explains that he met Oldacre late at night, stayed at a Norwood hotel, and learned of the accusation on his morning train.

It was a clang of the bell, followed instantly by heavy steps

John Hector McFarlane is arrested by Inspector Lestrade for the wilful murder of Jonas Oldacre. He says that Oldacre unexpectedly made him the beneficiary of a will, took him to Norwood to examine documents, and showed him out through a French window late at night.

When the detective departed, my friend rose and made his

John McFarlane is accused of murdering Jonas Oldacre, with his walking stick, footprints, and apparent right-thumb bloodprint used against him. Holmes's discovery that Oldacre is alive shows that McFarlane's apparent guilt was based on a false interpretation of the evidence.

Holmes smiled, and clapped Lestrade upon the shoulder.

McFarlane unknowingly placed his thumb on a soft wax seal while securing a packet. Oldacre used an impression of that mark, stained with blood, to implicate McFarlane in the alleged murder.
