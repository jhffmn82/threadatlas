Abstract written by a model

Alexander's army is a large, experienced military force of more than thirty thousand infantry and three thousand cavalry, containing Macedonians, Argyraspides, auxiliary Peloponnesians, Achaeans and Pythiots, Thessalians, Cretan archers, Agrians, Persian darters and slingers, the Social Regiment, scouts' children, and camp attendants. It is repeatedly organized into specialized divisions: assault forces, battle forces and reserves at Thebes; a half-moon formation against Persian forces; three brigades under Ptolemy, Leonatus and Alexander; five parts against the Sogdians; and separate columns toward India. Its central course is one of campaigning, victory, hardship, and reorganization. It is superior to the Theban battle in numbers, but suffers a rear attack near Lycia that kills many Macedonians and costs prisoners and carriage-horses. After crossing the Tigris, it defeats a Persian army, using noise, open formations, and javelins struck on locked shields to frighten hooked-chariot horses. It captures towns, takes Cyropolis and a seventh city, crosses a river, defeats Scythians, and later overruns much of the Sogdian country before assembling at Maracanda. The army also endures severe attrition. After eight years of campaigning, continuous marches, losses, damaged weapons, exhausted horses, and storms have worn it down. During a desert crossing, famine and attacks cause heavy suffering; another waterless march leaves many faint or unable to proceed, and excessive drinking kills some after water is reached. A storm scatters soldiers, causes deaths from cold, and sweeps away a thousand soldiers and camp attendants. The force rests, feasts, receives plunder and provisions, is mixed with Persian troops into ready obedience, enters Babylon, and finally advances from Bactria toward India, splitting near the Cophas between a route toward the Indus and Alexander's mountainous route.

Alexander's army included more than thirty thousand infantry and three thousand cavalry, described as experienced soldiers who had conquered in many battles under Philip. It was divided into assault forces, battle forces, and reserves during the attack on Thebes.

Alexander's army was attacked in the rear by the Marmareofies while marching toward Lycia. Many Macedonians were killed, and prisoners and carriage-horses were taken.

Alexander's army crossed the Tigris and encamped near the Persian force before forming for battle with cavalry in front. Its wings were arranged in a half-moon, and its infantry used noise and open formations to counter the hooked chariots. It ultimately defeated the Persian army despite suffering serious pressure on one wing.

Alexander’s army had campaigned for eight years and was worn down by continuous marches, losses, damaged weapons, exhausted horses, and prolonged storms. Alexander attempted to secure its support for the invasion of the Gandarites through speeches, plunder, and gifts of food to the soldiers’ families.

Then sailing with his familiar friends into the main ocean, he

Alexander divided his army into three brigades under Ptolemy, Leonatus, and himself, assigning them to the sea-coast, the inland country, and the mountainous regions. The army suffered heavily from famine and attacks during the desert crossing, then rested in a rich country and celebrated a seven-day feast to Bacchus.

CHAP. XI.

Alexander's army was reorganized by mixing Persian darters and slingers among the other soldiers, bringing it into a condition of ready obedience. It marched from Susa through Media, rested at several places, and later entered Babylon, where the soldiers were entertained by the citizens and enjoyed abundant provisions.

SUP. I.

Alexander's army crossed about four hundred furlongs without finding a lake or anything moist. Many soldiers became faint, some could not carry their arms or continue marching, and those who drank excessively vomited or died. A great part of the army was left behind before the force reached the river Oxus.

SUP. III.

Alexander’s army captured five towns in two days and then took Cyropolis and the seventh city. It also crossed the river, defeated the Scythians, killed about a thousand of them, and took about one hundred and fifty prisoners.

SUP, V,

Alexander's army was divided into five parts during the campaign against the Sogdians. Its forces overran much of the country, joined at Maracanda, and helped reduce the remaining revolted cities and forts.

SUP. X.

The army was terrified and disorganized by the lightning, thunder, and hail. Its soldiers dropped their arms, wandered through the woods in scattered groups, sought shelter and fires, and suffered many deaths from the cold.

SUP. XII.

Alexander's army advanced from Bactria toward India and was divided near the river Cophas. Part of it went with Hephaestion and Perdiccas toward the Indus, while Alexander led the remaining forces through mountainous country.
