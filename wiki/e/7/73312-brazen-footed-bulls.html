the fire-breathing bronze bulls in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · -0299/-0200

The fire-breathing bronze bulls are a pair of powerful beasts with bronze feet, flaming mouths, and deadly, resistless fire. Aeetes possesses them, and they pasture on the plain of Ares. He appoints yoking and driving them as the first ordeal of the contest, threatening that they will tear apart whoever attempts it. Medea fears they will kill Jason, but her charm protects him from their flames. The bulls rush from their hidden steading and attack with fire and horns; Jason forces them to their knees, yokes them, and drives them to plough the field.

brazen-footed bulls in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Brazen-footed bulls with iron-pointed horns and flame-breathing nostrils are enchanted by Medea, then Jason yokes them to plough a field.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK THE SEVENTH.

The brazen-footed bulls have iron-pointed horns and breathe flames from their adamantine nostrils. Protected by Medea’s enchantments, Jason strokes them, yokes them, and makes them plough the field.
