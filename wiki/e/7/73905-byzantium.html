Byzantium in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Byzantium first appears

Abstract written by a model

Byzantium is a city repeatedly drawn into Greek wars and changing control. It joined the Athenian confederacy as an early defector from Lacedaemonian rule, later was asked to help Thebes prepare a fleet and docks, and was brought under Theban obedience by Epaminondas. Athenian generals planned to besiege it, while opposing fleets moved toward the Hellespont. Ruled tyrannically by Clearchus, it later faced Philip’s siege, aided Perinthus, and received support from Athens and other Greeks until Philip withdrew. Naval fighting occurred near it, and Antigonus obtained Byzantine transports for an attack.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. IX,

Byzantium was asked to assist the Thebans in preparing their fleet and docks. Epaminondas later brought Byzantium under the obedience of Thebes.

CHAP. V.

The Athenian generals initially resolved to besiege Byzantium. The Chians and their confederates raised the siege of Samos and prepared to relieve Byzantium, leading the opposing fleets toward the Hellespont.

CHAP. XII.

Byzantium sent men, darts, shot, and other supplies to the defenders of Perinthus, including a commander and its best soldiers. Philip therefore marched against Byzantium and suddenly laid close siege to it.

CHAP, XIII.

Byzantium was besieged by Philip and received aid from Athens, Chios, Coos, Rhodes, and other Greeks. Philip raised the siege after being alarmed by the assembled Greek forces.

Byzantium in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Byzantium first appears

Abstract written by a model

Byzantium, named after Byzas, held a strong Peloponnesian garrison, was besieged and jointly attacked by Theramenes and Alcibiades, and was entered through betrayal by some Byzantines. It was restored to the Byzantines and renewed its alliance with Athenians. Later, amid sedition and war with Thracians, Clearchus ruled tyrannically until Lacedaemonians attacked him.

Byzantium was besieged by Theramenes and later attacked jointly by Theramenes and Alcibiades. Some Byzantines betrayed the city, allowing the Athenians to enter, after which the city was restored to the Byzantines and its league with the Athenians was renewed.

CHAP. III.

Byzantium suffered from internal seditions and wars with neighboring Thracians and requested a governor from the Lacedaemonians. Clearchus took control, murdered magistrates and prominent citizens, confiscated property, and ruled as a tyrant until the Lacedaemonians attacked him.
