Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Perdiccas was a Macedonian commander and protector of the kings who became the kingdom’s effective superintendent, exercised executive power, campaigned widely, and was eventually killed by his own commanders and horsemen after a failed invasion of Egypt. In Description of Greece, Perdiccas opposed Ptolemy while attempting to take Egypt, led Aridæus and the young Alexander, was driven out, lost support, and was assassinated by his Macedonian bodyguard.

Perdiccas in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Perdiccas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Perdiccas is a Macedonian commander and protector of the kings who becomes the kingdom’s effective superintendent after receiving Alexander’s ring under Philip III. He commands Macedonians, Oresteans, Lyncestians, and the king’s army, possesses the king’s forces, and exercises executive power. Early in his rule he submits costly plans to a council, suppresses revolt through executions, including turbulent soldiers and Meleager, and sends Python against revolting Greeks. He pursues Pausanias with Leonatus and Attalus, conquers Ariarathes and Cappadocia with King Philip, executes Ariarathes, gives Cappadocia to Eumenes, restores Samos and its long-exiled people, and campaigns in Pisidia. His rule is marked by cruelty, ambition, and conflict. He marries Nicæa to preserve an alliance with Antipater, then Cleopatra while seeking the kingdom and sole monarchy. He targets Antigonos, suspects Ptolemy’s increasing power, and sends Eumenes with troops to hold the Hellespont against Antipater and Craterus. After Antigonos flees, Perdiccas leads the kings and army against Ptolemy in Egypt. There the Nile frustrates his works and a fort assault; a failed crossing kills more than two thousand soldiers, alienates the army, and prompts commanders and horsemen to kill him after three years of sovereign command. Later records describe him as dead, with Alcetas and Attalus among his remaining friends and kindred capable of opposing Antigonus. One record also describes a Perdiccas who deserts Eumenes and is captured and punished after Eumenes sends Phenices against him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

9 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XVIII.

Perdiccas received Alexander's ring and was invested with the executive power of the kingdom under Philip III. He referred Alexander's costly plans to a general council, ordered the execution of thirty turbulent soldiers, privately caused Meleager to be executed, and sent Python against the revolting Greeks.

CHAP. II.

Perdiccas conquered Ariarathes and Cappadocia, executed Ariarathes, and delivered the province to Eumenes of Cardia. He later restored Samos and returned exiles who had been away for more than forty-three years.

Perdiccas and king Philip having overcome Ariarathes, delivered

Perdiccas and king Philip defeated Ariarathes, delivered Cappadocia to Eumenes, and proceeded into Pisidia. He destroyed Larissa and besieged the city of the Isaurians, then married Nicaea and Cleopatra while planning to obtain the kingdom. After Antigonos fled, Perdiccas marched toward Egypt with his army, sending Eumenes ahead to secure the Hellespont.

CHAP. IIL

Perdiccas suspected Ptolemy's growing power and prepared to lead the kings and his army on an expedition into Egypt against him. He gave Eumenes troops and officers and ordered him to march to the Hellespont to prevent Antipater and Craterus from crossing into Asia.

But after Antipater and Craterus had transported their army out

Perdiccas marched into Egypt after hearing of Eumenes's victory, but the Nile defeated his works and his assault on a fort failed. During an attempted crossing, the river swept away many of his soldiers, more than two thousand perished, and his army became disaffected. His commanders and soldiers turned against him, and a group of horsemen killed him after he had held sovereign command for three years.

Perdiccas, a chief commander of Eumenes, deserted with three thousand foot and five hundred horse. Eumenes sent Phenices against him, captured him and his followers, and put the chief leaders of the defection to death.

CHAP. IV.

Perdiccas is described as dead before the events of this unit. Alcetas and Attalus remained among the last of his friends and kindred capable of opposing Antigonus.

BOOK XXXI.

Perdiccas, who held the chief command after Alexander's death, sent Eumenes into Cappadocia. He is said to have died not long afterward while Antigonus and Seleucus were involved in war.

BOOK XVII.

He executed Meleager, affected the kingdom of Macedonia, and opposed Antigonus. He marched against Ptolemy in Egypt, suffered heavy losses at the Nile, and was killed.
