Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, the battle was described as a decisive Theban and Boeotian victory under Epaminondas that greatly weakened Lacedaemon. In Description of Greece, the battle was described as a Theban victory that reversed Lacedæmonian fortune, aided the restoration of the Messenians, and led to commemorative offerings. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the battle was described as a notable Theban victory over the Lacedæmonians, with heavy Lacedæmonian losses and political consequences in the Peloponnese.

battle of Leuctra in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where battle of Leuctra first appears

Abstract written by a model

The battle of Leuctra was a famous, decisive Theban and Boeotian victory under Epaminondas over the Lacedaemonians and Spartans. Epaminondas led the smaller Theban force in an oblique formation, killing about four thousand Lacedaemonians and about three hundred Boeotians. It destroyed much Lacedaemonian strength and encouraged the Thebans.

CHAP. VL

At the battle of Leuctra, Epaminondas led the smaller Theban force against the Lacedaemonians using an oblique formation. The Thebans won a decisive victory, killing about four thousand Lacedaemonians and about three hundred Boeotians.

CHAP. vn.

The battle of Leuctra had destroyed much of the strength of the Lacedaemonians, especially their young men. This loss left Sparta with few citizen fighting men when the allied army invaded Laconia.

BOOK XIV.

The Battle of Leuctra was a famous victory of Epaminondas and the Thebans over the Spartans.

battle of Leuctra in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where battle of Leuctra first appears

Abstract written by a model

The battle of Leuctra was won by the Thebans, with Aristomenes’ shield displayed as a trophy. Their victory reversed Lacedæmonian fortune, aided the restoration of the Messenians, prompted the recall of exiles, and was later commemorated by returning the shield as a votive offering.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIV.

The Theban victory in the battle of Leuctra marked the reversal of Lacedæmonian fortune and led to the restoration of the Messenians. Afterward, Theban messengers summoned the exiled Messenians from Italy, Sicily, and the Euesperitæ.

CHAPTER XXIX.

At the battle of Leuctra, the Messenians said that Aristomenes fought for the Thebans and helped cause the Lacedæmonian defeat. The Thebans displayed Aristomenes' shield, following an oracle of Trophonius, and later returned it as a votive offering.

battle of Leuctra in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where battle of Leuctra first appears

Abstract written by a model

The battle of Leuctra was a famous and especially notable Greek battle, fought at Leuctra, in which the Thebans defeated the Lacedæmonians. Cleombrotus and many high-ranking Lacedæmonians were killed; Lacedæmonian losses exceeded 1,000, versus 47 Theban and Bœotian dead. Directly afterward, Epaminondas released other Peloponnesians but detained Lacedæmonians, and the Thebans later removed Lacedæmonian decemvirates and Harmosts.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

The battle of Leuctra was a famous victory by the Thebans over the Lacedæmonians and is described as the most famous of the known battles between Greeks. After it, the Thebans put down the decemvirates established by the Lacedæmonians and expelled their Harmosts.

CHAPTER XIII.

The battle of Leuctra was a major victory for the Thebans over the Lacedæmonians and was described as the most notable victory won by Greeks over Greeks. Cleombrotus and many high-ranking Lacedæmonians were killed, and the Lacedæmonians lost more than 1,000 men compared with 47 Theban and Bœotian dead.

CHAPTER XIV.

Directly after the Battle of Leuctra, Epaminondas released the other Peloponnesians and detained the Lacedæmonians at Leuctra. The text connects the battle with the subsequent events involving the Thespians, the Peloponnese, and Sparta.
