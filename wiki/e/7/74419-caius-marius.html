Caius Marius

Also called: Caias Marlqs, Caius Marius, Gaius Marius, Marias, Marius

Abstract written by a model

Caius Marius, also called Gaius Marius, was a Roman commander whose rise began after being slighted as an ambassador and said to have been a publican. He diligently pursued military affairs, became an expert and famous soldier, and won the Roman army’s love through generosity and affability. His command brought repeated Roman victories; with Bocchus’s help he trapped Jugurtha and had him delivered to Lucius Sylla, and he defeated both Jugurtha and Bocchus in great battles. Renowned when young for ambition, courage, lack of covetousness, and achievements in Europe and Africa, he later sought Mithridates’ wealth and Sylla’s province, contributing to civil war and destruction in Italy. After opposing Caesar, fighting and losing to Sylla, and fleeing to Praeneste, he ended his life through a faithful servant when escape was impossible.

Gaius Marius, who had been consul six times, opposed Caius Julius Caesar over the command against Mithridates. He later fought Sylla, was defeated, and fled with fifteen thousand men to Praeneste. After being deserted and seeing no escape, he had himself killed by a faithful servant.

Caius Marius was slighted by the proconsul as one of the least important ambassadors because he was said to have been a publican. He devoted himself to military affairs, became an expert and famous soldier, won the affection of his soldiers through generosity and courtesy, and led the Romans to victory when he commanded them.

BOOK XXXVII.

Gaius Marius was renowned in his youth for ambition, courage, freedom from covetousness, and achievements in Europe and Africa. In old age he coveted the wealth of Mithridates and Asia and sought Cornelius Sylla’s province, bringing calamities upon himself and contributing to the civil wars and destruction in Italy.
