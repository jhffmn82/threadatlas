Corcyra in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Corcyra is an island important for control of the sea. Threatened by Lacedaemonians and supporters, its inhabitants resisted; Ctesias strengthened defenses, defeated the enemy, and helped nearly end the war. Cleonymus then captured it, took treasure, garrisoned it, and returned with forces after reverses in Italy.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. V.

Corcyra was threatened with seizure by the Lacedaemonians and their supporters. Its inhabitants resisted, and Ctesias later entered the city, strengthened its defenses, defeated the enemy, and helped bring the war nearly to an end.

In Sicily, Agathocles on a sudden, and without any provocation,

Cleonymus captured Corcyra, obtained a great treasure there, and placed a garrison in the city. He intended to use it as a base for managing affairs in Greece, and later returned there with his remaining forces after suffering reverses in Italy.

Corcyra in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Corcyra, of the same stock as Epidamnus, first refused it aid, then fought the Corinthian-supported settlement, defeated Corinth, and recovered Epidamnus. Later dominant at sea, it sought Athenian support amid violent oligarchic-democratic conflict ending in equal privileges.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. VI.

Corcyra refused initial aid to Epidamnus, but later sent a fleet against the Corinthian-supported settlement. Its fleet defeated Corinth, recovered Epidamnus by storm, and later became dominant in that part of the sea before seeking assistance from Athens.

CHAP. V.

Corcyra experienced a violent political conflict between supporters of oligarchy and democracy. The democrats sought Athenian assistance, expelled many oligarchic supporters, and later reached a settlement granting equal privileges.
