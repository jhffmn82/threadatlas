Colonel Sebastian Moran in The Return of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1903-09-26

Colonel Sebastian Moran, born in London in 1840, is the son of Sir Augustus Moran, educated at Eton and Oxford, and formerly served as a colonel in Her Majesty’s Indian Army and the 1st Bangalore Pioneers. A celebrated heavy-game shot, he served in several Indian campaigns, wrote two hunting books, and took part in a man-eating tiger hunt. After retiring, he developed a criminal career, became Professor James Moriarty’s chief of staff and bosom friend, and followed Moriarty in Switzerland. Holmes considers him London’s second most dangerous man. Moran is suspected, though never proved, to have caused Mrs. Stewart’s death; he shoots Ronald Adair after card cheating. At the Reichenbach Fall he attacks Holmes, then later fires an air-gun at Holmes’s apparent figure, is overpowered, and arrested.

Colonel Sebastian Moran is identified as a former member of Her Majesty’s Indian Army and an exceptionally skilled heavy-game shot. He enters the empty house, fires an air-gun at the apparent figure of Holmes, and is overpowered and arrested by Holmes, Watson, and the police. Holmes identifies him as the man who shot the Honourable Ronald Adair.
