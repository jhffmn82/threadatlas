Bannister

person · 1 work · 11 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: servant, the careless servant, the servant, your servant, your servant, Bannister

Abstract written by a model

Bannister is Hilton Soames’s servant of ten years, a man widely regarded as absolutely honest. Formerly butler to Sir Jabez Gilchrist, he remains loyal to Gilchrist’s son after Sir Jabez is ruined and Bannister serves at the college. When disturbed papers are discovered, Bannister leaves his key in Soames’s door, becomes deeply upset, and is questioned about his movements. Though Holmes and Watson consider him honest, Bannister’s conduct seems puzzling. He first denies that anyone hid in the bedroom, but Holmes reveals the lie. Bannister admits he recognized Gilchrist’s gloves, concealed him, and tried to protect him while urging him not to profit from the theft.

Bannister is in one work on this site, The Return of Sherlock Holmes. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

THE ADVENTURE OF THE THREE STUDENTS

Bannister is Hilton Soames’s servant of ten years and is described as honest, though he left his key in Soames’s door after entering to ask whether tea was wanted. He becomes extremely upset when the papers are found disturbed and says he nearly fainted. Holmes questions him about his movements and learns that he sat near the corner after Soames returned, though he had first been near the door.

When we were out in the darkness of the quadrangle, we again

Bannister, the servant, is regarded by both Holmes and Watson as a perfectly honest man. Holmes nevertheless finds Bannister's apparent honesty puzzling and asks what his role is in the matter.

Bannister initially denies that anyone was hiding in the bedroom, but Holmes exposes his lie. He admits that he concealed Gilchrist after recognizing his gloves and explains that he acted to protect the son of his former employer while urging him not to benefit from the theft.
