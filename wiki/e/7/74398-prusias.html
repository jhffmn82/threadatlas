Prusias in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Prusias, king of Bithynia, unsuccessfully attacked Attalus, then plundered sacred places and suffered divine punishment through disease and storm. He was portrayed as degrading royal dignity by imitating Roman libertines and flattering Rome before the Senate. Finally, his son Nicomedes defeated him; Prusias fled to Jupiter’s temple for shelter, where Nicomedes killed him and seized the kingdom.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XXXI.

Prusias was defeated by his son Nicomedes and fled to the temple of Jupiter for protection. Nicomedes killed him there and seized the kingdom.

77* After the Romans had conquered Perseus, (orratherCbaropea),

Prusias, king of Bithynia, destroyed the sacred grove of Nicephorius, plundered its temple, and removed its statues, images, and treasures after failing to capture Attalus. He was then punished when much of his land army died from the bloody flux and much of his naval force was destroyed by a storm in the Propontis.

BOOK XXXVIL

Prusias was portrayed as unworthy of royal dignity because of his sordid flattery of Rome. He dressed as a freed slave, called himself a Roman libertine, prostrated himself before the Senate, and received an answer suited to his excessive flatteries.
