Viriathus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Viriathus is a Lusitanian shepherd from childhood who becomes a general, captain, and prince rather than a thief or robber. Healthful by nature, exceptionally strong and agile, he lives sparingly, works hard, sleeps only as necessary, carries iron weapons, and despises luxury. He values sobriety, temperance, liberty won by valor, honesty, and faithfulness in treaties. Admired and loved by the Lusitanians, he leads them successfully against the Romans, distributes spoils according to merit, and aids those in need. After victories over Vitellius and Fabius, Ceepio repeatedly defeats him; he seeks peace and is treacherously assassinated.

5. The Lusitanians at first not having an expert general, were

Viriathus had been a shepherd from childhood and was exceptionally strong and agile because of his sparse diet, hard labour, and disciplined sleep. After becoming general, he led the Lusitanians successfully, distributed spoils fairly, defeated Vitellius, and forced Fabius to accept dishonourable terms. Ceepio later defeated him repeatedly, and Viriathus sued for peace before being treacherously assassinated.

98. Ptolemy Physcon, the brother of Philometor, began hb

Viriathus despised luxury, valued sobriety and temperance, and regarded the liberty of his country won by valor as the surest possession. He was faithful in treaties, honest in speech, vigilant and courageous as a commander, and beloved by his soldiers because he shared spoils fairly and aided those in need.
