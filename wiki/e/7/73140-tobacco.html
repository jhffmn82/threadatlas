tobacco

object · 1 work · 23 quoted facts · 10 connections
Also called: TOBACCO, old tobacco, tobacco juice, tobacco plants

Abstract written by a model

Tobacco is a plant with tall growth and broad green leaves, but it is described as a poison and narcotic because it contains nicotine, a strong poison. Its leaves may be pressed into cakes, rolled into cigars, or ground into snuff; working with it can make people pale and sickly. In any form it is called a great enemy to youth: while growing, it weakens the body, stunts growth, harms the mind, and prevents bones from growing as well as they otherwise would. Boys who smoke or chew may remember lessons less well and may have worse manners. Tobacco deadens nerves, so users may not feel cold or tired without being helped. It dries the mouth, increases saliva, discolors teeth, causes mouth sores and often throat disease, poisons blood, and makes air unpleasant and unfit to breathe. During the Greely party's ordeal it was no help and probably weakened stomachs. It also wastes money: buying and smoking it is likened to burning a dollar, with six hundred million dollars spent yearly in this country.

tobacco in Child's Health Primer for Primary Classes

Jane Andrews · 1885 · the work’s page · read it where tobacco first appears

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Child's Health Primer for Primary Classes .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Tobacco is described as harmful to men and especially harmful to boys while they are growing. It can stunt growth, hurt the mind, weaken the body, and prevent bones from growing as well as they otherwise would.

CHAPTER III. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Tobacco deadens or puts the nerves to sleep, so a person may fail to feel cold or tired without actually being helped. A boy who smokes or chews tobacco may not remember lessons as well and is usually not as polite or good as he otherwise would be.

CHAPTER VIII. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Tobacco grows tall and produces broad, green leaves. Its leaves can be pressed into cakes, rolled into cigars, or ground into snuff, and working with it can make people pale and sickly.

CHAPTER X. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Tobacco makes the mouth dry, causes extra saliva to flow, discolors the teeth, makes bad sores in the mouth, and often causes throat disease. Tobacco smoke and its strong smell can make the air unpleasant and unfit to breathe.

CHAPTER XVIII. / WHAT MAKES US WARM? .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Tobacco did not help the men of the Greely party during their hardship. It probably weakened their stomachs and made it harder for them to use poor food effectively.

CHAPTER XIX. / COST OF ALCOHOL. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Tobacco is compared to a dollar bill that is burned up, because buying and smoking it quickly wastes money. Smoking tobacco also hurts the person who uses it.
