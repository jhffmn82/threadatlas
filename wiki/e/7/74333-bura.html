Bura

place · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 1 connection

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Bura was an Achaean city that refused the Ionians' request for religious images and suffered in earthquakes and inundations. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Bura was an Achaean hill town devastated by the earthquake that destroyed Helice.

Bura in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Bura first appears

Abstract written by a model

Bura was an Achaean hill town, said to be named for Bura, daughter of Ion and Helice; an earthquake that destroyed Helice devastated it, leaving none of its old temple statues, despite temples to several deities.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXV.

Bura was a town on a hill, said to have been named for Bura, the daughter of Ion and Helice. It suffered a mighty earthquake when Helice was destroyed, and none of its old temple statues survived. Its temples included those of Demeter, Aphrodite, Dionysus, Ilithyia, and Isis.
