Abstract written by a model

Publius Scipio, natural son of Lucius iEmilius and adopted son of Scipio who conquered Hannibal and the Carthaginians, studied Greek learning from childhood and philosophy under Polybius from eighteen. After five years of struggle he attained habitual sobriety and temperance, and was noted for affability, generosity, chastity, courage, and hunting prowess beyond the Macedonian kings. As president of the council, he used inherited wealth generously: supporting Papiria, paying dowries immediately, giving money and inheritances to his brother and sisters, and helping fund funeral combats. When Antiochus offered his son's release and money for peace, Scipio refused the money and urged Antiochus not to resist the Romans.

74. Lucius iEmilius, who conquered Perseus, and was fi consul

Publius Scipio was the natural son of Lucius iEmilius and the adopted son of Scipio, who conquered Hannibal and the Carthaginians. He studied Greek learning and philosophy under Polybius of Megalopolis and became known for sobriety, affability, generosity, chastity, courage, and other virtues. He gave inheritances and money to his mother, sisters, and brother, paid dowries in full at once, helped fund his brother's funeral games, and surpassed the Macedonian kings in hunting.
