Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Cilicia is a region that supplies the Persian army with corn, contains Issus, Quinda, and the temple of Apollo of Sarpedon, serves as a route and provisioning source for the Persian fleet, and later is a province involved in the campaigns of Darius, Craterus, Antipater, Eumenes, and Ptolemy. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Cilicia contains Tarsus; Cyrus's army passes through its gates to Tarsus, and Syennesis, its king, promises Cyrus assistance while secretly informing Artaxerxes.

Cilicia in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Cilicia first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cilicia is a region that supplies the Persian army with corn and contains Issus, Quinda with the kings’ treasury and garrison, and the temple of Apollo of Sarpedon. In the expedition against Evagoras, it serves as a route and provisioning source for the Persian fleet, and Teribazus returns there after a sea battle. Darius marches toward it with his army and family as Alexander takes Issus. Later it is a province holding Macedonian soldiers under Craterus, Antipater’s destination against Perdiccas, and the place Eumenes enters before Ptolemy tries to block aid reaching him.

Cilicia served as a route and source of provisions in the expedition against Evagoras. The Persian fleet went there for corn, and Teribazus later returned there after the battle at sea.

CHAP. III.

Darius marched toward Cilicia with his large army and family. Alexander seized Issus, a considerable city of Cilicia, before confronting Darius nearby.

Cilicia was the province from which Craterus was summoned with thirty thousand Macedonian soldiers. Craterus had been sent there before and was returning toward Macedonia when Antipater requested his assistance.

But after Antipater and Craterus had transported their army out

Cilicia was the destination of Antipater's march after the army was divided, where he intended to fight Perdiccas. Antipater hastened there after receiving the survivors of Craterus's defeat.

CHAP. V.

Eumenes crossed Mount Taurus and entered Cilicia, where Antigenes, Tautamus, and the Argyraspides met him. Ptolemy later arrived at Zaphyrium in Cilicia and attempted to prevent support and funding from reaching Eumenes.
