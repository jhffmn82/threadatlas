Phayllus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Phayllus, brother of Onomarchus, became a Phocian general after his brother's death. Using vast treasure, he raised and commanded a large mercenary army, led the Phocians in war, and gained support from the Lacedaemonians, Achaians, Athenians, and other cities. Defeated at Orchomenum, Cephisus, and Coronea, he later routed the Boeotians, captured and destroyed Aryca, then died after lingering, painful illness; Phalaecus succeeded him.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAP. Vlll.

After his brother's death, Phayllus raised a large mercenary army and won the support of the Lacedaemonians, Achaians, Athenians, and other cities for the Phocians. He was defeated at Orchomenum, the river Cephisus, and Coronea, though he later routed the Boeotians near Aryca and captured and destroyed that city. He died after a long and painful illness, leaving Phalaecus as general of the Phocians.

Phayllus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Phayllus becomes the Phocians’ commander in chief with unlimited power after Onomarchus dies, then dreams of becoming a wasting brass old man among Apollo’s offerings and soon suffers the foretold wasting disease.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

After Onomarchus died, the Phocians chose his brother Phayllus as commander in chief with unlimited power. Phayllus dreamed that he had become like a wasting brass image of an old man among Apollo's offerings, and a wasting disease soon afflicted him and fulfilled the dream.
