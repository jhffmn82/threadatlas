golden ram

mythical animal · 3 works · 8 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: a ram, aries, aureus ille aries, golden-fleeced ram, ram, ram with a golden fleece, the golden ram, the ram, κριὸν, χρυσόμαλλον κριὸν
  • In Heroides and Amores, the golden ram carries Phrixus safely over the sea, and its golden fleece becomes an expedition’s prize and the speaker’s claimed dowry.
  • In The Argonautica, the golden ram, made entirely of gold by Hermes, carries Phrixus to Aea, where Phrixus sacrifices it to Zeus and its fleece remains.
  • In The Library, the golden-fleeced ram carries Phrixus and Helle through the sky before Phrixus sacrifices it to Zeus and its fleece passes to Aeetes.

the golden ram in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

The golden ram, remarkable for its deep fleece, carries Phrixus safely over the sea. Its golden fleece becomes an expedition’s prize, is obtained from a ploughed field, and is claimed as the speaker’s dowry, which her husband would refuse to return.

Quodsi forte preces praecordia ferrea tangunt,

The golden ram with its splendid, deep fleece is presented as part of the speaker's dowry. She says her husband would refuse to give it back if she demanded its return.

golden-fleeced ram in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Given by Nephele to Phrixus and Helle, it carries them through the sky; Phrixus sacrifices it to Zeus, and its golden fleece goes to Aeetes.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

IX. Τῶν δὲ Αἰόλον παίδων ᾿Αθάμας, Βοιωτίας

The golden-fleeced ram is given by Nephele to Phrixus and Helle and carries them through the sky over land and sea. Phrixus sacrifices it to Zeus and gives its fleece to Aeetes.
