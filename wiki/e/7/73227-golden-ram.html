golden ram

mythical animal · 3 works · 8 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: a ram, aries, aureus ille aries, golden-fleeced ram, ram, ram with a golden fleece, the golden ram, the ram, κριὸν, χρυσόμαλλον κριὸν

Abstract written by a model

In The Argonautica, the golden ram, made entirely of gold by Hermes, carries Phrixus to Aea, where Phrixus sacrifices it to Zeus and its fleece remains. In Heroides and Amores, the golden ram carries Phrixus safely over the sea, and its golden fleece becomes an expedition’s prize and the speaker’s claimed dowry. In The Library, the golden-fleeced ram carries Phrixus and Helle through the sky before Phrixus sacrifices it to Zeus and its fleece passes to Aeetes.

the golden ram in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · about 24 to 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where the golden ram first appears

Abstract written by a model

The golden ram, remarkable for its deep fleece, carries Phrixus safely over the sea. Its golden fleece becomes an expedition’s prize, is obtained from a ploughed field, and is claimed as the speaker’s dowry, which her husband would refuse to return.

The golden ram with its splendid, deep fleece is presented as part of the speaker's dowry. She says her husband would refuse to give it back if she demanded its return.
