Abstract written by a model

Bessus, a Bactrian lord-lieutenant of Bactria, treacherously murdered the fleeing Darius, then escaped to Bactria. There he stirred resistance to Alexander, organized forces and supplies, took the name of king, and was recognized by many. After a quarrel with Bagodares, his commanders seized and delivered him to Alexander. Handed to Darius’s brother and kindred, he was tortured and killed.

Bessus is in one work on this site, The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Bessus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. VU.

Bessus was the lord-lieutenant of Bactria. He treacherously murdered Darius while Darius was fleeing from Alexander.

CHAP. VIII.

Bessus escaped after the death of Darius and went to Bactria with Nabarzanes, Barzaentes, and others. Because he had been appointed lord-lieutenant of Bactria by Darius and was well known there, he persuaded the inhabitants to resist Alexander, organized forces and supplies, and was recognized by many as king.

CHAP. IX.

Bessus took the name of king and quarreled with Bagodares while drinking. After Bagodares fled to Alexander, Bessus's commanders conspired against him, seized him, and delivered him to Alexander, who handed him over to Darius's brother and kindred; they tortured and killed him.

BOOK XVII.

He stirred up the Bactrians after Darius’s death. He was brought before Alexander and punished.
