Africans in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Africans first appears

Abstract written by a model

Africans, Africa’s most ancient and numerous inhabitants, oppose Carthaginians during a plague, hating their severe government. In Agathocles’s army, their habitual treachery and shifting sides culminate when about five thousand desert at night, prompting mutual alarm and leaving Agathocles too weak to continue fighting.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Having thus settled afikirs id order to the establishing himself in

The Africans in Agathocles's army are described as treacherous and prone to change sides. About five thousand deserted during the night of the camp fires, and their movement caused both armies to mistake them for an enemy attack. Their desertion left Agathocles without sufficient forces to continue fighting.

Africans in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Africans first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sea-coast Africans inhabit Cyrene and Syrtes, claim Bacchus’s birthplace and deeds, preserve marks and writers’ support, and later ally with Dionysius against the Titans.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

The poet in his hymns confirms what we have before declared^

The Africans living on the sea-coasts claim that Bacchus was born among them and that his reported deeds occurred there. They cite surviving marks and signs, support their account through ancient and modern writers, and later joined Dionysius as allies against the Titans.
