Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, tyranny is described as oppressing people in Syracuse and Sicily through Dionysius and other rulers, while Dion opposed Dionysius and Timoleon was sent to restrain would-be tyrants and oppose monarchy. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, tyranny is described as the outcome the Syracusans feared when granting Dionysius sole command, after which he used his guard, mercenaries, political alliances, and control of the city to establish himself as ruler.

tyranny in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where tyranny first appears

Abstract written by a model

Tyranny in Syracuse and Sicily oppressed people through Dionysius and other rulers. Dion fled, raised mercenaries, and returned to oppose Dionysius; the Syracusans celebrated its overthrow despite continued resistance. Amid seditions, Timoleon was sent to restrain would-be tyrants and oppose monarchy.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. 11.

The tyranny of Syracuse was represented by Dionysius the younger, whose sloth and neglect of military affairs weakened his position. Dion's opposition to the tyranny led him to flee, gather mercenaries, and seek its overthrow.

CHAP. III.

Dion came to Sicily to put down the tyranny of Dionysius. The Syracusans celebrated the overthrow of tyranny, although Dionysius continued resistance from the castle.
