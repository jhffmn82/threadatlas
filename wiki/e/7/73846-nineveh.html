Nineveh in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Nineveh first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nineveh was a great city near the Euphrates, built by Ninus and named after him, the capital of the Assyrian empire and seat of Sardanapalus. Inhabited chiefly by wealthy Assyrians and also other nations, it had vast dimensions, strong walls, plentiful supplies, and fifteen hundred turrets. Confederates besieged it for three years; the Euphrates broke its wall, after which they entered, dispersed inhabitants, restored estates, and razed it when the Medes ruined the Assyrian empire.

DIODORUS SICULUS. / BOOK IL

Nineveh was a great city built by Ninus near the Euphrates and named after him. It was inhabited chiefly by wealthy Assyrians, though people of other nations were also allowed to settle there.

CHAP. IL

Nineveh was the seat of Sardanapalus and was defended by strong walls and plentiful supplies. The confederates besieged it for three years until the Euphrates tore down part of its wall; they then entered the city, dispersed its inhabitants into villages, restored their estates, and razed Nineveh to the ground.
