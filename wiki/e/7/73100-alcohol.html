alcohol in Scientific American Supplement No. 360, November 25, 1882

Various · 1881

Alcohol may be a comfort and blessing when properly used, but excess makes it a bane and curse. Up to about one and a half to two pints of beer it provides convertible energy; beyond that, considerable doses give temporary stimulus followed by depression and are generally avoided before or during work.

THE FOOD AND ENERGY OF MAN.

Alcohol may be a comfort and blessing when properly used but becomes a bane and curse in excess. alcohol in Child's Health Primer for Primary Classes

Jane Andrews · 1885

Alcohol looks like water. When a lighted match is held near it, it burns with a blue flame that gives much heat but little light and makes no smoke or soot. It preserves dead bodies from decaying and is contained in various drinks, though pure alcohol is not often used as a drink. As its amount increases, danger increases. It is a poison: after entering the blood, it affects the brain and then the nerves, deadens nerves, impairs the mind, damages muscles, and can make the heart beat too fast, tire it, and become fatty. It cannot make good muscles, bone, nerve, or brain or give real strength; instead, it may encourage people to take more, make them imagine they can do more, and later leave them weaker. Habitual use can impair the five senses, cause trembling hands, confused nerves, thick speech, difficulty walking straight, or seeing things not present.

alcohol in Child's Health Primer for Primary Classes

Jane Andrews · 1885

Alcohol is presented as a popular thin liquid poison, not food that makes a person strong. It is made from grain and fruit when sugar in fruit-juice changes, with yeast helping change sugar into gas and alcohol. It remains mixed with water, mixes readily with it, and becomes vapor with less heat than water requires. In distillation it is collected before water; repeated distillation makes it purer and stronger by increasing the proportion of alcohol. The records emphasize its harm to body, mind, life, and character. Small amounts harm anyone who drinks it, while large amounts can kill. It injures the brain and nerves, may prevent messages from being sent properly, cause headache and carelessness, and makes the person who takes it want more. It changes muscles partly into fat and takes away their strength. It harms the stomach by injuring gastric juice, causing burning in the throat and, with repeated use, sores that may sometimes be fatal. Alcohol may seem to warm a person because it rushes hot blood into tiny tubes near the skin, but this increases heat loss, deadens nerves’ warning of cold, and lessens power to resist cold. Beer, wine, cider, brandy, and other drinks contain it. From its making and strengthening through distillation to its effects on drinkers, it is portrayed as harmful and costly: money spent on it is wasted or worse than wasted because it does no good and causes harm.

Child's Health Primer for Primary Classes .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Alcohol is presented as a popular poison whose effects on the human body, life, and character pupils should learn about in time. The book includes chapters on what alcohol is, beer, distilling, and alcohol.

CHAPTER II. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Alcohol often changes part of the muscles into fat and thereby reduces their strength. Beer contains alcohol, so drinking beer may make a person fleshy without making the person stronger.

CHAPTER III. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Alcohol is described as a poison rather than food that makes a person strong. It injures the brain and nerves, prevents messages from being sent properly, and can make people careless. Even a little can make the head ache and hurt the brain and nerves.

_] CHAPTER IV. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Alcohol is a thin liquid and a poison formed when the sugar in fruit-juice changes. It remains mixed with the water, harms the body and mind, and can make a person want more alcohol.

] CHAPTER V. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Alcohol is produced when yeast helps change sugar into gas and alcohol. The unit calls alcohol a poison and says that people should not drink beer because it contains alcohol.

] CHAPTER VI. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Alcohol turns to vapor with less heat than water turns to steam. It is collected first from liquids containing it, but it is mixed with water and must be distilled many times to become pure. Each distillation makes it stronger by increasing the proportion of alcohol.

CHAPTER X. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Alcohol in wine, cider, brandy, and other drinks is carried away quickly but harms the stomach while it remains there. It injures the gastric juice, may cause sores in the stomach when used repeatedly, and can sometimes cause the drinker to die.

CHAPTER XVIII. / WHAT MAKES US WARM? .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Alcohol brings hot blood to the tiny tubes near the skin, creating a temporary feeling of warmth. It actually increases heat loss, dulls the nerves, and can make a person freeze more quickly.

CHAPTER XIX. / COST OF ALCOHOL. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Alcohol costs a great deal of money. Money spent on alcohol is described as wasted, or worse than wasted, because it does no good and only causes harm.
