Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Agesilaus is a Spartan generalissimo and commander who campaigns against the Thebans, Persians, and Egyptians before dying at Cyrene while returning to Sparta. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Agesilaus is one of the two Lacedaemonian kings and a military general who campaigns in Asia and Boeotia, wins at Coronea, and is severely wounded at Delphos. In Description of Greece, Agesilaus is the Lacedæmonian king son of Doryssus who campaigns against Corinth, Acarnania, and Egypt before dying while sailing home. In Description of Greece, Agesilaus is the Spartan king son of Archidamus who commands campaigns in Asia Minor and Greece, defeats Tissaphernes and the Thebans, and honors Athene Itonia while wounded.

Agesilaus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Agesilaus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Agesilaus is a Spartan/Lacedaemonian generalissimo and commander. Chosen for valor and prudence, he led a large force into Boeotia but avoided attacking the Thebans on difficult ground and retreated after their sally. Appointed to command war against Persia, he went to Ephesus and routed Persians at Sipylos. In Egypt he commanded Greek mercenaries, aided Tachos’s escape, defeated Egyptians, restored Tachos to his kingdom, and died at Cyrene while returning to Sparta.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Whilst these things were acting in Greece, Acoris^king of Egypt^

Agesilaus was chosen generalissimo of the Spartan army because of his reputation for valor and prudence. He entered Boeotia with more than eighteen thousand foot and fifteen hundred horse, but declined to assault the Thebans on difficult ground and later retreated when Thebes sallied against him.

CHAR XL

Agesilaus commanded Tachos's ten thousand Greek mercenaries and advised Tachos to remain in Egypt. He helped Tachos escape an Egyptian siege, defeated the Egyptians in a position protected by water, and thereby restored Tachos to Egypt. He later died at Cyrene while returning to Sparta.

BOOK XIV.

Agesilaus was appointed by the Lacedaemonians to command the war against Persia. He routed the Persians at Sipylos and later defeated Egyptian forces that pursued him, restoring Tachos to his kingdom.

Agesilaus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Agesilaus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Agesilaus is one of the two Lacedaemonian kings and a military general. Sent from Europe to Ephesus, he enlarged his army, campaigned in Asia, defeated Tissaphernes's forces, and made a truce with Tithraustes. Recalled, he marched through Macedonia and Thessaly into Boeotia, won at Coronea, and was severely wounded and taken to Delphos.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. vm.

Agesilaus, one of the two Lacedaemonian kings, was made general and crossed to Ephesus with six thousand men and thirty eminent citizens. He raised four thousand more, campaigned through the Caystrian plains, Mysia, and the country toward Sardis, defeated Tissaphernes's forces near Sipylus, and later made a six-month truce with Tithraustes.

CHAP. IX,

Agesilaus was recalled from Asia with his army and marched through Macedonia, Thessaly, and Thermopylae into Boeotia. At Coronea he defeated part of the opposing army, was severely wounded, and was taken to Delphos for treatment.

Agesilaus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Agesilaus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Agesilaus, son of Doryssus, was a Lacedæmonian king during Lycurgus’ legislation. He returned to Corinth with an army, lost Amyclæan troops to Athenians, compelled Acarnanians to end war after marching into Ætolia, aided revolted Egyptians, and died sailing home; the Lacedæmonians honoured his burial.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK III.--LACONIA.

Agesilaus, son of Doryssus, was a king of one Lacedæmonian royal house. Lycurgus legislated during his reign.

CHAPTER X.

Agesilaus returned to Corinth with an army and sent the natives of Amyclæ home for the festival of Hyacinthus; the Athenians under Iphicrates attacked and destroyed this detachment. He later marched into Ætolia and compelled the Acarnanians to end their war, then sailed to Egypt to aid Egyptians who had revolted from the great king. He died while sailing home, and the Lacedæmonians buried him with exceptional honours.

Agesilaus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Agesilaus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Agesilaus, son of Archidamus and a Spartan king, was chosen over Leotychides despite an oracle. He commanded land forces in Asia Minor, defeated Tissaphernes near the Hermus, became fleet commander, returned during the Corinthian War, defeated Thebans and allies at Coronea, and honored Athene Itonia while wounded.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

Agesilaus became king after the Lacedæmonians chose him over Leotychides, despite an oracle concerning a lame king. He led the expedition into Asia Minor, defeated Tissaphernes near the Hermus, later returned to Greece because of the Corinthian War, and defeated the Thebans and their allies at Coronea.
