Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Massaga was a four-day siege in which Macedonians used engines, a wooden tower, bridges, archers, and assaults until the governor’s death weakened defenders; Alexander captured it by storm, with twenty-five Macedonians killed.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

SUP. XIV.

The siege lasted four days. The Macedonians used engines, a wooden tower, bridges, archers, and repeated assaults, but the defenders held until their governor was killed and many men became unserviceable. Alexander then captured Massaga by storm after executing the mercenary Indians.
