Leuctra

place · 3 works · 7 quoted facts · 8 connections
Also called: Leuctra,, Leuctra^, Leuctrny, Leuctrus, Shady Leuctra

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Leuctra is the battlefield where the Lacedaemonians and Boeotians fought, and where Epaminondas and the Thebans defeated the Spartans. In Description of Greece, Leuctra is a place with temples and statues, and the site of a Theban victory that led to disaster for the Lacedæmonians and restoration of the Messenians. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Leuctra is a place in Bœotia where Scedasus lived and where the Thebans and Lacedæmonians fought, after which the Lacedæmonians were shut up there.

Leuctra in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Leuctra first appears

Abstract written by a model

Leuctra is a battlefield where Lacedaemonians and Boeotians encamped and fought; its tombs shaped Epaminondas’s response to ominous signs before he and the Thebans defeated the Spartans there.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. VL

Leuctra was the place where the Lacedaemonians and the Boeotians encamped and fought. The field was associated with the tombs of Leuctrus and Schedasus's daughters, which Epaminondas used to counter the soldiers' fears about ominous signs.

BOOK XIV.

Leuctra was the site of a famous battle in which Epaminondas and the Thebans defeated the Spartans.

Leuctra in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Leuctra first appears

Abstract written by a model

Leuctra, about twenty stades from Pephnos, is a place whose name has no known explanation. It has temples or statues of Æsculapius, Ino, Cassandra, Carnean Apollo, Athene, Eros, and Ithomatan Zeus; Eros has a winter-flowing grove, and Zeus was found after a fire. It was the site of a Theban victory that led to disaster for the Lacedæmonians and restoration of the Messenians.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIII.

Leuctra lies about twenty stades from Pephnos, though the reason for its name is unknown. It contains temples or statues of Æsculapius, Ino, Cassandra, Carnean Apollo, Athene, and Eros; a statue of Ithomatan Zeus was discovered there after a fire cleared part of a wood.

CHAPTER XXIV.

Leuctra was the place of the Theban victory after which the long-fated disaster came upon the Lacedæmonians. The victory enabled the Thebans to recall the Messenians and support their restoration.

Leuctra in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Leuctra first appears

Abstract written by a model

Leuctra is a place in Bœotia. It was home to Scedasus, whose memory Epaminondas honored there while vowing a battle. The Thebans and Lacedæmonians fought at Leuctra; the Lacedæmonians were then shut up there. Oracles foretold the lamentable battle, and its reverse preceded Megalopolis’s colonization.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXVII.

The colonization of Megalopolis took place a year and a few months after the reverse of the Lacedæmonians at Leuctra. The city was colonized in the second year of the 102nd Olympiad, when Phrasiclides was Archon at Athens and Damon of Thuria won the course.

CHAPTER XIII.

Leuctra in Bœotia was the site of the battle between the Thebans and the Lacedæmonians. Scedasus had lived there, and Epaminondas offered funeral rites to Scedasus and his daughters and vowed that a battle would take place there.

CHAPTER XIV.

Leuctra was the place where the Lacedæmonians were kept shut up after the battle. An oracle also described shady Leuctra as dear to it and foretold a lamentable battle there.
