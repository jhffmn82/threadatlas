Philomelus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Philomelus, a Phocian leader and sole general of the Phocians, was exceptionally impudent and wicked. He persuaded the Phocians to resist the Amphictyonic decree, raised an army with support from Archidamus, defeated the Locrians, seized and controlled the temple at Delphos, destroyed decree records, and sought Greek alliances. Claiming ancestral Phocian rights, he compelled Pythia to answer from the tripod and treated the response as license to act freely. As chief contriver of the seizure, he initiated sacrilege by plundering the temple to hire mercenaries. After defeat by the Boeotians, he was driven to a precipice and threw himself from it; another record says he was killed in battle.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. VI.

Philomelus persuaded the Phocians to resist the Amphictyonic decree and was appointed their sole and absolute general. With financial support from Archidamus, he raised an army, seized the temple at Delphos, defeated the Locrians, destroyed records of the Amphictyonic decrees, and sought alliances throughout Greece. He forced Pythia to ascend the tripod and interpreted her answer as permission to do what he pleased.

CHAP. VII.

Philomelus raised an army from the best of the Phocians and hired many soldiers. He defeated the Locrians at the Rocks of Phaedra, later rifled Apollo's temple to pay additional mercenaries, and was defeated by the Boeotians. After being driven to a precipice, he threw himself from the rock.

Philomelus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Philomelus, a Ledonian son of Philotimus and second in merit among the Phocians, took a golden shield, urged seizure of Delphi's temple over an unpaid fine, led war against the Thebans, and died after defeat near Neon.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

Philomelus, son of Philotimus and a native of Ledon, persuaded the Phocians that they could not pay their fine and urged them to seize the temple at Delphi. He led the Phocians at the beginning of their war with the Thebans, but after their defeat near Neon he threw himself from a steep crag and died.
