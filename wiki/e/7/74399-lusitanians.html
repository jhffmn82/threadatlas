Lusitanians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Lusitanians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Lusitanians were a people of Portugal, some inhabiting sea-coasts. Initially easily defeated by the Romans for lack of an expert general, they later, under the beloved Viriathus, defeated Memmius after his landing in Spain and won several battles. After Viriathus’s death, they honored him lavishly, then were broken, dispersed, intimidated by Ceepio, and accepted his terms.

Lusitanians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Lusitanians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Lusitanians are a people called the most valiant of the Cimbri. Agile fighters, they used shields, iron weapons, swords, helmets, and darts; they danced in peace, marched and sang before attacks in war, while young and needy men formed mountain bands to raid for wealth.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Having spoken of the Celts, we shall now give an account of tbck

The Lusitanians are called the most valiant of the Cimbri and are described as agile fighters who used small, strong shields, iron weapons, swords, helmets, and long-range darts. They performed nimble dances in peacetime, marched to measured rhythms in war, and sang paeans before attacking. Their young and needy men also gathered in mountain bands and raided for wealth.
