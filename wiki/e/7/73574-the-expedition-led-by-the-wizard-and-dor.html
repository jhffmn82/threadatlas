the expedition led by the Wizard and Dorothy in The Lost Princess of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1917

The expedition led by the Wizard and Dorothy is a search party that leaves the Emerald City to find Ozma of Oz and seek Ugu the Shoemaker, forcing him to surrender stolen magical possessions. Its members include Betsy Bobbin, Trot, Button-Bright, Scraps, the Woozy, the Cowardly Lion, Hank, the Sawhorse, and later Toto, who had followed secretly. The party crosses the Winkie River and the Rolling Prairie into dangerous unknown territory, then heads for Ugu’s Wicker Castle. A circle of fire drives it back, but Scraps and the Wizard show that an apparent army cannot harm them. Inside the castle, Ugu traps them in an upside-down hall; a failed human pyramid is followed by Dorothy using the Magic Belt to restore the room.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 6

The main search party consists of Dorothy, the Wizard, Betsy Bobbin, Trot, Button-Bright, Scraps, the Woozy, the Cowardly Lion, Hank, the Sawhorse, and later Toto. It leaves the Emerald City, crosses the Winkie River, enters the Rolling Prairie, and decides to proceed into dangerous unknown territory.

CHAPTER 19

The expedition led by the Wizard and Dorothy sets out to find Ugu the Shoemaker and force him to give up the magical possessions he stole. The Great Book of Records and the Magic Picture show Ugu that this expedition is headed straight for the Wicker Castle.

CHAPTER 21

The Wizard’s companions travel uphill toward the wicker castle and are driven back by the heat of the circle of fire. They cautiously pass through the apparent army after Scraps and the Wizard show that it cannot harm them.

CHAPTER 22

The Wizard's followers enter the wicker castle with him and become trapped in its central hall when Ugu turns the room upside down. They try to reach the stolen magical instruments by forming a human pyramid, but the attempt collapses. Dorothy's use of the Magic Belt eventually returns the room to its proper position.
