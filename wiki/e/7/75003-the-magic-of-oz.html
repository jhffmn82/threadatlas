The Magic of Oz in The Magic of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1919

The document is a 1919 Oz story introduced by L. Frank Baum, who writes to readers after an illness and signs as the Royal Historian of Oz. Its central danger begins when Kiki Aru, son of the former Sorcerer Bini Aru, steals his father's secret transformation method and leaves Oz as a bird. Beyond the Deadly Sandy Desert, Kiki meets Ruggedo, the former Nome King. The two villains plot to invade Oz, turn its people into beasts, turn beasts into people, and use the forest animals as an army, though each intends to betray the other. In the Forest of Gugu, Kiki and Ruggedo disguise themselves as strange Li-Mon-Eags and persuade many beasts that Oz's people mean to enslave them. Meanwhile, separate birthday errands for Ozma unfold. Dorothy and the Wizard seek monkeys for a surprise cake, while Trot, Cap'n Bill, and the Glass Cat seek the Magic Flower. Trot and Cap'n Bill reach its island but become rooted fast in its magical ground; the Glass Cat goes for help. Kiki disrupts the forest meeting by transforming Dorothy, the Wizard, the Cowardly Lion, the Hungry Tiger, Gugu, and Ruggedo into different forms and stealing the Wizard's black bag. Ruggedo later helps Kiki create giant monkey soldiers, but the Wizard, while a fox, overhears the magic word. He turns Kiki into a hickory-nut and Ruggedo into a walnut, restores his friends, recovers the bag through the Glass Cat, and frees the giant monkeys. The Wizard's party then rescues Trot and Cap'n Bill from the shrinking enchantment of the Magic Isle by changing them into bumblebees. Cap'n Bill retrieves the Magic Flower safely. Back at the Emerald City, the Wizard trains twelve monkeys, and Ozma's birthday is celebrated with Dorothy's monkey-filled surprise cake, the Magic Flower, and other gifts. Finally, beside the Fountain of the Water of Oblivion, Kiki and Ruggedo are restored from nuts, made to forget their wicked pasts, and kept in Oz by Ozma so they cannot resume their former ways.

Narrative, section by section

24 sections; each heading opens the text

To My Readers

L. Frank Baum addresses his readers about recent events in the outside world and the unusual nature of The Magic of Oz. He says that a long illness prevented him from answering many letters, but he hopes to respond promptly in the future. Baum expresses his affection for his readers and hopes the Oz Books will continue giving them pleasure. He signs the message as the “Royal Historian of Oz” from Oz-Cot in Hollywood, California, in 1919.

1. Mount Munch

Mount Munch is a steep, saucer-topped mountain in the Munchkin Country where the Hyups live apart from the Munchkins. Bini Aru, a former Sorcerer, obeys Ozma of Oz's ban on magic but secretly records his unique transformation method beneath a floorboard. His unhappy son, Kiki Aru, discovers and copies the instructions, then uses them to transform into a hawk-like bird. Kiki flies toward the country beyond the Deadly Sandy Desert, hoping to escape Oz's laws and explore the outside world.

2. The Hawk

Kiki Aru crosses the Deadly Desert, rests in Noland, and travels through Merryland, Ix, and the Land of Ev while changing into various birds. In Noland he receives food and lodging from a family, and he later visits the city of Nole and Queen Zixi's country as a white dove. In the Land of Ev, Kiki steals a gold piece from an old man's room after learning that money is required for food and lodging. A sparrow condemns his use of magic and theft, but Kiki says he is glad to be wicked before a loud-voiced stranger praises him.

3. Two Bad Ones

Kiki Aru meets Ruggedo, the former Nome King, at an inn and learns that Ruggedo possesses many jewels and hates the Oz people. Ruggedo tries to buy or force Kiki to reveal the secret of his magic transformations, but Kiki refuses. Ruggedo then proposes that they enter the Land of Oz as birds, change into beasts, gather savage beasts as an army, and conquer Oz without being detected by Glinda's Book of Records. Their plan would transform the Oz people into beasts and the beasts into men and women, while Ruggedo seeks revenge and hopes to regain the Kingdom of the Nomes.

4. Conspirators

Kiki Aru and Ruggedo conspire to conquer Oz, while each secretly plans to betray the other. Kiki intends to keep Ruggedo transformed into a marble statue, while Ruggedo hopes to learn Kiki's secret word and turn him into faggots to burn. Kiki transforms himself and Ruggedo into eagles so they can fly across the Desert at night without revealing the magic word. During the flight, Ruggedo explains that eggs are the only thing he fears and that the Oz people took away his former magical tools.

5. A Happy Corner of Oz

The Land of Oz is described as a beautiful, prosperous fairyland ruled by Ozma of Oz, whose birthday is approaching. Dorothy, Trot, and Cap'n Bill decide to find Ozma a Magic Flower that grows different flowers continuously in a golden pot. The Glass Cat reveals that the flower is hidden on an island in a river in the northern Munchkin Country and agrees to guide them there. Trot prepares to leave with Cap'n Bill and the Glass Cat for a journey expected to last about a week.

6. Ozma's Birthday Presents

Dorothy searches for a birthday present for Ozma after learning what Scraps, the Tin Woodman, the Scarecrow, and Glinda the Good plan to give her. Glinda suggests that Dorothy make a surprise birthday cake, so Dorothy asks the Wizard of Oz to help hide tiny dancing monkeys inside it. Dorothy and the Wizard plan to travel to the forests of the Gillikin Country to find large monkeys, make them three inches high, and train them to perform at Ozma's birthday banquet. They decide to take the Cowardly Lion and the Hungry Tiger instead of the Wooden Sawhorse and Red Wagon, keeping the journey secret from Ozma.

7. The Forest of Gugu

Gugu Forest is a vast forest in western Gillikin Country where most of the wild beasts of Oz live under laws enforced by King Gugu and his three King's Counselors. Ruggedo and Kiki Aru fly into the forest to recruit enough beasts to help them conquer the people of Oz. To gain the beasts' favor without appearing as ordinary members of any one species, they decide to assume forms combining lion heads, monkey bodies, eagle wings, and wild-ass tails with golden knobs. Kiki Aru performs the magical transformation separately from Ruggedo, and both reach the forest floor in their new forms.

8. The Li-Mon-Eags Make Trouble

After Chipo the Wild Boar causes a chain of trouble in the Forest of Gugu, Gugu the Leopard King convenes the Council of Beasts. Ruggedo and Kiki Aru arrive disguised as Li-Mon-Eags and falsely warn that the people of Oz plan to enslave the forest beasts. Ruggedo proposes that the beasts conquer the people of Oz, promising that magic will transform the two groups. Kiki Aru proves their magical power by transforming Loo the Unicorn into a man, and Gugu orders the forest beasts to assemble at sunrise to consider the plan.

9. The Isle of the Magic Flower

The Glass Cat leads Trot and Cap'n Bill through the Munchkin Country and into a forest in the Gillikin Country, where they find the river and the Isle of the Magic Flower. Cap'n Bill begins building a raft to reach the small island and retrieve the Magic Flower for Ozma's birthday. A Kalidah attacks, but Cap'n Bill knocks it down and pins it to the ground with a sharp stake. The travelers learn that Kalidahs avoid the magical island, although they can swim and are dangerous to other animals.

10. Stuck Fast

Trot, Cap'n Bill, and the Glass Cat cross the river on a raft to reach the Magic Flower's island. Trot and Cap'n Bill discover that the island's magical ground has grown roots around their fleshly feet, trapping them, while the Glass Cat remains free. The Kalidah escapes from Cap'n Bill's stake and leaves to seek help from the Kalidah King. The Glass Cat agrees to return to the Emerald City and tell the Wizard of Oz about Trot and Cap'n Bill's predicament.

11. The Beasts of the Forest of Gugu

The beasts of the Forest of Gugu gather at sunrise to hear Gugu the Leopard and the two strange Li-Mon-Eags discuss a danger from the Oz people. Ruggedo the Nome claims that Ozma and her people plan to capture, beat, and enslave the beasts, and he proposes transforming the beasts into people and the Oz people into beasts. The animals react with anger and uncertainty, and Gugu tells them to think carefully before deciding whether to accept the plan.

12. Kiki Uses His Magic

The Cowardly Lion, Dorothy, the Wizard of Oz, and the Hungry Tiger arrive before Gugu, King of the forest, while the animals debate whether to fight the Oz people or be transformed. Kiki Aru secretly uses his magic word to transform the Wizard into a fox, Dorothy into a lamb, the Cowardly Lion into a Munchkin boy, Ruggedo into a goose, and the Hungry Tiger into a rabbit. The Hungry Tiger discovers Kiki's role and attacks him, but Kiki escapes into a tree and transforms Gugu into a fat Gillikin woman. Terrified by Kiki's powers, the animals flee the clearing, leaving the transformed figures bewildered.

13. The Loss of the Black Bag

Kiki Aru hides the Wizard's black bag in a tree after stealing it, while the Wizard, Dorothy, the Cowardly Lion, the Hungry Tiger, and Gugu remain transformed. Ruggedo, disguised as a Goose, abandons the others and seeks Kiki so he can use the transformations to pursue his plans. The Glass Cat travels from the Emerald City to the Great Forest of Gugu, learns that the Wizard and Dorothy have been transformed, and joins their search for the bag and Kiki. The group decides to search for the Goose first, hoping he can help them find the Magician and recover the bag.

14. The Wizard Learns the Magic Word

Ruggedo, transformed into a Li-Mon-Eag, persuades Kiki Aru to turn fifty monkeys into giant soldiers so they can conquer the Land of Oz. The Wizard of Oz, hidden as a Fox in a hollow tree, overhears Kiki speak the Magic Word and uses it to transform Kiki into a hickory-nut and Ruggedo into a walnut. He then restores himself, Dorothy, and the others to their natural shapes, while the giant soldiers remain in the forest.

15. The Lonesome Duck

Trot and Cap'n Bill remain rooted to the Magic Isle while waiting for the Glass Cat and the Wizard, and Trot discovers that the Magic Flower can produce fruit when she wishes it. They eat fruit but cannot use wishes to free themselves, and the Magic Isle's roots are said to make prisoners grow smaller until nothing remains. A brilliantly colored bird called the Lonesome Duck visits them, explains that it has no family or friends, and refuses to help them escape. The Duck eventually uses an incantation to grow comfortable toadstools for them before swimming away.

16. The Glass Cat Finds the Black Bag

Kiki Aru transforms six monkeys into fifty-foot giant soldiers, but the trees trap them in the forest. Rango the Gray Ape asks the Wizard of Oz to break the enchantment, and the Wizard agrees after Rango permits twelve monkeys to be taken to the Emerald City for Ozma's birthday. The Glass Cat finds the Wizard's lost black bag, allowing him to use his Magic Tools to restore the six soldiers and shrink twelve volunteer monkeys into a golden cage. The Wizard, Dorothy, the Glass Cat, and the caged monkeys then leave for the Emerald City, welcomed by the forest animals as friends.

17. A Remarkable Journey

The Wizard, Dorothy, the Glass Cat, the Cowardly Lion, and their companions travel across the Gillikin Country to rescue Trot and Cap'n Bill from the Magic Isle. They learn that living things rooted on the isle gradually grow smaller and disappear, while the Magic Plant produces many kinds of flowers. The Wizard uses a magic axe to clear a path through a forest, and the travelers reach the river by the Magic Isle. They find Trot and Cap'n Bill rooted on the isle and begin preparing a rescue, though the Wizard does not yet know how to overcome the isle's magic.

18. The Magic of the Wizard

The Wizard tries many spells and tools to free Trot and Cap'n Bill from the Magic Isle, but none succeeds. He and Dorothy cross to the island on the raft, where the prisoners warn that cutting or pulling up their flesh roots would hurt them. Trot and Cap'n Bill continue shrinking, and they fear that they will soon disappear. Trot asks Dorothy to tell Ozma that they were captured while seeking a birthday present for her.

19. Dorothy and the Bumble Bees

Dorothy suggests that the Wizard transform Trot and Cap'n Bill into bumblebees so they can escape the Magic Isle. The transformation succeeds, and although the Hungry Tiger and the Cowardly Lion claim to have eaten the bees, Trot and Cap'n Bill land safely on Dorothy's shoulder and are restored to their normal forms. Cap'n Bill then uses a wooden bark sole to avoid the island's enchantment and retrieves the Magic Flower in its golden flower-pot as a birthday present for Ozma. The Glass Cat receives credit for discovering the flower and leading the others to it.

20. The Monkeys Have Trouble

The Wizard's party prepares to carry the Magic Flower to the Emerald City, using a magically made board secured to the Cowardly Lion's back. During the journey, the Glass Cat repeatedly pulls the monkeys' tails, until the monkeys escape their damaged cage and cover her in blue mud. The Wizard decides the Glass Cat deserves this punishment, though Dorothy helps clean her eyes and ears so she can travel. The Glass Cat later runs away to wash herself and is found clean at the Royal Palace, where she ignores the travelers.

21. The College of Athletic Arts

Dorothy and her friends arrive at the Royal Palace while Ozma hears Professor H. M. Wogglebug, T.E., appeal for punishment of students from the Royal Athletic College. The students rebelled after Professor Wogglebug tried to feed them Square-Meal Tablets instead of ordinary food, and the Senior Class threw him into a river. While Ozma considered the case, Cap'n Bill secretly carried the Magic Flower to Trot's room and the Wizard of Oz moved a cage of monkeys to his tower room. Trot cared for the Magic Flower, while the Wizard trained the intelligent monkeys for Ozma's birthday.

22. Ozma's Birthday Party

Ozma, the beloved fairy Ruler of the Fairyland of Oz, celebrates her birthday with festivities throughout the land and a grand feast at her palace. Her guests include many trusted friends, while her animal friends sit at a separate table. Dorothy presents Ozma with a Surprise Cake containing twelve performing monkeys, and other guests give her remarkable gifts, including the Magic Flower and an emerald gown made by Glinda. Ozma examines and gratefully accepts her birthday gifts after the banquet.

23. The Fountain of Oblivion

Dorothy, the Wizard, and Ozma decide to restore two enchanted nuts to their natural forms beside the Fountain of the Water of Oblivion. The Wizard transforms the hickory-nut into Kiki Aru, who drinks the Water of Oblivion and forgets everything, including his identity and magic. The walnut becomes Ruggedo, who threatens to conquer Oz before drinking the water and losing his memories. Ozma keeps both Kiki Aru and the now-innocent Ruggedo in the Land of Oz so they cannot return to their former wicked ways.
