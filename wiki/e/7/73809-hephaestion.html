Hephaestion

Abstract written by a model

Hephaestion is Alexander’s most intimate and trusted friend, loved above all others, as well as a brave commander and captain of the guard. Taller and more comely than Alexander, he was mistaken for him by Sisygambis when both wore alike. He commands major forces, including three divisions of Alexander’s army, leads troops with Perdiccas into Peucelaotis, besieges and captures Astes’s city after thirty days, and holds authority to restore Tyre’s principality, appointing Ballominus king. He falls ill from a surfeit and dies at Ecbatana. Alexander grieves deeply, sends his body with Perdiccas to Babylon, gives him an immense funeral, and orders sacrifices to him as a demigod.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

But to return to the kings themselves : Darius with all his army

Hephaestion accompanied Alexander on his visit to the queens. Because he and Alexander were dressed alike and Hephaestion was taller and more comely, Sisygambis mistakenly took him for Alexander.

CHAP. IV.

Hephaestion was given authority to restore the kingdom of Tyre to one of Alexander's friends. He offered the principality to a wealthy citizen who declined it and then granted it to Ballominus, a poor man of royal lineage.

CHAP. XI.

Hephaestion, whom Alexander loved above all others, became sick from a surfeit and died at Ecbatana. Alexander was greatly grieved and entrusted his body to Perdiccas to carry to Babylon for an elaborate burial.

CHAP. XII. /

Hephaestion was Alexander’s most intimate friend, whom Alexander distinguished by saying that Craterus was the king’s friend but Hephaestion was Alexander’s friend. After Hephaestion died, Alexander honored him with an immense funeral and ordered sacrifices to him as a demigod.

SUP. XII.

Hephaestion led forces with Perdiccas into Peucelaotis and besieged the city into which Astes had withdrawn. After thirty days he captured the city and Astes was killed.
