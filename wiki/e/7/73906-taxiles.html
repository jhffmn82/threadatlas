Taxiles

Abstract written by a model

Taxiles is presented chiefly as an Indian petty prince and former king beyond the Indus, later called Alexander’s constant, faithful friend and associate. He came with other princes, gave Indian presents, promised twenty-five elephants, accompanied commanders into Peucelaotis, and helped Alexander trust Sangius. The records also call Taxiles the province where Alexander refreshed his army before Porus; after his death Mophis succeeded him and was called Taxiles.

Taxiles is in one work on this site, The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAP. IX.

Taxiles had formerly reigned in the country beyond the Indus, and after his death his son Mophis succeeded him. At the end of the account, Mophis is also called Taxiles and is described as becoming Alexander's constant and faithful friend and associate.

CHAP. X.

Taxiles was the province where Alexander refreshed his army before marching against Porus.

SUP. XII.

Taxiles came to Alexander with other petty princes, brought large presents, and promised to send twenty-five elephants. He accompanied Hephaestion and Perdiccas into Peucelaotis with other commanders and later helped Alexander trust Sangius, who had deserted Astes.
