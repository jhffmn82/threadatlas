Macedonia in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Macedonia is a kingdom whose history in these records moves from vulnerability, through exceptional expansion and repeated struggles over rule, to conquest. It was ruled by Amyntas, who sought Lacedaemonian aid against the Olynthians. At Philip’s accession it lay under the servile yoke of the Illyrians and faced threats from the Pæones, Illyrians, Athenians, and other rivals. Philip restored and enlarged it through victories, captures, wealth, and mercenary recruitment. He defeated allied kings of Thrace, the Paeones, and the Illyrians, used Macedonia as his base for campaigns in Illyria, Thessaly, and Thrace, and made it mistress of many potent cities and countries and the greatest kingdom in Europe. After Philip’s death, Macedonia passed to Alexander. He trained its soldiers, secured it against opposition, returned after gaining recognition in Greece, and assembled forces there for an Asian expedition. Macedonia later faced the prospect of invasion associated with Memnon, while Antipater held the chief command in Europe. After Antipater’s death, it became the center of a succession conflict: Polyperchon became protector of the kings, Cassander sought the chief command, and Olympias, Eurydice, and Alexander’s son figured in changing control. Cassander entered Macedonia, gained support, recovered the passes toward Pydna, and ruled in practice after Olympias’s defeat and condemnation. Threats and claims continued, including Antigonus’s intended seizure of Macedonia and Polyperchon’s attempt to place Hercules on its throne. Cassander, then king of Macedon, allied with Lysimachus against Antigonus. In its final recorded stage, Macedonia was the kingdom of Perseus, its last king. After battles with Perseus, the Romans conquered it; its cities were proclaimed free, and the Macedonians were required to pay half the tribute formerly paid to their kings.

Macedon was under the servile yoke of the Illyrians when Philip came to the crown and was threatened by the Pæones, Illyrians, Athenians, and other rivals. Philip restored and expanded it, making it mistress of many cities and countries and the greatest kingdom in Europe.

CHAP. 11.

Macedonia was strengthened by Philip's victories over the Illyrians, captures of cities, accumulation of wealth, and recruitment of mercenaries. Philip's policies raised the kingdom's power and grandeur.

CHAP. V.

In Macedonia, the kings of Thrace, the Paeones, and the Illyrians allied against Philip because of his growing power. Philip defeated them before they could form their troops and brought them under the Macedonian kingdom.

CHAP. VII.

Macedonia was Philip of Macedon's base. After an initial withdrawal there following defeats by Onomarchus, Philip marched again from Macedonia into Thessaly and eventually defeated the Phocian army.

CHAP. XI.

Macedonia was the base from which Philip invaded the Illyrians and later campaigned in Thessaly and Thrace. Philip's victories increased Macedonian influence over neighboring Greek cities.

BOOK XVII.

Macedonia passed to Alexander after Philip's death. Alexander trained its soldiers, secured the kingdom against internal and external opposition, and returned there after obtaining recognition in Greece.
