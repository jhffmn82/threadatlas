Abstract written by a model

Craterus was a Macedonian commander renowned for good conduct. He led soldiers in Cilicia, commanded the siege of Cyropolis, where he was wounded, and later arrived from Cilicia with reinforcements for Antipater, taking chief command of their combined army. He helped defeat the Greeks, besiege Thessalian cities, establish peace, married Phila, campaigned against the Aetolians, and commanded forces jointly with Antipater. Opposed by Eumenes at the Hellespont, he led the force against him and the right wing in battle. Thrown from his horse, he was trampled unrecognized and killed.

Craterus is in one work on this site, The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAP. II.

Craterus arrived from Cilicia with reinforcements for Antipater and accepted the chief command of the combined army. He helped defeat the Greeks, besiege Thessalian cities, and establish peace, after which Antipater gave him Phila in marriage.

Craterus campaigned with Antipater against the Aetolians with thirty thousand infantry and two thousand five hundred cavalry. His soldiers later endured winter in huts and warm tents while the Aetolians suffered from snow, hunger, and cold.

Craterus took command of the force sent against Eumenes and promised his soldiers the field's pillage if they won. He commanded the right wing, but was thrown from his horse during the battle, trampled without being recognized, and killed.

SUP. III.

Alexander sent Craterus against Cyropolis and ordered him to fortify a camp near the city so that its defenders could not assist other cities. Craterus was wounded by darts and arrows during the fighting in Cyropolis.
