Abstract written by a model

Rebellious slaves in Sicily suddenly unite against masters in large numbers, spreading violence through cities and countryside. Some become armed shepherds and herdsmen; others follow Eunus or Cleon. They devastate settlements, rob travelers and barns, seize herds, kill indiscriminately, and make highways impassable, while masters fear further urban revolt.

BOOK XXXIV.

Many thousands of slaves in Sicily suddenly united against their masters, and their revolt brought cities and the countryside into violence. Some slaves became armed shepherds and herdsmen who robbed travelers, attacked villages, and killed those who resisted; others followed Eunus or Cleon.

BOOK XXXVI.

The rebellious slaves devastated Sicily, robbed towns, villages, barns, and travelers, drove away herds, killed people without distinction between slaves and free persons, and made the highways impassable. Slaves remaining within the cities were feared by their masters because they longed for an opportunity to revolt.
