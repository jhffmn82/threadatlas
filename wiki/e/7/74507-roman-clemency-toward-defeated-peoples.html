Roman clemency toward defeated peoples

topic · 1 work · 1 quoted fact · 2 connections
Also called: Roman clemency, clemency, extraordiuary clemency, great clemency and generosity, humanity, and clemency, mild and merciful

Abstract written by a model

Roman clemency toward defeated kings and peoples is presented as a policy that initially advanced Roman power through mercy. Under noble generals, Romans restored Antiochus and Philip, freed Macedonians, preserved Illyrian laws, and treated Perseus mildly. By making some conquered peoples friends, citizens, kin by marriage, or self-governing communities, they enlarged their dominion; later they mixed clemency with severity and destroyed cities to secure rule.

The Romans were described as merciful toward defeated peoples when led by noble generals. They restored Antiochus and Philip to their kingdoms after defeating them, made them allies, freed the Macedonians, allowed the Illyrians to retain their laws, and treated Perseus more mildly than his conduct deserved.

Roman clemency toward conquered peoples helped enlarge Roman conquests because the Romans treated many conquered people as friends rather than enemies. They made some free of the city, joined others to themselves by marriage, and allowed others to govern under their own laws. Later, after gaining dominion over much of the world, they combined clemency with severity and the destruction of cities to secure their rule.
