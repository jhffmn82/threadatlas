Roman clemency toward defeated kings and peoples is presented as a policy that initially advanced Roman power through mercy. Under noble generals, Romans restored Antiochus and Philip, freed Macedonians, preserved Illyrian laws, and treated Perseus mildly. The Romans were described as merciful toward defeated peoples when led by noble generals. They restored Antiochus and Philip to their kingdoms after defeating them, made them allies, freed the Macedonians, allowed the Illyrians to retain their laws, and treated Perseus more mildly than his conduct deserved.

77* After the Romans had conquered Perseus, (orratherCbaropea),

Roman clemency toward conquered peoples helped enlarge Roman conquests because the Romans treated many conquered people as friends rather than enemies. They made some free of the city, joined others to themselves by marriage, and allowed others to govern under their own laws. Later, after gaining dominion over much of the world, they combined clemency with severity and the destruction of cities to secure their rule.
