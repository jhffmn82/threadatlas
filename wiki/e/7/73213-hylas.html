Hylas

person · 2 works · 8 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: the boy, the son of Theiodamas, Ὕλας

Abstract written by a model

In The Argonautica, Hylas is Theiodamas’s young, brave son and Heracles’s childhood-nurtured companion, bears Heracles’s arrows, guards his bow, is sent to Pegae for water and Heracles’s evening meal, and is seized by a water-nymph who loves and marries him. In The Library, Hylas is the son of Thiodamas and beloved by Hercules, is sent to draw water in Mysia, is abducted by nymphs for his beauty, and is sought by Hercules and Polyphemus.

Hylas in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · 299 to 200 BC · the work’s page · read it where Hylas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hylas, Theiodamas’s young, brave son and Heracles’s childhood-nurtured companion, bears Heracles’s arrows and guards his bow. Sent to Pegae for water and Heracles’s evening meal, he is seized by a water-nymph, who loves and marries him; the Mysians pledge to keep searching.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Now at the hour when from the field some delver or ploughman goes

Hylas carries a bronze pitcher to Pegae to draw water for the evening meal and prepare everything for Heracles’s return. A water-nymph sees him, pulls him into the spring, and makes him her husband according to Glaucus’s account. The Mysians later give pledges that they will continue searching for him.
