Hanno in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Hanno, a Carthaginian general in Africa, was naturally ambitious and glory-seeking despite a private inherited feud with Bomilcar. He sought to train an idle army, reduce city expenses, and gain honorable profitable success. After taking Hecatompylos, he spared the city and estates and took three thousand hostages; at Tunis, he led the right wing and was killed attacking the Grecians.

Hanno was appointed one of the Carthaginian generals despite his feud with Bomilcar. He commanded the right wing, supported by the Sacred Brigade, and was killed while fiercely attacking the Grecians.

BOOK XXIV.

Hanno was ambitious and hoped to use an idle army to train the soldiers, reduce the city's expenses, and win an honorable and profitable success. After taking Hecatompylos, he chose mercy over severity, preserved the city and its citizens' estates, and took three thousand hostages.
