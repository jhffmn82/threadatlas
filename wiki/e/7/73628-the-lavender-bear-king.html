the Lavender Bear King in The Lost Princess of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1917

The Lavender Bear King is the huge light-lavender, stuffed and jointed toy-bear king and powerful sorcerer who rules Bear Center and its forest. He has bright pink eyes, wears a tin crown set with diamonds and amethysts, and carries a short glittering magical metal wand that produces visions and exact images. A wonderful inventor, he considers the Little Pink Bear his best magic and has absolute faith that it never makes a mistake. He arrives to confront Cayke, the Frogman, and other prisoners, questions Cayke and the Frogman, and displays an image of Cayke’s dishpan. Through the Little Pink Bear, he learns that Ugu the Shoemaker has the dishpan. Although he condemns the travelers to death ten years later for trespassing, with no immediate execution intended, he joins Cayke and the Frogman on the journey to Ugu’s castle, protects the united party, and shows them a vision of Ugu. He later permits the Little Pink Bear to be questioned, credits its answers with helping Ozma’s rescuers, accepts Ozma’s invitation, leaves Bear Center to Corporal Waddle, and visits Ozma with the Little Pink Bear.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 15

The Lavender Bear is the powerful sorcerer and king who rules Bear Center. He is a huge lavender toy bear with bright pink eyes, a tin crown set with diamonds and amethysts, and a glittering wand; he arrives to face the prisoners.

CHAPTER 16

The Bear King questions Cayke and the Frogman, magically displays an image of Cayke’s dishpan, and uses the Little Pink Bear to discover that Ugu the Shoemaker has it. He decides to join Cayke and the Frogman, carrying the Little Pink Bear, and formally condemns the travelers to death ten years later for trespassing, though no immediate execution is intended.

CHAPTER 17

The Lavender Bear King asks the Little Pink Bear whether it is safe to join the people from the Emerald City, then leads his party to unite with them. He uses his metal wand to show the travelers a vision of Ugu in his castle.

CHAPTER 24

The Bear King initially refuses to let the little Pink Bear be questioned because he believes the Bear never makes a mistake. He finally agrees, questions the Bear, and ends by triumphantly insisting that it never makes a mistake.

CHAPTER 25

The Bear King explains that the little Pink Bear’s answers helped Ozma’s rescuers and accepts Ozma’s invitation to visit her palace. He leaves his kingdom in Corporal Waddle’s care and brings the little Pink Bear with him.
