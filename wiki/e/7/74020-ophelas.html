Ophelas

Abstract written by a model

Ophelas, a captain of Alexander, led Ptolemy’s forces for the Cyrenian exiles, defeated and captured Thimbron, recovered the towns, and delivered Cyrene and neighboring cities to Ptolemy. He later ruled them, seeking to enlarge his dominions. Promised sovereignty of Africa, he agreed to aid his friend Agathocles against the Carthaginians and crossed a dangerous route with an army. Agathocles accused him of treachery, decoyed and killed him, and took his army.

Ophelas is in one work on this site, The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAP. II.

Ophelas led the forces sent by Ptolemy to assist the Cyrenian exiles. He defeated and captured Thimbron, recovered the towns, and delivered Cyrene and the neighboring cities to Ptolemy.

Having now gone through the affairs af Asia and Greece, we shall

Ophelas, who controlled Cyrene and neighboring cities, agreed to assist Agathocles in subduing the Carthaginians in exchange for the sovereignty of Africa. After leading his army across a difficult and dangerous route, he was unexpectedly accused of treachery by Agathocles and was killed.

Ophelas had been murdered by Agathocles, who then brought Ophelas's army over to himself. The text presents the later loss of Agathocles's two sons and army as divine retribution for the murder.
