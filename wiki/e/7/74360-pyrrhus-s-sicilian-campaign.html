Pyrrhus's Sicilian campaign

event · 2 works · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections
Also called: Pyrrhus’s Sicilian campaign, battle, crossed over into Sicily, engagement, invited into Sicily, war, war in Italy
  • The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian describes Pyrrhus's Sicilian campaign as beginning with his voyage from Tarentum to aid Syracuse against the Carthaginians, gaining support and conquering several places before failing to capture Lilybaeum.
  • Description of Greece describes Pyrrhus's Sicilian campaign as the occasion when Pyrrhus answered an embassy from Syracuse, crossed from Tarentum, compelled the Carthaginians to raise their siege, and then sought to fight them at sea.

Pyrrhus's Sicilian campaign in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

is_a campaign [Pyrrhus's war in Sicily] Pyrrhus's Sicilian campaign began when he sailed from Tarentum to aid Syracuse against the Carthaginians. He gained support from Taurominium, Catana, Syracuse, Agrigentum, and many other cities, conquered Heraclea, Azone, Egesta, Panormus, and other places, and reached the Carthaginian possessions near Lilybaeum. The campaign failed to capture Lilybaeum, after which Pyrrhus planned to obtain naval superiority and invade Africa.

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

During Pyrrhus’s Sicilian campaign, he answered an embassy from Syracuse, crossed from Tarentum, and compelled the Carthaginians to raise their siege. He then became overconfident and sought to fight the Carthaginians at sea.
