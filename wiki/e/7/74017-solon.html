Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Solon is an Athenian and one of the Seven Wise Men, distinguished for wisdom, learning, prudent counsel, and laws for Athens, and honored for good government and turning Athenians from luxurious manners toward virtue and honorable action. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Solon of Athens advised the Amphictyones to consecrate the land around Cirrha to Apollo and devised the plan of diverting the river Plistus and using hellebore to disable the besieged people of Cirrha.

Solon in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Solon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Solon is an Athenian and one of the Seven Wise Men, distinguished for wisdom, learning, prudent counsel, and laws for Athens. Honored for good government, he gradually turns Athenians from luxurious manners toward virtue and honorable action.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

*BOOKVI.

Solon, an Athenian, was highly accomplished in wisdom and learning and was regarded as one of the seven wise men. He was honored for making laws and admired for his prudence in discourse, counsel, and advice; by degrees he turned the Athenians from luxurious and effeminate manners toward virtue and honorable actions.
