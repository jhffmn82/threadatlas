Cleombrotus

person · 3 works · 9 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Cleombrotus,, Cleombro­tus, Oeombrotus, their king Cleombrotus

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Cleombrotus was king of Lacedaemon and the Spartans, urged Sphodrias to seize the Piraeus, led the Lacedaemonians against Thebes, and was killed at Leuctra. In Description of Greece, Cleombrotus was the Spartan king who succeeded Agesipolis, led the Lacedæmonians against the Bœotians at Leuctra, and was killed early in the battle. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Cleombrotus was king of the Lacedæmonians who turned aside to Ambrosus, passed through after killing Chæreas and his men, reached Leuctra, and fell in battle against the Thebans.

Cleombrotus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Cleombrotus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cleombrotus, brother and successor of Agesipolis, was king of Lacedaemon and the Spartans for nine years. He urged Sphodrias to seize the Piraeus without the Ephors' consent, then, as Lacedaemonian general, marched against Thebes. Wounded many times, he was killed at Leuctra; his death helped cause his army's disorder and flight.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAR III.

Cleombrotus succeeded his brother Agesipolis as king of Lacedaemon and reigned for nine years.

Whilst these things were acting in Greece, Acoris^king of Egypt^

Cleombrotus, king of the Lacedaemonians, urged Sphodrias to attempt the surprise seizure of the Piraeus without the Ephors' consent. He was later killed when the Lacedaemonians were defeated at Leuctra.

CHAP. VL

Cleombrotus was appointed general of the Lacedaemonians and led their army toward Thebes. He was killed at Leuctra after receiving many wounds, and his death contributed to the disorder and flight of the Lacedaemonian army.
