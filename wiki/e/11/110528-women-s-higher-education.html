women’s higher education

topic · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: college women, college-bred women, higher education of women, the higher education, the higher education of women, the highest culture of brain and heart, women’s cause, women’s education

Abstract written by a model

In An Astronomer's Wife, women’s higher education is presented as a pursuit of knowledge and virtue for women as well as men, grounded in women’s equal senses, consciousness, perception, and intellectual capacities; Angeline Hall vindicates it by earning a Bachelor of Arts while sustaining family life and sending her four sons through Harvard College. In Dr. Elsie Inglis, women’s higher education is described as still being in its early stages, with each advance fought for against obscurantism and professional jealousy, while university commissions’ supportive reports were often undermined by those seeking to exclude women from the medical profession.

An Astronomer's Wife

Women’s higher education is presented as something Angeline Hall’s life vindicates. She earned a Bachelor of Arts and nevertheless had four sons, all of whom she sent through Harvard College.

------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER XV.

The unit argues that women are naturally formed to pursue knowledge and virtue because they possess the same senses, consciousness, perception, and intellectual capacities as men. Angeline Hall was described as a pioneer among college women, and the ideal college woman was said to correspond closely to her balanced ideal of intellectual and domestic life.
