Lepreus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Lepreus was a city in Triphylia, long subject to Elis though its people claimed Arcadian belonging. Its name was variously linked to Lepreus or Leprea, children of Pyrgeus, or early leprosy. It had a spring and, later, a temple of Demeter amid remembered lost shrines and tombs.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER V.

Lepreus was a city in Triphylia whose people claimed to belong properly to Arcadia, though they had long been subject to Elis. Its name was variously attributed to Lepreus, son of Pyrgeus; Leprea, daughter of Pyrgeus; or the leprosy of its early inhabitants. In Pausanias’s time it contained a temple of Demeter but no remarkable tomb or other temple, although its people remembered former shrines and tombs.
