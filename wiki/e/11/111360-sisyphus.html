Sisyphus

person · 2 works · 8 quoted facts · 9 connections
Also called: Sisyphus the son of Æolus, Σίσυφον, Σίσυφος

Abstract written by a model

In The Library, Sisyphus founds Ephyra, later called Corinth, marries Merope, has Glaucus, reveals Zeus's secret abduction of Aegina to Asopus, and is punished in Hades by repeatedly rolling a stone upward only for it to rebound. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Sisyphus is identified as the son of Æolus and is shown laboriously rolling a stone up a hill.

IX. Τῶν δὲ Αἰόλον παίδων ᾿Αθάμας, Βοιωτίας

Sisyphus, son of Aeolus, founds Ephyra, later called Corinth, and has Glaucus by Merope. In Hades, he is punished by repeatedly rolling a stone toward the top, where it rebounds backward. He suffers this punishment after telling Asopus that Zeus had secretly carried off Aegina.
