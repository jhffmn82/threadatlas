Aristomenes

person · 1 work · 27 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Aristomenes himself, Aristomenes of Messene, Aristomenes the Messenian hero, he, his, the hero Aristomenes, this hero

Abstract written by a model

Aristomenes is presented as a Messenian hero, leader, king, and commander in chief in the second battle between the Messenians and Lacedæmonians. Said to be descended from the Æpytidæ and son of Nicomedes, he first brought honor to Messene. After the battle of Deræ, young leaders promoted resistance to Spartan rule and the Messenians elected him king. He fought boldly: he displayed a shield at Athene Chalciœcus, routed Lacedæmonian forces, raided Pharis, escaped Ceadas, defeated Corinthian auxiliaries, and offered Hecatomphonia after killing one hundred enemies. Captured by Cretan bowmen, he was saved by a Messenian maiden. During the siege of Eira, he led raids, rescued a merchant, defended and encouraged the Messenians, and was wounded. Though an oracle had shown him their cause was doomed, he organized the escape of the people after Theoclus died. His later plan to capture Sparta with 500 Messenians failed through Aristocrates' betrayal. He buried Messene's sacred records on Mount Ithome for its future recovery and declined leadership of a new colony. He arranged marriages for his sister Agnagora and two daughters, consulted Delphi, and died of illness at Rhodes while intending to seek vengeance. Damagetus honored him as the noblest Greek of his time and received a monument for him; Messene claimed his tomb. Messenians also said he aided the Thebans at Leuctra, while his shield was displayed there and later returned to Trophonius' temple.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

Aristomenes is presented as the Messenian hero who first brought honour to the name of Messene. Pausanias says he belonged to the second Messenian war and will discuss his exploits later.

CHAPTER XI.

Aristomenes was among the young Messenian leaders who promoted a general rising against Spartan rule. After the undecided battle of Deræ, he was elected king and appointed commander in chief, and he later went by night to Sparta and hung a shield at the temple of Athene Chalciœcus, claiming it was taken from Spartan spoils.

CHAPTER XVI.

Aristomenes routs Anaxander's division and then defeats the wider Lacedæmonian force, though he loses his shield near a wild pear tree. He raids Pharis, escapes from Ceadas by following a fox, defeats Corinthian auxiliaries, and is later freed from Cretan bowmen by a young woman after being captured. During the siege of Eira he leads raids and rescues a Cephallenian merchant, but is wounded shortly before the guard abandons the citadel.

CHAPTER XXI.

Aristomenes defended Eira, encouraged the Messenians during the fighting, and knew from the oracle about the he-goat and the wild fig tree that their cause was doomed. After Theoclus died, he organized the retreat of the Messenians, women, and children, led by Gorgus and Manticlus. At Mount Lycæus he planned to capture Sparta with 500 Messenians, but Aristocrates betrayed the plan.

CHAPTER XXIV.

Aristomenes refused leadership of those going to the new colony, arranged marriages for his sister and daughters, and consulted the oracle at Delphi. He died of illness in Rhodes before he could travel to Sardis and Ecbatana to seek vengeance against the Lacedæmonians, and the Rhodians built a monument to him and honored his memory.

CHAPTER XXIX.

Aristomenes was said by the Messenians to have fought for the Thebans at Leuctra and to have been a principal cause of the Lacedæmonian defeat. His shield, preserved at the temple of Trophonius, was displayed by the Thebans before the battle and returned there afterward; Messene also had his tomb and a bronze statue.
