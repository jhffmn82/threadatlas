Corycian cavern

place · 1 work · 8 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: it, the Corycian cavern

Abstract written by a model

The Corycian cavern, on Mount Parnassus, is named after the Nymph Corycia and is considered more remarkable than the other described caverns. Much of it can be traversed without torches; it has a high roof, springs, bubbling water, and roof-drip. Its inhabitants hold it sacred to Pan and the Corycian Nymphs.

Corycian cavern is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XXXII.

The Corycian cavern is named after the Nymph Corycia and is considered more remarkable than the other caverns described. Much of it can be traversed without torches; it has a high roof, springs, and water dripping from the roof, and the people of Mount Parnassus consider it sacred to Pan and the Corycian Nymphs.
