Ambrosus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Ambrosus, in Phocis beneath Mount Parnassus opposite Delphi, is traditionally named for the hero Ambrosus. During war against Philip and the Macedonians, Thebans built double walls; it has a small market-place of mostly broken statues and a downhill road to Anticyra.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXVI.

Ambrosus lies beneath Mount Parnassus and opposite Delphi, and is said to have been named after the hero Ambrosus. During the war against Philip and the Macedonians, the Thebans built double walls around it. It has a small market-place with mostly broken stone statues, and the road from it leads downhill toward Anticyra.
