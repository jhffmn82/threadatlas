Abstract written by a model

The statue of Apollo at Amyclæ is an ancient, inartistic work, not made by Bathycles, conjectured to be about 30 cubits high. Like a brazen pillar except for face, toes, and hands, it wears a helmet, holds lance and bow, and stands on Hyacinthus’s tomb-like altar base.

CHAPTER XIX.

The statue of Apollo at Amyclæ is an ancient and inartistic work, not made by Bathycles, and is conjectured to be about 30 cubits high. Except for its face, toes, and hands, it resembles a brazen pillar; it wears a helmet and holds a lance and bow. Its base resembles an altar and is said to be the tomb of Hyacinthus.
