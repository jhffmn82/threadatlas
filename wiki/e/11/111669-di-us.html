Diæus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Diæus is an Achæan general and leader whose actions shape the Achæans’ conflict with the Lacedæmonians and Rome. He first supports Menalcidas after accepting three talents, seeks Menalcidas’s acquittal, and deceives the Achæans about the Roman Senate’s decisions concerning Sparta. As successor to Damocritus, he agrees to await Roman arbitrators after Metellus’s second message, while winning over towns near Sparta and placing Achæan garrisons there. After being summoned before Orestes and later succeeded by Critolaus, he is again appointed commander-in-chief. He manumits slaves and raises an Achæan and Arcadian army, but, despite knowing of Critolaus’s defeat, sends only 4,000 men to Megara. After the Achæans’ defeat, he flees toward Megalopolis rather than defend Corinth, kills his wife to prevent her capture, and dies by poison.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XII.

Diæus, Menalcidas's successor as General of the Achæans, accepted three talents from Menalcidas and worked to secure his acquittal. He falsely told the Achæans that the Roman Senate allowed them to sentence a Spartan to death, opposed referring the matter back to Rome, and later persuaded the Achæans that the Lacedæmonians had been ordered to obey them in all things.

CHAPTER XIII.

Diæus succeeded Damocritus as General of the Achæans and agreed, after a second message from Metellus, to wait for the Roman arbitrators rather than continue the war openly. He then won over the towns around Sparta and placed Achæan garrisons in them.

CHAPTER XIV.

Diæus was summoned before Orestes with the principal Achæans. After serving his time as General of the Achæans, he was succeeded by Critolaus.

CHAPTER XV.

Diæus was again made commander-in-chief of the Achæan army, freed slaves for military service, and levied Achæans and Arcadians. Despite knowing that Critolaus and the Achæan army had been defeated, he sent only 4,000 men under Alcamenes to Megara; his folly later frustrated Metellus’ plan for peace.

CHAPTER XVI.

Diæus fled toward Megalopolis as soon as the Achæans were defeated instead of defending Corinth. He announced the impending ruin of Megalopolis, killed his wife to prevent her capture, and took poison.
