Philopœmen in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Philopœmen, son of Craugis, was an Arcadian warrior, Achæan general, cavalry leader, and ultimately celebrated public benefactor of Greece. Raised under Cleander after his father’s death, he was exceptionally strong though not handsome, farmed his own land, hunted, and studied sophists and military strategy. He modeled his aims on Epaminondas but was hot-tempered and avoided athletic competition. When Cleomenes captured Megalopolis, Philopœmen led roughly two-thirds of its lads and grown men, along with its women and children, safely to Messene. He resisted an unarmed return, fought in the cavalry at Sellasia despite spear wounds through both thighs, then served as a mercenary captain in Crete. Returning home, he became commander of the Achæan cavalry and made it the best in Greece, defeating enemy cavalry and killing Demophantus. As Achæan commander in chief and later general again, he reformed the infantry’s arms and equipment, commanded at Mantinea, defeated the Lacedæmonians and slew Machanidas, defended Megara, opposed Nabis, and repeatedly aided the Gortynians in Crete. He compelled the Lacedæmonians to join the Achæan League, acted forcefully at Sparta, reorganized it through exile, sale of Helots, demolition of walls, and altered youth training, and opposed concessions to the Romans. Honoured highly by Greeks, he was remembered as wise in counsel, brave in the field, above money, victorious over two Spartan tyrants, and an abolisher of slavery. In old age, feverish and over seventy, he went with Lycortas into Messenia, was wounded, captured, and killed by poison on Dinocrates’ orders; his remains were returned to Megalopolis.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IX.

Philopœmen is mentioned as a person with whom Lycortas had friendly relations and upon whom Lycortas relied. The text also refers to Messenians involved in Philopœmen’s death.

CHAPTER XXVII.

Philopœmen the son of Craugis saved about two-thirds of the lads and grown men of Megalopolis when Cleomenes captured the city. He fled with them and the women to Messenia.

CHAPTER XLIX.

Philopœmen was the son of Craugis and was raised under the guardianship of Cleander after his father’s death. He was strong, devoted to farming, hunting, reading works on sophistry and strategy, and modeling himself on Epaminondas, though he was hot-tempered rather than mild. After Cleomenes captured Megalopolis, he safely led two-thirds of the adult citizens and all the women and children to Messene, persuaded the exiles to return with arms, fought bravely at Sellasia despite being pierced through both thighs, became a mercenary captain in Crete, and later made the Achæan cavalry the best in Greece.

CHAPTER L.

Philopœmen persuaded the Achæans to replace their short spears and oblong shields with breastplates, greaves, Argolic shields, and long spears. As commander in chief, he defeated the Lacedæmonians at Mantinea and slew Machanidas, later defended Megara, and compelled Nabis to withdraw from Messene. He went twice to Crete to help the Gortynians, fought Nabis near Gythium, and raised his fame by defeating the Lacedæmonians in a night attack.

CHAPTER LI.

Philopœmen compelled the Lacedæmonians to join the Achæan League and, although a private individual, shut the gates of Sparta against Titus Flaminius and Diophanes. He became general of the Achæans again, reorganized Sparta by exiling leaders, selling Helots, demolishing the walls, and changing the youths’ training, and later opposed concessions to the Romans. While suffering from a fever and aged more than 70, he joined Lycortas in Messenia, was wounded and captured, and was killed by poison on Dinocrates’ orders; his remains were taken to Megalopolis.

CHAPTER LII.

Philopœmen, son of Craugis, is identified as the last public benefactor of Greece. He is praised in an inscription at Tegea as a wise counselor, brave warrior, distinguished cavalry leader, victor over two Spartan tyrants, and abolisher of slavery.
