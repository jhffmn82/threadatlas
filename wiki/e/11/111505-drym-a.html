Drymæa in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Drymæa, originally called Nauboles, lies beyond Tithronium and from Amphiclea. Local tradition names Phocus the son of Æacus as founder. Its chief feature is an ancient temple to Law-giving Demeter, with a stone statue honored annually at the Thesmophoria.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXIII.

Drymæa lies about 20 stades beyond Tithronium and about 80 stades from Amphiclea according to Herodotus. It was originally called Nauboles and was said to have been founded by Phocus the son of Æacus; it contained an ancient temple to Law-giving Demeter, whose erect stone statue was honored at an annual Thesmophoria.
