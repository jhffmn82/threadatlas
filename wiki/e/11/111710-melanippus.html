Melanippus

person · 2 works · 8 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Melanippus and Comætho, Μελανίππου

Abstract written by a model

In The Library, Melanippus, son of Astacus, wounded Tydeus in the belly before Amphiaraus cut off his head and gave it to Tydeus. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Melanippus was a Theban warrior who loved Comætho, violated Artemis Triclaria's temple, and killed Tydeus and Mecisteus before Amphiaraus slew him.

Melanippus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Melanippus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Melanippus, a Theban warrior of exceptional beauty and ability, deeply and mutually loved Comætho. Their violation of Artemis Triclaria's temple brought famine, sickness, mortality, and an oracle demanding annual human sacrifices. In the Argive siege of Thebes, he killed Tydeus and Mecisteus before Amphiaraus reportedly slew him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIX.

Melanippus was deeply in love with Comætho and surpassed his peers in other respects and in beauty. He and Comætho violated the temple of Artemis Triclaria, after which the country suffered famine, sickness, and increased mortality, and an oracle ordered annual human sacrifices.

CHAPTER XVIII.

Melanippus is described as one of the greatest warriors of the Thebans. During the Argive siege of Thebes, he slew Tydeus and Mecisteus, and Amphiaraus is said to have slain him.
