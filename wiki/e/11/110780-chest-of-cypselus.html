chest of Cypselus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The chest of Cypselus is a cedar votive offering decorated with ivory, gold, and carved cedar figures. It bears old Greek inscriptions, including ox-fashion writing, that identify or describe mythic scenes. From Pelops, Pelias, Hercules, and the Harpies, its imagery continues through Death and Sleep, Jason and Medea, Atlas, Achilles, and Memnon; its maker remains uncertain, though Eumelus the Corinthian is suspected.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XV.

The chest of Cypselus is made of cedar and decorated with figures in ivory, gold, and carved cedar. It bears old inscriptions, including writing in the ox-fashion style, and depicts mythological scenes such as the pursuit of Pelops, the games for Pelias, Hercules shooting the hydra, and the sons of Boreas driving away the Harpies.

CHAPTER XVIII.

The chest bears scenes from myth, including Death and Sleep, Justice and Injustice, Helen and Menelaus, Jason and Medea, Atlas and Hercules, Achilles and Memnon, and many other figures. Its inscriptions identify or describe several scenes, and its maker is considered impossible to determine, though suspicion points to Eumelus the Corinthian.
