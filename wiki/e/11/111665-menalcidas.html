Menalcidas

person · 1 work · 14 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: General of the Achæans, a Lacedæmonian by birth

Abstract written by a model

Menalcidas, a Lacedæmonian by birth, served first as General of the Achæans and later as General of the Lacedæmonians. He promised Callicrates half of the Oropian money for help securing Achaean assistance, received the bribe, and then deprived Callicrates of his share. When prosecuted, he bribed Diæus to obtain acquittal, later quarrelled bitterly with him before the Senate, and falsely described the Roman decision to the Lacedæmonians. Chosen against Diæus, he persuaded the Lacedæmonians to violate the truce, captured and sacked Iasus, and stirred renewed conflict with the Achæans. Accused by the citizens, he killed himself by poison. He is described as unscrupulous, treacherous, imprudent, and iniquitous.

Menalcidas is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XI.

Menalcidas was a Lacedæmonian by birth and was serving as General of the Achæans. He promised Callicrates half of the ten talents offered by the Oropians if he would help secure Achaean assistance, and he later tried to persuade the Achaean army to invade Attica.

CHAPTER XII.

Menalcidas received the money promised by the Oropians but tried to keep Callicrates from receiving his share and ultimately deprived him of it. Callicrates prosecuted him before the Achæans, so Menalcidas bribed Diæus with three talents from Oropus to obtain his acquittal. He later disputed bitterly with Diæus before the Roman Senate and deceived the Lacedæmonians about the Senate's decision.

CHAPTER XIII.

Menalcidas was chosen General of the Lacedæmonians and persuaded them to violate the truce because they lacked supplies and money and their land had been uncultivated. He captured and sacked Iasus, was accused by the citizens after renewing the conflict, and committed suicide by poison.
