Methydrium

Also called: village Methydrium

Abstract written by a model

Methydrium, formerly a town and now a village included in Megalopolis, lies 170 stades from Megalopolis and 137 from Tricoloni, reached by an inland Arcadian road near Anemosa and Phalanthum. Orchomenus built it on a high hill between the Malœtas and Mylaon. Before inclusion, its inhabitants won at Olympia; it has Poseidon Hippius’s temple, with Thaumasium and Rhea’s Cave above the Malœtas.

Methydrium is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXV.

Methydrium is 170 stades from Megalopolis and is reached by a road into the interior of Arcadia. A high road toward Methydrium passes near Anemosa and the mountain Phalanthum.

CHAPTER XXXVI.

Methydrium is 137 stades from Tricoloni and was built by Orchomenus on a high hill between the rivers Malœtas and Mylaon. Before it was included in Megalopolis, its inhabitants were victors at Olympia. It contains a temple of Poseidon Hippius near the Mylaon, and Mount Thaumasium and Rhea’s Cave lie above the Malœtas.
