Thyiades

group · 1 work · 5 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: the Thyiades

Abstract written by a model

The Thyiades are Athenian women named after Thyia, who annually travel with Delphian women to Parnassus to celebrate Dionysus. Along the way and at places including Panopeus, they hold dances; on Mount Parnassus they conduct mad revels honoring Dionysus and Apollo.

Thyiades is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER IV.

The Thyiades are Athenian women who annually go to Parnassus with the Delphian women to celebrate the orgies of Dionysus. They hold dances on the road from Athens and elsewhere, including Panopeus, which explains Homer’s description of Panopeus as delighting in the dance.
