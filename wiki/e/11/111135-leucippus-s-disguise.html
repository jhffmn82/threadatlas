Leucippus's disguise

object · 1 work · 2 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: his hair arranged like a girl’s, woman’s dress

Abstract written by a model

Leucippus’s disguise as Œnomaus’s daughter used grown hair and women’s dress to appear a girl, join Daphne’s hunt, and gain acceptance through appearance, status, skill, and attention.

Leucippus's disguise is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XX.

Leucippus let his hair grow to the Alpheus, wore women's dress, arranged his hair like a girl's, and claimed to be Œnomaus's daughter. He joined Daphne in hunting and was accepted as a girl because of his appearance, family distinction, hunting skill, and attention to her.
