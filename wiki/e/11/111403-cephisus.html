Abstract written by a model

In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Cephisus is a river rising at Lilæa in Phocis and flowing into Lake Cephisis; the Thebans said Hercules diverted it into the Orchomenian plain and blocked its sea outlet. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Cephisus is a roaring current that rises at Lilæa, flows powerfully near Ledon, especially at midday, and nourishes land described as Phocis’s best for planting, sowing, and pasture.

CHAPTER XXIV.

The river Cephisus rises at Lilæa in Phocis and has its outlet into Lake Cephisis.

CHAPTER XXXVIII.

The river Cephisus was said by the Thebans to have been diverted by Hercules into the Orchomenian plain. Its former outlet to the sea under the mountain was allegedly blocked by Hercules.

CHAPTER XXXIII.

The settlement called Ledon stood near the Cephisus, 40 stades from the ruins of the former town. The Cephisus rises at Lilæa and initially flows with such force that, especially at midday, it roars like a bull; the land near it is described as the best in Phocis for planting, sowing, and pasture.
