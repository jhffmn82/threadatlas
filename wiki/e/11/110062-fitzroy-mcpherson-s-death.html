Abstract written by a model

Fitzroy McPherson’s death occurs at the beach and bathing-pool after he staggers up from the beach, cries out about the Lion’s Mane, and collapses. Severe, patterned injuries and intense suffering make it seem extraordinary and possibly criminal; after an inconclusive inquest, Sherlock Holmes identifies Cyanea capillata as the cause rather than human assault.

THE ADVENTURE OF THE LION'S MANE

Fitzroy McPherson’s death occurs after he staggers up from the beach, cries out about the Lion’s Mane, and collapses. His death is treated as an extraordinary and apparently criminal event because his body shows signs of intense suffering and violent injury.

Fitzroy McPherson's death is initially treated as a possible murder because of the severe, patterned injuries on his body. Sherlock Holmes determines that the injuries and his last words are consistent with an attack by Cyanea capillata rather than a human assault.
