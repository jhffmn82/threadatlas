Codrus

person · 1 work · 5 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Codrus the son of Melanthus

Abstract written by a model

Codrus, son of Melanthus, was king when the Peloponnesians came to Athens. He fought for his own nation and devoted himself for Athens in accordance with the oracle at Delphi; after his death, the Peloponnesians no longer expected victory. He fathered Medon, Nileus, Androclus, and other sons, and became ancestor of leaders, settlers, and rulers connected with the Ionian cities.

Codrus is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

Codrus was the father of Medon, Nileus, Androclus, and other sons who took part in colonies or expeditions. His sons were described as having Messenian ancestry through Codrus and Melanthus and Athenian ancestry on their mother's side.

CHAPTER III.

Codrus was the ancestor of leaders and settlers connected with the Ionian cities. His descendants Damasichthon and Promethus ruled the Ionians, while Apœcus, Cleopus, Andræmon, Damasus, and Naoclus were also described as descendants of Codrus or associated with his line.

CHAPTER XXV.

Codrus, the son of Melanthus, was king when the Peloponnesians came to Athens. He devoted himself in accordance with the oracle at Delphi, and after his death the Peloponnesians no longer expected victory.
