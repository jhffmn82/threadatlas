Pelasgus

Abstract written by a model

In The Library, Pelasgus was the father of Lycaon, said by Acusilaus to be the son of Zeus and Niobe and by Hesiod to have been born from the soil. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Pelasgus was the first settler and king of the Arcadians, protecting and teaching his people.

Pelasgus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Pelasgus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pelasgus, first settler and king of the Arcadians, was esteemed for unmatched strength, beauty, and judgment. He protected people by devising huts and pig-hide garments, taught them to avoid unsuitable plants, and identified edible fruit-bearing trees, especially acorns. During his reign, Arcadia was said to be called Pelasgia.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK VIII.--ARCADIA. / CHAPTER I.

Pelasgus is said by the Arcadians to have been the first settler of their land and became their king because of his strength, beauty, and judgment. He introduced huts, garments made from pig hides, and instruction about edible plants and fruits, especially acorns. During his reign, Arcadia was said to have been called Pelasgia.
