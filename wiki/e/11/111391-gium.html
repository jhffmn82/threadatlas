Ægium in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Ægium is an Achaean town, 60 stades by sea from Erineus and about 40 fewer by land, watered by Phœnix and Miganitas and near Selinus. After Helice was destroyed, it occupied Helice’s territory. Its market-place holds temples of Apollo and Artemis, a tomb of Talthybius, and funeral rites in his honour; its sea-shore and precincts contain further temples, statues, and sacred enclosures. Its cults include Ilithyia, Athene, Hera, Dionysus, Aphrodite, Poseidon, Proserpine, Zeus Homagyrius, Pan-Achæan Demeter, Safety, and Æsculapius. It still hosts the Achæans’ general meeting.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIII.

Ægium had temples to Ilithyia, Athene, and Hera, a temple and statue of beardless Dionysus, and sacred precincts of Zeus Soter. It also contained a sacred enclosure of Æsculapius, a portico for the athlete Strato, and statues called the Argive gods.

CHAPTER XXIV.

Ægium has temples to Apollo, Artemis, Aphrodite, Poseidon, Proserpine, Zeus Homagyrius, Pan-Achæan Demeter, and Safety, as well as statues of several gods. It is still the place where the general meeting of the Achæans is held.
