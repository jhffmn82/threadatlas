Phigalia

place · 1 work · 11 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Phigalian, native town, town

Abstract written by a model

Phigalia is a town, originally founded by Phigalus and for a time called Phialia after Phialus, son of Bucolion. It stands on a mostly precipitous hill, with rock-built walls and a gentle approach, between Cotilius and the projecting mountain Elaion, near Lymax and Neda. It was captured by the Lacedæmonians and later recovered by its people. Its market-place held the stone statue of Arrhachion, whose inscription had been erased by time; its temples include Artemis the Preserver and Dionysus Acratophorus, and it has a gymnasium. Nearby are hot baths, Bassae, and the temple of Apollo the Helper. Its people neglected and then restored Black Demeter’s honours after a dearth.

Phigalia is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Phigalia in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXIX.

Phigalia lies on a hill that is mostly precipitous, with walls built on the rocks and a gentle ascent to the town. It was attacked and captured by the Lacedæmonians, but the Phigalians later recovered it. The town contains a temple of Artemis the Preserver, a gymnasium with a statue of Hermes, and a temple of Dionysus Acratophorus.

CHAPTER XL.

Phigalia has the stone statue of Arrhachion in its market-place. Its people said that the statue once had an inscription, but time had obliterated it.

CHAPTER XLI.

Phigalia lies near the rivers Lymax and Neda and is surrounded on the left by Cotilius and on the right by the projecting mountain Elaion. About 12 stades from Phigalia are hot baths, and about 40 stades away on Cotilius is Bassae and the temple of Apollo the Helper.

CHAPTER XLII.

Phigalia is associated with traditions about the amour of Poseidon with Demeter and the worship of Black Demeter. Its people neglected Demeter’s festivals and sacrifices after her earlier statue was destroyed, suffered a dearth, and later restored her honours and sacrifices.
