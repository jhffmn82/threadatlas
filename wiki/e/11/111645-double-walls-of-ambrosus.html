double walls of Ambrosus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The double walls of Ambrosus are a fortification surrounding Ambrosus, built by the Thebans during the war against Philip and the Macedonians. Made of the district’s black, very strong stone for immediate defence, they consist of two plain walls a fathom apart, without towers, battlements, or embellishment; where intact, each is 2½ fathoms high.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXVI.

The Thebans built the double walls during the war against Philip and the Macedonians. They were made of the district's black, very strong stone; each wall had a circumference of little less than a fathom and stood 2½ fathoms high where intact, with a fathom between the walls. Built for immediate defence, they had no towers, battlements, or other embellishment.
