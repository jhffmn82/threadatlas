Panopeus

Also called: ancient enclosure of Panopeus, town

Abstract written by a model

Panopeus is an ancient town in Phocis, about twenty stades from Chæronea. Though renowned and represented on the Phocian Council, it lacks major civic amenities; its people live in narrow dwellings by a ravine within an ancient enclosure. Accounts connect it with Epeus’s father or Phlegyans from Orchomenia. Its enclosure served against the Bœotians, and Thyiades’ dances may explain Homer’s name for it. It was later taken and razed.

Panopeus is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

CHAPTER IV.

Panopeus is a town in Phocis with no town-hall, gymnasium, theatre, market-place, or public fountain; its inhabitants live in narrow dwellings near a ravine, but it has boundaries and sends members to the Phocian Council. It is said to have been named after the father of Epeus, while another tradition says its inhabitants were Phlegyans who fled from Orchomenia. Its ancient enclosure is estimated at about seven stades, and the Thyiades hold dances there.
