Metellus

person · 1 work · 12 quoted facts · 8 connections

Abstract written by a model

Metellus is a Roman commander whose actions shape Rome’s conflict with the Achæans and campaigns in Macedonia. He and colleagues first urge kinder treatment of the Lacedæmonians; after the Achæans refuse without a Senate decree, he returns to Rome and reports against them, including untrue claims. He leads the Roman army against Andriscus in Macedonia, ends that war quickly, and orders envoys to restrain Achæan war against Sparta. He later proclaims terms inviting cities to leave the Achæan League, defeats opposing forces, occupies Thebes and Megara, protects Thebes, and offers peace and harmony before being sent to Macedonia after Mummius takes command.

Metellus is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IX.

Metellus and his colleagues urged the Achæans to treat the Lacedæmonians more kindly. When the Achæans refused to hear them without a decree from the Roman Senate, Metellus returned to Rome and reported many things against the Achæans, some of which were untrue.

CHAPTER XIII.

Metellus led the Roman army against Andriscus in Macedonia and finished the war with great despatch. He ordered Roman envoys to forbid the Achæans to attack Sparta and to tell them to await the Roman arbitrators.

CHAPTER XV.

Metellus sent a proclamation inviting the Lacedæmonians and other named cities to leave the Achæan League and promising that the Romans would not remain angry over earlier disobedience. He defeated Critolaus’ forces near Scarphea, defeated the Arcadians at Chæronea, occupied Thebes and Megara, and invited the Achæans to peace and harmony.
