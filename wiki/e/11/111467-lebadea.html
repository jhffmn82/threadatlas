Lebadea in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Lebadea, a town nearest the Orchomenians in the plain, began as high-ground Midea. After Lebadus came from Athens and settled there, its inhabitants descended and renamed it. Adorned like famous Greek towns, it contains Trophonius's oracle, once unfound by Bœotian messengers, and a Dædalus work.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXIX.

Lebadea was originally built on high ground and called Midea, but its inhabitants later descended to the plain after Lebadus came from Athens and settled there. The town was named Lebadea after him and is said to be adorned like the most famous Greek towns.

CHAPTER XL.

At Lebadea, the Bœotian messengers initially could not find the oracle of Trophonius. The place is also associated with a work of Dædalus representing Trophonius.

Lebadea in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855
