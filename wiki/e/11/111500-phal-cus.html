Phalæcus

person · 1 work · 7 quoted facts · 3 connections

Abstract written by a model

Phalæcus, son of Phayllus, gained chief power in Phocis after his father died. He was deposed for privately taking sacred money, then went to Crete with supporters and mercenaries. Besieging Cydonia for money, he lost most of his army and died.

Phalæcus is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER II.

After Phayllus died, power in Phocis passed to his son Phalæcus. Phalæcus was deposed for privately taking sacred money, then went to Crete with his supporters and some mercenaries; while besieging Cydonia, he lost most of his army and his own life.
