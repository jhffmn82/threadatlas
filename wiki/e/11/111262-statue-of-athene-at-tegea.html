statue of Athene at Tegea in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The statue of Athene at Tegea, called Hippia by the Manthurii and Alea by other Greeks and Peloponnesians, was taken from the Manthurii; Æsculapius and Hygiea stand beside it, made by Scopas in Pentelican marble.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XLVII.

The statue of Athene at Tegea is called Hippia by the Manthurii and Alea by other Greeks and Peloponnesians, and it was taken from the Manthurii. Æsculapius stands on one side of it and Hygiea on the other, both made by the Parian Scopas in Pentelican marble.
