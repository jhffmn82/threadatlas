pancratium

event · 1 work · 4 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: pancratiast, the pancratium

Abstract written by a model

The pancratium was an Olympic athletic contest combining boxing and wrestling. Introduced at Olympia in the eighth Olympiad after the 25th, it was initially scheduled after horse-races before the programme changed. A boys' event was added in the 145th Olympiad. Lygdamis, Phædimus, Polydamas, Artemidorus, Clitomachus, Caprus, and others won or contested it.

CHAPTER VIII.

The pancratium was introduced at Olympia in the eighth Olympiad after the 25th, when Lygdamis of Syracuse defeated the competitors. A pancratium for boys was added in the 145th Olympiad, and Phædimus of the Troad won it.

CHAPTER V.

The pancratium had several competitors with notable victories, including Polydamas, who gained crowns in the event.

CHAPTER XIII.

The pancratium appears as both a boys' and men's event. Artemidorus, Clitomachus, and Caprus are especially noted for victories or contests in it, while Aristophon of Athens, Theagenes of Thasos, and other athletes are also associated with pancratium victories.
