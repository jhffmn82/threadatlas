brazen statue of Athene in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The brazen statue of Athene, made by Phidias from Persian spoil taken at Marathon, bore Mys's shield carvings of Lapiths battling Centaurs, outlined by Parrhasius; sailors approaching Athens could see its spearpoint and helmet plume from Sunium.

CHAPTER XXVIII.

The brazen statue of Athene was made by Phidias from spoil taken from the Persians who landed at Marathon. Its shield depicted the battle of the Lapithæ with the Centaurs and other scenes, and its spearpoint and helmet plume could be seen from Sunium by sailors approaching Athens.

Athene Promachos in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Athene Promachos is a nine-foot statue by Pheidias from his youth, representing Athene standing protectively toward Athens with a spear and buckler. It was dignified and noble, though its stiff tunic folds and cold demeanor retained earlier conventions. No copy survives; its appearance is reconstructed largely from Athenian coins.

The Athene Promachos was a work of Pheidias’s youth, representing Athene standing and gazing toward Athens protectingly, with a spear in one hand and a buckler supported by the other. It was nine feet high and dignified and noble, but its stiff tunic folds and cold demeanor retained conventions of the earlier school. No copy survives, so its appearance is reconstructed largely from hints on Athenian coins.
