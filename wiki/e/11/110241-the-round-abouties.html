the Round-abouties in The Giant Horse of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1928

The Round-abouties are pudgy little people living in a round house, with rubber-soled, tennis-ball-like hands and feet. They continually circle through opposing moving, singing circles, draw Trot, the Scarecrow, and Benny onto a merry-go-round, then chase them up tower stairs.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 13

The Round-abouties are pudgy, round people who continually move in circles, with spinning heads, round limbs, and tennis-ball-like hands and feet. They draw Trot, the Scarecrow, and Benny through several revolving and singing circles and onto the merry-go-round. After the ring leader orders them to keep the travelers going around, they chase the travelers up the tower stairs.
