Professor Presbury in The Case-Book of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1921-10

Professor Presbury is a sixty-one-year-old, wealthy, widowed Camford physiologist, scientist, lecturer, and professor of European reputation. He is Edith Presbury’s only father, formerly known for an academic life, brilliant crowded lectures, a reliable memory, and a grave, portly appearance with unusually keen, cunning eyes. After a secret journey and secret correspondence with Dorak and, through a London agent, H. Lowenstein, his behavior changes: he becomes furtive, sly, intermittently fierce, and subject to queer fits and drug-related outbursts every nine days, though his intellect remains clear in ordinary periods. He secretly follows directions for a strong treatment supplied by a Bohemian intermediary, keeping its bottles and syringe in a locked box whose key hangs from his watch-chain. The treatment produces thick, horny knuckles and animal-like episodes in which he crouches, crawls, climbs ivy, and reverts to a black-faced Langur. When Holmes and Watson visit, he angrily denies calling them and blocks their exit. At the next crisis, Roy attacks him, tears his throat, and leaves him seriously wounded and unconscious; his box and letters reveal the Langur-based treatment from H. Lowenstein.

THE ADVENTURE OF THE CREEPING MAN

Professor Presbury is a sixty-one-year-old, wealthy and internationally renowned Camford physiologist, widower, and father of Edith Presbury. After returning from a secret journey, he becomes furtive, sly, intermittently fierce, and apparently capable of actions he does not remember, while his intellect and lectures remain unaffected.

Monday morning found us on our way to the famous University town--an

Professor Presbury receives Holmes and Watson but denies summoning Holmes and becomes intensely angry when they cannot prove their claim. He blocks their exit, rages incoherently, and later returns to the house with Mr. Bennett in an animated conversation, although his mind appears clear.

In the morning Mr. Bennett slipped round to bring us the latest report.

Professor Presbury appears normal during the day but at the expected ninth-day crisis leaves his house in a crouch, crawls on his hands and feet, climbs the ivy with unusual agility, and provokes the wolf-hound. Roy attacks him and tears his throat, leaving him seriously wounded and unconscious. His box contains serum bottles, a hypodermic syringe, and correspondence revealing that he has been receiving a Langur-based treatment from H. Lowenstein.
