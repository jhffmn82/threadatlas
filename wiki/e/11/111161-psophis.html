Psophis in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Psophis is a town in Arcadia, reached by the road through Soron and serving as the start of the road to Thelpusa. Its boundaries meet Clitor at Siræ and Thelpusa at a pillar with ancient writing. Formerly called Phegia, it was renamed by Echephron and Promachus after their mother. Competing accounts attribute its foundation to Psophis, child of Arrho or Xanthus, and to Psophis, daughter of Eryx. Near the Aroanius and Erymanthus, it has sanctuaries, Alcmæon’s tomb, and ruined Aphrodite Erycina’s temple.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIII.

Psophis is reached by the road through Soron. The boundary between the districts of Clitor and Psophis is at Siræ.

CHAPTER XXIV.

Psophis is associated with several founding traditions, including Psophis the son or daughter of named ancestors and Psophis, the daughter of Eryx. The settlement was formerly called Phegia, and Echephron and Promachus changed its name to Psophis after their mother. It lies near Siræ, the Aroanius, and the Erymanthus, and contains sanctuaries, hero-chapels, and the tomb of Alcmæon.

CHAPTER XXV.

Psophis is the starting point of the road to Thelpusa. A pillar with ancient writing marks the boundary between the territories of Psophis and Thelpusa.
