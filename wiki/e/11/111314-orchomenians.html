Orchomenians in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The Orchomenians were inhabitants of Orchomenian country, a broader people from whom the Phlegyas separated, also called Minyæ and distinguished from Arcadian Minyæ. Ruled by Erginus, then Ascalaphus and Ialmenus, they fought Hercules and the Thebans and were defeated, went to Troy and Ionia, were expelled by Thebans, and restored by Philip son of Amyntas.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XVII.

The Orchomenians fought Hercules and the Thebans and were defeated, along with their king Erginus the son of Clymenus.

CHAPTER XXXVI.

The Orchomenians were the broader people from whom the people of Phlegyas separated. They later continued to be distinguished from the Minyæ of Arcadia.

CHAPTER XXXVII.

The Orchomenians were ruled by Ascalaphus and Ialmenus, who led them to Troy. They later joined the expedition to Ionia with the sons of Codrus, were driven from their country by the Thebans, and were restored to Orchomenus by Philip the son of Amyntas.
