Big Wigs in The Hungry Tiger of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1926

Big Wigs are four huge creatures living in Immense City, associated with Big Wig Town and appearing to be Giants. They become gigantic by wearing Princess Elma's big wig, but are ordinary-sized without their wigs while asleep, making them frauds as false Giants. They attack the travelers, seize the Hungry Tiger, and throw the others into hay. Later, Reddy discovers their tiny sleeping forms, abandons plans to capture the city, and escapes through the gates.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 12

Four Giants see the travelers, and one of them seizes the Hungry Tiger while the other travelers are thrown into a hay field. The Giants live in or near Immense City, a place surrounded by high fences and a great wall.

CHAPTER 13

The Big Wigs live in the giant city beyond the wall. A Big Wig's voice calls for Betsy when the city door opens, and the foot thrust through the doorway seems familiar to Carter.

CHAPTER 17

The Big Wigs appear to be Giants in Immense City, but they shrink to ordinary size when they remove their wigs. Reddy sees them sleeping in tiny beds and decides not to capture the city. Big Wig Guards allow him to pass, and he escapes through the city gates.

the Giants in The Hungry Tiger of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1926

The Giants are feared inhabitants of Immense City who wear huge white wigs and may run over or tread on travelers. When Reddy and his companions encounter them, Carter warns the others to avoid them. In their castle, Reddy seems doll-sized; the King orders a stray kitten thrown into the pond, while the Queen worries about Elma.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 13

The Giants are feared inhabitants of the city, and Carter warns that Betsy and the others must avoid being run over or trodden upon by them.

CHAPTER 16

The Giants live in the castle of Immense City and are large enough for Reddy to appear about the size of a small doll. The Giant King orders that the stray kitten be thrown into the pond if it remains in the castle, while the Giant Queen worries about their daughter Elma. Their servants carry out household tasks and do not notice Reddy.
