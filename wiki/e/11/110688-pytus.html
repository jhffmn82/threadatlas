1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK IV.--MESSENIA.

Æpytus, the youngest son of Cresphontes, survived the murder of his family after being raised by Cypselus. The Arcadians and other Dorian rulers restored him to Messenia, where he punished the murderers and won support through gifts and favorable treatment.

Æpytus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Æpytus is presented as two men. One, son of Hippothous, succeeded him as king, entered Poseidon’s forbidden temple at Mantinea, was blinded, and died soon afterward. Another, son of Elatus, was bitten by a serpent while hunting, died on Sepia, and was buried there. A sister’s son of Olæas was restored to Messene.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER V.

Æpytus, son of Hippothous, succeeded his father and was blinded after entering the temple of Poseidon at Mantinea, where entry was forbidden; he died soon afterward. Another Æpytus, the sister’s son of Olæas, was restored to Messene by Olæas and the Heraclidæ from Lacedæmon and Argos.

CHAPTER XVI.

Æpytus, the son of Elatus, died on Sepia after being bitten by a serpent. His body was buried there because it could not be carried farther.
