Hyampolis in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Hyampolis was an ancient town, founded by Hyantes who fled Thebes and originally called the town of the Hyantes. It mattered as a Phocian defensive site against Thessalian cavalry and through Daiphantes. Xerxes burnt it and Philip razed it, yet it retained a market-place, council-chamber, theatre, and Adrian’s Portico, relying on one well and winter rainwater.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK X.--PHOCIS. / CHAPTER I.

Hyampolis was the place where the Phocians buried earthenware pots to obstruct the attacking Thessalian cavalry. The Phocians also associated Hyampolis with Daiphantes, who commanded their cavalry in the later battle.

CHAPTER XXXV.

Hyampolis was said to have been founded by Hyantes who fled Thebes and was originally called the town of the Hyantes. Although Xerxes burnt it and Philip razed it, the town retained remains of its market-place, council-chamber, and theatre, as well as a Portico built by the Emperor Adrian; its inhabitants relied mainly on one well and winter rainwater.
