Caphyæ

place · 1 work · 7 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: plain of Caphyæ

Abstract written by a model

Caphyæ is a town at the end of a plain beneath low hills. Named from Cepheus through Arcadian dialect, it is said by its inhabitants to have been settled by people from Attica after Ægeus expelled them from Athens. It has temples of Poseidon and Cnacalesian Artemis, and nearby Menelaus’ well and plane-tree.

Caphyæ is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XXIII.

Caphyæ lies at the end of a plain beneath low hills and is named clearly from Cepheus, though the Arcadian dialect has produced the name Caphyæ. Its inhabitants say they came from Attica after Ægeus expelled them from Athens and that they sought refuge from Cepheus; the town has temples of Poseidon and Cnacalesian Artemis.
