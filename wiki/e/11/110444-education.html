Abstract written by a model

Education is portrayed as a contested path from childhood schooling to college. Angeline’s early education includes district school, Edwin Ingalls, singing school, and Rodman Union Seminary, amid harsh school discipline. Despite financial hardship and opposition to women’s ambitions, she advances through teaching and study at New York Central College, where admission disregards race or sex but poor students work for food and lodging; Ruth leaves because of poor health. Asaph Hall 3rd begins serious study late, excels in geometry and algebra, and gains encouragement. The text criticizes conventional educational theory and treats college attendance as education’s intended continuation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER II.

Education in Angeline’s childhood included district school, instruction by Edwin Ingalls, singing school, and the Rodman Union Seminary. The text also describes harsh discipline in local schools, including beatings and the punishment of piercing a pupil’s lip with a needle.

------------------------------------------------------------------------ [Illustration: AN OLD DAGUERREOTYPE] ------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER V.

Education motivates Angeline Stickney to keep advancing despite financial hardship and opposition to women's ambitions. She pursues it through teaching attempts and ultimately through study at New York Central College.

------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER VI.

College education at New York Central College was available to students regardless of race or sex, but poor students often had to work for food and lodging. Angeline Stickney pursued it despite financial hardship, while Ruth Stickney left because of poor health.

opening

The text contrasts Asaph Hall 3rd's late start in serious study with conventional educational schedules. He began studying geometry and algebra with Mr. Rice and found that he was a better mathematician than his teacher. His ability as a scholar earned the admiration of fellow students and the encouragement of teachers, including Brünnow and Benjamin Apthorp Gould.
