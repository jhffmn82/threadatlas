Aratus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Aratus, son of Clinias, overthrows Nicocles at Sicyon, restores isonomy and refugees’ property, and brings Sicyon into the Achæan league. He campaigns against enemies and helps free Corinth, wins Olympia’s chariot-race, receives Corinthian honors, is poisoned by Philip, and is buried in Sicyon’s Arateum.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

Aratus overthrew Nicocles at Sicyon, restored isonomy, and returned the refugees' houses and property with compensation to purchasers. He brought Sicyon into the Achæan league, led campaigns against several enemies, helped free Corinth, and later was poisoned by Philip; his body was buried at Sicyon in the Arateum.

CHAPTER XII.

A statue of him at Olympia was a votive offering of the Corinthians. He was also proclaimed victor at Olympia in the chariot-race.

Aratus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Aratus, a benefactor of all Greece discussed in Sicyon, commands Sicyonians and Achæans, helps drive Agis from Pellene, then suffers defeat by Cleomenes at Dyme.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VII.

Aratus and the Sicyonians drove Agis, the son of Eudamidas, out of Pellene after Agis had captured the town. Aratus and the Achæans were later badly defeated by Cleomenes at Dyme.
