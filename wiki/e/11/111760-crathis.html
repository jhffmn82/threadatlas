Crathis in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Crathis is a perennial river rising in Mountain Crathis in Arcadia. It flows from its source to the sea near Aegae, a former town in Achaia that was deserted in the narrator’s time. Its chief significance is that it gave its name to the river near Croton and to the river Crathis in Bruttii, both in Italy.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXV.

Crathis was a perennial river that flowed into the sea and rose in an Arcadian mountain of the same name. The river near Croton in Italy was said to have received its name from this Crathis. The former town of Ægæ lay near it.

CHAPTER XV.

The river Crathis rises on Mount Crathis and flows into the sea near Aegae, which was once a town in Achaia but was deserted in the narrator’s time. A river in the district of the Bruttii in Italy received its name from this Crathis.
