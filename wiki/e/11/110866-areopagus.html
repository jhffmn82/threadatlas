Areopagus

Also called: the Areopagus

Abstract written by a model

In Description of Greece, the Areopagus was named for Ares because Ares was first tried there, and Orestes was also said to have been tried there for killing his mother. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Areopagus was a refuge and court invoked by Dodona over violating suppliants, and later served as a refuge for Lacedæmonians who had withdrawn from Attica.

Abstract written by a model

is_named_for Ares [because Ares was first tried there] The Areopagus was so called because Ares was first tried there, and Orestes was also said to have been tried there for killing his mother. Those acquitted before it offered sacrifices at the nearby temple of the Venerable Ones, and two white stones used by defendants and plaintiffs were called Rigour-of-the-law and Impudence. Ephialtes was remembered as having tried to discredit the legislation of the Areopagus.

CHAPTER XXV.

The Areopagus was invoked in the message from Dodona as a reminder of the consequences of violating suppliants. Some Lacedæmonians fled there for safety after their army withdrew from Attica.
