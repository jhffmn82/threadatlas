Areopagus

place · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: the Areopagus
  • In Description of Greece, the Areopagus was named for Ares because Ares was first tried there, and Orestes was also said to have been tried there for killing his mother.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Areopagus was a refuge and court invoked by Dodona over violating suppliants, and later served as a refuge for Lacedæmonians who had withdrawn from Attica.

the Areopagus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

is_named_for Ares [because Ares was first tried there] The Areopagus was so called because Ares was first tried there, and Orestes was also said to have been tried there for killing his mother. Those acquitted before it offered sacrifices at the nearby temple of the Venerable Ones, and two white stones used by defendants and plaintiffs were called Rigour-of-the-law and Impudence. Ephialtes was remembered as having tried to discredit the legislation of the Areopagus.

Narrative, section by section

CHAPTER XXVIII.

The Areopagus was so called because Ares was first tried there, and Orestes was also said to have been tried there for killing his mother. Those acquitted before it offered sacrifices at the nearby temple of the Venerable Ones, and two white stones used by defendants and plaintiffs were called Rigour-of-the-law and Impudence. Ephialtes was remembered as having tried to discredit the legislation of the Areopagus.

Areopagus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Areopagus, a refuge and court, was invoked by Dodona over violating suppliants; later Lacedæmonians fled there after withdrawing from Attica.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXV.

The Areopagus was invoked in the message from Dodona as a reminder of the consequences of violating suppliants. Some Lacedæmonians fled there for safety after their army withdrew from Attica.
