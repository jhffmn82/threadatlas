Alcathous

Abstract written by a model

Alcathous, son of Pelops and son-in-law of Megareus, killed the Cithæron lion, gained Megareus’s daughter and succeeded to Megara’s kingdom. As king he built temples, began Megara’s citadel and wall after sacrifice to the Prodromi, and rebuilt the wall after its destruction, with Apollo’s aid. His family suffered: Periboea went to Crete, Ischepolis died hunting, and Alcathous killed Callipolis in error.

CHAPTER XXXVIII.

Alcathous, the son of Pelops, killed the lion of Mount Cithæron and thereby received Megareus's daughter and the succession to the kingdom according to local tradition. After becoming king, he built a temple to Huntress Artemis and Hunter Apollo and later rebuilt Megara's wall after the Cretans had destroyed the old one.

CHAPTER XLII.

Alcathous is associated with the citadel and wall of Megara, which he began building after sacrificing to the Prodromi gods. Apollo is said to have assisted him, and Alcathous’s daughter Periboea was sent with Theseus to Crete. His sons Ischepolis and Callipolis died tragically: Ischepolis was killed fighting the Calydonian boar, and Alcathous killed Callipolis after mistaking his grief-stricken act at the altar for irreverence.
