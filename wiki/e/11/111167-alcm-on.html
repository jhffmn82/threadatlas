Alcmæon

person · 1 work · 9 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Alcmæon the son of Amphiaraus

Abstract written by a model

Alcmæon, son of Amphiaraus, fled Argos after killing his mother, married Alphesibœa at Phegia, then sought Delphi as madness persisted. Settling on land made by the Achelous, he married Callirhoe and fathered Acarnan and Amphoterus. Returning to Phegia for Eriphyle’s necklace, he was treacherously killed by Temenus and buried at Psophis beneath sacred cypresses called Maidens.

Alcmæon is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XXIV.

Alcmæon, the son of Amphiaraus, is buried at Psophis beneath a tomb shaded by sacred cypress trees called Maidens. After killing his mother, he fled Argos, married Alphesibœa at Phegia, and later consulted Delphi because his madness did not improve. He eventually settled on land formed by the Achelous, married Callirhoe, and was treacherously killed after returning to Phegia in pursuit of the necklace of Eriphyle.
