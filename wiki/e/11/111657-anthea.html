Œanthea in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Œanthea is a town near the sea, placed under the Achæans of Patræ by Augustus. It has a temple of Aphrodite and, above it, a cypress-and-pine grove with a temple and statue of Artemis and time-obscured paintings; its name may derive from a woman or Nymph.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXVIII.

Œanthea lay near the sea and was one of the towns, other than Amphissa, placed under the Achæans of Patræ by a grant from the Emperor Augustus. It had a temple of Aphrodite, and a nearby grove of cypress and pine containing a temple and statue of Artemis, along with wall paintings obscured by time. The text suggests that its name may have come from a woman or a Nymph.
