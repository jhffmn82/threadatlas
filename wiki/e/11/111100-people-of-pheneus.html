people of Pheneus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The people of Pheneus preserve traditions about their founder, pits, Demeter’s visit, Odysseus’s horses, and Myrtilus’s burial. They honor Iphicles and Myrtilus, especially Hermes through the Hermæa games, and recount hospitably receiving Eleusinian Demeter.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIV.

The people of Pheneus said that the pits near their plain were made by Hercules and that Pheneus, an autochthon, was their founder. They preserved traditions that Odysseus found his lost horses there and that Myrtilus was buried there, and they paid heroic honors to Iphicles and annual nighttime honors to Myrtilus. They especially honored Hermes and held games called Hermæa.
