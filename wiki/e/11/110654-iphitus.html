Iphitus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Iphitus, a relative of Oxylus and contemporary of Lycurgus, revived and renewed the Olympian games with Elis after their suspension. Directed by Delphi, he established their truce, persuaded Elis to sacrifice to Hercules, and served as sole original umpire. He is depicted crowned by Ecechiria and possessed an inscribed quoit.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK V.--ELIS.

Iphitus, a relative of Oxylus and a contemporary of Lycurgus, revived the contest at Olympia and established a truce for its duration. At Delphi’s direction, he joined the people of Elis in restoring the Olympian games and persuaded them to sacrifice to Hercules.

CHAPTER VIII.

Iphitus renewed the Olympian games after their suspension following the reign of Oxylus. He was originally the sole umpire, and the posterity of Oxylus succeeded him in that role.
