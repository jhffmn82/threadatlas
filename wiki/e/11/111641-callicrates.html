Callicrates in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Callicrates was an Achæan general and traitor to the Achæan cause who gained influence over the Achæans through friendship with the Romans and flattery of Roman Senators. He accused prominent Achæans and generals of favoring Perseus and the Macedonians, helping send more than 1,000 Achæans to Rome, and was called the plague of all Greece. After assisting the Oropians, he prosecuted Menalcidas when denied promised money. Sent with Diæus to Rome against the Spartan exiles, he died of illness on the road.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER X.

Callicrates ingratiated himself with the Roman Senators through flattery and persuaded one Senator to enter the Achæan League. He instigated accusations against prominent Achæans and helped cause more than 1,000 of them to be sent to Rome.

CHAPTER XI.

Callicrates was described as the plague of all Greece and as having great influence over the Achæans because of his friendship with the Romans. Menalcidas promised him half of the payment offered by the Oropians, and Callicrates helped bring about the decision to assist them.

CHAPTER XII.

Callicrates was denied his share of the Oropian money by Menalcidas and prosecuted him before the Achæans on a capital charge. He was sent with Diæus to Rome to oppose the Spartan exiles, but died of an illness on the journey.
