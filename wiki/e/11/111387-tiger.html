tiger in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Tiger is a wild animal identified with Ctesias's Indian martiora, called manslayer by the Greeks. Reports of three rows of teeth and tail stings are rejected. Its apparently uniform red color, often seen in sunlight, is explained as an effect of sunlight, speed, or constant motion.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXI.

The speaker identifies the Indian martiora, or manslayer described by Ctesias, as a tiger. The speaker rejects the reports that the tiger has three rows of teeth in each jaw and a tail that stings and shoots its stings like arrows, and explains its apparently uniform red color as an effect of sunlight, speed, or constant motion.
