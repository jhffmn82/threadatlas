Amyclæ in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Amyclæ is a village reached by the road from Sparta, past the Tiasa and a temple of the Graces. Destroyed by the Dorians, it remains notable for the sanctuary of Amyclæan Apollo, rites for Apollo at the festival of Hyacinthus, and weaving Apollo’s coat. Its chief gods are Apollo and Dionysus Psilax. Its monuments include Ænetus’s statue, tripods from Ægos-potamoi, statues and a temple of Alexandra, representations of Clytæmnestra and Agamemnon, and Agamemnon’s supposed tomb.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XV.

Amyclæ was reached by the road from Sparta, which passed the river Tiasa and a temple of the Graces Phaenna and Clete. Its notable monuments included the statue of the victorious pentathlete Ænetus, three large tripods dedicated after the victory at Ægos-potamoi, and the sanctuary of Amyclæan Apollo. The tripods carried statues of Aphrodite, Artemis, and Proserpine, and Amyclæ was also associated with the weaving of Apollo’s coat.

CHAPTER XIX.

Amyclæ was destroyed by the Dorians and is described as only a village, though it contains a temple and statue of Alexandra, whom its people identify with Cassandra, as well as representations of Clytæmnestra and Agamemnon and his supposed tomb. Amyclæan Apollo and Dionysus are the chief gods worshipped there, and Dionysus is called Psilax, or Winged.
