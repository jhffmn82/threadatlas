Trophonius

Abstract written by a model

In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Trophonius is Apollo’s son, a skilled builder who helped build Apollo’s temple at Delphi, killed Agamedes, and became the focus of an oracle at Lebadea. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Trophonius is Agamedes’s brother and a distinguished architect who helped build Apollo’s temple and King Hyrieus’s treasury, then beheaded Agamedes and became an oracle at Lebadea.

Trophonius in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Trophonius first appears

Abstract written by a model

Trophonius is said to be Apollo’s son rather than Erginus’s. A skilled builder with Agamedes, he helped build Apollo’s temple at Delphi, then killed Agamedes and was swallowed by the earth in Lebadea’s grove. At Lebadea he became the focus of an oracle: visitors sacrificed and descended underground to learn the future. Sought during a Bœotian drought, he instructed Saon in the oracle’s rituals. Dragons were sacred to him, and Demeter called Europa was said to be his nurse.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXVII.

Trophonius was said to be the son of Apollo rather than Erginus, and the text says this is supported by consultation of the oracle of Trophonius. He became a skilled builder with Agamedes, but after cutting off Agamedes's head, he was swallowed by the earth in the grove of Lebadea.

CHAPTER XXXIX.

Trophonius is associated with an oracle at Lebadea, where visitors sacrifice to him and descend into an underground sanctuary to learn the future. Dragons are said to be especially sacred to him, and Demeter called Europa is said to have been his nurse.

CHAPTER XL.

Trophonius was sought at Lebadea as the source of a cure for the Bœotian drought. He instructed Saon from Acræphnium in all the ritual and routine of his oracle.

Trophonius in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Trophonius first appears

Abstract written by a model

Trophonius, brother of Agamedes and a distinguished architect, helped build Apollo’s temple at Delphi and King Hyrieus’s treasury; after Agamedes was trapped robbing it, he beheaded him, was swallowed by earth, and gained an oracle at Lebadea.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

MYTH OF OSIRIS AND ISIS

Trophonius was the brother of Agamedes and a distinguished architect who helped build the temple of Apollo at Delphi and a treasury for King Hyrieus. After Agamedes was trapped while robbing the treasury, Trophonius cut off his brother's head and was later swallowed by the earth; his oracle was established at Lebadea.
