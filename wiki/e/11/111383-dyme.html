Dyme in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Dyme was an Achaean town, formerly called Palea, about 30 stades from the Larisus. It alone in Achaia was reduced in war by Philip the son of Demetrius; Sulpicius then permitted his army to plunder it. Augustus assigned it to Patræ. Its notable sacred and commemorative sites included temples or monuments of Athene, the Dindymene Mother, Attes, Sostratus, and Œbotas.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XVII.

Dyme was an Achaean town about 30 stades from the river Larisus and the only town in Achaia that Philip the son of Demetrius reduced in war; Sulpicius consequently allowed his army to plunder it, and Augustus later assigned it to Patræ. It was formerly called Palea, and contained temples or monuments connected with Athene, the Dindymene Mother, Attes, Sostratus, and Œbotas.
