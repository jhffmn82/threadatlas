Mænalus

Also called: Mæenalia, ruins of Mænalus

Abstract written by a model

Mænalus is reached from Megalopolis by the river Helisson. Near it lie ruins of the town, traces of a temple of Athene, and courses for athletic contests and horseraces. Its mountain is sacred to Pan, whose pipes nearby inhabitants say they hear.

Mænalus is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XXXVI.

A road from Megalopolis leads to Mænalus by the river Helisson. Near Mænalus are the ruins of the town of Mænalus, traces of a temple of Athene, and courses for athletic contests and horseraces. Mount Mænalium is considered sacred to Pan, and nearby inhabitants say they hear Pan playing his pipes.
