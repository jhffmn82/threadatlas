Epopeus

person · 1 work · 6 quoted facts · 2 connections

Abstract written by a model

Epopeus, king of Ægialea from Thessaly, gained the kingdom when Corax died childless. He defeated the Thebans over Antiope despite a wound, sacrificed to Athene, built temples to Athene, Artemis, and Apollo, and died from his neglected wound. His great shrine of Athene was struck by lightning, leaving its altar before his tomb.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

Epopeus came from Thessaly and obtained the kingdom after Corax died childless. He fought the Thebans over Antiope, won the battle despite being wounded, sacrificed to Athene, built her a temple, and later died from his neglected wound.

CHAPTER XI.

Epopeus formerly erected a shrine of Athene that surpassed those of its time in size and beauty, but time obscured its fame. Lightning struck the shrine, leaving only its altar; his tomb stood in front of the altar, near the Gods the Averters of Evil.
