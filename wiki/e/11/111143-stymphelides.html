Stymphelides in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Stymphelides are savage birds of the Arabian desert, about crane-sized and stork-like but with stronger, straight beaks. They attack would-be captors and kill men by beak wounds, piercing armor and thick robes. Birds of the same name were represented on the roof of the temple of Stymphelian Artemis.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXII.

The Stymphelides in Arabia are described as birds about the size of cranes, resembling storks but having stronger, non-crooked beaks. They are savage toward humans and can pierce armor and thick robes. Birds with the same name were represented on the roof of the temple of Stymphelian Artemis.
