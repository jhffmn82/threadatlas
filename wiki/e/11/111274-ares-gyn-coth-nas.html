Ares Gynæcothœnas in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Ares Gynæcothœnas is a statue of Ares on a pillar in Tegea’s market-place, named for women’s separate sacrifice after their victory over the Lacedæmonians.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XLVIII.

Ares Gynæcothœnas has a statue on a pillar in the market-place at Tegea. The name means “Women’s Feast” and refers to the women’s separate sacrifice after their victory over the Lacedæmonians.
