war between Rome and the Achæans in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The war between Rome and the Achæans began when Critolaus gathered an Achæan army at Corinth and persuaded the Achæans to fight Sparta and the Romans, encouraged by Pytheas and the Thebans. The conflict brought ruin to Critolaus and the Achæans, ending in Roman victory, Corinth’s destruction, and punishment of opposing cities.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIV.

Critolaus collected an Achæan army at Corinth and persuaded it to wage war immediately against Sparta and the Romans. Pytheas and the Thebans further encouraged the conflict, and the text says that this combination of audacity and weakness brought ruin to Critolaus and the Achæans.

CHAPTER XVI.

The war ended with the Roman victory over the Achæans, the destruction of Corinth, and the dismantling of the walls and arms of cities that had opposed Rome. It ended when Antitheus was Archon at Athens, in the 160th Olympiad, when Diodorus of Sicyon won the course.
