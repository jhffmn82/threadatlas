Asea

Abstract written by a model

Asea, named after Aseatas, is a place near Athenæum with ruins and remaining walls of a former hilltop citadel; nearby lie the Alpheus, Eurotas’s source, a roofless temple, and a road to Mount Boreum.

Asea is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XLIV.

Asea lies about 20 stades from Athenæum and retains remains of walls on the hill that formerly served as its citadel. About five stades from Asea are the Alpheus, the source of the Eurotas, and a roofless temple of the Mother of the Gods; a road from Asea also leads to Mount Boreum.
