Tithorea in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Tithorea is a place in Phocis, also called Neon, though Bacis calls it Tithorea. Originally the name covered the entire district; local tradition names the nymph Tithorea as its source. About 80 stades from Delphi by Mount Parnassus, it has a theatre, ancient market-place, Athene’s grove and shrine, Antiope and Phocus’s tomb, and water from the Cachales. Its people guard the sepulchre of Zethus and Amphion and seek earth from it for Antiope’s tomb to improve fertility. Its fortunes worsened, and roads lead to Ledon and toward Drymæa through Tithronium.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XVII.

The inhabitants of Tithorea sought to carry earth from the sepulchre of Zethus and Amphion and place it on Antiope’s tomb when the Sun was in Taurus. They believed this would increase the fertility of their land, though the Thebans believed it would cause their own land’s fruits to suffer blight.

CHAPTER XXXII.

Tithorea is about 80 stades from Delphi by way of Mount Parnassus, though a less mountainous carriage road is longer. It contains the remains of a theatre and ancient market-place, a grove and shrine of Athene, and the tomb of Antiope and Phocus; the river Cachales supplies its inhabitants with water. The people say that its name comes from the Nymph Tithorea, and that its fortunes worsened a generation before the account.

CHAPTER XXXIII.

Tithorea is the starting point of a road leading to Ledon. Another route from Tithorea leads toward Drymæa through Tithronium.

  1. “It seems probable therefore that Tithorea was originally the name for the entire district, but as time went on the people, flocking into the town from the villages, called it Tithorea and no longer Neon.”Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II · CHAPTER XXXII.
