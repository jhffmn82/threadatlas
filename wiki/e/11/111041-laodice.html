Laodice in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Laodice, Agapenor’s daughter and Helicaon’s wife, sends a robe from Cyprus to Tegea and erects a temple near Paphian Aphrodite. Not named among captive Trojan women, she is painted by an altar; Greeks probably release her through favor toward her husband’s family.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXVI.

Laodice is painted standing on the other side of the altar. Although she is not named by the cited poets among the captive Trojan women, it is considered probable that the Greeks let her go because Menelaus and Odysseus were favorably disposed toward her husband’s family.
