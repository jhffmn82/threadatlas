Abstract written by a model

In The Library, it is the necklace Polynices gives to Eriphyle, which persuades Amphiaraus to join the war and is later reclaimed by Alcmaeon before being dedicated at Delphi by his sons. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, it is the gold necklace originally given to Harmonia, later named for Eriphyle, sought by Callirhoe, and ultimately dedicated to Apollo at Delphi before being carried off by Phocian tyrants.

necklace of Eriphyle in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where necklace of Eriphyle first appears

Abstract written by a model

The necklace of Eriphyle is a gold necklace originally given to Harmonia. Later named for Eriphyle after her husband gave it to her, it was among Alcmæon’s gifts to Alphesibœa. Callirhoe sought it; Alcmæon died pursuing it. Temenus and Axion, or the sons of Phegeus, dedicated it to Apollo at Delphi, from where Phocian tyrants carried it off.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIV.

The necklace of Eriphyle was among the gifts Alcmæon gave to Alphesibœa. Callirhoe desired it and sent Alcmæon unwillingly to Phegia to obtain it; Temenus and Axion treacherously caused his death, and they were said to have offered the necklace to Apollo at Delphi.

CHAPTER XLI.

The necklace given to Harmonia was later called the necklace of Eriphyle because Eriphyle received it as a gift from her husband. The sons of Phegeus dedicated it at Delphi, and it was later carried off by the Phocian tyrants.
