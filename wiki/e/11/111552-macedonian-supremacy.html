Macedonian supremacy in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Macedonian supremacy unfolds from an end linked to Alexander’s early death in the author’s reflection, through later Philip’s defeat at Cynoscephalæ, to overthrow by the Romans with Perseus. Its collapse is presented as a fatal Roman blow and as the beginning of troubles leading to Achæan subjection to Rome.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VIII.

The Macedonian supremacy received a fatal blow from the Romans after Philip’s defeat at Cynoscephalæ. The text connects its collapse with the defeat of the later Philip.

CHAPTER X.

The Macedonian Empire was overthrown by the Romans along with Perseus. Its defeat marked the beginning of the troubles that eventually made the Achæans entirely subject to Rome.

CHAPTER VII.

The end of Macedonian supremacy is linked with Alexander’s early death. The text suggests that Philip’s conduct and disregard for good faith were connected with the gods ending Alexander’s life and Macedonian supremacy.
