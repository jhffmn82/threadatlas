Mount Lycæus

place · 2 works · 6 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Lycæus, Mountain Lycæus, the mountain, this mountain
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Mount Lycæus contains sacred sites including temples of Pan and Parrhasian Apollo, a Hippodrome, and Lycæan Zeus’s enclosure and altar; its enduring Hagno spring and high ridge overlook most of the Peloponnese.
  • In Description of Greece, Mount Lycæus is a place of refuge where the Arcadians met and welcomed the surviving Messenians after the fall of Eira; Aristomenes planned his attack on Sparta there, and Aristocrates’ betrayal was exposed before the Arcadians.

Mount Lycæus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Mount Lycæus, also called Olympus or Sacred Hill by some Arcadians, contains sacred sites including temples of Pan and Parrhasian Apollo, a Hippodrome, and Lycæan Zeus’s enclosure and altar. Its enduring Hagno spring and high ridge, overlooking most of the Peloponnese, complete its features.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXVIII.

Mount Lycæus contains the grove of Parrhasian Apollo, a temple of Pan, a Hippodrome, the bases of statues, an enclosure sacred to Lycæan Zeus, and the altar of Lycæan Zeus. Its Hagno spring generally has as much water in summer as in winter, and its highest ridge provides a view of most of the Peloponnese.
