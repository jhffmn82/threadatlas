Tritea

Also called: a town of Achaia

Abstract written by a model

Tritea is an inland town of Achaia, reckoned with Patræ by Imperial order and about 120 stades from Pharæ. Its chief features are temples of the Greatest Gods and Athene, with clay and stone statues, and sacrifices to Ares and Tritea. Tradition names either Celbidas or Melanippus as founder; Athene’s old statue was said to have gone to Rome.

CHAPTER XXII.

Tritea is an inland town of Achaia reckoned with Patræ by Imperial order and lying about 120 stades from Pharæ. It has a temple to the Greatest Gods with clay statues, a temple and stone statue of Athene, and traditions that its founder was either Celbidas of Cumæ or Melanippus, son of Ares and Tritea. The people of Tritea sacrifice to both Ares and Tritea, and the old statue of Athene was said to have been taken to Rome.
