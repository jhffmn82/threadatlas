Abstract written by a model

In Description of Greece, Emperor Adrian is presented as a benefactor to Athens and the peoples he ruled, building and adorning temples, avoiding war except to punish the Hebrews’ revolt, and recording his gifts in inscriptions. In Description of Greece, Adrian builds temples in Athens, including the temple of Olympian Zeus with a colossal ivory-and-gold statue, and restores horse-racing at Nemea’s winter games for the Argives.

Emperor Adrian in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Emperor Adrian first appears

Abstract written by a model

Emperor Adrian is presented as a benefactor, especially to Athens and the peoples he ruled. Honoured there with a statue, he built and adorned temples, including Apollo’s old white-stone temple. He avoided war except to punish the Hebrews’ revolt, and inscriptions recorded his gifts and benefactions.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Description of Greece / BOOK I.--ATTICA.

Emperor Adrian was honoured in Athens with a statue among the city’s benefactors and was described as especially beneficial to the peoples he ruled. He was said to have avoided war except when suppressing the revolt of the Hebrews, and inscriptions in the temple common to all the gods recorded his temples, gifts, and other benefactions.

Adrian in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Adrian first appears

Abstract written by a model

Adrian builds Athens temples, especially Olympian Zeus's with a colossal ivory-and-gold statue, then temples to Hera, Pan-Hellenian Zeus, and all gods, and a gymnasium; he also restores horse-racing at Nemea's winter games for the Argives.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIV.

Adrian built the temple of Olympian Zeus at Athens and placed in it a colossal ivory-and-gold statue of Olympian Zeus. He also erected temples to Hera and Pan-Hellenian Zeus and a temple for all the gods, as well as the Adrian gymnasium.
