Abstract written by a model

The sceptre of Agamemnon, a probably sole surviving work of Hephæstus, was made for Zeus and passed through Hermes, Pelops, Atreus, and Thyestes to Agamemnon. Found in Phocis on the Panopeans’ borders, it was Chæronea’s most venerated object: kept in a building, bright with divine properties, and given daily sacrifices and annual food offerings.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XL.

The people of Chæronea venerated most the sceptre that Hephæstus made for Zeus and that passed through Hermes, Pelops, Atreus, and Thyestes to Agamemnon. They called it the spear, kept it in a certain building, sacrificed to it daily, and spread a table of meats and pastry for it each year through its priest.

CHAPTER XLI.

The sceptre of Agamemnon is described as certainly being the work of Hephæstus. It is regarded as probably the only work of Hephæstus still extant.
