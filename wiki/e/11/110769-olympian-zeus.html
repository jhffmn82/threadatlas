Zeus statue in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Zeus statue is a gold-and-ivory statue depicting Olympian Zeus, seated on a throne in gold robe, sandals, and olive-leaf crown, holding Victory and a jewelled eagle sceptre; at Olympia, its ivory is protected with oil against grove dampness.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XI.

The statue is seated on a throne and wears a gold robe, sandals, and an olive-leaf crown; it holds a Victory in its right hand and a jewelled sceptre with an eagle in its left. Its ivory is protected at Olympia with oil because of the dampness of the grove, while water is used for Athena's ivory statue at Athens; the statue at Epidaurus needs neither because it and its throne stand over a well.

Olympian Jupiter in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Olympian Jupiter was a colossal chryselephantine statue of Jupiter, forty feet high on a twelve-foot pedestal, seated on a throne. It represented the supreme deity of the Hellenic nation, or Zeus, enthroned as a conqueror in majesty and repose, and was a wonder of the Greek world. Made of ivory and gold, including its throne, it held a gold-and-ivory Victory in its right hand and a sceptre of many metals in its left, topped by an eagle; its shoes, mantle, and decorations were gold. Its technical difficulty, almost absolute perfection, and intellectual beauty made it a noble creation whose beauty added to religion. The statue is now lost.

Chapter XXVIII

The Olympian Jupiter by Phidias was a colossal chryselephantine statue of Jupiter seated on a throne. It represented the supreme deity of the Hellenic nation in majestic repose, holding a sceptre and a statue of Victory; the statue is now lost.

The Olympian Zeus represented the greater divinity honored by all Hellas and was one of the noblest creations of the Greek imagination. Pausanias described Zeus seated on an ivory and gold throne, holding a gold-and-ivory Victory and a metal sceptre topped by an eagle, with gold shoes and mantle. The statue was chryselephantine, combining technical difficulty with near-perfect form and beauty of intellectual character; according to Quintilian, its beauty added a new element to religion.
