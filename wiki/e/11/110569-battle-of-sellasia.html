Battle of Sellasia

event · 2 works · 9 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: attacked Cleomenes at Sellasia, battle of Sellasia, battle which took place at Sellasia, fought against Cleomenes at Sellasia, reverse which the Lacedæmonians met with at Sellasia

Abstract written by a model

In Description of Greece, the battle was won by Antigonus and the Achæans, who defeated Cleomenes at Sellasia; Sellasia was enslaved, Lacedæmon was captured, and the old political order of the Lacedæmonians was restored. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the battle was fought at Sellasia between Cleomenes and the Lacedæmonians and the Achæans, Arcadians, Antigonus, and a Macedonian army; it ended in the defeat of Cleomenes and the Lacedæmonians and an Achæan victory.

battle of Sellasia in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where battle of Sellasia first appears

Abstract written by a model

The battle of Sellasia was fought at Sellasia between Cleomenes and the Lacedæmonians and the Achæans, Arcadians, Antigonus, and a Macedonian army. Philopœmen joined the infantry, fought courageously, and was wounded in both thighs. The battle ended in the defeat of Cleomenes and the Lacedæmonians and an Achæan victory.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XLIX.

At the battle of Sellasia, the Achæans and Arcadians fought Cleomenes and the Lacedæmonians, with Antigonus and a Macedonian army also taking part. Philopœmen shifted from the cavalry to the infantry when he saw that the infantry would determine the battle, fought with notable courage, and was wounded through both thighs before Cleomenes and the Lacedæmonians were defeated.
