town of Clitor in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Clitor is a town in a plain surrounded by low hills, 60 stades from the springs of Ladon, named after Clitor, son of Azan. Its notable temples honor Demeter, Æsculapius, and Ilithyia; the Great Gods’ temple lies 4 stades away, and Athene Coria’s temple and statue 30 stades away on a hilltop.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXI.

The town of Clitor lies in a plain surrounded by low hills and takes its name from the son of Azan. It has notable temples to Demeter, Æsculapius, and Ilithyia, and a temple to Castor and Pollux under the name of the Great Gods about 4 stades away; a temple and statue of Athene Coria stand about 30 stades away on a hilltop.
