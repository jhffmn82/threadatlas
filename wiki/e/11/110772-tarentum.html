Tarentum

Abstract written by a model

In Description of Greece, Tarentum is described as inviting Pyrrhus of Epirus for aid after being unable to resist Rome alone. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Tarentum is described as a maritime city named after Taras and captured by Phalanthus after he understood a Delphic oracle.

Tarentum in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Tarentum first appears

Abstract written by a model

Tarentum, at war with the Romans and unable to resist alone, invited Pyrrhus of Epirus, citing prior naval aid against Corcyra. After defeat in Italy, Pyrrhus sailed there with his remaining fleet and summoned its chief men, promising aid.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VII.

Tarentum invited Pyrrhus of Epirus to fight the Romans because it could not resist them alone. Its envoys appealed to Pyrrhus as a friend and suppliant people, and Tarentum had previously aided him with its fleet against Corcyra.

CHAPTER XIII.

Tarentum was where Pyrrhus sailed after his defeat with the remnant of his fleet. Pyrrhus later summoned the chief men of Tarentum to hear that aid would come to him.

Tarentum in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Tarentum first appears

Abstract written by a model

Tarentum, named after Taras and shared with a river, is a maritime city, greatest and most prosperous among barbarian-held Italian cities, captured by Phalanthus after he understood a Delphic oracle.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER X.

Tarentum was described as the greatest and most prosperous of the maritime cities held by the barbarians in Italy. Phalanthus captured it after understanding an oracle from Delphi.
