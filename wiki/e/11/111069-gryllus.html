Gryllus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Gryllus, son of Xenophon, was honored by the Mantineans for bravery in the battle against Epaminondas and the Thebans. The Athenians, supported by the Thebans, said he mortally wounded Epaminondas. He received a public funeral, a statue on a pillar where he fell, and a horseback carving on a pillar above a tomb near the theatre.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IX.

Gryllus the son of Xenophon is represented on a pillar above a tomb near the theatre, carved as a man on horseback. The Mantineans say that he was the bravest of their own men or allies in the battle against Epaminondas and the Thebans.

CHAPTER XI.

According to the Athenians, with the Thebans supporting their account, Gryllus mortally wounded Epaminondas. The Mantineans gave Gryllus a public funeral and erected his statue on a pillar where he fell, honoring him as the bravest man in the allied army.
