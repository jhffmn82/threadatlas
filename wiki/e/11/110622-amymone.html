Amymone in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Amymone, a daughter of Danaus, searches drought-stricken country for water, accidentally angers a satyr, and is rescued by Poseidon. Poseidon shows her Lerna’s springs; they marry and have Nauplius.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE LIBRARY, II. 1. 1-3

Amymone, one of Danaus’s daughters, searched for water in the drought-stricken country and accidentally struck a sleeping satyr with a dart aimed at a deer. Poseidon intervened when the satyr attacked her, slept with her, and showed her the springs at Lerna. Amymone later bore Nauplius to Poseidon.

Amymone in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Amymone, Danaus’s daughter, lends her name to a river that bounds a sacred grove at Lerna and rises near a plane-tree.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXV.

Amymone was the daughter of Danaus, and the river Amymone is said to take its name from her. A sacred grove at Lerna is bounded on one side by this river, and a plane-tree grows near its source.
