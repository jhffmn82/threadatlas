Nonacris in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Nonacris, once a small town in Arcadia named after Lycaon’s wife, is now ruins, many difficult to trace. Near it, water called the Styx drips from a cliff onto a lofty rock, enters the Crathis, and is described as deadly and destructive.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XVII.

Nonacris was formerly a small town in Arcadia named after the wife of Lycaon. In the present day it is in ruins, and even many parts of those ruins are difficult to trace.

CHAPTER XVIII.

Near Nonacris, water identified as the Styx drips from a cliff onto a lofty rock and then oozes into the river Crathis. The water is described as deadly and as having destructive effects on glass, crystal, stone, pottery, metals, and other materials.
