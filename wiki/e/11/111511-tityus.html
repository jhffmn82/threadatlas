Tityus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Tityus, gigantic son of Zeus and Elare, rose from beneath the earth, attacked Leto from lust, was shot by her children, and in Hades vultures ate his heart.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

IV. Τῶν δὲ Κοίου θυγατέρων ᾿Αστερία μὲν

Tityus was described as a son of Zeus and Elare and as a monstrous-sized child brought from beneath the earth. He attempted to seize Leto, was shot by her children, and was punished after death when vultures ate his heart in Hades.

Tityus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Tityus is a wounded figure in a picture near the treasury of the Sicyonians, worn by continuous punishment to a shadow. He is associated with a sepulchre near the ravine at Panopeus. Homer describes him across nine roods, though this may name where he lay.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IV.

Tityus is associated with a sepulchre near the ravine at Panopeus, whose mound has a circumference of about a third of a stade. Homer describes him as lying across nine roods, though some interpret the passage as naming the place where he lay; Cleon claimed that Tityus and others may have been as large as tradition represented.
