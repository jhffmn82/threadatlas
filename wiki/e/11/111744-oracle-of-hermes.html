oracle of Hermes in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The oracle of Hermes is consulted at evening near the statue of Hermes of the Market-place. A person burns frankincense, lights brazen lamps, lays a brass coin on the altar, whispers a question into the statue’s ear, then takes the next heard sound as the response.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXII.

A person consulting the oracle at evening burns frankincense on the hearth, fills and lights the brazen lamps, places a brass coin on the altar to the right of the statue, and whispers a question into the statue’s ear. The person then leaves, stops up the ears, and treats the next thing heard after removing the hands as the oracular response.
