Tiryns in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Tiryns, a Cyclops-fortified city, became Proetus’s realm after restoration and Argive division, then the place where Hercules lived while serving Eurystheus for twelve years.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE LIBRARY, 11. 1. 5—u. 1

Tiryns was occupied by Proetus after his father-in-law restored him, and the Cyclopes had fortified it for him. Proetus reigned over Tiryns after the division of the Argive territory.

THE LIBRARY, II. tv. 7-8

Tiryns is designated as the place where Hercules must live while serving Eurystheus for twelve years.

Tiryns in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Tiryns, in Prœtus’s kingdom, had visible palace remains and Cyclopean-built walls; it was destroyed by Argives after the Persian war.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XII.

Tiryns belonged to the kingdom of Prœtus, and remains of his palace were still visible there. Its walls were said to have been built by the Cyclopes.
