Tiryns

place · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Τίρυνθος

Abstract written by a model

In The Library, Tiryns was a Cyclops-fortified city that became Proetus’s realm after restoration and Argive division, and the place where Hercules lived while serving Eurystheus for twelve years. In Description of Greece, Tiryns had visible palace remains and Cyclopean-built walls in Prœtus’s kingdom and was destroyed by Argives after the Persian war.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

THE LIBRARY, 11. 1. 5—u. 1

Tiryns was occupied by Proetus after his father-in-law restored him, and the Cyclopes had fortified it for him. Proetus reigned over Tiryns after the division of the Argive territory.

THE LIBRARY, II. tv. 7-8

Tiryns is designated as the place where Hercules must live while serving Eurystheus for twelve years.

Abstract written by a model

Narrative, section by section written by a model

CHAPTER XII.

Tiryns belonged to the kingdom of Prœtus, and remains of his palace were still visible there. Its walls were said to have been built by the Cyclopes.
