CHAPTER XV.

The Orthian Artemis cult at Limnæum centered on a wooden image believed by the Lacedæmonians to be the statue stolen from the Tauric Chersonese by Orestes and Iphigenia. The cult was linked to an old tradition of human sacrifice: after conflicts and killings at the altar, an oracle required human blood, but Lycurgus replaced the sacrifice with the flogging of young men. During the flogging, the priestess held the statue, which was said to become heavy whenever the scourgers spared a young man because of beauty or rank; the goddess was also called Bound-with-willow-twigs because willows had held the image upright.
