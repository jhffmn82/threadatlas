Abstract written by a model

The first Messenian War was a twenty-year conflict between the Messenians and Lacedæmonians. It began with a Lacedæmonian night attack and capture of Amphea during Euphaes’ reign, with Theopompus playing a leading role on the Lacedæmonian side. At its height under Polydorus, it involved repeated battles, raids, sieges, bribery, omens, divine assistance, and resistance associated with Aristomenes, including defeat at the Great Trench and resistance at Eira. It ended with Ithome’s capture and evacuation, followed by Lacedæmonian conquest, tribute, submission oaths, and compulsory mourning.

BOOK III.--LACONIA.

The first Messenian War was at its height during the reign of Polydorus. Theopompus, king of the other royal house, is said to have played the greatest role in its beginning, and the war ended with the conquest of Messenia by the Lacedæmonians.

BOOK IV.--MESSENIA.

The First Messenian War began with the Lacedæmonian night attack on Amphea during the reign of Euphaes. The Lacedæmonians had sworn to continue the war despite its length or reverses until they had won Messenia.

CHAPTER XI.

The first Messenian War ended after the Messenians had resisted for twenty years and evacuated Ithome. The Lacedæmonians then conquered the remaining Messenian cities and imposed tribute, oaths of submission, and compulsory mourning.

CHAPTER XVI.

The war features repeated battles between Aristomenes and the Lacedæmonians, including the defeat at the Great Trench and the prolonged resistance at Eira. Bribery, raids, sieges, omens, and divine assistance all shape its course, while Aristomenes preserves sacred records intended to support a future Messenian return.
