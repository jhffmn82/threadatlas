Oropus

place · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Oropians, townsmen of Oropus
  • In Description of Greece, Oropus is described as a place near the sea between Attica and Tanagra, originally in Bœotia and later Athenian, containing Amphiaraus’ temple, worship, oracle, and healing fountain for coin offerings.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Oropus is described as a town subject to the Athenians, who plundered it twice and occupied it with a garrison before withdrawing.

Oropus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Oropus is a place near the sea between Attica and Tanagra, originally in Bœotia and later Athenian after Thebes’ capture; it contains Amphiaraus’ temple, worship, oracle, and healing fountain for coin offerings.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXI.

Oropus lies near the sea between Attica and Tanagra and contains the temple of Amphiaraus, whose worship began there. Near the temple is a fountain into which people throw gold or silver coins after being cured through the oracle.
