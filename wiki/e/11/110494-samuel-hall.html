Samuel Hall in An Astronomer's Wife

Angelo Hall · 1908

Samuel Hall, also called Sam, is Angeline Hall’s son and one of four boys raised at 18 Gay Street. As a child, he survives the narrator’s attempted attack. In 1887 he studies intensely at college, writes Angeline, reads Goethe aloud, translates Aeschylos, helps with her literary work, and advises on Percival’s furniture.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER XIV.

Samuel Hall, referred to in the unit as Sam, was one of the four boys raised at 18 Gay Street. When the narrator was about six years old, he attempted to kill Sam with a piece of wood but was unsuccessful.

------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER XVI.

Samuel Hall is one of Angeline’s sons. He studies intensely at college, writes to his mother, reads Goethe’s Faust aloud to her, helps her prepare Prometheus Unbound, and translates fragments of Aeschylos; he also assists with decisions about Percival’s furniture.
