Antinous

person · 1 work · 7 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: him

Abstract written by a model

Antinous, born in Bithynium, was excessively beloved by Emperor Adrian. At Mantinea he is regarded as a god, with the city’s latest temple, annual rites, and games every fifth year. Honored through Mantinea’s claimed connection with Bithynium, he is depicted in the Gymnasium as young Dionysus.

Antinous is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER IX.

Antinous is regarded as a god at Mantinea, where he has the city’s latest temple and annual rites, with games held every fifth year. The Mantineans honor him because he was born at Bithynium and the Bithynians were originally Arcadians from Mantinea; his images and paintings in the Gymnasium make him resemble young Dionysus.
