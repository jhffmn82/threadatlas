fugitive slave law in An Astronomer's Wife

Angelo Hall · 1908

The fugitive slave law is condemned by Angeline Stickney as detestable, abominable, infamous, tyrannical, and unjust, chiefly because it pays a commissioner ten dollars for deciding for slavery but five for freedom; she contrasts it with Deuteronomy 23:15–16.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER VII.

Angeline Stickney condemned the fugitive slave law as detestable, abominable, infamous, tyrannical, and unjust. She objected to the provision paying a commissioner ten dollars for deciding in favor of slavery but only five dollars for deciding in favor of freedom, and contrasted the law with Deuteronomy 23:15–16.
