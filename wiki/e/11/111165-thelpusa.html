Thelpusa in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Thelpusa, said to be named after the River-Nymph Thelpusa, stands on a lofty ridge by the Ladon. It has few inhabitants and temples of Æsculapius, the twelve gods, and Eleusinian Demeter. Its marketplace shifted from the centre to the town’s end, and it is associated with Demeter and Æsculapius.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXV.

Thelpusa is about 40 stades from the temple of Causian Æsculapius and is said to have been named after the River-Nymph Thelpusa, daughter of Ladon. It stands on a lofty ridge by the Ladon, has few inhabitants, and contains temples of Æsculapius, the twelve gods, and Eleusinian Demeter. Thelpusa is also associated with the worship of Demeter as Erinys and Lusia and with the exposure of Æsculapius.
