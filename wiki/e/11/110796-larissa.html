Larissa

Abstract written by a model

Larissa is the citadel of Argos, named in Argive tradition after the daughter of Pelasgus and associated with two Thessalian cities of the same name. Approaching it are temples of Hera Dwelling on the Heights and Apollo of the Ridgeway, a race-course for games to Nemean Zeus and Hera, and a monument to the sons of Ægyptus. On its summit stands the roofless temple of Larissæan Zeus; it also contains temples, offerings, and Athene’s temple with Priam’s wooden three-eyed Zeus.

Larissa is in one work on this site, Description of Greece. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XXIV.

Larissa is the citadel of Argos, named after the daughter of Pelasgus and associated with two cities called Larissa in Thessaly. It contains temples of Hera Dwelling on the Heights, Apollo of the Ridgeway, Sharp-eyed Athene, Larissæan Zeus, and Athene, as well as monuments, votive offerings, and the race-course for games to Nemean Zeus and Hera. Its temple of Larissæan Zeus has no roof, and the temple of Athene contains a wooden three-eyed Zeus said to have belonged to Priam and to have been brought there by Sthenelus.
