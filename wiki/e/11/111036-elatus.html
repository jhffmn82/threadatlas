Elatus

person · 2 works · 15 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: EXarTos, Ἔλατος

Abstract written by a model

In The Library, Elatus, a centaur son of Arcas and Leaneira, held power over his land share, married Laodice, and fathered Stymphalus and Pereus. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Elatus first received Mount Cyllene before it was named, later moved to Phocis, aided its people against the Phlegyes attacking Delphi’s temple, founded Elatea, and was honored by a market-place pillar figure as founder or to mark his tomb.

Elatus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Elatus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Elatus, a son of Arcas, first receives Mount Cyllene before it is named, later moves to Phocis, aids its people against the Phlegyes attacking Delphi’s temple, and founds Elatea. He has five sons; a market-place pillar figure honors him as founder or marks his tomb.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXIV.

Elatus, said to be the son of Areas, defended the god when the men of Phlegyas attacked the temple at Delphi and then founded Elatea. A figure of Elatus stands on a pillar in the market-place at Elatea, either in his honour as founder or to mark his tomb.
