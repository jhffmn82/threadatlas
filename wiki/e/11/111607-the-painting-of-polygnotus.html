the painting of Polygnotus

object · 1 work · 22 quoted facts · 9 connections
Also called: a painting, painting, the painting, this painting
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II: The painting of Polygnotus depicts the capture of Ilium and the Greeks’ return. It unfolds along a pebbled seashore, with Menelaus preparing to sail while companions descend, carry water, and dismantle tents. Helen is seated and admired, while Helenus is dejected; Briseis looks at Helen with Diomede and Iphis. The scene includes wounded warriors, Æthra with shaven head, Demophon considering her rescue, Andromache nursing a baby, Trojan women, and Nestor. It also shows Trojan dead, including Priam and Coroebus, Sinon and Anchialus carrying Laomedon’s corpse, and Antenor’s household loading belongings onto an ass with a child seated on it. A couplet identifies Polygnotus of Thasos as the painter.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXV.

The painting of Polygnotus depicts the capture of Ilium and the Greeks’ return, including Menelaus’s ship, tents being taken down, Helen, Trojan women, wounded warriors, Nestor, and a horse rolling near the seashore. It includes a shoreline with visible pebbles, although the rest of the scene does not resemble a sea view.

CHAPTER XXVII.

The painting shows Trojan dead, including Priam and Coroebus, as well as Sinon and Anchialus carrying Laomedon’s corpse. It also depicts Antenor’s household, servants loading belongings onto an ass, and a child sitting on the animal; Simonides’ couplet identifies Polygnotus of Thasos as its painter.
