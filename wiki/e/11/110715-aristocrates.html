Aristocrates in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Aristocrates, son of Icetas, is king and general of the Arcadians and native of Trapezus. Bribed by the Lacedæmonians, he refuses to aid the Messenians, orders the Arcadians to flee at the Great Trench, and abandons the Messenian forces. He later reveals the plan of 500 Messenian volunteers to Anaxander. Exposed as a traitor, he is stoned by the Arcadians and cast beyond their borders.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XVI.

Aristocrates, son of Icetas and king and general of the Arcadians, accepts a bribe from the Lacedæmonians. At the Great Trench he orders the Arcadians to flee, abandoning the Messenian centre and left wing and contributing to the Lacedæmonian victory. He completes his treason by fleeing through the Messenian lines.

CHAPTER XXI.

Aristocrates refused to lead the Arcadians to aid the Messenians because he had been bribed by the Lacedæmonians. He twice betrayed the Messenians, first by abandoning them and later by sending Aristomenes’ plan to Anaxander, and the Arcadians stoned him to death and cast him outside their borders.
