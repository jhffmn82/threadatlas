Athene Poliuchus and Chalciœcus

deity · 1 work · 3 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Athena Chalciœcus, Athene Chalciœcus, Athene called Poliuchus and Chalciœcus

Abstract written by a model

Athene Poliuchus and Chalciœcus is a deity whose temple stood on Sparta’s citadel. Tradition says Tyndareus began it and the Lacedæmonians completed it. Gitiadas made her bronze statue and decorated the temple; Aristomenes hung a shield there. The surrounding citadel held other temples, statues, and figures.

Athene Poliuchus and Chalciœcus is in one work on this site, Description of Greece. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XV.

The temple of Athene Poliuchus and Chalciœcus stood on Sparta’s citadel and was begun, according to tradition, by Tyndareus before being completed by the Lacedæmonians. Gitiadas of Sparta made its bronze statue and decorated the temple with scenes including the Labors of Hercules, the deeds of Castor and Pollux, the birth of Athene, and the figures of Amphitrite and Poseidon. Nearby stood temples of Athene the Worker and Martial Aphrodite, the temple of the Muses, statues of Lysander’s victories, and figures of Pausanias.
