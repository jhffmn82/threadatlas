Demaratus

Abstract written by a model

Demaratus was a well-regarded Spartan king who cooperated with Cleomenes in freeing Athens from the Pisistratidæ. Cleomenes’ hatred, Aristo’s statement about his birth, and a bribed priestess led to his unjust deposition. He then went to Persia to serve Darius.

BOOK III.--LACONIA.

Demaratus was king of the other Lacedæmonian royal house and opposed Cleomenes’ actions. Cleomenes arranged his deposition by using Leotychides and a bribed priestess at Delphi, though the text says Demaratus was removed through hatred rather than on just grounds.

CHAPTER VI.

Demaratus was king and was otherwise well regarded at Sparta, having cooperated with Cleomenes in freeing Athens from the Pisistratidæ. Aristo’s remark about his birth, combined with Cleomenes’ hatred, led to his deposition, after which he went to Persia to serve King Darius.
