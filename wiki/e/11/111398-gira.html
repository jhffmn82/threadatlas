Ægira

Also called: Aegira, the seaport, the town

Abstract written by a model

Ægira, formerly called Hyperesia in Homer, is an Achaean town and seaport whose upper town lies 12 stades from its port; the port is 72 stades from the temple of Hercules Buraicus. Its ancient Apollo temple and temples of Zeus and Artemis, including Artemis the Huntress on the port road, mark its sacred character. It contains statues and cults of many deities, worshipping Celestial Aphrodite most of all. Roads and sea routes connect it to Phelloe and Aristonautæ.

Ægira is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XXVI.

Ægira is both a town and a seaport, with the seaport 72 stades from the temple of Hercules Buraicus and the upper town 12 stades from the port. It contains temples and statues of Zeus, Artemis, Apollo, Æsculapius, Serapis, Isis, Celestial Aphrodite, and the Syrian goddess, as well as a building with statues of Fortune, Cupid, and a grieving family group. A road from Ægira leads about 40 stades over the mountains to Phelloe, and the seaport of Pellene, Aristonautæ, is 120 stades away by sea.
