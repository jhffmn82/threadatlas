Anaximenes of Lampsacus

person · 1 work · 4 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Anaximenes

Abstract written by a model

Anaximenes of Lampsacus was a historian and sophist, noted for histories of Greece, Philip and Alexander and for early extempore oratory. Honored with a statue at Olympia, he saved Lampsacus by persuading Alexander to do the opposite of his oath, and his imitation of Theopompus incurred hostility toward Theopompus.

Anaximenes of Lampsacus is in one work on this site, Description of Greece. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XVII.

Anaximenes had a statue at Olympia through the honor of the people of Lampsacus. He persuaded Alexander to pardon Lampsacus by asking him, under oath, to enslave its people and destroy the city; he also wrote histories, practised extempore oratory, and composed a work imitating Theopompus that brought hatred upon Theopompus.
