The Seven Wise Men

group · 1 work · 11 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: These Seven Wise Men

Abstract written by a model

The Seven Wise Men are a Greek-named group of seven sages: Thales, Bias, Pittacus, Cleobulus, Solon, Chilo, and, according to Plato, Myson instead of Periander. They came to Delphi, offered Apollo the sayings “Know thyself” and “Not too much of anything,” and inscribed them in the temple vestibule.

The Seven Wise Men is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XXIV.

The Seven Wise Men are identified as Thales of Miletus, Bias of Priene, Pittacus of Mitylene, Cleobulus of Lindus, Solon of Athens, Chilo of Sparta, and, according to Plato, Myson of Chenæ instead of Periander. They came to Delphi, offered Apollo the sayings “Know thyself” and “Not too much of anything,” and inscribed them in the vestibule of the temple at Delphi.
