Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXII.

Pharæ is a town in Achaia reckoned with Patræ since the days of Augustus. It lies about 150 stades from Patræ and about 70 stades from the sea, near the river Pierus, and has a large market-place, Hermes’ well, sacred stones, and a grove of Castor and Pollux nearby. Its founder is uncertain: some associate it with Phares, son of Phylodamia, while others suggest someone of the same name.
