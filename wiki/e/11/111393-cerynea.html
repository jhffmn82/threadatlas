Cerynea in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Cerynea is an Achaean town on a hill above the high-road, possibly named for the river Cerynites. It grew more powerful through an influx of people of Mycenæ and later became notable for its temple of the Eumenides.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXV.

Cerynea was a town built on a hill above the high-road, named either for a local ruler or for the river Cerynites. It became more powerful after receiving more than half of the people of Mycenæ and was later notable for its temple of the Eumenides.
