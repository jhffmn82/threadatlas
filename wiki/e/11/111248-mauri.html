Mauri in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The Mauri are the most important independent Libyan tribe, nomadic and formidable because men and wives ride horses. The elder Antonine drives them from all their territory into remote regions, including Mount Atlas and its neighbourhood.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XLIII.

The Mauri were described as the most important tribe of independent Libyans, as Nomads, and as more formidable than the Scythians because they and their wives rode horses instead of traveling in wagons. The elder Antonine drove them from all their territory into remote parts, including Mount Atlas and its neighbourhood.
