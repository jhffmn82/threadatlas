battle of Chæronea in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The battle of Chæronea was a battle and fatal reverse for the Greeks, with Philip victorious; it fell most heavily on the Thebans. Phocians participated after Athenians and Thebans helped restore their towns. Afterwards Philip placed a Macedonian garrison in Thebes and restored the Platæans; Romans also cut down 1,000 Arcadians returning from Elatea at Chæronea.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XV.

Metellus and the Romans met the 1,000 Arcadians returning from Elatea at Chæronea. The Romans cut the Arcadians to pieces.

BOOK IX.--BŒOTIA. / CHAPTER I.

After the battle of Chæronea, where Philip was victorious, Philip introduced a garrison into Thebes and restored the Platæans.

CHAPTER VI.

The battle of Chæronea is described as a reverse that befell all the Greeks, falling most heavily on the Thebans. A Macedonian garrison was subsequently placed in Thebes.

CHAPTER III.

The Battle of Chæronea is described as the fatal defeat of the Greeks. The Phocians participated in it, after the Athenians and Thebans had helped restore the Phocian towns.
