Abstract written by a model

Larymna is a Boeotian coastal city, named after Larymna, daughter of Cynus. Formerly reckoned with Opus, its inhabitants voluntarily joined the Boeotians when the Thebans became powerful. It has a deep harbour, a temple and standing statue of Dionysus, and mountains above it for wild-boar hunting.

Larymna is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XXIII.

Larymna is a Boeotian coastal city named after Larymna, the daughter of Cynus. It was formerly reckoned with Opus, but when the Thebans became powerful its inhabitants voluntarily transferred themselves to the Boeotians. The city has a temple and standing statue of Dionysus, a deep harbor, and mountains above it that provide good wild-boar hunting.
