Polybius

person · 1 work · 9 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Polybius the son of Lycortas, Polybius’

Abstract written by a model

Polybius, son of Lycortas, was a historian, Roman ally, and lawgiver for Greek cities in the Achæan League. He traveled over every land and sea, wrote the history of Rome and the Carthaginian war, and appeased Roman wrath against Greece. Statues and a painted tablet commemorate him; his inscription credits his advice with potentially saving Greece.

Polybius is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XXX.

Polybius the son of Lycortas is represented by a statue on a pillar in the market-place of Megalopolis. An inscription says that he traveled over every land and sea, was an ally of the Romans, and appeased their wrath against Greece. He wrote the history of Rome and the Carthaginian war, and Greek cities in the Achæan League had him establish their constitutions and frame their laws.
