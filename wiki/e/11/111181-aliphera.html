Aliphera in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Aliphera is a partly abandoned small town founded by Alipherus. Many inhabitants left for the Arcadian colony at Megalopolis. It chiefly worships Athene and has temples, an altar, fountain, bronze statue, and a public festival beginning with sacrifice to Muiagrus against flies.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXVI.

Aliphera was a small town that many inhabitants abandoned when the Arcadian colony was formed at Megalopolis. Named for Alipherus son of Lycaon, it has temples of Æsculapius and Athene, an altar to Zeus Lecheates, the fountain Tritonis, and a notable bronze statue of Athene by Hypatodorus. Its people hold a public festival at which they first sacrifice to Muiagrus, whom they invoke against flies.
