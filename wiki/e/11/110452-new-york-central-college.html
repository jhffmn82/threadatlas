New York Central College in An Astronomer's Wife

Angelo Hall · 1908

New York Central College, also called Central College, is a college at McGrawville in Cortland County and a forerunner of Cornell University. An unpopular institution in the surrounding world, it is a stronghold of reformers and abolitionists, supported by John Pierpont, Gerrit Smith, and Horace Greeley. It admits white and Black students of both sexes, welcomes all regardless of tongue or color, and offers knowledge without exclusive rights. In summer 1852 it has about ninety students, low fees, and work opportunities for poor students. Angeline Stickney enters as a junior in 1853, begins senior study in 1854, and remains intensely loyal to her Alma Mater.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

------------------------------------------------------------------------ [Illustration: AN OLD DAGUERREOTYPE] ------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER V.

New York Central College is the institution at which Angeline Stickney studies after arriving at McGrawville. She enters its junior year in September 1853 and begins her senior year there in September 1854.

------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER VI.

New York Central College at McGrawville admitted white and Black students of both sexes and was described as a stronghold of reform, especially abolition. It had about ninety students in the summer of 1852, charged five dollars per three-month term, and encouraged poor students to work their way through college.

------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER VII.

Central College was Angeline Stickney’s Alma Mater and the subject of her intense loyalty. She praised its commitment to admitting people of every tongue and hue and to offering education without exclusive limits, despite expecting to be called a fanatic for attending it.
