Abæ in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Abæ is a Phocian town, said to have been founded by Abas and settled by people from Argos. Sacred to Apollo, it had an oracle, theatre, and market-place. It avoided impiety, temple seizure, and the Phocian or Sacred War, and Romans granted it autonomy for Apollo’s sake.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXV.

Abæ was said by its people to have been founded by Abas, son of Lynceus and Hypermnestra, and its inhabitants claimed to have come to Phocis from Argos. They considered the town sacred to Apollo and possessed an ancient theatre and market-place.
