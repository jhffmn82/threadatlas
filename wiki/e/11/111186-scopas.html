Scopas

person · 2 works · 8 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Parian Scopas, the Parian, the Parian Scopas, the artists of Pergamon

Abstract written by a model

In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Scopas the Parian was the architect of Athene Alea’s great temple and made statues across old Greece, Ionia, and Caria, including Artemis Euclea. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Scopas was a Greek sculptor who decorated the temple of Athena at Tegea and the Mausoleum; his art emphasized violent attitudes, pathos, and tragic action, and the Niobe group was attributed to him.

Abstract written by a model

Scopas was a Greek sculptor born at Paros and active in the first half of the fourth century. He decorated the temple of Athena at Tegea and the Mausoleum. His art emphasized violent attitudes, pathos, and tragic action; the Niobe group was attributed to him.

Scopas was born at Paros and lived during the first half of the fourth century. He did decorative work at the temple of Athena at Tegea and at the Mausoleum, and his art was associated with violent attitudes, pathos, tragic action, and the Niobe group and Raging Bacchante.
