Amphiclea

place · 1 work · 6 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Amphicæa, Ophitea (_Snake-town_)

Abstract written by a model

Amphiclea, 60 stades from Lilæa, was also called Amphicæa and locally Ophitea. Its inhabitants linked it to a snake that protected a child but was killed with him. Its most notable observances were secret Dionysian orgies, an oracle through dreams, prophetic possession of the priest, and dream cures for sickness.

Amphiclea is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XXXIII.

Amphiclea lies 60 stades from Lilæa, and its name differed from the Amphicæa used by Herodotus and the Amphiclea used by the Amphictyones. Its inhabitants associated the place with a story in which a snake protected a child and was accidentally killed with him, and they also performed secret orgies to Dionysus, who was believed to give prophecies and cure sickness through dreams.
