Pellene in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Pellene is an Achaean town next to Ægira, the last Achaean place near Sicyon and Argolis, with Aristonautæ as its seaport and Chelydorea as its mountain boundary. Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXVI.

Pellene is next to Ægira and is the last of the Achaean towns near Sicyon and Argolis. Its people said that it was named after Pallas, one of the Titans, while the Argives attributed the name to the Argive Pellen. Pellene’s seaport was called Aristonautæ.

CHAPTER XXVII.

Pellene is built on the more level parts of a steep hill whose precipitous peak divides the town into two sections. It contains temples and sacred places dedicated to Hermes, Athene, Artemis, Dionysus, Apollo Theoxenius, Ilithyia, and Poseidon, as well as a market-place conduit, an old gymnasium, and civic rules requiring prospective citizens to reach manhood before enrollment. The people of Pellene honor Promachus highly, but do not care to mention Chæron because he accepted a large bribe from Alexander the son of Philip to become tyrant of Pellene.
