Abstract written by a model

The temple of Artemis at Aulis contains two stone statues, one with torches and one like an archer. Near its plane-tree well, it is associated with a doe sacrificed for Iphigenia, Homer’s plane-tree remains, and accepting all victims.

CHAPTER XIX.

The temple of Artemis at Aulis contains two stone statues of Artemis, one holding torches and the other resembling an archer. It is associated with the sacrifice of a doe in place of Iphigenia, the remains of the plane-tree mentioned by Homer, and a custom of accepting all kinds of victims.
