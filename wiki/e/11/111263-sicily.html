Sicily in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Sicily is a small hill near Athens, but an oracle led Athenians to mistake it for a land beyond their borders, contributing to the fatal war against Syracuse. It also appears in Antiochus the Syracusan’s account of Cnidian colonists, expelled from Pachynus before occupying islands including Lipara, and as the intended destination of an Athenian expedition warned against by prodigies.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XI.

Sicily was the subject of an oracle telling the Athenians to colonize it. The Athenians failed to recognize that the oracle referred to a small hill called Sicily near Athens and instead went beyond their borders, leading to the fatal war against Syracuse.

CHAPTER XI.

Sicily is discussed through Antiochus the Syracusan's account of the Cnidian colonists. The colonists built a town at Pachynus but were expelled by the Elymi and Phœnicians before occupying islands including Lipara.

CHAPTER XV.

Sicily is the destination of the expedition for which the Athenians were making preparations when the crows appeared at Delphi. Clitodemus interpreted the crows' destruction of the statue and related objects as one of several prodigies intended to deter the Athenians from the expedition.
