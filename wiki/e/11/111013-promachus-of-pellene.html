Promachus of Pellene in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Promachus of Pellene, son of Dryon, was a pancratiast who won once at Olympia, three times at the Isthmus, and twice at Nemea. Pellene erected statues of him at Olympia and its gymnasium, the latter stone. Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXVII.

Promachus of Pellene, the son of Dryon, won one pancratium victory at Olympia, three at the Isthmus, and two at Nemea. The people of Pellene erected statues of him at Olympia and in the gymnasium, and he is said to have killed most of the enemy facing him in the war between Corinth and Pellene. He is also said to have defeated Polydamas of Scotussa at Olympia, though the Thessalians dispute this.
