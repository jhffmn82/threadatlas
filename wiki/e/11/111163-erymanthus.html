Abstract written by a model

In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Erymanthus lies about 20 stades from the Ladon on the route from Heræa to Elis and forms Heræa’s boundary with Elis. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Erymanthus is a river rising in Mount Lampea, sacred to Pan, flowing by Psophis and through Arcadia before joining the Alpheus as a tributary.

Erymanthus (river) in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Erymanthus (river) first appears

Abstract written by a model

Erymanthus is a river rising in Mount Lampea, sacred to Pan. It flows by Psophis, through Arcadia between Mount Pholoe and Thelpusa, where Psophis honors it with a temple and statue, before joining the Alpheus as a tributary.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIV.

The Erymanthus flows by Psophis after rising in Mount Lampea, which is sacred to Pan and may form part of Mount Erymanthus. It passes through Arcadia, leaving Mount Pholoe on the right and Thelpusa on the left, and becomes a tributary of the Alpheus. The people of Psophis have a temple and statue dedicated to the River-God Erymanthus on its banks.
