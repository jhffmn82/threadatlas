Aleus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Aleus, son of Aphidas and grandson of Arcas, inherited Aphidas’s share and succeeded Æpytus as king by seniority. He founded Tegea in its later form, made it the seat of his kingdom, and built its old temple to Athene Alea. He had sons Lycurgus, Amphidamas, and Cepheus, and daughter Auge, whom he ordered Nauplius to drown.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IV.

Aleus inherited Aphidas’s share and succeeded Æpytus as king because he was his grandson. He built the old temple to Athene Alea at Tegea and had three sons, Lycurgus, Amphidamas, and Cepheus, and one daughter, Auge.

CHAPTER XLV.

Aleus was the founder of the town of Tegea in its later form. He also built the ancient temple of Athene Alea.

CHAPTER XLVIII.

Aleus is said to have ordered Nauplius to take his daughter Auge and drown her in the sea.
