Mothone in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Mothone, formerly called Pedasus, was inhabited by Nauplia after the Nauplians’ expulsion. Its inhabitants linked its later name to Mothone, daughter of Œneus, but the account favors the Rock of Mothon, whose narrowing entrance makes a harbour and breakwater. Trajan granted it a free constitution; it has temples and a pitch-mixed well.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXIV.

Mothone was formerly called Pedasus and was said by its inhabitants to have taken its later name from Mothone, the daughter of Œneus. The account instead suggests that it was named after the Rock of Mothon, which forms a natural harbour and breakwater. Trajan granted the people of Mothone a free constitution, and the town contains temples of Athene and Artemis as well as a well whose water is mixed with pitch.
