Areus

person · 1 work · 6 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Areus, the son of Acrotatus

Abstract written by a model

Areus, son of Acrotatus, was chosen king of the Lacedæmonians instead of Cleonymus. He led them in support of Athens against Antigonus, but withdrew when provisions were consumed. Olympia had statues of him, including one on horseback and one dedicated by the men of Elis.

Areus is in one work on this site, Description of Greece. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

Areus was chosen king instead of Cleonymus and led the Lacedæmonians in support of Athens against Antigonus. Although urged by Patroclus to attack Antigonus, Areus withdrew because the army’s provisions had been consumed.

CHAPTER XII.

He was king of the Lacedæmonians. Olympia contained a statue of him and a second statue of him on horseback; one was dedicated by the men of Elis.
