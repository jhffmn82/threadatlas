Amphea in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Amphea is a small Messenian town near Laconia on a high, well-watered hill, valuable as a war base. Lacedæmonians capture it by night through open gates, kill those found there, including suppliants, occupy it with a garrison, and later use that garrison to ambush Tisis.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK IV.--MESSENIA.

Amphea was a small Messenian town near Laconia, situated on a high, well-watered hill and useful as a base for war. The Lacedæmonians captured it in a night attack while its gates were open and killed the Messenians they found there, including suppliants at temples and altars.

CHAPTER VI.

Amphea is captured by the Lacedæmonians before the events described in the main narrative. Its survivors report the capture to the Messenians, and its Lacedæmonian garrison later ambushes the returning envoy Tisis.
