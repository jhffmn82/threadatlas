Abstract written by a model

Mummius, a newly chosen consul and Roman commander, is ordered to lead naval and land forces against the Achæans, though Metellus hastens to finish the campaign first. Mummius then takes command, defeats the Achæans, captures and burns Corinth, kills or sells many inhabitants, removes treasures, punishes opposing cities, imposes fines, and establishes property-based chief-magistracies.

Mummius is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XV.

Mummius had just been chosen consul when the Romans ordered him to lead naval and land forces against the Achæans. He set out with an army, but Metellus hurried to win the campaign before he arrived.

CHAPTER XVI.

Mummius took command of the Roman army, defeated the Achæans, and captured Corinth on the third day after the battle. He burned Corinth, killed many inhabitants, sold the women, children, and surviving manumitted slaves who had fought for the Achæans, removed its treasures, and destroyed the walls and arms of cities that had fought Rome. He also imposed fines and established property-based chief-magistracies after advisers arrived from Rome.
