Abstract written by a model

In Description of Greece, the Theatre was one of the great sights of Corinth; near it stood the temple of Athene the Bridler, a wooden statue of Hercules attributed to Daedalus, a temple of Jupiter Capitolinus, and an old gymnasium. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Corinthian theater was an immense open-air, daytime venue accommodating ten to thirty thousand spectators; during a performance, cranes and mention of Ibycus exposed his murderers.

Chapter XVIII

The theater associated with Corinth was an immense, open-air structure that could hold thousands of spectators. During a performance there, the appearance of cranes and the reference to Ibycus led the audience to identify his murderers.
