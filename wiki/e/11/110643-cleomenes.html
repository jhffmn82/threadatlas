Abstract written by a model

In Description of Greece, Cleomenes is presented as a Spartan Agid king who seized power, fought in Greece, was defeated at Sellasia, fled to Egypt, and died by self-inflicted violence in madness. In Description of Greece, Cleomenes, son of Leonidas, is identified as the last Agiad king at Sparta, whose deeds and death in Egypt were recorded in connection with Aratus of Sicyon. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Cleomenes is portrayed as a Spartan ruler who replaced the kingdom with autocracy, captured and destroyed Megalopolis, and later made peace with the Achæans alongside Antigonus.

Cleomenes in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Cleomenes first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cleomenes is presented as a Spartan king of the Agid royal house, variously named son of Anaxandrides or Leonidas. He removed Eurydamidas by poison, weakened or abolished the Ephors and Senate in favor of the Great Council of Patronomi, and manipulated a Delphic priestess to depose Demaratus. He fought Argives and Achæans, captured Megalopolis during a truce, and was credited with actions in Athens and Ægina. Defeated by Antigonus and the Achæans at Sellasia, he fled to Egypt, escaped prison, stirred trouble in Alexandria, and died by self-inflicted violence in madness.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

Cleomenes became king in Sparta, removed Eurydamidas by poison, curtailed the authority of the Ephors and Senate, and attacked the Achæans. Antigonus and the Achæans defeated him at Sellasia; he fled to Egypt, later escaped from prison, caused trouble at Alexandria, and killed himself when captured.

CHAPTER XIX.

Cleomenes, the son of Anaxandrides, led the Lacedæmonians against the Argives and caused many of them to perish. He also led them into an Argos stripped of men before Telesilla organized its defense.

BOOK III.--LACONIA.

Cleomenes defeated the Argives, burned the grove where their fugitives had taken refuge, intervened in Athens, and arrested leading men in Ægina for supporting the Medes. He engineered Demaratus’ deposition by bribing the priestess at Delphi and later died in a fit of madness by stabbing and hacking himself.

CHAPTER XXIX.

Cleomenes, son of Leonidas and grandson of Cleonymus, captured Megalopolis during a truce. He was later defeated at Sellasia, where the Messenians joined Aratus and the Achæans in taking Sparta.

Cleomenes in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Cleomenes first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cleomenes, son of Leonidas and king of the other Spartan royal family, is portrayed as a ruler who replaced the kingdom with his own autocracy. He captured Megalopolis in peacetime, killed captives, destroyed and burned the city, and later offered its people return under a treaty. He also made peace with the Achæans alongside Antigonus.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXVII.

Cleomenes the son of Leonidas captured Megalopolis in time of peace. He killed the inhabitants he captured, razed the city to the ground, and burned it, while the account also notes that he had changed the Lacedæmonian constitution from a kingdom to an autocracy.

CHAPTER XLIX.

Cleomenes captured Megalopolis, prompting Philopœmen to lead many of its inhabitants to Messene. He later sent word that he regretted the capture and offered to let the people return if they signed a treaty, but Philopœmen persuaded them to return only with arms.
