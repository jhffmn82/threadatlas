Science in An Astronomer's Wife

Angelo Hall · 1908

Science is presented as a friend of Faith and a force for regenerating humanity through discoveries, knowledge, material comforts, and increased good sense. It supports progress, but material civilization requires ethical teaching and a divine enthusiasm nourished by religion. Angeline and Professor Hall regard science and faith as compatible: science can widen inherited belief toward broader truths. Even before the physicist’s last analysis, faith endures. In the epilogue, Science is figuratively the bride of the mother’s husband, joined with him in love and learning.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER XV.

Angeline Hall and Professor Hall looked to science as a means of regenerating humanity, although the text argues that knowledge also depends on a divine enthusiasm nourished by some form of religion. In her later thought, science and faith were not necessarily enemies: science could lead faith from narrow inherited beliefs toward wider truths.

------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER XVI.

Science is discussed as a force that advances knowledge, material comforts, and good sense through discoveries and their application. Angeline argues that philosophy and science should remain united, while ethical teaching must prevent material civilization from standing alone.

------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER XVII.

The unnamed woman could face the last analysis of the physicist while maintaining her faith in God and the witness of her own being.

------------------------------------------------------------------------ EPILOGUE.

Science is portrayed as the bride whom the mother's husband wed. Science and the mother's husband together loved and learned.
