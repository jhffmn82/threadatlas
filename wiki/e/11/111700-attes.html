Attes in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Attes is a mysterious figure associated with the temple of the Dindymene Mother at Dyme and honoured by Lydia after celebrating her rites. His identity remains uncertain: traditions make him Calaus’s son, incapable of procreation, or Sangarius’s grandson, goat-raised and self-mutilated after Agdistis disrupted a wedding.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XVII.

Attes was associated with the temple of the Dindymene Mother at Dyme, but the narrator could not ascertain who he was because the subject was a mystery. One tradition made him the son of the Phrygian Calaus and incapable of procreation; another said he was born to the daughter of the river-god Sangarius, brought up by a goat, and mutilated himself during a wedding disrupted by Agdistis.
