Pheneus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Pheneus is a district and settlement reached by a road near Amilus through a ravine, lying below Caryæ. Its territory is bounded by mountains and neighboring places: it shares a mountain boundary with Orchomenus and Caphya, is separated from Pellene by Chelydorea, and meets Stymphelus at Geronteum. It has a precipitous, partly fortified citadel, temples of Hermes and Eleusinian Demeter, a ruined temple of Athene Tritonia, and tombs of Iphicles and Myrtilus. The people honor Iphicles as a hero, observe rites likened to those at Eleusis, preserve traditions concerning Demeter, Naus, Trisaules, and Damithales, and sacrifice to Apollo and Artemis at the ruined temple of Pythian Apollo. Old Pheneus was said to have been destroyed by a deluge; Hercules was said to have lived there and made a canal and water pits, though the river later left his canal. Roads lead west to Clitor or Nonacris and the Styx, and toward Geronteum; nearby Styx water is harmful to people.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIII.

Pheneus was reached by a road branching near Amilus and passing through a ravine. Its territory shared a mountain boundary with Orchomenus and Caphya, and Caryæ lay at the end of the ravine leading toward it.

CHAPTER XIV.

Pheneus lies below Caryæ, and the old Pheneus was said to have been destroyed by a deluge. Hercules was said to have lived there, dug a channel for the river Olbius, and made pits to receive water from the plain; the river later abandoned his canal. Pheneus had a precipitous citadel with a partly fortified area, a ruined temple of Athene Tritonia, the tomb of Iphicles, a temple of Hermes, and the tomb of Myrtilus.

CHAPTER XV.

Pheneus has a temple of Eleusinian Demeter and celebrates rites said to be like those at Eleusis. Its people maintain traditions about Demeter, Naus, Trisaules, and Damithales, and they still sacrifice to Apollo and Artemis at the ruins of the temple of Pythian Apollo.

CHAPTER XVI.

Pheneus is the district reached when traveling eastward from it toward Geronteum and through which one passes after leaving Geronteum on the left. Geronteum forms its boundary with Stymphelus.

CHAPTER XVII.

Pheneus is separated from Pellene by the mountain Chelydorea. From Pheneus, the westward road branches left toward Clitor and right toward Nonacris and the water of the Styx.

CHAPTER XIX.

Pheneus has the water called Styx near it, which is harmful to people. A westward road from Pheneus leads toward Clitor along the canal dug by Hercules for the river Aroanius, passing through Lycuria.
