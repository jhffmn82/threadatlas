Heræa

Abstract written by a model

Heræa is a town founded by Heræus son of Lycaon, mostly on a gentle eminence beside the right bank of the Alpheus, with part reaching the river. It has a route to Elis crossing the Ladon, race-courses, baths, Dionysus and Pan temples, Hera ruins, and Damaretus, foremost Arcadian athlete and first Olympia heavy-armour race victor.

Heræa is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXVI.

Heræa was founded by Heræus son of Lycaon and lies mostly on a gentle eminence on the right of the Alpheus, with part of the town extending to the river. It has race-courses, baths, temples of Dionysus, a building for the orgies of Dionysus, a temple of Pan, and ruins of a temple of Hera. Damaretus of Heræa was the foremost of the Arcadian athletes and the first victor at Olympia in the race in heavy armour.
