temple of Athene at Tegea in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The temple of Athene at Tegea is a place containing notable votive and sacred objects: the Calydonian boar’s hide, rusty fetters of Lacedæmonian captives, Athene’s bed, Auge’s effigy, Marpessa’s armour, statues of the Muses and Mnemosyne, and an altar said to be made by Melampus.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XLVII.

The temple contains the hide of the Calydonian boar, rusty fetters once worn by Lacedæmonian captives, the bed of Athene, an effigy of Auge, and the armour of Marpessa. It also contains statues of the Muses and Mnemosyne, and its altar is said to have been made by Melampus.
