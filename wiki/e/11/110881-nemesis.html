Nemesis

deity · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Nemeses

Abstract written by a model

In Description of Greece, Nemesis is honoured in a seaside temple at Rhamnus and is especially implacable toward haughty men; Phidias fashioned her marble statue from Persian-brought stone, with a crown, stags, Victories, an apple branch, and a bowl. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Nemesis is a goddess worshiped as two goddesses by Smyrna, linked by differing parentage to Night or Oceanus, and appearing with another goddess in Alexander's dream to direct him to build a town and move Smyrna's inhabitants.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXI.

Nemesis has a temple at Rhamnus and is described as especially implacable toward haughty men. Phidias made her statue from the Parian marble brought by the Persians for a projected trophy, depicting her with a crown, figures of stags, small Victories, an apple branch, and a bowl.

Nemesis in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Nemesis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nemesis is a goddess, worshiped as two goddesses by Smyrna and linked by differing parentage to Night or Oceanus. She appears with another goddess in Alexander's dream, directing him to build a town and move Smyrna's inhabitants.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER V.

Nemesis appeared to Alexander the son of Philip in a dream, together with another goddess, and instructed him to build a town and relocate Smyrna's inhabitants. The people of Smyrna worshiped two Nemeses and said that their mother was Night, while the Athenians at Rhamnus said Nemesis was the daughter of Oceanus.
