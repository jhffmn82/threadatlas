Fitzroy McPherson in The Case-Book of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1921-10

Fitzroy McPherson is a science master at The Gables, an athlete and year-round swimmer despite heart trouble following rheumatic fever. Secretly engaged to Maud Bellamy, he is found staggering from the beach with severe injuries and dies at the bathing-pool after what appears to be an assault. The inquest initially fails to explain his death, but it is ultimately attributed to contact with the radiating, stinging filaments of Cyanea capillata.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE ADVENTURE OF THE LION'S MANE

Fitzroy McPherson, the science master at The Gables, is found staggering up from the beach and dies after suffering severe unexplained pain. He had prepared to swim but apparently never entered the water, and he was secretly engaged to Maud Bellamy.

A week passed. The inquest had thrown no light upon the matter and had

Fitzroy McPherson died at the bathing-pool with strange injuries that initially suggested a violent assault. Sherlock Holmes concludes that he was killed by the stinging filaments of Cyanea capillata, and that his final words, “Lion's Mane,” warned of the creature.
