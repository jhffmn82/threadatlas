Hermione

Abstract written by a model

Hermione is a neighboring town in the Trœzenian region, with an old city on a rocky headland and a new city four stades away on a gentle slope. Its people say Hermion, son of Europs, founded it, and Dorians from Argos later colonized it amicably. It is especially marked by many temples, including those of Poseidon, Athene, Demeter, Proserpine, Apollo, Dionysus Melanægis, Artemis Iphigenia, and Demeter Chthonia, called most worthy of notice. It has two wells, uses Mases as its port, and its old well never runs dry.

Hermione is in one work on this site, Description of Greece. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXII.

Hermione was a neighboring town within the Trœzenian region, whose old city occupied a rocky headland and whose new city stood about four stades away on a gentle slope. The old and new towns contained temples of Poseidon, Athene, Demeter, Proserpine, the Sun, the Graces, Serapis, Isis, and several forms of Aphrodite. The people of Hermione said that their old city was founded by Hermion, son of Europs, and that Dorians from Argos later colonized it amicably.

CHAPTER XXXV.

Hermione has temples of Dionysus Melanægis, Artemis under the name of Iphigenia, Poseidon, Vesta, Apollo, Fortune, Demeter Chthonia, Clymenus, Ares, and Ilithyia. It also has two wells, one supplied by a hidden channel, and uses Mases as its port.
