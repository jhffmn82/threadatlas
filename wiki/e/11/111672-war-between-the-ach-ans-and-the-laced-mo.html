war between the Achæans and the Lacedæmonians in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

A war between the Achæans and Lacedæmonians arose from disputes over Lacedæmonian jurisdiction and Roman authority. Though the Lacedæmonians sought alternatives to fighting, the war resumed despite Roman orders to await arbitration, ending in their defeat, Achæan occupation of nearby towns, and Menalcidas's truce violation.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XII.

War broke out after disputes over whether the Lacedæmonians could pass sentence on themselves and whether the matter could be referred privately to the Roman Senate. The Lacedæmonians believed they were unable to fight the Achæans and sought other means of responding.

CHAPTER XIII.

The war was renewed after differences between the Achæans and the Lacedæmonians, despite Roman orders that the Achæans wait for arbitrators. It continued through the defeat of the Lacedæmonians, the Achæan occupation of nearby towns, and Menalcidas's violation of the truce.
