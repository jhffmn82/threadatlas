Miltiades in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Miltiades, Athens’s archon and a Marathon general, failed to capture Paros, then died; his monument was at Marathon, where he was depicted in the Painted Chamber.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIV.

Miltiades is shown prominently among the Athenian commanders in the Painted Chamber's depiction of Marathon. His son Cimon later drove the inhabitants of Scyrus away and brought Theseus's bones to Athens.

Miltiades in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Miltiades, Cimon’s son and Athens’ chief magistrate, became Greece’s first public benefactor by defeating barbarians at Marathon and checking the Persian host.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER LII.

Miltiades, son of Cimon, defeated the barbarians who landed at Marathon and checked the Persian host. He is identified as the first public benefactor of all Greece.
