Critolaus

person · 1 work · 5 quoted facts · 1 connection

Abstract written by a model

Critolaus, son of Hicetaon, succeeded Diæus as General of the Achæans. Driven by an unreasoning desire to fight Rome, he deceived Roman envoys, gathered and commanded an Achæan army, and led it toward war against Sparta and Rome. Rejecting Metellus’ peace proclamation, he blockaded Heraclea, then fled toward Scarphea; after battle, he was neither seen alive nor found among the dead.

Critolaus is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XIV.

Critolaus succeeded Diæus as General of the Achæans and was possessed by an unreasoning desire to fight the Romans. He deceived the Roman envoys at Tegea, collected an Achæan army at Corinth, and persuaded it to war against Sparta and Rome; the text identifies his audacity and weakness with the ruin of himself and the Achæans.

CHAPTER XV.

Critolaus rejected Metellus’ peace-oriented proclamation and blockaded Heraclea with the Achæans. He fled toward Scarphea when Metellus crossed the Spercheus, and after the ensuing battle he was not seen alive or found among the dead.
