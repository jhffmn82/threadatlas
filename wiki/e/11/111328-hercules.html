Hercules

person · 1 work · 10 quoted facts · 9 connections
Also called: Egyptian Hercules, Hercules Charops, Hercules Hippodetes, Hercules surnamed Hippodetes, Idæan Hercules, Maceris, the god

Abstract written by a model

Hercules is variously identified as one of the Idæan Dactyli and as having reached Delphi before the son of Amphitryon; Egyptians and Libyans called him Hercules. He was about a cubit high beside Demeter and was made as a brazen statue by Ageladas. Associated with Cerberus, a raft from Tyre to Erythræ, temples, and the Labours, he took Orchomenians’ horses by night, tied them to chariots, and became Hippodetes.

Hercules is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER V.

Hercules was associated with a wooden raft said to have sailed from Tyre in Phœnicia to Erythræ. His temple there was considered exceptionally ancient.

CHAPTER XXXII.

A joint temple to Hercules and Hermes was no longer in existence except for its altar. Hercules was also represented among the square Workers and was famed for completing his many Labours.

CHAPTER XXVI.

Hercules Hippodetes is said to have taken the horses of the Orchomenians by night and tied them to their chariots, giving him the surname Hippodetes.
