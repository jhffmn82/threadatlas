Thamyris

person · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Thracian Thamyris, Θάμυρις

Abstract written by a model

In The Library, Thamyris is a beautiful minstrel, son of Philammon and Argiope, who loved Hyacinth, fathered Rhesus, challenged the Muses in music, and, after being defeated, lost his eyes and minstrelsy. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Thamyris is depicted near Pelias as a blind, dejected figure with thick hair and beard, handling a broken lyre with torn strings.

III. Ζεὺς δὲ γαμεῖ μὲν “Ἥραν, καὶ τεκνοῖ

Thamyris was the son of Philammon and the nymph Argiope. He fell in love with Hyacinth and was the first to become enamoured of males. He challenged the Muses to a musical contest, was defeated, and lost both his eyes and his minstrelsy.
