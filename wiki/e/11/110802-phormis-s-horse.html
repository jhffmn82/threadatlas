Phormis’s horse in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

A brazen horse, made by Dionysius the Argive and dedicated by Phormis, stands on a polished basement with its tail lost. Though smaller and less beautiful than many horses at Olympia, it attracts stallions year-round, provoking mounting attempts stopped only by force or whips.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXVI.

One of Phormis’s bronze horses bore an inscription identifying it as dedicated by Phormis the Arcadian from Mænalus, then a Syracusan. Though inferior in size and beauty to many horses at Olympia and damaged by the loss of its tail, it irresistibly aroused stallions, which repeatedly tried to mount it until restrained by force or whips; the narrator attributes this to a magician’s cunning.
