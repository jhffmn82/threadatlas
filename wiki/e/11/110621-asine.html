Asine in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Asine is a ruined seaside town in Messenia, formerly the chief town of Asinæa. Its Dryope inhabitants preserve their identity through annual mysteries of Dryops and a temple with an ancient statue. Descended from people defeated by Hercules, they settled first in Asine in Argolis, then in Messenia with Lacedæmonian permission. After aiding the Lacedæmonians against Argive territory, Asine was captured and razed by the Argives, though Apollo’s temple remained.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXV.

Asine was formerly the chief town of the district called Asinæa, and its ruins lie near the sea. Its people joined the Lacedæmonians in ravaging Argive territory, but after the Lacedæmonians withdrew, the Argives captured and razed the town, leaving the temple of Apollo standing.

CHAPTER XXXIV.

Asine lies by the sea, about 40 stades from Colonides and about the same distance from the Promontory of Acritas. Its people, descendants of the Dryopes, say that they were defeated by Hercules, later settled in Asine in Argolis, and were eventually allowed by the Lacedæmonians to settle in Messenia. They retained their settlement after the restoration of the Messenians and celebrate annual mysteries of Dryops.
