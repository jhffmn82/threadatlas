Abstract written by a model

Thisbe is a coastal city in Bœotia beyond a coastal mountain and plain. It has a temple and standing stone statue of Hercules, for whom its inhabitants celebrate a festival. A strong embankment and annual water diversion keep the plain from becoming a lake and allow dry land to be cultivated. The city is said to be named for the local Nymph Thisbe.

Thisbe is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XXXII.

Thisbe is a city on the coast of Bœotia, situated beyond a coastal mountain and a plain at the foot of another mountain. It has a temple and standing stone statue of Hercules, and its inhabitants hold a festival for him; the plain nearby is kept from becoming a lake by an embankment and the diversion of water. The city is said to have taken its name from Thisbe, a local Nymph.
