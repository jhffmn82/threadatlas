Attalus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Attalus, a king, son of Attalus and nephew of Philetærus, received the kingdom from his cousin Eumenes. His greatest achievement was driving the Galatians from the coast into inland Galatia. He also made portrayals of giants, Amazons, Marathon, and Galati, and created the Lycian Apollo; his exploits later lacked adequate record.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

Attalus is mentioned among the rulers whose actions had become ancient and whose exploits had not been adequately recorded.

CHAPTER VII.

Attalus I, nephew of Philetærus, received the kingdom from his cousin Eumenes. His greatest stated achievement was compelling the Galati to leave the coast and settle inland in Galatia.

Attalus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Attalus, king of Pergamus from the East, cooperates with Romans, commands troops under Philopœmen, and is foretold as a bull-horned helper bringing destruction to the Galati.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XV.

Attalus, king of Pergamus, is identified as the bull-horned helper in Phaennis's oracle. The bull image signifies that he would bring a day of destruction to the Galati.
