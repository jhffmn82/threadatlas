Potniæ in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Potniæ is a ruined place about 10 stades from Thebes, with a grove of Demeter and Proserpine, statues by the river, rites involving sucking pigs, a temple of Dionysus Ægobolus, and a well believed to make local horses mad.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VIII.

Potniæ was a ruined place about 10 stades from Thebes, with a grove dedicated to Demeter and Proserpine and statues by the river called the Potnian goddesses. Its people performed rites involving sucking pigs, maintained a temple of Dionysus Ægobolus, and showed a well believed to make local horses mad if they drank from it.
