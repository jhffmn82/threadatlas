Platæans

group · 1 work · 11 quoted facts · 9 connections
Also called: Plataeans, the Platæans

Abstract written by a model

The Platæans are thought to be the original inhabitants of their land, named for Platæa, probably daughter of Asopus. In early history they were ruled by kingship. They fought alongside the Athenians after Xerxes’ landing, helped defeat Mardonius on their own soil, repulsed the barbarian, and received spoil for a temple to Arean Athene after the Battle of Marathon. Twice driven from Platæa and twice restored, they returned to Athens after the first capture and later occupied Platæa with a strong garrison. Their religious life includes temples to Arean Athene and Eleusinian Demeter, offerings to Apollo, restoration of Gargaphia, and the smaller Dædala procession to Mount Cithæron.

Platæans is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Platæans in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK IX.--BŒOTIA. / CHAPTER I.

The Platæans are thought to have been the original inhabitants of their land and to have taken their name from Platæa, who was probably the daughter of the king Asopus. They fought with the Athenians after Xerxes's landing, helped repel Mardonius at Platæa, were twice driven from their country, and were twice restored.

CHAPTER III.

The Platæans held a smaller Dædala in an oak-coppice near Alalcomenæ by identifying the tree on which a crow perched after taking boiled meat. They carved a wooden image from that tree, and at the great festival they decorated images, carried them to the Asopus, and brought them by wagon to Mount Cithæron.

CHAPTER IV.

The Platæans have a temple to Arean Athene built from spoil given to them by the Athenians after the Battle of Marathon. They later made the water of the fountain Gargaphia pure again after it had been fouled by Mardonius and the Persian cavalry.
