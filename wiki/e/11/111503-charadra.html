Charadra in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Charadra is a settlement on a lofty ridge about 20 stades beyond Lilæa. Its chief constraint is scarce water, supplied only by the Charadrus three stades downhill, while its chief advantage is the best planting, sowing, and pasture land near the Cephisus. Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXIII.

Charadra lies about 20 stades beyond Lilæa on a lofty ridge. Its inhabitants had little water, relying on the Charadrus, a stream three stades down the hillside that flows into the Cephisus; the market-place contained altars to Heroes, identified by some as Castor and Pollux and by others as local heroes.
