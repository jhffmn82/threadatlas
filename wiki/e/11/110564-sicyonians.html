Sicyonians

group · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 7 connections
Also called: people of the Sicyonians, the Sicyonians
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the Sicyonians planned to invade Hyperesia, withdrew after mistaking goat-borne torches for allied campfires, later destroyed Donussa, and had an empty treasury at Delphi.
  • In Description of Greece, the Sicyonians preserved traditions about their rulers, foundations, tombs, temples, festivals, and sacred images; sacrificed to Aphrodite by burning victims' thighs except those of wild boars; became Dorians, joined the Achæan league under Aratus, and suffered a devastating earthquake.

CHAPTER VI.

The Sicyonians preserved traditions about their rulers, foundations, tombs, temples, festivals, and sacred images. They became Dorians, joined the Achæan league under Aratus, and suffered a devastating earthquake that left the city nearly depopulated and destroyed many works of art.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXVI.

The Sicyonians planned to invade the land of the Hyperesians. They withdrew after mistaking torches fastened to goats’ horns for the campfires of allied troops, and they later destroyed Donussa.

CHAPTER XI.

The Sicyonians have a treasury at Delphi, although no money is present in it.
