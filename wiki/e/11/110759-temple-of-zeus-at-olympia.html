Abstract written by a model

The Temple of Zeus at Olympia is a Doric stone building designed by Libon of Elis, 68 feet high, 95 feet wide, and 230 feet long. Its Pentelican marble-tile roof and gables show the chariot race between Pelops and Œnomaus and the Lapithæ-Centaurs fight. It contains a gold-and-ivory statue, veil, offerings, statues, crowns, shields, pillars, and dedications.

CHAPTER VIII.

The temple of Zeus at Olympia was a Doric stone building designed by Libon of Elis, measuring 68 feet high to the gable, 95 feet wide, and 230 feet long. Its roof was made of Pentelican marble tiles, and its gables displayed the chariot race between Pelops and Œnomaus and the fight between the Lapithæ and Centaurs.

CHAPTER XI.

The Temple of Zeus at Olympia houses the gold-and-ivory statue, a woollen purple veil, votive offerings, imperial statues, crowns, shields, pillars, and other dedications. Its ante-chapel contains the horses of Cynisca, while one pillar records a hundred-year alliance between Elis, Athens, Argos, and Mantinea.
