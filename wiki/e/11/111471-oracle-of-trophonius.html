oracle of Trophonius

place · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 2 connections

Abstract written by a model

In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the oracle of Trophonius is described as a small circular walled enclosure containing an artificial, oven-shaped chamber entered by a consulter lying flat and offering honey cakes. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the oracle of Trophonius is located at Lebadea in Boeotia, where visitors perform ceremonies before entering a cave and return walking backward, melancholy and dejected.

oracle of Trophonius in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where oracle of Trophonius first appears

Abstract written by a model

The oracle of Trophonius is a small circular walled enclosure above the grove on the mountain, with brazen pillars, girders, and doors. It contains an artificial, oven-shaped chamber; a consulter enters its narrow opening lying flat, offering feet, knees, body, and honey cakes.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXIX.

The oracle of Trophonius is situated above the grove on the mountain inside a small circular stone wall with brazen pillars, girders, and doors. Its artificial chamber is shaped like an oven, is about four cubits wide and eight cubits deep, and has a narrow opening through which the consulter is drawn after lying flat and presenting his feet, knees, body, and honey cakes.
