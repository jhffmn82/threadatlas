Galatian invasion of Greece in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The Galatian invasion of Greece, during Anaxicrates' archonship, threatened Greek liberty and security. Greeks assembled allied forces at Thermopylæ to oppose the Galati. The invasion culminated in a march on Delphi; the Greeks defeated and harried the invaders in retreat, destroying them in Phocis and preventing their return.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XX.

During the Galatian invasion, the Greeks believe that not only their liberty but also their future security is at stake. They assemble a substantial allied force at Thermopylæ, including infantry, cavalry, ships, and contingents from Bœotia, Phocis, Locri, Megara, Ætolia, Athens, Macedonia, and Asia.

CHAPTER XXIII.

The Galatian invasion of Greece culminated in the march on Delphi and the destruction of the invading force in Phocis. The Galati were defeated by the Greeks, repeatedly harried during their retreat, and prevented from returning home.
