Mr. and Mrs. Hall in An Astronomer's Wife

Angelo Hall · 1908 · the work’s page · read it where Mr. and Mrs. Hall first appears

Abstract written by a model

Asaph and Angeline Hall, a married couple, had four sons and later grandchildren. They lived modestly in Cambridge, first on Concord Avenue near the observatory, rented rooms and sublet one, then occupied Bond cottage for three years from spring 1859 and later lived on North Avenue. Initially snubbed, they formed lasting intellectual, religious, and antislavery friendships. Though they quarreled, their conflicts clarified household duties and labor, and their marriage was remembered as happy.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER XI.

The Halls lived very modestly in Cambridge, renting rooms and even subletting one to a German pack-peddler. Although they were initially snubbed because they were poor and without social connections, they later formed lasting friendships and became involved in local intellectual, religious, and antislavery circles.

------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER XII.

Although Mr. and Mrs. Hall quarreled, Sarah Waitt later said that they had one of the happiest married lives she had known. Their conflicts eventually contributed to a clearer understanding of their separate duties and a division of labor in their household.
