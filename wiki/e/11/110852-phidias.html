Phidias in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Phidias first appears

Abstract written by a model

Phidias, an Athenian son of Charmides and brother of Panænus, was a maker of statues. He made a brazen Athene from Marathon spoil, the Lemnian Athene, and the gold-and-ivory Zeus at Olympia, whose throne he decorated. His workshop worked on his statues, while others contributed shield carvings. An inscription identifies him as Zeus’s maker, and tradition says Zeus answered his prayer after the work was finished.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXVIII.

Phidias made the brazen statue of Athene from spoil taken from the Persians who landed at Marathon. He also made the Lemnian Athene, described as one of his finest works. The carvings on the shield of the brazen Athene were said to be by Mys, with Parrhasius drawing their outlines for him.

CHAPTER VIII.

Phidias of Athens, son of Charmides, is identified by an inscription as the maker of the statue of Zeus at Olympia. Alcamenes is described as his contemporary and second only to him as a statuary.

CHAPTER XI.

Phidias made the gold-and-ivory statue of Zeus and decorated its throne with figures and scenes. He represented the three Graces and three Seasons above Zeus's head, and tradition says that Zeus answered Phidias's prayer about the finished work by striking the pavement with lightning.
