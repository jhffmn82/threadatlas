Amyclæan Apollo throne in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The throne of Amyclæan Apollo is an object covered with mythological carvings. Graces and Seasons stand at its front and back, while Hydra and Typhos occupy the left and Tritons the right. Its scenes include Atlas; Hercules’ contests, labors, and victory over the Nemean lion; Perseus and Medusa; Theseus leading the Minotaur; gods bringing gifts to Harmony; Achilles fighting Memnon; Bellerophon killing the Chimæra; and other figures and episodes. Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XV.

The throne of Amyclæan Apollo had Graces and Seasons at its front and back, the Hydra and Typhos on the left, and Tritons on the right. Its many carvings included the carrying off of Taygetes and Alcyone, Atlas, Hercules’ contests and labors, the Centaurs, the Minotaur, Perseus and Medusa, the rape of the daughters of Leucippus, Dionysus, Athene and Hercules, Achilles and Memnon, Bellerophon and the Chimæra, Castor and Pollux, and numerous other mythological scenes. Beneath the throne were further scenes including the Calydonian boar, the Harpies, Helen’s abduction, the Nemean lion, Apollo and Artemis killing Tityus, and the offerings of the Trojans at Hector’s grave.
