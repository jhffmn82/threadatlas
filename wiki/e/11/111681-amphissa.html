Amphissa

Also called: town

Abstract written by a model

Amphissa was a town about 120 stades from Delphi, the largest and most famous of the Ozolian Locrians. Its original inhabitants were Locrians; later, ashamed of the Ozolian title, they joined the Ætolians. Its notable sights were tombs of Amphissa and Andræmon and a citadel temple and statue of Athene, and it celebrated Anactes rites.

Amphissa is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XXXVIII.

Amphissa was about 120 stades from Delphi and was the largest and most famous town of the Ozolian Locrians. It contained the tombs of Amphissa and Andræmon, a temple and statue of Athene in its citadel, and celebrated rites for youths called Anactes. The inhabitants later joined themselves to the Ætolians, and many Ætolians may also have migrated there after Augustus established Nicopolis.
