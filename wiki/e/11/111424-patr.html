Patræ in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Patræ is a town in Achæan territory, about 80 stades from Pirus and near Glaucus at the river’s mouth. Preugenes and his son Patreus built it from Lacedæmon; after Patreus enlarged walls near Aroe, it developed from Aroe, Anthea, and Mesatis and took his name. Its people allied with the Achæans against the Lacedæmonians, aided the Ætolians against the Galati, endured reverses and poverty, and later gained inhabitants from various towns under Augustus. It received the statue of Laphrian Artemis from Calydon and celebrates Laphria. Its temples, statues, sea grove, oracle well, and devotion to Aphrodite are prominent. By Imperial order it is reckoned with Pharæ and Tritea.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

Patræ was a city built in Achæan territory by Preugenes and his son Patreus from Lacedæmon, and it was named after Patreus. The Achæans later allied themselves with the people of Patræ against the Lacedæmonians and were friendly with the Athenians.

CHAPTER XVIII.

Patræ developed from Aroe, Anthea, and Mesatis after Patreus enlarged the walls near Aroe and gave the city his name. Its people later aided the Ætolians against the Galati, suffered severe military reverses and poverty, and were eventually augmented by inhabitants from various towns under Augustus. Patræ also received the statue of Laphrian Artemis and celebrated the festival of Laphria.

CHAPTER XXI.

Patræ has temples and statues of Dionysus, Poseidon, Aphrodite, Apollo, Demeter, Serapis, and Æsculapius. It has a grove near the sea with a race-course and a well whose water, viewed through a mirror after prayer and incense, gives an oracle about whether a sick person will recover or die. The women at Patræ are twice as numerous as the men, are devoted to Aphrodite, and many earn their living by making hair nets and other dress items from flax grown in Elis.

CHAPTER XXII.

Patræ is the town with which Pharæ and Tritea are reckoned by Imperial order. Pharæ is about 150 stades from Patræ, while the promontory of Rhium is about 50 stades from Patræ and the river Milichus and temple of Triclaria are not far from it.
