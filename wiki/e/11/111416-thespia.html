Thespia in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Thespia is a town under Mount Helicon, named in differing accounts for Thespia, daughter of Asopus, or Thespius. Its notable features are temples, statues, a theatre, and a market-place worth seeing, including an old stone Eros and statues of Zeus Soter, Dionysus, Fortune, Hygiea, Athene the Worker, and Plutus. Its Eros by Praxiteles went to Rome, returned under Claudius, was removed by Nero, and burned.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXVI.

Thespia lies under Mount Helicon, about 50 stades from the temple of the Cabiri by the leftward route. It is said to have been named after Thespia, the daughter of Asopus, or after Thespius, the son of Erechtheus from Athens. The town contains statues of Zeus Soter, Dionysus, Fortune, Hygiea, Athene the Worker, and Plutus.

CHAPTER XXVII.

Thespia is described as having temples, statues, a theatre, and a marketplace worth seeing. Its statue of Eros by Praxiteles was removed to Rome, restored to Thespia by Claudius, removed again by Nero, and eventually destroyed by fire.
