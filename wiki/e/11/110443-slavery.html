slavery in An Astronomer's Wife

Angelo Hall · 1908

Slavery is presented in Angeline’s 1845 composition as an unjust robbery and piracy that deprives people of liberty, condemned by the Bible and maintained in the United States. Angeline argues that slaves value freedom, hopes the country renounces slavery, and later urges dissolving the Union rather than obeying the fugitive slave law to strike it a death blow.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER II.

Slavery is condemned in Angeline’s 1845 composition as an unjust practice maintained even in the United States. She argued that slaves valued freedom as much as their masters and hoped that the country would follow places that had renounced slavery.

------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER VII.

Angeline Stickney argued that the Union should be dissolved rather than submit to the fugitive slave law. She described slavery as a robbery and piracy, said the South should be left to repent, and maintained that dissolution would strike slavery a death blow.
