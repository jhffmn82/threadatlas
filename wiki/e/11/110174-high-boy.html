Narrative, section by section

8 sections; each heading opens the text

The Giant Horse of Oz

The Giant Horse is identified by the book’s title. The poem describes a real high horse whose legs stretched up and down and which could run very fast.

CHAPTER 9

High Boy is Joe King's giant purple horse with telescope legs, a white mane, an umbrella tail, and red, roguish eyes. He carries Joe King, Philador, and Herby rapidly up the purple mountain and stops at the city gates.

CHAPTER 10

High Boy carries Joe King, Philador, and Herby into Up Town and greets Queen Hyacinth. After the storm, he searches for and finds Philador and Herby, then carries them toward the Emerald City at a very fast pace.

CHAPTER 14

High Boy, described as the only high horse in Oz, meets the travelers and briefly mistakes the Scarecrow's greeting for "hay." He agrees to carry Trot and her companions to the Emerald City, travels at several heights and speeds, and lets a herd of cattle pass beneath him.

CHAPTER 15

High Boy steps over Shutter Town's wall, drops the Out Keeper into pickle bushes, and carries the travelers across a river by standing on its river bed with his riders above the water. His umbrella tail releases a deluge of water on the travelers, and after taking Herby's pills he races toward the Emerald City.

CHAPTER 17

High Boy carries Trot, the Scarecrow, and the others through the Emerald City and performs proudly for the crowd. He objects to being called a pack horse, helps gain entry to the Castle of Cheeriobed, and repeatedly complains about being hungry.

CHAPTER 19

High Boy carries Ozma, Scraps, Trot, Betsy, and Dorothy through Sapphire City. He greets the returning sea horses in the high horse tongue and later stands at the center of the magic ring used for travel.

CHAPTER 20

High Boy takes part in the homecoming celebration and sits with the palace pets at the banquet. He accepts the appointment of Joe and Hyacinth as rulers of the North, rushes off to tell Joe, and promises Trot and Philador that they will have high positions at the northern court.
