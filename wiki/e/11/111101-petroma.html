Also called: By Petroma

Abstract written by a model

Petroma is a pair of large fitting stones near the temple of Eleusinian Demeter, enclosing writings concerning the rites beneath a round covering bearing the likeness of Cidarian Demeter. Each year, during the great rites, it is opened, the writings are read to initiates, and it is closed that night; it also anchors a solemn oath.

Petroma is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XV.

Petroma consists of two large stones fitted together near the temple of Eleusinian Demeter. During the annual great rites, the stones are separated, writings about the rites are read to the initiated, and the stones are replaced that same night; many inhabitants of Pheneus regard “By Petroma” as their most solemn oath.
