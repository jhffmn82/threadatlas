Tritons in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Tritons are marine creatures that speak with a human voice and reportedly sing through perforated shells. They have frog-wort-like hair, shark-like scales, gills, human-like noses, large mouths, animal teeth, greyish-blue eyes, shellfish-like hands, and dolphin-like fins. The speaker saw them at Rome and Tanagra, Rome's being smaller.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXI.

Tritons are described as having frog-wort-like hair, shark-like scales, fish gills under their ears, a human-like nose, a large mouth, animal teeth, greyish-blue eyes, shellfish-like hands and nails, and dolphin-like fins under the breast and belly. The speaker has seen Tritons among the curiosities at Rome and at Tanagra, with the one at Rome being smaller.
