Epidaurian sanctuary of Æsculapius

place · 1 work · 5 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: grove, precincts, sacred grove of Æsculapius, temple of Æsculapius

Abstract written by a model

The Epidaurian sanctuary of Æsculapius is a walled sacred district where no births or deaths occur, though sacrifices are consumed. Centered on Æsculapius’s temple and ivory-and-gold statue, it includes a sleeping-place for suppliants, healing inscriptions, Rotunda paintings, Polycletus’s theatre, Artemis and Maleatian Apollo temples, stadium, fountain, mountains, and Antonine additions. Its traditions culminate in healing, including Hippolytus’s restoration to life.

CHAPTER XXIV.

The Epidaurian sanctuary of Æsculapius is enclosed by walls, and no births or deaths take place within its precincts, although sacrifices are consumed there. It contains a temple and an ivory-and-gold statue of Æsculapius by Thrasymede, a sleeping-place for suppliants, the Rotunda with paintings by Pausias, healing inscriptions, a theatre designed by Polycletus, temples, a stadium, a fountain, and buildings added or restored by Antonine. The sanctuary also includes the mountains Titthion and Cynortion, with a temple of Maleatian Apollo, and records traditions of healing, including the restoration of Hippolytus to life.
