Abstract written by a model

Euphaes, king of the Messenians, succeeds his father Antiochus as king of Messenia. After the Lacedæmonians capture Amphea, he urges resistance, trains and commands his people, is mortally wounded fighting Theopompus, dies after thirteen years, and is succeeded by Aristodemus.

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK IV.--MESSENIA.

Euphaes succeeded his father Antiochus as king of Messenia. During his reign, the Lacedæmonians launched their first attack by capturing Amphea.

CHAPTER VI.

Euphaes urges the Messenians not to despair after Amphea falls and trains them for war. He leads them in battle, is mortally wounded while fighting Theopompus, survives briefly, and dies after ruling for thirteen years.
