Massaliotes in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The Massaliotes were colonists of the Phocæans in Ionia, descended from refugees fleeing Phocæa from Harpagus the Mede. They later defeated the Carthaginians at sea, prospered, and offered a large brass statue to Athene Pronoia for victory.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VIII.

The Massaliotes offered a large brass statue in the ante-chapel of the temple of Athene Pronoia. They were colonists of the Phocæans in Ionia, descended from those who fled Phocæa from Harpagus the Mede, and they later prospered after defeating the Carthaginians in a naval engagement.
