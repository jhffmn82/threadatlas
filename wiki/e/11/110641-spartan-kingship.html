Spartan kingship

topic · 1 work · 2 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: hereditary office, kingdom, royal houses, two royal houses

Abstract written by a model

Spartan kingship was a divided monarchy in Lacedæmon, held by two royal houses descended from Procles and Eurysthenes, twin sons of Aristodemus. Though succession generally favored primogeniture, it repeatedly faced disputes and depositions, ending with Cleomenes, son of Leonidas.

BOOK III.--LACONIA.

Spartan kingship was divided between two royal houses descended from Procles and Eurysthenes, the twin sons of Aristodemus. Succession generally followed primogeniture, as shown when Cleomenes received the throne over the more highly regarded Dorieus.

CHAPTER VI.

The Spartan kingship passed through repeated disputes and depositions, including the contests involving Cleonymus and Areus, Leonidas and Cleombrotus, and Demaratus and Leotychides. The unit also records the eventual end of the royal line with Cleomenes, son of Leonidas.
