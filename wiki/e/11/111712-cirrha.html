Cirrha

place · 1 work · 6 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: the seaport of Delphi, the town

Abstract written by a model

Cirrha, formerly called Crisa in Homer and the Hymn to Apollo and reportedly named after Nymph Cirrha, lay about 60 stades from Delphi. Its people impiously ravaged Apollo’s sacred land; the Amphictyones captured and punished them. It then became Delphi’s seaport, containing a temple of Apollo, Artemis, and Leto and statues.

Cirrha is in one work on this site, Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER XXXVII.

Cirrha was also called Crisa by Homer and was said to have taken its present name from the Nymph Cirrha. Its people committed acts of impiety against Apollo and ravaged land sacred to him, after which the Amphictyones captured the town and punished its inhabitants. Cirrha then became the seaport of Delphi and contained a temple of Apollo, Artemis, and Leto, with Attic statues, as well as a smaller statue of Adrastea.
