Lord Aylmer

Abstract written by a model

Lord Aylmer was governor of Canada from 1830, instructed to carry out Lord Goderich's proposed financial compromise. Though described as initially friendly, conflict with Papineau helped drive him toward the Chateau Clique amid worsening racial and political tension in Lower Canada. The Patriote press blamed him for deaths in the 1832 Montreal riot; Papineau accused him of slaughtering victims, and the Patriotes alleged that he deliberately introduced cholera-infected immigrants. The Ninety-Two Resolutions demanded his removal and impeachment. He was recalled by the British government so a governor could also be chief royal commissioner. British authorities cleared him of censure but judged his poor relations with the Assembly an obstacle to settling the dispute.

{21} CHAPTER IV THE RISE OF PAPINEAU In the year 1812 a young man took his seat in the House of Assembly for Lower Canada who was destined to play a conspicuous part in the history of the province during the next quarter of a century.

Lord Aylmer became governor of Canada in 1830 and was instructed to implement Lord Goderich's proposed financial compromise. The text describes him as disposed to be friendly, but Papineau's attitude toward him helped drive him toward the Chateau Clique.

{33} CHAPTER V THE NINETY-TWO RESOLUTIONS After 1830 signs began to multiply that the racial feud in Lower Canada was growing in intensity.

Lord Aylmer was blamed by the Patriote press for the deaths in the Montreal riot and was accused by Papineau of slaughtering victims. The Ninety-Two Resolutions demanded his removal and impeachment, while the Patriotes also accused him of deliberately bringing cholera-infected immigrants into the province.

{44} CHAPTER VI THE ROYAL COMMISSION A general election followed soon after the passing of the Ninety-Two Resolutions and revealed the strength of Papineau's position in the country.

Lord Aylmer was recalled to make way for the appointment of a governor who could also serve as chief royal commissioner. The British authorities expressly relieved him from censure, while recognizing that his relations with the Assembly made him unlikely to help resolve the dispute.
