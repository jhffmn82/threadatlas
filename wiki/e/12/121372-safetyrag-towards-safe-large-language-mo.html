SafetyRAG: Towards Safe Large Language Model-Based Application through Retrieval-Augmented Generation

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

This paper presents SafetyRAG, a Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework intended to make LLM-based applications more resistant to unsafe requests, including jailbreaks, adversarial prompts, and prompt injections. It addresses the continuing vulnerability of safety-aligned LLMs and positions SafetyRAG as a comparatively simple alternative or complement to measures such as instruction tuning, reinforcement learning, ToxicChat, XSTest, and NeMo Guardrails. Rather than requiring additional model training, SafetyRAG retrieves explicit safety facts relevant to a user prompt and adds them to the model’s response context alongside application knowledge. The framework is tested in a medical-assistance question-answering application. Its safety-facts document was built from 521 harmful AdvBench requests and contains 1,563 facts spanning unsafe topics. The study compares the application with and without this document using GPT-3.5-turbo, Llama-2-13B, Gemma-7B, and Mistral-7B-instruct, including 100 paraphrased unsafe AdvBench prompts. Without prompt injection, SafetyRAG raised blocking to 100% for GPT-3.5-turbo, Llama-2, and Gemma-7B and improved results for all tested models. Under prompt injection, interception reached 65% for GPT-3.5, 93% for Llama-2, 59% for Mistral-7B-instruct, and 88% for Gemma-7B. The findings show that retrieved safety facts can improve refusals, but effectiveness depends partly on the underlying model’s alignment; Mistral-7B sometimes produced harmful material despite relevant facts. The paper concludes that SafetyRAG is a cost-effective, implementable way to improve safer outputs across applications, especially against prompt injection, while calling for optimization for less aligned models, real-world evaluation, study of long-term user effects, and coverage of more unsafe behaviors. Sihem Omri led the research, experiments, analysis, and writing; Manel Abdelkader and Mohamed Hamdi provided supervision and feedback. The authors report no conflict of interest and thank Dr. Karen McNeil for review and feedback.

I. INTRODUCTION

The unit concerns Large Language Models (LLMs), their use in business applications, and the risks of prompt injection attacks. It explains how Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) indexes, retrieves, and supplies external document context to an LLM without domain-specific retraining. The paper introduces SafetyRAG, which adds safety-related information to the RAG context to improve resistance to malicious requests. Experiments with GPT-3.5-turbo, Llama-2-13B, Gemma-7B, and Mistral-7B-Instruct in a medical question-answering application indicate that SafetyRAG is more robust to prompt injection than an application without the layer.

II. LITERATURE REVIEW / A. Threats of Unsafe Prompts for LLMs .. B. Unsafe Prompt Mitigation

The unit reviews how LLMs remain vulnerable to unsafe prompts, jailbreak prompts, adversarial suffixes, prompt injection, and malicious fine-tuning despite safety alignment. It discusses mitigation methods including instruction tuning, reinforcement learning, ToxicChat, XSTest, and NeMo Guardrails, while noting their limitations in detecting or preventing harmful behavior. The unit presents SafetyRAG as a simpler mitigation framework that uses a RAG architecture to provide explicit information about harmful prompts and encourage safer LLM behavior.

III. MATERIALS AND METHODS / A. Overall Framework .. 2) Baseline LLM models

The unit presents SafetyRAG, a framework based on the RAG architecture that helps a large language model recognize unsafe user prompts through retrieval of relevant safety facts. Its safety facts document was constructed from 521 harmful requests in the AdvBench benchmark, producing 1,563 facts covering diverse unsafe topics. The framework was evaluated in a medical assistance application that retrieves information from a medical knowledge base, both with and without the safety facts document. Experiments used GPT-3.5-turbo, Llama2-2-13B, Gemma-7B, and Mistral-7b-instruct as baseline LLM models.

IV. RESULT AND DISCUSSION .. C. Discussion

The study evaluates SafetyRAG as a method for improving the resilience of a medical assistant application against 100 paraphrased unsafe prompts from AdvBench. Without prompt injection, SafetyRAG increased the blocking rate to 100% for GPT-3.5-turbo, Llama-2, and Gemma-7b, while improving performance across all tested models. With prompt injection, SafetyRAG increased interception rates to 65% for GPT-3.5, 93% for Llama-2, 59% for Mistral-7B-instruct, and 88% for Gemma-7b. The results indicate that SafetyRAG is most effective when combined with strong embedded model alignment, while Mistral-7B sometimes generated harmful content despite receiving relevant safety facts.

V. CONCLUSION

The paper presents SafetyRAG, a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework for improving the safety of LLM-based applications without further training. SafetyRAG helps LLMs incorporate appropriate safety facts, significantly improving interception of unsafe prompts, especially prompt injection, and producing safer outputs. Its effectiveness depends in part on the underlying alignment of the base LLM, while the framework is described as cost-effective and easy to implement across industry applications. Future work includes optimizing SafetyRAG for less aligned models, evaluating real-world effectiveness, studying long-term user impacts, and mitigating a wider range of unsafe behaviors.
