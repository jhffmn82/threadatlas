medical practice in Dr. Elsie Inglis

Lady Frances Balfour · 1918

Medical practice is portrayed as a demanding, compassionate vocation spanning hospital and private work. In London and Dublin during 1892–1894, it included ward rounds, temperature monitoring, cautery, anaesthesia, operations, outpatient care, consultations, and disputes over diagnosis and observation. Dr. Inglis later practised in Edinburgh with Dr. Jessie MacGregor, moving from Atholl Place to 8 Walker Street and earning a substantial income before combining practice with suffrage work and the Hospice. Elsie’s work served both paying and very poor patients, relying on kindness, sympathy, prescriptions, and disciplined acceptance of professional anxiety.

The unit shows medical practice involving ward rounds, high-temperature monitoring, galvanic cautery, anaesthesia, operations, outpatient care, and consultation. It also records disputes over diagnoses, the treatment of illnesses labeled hysteria, and differing Scottish and London approaches to observing patients under anaesthesia.

Dr. Inglis began practice in Edinburgh with Dr. Jessie MacGregor, first in Atholl Place and later at 8 Walker Street. Her practice was eventually combined with political work, suffrage activity, and the founding and administration of the Hospice, although she had earlier made quite a large income from it.

Sorrow and disease are much the same whether they come to the rich or the poor,

Elsie’s medical practice required sympathy and understanding among both paying patients and the very poor. She could awaken hope in patients through personal kindness as well as prescription, and she continued to manage professional anxiety through disciplined acceptance of what she had done.
