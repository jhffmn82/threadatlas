Mrs. Inglis in Dr. Elsie Inglis

Lady Frances Balfour · 1918

Mrs. Inglis, Harriet Louis Thompson, was John Inglis’s wife, daughter of an early Indian civilian, and one of nine sisters, most of whom married into the Indian Civil Service. An accomplished rider and driver and deeply religious, she lived in Southampton during a separation from her husband, where she founded and ran a large working-men’s club and gave Sunday religious lectures. As mother of Elsie Maud Inglis and her sisters, she carefully recorded their early development, taught them prayers and Bible lessons, and guided daily life. Remembered as quiet and strong, with an enduringly influential smile and eyes, she moved with her family from India to Tasmania and Edinburgh, corresponded with Elsie in Paris, and helped decide, through prayer with Mr. Inglis, that Elsie should go there. Soon after Elsie returned, Mrs. Inglis died of a virulent attack of scarlet fever.

Harriet Louis Thompson, John Inglis’s wife, was the daughter of an early Indian civilian and one of nine sisters who mostly married into the Indian Civil Service. She was an accomplished rider and driver, deeply religious, and while living in Southampton during a separation from her husband, she founded and ran a large working-men’s club where she gave Sunday religious lectures.

CHAPTER II ELSIE MAUD INGLIS 1864-1917 ‘Lo, children are an heritage of the Lord: and the fruit of the womb is His reward.

Mrs. Inglis cared for Elsie and her sisters, recorded their early development, taught them prayers and Bible lessons, and guided their daily life. She is remembered as a quiet, strong mother whose eyes and smile remained influential in her children’s memories.

CHAPTER III THE LADDER OF LEARNING 1876-1885 ‘Hast thou come with the heart of thy childhood back: The free, the pure, the kind?

Mrs. Inglis moved with her family from India to Tasmania and Edinburgh and corresponded with Elsie during her time in Paris. She helped decide that Elsie should go to Paris and joined Mr. Inglis in praying for guidance about the decision. After Elsie returned, Mrs. Inglis died from a virulent attack of scarlet fever.
