Abstract written by a model

Colonel Charles Gore, a Waterloo veteran, commands British troops in the Battle of St Denis and leads the column in the rebellion’s first engagement. As Lower Canada grows threatening in autumn 1837, he takes five companies of the 24th regiment and a twelve-pounder from Sorel to St Denis. After an overnight march, his force attacks the defended village but withdraws after more than five hours, short of ammunition. Checked on November 23, he returns December 1, recovers a howitzer and five wounded men, and repudiates ordering house burnings without stopping excesses.

] {69} CHAPTER VIII THE DOGS OF WAR As the autumn of 1837 wore on, the situation in Lower Canada began to assume an aspect more and more threatening.

Colonel Charles Gore led five companies of the 24th regiment and a twelve-pounder from Sorel toward St Denis. After a difficult overnight march, he attacked the strongly defended village but withdrew after more than five hours when his force made little progress and ammunition was running low.

{82} CHAPTER IX _FORCE MAJEURE_ The check administered to Colonel Gore's column at St Denis, in the first engagement of the rebellion, was the only victory which fell to the rebel forces.

Colonel Gore led a column up the river from Sorel and reached St Denis on November 23, where his forces were checked. He returned to St Denis on December 1, recovered a howitzer and five wounded men, and later repudiated ordering the burning of insurgents' houses without preventing the excesses.
