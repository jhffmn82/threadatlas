Abstract written by a model

Russians are the unit’s official and local hosts, supporters, patients, and allies. They provide base hospitals for Serbian wounded and their own troops; near Odessa, officials, hospitals, soldiers, and officers assist the unit, while Russian wounded form most of its patients. Soldiers and citizens express gratitude and hope in the Allied cause, welcome changes in Petrograd, and support the hospital. The records end by calling them, especially common soldiers, fascinating people treated by the English Sisters.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

‘NEARING ODESSA, ‘_Sep.

Russian officials along the railway and in Odessa assisted the unit generously. Russian hospitals took Serbian patients when possible, Russian soldiers helped the unit obtain water, and Russian officers praised the Serbian troops; Russian wounded also formed most of the patients received by the unit.

’ ‘IN AN AMBULANCE TRAIN, ‘NEAR ODESSA, _Jan.

Russian soldiers and citizens express respect and gratitude toward Elsie and her unit. They welcome the changes in Petrograd, attend religious services, and emphasize their hope in the Allied cause and England.

‘_On Active Service.

The Russians are described as a fascinating people, especially the common soldier. Russian soldiers support the hospital, while Russian patients receive treatment from the English Sisters.
