Thomas Storrow Brown

Abstract written by a model

Thomas Storrow Brown was a Montreal iron-merchant who became a leader in the 1837 rebellion in Lower Canada. A lieutenant of Papineau, he asked William Lyon Mackenzie to begin an uprising in Upper Canada to draw troops from Lower Canada. By what he later called accident, he became commander of the Patriote force at St Charles, though he had no military experience, was severely injured after Montreal rioting, and was a stranger to most of his fewer than two hundred men. During the engagement, he tried to rally them but, finding his authority insufficient, abandoned the command and rode to St Denis. He is also remembered by Canadian numismatists as an early issuer of a halfpenny token bearing his name.

Thomas Storrow Brown asked William Lyon Mackenzie to begin an uprising in Upper Canada to draw troops away from Lower Canada. He became commander of the Patriote force at St Charles, later explaining that a mere accident had placed him at the head of the rebel force.
