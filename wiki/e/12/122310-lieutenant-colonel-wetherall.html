Lieutenant-Colonel Wetherall in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

Lieutenant-Colonel Wetherall commands British forces—infantry, volunteer cavalry, and two field-guns—ordered to attack the rebel position at St Charles. After delaying on Major Gugy’s advice, he advances, defeats the rebels at St Charles, and does not continue to pacify St Denis.

1 section; each heading opens the text

{82} CHAPTER IX _FORCE MAJEURE_ The check administered to Colonel Gore's column at St Denis, in the first engagement of the rebellion, was the only victory which fell to the rebel forces.

Lieutenant-Colonel Wetherall marched with infantry, volunteer cavalry, and two field-guns against St Charles. He delayed his advance after advice from Major Gugy and later moved on St Charles, where his troops defeated the rebels; he did not proceed to pacify St Denis.
