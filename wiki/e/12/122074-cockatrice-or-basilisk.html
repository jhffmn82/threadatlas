cockatrice or basilisk in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The cockatrice or basilisk, king of the serpents, is produced from a cock’s egg hatched under toads or serpents. It has several species, a crowned crest or comb, and advances lofty and upright. It may burn what it approaches, kill by look, breath, or poison, kill shrubs, and split rocks. Rue repels it; a rue-eating weasel defeats it, and a cock’s crow makes it expire. Its carcass protects places from spiders and swallows.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXIX

The cockatrice or basilisk is described as the king of serpents, produced from a cock’s egg hatched under toads or serpents, and capable of killing through fire, breath, gaze, or poison. It is said to flee from a weasel after the weasel eats rue, and to die when it hears a cock crow; its carcass was also believed to protect places from spiders and swallows.
