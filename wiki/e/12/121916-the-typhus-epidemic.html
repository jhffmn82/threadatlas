the typhus epidemic in Dr. Elsie Inglis

Lady Frances Balfour · 1918

The typhus epidemic was an epidemic in Serbia during the First World War. Beginning in filthy, overcrowded hospitals after the Austrian invasion, it swept across Serbia in spring 1915 and raged for three months, devastating Serbian people. It killed 125 of Serbia’s 425 doctors and affected two-thirds of the remainder. It made crowded-hospital work dangerous and harsh for the Scottish women. After the fever died out, the Scottish unit feared its return, but cleaning, sanitation, laundry, bathing, and education about dirt prevented cases in its hospital.

The typhus epidemic was devastating the Serbian people in the spring of 1915. It formed the setting for the Scottish women’s work in crowded hospitals and contributed to the severe dangers and privations they endured.

opening

The typhus epidemic began in hospitals left filthy and overcrowded after the Austrian invasion and swept across Serbia. It raged for three months, killed 125 of Serbia’s 425 doctors, and affected two-thirds of the remainder.

’ As the fever died out, a worse enemy came in.

Typhus had been a major earlier calamity, but the Scottish unit feared its return amid overcrowding, underfeeding, fatigue, and depression. Through cleaning, sanitation, laundry, bathing, and education about the connection between dirt and typhus, the unit recorded no cases of the disease in its hospital.
