St. Columba

person · 1 work · 11 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Columba, apostle of the Highlands

Abstract written by a model

St. Columba was an Irish presbyter and monk, called the apostle of the Highlands. In 563 he traveled in a wicker boat with twelve friends, landed on Iona, received it from the king, and founded its monastery. He overcame Druids and hostile neighboring peoples, led Highlands inhabitants and adjacent districts to profess Christianity, and spread the Scriptures through the Highlands and Islands of Scotland. Though not a bishop, he held authority over bishops and advised princes.

St. Columba is in one work on this site, Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXXIV

St. Columba, described as the apostle of the Highlands, arrived in the region and supplanted the Druids. He led the inhabitants of the Highlands and adjacent areas to profess Christianity.

IONA

Columba was an Irishman related by birth to the princes of Ireland who landed on Iona with twelve followers in 563. He overcame opposition from Druids and hostile neighboring peoples, obtained the island from the king, founded a monastery, and labored to spread knowledge of the Scriptures through the Highlands and Islands of Scotland. Though only a presbyter and monk, he exercised authority over the province and its bishops, and princes sought his counsel.
