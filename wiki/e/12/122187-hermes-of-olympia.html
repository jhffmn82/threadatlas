Hermes of Olympia

object · 1 work · 3 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: The Hermes, the great Hermes of Olympia

Abstract written by a model

An authentic work of Praxiteles, discovered in 1877, showing Hermes as a beautiful man without traditional divine symbols, tenderly supporting Dionysus. Though missing its right arm and lower legs, its head and torso remain remarkable.

Hermes of Olympia is in one work on this site, Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

In Athens, after Pheidias, the greatest sculptures were those

The Hermes of Olympia is the one authentic work of Praxiteles that has come down from the master's own hand. Though missing the right arm and both legs below the knees, it preserves a marvellous head and torso and shows Hermes as a beautiful man tenderly supporting the child Dionysus.
