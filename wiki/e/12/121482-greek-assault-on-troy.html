Greek assault on Troy

Abstract written by a model

The Greek assault on Troy is a battle in which Neoptolemus leads the Argive army against Trojan defenders and the city’s gates and walls. Massed forces clash beneath Troy, with Neoptolemus and Deiphobus leading opposing slaughter as Apollo and Poseidon aid the sides. The Argives drive Trojans into the city, then attack its defenses with missiles, ladders, twibills, shields, and levers. Trojans resist from the walls, and Aeneas prevents decisive Argive progress. Though Troy’s walls and food suggest endurance, omens foretell its downfall.

BOOK VIII

The Greek assault drives the Trojans into Troy, after which the Argives attack the city's gates and walls. They hurl themselves against the gates while arrows, stones, and javelins fall from the defended towers.

BOOK IX

The battle before Troy begins with massed Greek and Trojan forces charging each other beneath the walls. Neoptolemus and Deiphobus lead opposing slaughter, while Apollo and Poseidon intervene on behalf of the two sides.

BOOK X

The Trojans fear that the Achaeans will attack their walls, but Polydamas argues that Troy’s strong walls and stored food can withstand a long siege. The later omens indicate that the city will nevertheless be brought low.
