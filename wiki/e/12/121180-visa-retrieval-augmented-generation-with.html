Abstract written by a model

The document presents Retrieval Augmented Generation with Visual Source Attribution (VISA), a visual end-to-end retrieval-augmented generation framework designed to make generated answers easier to verify. Instead of citing whole retrieved documents, VISA uses document screenshots and vision-language models to generate an answer, identify a relevant document, and draw a bounding box around the exact region that supports the answer. This preserves evidence in its original visual context, including structured or multimodal document content. The work positions VISA against text-only RAG with document-level citations and distinguishes it from object detection, visual grounding, and GUI-agent systems. VISA treats answer generation, document selection, and box-coordinate prediction as next-token prediction. Because zero-shot prompting is insufficient for precise attribution, the authors fine-tune models initialized from Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct and Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct on dedicated data. The authors introduce Wiki-VISA, built from Natural Questions and rendered Wikipedia webpages, and Paper-VISA, based on medical-domain scientific-paper layouts from PubLayNet. Paper-VISA uses instruction-tuned VLMs to create synthetic queries and short answers from highlighted regions. FineWeb-VISA, made from FineWeb-edu webpage screenshots, is used for training only. Evaluation pairs each query with a positive document, answer, and evidence box, adds hard negatives, and sometimes removes the ground-truth document to test “No answer” detection. A Document Screenshot Embedding model retrieves candidates. Evaluation measures answer quality with relaxed exact match and grounding with IoU at 0.5, including single-oracle and multi-candidate settings. Results show that zero-shot Qwen2-VL-72B can attain moderate answer accuracy but has poor bounding-box accuracy. Fine-tuning substantially improves visual grounding, though outcomes vary by document length, evidence position, and modality. VISA-7B can often identify relevant sources and handle “No Answer” cases when the oracle document is absent. Further analysis finds that a modular text-based pipeline using PubLayNet boxes, pytesseract OCR, and Qwen2-VL’s language model has stronger zero-shot box accuracy but weaker answer accuracy because of OCR errors. Cross-domain transfer is difficult, while combined training data improve generalization. Main errors are wrong source attribution, position misalignment, and granularity mismatch. The document concludes that VISA provides finer-grained visual attribution, while noting limits for long answers, multi-section evidence, complex documents, large candidate sets, and untested user expectations.

Abstract

The abstract concerns Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Visual Source Attribution (VISA), an approach for improving source attribution in retrieval-augmented generation systems. VISA uses large vision-language models to identify evidence and highlight exact supporting regions with bounding boxes in retrieved document screenshots. The authors introduce two evaluation datasets, Wiki-VISA and Paper-VISA, based on Wikipedia webpages and medical-domain document layouts. Experimental results show that VISA can perform visual source attribution on documents’ original appearance while also revealing challenges for further improvement.

Introduction

Retrieval-augmented generation has become a key technique for enhancing reliability in information-seeking processes. The traditional RAG pipeline directly generates an answer to a user query from retrieved candidate documents. Users may struggle to verify sources and appropriately trust generated answers because models can produce hallucinated content. Recent works have introduced the generation with citation paradigm.

opening

Retrieval Augmented Generation with Visual Source Attribution (VISA) is a visual, end-to-end retrieval-augmented generation framework that uses document images to generate answers and identify supporting evidence. A vision-language model returns a bounding box for the relevant region in one or more retrieved document images, enabling users to inspect evidence directly in its original context. The authors introduce Wiki-VISA and Paper-VISA to evaluate visual source attribution across webpages and scientific papers. Experiments find that zero-shot QWen2-VL-72B struggles with precise attribution, while fine-tuning VISA improves visual attribution accuracy.

This unit reviews source attribution in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and bounding box drawing with vision-language models (VLMs). This section defines VISA as a source attribution method for vision-based RAG systems, where a system answers a textual query, identifies the most relevant document, and locates supporting content with a bounding box. VISA uses VLMs to process query text and document screenshots, generating the answer, document identifier, and bounding-box coordinates as a next-token prediction task. Because zero-shot prompting is challenging, the method requires supervised fine-tuning on a dedicated VISA task dataset. The authors introduce Wiki-VISA, derived from Natural Questions and rendered Wikipedia webpages, and begin describing Paper-VISA, derived from PubLayNet.

The unit describes how Paper-VISA uses instruction-tuned VLMs to generate synthetic queries and short answers from highlighted regions in PubLayNet paper screenshots, while FineWeb-VISA is created from screenshots of sampled FineWeb-edu webpages. It explains that Paper-VISA supports VISA training and evaluation for medical-domain scientific paper PDFs, whereas FineWeb-VISA is used only for training because its quality is not guaranteed to match human-annotated data. For RAG evaluation, each query is paired with a positive document, answer, and evidence box, then combined with hard negative candidates, with a 20% chance of replacing the ground-truth document to test no-answer detection. The system uses a Document Screenshot Embedding model as a visual retriever with k=20 and m=3.

This unit describes how VISA is evaluated and trained for answer generation and visual attribution using Wiki-VISA and Paper-VISA. Evaluation measures generated answers with relaxed exact match and bounding box predictions with IoU at a 0.5 threshold, while reporting performance across passage and non-passage evidence categories. VISA is evaluated in Single oracle candidate and Multi-candidate settings, including cases where the ground-truth document is or is not among the candidates. Models initialized from Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct and Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct are fine-tuned to generate answers and bounding boxes, and in the multi-candidate setup to identify relevant documents or output “No answer.”

5

The unit reports experimental results for VISA on the Wiki-VISA and Paper-VISA datasets under zero-shot, fine-tuning, single-candidate, and multi-candidate settings. Zero-shot prompting with Qwen2-VL-72B produces moderate answer accuracy but poor bounding-box accuracy, showing limitations in visual source attribution. Fine-tuning substantially improves grounding, although performance varies with document length, content location, and modality. In the multi-candidate setting, VISA-7B can identify relevant sources and handle many “No Answer” cases, including when the oracle document is absent.

This unit analyzes VISA’s visual source attribution performance, comparing it with a modularized text-based RAG pipeline and examining out-of-domain transfer, data augmentation, and errors. The text-based pipeline uses PubLayNet bounding boxes, pytesseract OCR, and Qwen2-VL’s language model, achieving stronger zero-shot bounding box accuracy but lower answer accuracy because of OCR errors. Experiments with Wiki-VISA, Paper-VISA, and FineWeb-VISA show that cross-domain transfer is difficult, while combining training datasets improves generalization. An error analysis of 50 Wiki-VISA cases identifies wrong source attribution, position misalignment, and granularity mismatch as the main failure types.

7

The unit concludes by presenting VISA, a visual source attribution approach for retrieval-augmented generation. VISA generates answers while providing bounding boxes that locate evidence. Curated datasets demonstrate its effectiveness across diverse document types, including complex multi-page and multimodal content. Experimental results show that VISA connects information retrieval and answer generation through finer-grained, visually grounded attribution.

Limitations

This unit describes limitations of VISA for answer generation and content grounding in vision-based RAG systems. VISA focuses on short answers and localized evidence, while real-world scenarios may require detailed responses and evidence spanning multiple sections or documents. Its cross-domain performance is weaker on scanned, handwritten, and otherwise complex documents, and scaling candidate sets creates a trade-off between accuracy and efficiency. The unit also notes that user studies are needed to assess whether VISA’s visual attribution matches user expectations for granularity and practical usefulness.
