Sir James Craig in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

Sir James Craig, a distinguished soldier with service in South Africa and India, became governor of Canada in 1807 despite inexperience in civil government. Suspicious and prejudiced against French Canadians, he relied on Herman W. Ryland and John Sewell, dismissed militia officers linked to Le Canadien, opposed habeas corpus, suppressed the newspaper, and arrested its printer and proprietors. His policy failed, embittering relations between the races; he reportedly later admitted deception.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

{13} CHAPTER III 'THE REIGN OF TERROR' During the session of 1805 the Assembly was confronted with the apparently innocent problem of building prisons.

Sir James Craig became governor of Canada in 1807 and was suspicious of the loyalty of the French Canadians. He dismissed militia officers linked to Le Canadien, suppressed the newspaper in 1810, and arrested its printer and three proprietors. His policy failed to suppress opposition and embittered relations between the two races, and he later reportedly acknowledged that he had been deceived.
