Loki in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Loki is a handsome but fickle, evil-working deity of giant race, a calumniator of the gods and contriver of fraud and mischief. He repeatedly causes the gods difficulty: he advises acceptance of a giant artificer’s bargain, then prevents its completion by becoming a mare. Father of Fenris, the Midgard serpent, and Hela, he travels with Thor and Thialfi, claims skill at eating, and loses to Logi, who is Fire. Loki later discovers mistletoe can harm Baldur, directs Hodur to kill him, hides as a salmon, and is bound beneath a venomous serpent.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXXI

Loki is a handsome but fickle and evil deity of fraud and mischief who is of the giant race and repeatedly causes difficulties for the gods. He advises the gods to accept the mountain giant artificer's bargain, later prevents the work from being completed by transforming into a mare, and is the father of Fenris, the Midgard serpent, and Hela.

Chapter XXXII

Loki travels with Thor and Thialfi to the giants' country. At Utgard he competes with Logi in eating and is defeated because Logi consumes the meat, bones, and trough, while Logi is really Fire.

Chapter XXXIII

Loki discovers that mistletoe can harm Baldur, gives it to Hodur, and directs Hodur's throw. He is suspected of disguising himself as Thaukt, later changes into a salmon while hiding from the gods, and is bound with a serpent whose venom causes him torment and is said to produce earthquakes.
