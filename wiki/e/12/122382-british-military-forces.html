British military forces in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

British military forces serving in British North America were sent in autumn 1837 to arrest Patriote leaders and disperse armed gatherings at St Denis and St Charles. Colonel Charles Gore's force was defeated at St Denis and withdrew after six killed and eighteen wounded, the rebellion's only rebel victory. Lieutenant-Colonel Wetherall's force then advanced on St Charles, where British troops defeated the rebel force, losing three killed and eighteen wounded. Later, some burned much of St Denis without opposition.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

] {69} CHAPTER VIII THE DOGS OF WAR As the autumn of 1837 wore on, the situation in Lower Canada began to assume an aspect more and more threatening.

The British military forces were sent to arrest Patriote leaders and disperse armed gatherings at St Denis and St Charles. A force under Colonel Charles Gore was defeated at St Denis and withdrew after suffering six killed and eighteen wounded, while a force under Lieutenant-Colonel Wetherall was intended to advance on St Charles.

{82} CHAPTER IX _FORCE MAJEURE_ The check administered to Colonel Gore's column at St Denis, in the first engagement of the rebellion, was the only victory which fell to the rebel forces.

The British troops advancing on St Charles numbered between three and four hundred and defeated the rebel force there. They lost three killed and eighteen wounded, and later some of them burned a large part of St Denis despite the absence of opposition.
