Iona

Also called: Icolmkill, Ionia

Abstract written by a model

Iona, also called Icolmkill, is one of the smallest British Isles, at Mull’s extremity, separated from Mull by a half-mile strait and thirty-six miles from mainland Scotland. It sheltered Druids after their retreat from the mainland until St. Columba arrived. It then became Columba’s monastic seat and a center of civilization, religion, and gospel spread amid heathenism in Northern Europe. Repeated Norwegian and Danish attacks and the suppression of the Culdees brought decline. Its cathedral, nunnery chapel, tombs, and circular cairns remain travelers’ chief attractions.

Iona is in one work on this site, Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Chapter XXXIV

Iona provided shelter for the Druids after they were harassed by the Romans on the mainland. The Druids continued their rites there for a time, until the arrival of St. Columba.

IONA

Iona is a small island at the extremity of Mull, separated from it by a strait and lying thirty-six miles from mainland Scotland. It became the seat of Columba's monastery and a center for the spread of the gospel, but later declined after repeated attacks by Norwegian and Danish rovers and the suppression of the Culdees. Its remaining cathedral, nunnery chapel, tombs, and circular cairns attract travelers.
