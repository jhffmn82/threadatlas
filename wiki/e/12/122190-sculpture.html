sculpture in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Sculpture is best suited to expressing permanent character and repose; when it pursues detailed situations and intense emotions, it becomes less adequate and approaches painting.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

In Athens, after Pheidias, the greatest sculptures were those

Sculpture is described as best suited to permanent character and repose rather than temporary or accidental feeling and violent action. When artists pursued detailed situations and intense emotions, sculpture became less adequate to the conception and increasingly approached the province of painting.
