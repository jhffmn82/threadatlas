Antigone’s family curse in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912

Antigone’s family curse is a persistent affliction of the Labdacidae, centered on Oedipus. After recognizing that he may have killed Laius, Oedipus sees himself as cursed through birth, marriage, and parricide; his established guilt requires exile, while Jocasta seeks cleansing for the land.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CREON.

Oedipus recognizes that he has pronounced a dreadful curse against himself after hearing that Laius was killed at a place where three roads meet. He says that the curse would make him unclean and subject to banishment if he is Laius’s killer.

[Enter JOCASTA.]

Jocasta prays for Apollo to cleanse the land from its curse. Oedipus ultimately describes himself as cursed in birth, marriage, and parricide after learning the truth about his identity.
