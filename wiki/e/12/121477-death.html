death in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

Death is an unavoidable fate for mortals and a fatal event that overtakes warriors in battle. From Book VI through Book VIII, it strikes named and unnamed Greek and Trojan fighters amid repeated fighting, leaving blood, agony, and struggles over the fallen. As the losses mount, Nestor teaches that all mortals share death’s path and the living must endure grief.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK VI

Death strikes many warriors in the battle, including Nireus and Machaon. The fighting leaves the earth red with blood, and both sides struggle over the fallen.

By this to Podaleirius tidings came

Death takes many warriors in the fighting, including Cleitus, Lassus, Laophoon, Echemmon, and numerous unnamed men. The dead are left amid blood and agony, while Aias survives despite being knocked down.

BOOK VII

Death takes Machaon, Nireus, Peneleos, Achilles, and many other warriors in the fighting. Nestor teaches that all mortals share one path toward death and that the living must endure the grief sent by the Gods.

BOOK VIII

Death spreads throughout the battle as Greeks and Trojans kill one another. Warriors fall to spears, stones, swords, darts, and arrows, and the plain and walls become stained with blood.
