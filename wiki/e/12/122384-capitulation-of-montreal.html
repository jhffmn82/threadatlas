Capitulation of Montreal in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

The Capitulation of Montreal in 1760 set terms for defeated French inhabitants of Canada, assuring property and civil rights and guaranteeing free exercise of religion; the British did not treat them as a conquered people.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

{7} CHAPTER II THE RIGHTS OF THE DEFEATED The British did not treat the French inhabitants of Canada as a conquered people; not as other countries won by conquest have been treated by their victorious invaders.

The Capitulation of Montreal in 1760 assured the Canadians of their property and civil rights and guaranteed them the free exercise of their religion.
