Sophocles · 1912

Antigone’s burial of Polyneices is a deliberate act of piety that honors her unsepulchered brother and violates Creon’s interdict. Motivated by conscience, she gathers dry dust, pours a lustral stream three times over the body, is witnessed and arrested by the Guard, and later affirms that she performed the proper rites.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

[Enter GUARD bringing ANTIGONE]

Antigone’s burial consists of gathering dry dust and pouring a lustral stream from a brazen urn three times over the dead. The Guard witnesses the act after the body has been uncovered and arrests her.

[Enter HAEMON]

Antigone says that she buried and performed the proper rites for Polyneices after he was left unsepulchered. She maintains that she would have done the same for a dead child or husband, but that Polyneices was her only possible brother because her parents were dead.
