Quebec Act of 1774 in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

The Quebec Act of 1774 was a law passed after the conquest of Canada by British arms and before the American invasion of Canada in 1775. It granted concessions to French inhabitants of Canada, giving French civil law almost complete exclusion of English common law and legally enforcing Catholic tithes, thereby virtually establishing the Roman Catholic Church and helping produce French-Canadian loyalty to Great Britain.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

{1} CHAPTER I CANADIANS, OLD AND NEW The conquest of Canada by British arms in the Seven Years' War gave rise to a situation in the colony which was fraught with tragic possibilities.

The Quebec Act, passed in the year before the American invasion of Canada in 1775, granted concessions that helped produce French-Canadian loyalty to Great Britain.

{7} CHAPTER II THE RIGHTS OF THE DEFEATED The British did not treat the French inhabitants of Canada as a conquered people; not as other countries won by conquest have been treated by their victorious invaders.

The Quebec Act of 1774 granted the French civil law almost complete exclusion of the English common law. It also virtually established the Church of the vanquished through legal enforcement of Catholics' obligation to pay tithes.
