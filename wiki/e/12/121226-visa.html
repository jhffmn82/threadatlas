Also called: Our VISA attribution method, Retrieval Augmented Generation with Visual Source Attribution (VISA), Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Visual Source Attribution, VISA attribution method, VISA method, Visual Source Attribution (VISA), multi-document VISA, visual source attribution approach
  • In VISA: Retrieval Augmented Generation with Visual Source Attribution: VISA is a novel visual source attribution approach for vision-based retrieval-augmented generation. It uses a large vision-language model to process retrieved document screenshots, generate a short answer, identify the most relevant candidate document, and return bounding-box coordinates for the passages, tables, or images that support that answer. Rather than providing lists of textual citations, it makes attribution visually grounded by drawing boxes around potentially supportive content, bridging retrieval and answer generation with finer-grained evidence locations. The method operates end to end and generally does not rely on the retriever type. In its multi-document setting, it receives a query, multiple retrieval candidates, and, during training, a ground-truth document, short answer, and evidence box; some cases have no ground-truth document. VISA is trained with synthetic queries and answers from Paper-VISA and FineWeb-VISA. Models are first trained with a single oracle document image and then with multiple document candidates. Because zero-shot performance remains difficult, supervised fine-tuning is required. Evaluation on Wiki-VISA and Paper-VISA measures answer accuracy and bounding-box precision. Fine-tuning and diverse training data improve both results: VISA-7B reaches 32.3% bounding-box accuracy and 41.8% answer accuracy with three candidates, while the full Wiki+Paper+FineWeb training combination reaches 58.1% bounding-box accuracy on Wiki-VISA and 67.6% on Paper-VISA. VISA is presented as a step toward more verifiable and user-friendly RAG systems, but it is limited by short answers and single localized evidence regions. Challenges include evidence across sections or documents, implied answers, scanned or handwritten documents and complex layouts, lower precision for long documents and zero-shot generalization, and higher training cost or out-of-memory issues as candidate sets grow.

VISA in VISA: Retrieval Augmented Generation with Visual Source Attribution

Xueguang Ma, Shengyao Zhuang, Bevan Koopman, Guido Zuccon, Wenhu Chen, Jimmy Lin · 2025

VISA is a novel visual source attribution approach for vision-based retrieval-augmented generation. It uses a large vision-language model to process retrieved document screenshots, generate a short answer, identify the most relevant candidate document, and return bounding-box coordinates for the passages, tables, or images that support that answer. Rather than providing lists of textual citations, it makes attribution visually grounded by drawing boxes around potentially supportive content, bridging retrieval and answer generation with finer-grained evidence locations. The method operates end to end and generally does not rely on the retriever type. In its multi-document setting, it receives a query, multiple retrieval candidates, and, during training, a ground-truth document, short answer, and evidence box; some cases have no ground-truth document. VISA is trained with synthetic queries and answers from Paper-VISA and FineWeb-VISA. Models are first trained with a single oracle document image and then with multiple document candidates. Because zero-shot performance remains difficult, supervised fine-tuning is required. Evaluation on Wiki-VISA and Paper-VISA measures answer accuracy and bounding-box precision. Fine-tuning and diverse training data improve both results: VISA-7B reaches 32.3% bounding-box accuracy and 41.8% answer accuracy with three candidates, while the full Wiki+Paper+FineWeb training combination reaches 58.1% bounding-box accuracy on Wiki-VISA and 67.6% on Paper-VISA. VISA is presented as a step toward more verifiable and user-friendly RAG systems, but it is limited by short answers and single localized evidence regions. Challenges include evidence across sections or documents, implied answers, scanned or handwritten documents and complex layouts, lower precision for long documents and zero-shot generalization, and higher training cost or out-of-memory issues as candidate sets grow.

Narrative, section by section

Abstract

Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Visual Source Attribution (VISA) combines answer generation with visual source attribution. It uses large vision-language models to identify supporting evidence and mark the relevant regions in retrieved document screenshots with bounding boxes.

opening

Retrieval Augmented Generation with Visual Source Attribution (VISA) is proposed as a fully visual, end-to-end verifiable RAG pipeline. It uses a large vision-language model to process retrieved document images, generate an answer, and return bounding boxes for relevant evidence regions.

2 / 2.1 .. 2.2

It is proposed as a method for vision-based RAG that directly draws bounding boxes around potentially answer-supporting content in retrieved document screenshots. It attributes answers to specific passages, tables, or images and uses visual bounding boxes instead of lists of textual citations.

3 / 3.1 .. 3.3

VISA is a source attribution method designed primarily for vision-based RAG systems. It generates an answer, the identifier of the most relevant candidate document, and bounding-box coordinates for the content supporting the answer.

opening .. 3.4

VISA is trained using synthetic queries and answers from Paper-VISA and FineWeb-VISA. Its multi-document setting provides a query, a ground-truth document, a short answer, an evidence bounding box, and additional candidate documents, including cases where no ground-truth document is present.

4 / 4.1 .. 4.2

VISA is evaluated on generated answer accuracy and bounding box precision across Wiki-VISA and Paper-VISA. Its models are trained first with a single oracle document image and then with multiple document candidates.

5

VISA is evaluated for visual source attribution on Wiki-VISA and Paper-VISA. Fine-tuned VISA models substantially improve bounding-box and answer accuracy, while VISA-7B achieves 32.3% bounding-box accuracy and 41.8% answer accuracy with three candidate documents.

6 / 6.1 .. 6.4

VISA performs retrieval-augmented generation and visual source attribution end to end. Its performance improves with fine-tuning and diverse training data, with the full Wiki+Paper+FineWeb combination achieving 58.1% bounding box accuracy on Wiki-VISA and 67.6% on Paper-VISA.

7

VISA is introduced as a visual source attribution approach for retrieval-augmented generation. It generates answers while providing bounding boxes to locate evidence, and its effectiveness is demonstrated across diverse document types.

Limitations

VISA demonstrates promising results for answer generation and content grounding in vision-based RAG systems, but it focuses on short answers and evidence from a single localized region. Its effectiveness is limited when evidence spans multiple sections or documents, when answers are implied rather than exact substrings, or when documents contain scanned, handwritten, or complex layouts. Increasing candidate-set size also raises VISA’s training costs and can cause out-of-memory issues.
