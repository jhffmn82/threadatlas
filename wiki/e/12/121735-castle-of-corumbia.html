Castle of Corumbia in The Yellow Knight of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1930

The Castle of Corumbia is a centuries-old golden castle, once inhabited by its former monarch. It stands abandoned, ruined, and nearly buried beneath vines, with broken windows, grass between golden bricks, statue page boys on its tower, and surviving solid-gold furniture amid decayed fabrics. When the restored Corumbians return, the King casts a magic date seed into a dining-hall fire, restoring its walls, furnishings, food, and decorations. It becomes active again, filled with gardeners, pages, footmen, and courtiers; the Yellow Knight is restored there before departing.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER TEN

The golden castle is tall, strange, and nearly buried beneath vines, with broken windows and grass growing between its golden bricks. Three golden statue page boys blow trumpets from its tallest tower, but no living inhabitants are found inside. Its solid-gold furniture has survived, while its curtains and tapestries have crumbled away.

CHAPTER SEVENTEEN

The Golden Castle is found ruined and decayed when the restored Corumbians return. Its restoration begins when the King casts a magic date seed into a fire in the dining hall, causing the walls, furnishings, food, and decorations to return to their former condition.

CHAPTER EIGHTEEN

The Castle of Corumbia has been restored to bustling activity, with gardeners, pages, footmen, and courtiers filling it as they did before the Sultan's transformations. The Yellow Knight is restored there before riding away.
