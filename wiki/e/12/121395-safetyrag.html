SafetyRAG

framework · 1 work · 38 quoted facts · 11 connections
Also called: SafetyRAG framework, SafetyRAG layer, SafetyRAG method, SafetyRAG solution
  • In SafetyRAG: Towards Safe Large Language Model-Based Application through Retrieval-Augmented Generation: SafetyRAG is a straightforward framework, method, and approach for improving the safety of LLM-based applications, particularly against unsafe prompts and prompt injection attacks. It is based on Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and is intended to provide explicit safety information and safety facts about harmful or unsafe behavior to an LLM during response generation, without further training. Its central mechanism comprises an input encoder module, a retrieval module, and an augmented-generation module. Using a knowledge database, it retrieves relevant safety-facts documents for a user prompt and supplies those facts as context to the language model. This is intended to increase the model’s awareness of unsafe situations, encourage refusal of harmful requests, incorporate appropriate safety facts into responses, and produce safer outputs. SafetyRAG was evaluated with a combination of jailbreak prompts. Across all tested models, it improved interception or blocking of direct unsafe prompts and significantly improved the ability to decline unsafe requests. It also substantially increased robustness to indirect unsafe prompts and prompt injection compared with an application without the SafetyRAG layer. Its overall efficacy, however, depends on the underlying alignment of the base LLM, with weaker internal alignment limiting effectiveness. The framework is presented as cost-effective, incurring no additional costs compared with existing alternatives, and as easy to implement across various industry applications. It can enhance safety in applications such as a medical assistant by retrieving safety facts from its knowledge base and improving awareness of unsafe behaviors.

SafetyRAG in SafetyRAG: Towards Safe Large Language Model-Based Application through Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Sihem Omri, Manel Abdelkader, and Mohamed Hamdi · 2025-02-17

SafetyRAG is a straightforward framework, method, and approach for improving the safety of LLM-based applications, particularly against unsafe prompts and prompt injection attacks. It is based on Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and is intended to provide explicit safety information and safety facts about harmful or unsafe behavior to an LLM during response generation, without further training. Its central mechanism comprises an input encoder module, a retrieval module, and an augmented-generation module. Using a knowledge database, it retrieves relevant safety-facts documents for a user prompt and supplies those facts as context to the language model. This is intended to increase the model’s awareness of unsafe situations, encourage refusal of harmful requests, incorporate appropriate safety facts into responses, and produce safer outputs. SafetyRAG was evaluated with a combination of jailbreak prompts. Across all tested models, it improved interception or blocking of direct unsafe prompts and significantly improved the ability to decline unsafe requests. It also substantially increased robustness to indirect unsafe prompts and prompt injection compared with an application without the SafetyRAG layer. Its overall efficacy, however, depends on the underlying alignment of the base LLM, with weaker internal alignment limiting effectiveness. The framework is presented as cost-effective, incurring no additional costs compared with existing alternatives, and as easy to implement across various industry applications. It can enhance safety in applications such as a medical assistant by retrieving safety facts from its knowledge base and improving awareness of unsafe behaviors.

Its evaluation showed significant improvements in the ability of all tested models to decline unsafe requests, including requests with prompt injections.

I. INTRODUCTION

SafetyRAG is introduced as an approach that uses the RAG architecture to reduce the risk of prompt injection attacks in LLM applications. It provides the LLM with explicit safety information about harmful behavior, and experiments indicate that applications using it are more robust than applications without it.

SafetyRAG is proposed as a straightforward framework for unsafe prompt risk mitigation. It was tested using a combination of jailbreaking prompts and uses explicit information about harmful prompts to encourage LLMs not to comply with unsafe requests.

III. MATERIALS AND METHODS / A. Overall Framework .. 2) Baseline LLM models

SafetyRAG is based on the RAG architecture and comprises an input encoder module, a retrieval module, and an augmented-generation module. It retrieves relevant safety facts for user prompts to increase awareness of unsafe situations and encourage the LLM to refuse unsafe requests.

IV. RESULT AND DISCUSSION .. C. Discussion

SafetyRAG retrieves relevant safety facts and supplies them to language models to improve their interception of unsafe prompts. It raised direct-prompt blocking performance across all tested models and substantially improved resistance to prompt injection, although its effectiveness was limited when a model had weaker internal alignment.

V. CONCLUSION

SafetyRAG is presented as a framework that improves the safety of LLM-based applications through a Retrieval-Augmented Generation architecture without further training. It improves interception of unsafe prompts, especially prompt injection, helps LLMs incorporate appropriate safety facts, produces safer outputs, and is described as cost-effective and easy to implement across industry applications.
