Ninety-Two Resolutions

Abstract written by a model

The Ninety-Two Resolutions were Assembly of Lower Canada resolutions introduced in 1834 by Elzear Bedard, reportedly drafted by A. N. Morin under Papineau's inspiration. Passing fifty-six to twenty-four, they listed genuine abuses, criticized the Legislative Council as subservient to the Executive Council, and made an elective Legislative Council their chief constitutional demand. They also attacked the colonial constitution, invoked the American Revolution, and sought Lord Aylmer's removal and impeachment, without clearly proposing responsible government. Their passage divided the Patriote party; once forwarded to Westminster through the Assembly's London agent and Lord Aylmer, they prompted a British House of Commons special committee on the grievances.

{33} CHAPTER V THE NINETY-TWO RESOLUTIONS After 1830 signs began to multiply that the racial feud in Lower Canada was growing in intensity.

The Ninety-Two Resolutions were introduced in 1834 by Elzear Bedard and were reputedly drafted by A. N. Morin under Papineau's inspiration. They identified numerous genuine abuses but also attacked the colonial constitution, invoked the American Revolution, demanded Lord Aylmer's impeachment, and failed to propose a clear system of responsible government. They passed by fifty-six votes to twenty-four and caused significant divisions within the Patriote party.

{44} CHAPTER VI THE ROYAL COMMISSION A general election followed soon after the passing of the Ninety-Two Resolutions and revealed the strength of Papineau's position in the country.

The Ninety-Two Resolutions were forwarded to Westminster through the Assembly's agent in London and through Lord Aylmer. They led the British House of Commons to appoint a special committee to investigate the grievances they described.
