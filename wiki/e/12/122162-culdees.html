Culdees in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The Culdees were religious persons united for preaching, teaching youth, and worship. Bound by obedience but not poverty or celibacy, many married and labored for life’s comforts. As Romish Church power advanced, their communities were suppressed in the thirteenth century; individuals continued to labor and resist papal usurpation.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

IONA

The Culdees were religious persons associated for preaching the gospel, teaching youth, and maintaining devotion through united worship. They were bound by obedience but not by poverty or celibacy, and most appear to have married, with their wives living in an adjacent locality. Their communities were suppressed in the thirteenth century as the power of the Romish Church advanced, though individuals continued to labor and resist papal usurpation.
