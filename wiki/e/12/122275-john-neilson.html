John Neilson

person · 1 work · 4 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Neilson

Abstract written by a model

John Neilson was the Scottish editor of the Quebec Gazette and a Lower Canada political figure. In 1822 he accompanied Louis Joseph Papineau to London as an agent opposing the Union Bill, helping secure its defeat; in 1827 he joined Augustin Cuvillier and Denis B. Viger in seeking redress of grievances and Lord Dalhousie’s removal. Initially a strong Papineau supporter, he later became alienated under Lord Aylmer, opposing Mondelet’s expulsion and an elective or abolished Legislative Council, rebuking Papineau on judicial independence, and voting against the Ninety-Two Resolutions.

{21} CHAPTER IV THE RISE OF PAPINEAU In the year 1812 a young man took his seat in the House of Assembly for Lower Canada who was destined to play a conspicuous part in the history of the province during the next quarter of a century.

John Neilson, the Scottish editor of the Quebec Gazette, accompanied Louis Joseph Papineau to London in 1822 to oppose the Union Bill. He helped secure its defeat and later joined Augustin Cuvillier and Denis B. Viger in a 1827 deputation that sought redress of grievances and Lord Dalhousie's removal.

{33} CHAPTER V THE NINETY-TWO RESOLUTIONS After 1830 signs began to multiply that the racial feud in Lower Canada was growing in intensity.

John Neilson, once a strong supporter of Papineau, became alienated from him under Lord Aylmer. He opposed the expulsion of Mondelet, resisted the abolition of the Legislative Council, rebuked Papineau during the debate on judicial independence, and voted against the Ninety-Two Resolutions.
