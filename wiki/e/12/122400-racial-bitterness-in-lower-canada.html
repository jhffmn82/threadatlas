racial bitterness in Lower Canada in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

Racial bitterness in Lower Canada was a serious political and ethnic conflict between French Canadians and English Canadians. It was absent from the earliest Assembly debates, which retained decorum and social intercourse, but began during Sir James Craig’s regime. It intensified in 1805 when debate over funding prisons by land tax or customs duties led English Canadians to oppose French-Canadian domination. After 1830 the feud grew stronger, contributed to the Montreal riot of 1832, and shaped the largely racial political struggle before 1837.

Racial conflict intensified during the 1805 debate over whether to fund prisons through a land tax or customs duties. The dispute led English Canadians to oppose French-Canadian domination and contributed to worsening relations between the two groups under Sir James Craig.

The racial feud in Lower Canada grew more intense after 1830. It contributed to the Montreal riot of 1832 and shaped the largely racial political struggle before 1837.
