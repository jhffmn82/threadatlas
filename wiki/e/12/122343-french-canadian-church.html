French-Canadian Church in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

The French-Canadian Church was a Roman Catholic body in Canada whose position was strengthened through the Quebec Act of 1774, which virtually established it and legally enforced Catholic tithes. Although Roman Catholics were barred from sitting in the House of Commons at Westminster, Church leaders believed Catholics enjoyed considerable liberty under British rule. When revolutionary agitation grew in 1837, the Church openly and officially opposed the rebellion in Lower Canada, warning people that rebellion was sinful. Only the cures of St Charles and St Benoit gave the movement encouragement.
