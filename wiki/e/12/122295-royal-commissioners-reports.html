royal commissioners' reports in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

The royal commissioners' reports were six reports completed in autumn 1836 after an election showed Papineau's strength. They had little direct influence on events in Canada. The final report rejected responsible government and an elective Legislative Council, and considered whether a crisis might warrant total suspension of the colonies' constitution rather than partial interference.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

{44} CHAPTER VI THE ROYAL COMMISSION A general election followed soon after the passing of the Ninety-Two Resolutions and revealed the strength of Papineau's position in the country.

The royal commissioners' reports, six in number, were completed in the autumn of 1836 and had little direct influence on events in Canada. They rejected responsible government and an elective Legislative Council and considered whether suspending the constitution might be preferable to partial interference with it.
