women’s medical service in wartime in Dr. Elsie Inglis

Lady Frances Balfour · 1918

Women’s medical service in wartime developed despite official assumptions that women could not fight and were therefore not useful. Women doctors, nurses, ambulance workers, and orderlies organised professional service through the Scottish Women’s Hospitals. Dr. Elsie Inglis brought its units and equipment into existence, supported by Scottish suffrage societies. The service earned praise from allied nations.

Women’s medical service in wartime is represented through the units and equipment that Dr. Elsie Inglis brought into existence for the Scottish Women’s Hospitals. These services earned praise from allied nations and grew out of the organisation and support of Scottish suffrage societies.

Women’s military medical service was initially restricted by official assumptions that women could not fight and were therefore not useful in wartime. Women doctors, nurses, ambulance workers, and orderlies nevertheless organised professional medical service through the Scottish Women’s Hospitals.
