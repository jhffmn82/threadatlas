Legislative Council

group · 1 work · 4 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Legislative Council of Lower Canada, an elective Legislative Council, legislative councils, the Legislative Council, the existing nominated and reactionary Legislative Council, upper chamber

Abstract written by a model

The Legislative Council of Lower Canada was a nominated, reactionary legislative body that served as a buffer between the governor’s Executive Council and the House of Assembly. Composed mainly of people of English birth and including many office-holders, it remained under executive influence. It repeatedly rejected Assembly measures and blocked budgets during the civil-list dispute. After 1830, Papineau and his allies made its conversion to an elective body their principal constitutional reform in the Ninety-Two Resolutions. In 1836 it rejected a supply bill after the Assembly offered only six months’ supply, contributing to distress among public officials and their families.

{21} CHAPTER IV THE RISE OF PAPINEAU In the year 1812 a young man took his seat in the House of Assembly for Lower Canada who was destined to play a conspicuous part in the history of the province during the next quarter of a century.

The Legislative Council repeatedly rejected measures passed by the House of Assembly and blocked budgets during the dispute over the civil list. Its members were mainly of English birth, and many office-holders sat in it, leaving it under the influence of the executive government.

{33} CHAPTER V THE NINETY-TWO RESOLUTIONS After 1830 signs began to multiply that the racial feud in Lower Canada was growing in intensity.

The Legislative Council of Lower Canada was described as nominated, reactionary, and subservient to the Executive Council. Papineau and his allies sought to make it elective, and the Ninety-Two Resolutions treated this as their principal constitutional reform.

{44} CHAPTER VI THE ROYAL COMMISSION A general election followed soon after the passing of the Ninety-Two Resolutions and revealed the strength of Papineau's position in the country.

The Legislative Council rejected the supply bill in 1836 after the Assembly refused to grant more than six months' supply. Its opposition contributed to the distress of public officials and their families.
