Lord Gosford in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

Lord Gosford was an Irish peer with no previous public-life or civil-government experience who became governor of Canada and chief royal commissioner. Arriving at the end of summer 1835, he pursued conciliation toward the Canadian Patriotes and French Canadians: he promised redress of grievances, supported French in official affairs, offered a civil list for legislative control of Crown revenues, and disbanded volunteer rifle corps because he judged the constitution safe. His mild policy in Lower Canada was weakened by publication of Sir Francis Bond Head's instructions. In autumn 1836 he pressed the legislature over estimates and unpaid arrears. By September 2, 1837, he judged conciliation impossible, sought resignation, reported that Papineau sought separation and republican government, and suggested suspension of the constitution might be necessary.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

{44} CHAPTER VI THE ROYAL COMMISSION A general election followed soon after the passing of the Ninety-Two Resolutions and revealed the strength of Papineau's position in the country.

Lord Gosford became governor of Canada and chief royal commissioner despite having no previous experience in public life or civil government. He pursued conciliation through social and official gestures, promised redress of grievances, and proposed financial and administrative concessions, but his efforts were undermined by the publication of Sir Francis Bond Head's instructions.

{57} CHAPTER VII THE RUSSELL RESOLUTIONS When the legislature of Lower Canada met in the autumn of 1836, Lord Gosford earnestly called its attention to the estimates of the current year and the accounts showing the arrears unpaid.

Lord Gosford urged the legislature to address current estimates and unpaid arrears, but later acknowledged that conciliation had failed. He asked to resign, warned that Papineau sought separation from England and republican government, and suggested that suspending the constitution might become necessary.
