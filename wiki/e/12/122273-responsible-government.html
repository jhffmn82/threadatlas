responsible government

topic · 1 work · 2 quoted facts · 5 connections

Abstract written by a model

Responsible government is a provincial cabinet system responsible to the legislature, with the governor bound to accept its advice on provincial affairs. It was initially absent because the governor and Executive Council answered to the Colonial Office rather than the Assembly. Sherbrooke advanced it through constitutional recommendations, including having the speaker sit on the Executive Council; Mondelet’s appointment was a further step. The Ninety-Two Resolutions did not clearly support it, and royal commissioners rejected it as incompatible with colonial status. After the rebellions, Durham recommended it for matters outside strictly imperial interests, a proposal later introduced in substance by Lord Elgin. The LaFontaine-Baldwin administration ultimately ushered in full responsible government, a new order with which Papineau was out of harmony.

{21} CHAPTER IV THE RISE OF PAPINEAU In the year 1812 a young man took his seat in the House of Assembly for Lower Canada who was destined to play a conspicuous part in the history of the province during the next quarter of a century.

Responsible government was not yet established because the governor and Executive Council were responsible to the Colonial Office rather than to the Assembly. Sherbrooke's recommendation that the speaker sit on the Executive Council, and Mondelet's appointment to it, were described as steps toward responsible government.

{33} CHAPTER V THE NINETY-TWO RESOLUTIONS After 1830 signs began to multiply that the racial feud in Lower Canada was growing in intensity.

The Ninety-Two Resolutions did not clearly advocate responsible government. Papineau and his allies did not appear to understand the eventual solution of a provincial cabinet drawn from and responsible to the legislature, although Papineau unwittingly helped hasten its arrival.

{44} CHAPTER VI THE ROYAL COMMISSION A general election followed soon after the passing of the Ninety-Two Resolutions and revealed the strength of Papineau's position in the country.

The royal commissioners advised against responsible government in the modern sense, considering it incompatible with the status of a colony.

{104} CHAPTER X THE LORD HIGH COMMISSIONER The rebellions in Upper and Lower Canada profoundly affected public opinion in the mother country.

Durham recommended responsible government in matters not involving strictly imperial interests. The text describes this proposal as the path leading out of pre-rebellion politics, and states that Lord Elgin later introduced its substance.

{128} CHAPTER XII A POSTSCRIPT The rebellion of 1837 now belongs to the dead past.

The LaFontaine-Baldwin administration ushered in full responsible government. Papineau was described as being out of harmony with the new order of things under responsible government.
