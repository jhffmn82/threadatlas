Canadians

group · 1 work · 4 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Canadian, The people of Canada, the Canadians of 1760

Abstract written by a model

Canadians are the English and French people of Canada. After the conquest of Canada by British arms created tragic possibilities, their two communities experienced about a hundred and fifty years of general peace and amity, aside from one limited, brief civil-war-like outbreak. The rebellion of 1837 is now dead past: Canadians are urged to acknowledge good and bad on both sides and to unite loyalty with liberty.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

{1} CHAPTER I CANADIANS, OLD AND NEW The conquest of Canada by British arms in the Seven Years' War gave rise to a situation in the colony which was fraught with tragic possibilities.

Canadians, whether English or French, have reason to view the record of relations between the two communities with satisfaction. The text describes a hundred and fifty years of general peace and amity, interrupted by only one limited and brief outbreak resembling civil war.

{128} CHAPTER XII A POSTSCRIPT The rebellion of 1837 now belongs to the dead past.

Canadians are urged to recognize the good and bad on both sides of the rebellion of 1837. The present generation is said to be able to combine loyalty and liberty in a way the men of that period could not.
