Abstract written by a model

The Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh is a hospital and infirmary in Edinburgh, described as the second-largest hospital mentioned. It refused women admission to medical-profession clinical teaching in its wards unless they raised seven hundred pounds to furnish two wards. Women were then mainly confined to those wards, with limited access to other subjects, prompting one speaker to hope for entry or otherwise build a hospital.

The Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh refused to admit women to clinical teaching in its wards until they raised seven hundred pounds to furnish two wards for their use. Women remained confined mainly to those two wards, with only limited access to other subjects.
