battle of St Charles in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

The battle of St Charles was a brief engagement in the rebellion, lasting less than an hour. British troops defeated and dispersed fewer than two hundred inadequately armed, poorly led rebels, taking thirty prisoners; local tradition reported forty-two rebel deaths and many wounded. The defeat influenced events at St Eustache and the decision to suppress the northern rebellion.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

{82} CHAPTER IX _FORCE MAJEURE_ The check administered to Colonel Gore's column at St Denis, in the first engagement of the rebellion, was the only victory which fell to the rebel forces.

The battle of St Charles lasted less than an hour and ended in a British victory. The rebels were fewer than two hundred, inadequately armed, and poorly led; thirty were taken prisoner, while the local tradition reported forty-two rebel deaths and many more wounded.

{89} But the end of rebellion was not yet in sight.

The battle of St Charles ended in the defeat and dispersion of the insurgents. Its outcome influenced events at St Eustache and contributed to the decision to concentrate on suppressing the northern rebellion.
