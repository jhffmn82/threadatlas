civil list in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

The civil list was proposed as permanent funding for government salaries and civil expenses, potentially for the sovereign’s lifetime. In Lower Canada, the Assembly insisted that it be voted annually. Lord Goderich offered provincial-revenue control for a £19,000 civil list lasting the king’s lifetime. Later, Lord Gosford likewise sought a permanent civil list in exchange for Crown revenues, but Papineau and his supporters refused after Sir Francis Bond Head’s instructions were published.

{21} CHAPTER IV THE RISE OF PAPINEAU In the year 1812 a young man took his seat in the House of Assembly for Lower Canada who was destined to play a conspicuous part in the history of the province during the next quarter of a century.

The civil list was the permanent funding proposed for the salaries and civil expenses of the government. The Assembly insisted on voting it annually rather than for the sovereign's lifetime, while Lord Goderich later offered control of provincial revenue in exchange for a civil list of L19,000 voted for the lifetime of the king.

{44} CHAPTER VI THE ROYAL COMMISSION A general election followed soon after the passing of the Ninety-Two Resolutions and revealed the strength of Papineau's position in the country.

Lord Gosford sought a permanent civil list in exchange for transferring the casual and territorial revenues of the Crown to the legislature's control. Papineau and his supporters refused to grant a permanent civil list after the publication of Sir Francis Bond Head's instructions.
