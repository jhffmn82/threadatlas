SKETCH OF THE HISTORY OF GREEK SCULPTURE

Pheidias was an Athenian sculptor trained first by Hegias and then by Ageladas. Summoned by Pericles, he directed the monumental artistic works of Athens and was credited with the highest Greek conceptions of divinity in the Athene of the Parthenon and the Zeus of Olympus.

The fame of Pheidias himself, however, rested very largely on

Pheidias’s fame rested largely on three great works: the Athene Promachos, the Athene Parthenos, and the Olympian Zeus. His methods were later continued by sculptors who had worked under his influence, though their works increasingly fell short of his perfection.

Pheidias represents the greatest Greek art and an ideal of strict reserve, earnest simplicity, heroic and divine character, repose, and general beauty. His works have not come down to us, though Agoracritos produced works at Athens that were scarcely distinguishable from them.
