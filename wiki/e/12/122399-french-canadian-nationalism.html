French-Canadian nationalism

topic · 1 work · 1 quoted fact · 3 connections
Also called: French national identity in Canada, French-Canadian nationality, the position of the French race in Canada, their national identity

Abstract written by a model

French-Canadian nationalism concerns the French inhabitants of Canada’s attachment to national identity and ambitions for wider liberties. British concessions after conquest strengthened their position and sharpened this identity, contributing to further demands. After the rebellions, Durham proposed union partly to weaken nationalist ambitions, while Lord Elgin opposed such a campaign and argued that respecting French religion, habits, and traditions would strengthen attachment to the British flag.

{7} CHAPTER II THE RIGHTS OF THE DEFEATED The British did not treat the French inhabitants of Canada as a conquered people; not as other countries won by conquest have been treated by their victorious invaders.

The concessions granted by the British strengthened the position of the French race in Canada and made Canadians more jealous of their national identity. This contributed to demands for still wider liberties.

{104} CHAPTER X THE LORD HIGH COMMISSIONER The rebellions in Upper and Lower Canada profoundly affected public opinion in the mother country.

Durham was unfavorable toward French-Canadian nationalist ambitions and proposed union partly to weaken them. Lord Elgin opposed a campaign against French-Canadian nationalism, maintaining that respect for French religion, habits, and traditions could strengthen attachment to the British flag.
