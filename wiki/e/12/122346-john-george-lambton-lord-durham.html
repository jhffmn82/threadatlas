John George Lambton, Lord Durham in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

Lord Durham, a Radical-appointed Lord High Commissioner for Canada, was sent to investigate grievances after rebellions in Upper and Lower Canada. He arrived in Quebec with a regal retinue, reorganized administration, granted amnesty excepting twenty-four rebels, and ordered eight political prisoners sent to Bermudas. After his ordinance was disallowed, he resigned. His Report recommended responsible government and union of Upper and Lower Canada to absorb the French population.

The Earl of Durham was appointed Lord High Commissioner with extensive powers to investigate Canadian grievances and recommend remedies. He reorganized the administration, granted a general amnesty with twenty-four exceptions, resigned after the disallowance of his ordinance, and produced a Report recommending responsible government and legislative union.

John George Lambton in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

John George Lambton, the first Earl of Durham, was a wealthy, powerful Whig nobleman with Liberal or Radical views. He helped frame the Reform Bill of 1832, was sent to St Petersburg by the Melbourne government, and later sent to Canada after rebellions in Upper and Lower Canada affected public opinion.
