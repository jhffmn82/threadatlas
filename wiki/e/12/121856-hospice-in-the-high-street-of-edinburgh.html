Abstract written by a model

The Hospice in the High Street of Edinburgh opened in 1904 as an eight-bed maternity home with an operating theatre in a poorer district. It grew into a centre for maternity, infant-welfare, nursing, and medical education for women, training nurses for the Central Midwives Board examination and offering opportunities unavailable elsewhere. Its later facilities included a baby clinic, milk depot, health centres, and a malnutrition ward. Through its patients, it is associated with Elsie Inglis’s living memorials.
