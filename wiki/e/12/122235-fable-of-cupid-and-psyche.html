fable of Cupid and Psyche in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The fable of Cupid and Psyche is an allegory, first appearing in Apuleius's works, that represents the human soul and illustrates its purification through suffering and misfortune.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

The fable of Cupid and Psyche is usually considered allegorical.

The fable is usually considered allegorical and is understood as illustrating the purification of the soul through suffering and misfortune. It first appears in the works of Apuleius, a writer of the second century of our era.
