Venus of Melos

Abstract written by a model

Venus of Melos is a broken statue found on Melos in 1820 and sold to the French consul. Missing several pieces, especially its arms, it has an uncertain title, date, and identity: often called Venus, it may instead represent Nike or Victory, since its drapery resembles the Roman type of Victory. Despite this debate, it is valued for expressing purity, sweetness, maidenly dignity, and conquering fairness.

Chapter XXVIII

The Venus of Melos was found on the island of Melos in 1820 and sold to the French consul. It was broken and missing several pieces, especially its arms, and debate continues over whether it represents Venus or another goddess, with some identifying it as Nike or Victory.

The proper title and date of the Venus of Milo are uncertain because its arms are broken and its drapery resembles that of the Roman type of Victory. It represents an ideal of purity, sweetness, maidenly dignity, and conquering fairness, and is described as a worthy companion to the Hermes of Praxiteles.
