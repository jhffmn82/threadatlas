Serbia in Dr. Elsie Inglis

Lady Frances Balfour · 1918

Serbia is a country and a major field of service for the Scottish Women’s Hospitals during the First World War. It has the Serbian army as its national army and is described as a lovely country with a beautiful landscape, but its central record is of devastation: Austrian invasion, a severe typhus epidemic, famine, and the 1915 German, Bulgarian, Austrian, and Austro-German invasion. Epidemics and disease followed the Austrians’ invasion, while enemy occupation brought hunger, cold, displacement, losses, and the imprisonment of soldiers. Outnumbered by Austrians, Germans, Magyars, and Bulgarians, Serbia’s army and people endured the Serbian retreat of 1915. Amid this suffering, Serbia received medical and nursing aid from the Scottish Women’s Hospitals for Foreign Service and assistance from Foreign Missions. The Scottish Women’s Hospitals treated Serbian people, sought to work with the Serbian army, and made Serbia one of their principal countries of service. Dr. Elsie Inglis became a committed supporter of the Serbian cause, worked there despite difficult travel and broken transport, returned determined to aid its welfare, and sought to maintain Serbian hospitals and transport. Serbian medical workers and civilians supported the hospitals, while Serbian women maintained agriculture during wartime; plans were made for a Serbian women’s medal to honor them after the war.
