Wiki-VISA

dataset · 1 work · 26 quoted facts · 7 connections
Also called: Wiki

Abstract written by a model

Wiki-VISA is a dataset derived from Natural Questions for evaluating visual source attribution on reconstructed Wikipedia webpages. It is based on crawled webpage screenshots rendered from original URLs with history version stamps. Short answers are answer targets, while bounding boxes around HTML elements corresponding to long answers are source-attribution targets. The dataset supports multi-document, multi-page, and multi-modal evaluation, including passages on the first page, passages beyond it, and non-passage material such as tables and figures; overall accuracy is a macro average across these categories. It has 87k training examples and 3,000 test examples. Its long, multi-page documents make evidence grounding and bounding-box prediction difficult, with first-page passages performing better than later-page passages. In-domain training yields 54.2% average bounding-box accuracy, rising to 58.2% when Wiki is combined with FineWeb. Wiki-VISA transfers better to Paper-VISA than the reverse, and VISA-7B correctly marks “No Answer” in 82.2% of relevant multi-candidate cases on average.

Wiki-VISA is in one work on this site, VISA: Retrieval Augmented Generation with Visual Source Attribution. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Abstract

Wiki-VISA is based on crawled Wikipedia webpage screenshots and is used to evaluate visual source attribution.

opening

Wiki-VISA is derived from the Natural Questions dataset and reconstructs original Wikipedia webpages. It uses short answers as generation targets and the corresponding long-answer HTML bounding boxes as source-attribution targets, supporting evaluation across multi-document, multi-page, and multi-modal content.

3 / 3.1 .. 3.3

Wiki-VISA is derived from the Natural Questions dataset and uses short answers as answer targets. Its webpage screenshots are rendered from original URLs with history version stamps, while long answers are used to identify HTML elements around which bounding boxes are drawn.

4 / 4.1 .. 4.2

Wiki-VISA is evaluated across first-page passages, passages beyond the first page, and non-passage content such as tables and figures. Its overall accuracy is computed as a macro average across these categories.

5

Wiki-VISA shows difficulty for models grounding evidence in long, multi-page documents. Bounding-box accuracy is higher for passages on the first page than for passages beyond the first page, and VISA-7B correctly indicates “No Answer” in 82.2% of relevant multi-candidate cases on average.

6 / 6.1 .. 6.4

Wiki-VISA is a multi-page dataset used to study in-domain performance, transfer, augmentation, and errors. Training on Wiki-VISA gives 54.2% average bounding box accuracy in-domain, and combining Wiki with FineWeb raises this to 58.2%; Wiki-VISA also transfers better to Paper-VISA than the reverse.
