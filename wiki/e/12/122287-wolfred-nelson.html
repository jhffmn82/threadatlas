Wolfred Nelson in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

Wolfred Nelson was a leader of the Patriotes and one of the leaders of the 1837 rebellion. He organized the St Ours meeting, chaired the large St Charles meeting, and declared that spoons should be melted into bullets. As conflict approached, he warned Patriotes to arm, commanded the rebel force at St Denis, prepared its defenses by including bridge destruction, and ordered Lieutenant Weir detained. Known as the victor of St Denis, he was later believed still to be holding out near St Cesaire. After great privations near the frontier, loyalist militia captured him and took him to Montreal. He eventually returned to Montreal and his medical practice.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

{57} CHAPTER VII THE RUSSELL RESOLUTIONS When the legislature of Lower Canada met in the autumn of 1836, Lord Gosford earnestly called its attention to the estimates of the current year and the accounts showing the arrears unpaid.

Dr Wolfred Nelson organized the St Ours meeting and chaired the large meeting at St Charles. At St Charles he declared that the time had come to melt spoons into bullets, becoming one of the leaders of the revolt at the end of 1837.

] {69} CHAPTER VIII THE DOGS OF WAR As the autumn of 1837 wore on, the situation in Lower Canada began to assume an aspect more and more threatening.

Wolfred Nelson warned the Patriotes at a public meeting to be ready to arm and commanded the Patriote force at St Denis. He helped prepare the village's defenses, including the destruction of bridges, and ordered Lieutenant Weir placed in detention. Nelson later denied advising Papineau to leave St Denis before the battle ended.

{89} But the end of rebellion was not yet in sight.

Wolfred Nelson was believed by the invaders from Swanton to be still holding out, possibly at St Cesaire. Reports identified him as the victor of St Denis.
