Red Jinn's bell in Jack Pumpkinhead of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1929

Red Jinn's bell is a famous silver magic dinner bell, carved with its name and belonging to the Red Djinn/Jinn. When rung, it summons a little black slave or slave of the bell carrying food on a silver tray; it can rapidly provide dinners, though its fifth use produces only a small bowl of soup. Jack Pumpkinhead first uses it for dinner, later feeds Peter and Belfaygor, and keeps it among the group's magical objects after leaving Mogodore's palace. During Peter and Jack's conquest of Scare City, it is useful against the Scares. The bell brings Jack to the Palace of the Red Jinn, where the Red Jinn declines its return, saying its theft connected him to the outside world and brought him two extra slaves.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 3

The Red Djinn's dinner bell is a silver magic bell with its name carved on it. When Jack Pumpkinhead rings it, a black slave appears with a tray of dinner, and the bell later vanishes with the tray before Jack keeps it.

CHAPTER 4

When Jack Pumpkinhead rings the magic dinner bell, a little slave appears and sets down a tray of food before disappearing. It provides five dinners in less than five minutes, but on the fifth ringing it produces only a small bowl of soup.

CHAPTER 7

The magic dinner bell summons a little black slave carrying food on a silver tray. Jack Pumpkinhead rings it to provide meals for Peter and Belfaygor while they wait for night.

CHAPTER 13

Jack checks that the famous dinner bell is safe before the group leaves Mogodore's palace. It remains one of the magical objects they carry with them.

CHAPTER 16

The magic dinner bell brought Jack Pumpkinhead to the Palace of the Red Jinn. Although the bell belongs to the Red Jinn, he does not want it returned and says that its theft has connected him with the outside world and brought him two extra slaves.
