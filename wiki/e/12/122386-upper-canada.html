Upper Canada in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

Upper Canada was a predominantly English province created in 1791 as one of two provinces. It received political rights equal to Lower Canada, had more disloyalty during the War of 1812, and was profoundly affected by rebellion, for which Samuel Lount and Peter Matthews were hanged.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

{7} CHAPTER II THE RIGHTS OF THE DEFEATED The British did not treat the French inhabitants of Canada as a conquered people; not as other countries won by conquest have been treated by their victorious invaders.

Upper Canada was created in 1791 as one of two provinces, predominantly English in population. It received political rights equal to those of Lower Canada.

{104} CHAPTER X THE LORD HIGH COMMISSIONER The rebellions in Upper and Lower Canada profoundly affected public opinion in the mother country.

Upper Canada was one of the provinces affected by rebellion. Samuel Lount and Peter Matthews were hanged there for rebellion.
