Russell resolutions

topic · 1 work · 4 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Russell Resolutions, resolutions, ten resolutions

Abstract written by a model

The Russell resolutions responded to Lower Canada’s 1836 legislative deadlock, saying the Assembly had withheld supplies and failed to fund justice and civil government while pursuing political demands. They rejected an elective Legislative Council and an Executive Council responsible to the Assembly, offered Crown revenues for a permanent civil list, and authorized the governor to pay government costs until April 10, 1837, contributing to Robert Shore Milnes Bouchette’s rebellion.

{57} CHAPTER VII THE RUSSELL RESOLUTIONS When the legislature of Lower Canada met in the autumn of 1836, Lord Gosford earnestly called its attention to the estimates of the current year and the accounts showing the arrears unpaid.

The Russell resolutions stated that the Assembly had failed to provide for the administration of justice and civil government and had refused supplies until political demands were met. They rejected making the Legislative Council elective and making the Executive Council responsible to the Assembly, while authorizing payment of government costs through April 10, 1837.
