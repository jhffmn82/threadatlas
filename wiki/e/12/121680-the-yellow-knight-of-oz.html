The Yellow Knight of Oz in The Yellow Knight of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1930

The Yellow Knight of Oz is a 1930 Oz story by Ruth Plumly Thompson, continuing the Oz stories of L. Frank Baum and illustrated by John R. Neill. It begins when Sir Hokus of Pokes secretly leaves the Emerald City on a quest to uncover his lost past, avoiding an unwanted crowd of companions. In Marshland, he escapes Queen Marcia’s forced marriage plan with help from the giant mud turtle Ploppa. Meanwhile, the Sultan of Samandra sends Tuzzle to recover the Comfortable Camel, Camy, whom he believes carries valuable magic dates. A separate adventure follows Speedy, whose Skyrocket crashes into the Underworld. He rescues the golden statue Princess Marygolden, and the two escape through strange lands. In Oz, a transformed chestnut burr becomes the talking horse Stampedro. Sir Hokus, reunited with Camy and joined by the transformed jester Peter Pun, loses Camy when Tuzzle captures him and takes him to Samandra. Speedy, Marygolden, Stampedro, Sir Hokus, and Peter Pun meet and determine that the magic dates are tied to the enchantment of Stampedro, Peter, the Yellow Knight, and whole kingdoms. They enter Samandra, evade the Sultan’s guards, recover a magic date seed, and grow a palm bearing six magic dates. These restore the King and Queen of Corumbia, their people, and the Golden Castle. By morning, Sir Hokus is restored as the Yellow Knight, Prince of Corumbia, though he initially remembers nothing. The companions then restore Corabia, whose people had become fishes and frogs. In a contest for the still-enchanted Princess of Corabia, Sir Hokus uses a final date to restore her, revealing her as Marygolden. They marry. Ozma and Dorothy arrive after the Wizard’s searchlight locates Sir Hokus and Camy. Ozma strips the Sultan of his magic and confines him to Samandra, while Speedy returns to Uncle Billy’s workshop after recounting his adventures.

The Yellow Knight of Oz

The Yellow Knight of Oz is a 1930 book by Ruth Plumly Thompson, founded on and continuing the Oz stories by L. Frank Baum and illustrated by John R. Neill. Its introduction announces a quest by Sir Hokus of Pokes involving a strange Yellow Knight and his horse, Stampedro. The contents list adventures involving Queen Marcia, Ploppa, a camel, Tuzzle, a golden princess, and the restoration of Corumbia, culminating in the Yellow Knight's return and the marriage of Marygolden.

CHAPTER ONE

Sir Hokus of Pokes announces that he intends to go on a quest, and Dorothy agrees to accompany him on Monday. Scraps spreads the news, causing nearly everyone in the Emerald City and Ozma's palace to prepare to join the adventure. Overwhelmed by the prospect of traveling with an entire army, Sir Hokus secretly leaves the palace at night with his battle-axe and second-best helmet. The nature of the quest has not yet been revealed.

CHAPTER TWO

Sir Hokus leaves the Emerald City and travels east toward the Winkie Country to learn more about his lost past. After an unhelpful encounter with a night owl, he meets Getsom and Gotsom, Mud Guards of Queen Marcia of Marshland, who offer him kingship and marriage to their one-eyed queen. Sir Hokus refuses, escapes into a dangerous marsh, and is nearly dragged under by frog goblins. A giant mud turtle named Ploppa rescues him and carries him across the swamp.

CHAPTER THREE

Sir Hokus and Ploppa travel through Marshland, where the Marshlanders use stilts and live in mud-thatched houses high in the trees. They arrive at the Royal Hut of Queen Marcia, whose Mud Guards seize Sir Hokus and proclaim him King. Queen Marcia declares that she will marry Sir Hokus, while her sister Mira also claims him. Sir Hokus eats the royal duck, is locked in a room, and escapes through a barred window with Ploppa's help beginning to take shape.

CHAPTER FOUR

Sir Hokus is held in the Queen of the Marshes' hut while preparations for his unwanted wedding to Marcia proceed. When Marcia attacks him with her bouquet, three calls from the window signal Ploppa, allowing Sir Hokus to escape through the window onto the turtle's shell. Ploppa has knocked down the nearby stilts, preventing the Stick-in-the-Muds from pursuing them. Sir Hokus leaves the Marshes to seek adventure, while Ploppa returns sadly to the mud.

CHAPTER FIVE

The Sultan of Samandra is desperate to recover his lost favorite camel, which the Seer Chinda reveals is in the Emerald City at the Royal Stable of Queen Ozma of Oz. The Sultan sends his Grand Vizier, Tuzzle, with the Royal Sampan and twenty slaves down the Winkie River to retrieve the camel using a golden halter. The Sultan secretly wants the package hidden in the camel's left hand saddle sack rather than the camel itself. Tuzzle's party reaches the outskirts of the Emerald City on the second evening.

CHAPTER SIX

Tuzzle brings greetings from the Sultan of Samandra to Ozma and asks for the return of the Comfortable Camel, which he claims was lost from the Sultan's herd ten years earlier. Ozma and her companions discover that the camel and Sir Hokus are missing from the Royal Stables and the palace, so Tuzzle returns to Samandra without receiving the camel. The magic picture, which could have located them, is also missing. The Wizard offers to find them with a new searchlight if they do not soon return.

CHAPTER SEVEN

Speedy and his Uncle Billy attempt to travel to Mars in the Skyrocket, but the craft launches before Uncle Billy can board. Speedy turns the Skyrocket back toward Earth, causing it to crash through the ground and travel deep into the Underworld. The craft stops in Subterranea, where the Shah orders Speedy to repair the hole he made in the underground sky or face being thrown to the fire fish in Lava Lake. Zunda tells Speedy about the Underworld's nine levels and warns him that his execution is scheduled for the next morning.

CHAPTER EIGHT

Speedy is led through Subterranea and escapes a sun storm by running through the underwood and into the cavernous country beyond. In a cave, he discovers a golden statue that comes to life as Princess Marygolden, and he rescues her from a groper. When the Subterraneans react angrily to Marygolden's return, Speedy takes her into the Skyrocket and uses the parashuter to try to escape upward through the shaft. The parashuter misses the shaft and begins tearing through earth, rock, and sand.

CHAPTER NINE

Sir Hokus travels toward a strange, tall castle and is captured by a serpent tree while resting in a wood. He escapes when his battle song makes the serpents sway and release him, then reunites with Camy, the Comfortable Camel, who has followed him from the capital. Sir Hokus and Camy camp in a field, but during the night the entire field flies through the sky. At dawn the field descends and tilts, throwing them off before flying away without them.

CHAPTER TEN

Sir Hokus and the Comfortable Camel arrive in a deserted golden castle after traveling on a flying field, but find no inhabitants despite hearing trumpets. They enter an ancient forest, where strange plants and animals seem enchanted, and Sir Hokus discovers that a funnysuckle vine has been transformed into a jester. The jester joins Sir Hokus and the Comfortable Camel on their journey, although he remembers nothing about the castle or the King he once served. Sir Hokus continues his quest to assist a maiden, slay a monster, or serve a monarch as they hurry out of the bewitched forest.

CHAPTER ELEVEN

Sir Hokus, Peter Pun, and the Comfortable Camel travel onward while Peter reveals his name and continues making jokes and puns. A boat carrying Tuzzle and twenty slaves captures the Comfortable Camel with a rope and drags him away. Sir Hokus and Peter attempt to pursue the royal barge but quickly lose sight of it. They reach a fork in the river, choose the right branch by tossing Sir Hokus's sword, and continue the rescue attempt.

CHAPTER TWELVE

Camy, the Comfortable Camel, is captured by Tuzzle and brought back to Samandra, where the Sultan welcomes him only because he believes the missing magic dates are in Camy's saddle sack. Camy cannot speak and remembers his former life as a mistreated beast of burden, but he resolves to escape and hopes Sir Hokus and Peter will help him. The Sultan discovers that the dates are gone and learns from Chinda's magic telescope that part of what he seeks will be found in the middle of the night. Chinda begins trying to determine the missing package's exact location through further divination.

CHAPTER THIRTEEN

Speedy and Marygolden escape Subterranea in Uncle Billy's parashuter and emerge into the land of the Quix. The Quix declare Speedy their king because he fulfills a prophecy, and he is crowned with the Lively Hood. The hood causes Speedy and the Quix to cycle rapidly through ages, but Marygolden removes it at the right moment. They flee Quick City and find temporary shelter in a quiet forest.

CHAPTER FOURTEEN

Speedy and Marygolden rest after leaving Quick City, then travel through the Enchanted Forest. Speedy cuts away Marygolden’s old-fashioned dress trimmings, and she throws away her crown. A chestnut burr transforms into the talking horse Stampedro, who carries them toward the country of the Yellow Knight and reveals that they are in Oz. They find a yellow basket of food in a river and then see an unrelated silver-armored knight across the water.

CHAPTER FIFTEEN

Speedy, Marygolden, and Stampedro meet Sir Hokus of Pokes and Peter Pun in Samandra, where animals cannot talk. They exchange stories and conclude that the magic dates connected to the Comfortable Camel may have caused the enchantment of Stampedro, Peter Pun, the Yellow Knight of Oz, and an entire kingdom. Speedy agrees to help Sir Hokus and Peter Pun find the stolen Comfortable Camel and rescue the enchanted King. The five travellers then set off together toward the yellow boat's trail, riding Stampedro.

CHAPTER SIXTEEN

Sir Hokus, Speedy, Marygolden, Peter Pun, and Stampedro reach Samandra, where the Sultan recognizes them as threats and orders his spearmen to seize them. Stampedro carries Marygolden and Peter Pun away while Sir Hokus fights through the guards, and Speedy escapes the palace tower with Confido after discovering the magic date seed's location through Chinda's magical telescope. Speedy reunites with his companions, leads them to the horse chestnut tree, and retrieves the shining seed. Confido initially refuses to explain how to release the enchanted people, but Marygolden persuades him to whisper the secret to her.

CHAPTER SEVENTEEN

Speedy plants the magic date seed, is carried high into a rapidly growing palm, and returns with six ripe magic dates. Sir Hokus of Pokes arrives with Camy, and Speedy, Marygolden, Peter Pun, Stampedro, and Confido use the dates to restore the King of Corumbia, the Queen of Corumbia, the Corumbians, and the Golden Castle. The Yellow Knight of Oz remains enchanted until morning, when the Queen must use another date from the tallest tower. The restored Corumbians feast and prepare to defend Corumbia against the Sultan.

CHAPTER EIGHTEEN

Marygolden, Speedy, Confido, and the Comfortable Camel witness the restoration of Sir Hokus of Pokes as the Yellow Knight, Prince of Corumbia, but he does not remember them and rides away to seek the Princess of Corabia. They follow him to the deserted Castle of Corabia, where they learn that the Corabians have been transformed into fishes and frogs and that the castle and Princess remain enchanted. Using the remaining magic dates, Speedy restores the Corabians and Castle of Corabia. The Yellow Knight and four other Knights then prepare to compete in the grand test of skill and courage for the hand of the still-enchanted Princess of Corabia.

CHAPTER NINETEEN

The King of Corabia announces that the grand contest for Princess Marygolden's hand must continue so that its winner can disenchant her. Sir Hokus, disguised as the Yellow Knight, bravely chooses the center of three brass rings and uses a magic date to restore the Princess, who remembers Speedy, the Comfortable Camel of Oz, and her former adventures. Sir Hokus and Princess Marygolden prepare to marry, while Speedy and the Camel agree to remain for the wedding. Ozma and Princess Dorothy suddenly arrive in a flash of magic and are greeted by the rulers and people of Corabia and Corumbia.

CHAPTER TWENTY

Ozma and Dorothy arrive in Corabia after the Wizard of Oz's searchlight locates the Comfortable Camel and Sir Hokus. They learn that Sir Hokus has been restored as the Yellow Knight and Prince of Corumbia, while Speedy helped restore Corumbia and Corabia and discover Princess Marygolden. Ozma summons the Sultan of Samandra, removes his magic powers, and sends him back to Samandra after the rulers sentence him to return what he stole and remain within his country for five hundred years. Marygolden marries Sir Hokus, and Ozma returns Speedy to Uncle Billy's workshop, where he recounts his adventures and says Mars would seem dull after Oz.
