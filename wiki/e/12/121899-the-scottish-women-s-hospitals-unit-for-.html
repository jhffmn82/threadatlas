the Scottish Women’s Hospitals unit for the Serbian Army in Dr. Elsie Inglis

Lady Frances Balfour · 1918

The Scottish Women’s Hospitals unit for the Serbian Army was a medical, hospital, nursing, and transport unit serving Serbian wounded and volunteers in wartime. In Serbia, the First Serbian Unit, led by Dr. Eleanor Soltau, worked during the typhus epidemic in crowded hospitals treating dying patients. At Kragujevatz, it nursed 550 beds across three hospitals: Reserve No. 3, a surgical hospital with an X-ray room and operating theatre; No. 6 Reserve, for typhus; and No. 7 Reserve, for relapsing fever and general diseases. As fever declined, it reorganised as tent hospitals near the front and prepared a disinfecting centre at Mladanovatz. During the Austro-German invasion and the 1915 retreat, 115 personnel remained with Serbian wounded. Under occupation at the Serbian military hospital at Krushieevatz, they treated extreme numbers of patients and fought typhus through sanitation, laundry, and bathing before being sent north under guard in February 1916. In 1917, an eighty-woman hospital and transport section travelled through Russia via Archangel and Moscow with fourteen cars to serve Serbian Volunteers on the Rumanian Russian front. Led by Dr. Elsie Inglis, it worked through evacuations, safeguarded its equipment, travelled west with Serbian soldiers, and cared for her during her final illness.

The First Serbian Unit was led by Dr. Eleanor Soltau. It worked in Serbia during the typhus epidemic and was among the Scottish women who went directly to crowded hospitals treating dying patients.

opening

The Scottish Women’s Hospitals unit at Kragujevatz nursed 550 beds in three hospitals, including surgical, typhus, and general-disease wards. It equipped a surgical hospital with an X-ray room and operating theatre and later prepared to establish a disinfecting centre at Mladanovatz.

’ As the fever died out, a worse enemy came in.

The Scottish hospital unit reorganised itself as tent hospitals, worked near the front, and later remained with Serbian wounded during the Austro-German invasion. At Krushieevatz it treated extreme numbers of patients, managed sanitation and laundry, and endured occupation before being sent north under guard in February 1916.

CHAPTER X RUSSIA 1917 ‘Even so in our mortal journey, The bitter north winds blow, And thus upon life’s red river, Our hearts as oarsmen row.

The Scottish Women’s Hospitals unit for the Serbian Army consisted initially of eighty women and a transport section. It sailed with fourteen cars, traveled via Archangel and Moscow, and was prepared to treat wounded Serbian forces on the Rumanian Russian front.

’ CHAPTER XI THE MOORINGS CUT ‘Not I, but my Unit.

The unit worked night and day during the evacuations, overcoming disorder and transporting its equipment safely. Its members accompanied Dr. Inglis and Serbian soldiers on the journey west, cared for her during her final illness, and recorded her courage, discipline, kindness, and consideration.
