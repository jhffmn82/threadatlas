Abstract written by a model

This document is the paper “TurboRAG: Accelerating Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Precomputed KV Caches for Chunked Text,” by Songshuo Lu, Hua Wang, Yutian Rong, Zhi Chen, and Yaohua Tang of Moore Threads AI, published in the Proceedings of the 2025 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing. It addresses the online prefill cost and time-to-first-token (TTFT) bottleneck in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), where a large language model uses retrieved external documents to answer questions. TurboRAG uses a hybrid offline-online design: it precomputes key-value caches for document chunks offline, retrieves those caches at inference, and stitches them into a contextual cache rather than recomputing retrieved text. Independent Attention prevents cross-attention among document chunks, while Composite Positions and Reordered Positions address RoPE positional encoding when chunks are concatenated or reordered. The reordered approach applies RoPE at inference with negligible added cost. Lightweight supervised fine-tuning adapts the model to the new attention masks and position handling, then inference retrieves documents, forms the cache, prefills the model, and generates an answer. The paper situates TurboRAG among approaches that shorten retrieved text, accelerate decoding, reuse or compress caches, and optimize attention; it presents TurboRAG as complementary to cache compression, speculative decoding, prefix caching, and decoding-kernel optimization. Experiments compare Naïve RAG, TurboRAG variants, and GPT-4o-2024-08-06 on RGB and LongBench document question answering, general capabilities, TTFT, and batch scaling. Fine-tuned models retain broadly comparable accuracy, with TurboRAG-reordered generally stronger than TurboRAG-composite. Reported LongBench average TTFT reduction is 8.6×, up to 9.4× in the main results; Qwen-2 results reach 11.23× for the 72B model. Tested batch scaling shows about 98.46% fewer TFLOPs than Naïve RAG, and advantages persist as batch size grows. Supplementary material gives positional-encoding pseudocode, fine-tuning data proportions, an RGB example, LLaMA-3.1-8B results across noise ratios, and FLOPS calculations. The conclusion emphasizes reduced online computation but notes storage, memory, distributed deployment, dynamic knowledge bases, and continued fine-tuning as limitations; KV-cache compression is proposed as an orthogonal future direction.

Abstract

The unit concerns TurboRAG, a hybrid offline-online approach for reducing the computation overhead and time-to-first-token (TTFT) of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems. TurboRAG pre-computes chunk-level key-value (KV) caches and stitches them together during inference using independent-attention and reordered-RoPE techniques. It applies to most existing large language models and their applications without modifying model architectures or inference systems. Across RAG benchmarks, TurboRAG reduces TTFT by up to 9.4x, with an average reduction of 8.6x, while preserving performance comparable to standard RAG systems.

1 / Introduction .. Contributions

This unit concerns Retrieval–Augmented Generation (RAG), which combines a large language model with a dense retriever to ground generation in external knowledge. It introduces TurboRAG, a framework that precomputes chunk-level KV caches offline and reuses retrieved caches during inference with independent attention masks and reordered position IDs. TurboRAG reduces TTFT by up to 9.4× while maintaining comparable accuracy to standard RAG, and it substantially lowers online computational resource utilization. The approach is evaluated on LongBench multi-document question answering benchmarks and is supported by lightweight supervised fine-tuning.

2 / Related Work

The unit reviews methods for accelerating Retrieval-Augmented Generation by reducing retrieved text, making decoding more efficient, and reusing caches. It describes TurboRAG as a cache-reuse approach that uses RoPE-consistent position reordering and an independent-attention mask to stitch passage caches despite changes in passage order. TurboRAG aims to eliminate online prefill computation for retrieved content by loading precomputed passage caches from storage. The unit also explains that TurboRAG is complementary to cache compression, speculative decoding, cross-request prefix caching, and decoding-kernel optimization.

3 / Methodology .. 3.4 / The TurboRAG Pipeline

TurboRAG is a retrieval-augmented generation approach that improves conventional RAG performance by precomputing document KV caches and concatenating them during inference. It introduces Independent Attention, which prevents cross-attention between document chunks, and rearranges position IDs so that concatenated caches reflect the documents' original token offsets. TurboRAG fine-tunes a large language model with the new attention masks and position embeddings to reduce question-answering accuracy loss. Its inference pipeline encodes and retrieves documents, forms a contextual KV cache, prefills the model, and generates an answer.

4 / Experiments .. 4.5 / Batch Scaling

This unit evaluates TurboRAG and its composite and reordered variants against Naïve RAG and GPT-4o-2024-08-06 on document question-answering, general capabilities, TTFT, and batch scaling. On RGB Benchmark and LongBench, fine-tuned TurboRAG models achieve accuracy comparable to Naïve RAG, while TurboRAG-reordered generally outperforms TurboRAG-composite. TurboRAG-reordered reduces average TTFT by 8.6x on LongBench multi-document QA and maintains a substantial advantage as batch size increases. The experiments also find minimal regression on general capabilities and an approximately 98.46% reduction in TFLOPs compared with Naïve RAG under the tested batch-scaling setup.

5 / Conclusion and Discussion .. Limitations

This unit concerns TurboRAG, a retrieval-augmented generation approach that reduces prefill computation and time to first token by pre-computing and concatenating the KV caches of retrieved text fragments without inter-document cross-attention. The reported Qwen-2 results show increasing TTFT speedups as model size and batch size grow, reaching up to 11.23× for the 72B model. The discussion addresses deployment challenges involving dynamic knowledge bases, KV-cache storage, memory, and distributed systems. The stated limitations are storage overhead and the continued need for model fine-tuning, while KV cache compression is identified as an orthogonal future direction.

Abstract written by a model

TurboRAG is a hybrid offline–online retrieval-augmented generation framework for latency-optimized large-language-model use. Offline, it precomputes and stores chunk- or passage-level key-value caches. At inference time, it retrieves relevant caches and stitches or concatenates them rather than performing online prefill computation for the retrieved content. This stitching uses Independent Attention, which masks cross-chunk or inter-document attention, and reordered, RoPE-consistent positions, with position IDs reordered into monotone increasing numbers within chunks. These techniques support order-agnostic cache stitching while preserving positional semantics. The system is intended for most existing large language models without changing model architecture or inference systems, although its pipeline uses lightweight supervised fine-tuning so a model can consume the new cache layout and attention setting. Its reported goal and main result are sharply reduced time to first token while retaining accuracy comparable to conventional or Naïve RAG. Reported reductions include up to 9.4× TTFT improvement, 8.6× on average, about 98.5% lower online GPU resource use, and 98.46% fewer TFLOPs in a batch-scaling experiment. With host-to-device transfer, reported speedups range from 4.1× at batch size 1 to 4.7× at batch size 6. Reduced resource use also increases supported batch size. TurboRAG is evaluated on LongBench multi-document QA benchmarks. It can complement retrieval sparsification, cache compression, speculative decoding, cross-request prefix caching, and decoding-phase attention optimization. Its positional-encoding options include Composite Positions and Reordered Positions. From setup through inference, it shifts document-cache computation offline, retrieves and joins caches online with modified masks and positions, then generates with lower prefill latency. Its stated trade-off is increased storage space and memory pressure, and the current pipeline still requires fine-tuning.

Abstract

TurboRAG is a hybrid offline-online paradigm that pre-computes chunk-level key-value caches and stitches them together at inference time using independent-attention and reordered-RoPE techniques. It is applicable to most existing large language models and their applications without modifying the model architecture, and it reduces time-to-first-token by up to 9.4x compared with conventional RAG systems.

1 / Introduction .. Contributions

TurboRAG is a hybrid offline–online RAG framework that precomputes and stores KV caches for passages, retrieves relevant caches at inference time, and stitches them using independent attention and reordered positions. It reduces TTFT by up to 9.4× compared with conventional RAG, with comparable accuracy, and reduces online computational resource utilization by 98.5%.

2 / Related Work

TurboRAG introduces RoPE-consistent position reordering and an independent-attention mask for order-agnostic stitching of precomputed passage caches with negligible accuracy loss. It aims to eliminate online prefill computation for retrieved content and is compatible with retrieval sparsification, cache compression, speculative decoding, cross-request prefix caching, and decoding-phase attention optimization.

3 / Methodology .. 3.4 / The TurboRAG Pipeline

TurboRAG improves conventional retrieval-augmented generation by precomputing KV caches for documents and concatenating the retrieved caches during inference. It uses Independent Attention and reordered positions, and it fine-tunes the large language model to handle these patterns.

4 / Experiments .. 4.5 / Batch Scaling

TurboRAG is evaluated as a modified RAG system using offline document KV-cache computation and non-standard attention masks and position IDs. Its fine-tuned variants achieve document-QA accuracy comparable to Naïve RAG, substantially reduce TTFT, and use approximately 98.46% fewer TFLOPs in the reported batch-scaling experiment.

5 / Conclusion and Discussion .. Limitations

TurboRAG pre-computes and concatenates the KV caches of retrieved text fragments without inter-document cross-attention to reduce prefill latency. It trades storage space and memory pressure for substantially lower time to first token, and its current pipeline still requires model fine-tuning.

B / Positional-Encoding Options in

TurboRAG is discussed in connection with two approaches for handling positional encodings: Composite Positions and Reordered Positions.
