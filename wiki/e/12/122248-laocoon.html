Laocoon in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Laocoon is a Rhodian sculptural group by Agesandros with Athanodorus and Polydorus, showing the priest Laocoon and his two sons destroyed by Apollo’s serpents. Created during the decline of Greek art, it culminates pathetic action and technical skill, yet is judged a tour de force of accidental horror with little serious meaning.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

In Athens, after Pheidias, the greatest sculptures were those

The Laocoon represents the priest Laocoon and his two sons being destroyed by the serpents of Apollo. It displays extraordinary technical skill and extreme pathetic action, but the unit judges it a tour de force whose interest rests on accidental horror rather than ethical significance or a serious conception of life.
