Serbian prisoners in Dr. Elsie Inglis

Lady Frances Balfour · 1918

Serbian prisoners, held in the military hospital at Krushieevatz after forced marches, suffer cold, hunger, scant rations, inadequate shelter, and lice; arrivals grow to thousands daily, with many sent north while the sick remain in crowded wards.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

’ As the fever died out, a worse enemy came in.

Serbian prisoners arrived daily, sometimes by the thousands, and were sent north while the sick remained at the hospital. They endured hunger, cold, forced marches, inadequate shelter, and the loss of firewood, while those placed in the wards often arrived unwashed and infested with lice.
