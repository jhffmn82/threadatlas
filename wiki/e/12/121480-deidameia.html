Deidameia in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

Deidameia, Lycomedes’ daughter and queen of Scyros, is Achilles’ grieving partner and Neoptolemus’ mother. After Achilles’ death, she fears war will take her son, begs him to stay, then mourns his departure, his bed, toys, and belongings as his ship vanishes.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK VII

Deidameia is grief-wasted by Achilles' death and fears that her son will also perish in war. She pleads with him to remain in Scyros, but after he departs she mourns over his bed, childhood toys, and belongings.

So came they to the heavy-plunging sea,

Deidameia remains in her bower grieving for Neoptolemus as he sails away. She sighs and weeps while watching the ship disappear into the sea-haze, and her mourning continues throughout the day.
