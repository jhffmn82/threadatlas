Abstract written by a model

The Battle of St Denis was a battle between British troops and the Patriotes at St Denis, and the first engagement of the rebellion. Colonel Charles Gore attacked a strongly defended Patriote position under Wolfred Nelson. After more than five hours, Gore withdrew, leaving six soldiers killed, eighteen wounded, and his twelve-pounder abandoned in the mud; the Patriotes reportedly lost twelve men. It was the only victory for the rebel forces and was identified with Nelson’s victory. Its news encouraged rebels at St Eustache, though Gore’s column later entered St Denis without opposition.

] {69} CHAPTER VIII THE DOGS OF WAR As the autumn of 1837 wore on, the situation in Lower Canada began to assume an aspect more and more threatening.

The Battle of St Denis began when Colonel Charles Gore attacked a strongly defended Patriote position under Wolfred Nelson. After more than five hours, Gore withdrew with six soldiers killed and eighteen wounded, abandoning his twelve-pounder in the mud; the Patriotes reportedly lost twelve men.

{82} CHAPTER IX _FORCE MAJEURE_ The check administered to Colonel Gore's column at St Denis, in the first engagement of the rebellion, was the only victory which fell to the rebel forces.

The engagement at St Denis was the first engagement of the rebellion and produced the only victory for the rebel forces. Colonel Gore's column reached St Denis on November 23 but was later able to enter the place without opposition.

{89} But the end of rebellion was not yet in sight.

The battle of St Denis was identified with Wolfred Nelson's victory. News of that victory encouraged the rebels at St Eustache before the later news of defeat at St Charles moderated them.
