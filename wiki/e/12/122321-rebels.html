rebels in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

The rebels' only victory was over Colonel Gore's column at St Denis, the rebellion's first engagement. Fewer in number at St Charles, they were defeated and disbanded as leaders fled. At St Eustache, nearly a thousand poorly armed, undisciplined men defended the village, expecting to capture Montreal, but were defeated; many were killed, captured, or forced to flee.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

{82} CHAPTER IX _FORCE MAJEURE_ The check administered to Colonel Gore's column at St Denis, in the first engagement of the rebellion, was the only victory which fell to the rebel forces.

The rebel forces won at St Denis but were defeated at St Charles. After the defeat at St Charles, they immediately disbanded, while their leaders sought safety in flight.

{89} But the end of rebellion was not yet in sight.

The rebels at St Eustache were poorly disciplined, frequently drank, lacked adequate weapons, and believed they could capture Montreal. They defended the village but were defeated, with many killed, captured, or forced to flee.
