Venus de' Medici in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Venus de' Medici is a statue depicting Venus, described as a degenerate and sensuous late Greek conception. Named for Medici princes who owned it in Rome, it bears a doubtful Cleomenes attribution and was praised as an ideal of female beauty.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXVIII

The Venus de' Medici received its name from having belonged to the princes of that name in Rome. Its inscription attributes it to Cleomenes, but that attribution is doubtful; the statue was associated with the ideal of female beauty and was praised by Thomson and Byron.
