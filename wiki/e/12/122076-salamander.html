salamander in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Salamander is an imaginary being and kind of lizard, traditionally believed to resist or extinguish fire. The legend was linked to its milky secretion and winter hibernation in cavities that could be carried into fires. Its supposed skin or wool was made into fire-proof cloth, later confused with asbestos. Actual salamanders could escape fires but could be burned; their secretion protected them only briefly.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXIX

The salamander was believed by Aristotle, Pliny, Benvenuto Cellini, and others to resist or extinguish fire. The text explains the legend through the real salamander’s milky secretion, its habit of hibernating in places that might be carried into fires, and the mistaken identification of asbestos cloth as salamander’s wool. Actual salamanders could escape fire but were sometimes burned, and their secretion would protect them only briefly.
