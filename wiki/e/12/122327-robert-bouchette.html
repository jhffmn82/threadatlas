Robert Bouchette in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

Robert Bouchette, grandson of Bouchette's grandfather and member of a long-loyal French-Canadian family, became a leader in the 1837 Lower Canada rebellion after the Russell Resolutions drew him to the extreme Patriotes. Devoted to liberty and rights he considered inherent to British subjects, he joined the invasion from Swanton, commanded its advance guard, and was wounded and captured at Moore's Corners. Though a stranger to his men and arrested for disloyalty, he recovered and later became commissioner of customs at Ottawa.

{89} But the end of rebellion was not yet in sight.

Robert Shore Milnes Bouchette, a member of a long-loyal French-Canadian family, joined the extreme Patriotes after the Russell Resolutions. He took part in the invasion from Swanton, commanded its advance guard, and was wounded and captured at Moore's Corners.

{128} CHAPTER XII A POSTSCRIPT The rebellion of 1837 now belongs to the dead past.

Robert Bouchette is presented as a rebel who showed devotion to liberty and the rights of the people. He later recovered from a serious wound sustained at Moore's Corners and became Her Majesty's commissioner of customs at Ottawa.
