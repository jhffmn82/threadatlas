Abstract written by a model

Odessa is a port city with major shipping and wharf facilities. It hosts the British medical unit as guests and is the destination of its journey through Russia. Near Odessa, Elsie writes from an ambulance train in January 1917. The city then gives the unit an enthusiastic reception, providing officials, transport, quarters, and public celebrations, and shows deep interest in it. Elsie later returns to investigate absent Serbian patients. From Odessa, Inglis writes while Reni work closes and the unit prepares to join the Serbian Division; her last June 1917 letter praises that division.

CHAPTER X RUSSIA 1917 ‘Even so in our mortal journey, The bitter north winds blow, And thus upon life’s red river, Our hearts as oarsmen row.

Odessa was the destination of the unit’s journey through Russia and the place from which Dr. Inglis sent her last letter in June 1917. Her final words from Odessa praised the Serbian Division as superb and expressed pride in being attached to it.

‘NEARING ODESSA, ‘_Sep.

Odessa gave the unit an enthusiastic reception, with Russian officials, the consul, Serbian officers, transport, quarters, and public celebrations assisting or welcoming them. The city was deeply interested in the unit, and Elsie later returned there to investigate why Serbian patients were not being sent to the hospital.

’ ‘IN AN AMBULANCE TRAIN, ‘NEAR ODESSA, _Jan.

Odessa is named as the place near which Elsie writes on January 25, 1917, from an ambulance train.

‘_On Active Service.

Inglis writes from Odessa in June and August 1917 while the Reni work is being wound up and arrangements are made to join the Serbian Division. She reports that the Reni hospital is full and busy before its closure.
