English members of the Assembly in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

English members of the Assembly owed their election to French inhabitants of Canada and advocated a direct land tax to fund prisons. They opposed Jean Antoine Panet’s election, pressed for dealings with the king’s representative and official business in English, and sought assimilation and greater loyalty among French Canadians.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

{7} CHAPTER II THE RIGHTS OF THE DEFEATED The British did not treat the French inhabitants of Canada as a conquered people; not as other countries won by conquest have been treated by their victorious invaders.

The English members of the Assembly opposed the election of Jean Antoine Panet and argued that dealings with the king's representative should occur in English. They also argued that exclusive official use of English would assimilate the French Canadians and make them more loyal.
