Abstract written by a model

Women doctors emerge as capable but contested medical practitioners. Though qualified and active in hospital work, they face exclusion from medical societies, college fellowships, and clinical opportunities often given to men, alongside assumptions that women cannot be doctors. Their work demonstrates demand: the New Hospital for Women records more than 20,000 outpatient visits in the year to November, while figures such as Miss D. and Miss MacGregor show professional achievement. At the Hospice, medical women gain opportunities denied in other maternity hospitals, as Dr. Inglis supports their education, clinical practice, laboratory access, and professional recognition.

CHAPTER V LONDON THE NEW HOSPITAL FOR WOMEN DUBLIN THE ROTUNDA 1892-1894 ‘We take up the task eternal and the burden and the lesson, Pioneers, O Pioneers.

Women doctors at the New Hospital for Women had achieved substantial work despite exclusion and discouragement, but some, including Garrett Anderson and Mrs. Scharlieb, were barred from fellowship of the colleges and membership of certain societies. The hospital’s success, including more than 20,000 outpatient visits in the year up to November, was presented as evidence that women doctors were wanted.

’ The next course Elsie decided on taking was one of three months in Midwifery i

The unit shows that women doctors face assumptions that women cannot be doctors and are often overlooked for clinical opportunities given to men. Elsie also describes capable women doctors, including Miss D., who holds an M.B. and receives a travelling bursary, and Miss MacGregor, whom she considers a leading woman pathologist.
