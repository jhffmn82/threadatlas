unicorn in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The unicorn is an imaginary modern monster described as a fierce horse-like creature with a deer’s head, elephant’s feet, a boar’s tail, a deep bellowing voice, and a two-cubit black horn. It cannot be taken alive, yet hunters reportedly capture it by using a virgin: it approaches, rests its head in her lap, and falls asleep. Modern zoologists generally disbelieve it.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXIX

The unicorn is described by Pliny as a fierce horse-like beast with a deer’s head, elephant’s feet, a boar’s tail, and one long black horn. Hunters were said to capture it by placing a young virgin in its path, because the unicorn approached her, rested its head in her lap, and fell asleep. Modern zoologists generally disbelieve in it, though rhinoceros, giraffe, or other bony protuberances may have helped inspire the story.
