political enfranchisement in Dr. Elsie Inglis

Lady Frances Balfour · 1918 · the work’s page · read it where political enfranchisement first appears

Abstract written by a model

Political enfranchisement is the common cause won by the generation’s suffrage movement, centered on ending women’s exclusion on the basis of sex through the Representation of the People’s Bill. Initially widely treated as a crank cause, it became a major question of democratic citizenship and national politics after the 1906 Liberal victory and elections. Organised pressure on Parliament sought women’s free rights of citizenship. Dr. Inglis placed enfranchisement above party organisation and left the Liberal Party when independent action better served women’s wider political freedom. During the war, women’s emancipation was connected to women replacing men who were fighting and dying; this challenged their treatment as irresponsible and was presented as opening a new era for women.
