the Guard in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912

The Guard, a servant of Creon assigned to Polyneices’ corpse, reports that someone has performed burial rites and fears punishment. After guards uncover and watch the body, he sees Antigone scatter dust and pour rites, arrests her, and brings her to Creon, relieved to escape anger despite grieving her disaster.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ANTIGONE

The Watchman reports that Polyneices’ corpse has been covered with dust and given the proper ritual, though no burial has been completed. He denies committing or witnessing the act and fears Creon’s punishment.

[Enter GUARD bringing ANTIGONE]

The Guard brings Antigone to Creon after finding her burying the corpse. He recounts how the guards uncovered the body, watched through the night, saw Antigone scatter dust and pour burial rites, and then seized her. He is relieved to have escaped Creon’s anger but says that bringing disaster to a friend is grievous.
