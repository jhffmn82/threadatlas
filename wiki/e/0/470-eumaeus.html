Eumaeus in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Eumaeus is Ulysses’s swineherd, a thrifty household servant who lives at the swineherd’s station beside the pig yards, manages and guards the pigs with dogs and herdsmen, and has enough to eat and drink. He is especially devoted to Ulysses, Penelope, and Telemachus. Though he believes Ulysses has died or disappeared, he mourns him, hopes for his return, defends his name, and worries for Telemachus. Born on Syra, Eumaeus was abducted from his father’s household by Phoenicians as a child, brought to Ithaca, and sold to Laertes. He was raised with Ctimene in Laertes’s household. During Ulysses’s absence he purchased Mesaulius. When the disguised Ulysses comes to his hut, Eumaeus receives him generously despite doubting his story. Out of respect for Jove and hospitality, he gives the stranger food, drink, supper, a bed, and a cloak; sacrifices a boar for the stranger and the gods; and continues guarding the pigs through a storm. He advises the stranger not to confront the suitors prematurely and recounts his own past. Eumaeus joyfully receives Telemachus, feeds him, and secretly tells Penelope of his return. He later guides Ulysses toward the city, supplies him with a stick, rebukes Melanthius for insulting him, brings him into the house, and defends him against Antinous. He reports what he sees, returns repeatedly to the pigs, and prays for Ulysses’s homecoming. Moved to tears by Ulysses’s bow, he declares he would fight for his master. After recognizing Ulysses by his scar, he carries the bow to him, helps secure the household, arms himself with Telemachus and Ulysses, and fights against the suitors, helping bind Melanthius, killing Elatus and Polybus, and wounding Ctesippus.

BOOK XIII

The swineherd is in charge of Ulysses’ pigs and is described as well affected toward Ulysses and devoted to Penelope and his son. Minerva directs the disguised Ulysses to stay with him near the rock called Raven by the fountain Arethusa.

BOOK XIV

Eumaeus is described as Ulysses’s most thrifty servant and as a loyal swineherd who deeply mourns his master. He welcomes the disguised Ulysses, gives him food and drink, and explains the condition of Ulysses’s household. He believes that Ulysses is dead but wishes that he could return, and he worries about Telemachus’s safety.

Eumaeus is disturbed by Ulysses’ plan to approach the suitors and advises him to remain at the station until Telemachus returns. He recounts that he was born on Syra, was carried away by Phoenicians, and was sold to Laertes.

Eumaeus joyfully welcomes Telemachus at the station and provides food and a place to sit. At Telemachus's instruction, he goes secretly to tell Penelope that Telemachus has returned.

Eumaeus privately tells Penelope that Telemachus has returned and then goes back to his pigs. He later reports to Telemachus and Ulysses that he saw an armed ship enter the harbour, without knowing for certain that it belonged to the suitors.

Eumaeus obeys Telemachus by taking Ulysses toward the city, giving him a stick for the rough road. He rebukes Melanthius for insulting Ulysses and advises Ulysses about entering the house.

Eumaeus brings Ulysses into the house and gives him the food sent by Telemachus. He defends his decision to bring the stranger against Antinous's criticism and tells Penelope about the stranger's supposed history and his claims concerning Ulysses. He later returns to the pigs.

BOOK XX

Eumaeus brings three pigs and asks Ulysses whether the suitors are treating him better. He prays that Ulysses will return home and shares Philoetius's loyalty to his absent master.

BOOK XXI

Penelope orders Eumaeus to place the bow and the pieces of iron before the suitors. He weeps as he carries them, moved by the sight of his master’s bow.

Eumaeus declares that he would fight for Ulysses if Ulysses returned, and he recognizes Ulysses when shown the scar. He carries the bow to Ulysses after Telemachus orders him to do so and instructs Euryclea to keep the women in their apartments.

BOOK XXII

Eumaeus helps Telemachus and Ulysses arm themselves and fights beside them against the suitors. He helps capture and bind Melanthius, kills Elatus and Polybus, wounds Ctesippus, and taunts Ctesippus before he dies.
