the Trojan assault on the Achaean ships in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

The Trojan assault is an attack on the Achaean ships and tents, led by Hector after the Achaean wall and gates are broken. With Apollo before him and later Jove’s encouragement, Hector brings the Trojans forward in ordered ranks toward the ships, intending to set them on fire. The attack puts the Achaeans in great danger: they are hard pressed, killed, and driven into confusion, while Nestor fears the ships will burn without help. The Achaeans retreat to the ships, arm themselves with long pikes, and resist at close quarters. Ajax and other defenders initially stand firm, checking Hector’s testing of their lines, and fighting continues around the ships’ sterns. Ultimately the assault reaches the ships, forces Ajax to yield ground, and culminates in Hector seizing a ship’s stern and calling for fire, though the Achaeans continue to resist.
