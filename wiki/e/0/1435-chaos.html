Chaos

primordial deity · 3 works · 10 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: a rude and undigested mass, an inert weight, pre-existent to the Earth in its present shape, the discordant atoms of things not harmonizing
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Chaos is the primeval primordial deity and first being, bringing forth Erebus and black Night.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Chaos is the original, unformed state of the universe from which the ordered world is formed.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Chaos is a rough heap of inert weight and discordant beginnings, described as the origin from which Earth and Heaven sprang in one account.

Chaos in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Chaos is the original state of the universe: pre-existent matter, a rude and undigested mass in which sea, earth, heaven, stars, and discordant atoms are heaped together without form or order. At the beginning of nature, God, the Deity, and bounteous Nature end its discord by separating, arranging, and dividing it into four Elements, from which the ordered world is formed.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

front matter

Chaos is divided by the Deity into four Elements at the beginning of Book I.

opening

Chaos is the name men give to the original state of the universe, in which the sea, the earth, and the heaven form a rude, undigested mass. Its discord is ended when God and bounteous Nature separate and arrange its elements.

notes .. notes

Chaos is described as pre-existent matter without form or order, from which the ordered world was formed. The ancient philosophers believed that a powerful cause arranged its elements according to their qualities.
