Abstract written by a model

Sparse retrieval methods use sparse representations and are lightweight, simple, and efficient, but semantic matching suffers when relevant passages lack lexical overlap. Existing OpenQA methods rely solely on question information. GAR enriches queries with information extracted from PLMs before retrieval while retaining exact lexical matching, enabling comparable or better performance than dense representations of original queries. Sparse representations can also be combined with dense representations for better results.

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Introduction

Sparse retrieval methods use sparse representations and are lightweight and efficient, but they do not perform semantic matching well when relevant passages lack lexical overlap. With GAR, sparse retrieval can handle semantics before retrieval by enriching queries while retaining exact lexical matching.

Related Work

Existing sparse retrieval methods for OpenQA rely solely on question information. GAR extends them with information extracted from PLMs and helps them achieve comparable or better performance than dense methods while retaining the simplicity and efficiency of sparse representations.

Conclusion .. Future Extensions

Sparse representations used by Generation-Augmented Retrieval perform similarly to or better than state-of-the-art methods based on dense representations of the original queries. They can also be combined with dense representations to produce better results.
