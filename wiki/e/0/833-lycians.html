Lycians in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

The Lycians are a warrior people from Xanthus, situated in the Trojan camp and fighting for Troy. Led chiefly by Sarpedon, with Glaucus and Hector also guiding them, they suffer heavy losses against Ulysses, defend the wall beside Hector, and follow him through a breach. They flee, regroup at Sarpedon’s rebuke, and fight to defend him after his death, but retreat when they see their king dead.

The Lycians fight for Troy under Sarpedon. Ulysses kills several of them after Minerva directs him against their main body, and Hector intervenes when their losses become severe.

The Lycians draw closer around Hector after he rebukes them and fight the Argives for possession of the wall. They cannot initially break through the wall or force their way to the ships, but they follow Hector into the breach.

The Lycians initially flee when Patroclus overcomes Sarpedon, but Sarpedon rebukes them and calls them to fight. After Sarpedon’s death, Glaucus rallies them to defend his body, though they later retreat when they see their king dead.
