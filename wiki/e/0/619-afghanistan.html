Abstract written by a model

In Vestiges of the Mayas, or, Facts tending to prove that Communications and Intimate Relations must have existed, in very remote times, between the inhabitants of Mayab and those of Asia and Africa, Afghanistan is described as containing Maya tribes and place-names, as a destination reached by some Maya tribes, and as home to descendants living near the Kabul River whose names, physical types, and warlike habits are said to resemble those of the Mayas. In A Study in Scarlet, Afghanistan is where Dr. Watson served during the second Afghan war and was wounded at the battle of Maiwand; it is also the place from which Sherlock Holmes identifies that Watson had recently come.

Afghanistan in Vestiges of the Mayas, or, Facts tending to prove that Communications and Intimate Relations must have existed, in very remote times, between the inhabitants of Mayab and those of Asia and Africa

Augustus Le Plongeon · 1881 · the work’s page · read it where Afghanistan first appears

Abstract written by a model

Afghanistan is described as still containing Maya tribes and place-names that the text interprets through the Maya language. The names of the Kabul River, valleys, clans, and the Khiber Pass are presented as having possible Maya meanings, and Afghan physical types are compared with figures at Chichen-Itza. Afghanistan is presented as a destination reached by some Maya tribes after leaving a homeland south of Hindostan. The text claims that their descendants still lived near the river Kabul and that some village and tribal names, physical types, and warlike habits resembled those attributed to the Mayas.

Afghanistan in A Study in Scarlet

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1886 · the work’s page · read it where Afghanistan first appears

Abstract written by a model

Afghanistan is where Dr. Watson served during the second Afghan war and was wounded at the battle of Maiwand. Holmes recognizes that Watson has been there, and Watson later reflects that he has experienced enough noise and excitement in Afghanistan to last him a lifetime. Afghanistan is identified by Sherlock Holmes as the place from which Dr. John Watson had recently come. Holmes reaches this conclusion by combining observations about Watson’s medical and military appearance, tropical complexion, illness, hardship, and injured left arm.
