Iron Age

mythic age · 2 works · 7 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: fifth generation, iron, last {Age}, men who are upon the bounteous earth, race of iron
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the Iron Age is the fifth and present generation of men, marked by unending labor, sorrow, wrongdoing, violence, conflict, and dishonor, ending when Zeus destroys it after corruption becomes complete.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the Iron Age is the last age, succeeding the Brazen Age and still continuing, marking humanity’s degeneration into wickedness and impiety, with vice and every species of crime.

Iron Age in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The Iron Age is the last age, succeeding the Brazen Age and still continuing. It marks humanity’s degeneration into wickedness and impiety, with vice and every species of crime: fraud, deceit, treachery, violence, greed, warfare, murder, and collapsed family loyalty.
