Penelope's web

object · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Penelope's woven pall, a great tambour-frame, a pall, an enormous piece of fine needlework, her work, my great web, the funeral canopy of Laertes, the robe
  • In The Odyssey, Penelope's web is a burial pall for Laertes, woven by day and secretly unpicked by night for three years until maids reveal the deception and suitors force its completion.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Penelope's web is the robe she claimed to be preparing for the funeral canopy of Laertes, woven by day and unraveled at night as a proverb for work continually done but never finished.

BOOK XIX

Penelope set up a large tambour-frame and worked on a burial cloth for Laertes during the day. For three years she secretly unpicked the day's work at night, until her maids betrayed the deception and the suitors forced her to finish the cloth.

Penelope's web in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

is_a proverbial expression [for anything perpetually doing but never done] Penelope's web was the robe she claimed to be preparing for the funeral canopy of Laertes. She wove it during the day and unraveled it at night, making it a proverb for work that is continually done but never finished.
