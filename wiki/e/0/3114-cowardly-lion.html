  • In The Emerald City of Oz, the Cowardly Lion serves Princess Ozma as a forest beast and bodyguard, fights successfully despite fear, and is frightened by Aunt Em’s stare.
  • In Little Wizard Stories of Oz, the Cowardly Lion serves as one of Princess Ozma’s guardians, rejects cruelty, carries an injured baby’s mother home, and concludes that cowardice is better than wrongdoing.
  • In Tik-Tok of Oz, the Cowardly Lion rests in Ozma’s stable, welcomes Dorothy, accepts Hank as a friend, and offers help or advice.
  • In The Lost Princess of Oz, the Cowardly Lion joins the search for Ozma, leads the procession, shelters Toto, travels with the Woozy, and frightens enemies with his roar.
  • In The Magic of Oz, the Cowardly Lion protects Dorothy and the Wizard, is transformed into a Munchkin boy, helps seek the black bag, retrieves a raft, and carries the Magic Flower.
  • In Glinda of Oz, the Cowardly Lion guards Ozma, joins the rescue expedition, leads the travelers through the Great Gillikin Forest, and protects them from dangerous beasts.
  • In The Royal Book of Oz, the Cowardly Lion travels with Dorothy and Sir Hokus, is knighted, repeatedly acts courageously, recognizes the Scarecrow, and is blown toward the Emerald City.
  • In The Cowardly Lion of Oz, the Cowardly Lion seeks courage, protects Notta Bit More and Bob Up, rejects Mustafa’s plan, is turned to stone, and ends among friends in Oz.
  • In The Cowardly Lion of Oz, the Cowardly Lion is cut in half, seeks restoration by a wizard, is bound to Notta, is caught by Crunch, and travels toward Mudge to confront Mustafa.
  • In The Hungry Tiger of Oz, the Cowardly Lion lives in Emerald City, enjoys Betsy’s birthday party, eats cake, jokes, and starts a game of hide-and-seek.
  • In The Gnome King of Oz, the Cowardly Lion lives among Emerald City’s talking animals, befriends the Hungry Tiger, forgives him after their quarrel, seeks the Patchwork Girl, and helps prepare a party.

Cowardly Lion in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04

Cowardly Lion is a lion as large as a small horse who is regarded as the King of Beasts but is deeply unhappy because he believes he is cowardly and lacks courage. Though afraid of everything, especially Kalidahs, he joins the little company traveling to Oz because he seeks courage. He becomes Dorothy’s friend and comrade, later also becoming friends with Toto. On the journey, fear repeatedly accompanies useful and brave acts: he carries his companions over a ditch, faces the Kalidahs with a roar, helps pull a raft, guards Dorothy, and travels onward despite terror. The poppy flowers put him to sleep, and field mice rescue him from the poisonous bed. At the Emerald City, Oz demands proof that the Wicked Witch is dead before granting his request. The Lion retreats from Oz’s fiery appearance in fear, but continues with Dorothy. He frightens away attacking Winkies, is captured by Winged Monkeys, confined in the Witch’s fenced yard, and refuses to be harnessed like a horse. Dorothy feeds him secretly and frees him after the Witch is melted. The Winkies give him a golden collar. When he returns to Oz, he roars loudly enough to expose Oz’s hidden screen. Oz tells him that facing danger while afraid already shows courage, then gives him a potion that the Lion drinks as courage. Feeling courageous, he leaves city life with Dorothy because he loves woods and country and wants to protect her. He helps the travelers, attacks the Hammer-Heads though he is knocked down, and longs to return to the forest. In the forest, he defeats the great spider to protect the animals, is recognized as braver and larger than other lions, and becomes King of the Forest, ruling the forest animals in his own kingdom after Dorothy is safely on her way to Kansas.

Narrative, section by section

17 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter VI

The Cowardly Lion attacks the group but admits that he is a coward after Dorothy confronts him. He explains that he frightens animals by roaring but becomes afraid whenever there is danger. He joins the journey to ask Oz for courage and later becomes good friends with Toto.

Chapter VII

The Cowardly Lion offers to kill a deer for breakfast, then goes into the forest to find his own supper. He carries the Scarecrow, Dorothy, and the Tin Woodman across the first ditch and faces the Kalidahs during their attack. Although he is terrified, he gives a loud roar and is ready to fight before the tree bridge is cut away.

Chapter VIII

The Cowardly Lion helps pull the raft to shore by swimming while the Tin Woodman holds his tail. He runs ahead through the poppy field but eventually falls asleep from the flowers' odor and is too heavy for the others to carry.

Chapter IX

The Cowardly Lion is asleep in the poppy bed and is in danger from the poisonous scent of the flowers. The field mice pull him out on a truck to the green fields, where he can breathe fresh air, and he remains asleep afterward.

Chapter X

The Cowardly Lion awakens safely after the field mice rescue him from the poppies. He travels with Dorothy and the others, guards Dorothy’s room overnight, and is fitted with locked green spectacles at the Emerald City gate.

Chapter XI

The Cowardly Lion asks Oz for courage, but Oz requires proof that the Wicked Witch of the West is dead before granting it. After seeing Oz as an intensely hot Ball of Fire, the Lion retreats in fear and tells his friends that they must seek out and destroy the Witch. He agrees to go with them despite being too cowardly to kill her.

Chapter XII

The Cowardly Lion travels with Dorothy and helps frighten away the Winkies sent to attack them. The Winged Monkeys tie him with rope and take him to the Witch’s castle, where he is kept in a fenced yard. Dorothy secretly brings him food each night, and he refuses to be harnessed like a horse.

Chapter XIII

The Cowardly Lion is freed from prison after the Wicked Witch is melted. He helps Dorothy rescue the Tin Woodman and the Scarecrow, then prepares to return to Oz to obtain his courage. The Winkies give him a golden collar.

Chapter XIV

The Cowardly Lion says that he lacks the courage to keep walking without reaching anywhere. He is carried by the Winged Monkeys to the Emerald City and calls the ride a quick way out of their troubles.

Chapter XV

The Cowardly Lion roars loudly to frighten Oz, accidentally knocking over the screen that hides him. He asks for courage, and Oz explains that he already possesses courage because he faces danger while afraid, but agrees to give him the kind of courage he wants.

Chapter XVI

The Cowardly Lion goes to Oz to receive courage. Oz pours liquid from a green bottle into a dish and tells the Lion to drink it, explaining that it will be courage once it is inside him. The Lion drinks it and returns joyfully, feeling full of courage.

Chapter XVIII

The Lion is content to know that he is as brave as any beast, if not braver. He decides to leave the Emerald City and travel with Dorothy because he longs for the woods and country and wants to protect her.

Chapter XIX

The Lion is delighted to leave city life and return to the country, where he hopes to show the other beasts how courageous he has become. He helps recognize that the trees are fighting them and stopping their journey.

Chapter XX

The Lion sleeps before climbing the wall and later helps the group reach the far wall. When he jumps onto it, his tail upsets a china church and smashes it.

Chapter XXI

The Lion enjoys the forest and is not afraid when the animals gather there. He agrees to fight their enemy, defeats the great spider, and is made King of the Forest.

Chapter XXII

The Lion attacks the Hammer-Heads after they laugh at the Scarecrow, but a shooting head knocks him down the hill. He concludes that no one can withstand the Hammer-Heads by fighting them.

Chapter XXIII

The Lion wishes to return to the forest beyond the hill of the Hammer-Heads, where the beasts have made him their King. Glinda promises that the Winged Monkeys will carry him there.
