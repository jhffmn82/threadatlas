violence against the mistress

event · 2 works · 1 quoted fact · 2 connections
Also called: blows, caedis, crime, criminality, frenzy has raised my rash arms against my mistress, hurt, injured, laesa, offence, saevus, struck, the beating of the mistress
  • In The Amores, or Amours, the beating of the mistress is presented as an act of frenzy and criminal violence for which the speaker believes he deserves chains and punishment; he considers it worse than violence against enemies or divine beings because he injured the woman he claimed to love.
  • In Heroides and Amores, violence against the mistress is committed by the lover in madness; after threatening her and tearing her gown, he injures Corinna, then recognizes his crime from her fear and tears.

violence against the mistress in Heroides and Amores

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

VI

The lover tears the mistress's hair from her brow and marks her free-born cheeks with his nails after shouting threats and pulling apart her gown. She becomes bloodless and trembles, and her tears make the lover realize that he has committed a crime.
