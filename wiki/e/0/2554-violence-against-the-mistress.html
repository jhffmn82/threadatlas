violence against the mistress

Also called: blows, caedis, crime, criminality, frenzy has raised my rash arms against my mistress, hurt, injured, laesa, offence, saevus, struck, the beating of the mistress

Abstract written by a model

In Heroides and Amores, violence against the mistress is committed by the lover in madness; after threatening her and tearing her gown, he injures Corinna, then recognizes his crime from her fear and tears. In The Amores, or Amours, the beating of the mistress is presented as an act of frenzy and criminal violence for which the speaker believes he deserves chains and punishment; he considers it worse than violence against enemies or divine beings because he injured the woman he claimed to love.

VI

The lover tears the mistress's hair from her brow and marks her free-born cheeks with his nails after shouting threats and pulling apart her gown. She becomes bloodless and trembles, and her tears make the lover realize that he has committed a crime.
