Abstract written by a model

In The Odyssey, Odysseus's twelve ships carry Ulysses and his men from Troy, survive a hurricane after shelter and repair, escape the Lotus-eaters, and reach a wooded, fertile island, where one ship carries twelve companions into Polyphemus's country while the others remain nearby. In The Library, the ships of Odysseus initially carry him with several ships, leave the others at a neighboring island, and later lose the remaining ships to the Laestrygones, leaving one to reach the Aeaean isle.

Abstract written by a model

Odysseus’s twelve ships carry Ulysses and his men from Troy. A hurricane tears their sails, but after shelter and repair they sail again and escape the Lotus-eaters. They later beach on a wooded, fertile island; Odysseus’s ship carries twelve companions into Polyphemus’s country while the others remain nearby.

BOOK IX

The ships carry Ulysses and his men from Troy to Ismarus and onward. They are battered by a hurricane that tears their sails, forcing the crews to row toward land; after sheltering for two days and nights, they are repaired for sailing, and the men later board them to escape the Lotus-eaters.
