Abstract written by a model

GRAG is a strategy evaluated for graph multi-hop reasoning, where LLMs face challenges from complex graph structures despite strengths in text embedding and contextual reasoning. It retrieves topology-preserving subgraphs rather than text-matched individual entities, producing particularly strong improvements over LLM and RAG-based LLM baselines for detailed reasoning on textual graphs.

graph multi-hop reasoning tasks in GRAG: Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Yuntong Hu, Zhihan Lei, Zheng Zhang, Bo Pan, Chen Ling, Liang Zhao · 2025-07-12 · the work’s page · read it where graph multi-hop reasoning tasks first appears

Abstract written by a model

Graph multi-hop reasoning tasks are empirical benchmarks and a reasoning requirement used to evaluate Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation on textual graphs. Across experiments including WebQSP, they compare GRAG with RAG retrievers and Large Language Model baselines. GRAG significantly outperforms these methods; even a frozen Large Language Model with GRAG exceeds a fine-tuned Large Language Model. The results emphasize retrieving and encoding graph structure over text retrieval or fine-tuning alone.
