Pytheas in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

Pytheas, son of Lampon and brother of Phylakidas of Aigina, is a strong-limbed pankratiast. Trained by Menander of Athens, he won the boys’ pankration crown at Nemea before showing the first down of manhood, becoming a successor of his mother’s brother Euthymenes. His family’s victories include Nemean and Isthmian successes. He later aided Phylakidas in a struggle, guiding his blows to defeat a cunning opponent.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Nemean Ode V

Pytheas, son of Lampon, won the pankratiast's crown at Nemea while still too young to show the first down of manhood. He is a kinsman and successor of Euthymenes, and his trainer was Menander of Athens.

Isthmian Ode IV

Pytheas is identified as Phylakidas’s brother and is also praised as a pankratiast. He guided Phylakidas’s blows in the struggle and helped him defeat a cunning opponent.

Isthmian Ode V

Pytheas is named with Phylakidas and Euthymenes as part of the family whose victories the poet celebrates. He is also identified as the athlete associated with the earlier Nemean victory.
