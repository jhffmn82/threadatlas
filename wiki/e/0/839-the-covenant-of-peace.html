the covenant of peace in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

The covenant of peace is an agreement between Trojans and Achaeans: they lay down arms, accept the victor's claim to Helen and her wealth, with Achaeans returning home and Trojans remaining in Troy. Leaders swear and seal it with sacrifices, but Venus removes Alexandrus and Pandarus attacks Menelaus, violating it.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK III.

The covenant requires the Trojans and Achaeans to lay down their arms and accept the victor's claim to Helen and her wealth. Priam, Agamemnon, and the assembled leaders swear that the Achaeans will return home and the Trojans will remain in Troy, but the covenant is not fulfilled because Venus removes Alexandrus from the fight.

BOOK IV.

The oaths, sacrifices, drink-offerings, and gestures of fellowship were intended to preserve the agreement between the Trojans and Achaeans. Minerva causes Pandarus to violate them by attacking Menelaus.
