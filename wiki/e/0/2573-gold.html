  • In The Amores, or Amours, gold is treated as a potent and valuable substance that rewards Jupiter’s ravished damsel and determines honors and access to the Senate-house.
  • In Heroides and Amores, gold is described as a powerful price for Danae’s seduction and as something humans dig from the earth instead of cultivating agricultural abundance.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, gold is naturally mined, washed, purified, and made into ornaments and temple scatterings.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, gold is the precious metal into which everything Midas touches is transformed, including the Pactolus’s sands and surrounding fields.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, gold is demanded by Aglauros as payment for helping Mercury reach Herse and is considered by Minerva as a possible source of Aglauros’s enrichment.
  • In Bulfinch’s Mythology: The Age of Fable, gold is produced by Midas’s touch and later enters the river Pactolus, turning its sands to gold. (Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable)
  • In The Sea Fairies, gold is abundant in Zog’s castle and the ocean, but its softness makes it unsuitable for a durable sword.

gold in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

Gold, perishable yet treated as supremely potent and more valuable than genius, is dug from earth instead of corn, rewards a ravished damsel in Jupiter’s example, and determines honors and Senate-house access.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ELEGY VIII.

Gold is treated as more powerful than anything else and as more valuable than genius. Jupiter is said to have used gold as the reward for a ravished damsel, and in the present age people dig solid gold from the earth instead of corn. Gold also helps determine social honors and access to the Senate-house.

gold in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Gold is a material found naturally pure and unrefined in Arabian hollows, and dug from white shining veins in black soil or collected from Gaulish rivers. It is crushed and washed so its weight retains it while earth runs off, then purified with sponges and heat in sealed earthen pots into slightly lighter pure gold. Mining and refining bring toil and vexation; gold becomes ornaments and temple scatterings.

gold in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Gold was produced whenever Midas touched something while under Bacchus's gift. After Midas washed in the river Pactolus, the power passed into the water and the river sands became gold.

Narrative, section by section
