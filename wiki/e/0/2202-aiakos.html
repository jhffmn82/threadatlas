Aiakos

person · 1 work · 9 quoted facts · 8 connections
Also called: Hero, portals of Aiakos, the man whom Leto's son and far-ruling Poseidon

Abstract written by a model

Aiakos is a son of Zeus and Aigina, born of a mortal mother, who becomes king of Oinone and ruler of fortunate cities. Regarded as the best of men, mighty in hand and counsel, he even decides strife among divinities. Apollo and Poseidon call him to help build Troy’s wall; after dragons attack it, Apollo reads the surviving dragon as foretelling Troy’s capture through Aiakos’ descendants, including Telamon and Neoptolemos. Aiakos is husband of Endais, father of Phokos and Telamon, and founding ancestor of the Aiakids, his race, and the sons who rule and win glory in Aigina. His lineage’s ancient valour and broad fame are repeatedly praised. His altar stands on Aigina, where descendants pray for the city, and he is invoked with Zeus and Aigina as its protector; the speaker also approaches his holy knees as a suppliant.

Aiakos is in one work on this site, The Extant Odes of Pindar. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Aiakos in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Narrative, section by section written by a model

8 sections; each heading opens the text

VIII.

Aiakos was called by Apollo and Poseidon to help build the wall of Troy. After three fiery-eyed dragons attacked the new wall, Apollo interpreted the surviving dragon as a sign that Troy would be taken through Aiakos’ descendants, including Telamon and Neoptolemos.

VIII.

Aiakos is named among the heroic protectors associated with Aigina. The final prayer asks Aigina, together with Zeus and Aiakos, to guard the city.

Nemean Ode III

Aiakos is the ancestor whose race the poet asks the Muse to praise. His lineage is presented as a fitting subject because its ancient valour provides glory close to home.

Nemean Ode V

Aiakos is the grandsire whose altar stood on Aigina, where his descendants prayed for the city's prosperity and fame. He is the father of Phokos, whom Psamatheia bore, and the husband of Endais.

Nemean Ode VII

Aiakos is the ancestor of the sons who rule and gain glory in Aigina. Zeus begat him by a mortal mother, and he became ruler of cities in his own fortunate land.

Nemean Ode VIII

Aiakos was born from Zeus and Aigina and became king of Oinone. The speaker clasps Aiakos's holy knees as a suppliant for Aigina and its citizens.

Isthmian Ode V

Aiakos is identified as the father of Telamon and as the ancestor of the heroic line celebrated in the ode. His descendants are praised for their widespread fame.

Isthmian Ode VII

Aiakos was born to Zeus and Aigina and was regarded as the best of men upon the earth. Even among divinities he became a decider of strife, and his godlike sons and grandsons were foremost in battle.
