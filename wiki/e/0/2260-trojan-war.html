Trojan War in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

The Trojan War is a ten-year armed conflict and battle at Troy, fought continuously between the Trojans and the Achaeans, Argives, Danaans, and Achaean men. Its central aim is the taking of Troy and its towers, while the Trojans defend Ilium. It brings heroic glory and renown, but also slaughter, wounds, panic, grief, stripping of corpses, burial, and long travail. The Iliad covers only its final part, and The Fall of Troy recounts events leading toward Troy's capture. After Hector's death, Penthesileia and the Amazons initially strengthen the Trojan side. Achilles later dies, leaving Agamemnon fearful for the Achaeans and the ships threatened. Yet the conflict continues through repeated assaults around the ships, gates, walls, and city, with heavy losses on both sides, including Nireus and Machaon. Achilles' son joins the Danaans to win deeds worthy of his father, while battles repeatedly end only with night, weariness, burial, or burning of the dead. The Achaeans cannot take Troy by direct assault. Prophecy delays their success until Philoctetes returns and recovers; then they form for battle again. Zeus foresees Troy's destruction, though also later ruin for the victors. Finally, after prolonged failure, the Achaeans turn to the Wooden Horse stratagem as their means of ending the war and seeking Troy's capture.
