Nysa

place · 2 works · 8 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Nysa in Arabia, a mountain most high and richly grown with woods, plain of Nysa, sacred Nyse, this Nysa

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Nysa is a very high, richly wooded mountain where Dionysus is born and grows up under the Nymphs’ care. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Nysa is a town in Arabia the Happy where Osiris was said to be raised and to discover, drink, and teach the use of wine.

Nysa in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · about 699 BC · the work’s page · read it where Nysa first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nysa is a very high, richly wooded mountain in far-off Phoenice near the streams of Aegyptus. It has shrines, dells, and a sweet-smelling cave. Dionysus is truly born there and grows up in its cave under the Nymphs’ care.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE HOMERIC HYMNS / I. TO DIONYSUS 2501

Nysa is described as a very high mountain richly grown with woods. It is said to be far off in Phoenice, near the streams of Aegyptus, and is the place associated with Dionysus's true birth.

XXVI. TO DIONYSUS

Nysa contains the dells and a sweet-smelling cave where Dionysus grows up under the care of the Nymphs.

Nysa in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Nysa first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nysa is a town in Arabia the Happy near Egypt, between Phoenicia and the river Nile, where Osiris was said to be raised and to discover, drink, and teach the use of wine.
