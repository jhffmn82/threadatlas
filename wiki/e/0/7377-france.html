France in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860 · the work’s page · read it where France first appears

Abstract written by a model

France is a European power entering relations with Morocco shortly after England, sending flags to Moroccan ports in 1555 under Francis I and gaining influence while England occupied Tangier. It later seeks peace, is linked to protection of a Jewish former consul, and may aid Moroccan Jews. Its influence and intervention are presented as potentially awakening energies and reconstructing declining royal cities amid Fez hostilities.

France entered relations with the western Moors shortly after England and began sending its flag to Moroccan ports in 1555 under Francis I. France gained influence while England occupied Tangier, later sought a treaty of peace, and was associated with protection for a Jewish former consul who was decapitated by the Emperor.

CHAPTER V.

France is mentioned in connection with hostilities provoked by the fanatics of Fez and with the possibility of awakening the population’s energies. The text suggests that European intervention, including that of France, might help reconstruct the declining royal cities.
