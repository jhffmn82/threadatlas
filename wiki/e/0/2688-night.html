night

time of day · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: darkness, hours of the night, midnight, nocte, nox

Abstract written by a model

In Heroides and Amores, night encourages the lover toward lack of restraint with love and wine, overcoming his fear and enabling him to travel and keep watch at the door as dawn approaches. In The Amores, or Amours, night shelters the lover, gives him sleep and time with his mistress, and ends by making way for Aurora as Lucifer moves and a bird wakes mortals to work.

night in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where night first appears

Abstract written by a model

Night shelters the lover without shame, passing as he waits outside a door while the city falls silent and dew gathers. It gives the speaker sleep and time with his mistress, but ends by making way for Aurora as Lucifer moves and a bird wakes mortals to work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ELEGY VI.

The night passes while the lover waits outside the door, and the city becomes silent as the hours grow damp with dew. At its end, Lucifer begins moving his frost-covered wheels and a bird awakens mortals to their work.

ELEGY XIII.

Night gives the speaker sleep and time with his mistress, so he wishes it would not make way for Aurora. He imagines the horses of the night being told to run slowly, and recalls that Jupiter joined two nights together to fulfill his desires.
