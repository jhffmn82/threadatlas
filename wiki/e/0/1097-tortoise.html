tortoise

animal · 2 works · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections
Also called: comrade of the feast, creature, lovely in shape, mountain-tortoise, χελώνην
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the tortoise was treated by Hermes as an omen of good luck, and its shell became the body of the first lyre.
  • In The Library, the tortoise was found by Hermes browsing before the cave at Cyllene; its shell was used to make a lyre.

tortoise in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Hermes finds a tortoise feeding near Maia's dwelling and treats it as an omen of good luck. He carries it inside, removes its limbs and marrow, and transforms its shell into the body of the first lyre.
