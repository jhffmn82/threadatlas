grief for the dead and missing

topic · 1 work · 1 quoted fact · 1 connection
Also called: I often grieve, all this weeping, heavy loss, sorrow, those that are dead and gone

Abstract written by a model

Grief over Odysseus’s absence and Troy’s dead afflicts Penelope and Menelaus, expands into shared remembrance and weeping with Pisistratus and Telemachus, then Helen eases it.

BOOK IV

Menelaus grieves for those who died at Troy and especially for Odysseus, whose fate is unknown. Pisistratus remembers his dead brother Antilochus, while Telemachus, Helen, Menelaus, and Pisistratus all weep before Helen drugs the wine to ease their sorrow.
