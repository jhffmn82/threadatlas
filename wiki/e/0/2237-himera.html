Himera in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Himera is a Sicilian Greek city defended by Theron and later threatened by Carthaginian armies. After Amilcar's siege, Gelon's arrival helps defeat and drive off the attackers, making it a site of a Greek victory over Carthage. Its people later plot with Hiero against Theron, are punished, and receive Dorian settlers. After Selinus's destruction, Hannibal besieges, captures, plunders, burns, and razes Himera.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. U.

Himera was besieged by the Carthaginian army after Amilcar landed at Panormus and marched against it. Gelon's arrival revived the city's defenders, and the Carthaginians were ultimately defeated and driven from the area.

CHAP. VII.

The people of Himera, alienated by Thrasideus's harsh government, offered to surrender their city to Hiero and assist him against Theron. Hiero revealed their plot, and Theron put many of the traitors to death; he later settled Dorians in the largely depopulated city.

CHAP. VII.

Himera was besieged by Hannibal after the destruction of Selinus. Although its defenders initially repelled the Carthaginians and killed many of them, the city was eventually entered, plundered, burned, and razed to the ground.

Himera in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

Himera, a wide-ruling city in Sicily, seeks Fortune's safety, naturalizes Ergoteles, and sees its hot springs glorified by victories.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

XII.

Himera is prayed to be kept safe by Fortune. It naturalized Ergoteles as a citizen after he left Knosos, and its hot springs are glorified by his victories.
