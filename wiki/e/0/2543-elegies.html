Elegies in The Amores, or Amours

A three-book work preferred by Naso after two books were removed, Elegies resumes endearment and trifling after an earlier subject is abandoned. Its unwarlike, sportive verses are Naso’s poetic weapons against an obdurate door. At their extreme turning-place, he bids them farewell, saying another work will outlive him.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

AN EPIGRAM ON THE AMOURS.

The Amours is described as having been reduced from five books to three, with two books removed. The work has been preferred by its author in this shortened form, which requires less labour to read.

BOOK THE SECOND ELEGY I.

Naso resumes his language of endearment and trifling Elegies after abandoning his earlier subject. These Elegies serve as his own weapons and help him prevail over the obdurate door.

ELEGY X.

The Elegies have reached their extreme turning-place, and the poet says farewell to them. He describes them as unwarlike and sportive and says that another work will survive long after he is dead and gone.
