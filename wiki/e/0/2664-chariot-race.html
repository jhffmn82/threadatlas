chariot race

event · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: course, cursus, greatest sight of all, pompa, procession, race, spectacula
  • In The Amores, or Amours, the chariot race begins with a procession at Circus Maximus, starts from a starting barrier, pauses for a restart, and ends in a final contest in which the mistress’s favored competitor wins the palm of victory.
  • In Heroides and Amores, the chariot race begins when the praetor releases four-horse teams from equally placed starting barriers; the poet supports the woman’s favored charioteer, urges a restart when he loses ground, and sees him win after the second start, while his own desired prize remains to be won.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ELEGY II.

The chariot race includes a procession, a first start, a call to stop and restart, and a final contest across the Circus. The mistress's favored competitor wins, while the speaker treats the victory as hers and continues seeking his own romantic victory.
