Charybdis

whirlpool · 5 works · 12 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: dread Charybdis, gulf, sea monster, terrible Charybdis, that Charybdis, the sucking whirlpool, the whirlpool, whirlpool, Χάρυβδις, Χαρύβδεως

Abstract written by a model

In The Odyssey, Charybdis is a sucking whirlpool beneath a fig tree opposite Scylla that swallows and vomits seawater, draws in Ulysses’s raft, and from which he escapes by clinging to the tree. In The Argonautica, Charybdis is a horribly belching, ceaselessly spouting whirlpool feared capable of swallowing the heroes, though Argo passes safely by. In The Aeneid, Charybdis is a whirlpool between Sicily and Italy that sucks in the tides and spouts them upward as the Trojan ships pass through violent waves near her. In The Library, Charybdis is a sea monster dwelling in a cliff that draws up and releases water three times daily and swallows Ulysses’s ship’s mast after destroying the ship. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Charybdis is a gulf guarding a strait with Scylla that thrice daily draws in and disgorges the sea, engulfing vessels caught during intake.

Charybdis in The Odyssey

Homer · about 632 to 599 BC · the work’s page · read it where Charybdis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Charybdis is a sucking whirlpool beneath a large fig tree on a lower rock, opposite Scylla in the Straits of Scylla and Charybdis. Three times a day she sucks down and vomits forth salt water like a boiling cauldron, making a deafening whirlpool. Ships caught in her suction cannot be saved; after a shipwreck, she draws Ulysses’s raft, but he escapes by clinging to the fig tree and returning to it.
