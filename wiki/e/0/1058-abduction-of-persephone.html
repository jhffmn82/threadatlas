Also called: Persephone’s abduction, bearing her away, bore me away, carried her off secretly, carried me off, he rapt me away, how he rapt you away, rape of Persephone, rapt away, seized her, the abduction of Persephone, took her loudly crying, ἥρπασεν αὐτὴν

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the abduction of Persephone is described as Hades seizing Persephone with Zeus’s permission and carrying her to the underworld as she cries for her father. In The Library, the abduction of Persephone is performed by Pluto with Zeus’s help, as Pluto secretly carries Persephone off.

abduction of Persephone in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · about 699 BC · the work’s page · read it where abduction of Persephone first appears

Abstract written by a model

The abduction of Persephone is an event in which, as Persephone gathers flowers with other maidens, the earth opens beneath her. With Zeus’s permission and plan, Hades emerges in a golden chariot, seizes her against her will, and carries her away to the underworld as she cries for her father.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

II. TO DEMETER

Persephone is taken by Hades when the earth opens beneath her as she gathers flowers. Zeus has given her to Hades, and Hades carries her away unwillingly while she cries for her father.

(ll. 301-320) Now when they had finished building and had drawn back

Persephone says that while she and other maidens were gathering flowers, the earth opened and Hades sprang forth in his golden chariot to carry her away beneath the earth. She describes the abduction as having been carried out through the deep plan of Zeus.
