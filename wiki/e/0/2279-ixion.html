Ixion in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Ixion, Antion’s son, marries Dia but kills her father after withholding a promised dowry; purified by Jupiter, he pursues Juno, fathers Centaurs through a cloud, then is wheel-bound and tortured.

Ixion married Dia after promising her father Hesioneus a dowry, but failed to fulfill the promise. He killed Hesioneus in a fiery furnace, was purified by Jupiter, pursued Juno, and fathered the Centaurs through a cloud shaped like her; he was later bound to a wheel and tortured.

Ixion in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

Ixion, a mythical sinner, prospers among the Kronidai, then sheds kindred blood and lusts after Hera, lying with her cloud phantom. Punished by the gods, he is bound without escape to Ixion’s winged wheel, becoming a proclaimer that service merits fair recompense.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

II.

Ixion is punished for shedding the blood of his kindred and attempting Hera, the wife of Zeus. After enjoying prosperity among the Kronidai, he lusts after Hera, lies with a cloud made in her form, and becomes an example proclaiming that service should be repaid with a fair reward.

Ixion, father of Pirithous, falls in love with Hera and attempts to force or violate her. Zeus tests and deludes him with a cloud resembling Hera; Ixion boasts of enjoying her favours, then is punished, bound to a wheel and whirled through the air by winds.

Ixion in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Ixion, associated with Juno, was said to father Centaurus by a cloud and Pirithoüs by Dia; tradition links his Centaurs to the beginning of riding among Thessalians during his reign.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

EXPLANATION.

Ixion was said to be the father of Centaurus and Pirithoüs, the former by a cloud and the latter by Dia. The account suggests that the association of the Centaurs with Ixion may reflect the beginning of riding among the Thessalians during his reign.
