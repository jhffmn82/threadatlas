Titans

Also called: Titan, Titan gods, Titan gods and as many as sprang from Cronos, Titanes, Tiuns, elder gods, lordly Titans, the Titan race, the Titans, Τιτάνων, Τιτᾶνας

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the Titans are a divine race born to Earth and Heaven who revolt under Cronos, war against Zeus and the gods, and are defeated, bound, and hidden in Tartarus. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Titans are a group of eighteen children of Uranus and Titea who dwelt near Gnossus, were credited with useful inventions, warred on Ammon, attacked Dionysius, and were later destroyed. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the Titans are inhabitants of heaven, described in notes as possibly arising from traditions about the fall of the angels. In The Library, the Titans are children of Sky and Earth who attack Sky, free their brothers from Tartarus, give Cronus rule, and later fight Zeus, lose, and are imprisoned in Tartarus. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Titan is identified as the divine race to which Prometheus belongs; Prometheus favors mankind, gives it knowledge, arts, and protection, and is bound to a rock on Mount Caucasus for aiding humanity and defying Jupiter. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Titans are children of Earth and Heaven who ruled before Jove and his brothers overthrew them, with some imprisoned in Tartarus and Atlas condemned to bear the heavens.

Titans in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · about 699 BC · the work’s page · read it where Titans first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Titans are a divine race born to Earth and Heaven. Oppressed by Heaven, they revolt at Earth’s instigation under Cronos, who attacks Heaven; Heaven calls them Titans in reproach. They later wage a ten-year, initially balanced war against Zeus and the gods, aided by Aegaeon according to Eumelus, until Zeus releases mighty allies. Defeated and driven from heaven, they are bound and hidden beneath the earth in Tartarus, where they remain confined with Cronos and tremble during Zeus’s conflict with Typhoeus.

Titans in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Titans first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Titans are a group said to be the eighteen children of Uranus and Titea, named for their mother, though their number and parentage were disputed. Contemporary with the Curetes, they dwelt near Gnossus in Crete and included Saturn, Hyperion, Rhea, Themis, and Mnemosyne, and were credited with useful inventions. Under Saturn they warred on Ammon, attacked Dionysius, were defeated and pardoned into his service, and were later destroyed.

Abstract written by a model

The Titans, children of Sky and Earth, include Ocean, Coeus, Hyperion, Crius, Iapetus, and youngest Cronus. Urged by Earth, all but Ocean attack Sky, free brothers from Tartarus, and give Cronus rule. They later fight Zeus for ten years, lose, and are imprisoned in Tartarus.

Titans in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Titans first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Titans are children of Earth and Heaven, springing from Chaos through them. Including Saturn, Rhea, Oceanus, Hyperion, Iapetus, Ophion, Themis, Mnemosyne, and Eurynome, they ruled the sea before Jove and his brothers overthrew them; some were imprisoned in Tartarus and Atlas was condemned to bear the heavens.
