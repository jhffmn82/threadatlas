Abstract written by a model

In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Tenedos is a city sustained by Aristagoras and fellow senators honoring Hestia and Zeus through sacrifices, music, songs, and rites. In The Aeneid, Tenedos is an island in sight of Troy that conceals the Greek fleet until it returns to Troy at night after deceiving the Trojans. In The Library, Tenedos, formerly called Leucophrys and named after Tenes, is an island or offshore anchorage off the Trojan coast where Tenes rules and resists the arriving Greeks until Achilles kills him, and where later Greeks touch, lie offshore, return to Troy, and from which Neoptolemus departs after two days. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Leucophrys is an island that safely receives Tennes and Hemithea after they are sent to sea in a chest, then is renamed Tenedos after Tennes. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Tenedos, formerly Leucophrys, is named from Tennes and inhabited by its people, who are eventually joined to Alexandria because they are weak. In The Fall of Troy, Tenedos is an island stronghold off the Trojan coast where the Achaean fleet waits for a signal-torch while hidden warriors remain near Troy.
