Washington is a city, the seat of wartime government activity, containing the U.S. Naval Observatory. During the Civil War it is turbulent, unhealthy, expensive, crowded with sick and wounded soldiers, and threatened by disease, Confederates, and hostility; later letters depict Angeline and Asaph Hall's household life, weather, flowers, and correspondence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER XIII.

Washington was described as turbulent, unhealthy, expensive, and crowded with sick and wounded soldiers during the Civil War. The city faced disease outbreaks, Confederate threats, and local hostility toward Union soldiers, while also hosting figures such as Grant, Lincoln, Stanton, and John Pierpont.

------------------------------------------------------------------------ CHAPTER XVI.

Washington, D.C., is where Angeline and Asaph Hall live during several of the letters. Angeline describes the spring weather and flowers there, performs household work, receives and sends family correspondence, and writes from addresses on N Street.
