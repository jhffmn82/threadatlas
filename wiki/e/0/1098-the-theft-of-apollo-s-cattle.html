the theft of Apollo's cattle

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the theft of Apollo's cattle is committed by Hermes through trickery: on the day of his birth, he steals the herd at night, reverses its tracks, drives it to Pylos, sacrifices two cattle, and conceals the evidence and meat; Apollo follows the strange tracks, confronts Hermes, and reaches a settlement after the cattle and cave evidence are recovered. In The Library, cattle theft by Hermes is the event in which Hermes steals the cattle Apollo was herding, takes them to Pylus, attempts to conceal the tracks, and is identified and accused by Apollo through divination. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the theft of Apollo's cattle is the event in which Mercury steals Apollo's cattle while Apollo is away, and Battus first conceals the theft for a cow, then exposes it for greater reward.

IV. TO HERMES

Hermes steals Apollo's cattle at night on the day of his birth. He disguises their tracks with reversed hoofprints and woven brushwood sandals, sacrifices two of the cattle, burns the hoofs and heads, and hides the remaining meat. Apollo investigates the missing herd, follows the tracks, and confronts Hermes.
