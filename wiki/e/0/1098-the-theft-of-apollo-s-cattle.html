the theft of Apollo's cattle

event · 3 works · 4 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: He stole away my cows, cattle theft by Hermes, cattle-lifter, he stole the cattle, stole the kine, stole your cows, the cattle theft, the theft, theft, theft of cattle, thief, this pitiless inquisition, youthful theft
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the theft of Apollo's cattle is committed by Hermes through trickery: on the day of his birth, he steals the herd at night, reverses its tracks, drives it to Pylos, sacrifices two cattle, and conceals the evidence and meat; Apollo follows the strange tracks, confronts Hermes, and reaches a settlement after the cattle and cave evidence are recovered.
  • In The Library, cattle theft by Hermes is the event in which Hermes steals the cattle Apollo was herding, takes them to Pylus, attempts to conceal the tracks, and is identified and accused by Apollo through divination.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the theft of Apollo's cattle is the event in which Mercury steals Apollo's cattle while Apollo is away, and Battus first conceals the theft for a cow, then exposes it for greater reward.

the theft of Apollo's cattle in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

The theft of Apollo's cattle is committed by Hermes through trickery. On the day of his birth, Hermes steals the herd at night, reverses its tracks, drives it to Pylos, sacrifices two cattle, and conceals evidence and meat. Apollo follows the strange tracks, confronts Hermes, and, despite Hermes' denial, reaches a settlement after the cattle and cave evidence are recovered.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

IV. TO HERMES

Hermes steals Apollo's cattle at night on the day of his birth. He disguises their tracks with reversed hoofprints and woven brushwood sandals, sacrifices two of the cattle, burns the hoofs and heads, and hides the remaining meat. Apollo investigates the missing herd, follows the tracks, and confronts Hermes.

Apollo accuses Hermes of stealing his cattle and describes the strange tracks leading toward Pylos and Hermes’ concealment of the herd. Hermes denies the theft, but the recovered cattle and the evidence at the cave lead to a settlement between the two gods.
