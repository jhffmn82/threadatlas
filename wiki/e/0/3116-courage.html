courage in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04

Courage is what the Cowardly Lion believes he lacks and seeks from Oz. He asks for it to become King of Beasts, and Oz promises it when the Lion brings proof that the Wicked Witch of the West is dead.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter VI

Courage is what the Cowardly Lion believes he lacks and wants Oz to give him. The Scarecrow says Oz could give the Lion courage as easily as brains.

Chapter XI

The Cowardly Lion asks Oz to give him courage so that he can truly become the King of Beasts. Oz tells him that he will receive courage when the Lion brings proof that the Wicked Witch of the West is dead.

courage in The Cowardly Lion of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1923

Courage is presented as brave action rather than a subjective feeling. The Cowardly Lion values it above all and seeks it by wanting to swallow a brave person, believing this will make him courageous. Unable to attack or accept hospitality from woodcutters and afraid of a gun, he still fights terrifically when frightened and protects Notta and Bob, remaining troubled by cowardice.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter 8

The Cowardly Lion considers courage the finest thing in the world and wants to feel it rather than merely act bravely. The characters discuss whether courage comes from feelings, actions, or perhaps from swallowing a brave person.

Chapter 9

The Cowardly Lion believes that eating a brave man will give him courage, but he cannot attack a woodcutter who calmly continues working or accept the hospitality of the woodcutters. He is afraid of the huntsman's gun and later explains that when he is frightened, he fights terrifically. His encounters show that he remains troubled by his cowardice while acting protectively toward Notta and Bob.
