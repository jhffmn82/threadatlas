husband (Corinna's husband) in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

Corinna's husband is portrayed as a madman, irrationally guarding a mistress whose universal appeal makes surveillance futile. Accusations disturb him, yet her tears and denials can make him distrust evidence and punish an informer.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ELEGY II.

The mistress's husband is described as irrationally concerned with guarding what would not be lost even without surveillance. Ovid says that the husband may believe the mistress chaste because she pleases everyone, and that her tears and denials may make him distrust his own eyes and punish an informer.

the hard husband in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

A husband relies on surveillance, placing a keeper over his wife; told her will cannot be guarded, he is advised to abandon sternness and indulge her friends.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

III

The hard husband places a keeper over his wife, but the speaker says that this achieves nothing because her will cannot be guarded. He is advised to abandon sternness, indulge his wife, and cultivate the friends she brings.
