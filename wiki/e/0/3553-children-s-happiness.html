children's happiness in The Life and Adventures of Santa Claus

L. Frank Baum · 1902

Children's happiness is available to rich and poor alike and is sought by Santa Claus for every child if possible. It matters more than thoughtless parents' wishes or indifference, and Santa Claus works with helpers, parents, and toy-shops to preserve it through gifts.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

5. How Bessie Blithesome Came to the Laughing Valley

The Queen of the Fairies teaches Claus that rich children can be as lonely, unhappy, gay, and happy as poor children. Children’s happiness therefore should not depend on whether they live in palaces or cottages.

3. The Deputies of Santa Claus

Santa Claus treats children's happiness as more important than the wishes or indifference of thoughtless parents. He works with helpers, parents, and toy-shops so that children can receive gifts and remain happy.
