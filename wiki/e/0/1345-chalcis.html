Chalcis

place · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Chaki- dians, Chal-* cis, Chalcidian, Chalcisj, Chalds, Chaleidiaos
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Chalcis was a city in Euboea that alone was garrisoned by the enemy; Cassander guarded it through Plistarchus until Ptolemy secured its surrender and removed the garrison.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Chalcis in Euboea was the place where Homer and Hesiod were said to have met and competed at Amphidamas's funeral gathering, where its leading men and Paneides judged the contest and declared Hesiod the winner.

Chalcis in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Chalcis, a city in Euboea, alone was garrisoned by the enemy; Cassander guarded it through Plistarchus until Ptolemy secured surrender and removed the garrison.

Narrative, section by section

THE affiiirs of this year being brought to this conclusion^ Theo->

It was the only Euboean city described as garrisoned by the enemy. Cassander was suspicious of it and left Plistarchus to guard it, but Ptolemy later obtained its surrender and removed the garrison.
