colored toy-making in The Life and Adventures of Santa Claus

L. Frank Baum · 1902

Claus’s toy-making begins with colored wooden cats made to delight children without scratching or biting. As his skill grows, it expands into rabbits, lambs, other animal figures, dolls, drums, cymbals, whistles, and horns. The Knooks supply wood, the Ryls supply paints and brushes, and the Fairies bring tools, enabling continuous work in his workshop through winter.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

3. How the Ryls Colored the Toys

Claus begins making better wooden cats after deciding children will enjoy toys that cannot scratch or bite. With the Ryls' paints, he colors the cats' features and body to imitate real cats, beginning his great work of making toys.

4. How Little Mayrie Became Frightened

Claus's toy-making grows from carving cats into a larger enterprise producing rabbits, lambs, and other animal figures. The Knooks provide wood, the Ryls provide paints and brushes, and the Fairies bring tools, allowing Claus to work continuously in his workshop.

8. The First Journey with the Reindeer

Toy-making keeps Claus busy through the winter while deep snow prevents him from traveling. After observing children, he decides to make more dolls, animal figures, drums, cymbals, whistles, and horns.
