  • In Heroides and Amores, Penelope is Icarius’s daughter and Telemachus’s mother who faithfully awaits Ulysses, beguiles her suitors with her web, writes to him, and tests suitors with the bow.
  • In The Odyssey, Penelope is Ulysses’s faithful wife, Telemachus’s mother, queen of Ithaca, and protector of her household who delays remarriage and recognizes Ulysses upon his return.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Penelope is transported with Odysseus’s body and Telemachus to Circe’s island, made immortal by Circe, and married to Telegonus.
  • In The Library, Penelope is Icarius and Periboea’s daughter, Odysseus’s wife, and Telemachus’s mother who delays remarriage and later marries Telegonus in one tradition.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Penelope is represented as Ptoliporthes’ mother, though another tradition says Odysseus sent her away and she died at Mantinea.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Penelope is Icarius’s daughter and Ulysses’s faithful wife who delays remarriage and proposes a contest with his bow.

Penelope in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Penelope, daughter of Icarius and mother of Telemachus, faithfully awaits Ulysses amid loneliness and suitors. She preserves herself, uses her web to beguile them, writes Ulysses seeking his return, and finally tests young men with the bow.

Penelope in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Penelope is Ulysses’s wife, Telemachus’s mother, Icarius’s daughter, and queen of Ithaca and Ulysses’s household. During Ulysses’s long absence, she remains in his house with her maids, mourns and weeps for him continually, and suffers as the suitors consume his property and press her to remarry. Her central concern is loyalty to Ulysses and protection of their household and son. She learns of the plot against Telemachus, grieves, prays to Minerva for him, and is comforted by a dream promising his return. She rebukes the suitors for their conduct, seeks revenge upon them, protects guests from their mistreatment, and questions travelers and a stranger for news of Ulysses. Though pressured by her father, brothers, and the suitors, she postpones remarriage by weaving Laertes’s burial pall by day and secretly unpicking it at night for three years. In distress and uncertainty, she dreams, prays for release from misery, and finally sets the contest of Ulysses’s bow and twelve axes as the condition for choosing a husband. After the slaughter of the suitors, she does not immediately accept Ulysses’s return. She tests him by speaking of their bed; when he proves knowledge shared only by them and one maidservant, she recognizes and embraces him, and they share the sufferings of his absence.

Penelope in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Penelope is the daughter of Icarius and Periboea, wife of Odysseus or Ulysses, and mother of Telemachus; she is also said to bear Poliporthes or Polypoethes to Ulysses. When suitors, including men from Ithaca, believe Ulysses dead, they pressure her to remarry. She delays them for three years by weaving Laertes’ shroud by day and undoing it by night. Ulysses returns, kills the suitors, reveals himself, and is recognized by her. Later traditions diverge: Telegonus takes her and Odysseus’s corpse to Circe and marries her, after which Circe sends them to the Islands of the Blest; other accounts allege seduction, exile to Icarius, or her death.

Penelope in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Penelope is represented as Ptoliporthes’ mother after Odysseus’ return from Troy; another tradition says Odysseus sent her away after discovering her encouraging the suitors, and she traveled through Lacedæmon to Mantinea, where she died.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XII.

The Thesprotian Poem represents Penelope as bearing Ptoliporthes to Odysseus after his return from Troy. The Mantineans instead say that Odysseus discovered her encouraging the suitors, sent her away, and that she went first to Lacedæmon, then to Mantinea, where she died.

Penelope in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Penelope, daughter of Icarius and wife of Ulysses, accompanies him to Ithaca and remains faithful during his long absence. Resisting suitors, she delays remarriage by undoing at night the robe she weaves by day. Still awaiting Ulysses, she finally proposes a contest with his bow to choose a husband.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter XVI

Penelope, the daughter of Icarius and wife of Ulysses, chose to accompany him to Ithaca and remained faithful during his long absence. She resisted her suitors by delaying her choice and undoing at night the robe she wove by day.

Chapter XXIII

Penelope has delayed choosing among the suitors while awaiting Ulysses. She finally agrees to a contest of skill with Ulysses' bow, offering the winner the right to marry her.
