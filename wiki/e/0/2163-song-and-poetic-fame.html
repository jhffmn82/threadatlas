song and poetic fame in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

Song and poetic fame are presented as forces that preserve rare, noble, and victorious deeds from obscurity after people die. Through bards’ wisdom, voice, verse, and music, praise comforts, rewards achievement, and carries a clan’s inherited renown forward. The records move from song’s general power to save old glory, to victory praise elevating Timasarchos’s clan, to the Bassids’ continuing fame, to Odysseus’s enlarged honor, and finally to Strepsiades’s newly sung triumph after sorrow.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Nemean Ode IV

Praise joined to music is said to comfort and to outlive a deed. Songs of victory give Timasarchos's clan renown, and praise of a person's rare deeds can make that person the peer of kings.

Nemean Ode VI

Poetic fame preserves noble deeds after people have vanished. The ode presents song as a means of carrying the Bassid clan's inherited renown forward and compares its own praise to an archer's accurate shot.

Nemean Ode VII

Song preserves and rewards great deeds that would otherwise fall into darkness, with Mnemosyne enabling their remembrance through voice and verse. Pindar suggests that poetic song has made Odysseus' fame greater than his actual toils and can increase honor even for the dead.

Isthmian Ode VI

Song preserves the glory of the past for mortals who attain the full reward granted by the wisdom of bards. The poet now leads Strepsiades’s new triumph in a melodious hymn and says that he will sing after receiving calm following sorrow.
