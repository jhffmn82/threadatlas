Hebe

deity · 4 works · 15 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: cupbearer, goddess of youth, her, that Goddess, the daughter of Juno, the goddess of youth

Abstract written by a model

In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Hebe is Eileithuia's goddess sister who becomes Herakles' bride after his labors, marrying him in Olympus. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Hebe is the Goddess of Youth, Juno's daughter and Jupiter's stepdaughter and daughter-in-law; she restores Iolaüs to youth before Themis stops her from fulfilling her vow to refuse others. In Description of Greece, Hebe is a goddess identified at Phlius with Ganymeda and married to Hercules; her ancient sanctuary grants complete immunity to suppliant slaves. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Hebe serves as cupbearer to the gods until she resigns or is dismissed after a fall, and Juno gives her in marriage to Hercules.

Hebe is a goddess, identified at Phlius with Ganymeda, married to Hercules, whose ancient sanctuary with cypress grove grants complete immunity to suppliant slaves.

CHAPTER XII.

Hebe is identified by later Phliasians with the goddess originally called Ganymeda. Her precinct at Phlius includes a cypress grove and an ancient temple, and slaves who come there as suppliants receive complete immunity.

Hebe was the daughter of Juno, goddess of youth, and cupbearer to the gods. She either resigned her office when she married Hercules or was dismissed after falling while attending the gods. Juno later gave her to Hercules in marriage.
