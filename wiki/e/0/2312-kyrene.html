Kyrene in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

Kyrene is a city and colony in Libya, portrayed as a place of royal, sacred, and athletic importance. It is founded through Apollo, foundation of Kyrene, Battos, and a line descended from Euphemos, after Thera; its fountain Kyra lies at its heart. Its royal house is linked to Apollo, Pytho, and the Argonauts, while Arkesilas is urged to restore and rule it well. The city honors Aphrodite and Apollo’s Karneia, celebrates horses and charioteers, and gains glory through Telesikrates’s victory.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

IV.

Kyrene is a city in Libya founded through the line descended from Euphemos and Battos. Its royal house is connected with Apollo, Pytho, and the Argonauts, and Arkesilas is urged to restore and govern it well.

[Footnote 1: Libya. Epaphos was son of Zeus by Io.]

Kyrene was to be founded in Libya by Battos after the intervening colonization of Thera. The fountain Kyra was located in the heart of the city Kyrene.

V.

Kyrene is Arkesilas's city and the destination of the victorious horses and their charioteer. Its people sing Arkesilas's praise around Aphrodite's garden and honor Apollo's Karneia. Kyrene was founded by Battos, and its sacred street, groves, market-place, tombs, and heroic traditions are described.

IX.

Kyrene is praised as a city of charioteers that received glory through Telesikrates’s victory. It is also described as the city founded through the destiny of the huntress-maiden Kyrene.
