Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Eumaeus’s hut, a far-visible dwelling beside the pig yards, houses Eumaeus, his men, and Ulysses’s hearth. Eumaeus welcomes Ulysses with a rush bed, chamois skin, food, and conversation; Ulysses, Eumaeus, and the men eat, drink, and discuss hardships until nearly daybreak.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XIV

Eumaeus’s hut stands beside the pig yards on a site visible from far away. He gives Ulysses a thick bed of rushes covered with a shaggy chamois skin and welcomes him inside for food and conversation.

Meanwhile Ulysses and the swineherd were eating their supper in the

Ulysses and Eumaeus eat and drink in the swineherd’s hut with the other men. They remain there talking about their hardships until nearly daybreak.
