  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Mycenae was an independent city that was besieged, conquered, and razed by the Argives.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the kingdom of Mycenae is feared by Eurystheus as a possession the Heraclidae might take, and Atreus obtains it after Eurystheus’s death.
  • In Iphigenia in Tauris, Mycenae is named as Orestes’ home and as the place associated with the family whose fate Iphigenia investigates.
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Mycenae is Eurystheus’s royal seat, from which he sends Copreus and leads his forces, and whose walls were built by the Cyclops.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Mycenae is the realm ruled by Eurystheus, later described as Orestes’ homeland and Iphigenia’s ancestral country.
  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Mycenae is the city before which the action takes place and the ancestral seat of the Pelopidae palace, to which Orestes returns.
  • In The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies, Mycenae is the city where the Palace of Atreus stands and where the Chorus awaits news of the Greek army.
  • In The Iliad, Mycenae sends a hundred ships with Corinth, Cleonae, and surrounding lands, under Agamemnon’s command.
  • In The Library, the kingdom of Mycenae is sought by the Mycenaeans for a Pelopid ruler, passing from Thyestes to Atreus and later back to Thyestes through Aegisthus.
  • In The Library, Mycenae is ruled by Agamemnon, becomes the place of his murder and Aegisthus’s reign, and later receives Orestes when he kills Aegisthus and Clytaemnestra.
  • In The Library, Mycenae is ruled by Eurystheus, Perseus, and Electryon, while Eurystheus resides there and has Hercules display captured beasts before its gates.
  • In Description of Greece, Mycenæ was founded by Perseus, later destroyed by the Argives, and left with ruins, a lion gate, tombs, and underground treasuries.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Mycenæ was a Cyclopes-built fort and city that led Greece against Ilium before being evacuated and left ruined.

Mycenae in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Mycenae is a city and the realm ruled by Eurystheus. It is described as prosperous and much praised for valor in war, yet as secretly angry with the speaker’s country. Eurystheus claims power to punish and recover the exiled Heraclids, while Mycenae’s friendship is presented as something Demophoon might gain. Eurystheus then leaves Mycenae with spearmen to attack Athens, but his forces are defeated and he is captured. Later called formerly prosperous, Mycenae is Orestes’ homeland and Iphigenia’s ancestral country; Iphigenia hopes to return there with Orestes and restore her ancestral house.

Mycenæ in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Mycenæ, a place founded by Perseus after exchanging kingdoms with Megapenthes, was associated with a fallen scabbard or discovered fungus and water. Argives destroyed it from jealousy; its Thermopylæ contingent, ruins, lion gate, tombs, and underground treasuries remained.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XII.

Mycenæ was founded by Perseus after he exchanged kingdoms with Megapenthes, either because his sword scabbard fell there or because he found a fungus and water. The Argives later destroyed it out of jealousy, although its people had sent 80 men to Thermopylæ; ruins, the gate with lions, tombs, and underground treasuries remained.

Mycenæ in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Mycenæ, a Cyclopes-built fort and city, led Greece against Ilium; unconquered by Argives, it was evacuated when supplies failed and later lay ruined and uninhabited.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXV.

Mycenæ could not be captured by the Argives because of the strength of its fort, which had been built by the Cyclopes. Its people were forced to evacuate when their supplies failed.
