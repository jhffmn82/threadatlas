Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the marriage is attended by the gods on Mount Pelion, who feast and bring Peleus gifts; Strife’s arrival begins the dispute among Hera, Athena, and Aphrodite. In Hecuba and other plays, the marriage is a divine-human marriage and a source of both descendants and calamity, whose ties Thetis recalls and whose implications Peleus discusses. In The Library, the marriage is a Greek myth marriage story and the tenth appendix heading, taking place on Pelion as the gods celebrate with feast and song. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the marriage follows Peleus’s restraint of Thetis, produces Achilles, and leaves Peleus happy in his wife and son apart from his guilt over Phocus’s death.

The Marriage of Peleus and Thetis in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where The Marriage of Peleus and Thetis first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Marriage of Peleus and Thetis is a Greek myth marriage story, known through Apollodorus and listed as the tenth appendix heading. It takes place on Pelion, where the gods celebrate with feast and song; Chiron gives Peleus an ashen spear and Poseidon immortal horses. A comparison with a modern Cretan tale suggests that Thetis later vanishes into the sea after Peleus reproaches her while she tries to make their child immortal.
