Abstract written by a model

Euryclea is an aged, trusted nurse and household servant connected to Ulysses, Telemachus, and Penelope. Daughter of Ops, she received and nursed Ulysses from birth, nurses Telemachus, serves as Penelope’s old nurse, and acts as Ulysses’ faithful housekeeper, responsible for stores and set over the household women. She knows the women’s conduct and keeps Telemachus’s voyage secret from Penelope. In Book XIX, she shuts the women in their room at Telemachus’s request, then washes a stranger’s feet at Penelope’s command. His figure, voice, and feet remind her of Ulysses; touching the scar from his wild-boar hunt confirms his identity. Though she privately identifies him, Ulysses requires silence, and she loyally keeps it. In Book XXII, she reports that fifty women are in the house and identifies twelve who have misconducted themselves, summons women, and brings fire and sulphur as commanded. In Book XXIII, she joyfully tells Penelope that Ulysses has returned and killed the suitors, helps prepare the bedchamber, and urges Ulysses to wear clean clothes.

BOOK XIX

Euryclea shuts the women in their room when Telemachus asks her to do so. At Penelope's command, she washes the stranger's feet and observes that his figure, voice, and feet closely resemble Ulysses's, though she has not yet openly identified him.

Then the old woman took the cauldron in which she was going to wash his

Euryclea washes Ulysses’ feet and recognizes him by the scar from his boar-hunt on Mount Parnassus. She drops his foot, overturning the bath, and privately identifies him as Ulysses. After he threatens her into silence, she promises not to reveal him and offers to name the women in the house who have behaved well or badly.

BOOK XXII

Euryclea comes to Ulysses after he summons her and confirms that there are fifty women in the house. She tells him that twelve have misconducted themselves and that she can identify them.

Euryclea left the cloister to tell the women, and make them come to

Euryclea leaves the cloister to summon the women, later brings fire and sulphur as Ulysses commands, and goes to call Penelope and the other maidservants. She also urges Ulysses to change out of his rags into clean clothes.

BOOK XXIII

Euryclea joyfully tells Penelope that Ulysses has returned and killed the suitors. She says that she recognized him by the scar from the wild boar and that Ulysses prevented her from revealing this proof. She later helps prepare the bedchamber.
