Phaeacian hospitality in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Phaeacian hospitality is a custom toward strangers and foreigners in distress, who are under Jove’s protection. It begins with kindness, food, drink, clothes, washing, and guidance, then provides a feast, music, dancing, sports, clean linen, warm baths, good beds, and a ship, crew, and escort.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK VI

Nausicaa says that strangers and foreigners in distress are under Jove’s protection and should receive kindness. She orders her maids to give Ulysses food, drink, clothes, and a washing place, and promises to direct him to the town.

BOOK VIII

Phaeacian hospitality includes arranging an escort for every stranger, preparing a ship and crew, providing a feast, and entertaining guests with music, dancing, and athletic sports. Alcinous emphasizes that the Phaeacians are especially fond of good dinners, music, dancing, clean linen, warm baths, and good beds.
