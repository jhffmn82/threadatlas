Enoch Drebber in A Study in Scarlet

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1886

Enoch J. Drebber is an American gentleman from Cleveland, Ohio, a former member of the Latter Day Saints, and the murder victim found in an empty house at 3, Lauriston Gardens. Identified by cards in his pockets, he had a gold watch and was apparently preparing to sail from Liverpool to New York. Before his death, he and his private secretary, Joseph Stangerson, had boarded at Charpentier’s establishment for nearly three weeks. Madame Charpentier describes Drebber as often drunk, coarse, and offensive toward her daughter Alice; his distorted, baboon-like face strikes Watson as vicious. In his younger days, Drebber was selected to marry Lucy Ferrier when the Prophet settled a dispute in favor of Drebber’s stronger party. The marriage was forced, and Drebber acquired much of John Ferrier’s property through it, later becoming wealthy and leaving Utah to travel through the United States and Europe. Jefferson Hope regards him as guilty for forcing Lucy into marriage and helping cause her death, and pursues him as an old rival in love. In Brixton, Hope lures Drebber to the empty house, compels him to choose between two pills, and kills him when he takes the poisoned one. Drebber’s death leads Hope to pursue Stangerson as well. Although others initially suspect Arthur Charpentier, Holmes identifies Hope as Drebber’s murderer and connects the crime to its personal motive.

Narrative, section by section

8 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER III.

Enoch J. Drebber is identified by cards found in the pockets of the dead man at 3, Lauriston Gardens. He was from Cleveland, Ohio, and appears to have been preparing to return to New York.

CHAPTER V.

Enoch J. Drebber’s distorted face continues to trouble Dr. Watson, who regards him as deeply vicious despite believing that justice must be done. Holmes thinks the murderer dropped a wedding ring while stooping over Drebber’s body.

CHAPTER VI.

Enoch J. Drebber is identified in the newspapers as the American found dead in an empty house in Brixton Road. Madame Charpentier describes him as frequently drunk, coarse, and offensive toward her daughter Alice; Gregson believes Arthur Charpentier killed him after following him from the boarding house.

CHAPTER VII.

Enoch Drebber’s purse is found in Joseph Stangerson’s possession, apparently because Stangerson usually handled the paying. Sherlock Holmes identifies Jefferson Hope as the murderer of Drebber.

CHAPTER V.

Drebber is chosen over Stangerson to marry Lucy Ferrier after their dispute is decided by the Prophet and Drebber's party proves stronger. He later leaves Utah with his property, travels through the United States and Europe, and is pursued by Jefferson Hope.

CHAPTER VI.

Enoch Drebber is identified by Jefferson Hope as the man who forced Lucy Ferrier into marriage and helped cause her death. Hope lures Drebber to the house in the Brixton Road, makes him choose between two pills, and watches him die after taking the poisoned one.

“That was how Enoch Drebber came to his end. All I had to do then was

Jefferson Hope says that Enoch Drebber’s death led him to pursue Stangerson in order to pay off John Ferrier’s debt.

CHAPTER VII.

Enoch Drebber was poisoned after Jefferson Hope forced poison upon him. Holmes learns from Cleveland that Drebber had sought legal protection against Hope, an old rival in love, and uses a ring found at the scene to infer the personal motive.
