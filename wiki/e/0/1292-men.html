Aeschylus · -0471

Mortal men begin as a purblind, artless race, living in caves without understanding, reason, or foresight, while Zeus intends their destruction. Prometheus saves them, gives fire and blind hopes, and instructs them in consciousness, practical arts, medicine, divination, sacrifice, and metal use.

men in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Men are mortal people whose lives depend on work, justice, the gods, and the earth. Formerly they dwelled in mountain caves and resembled wild beasts; through Hephaestus they learned crafts and came to live peacefully in their own houses throughout the year. They receive fire after Prometheus steals it from Zeus, burn white bones to the deathless gods, and lay up offerings in Nysa’s shrines, yet they lack a seer who knows Zeus’s mind. Zeus gives them right unlike beasts and birds, judging justice and violence, while their lot includes toil, sickness, sorrow, marriage, poverty, and death. They must plough, sow, harvest, make tools, store grain, and heed seasonal signs, since idleness brings hunger. Demeter’s anger causes famine and threatens their destruction and their gifts to the gods; through her they receive the Eleusinian Mysteries. Earth can bless them with children, harvests, wealth, cattle, and orderly cities.

mortals in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Mortals, or mankind, begin in the Golden Age living safely and peacefully, without avengers, war, or shores beyond their own, sustained by freely produced natural food. They become universally corrupt and wicked, so Jupiter condemns them to deserved vengeance. A universal deluge destroys the human race beneath the waves; most perish or later succumb to hunger. In the end, only Deucalion and Pyrrha remain, and Deucalion longs to restore the lost people by molding earth and giving it souls.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Men live safely and peacefully during the Golden Age, without fear, soldiers, or warlike weapons. They gather fruits, berries, cornels, and acorns, and eat food produced freely by the Earth.

opening

Men are described as if they had conspired to be wicked. Jupiter determines that all men should quickly experience the vengeance they deserve to endure.

opening

The human race is condemned by Jupiter because of its universal corruption. It is overwhelmed by the flood, with most people carried away or destroyed and the survivors eventually overcome by hunger.

opening

The mortal race is reduced to Deucalion and Pyrrha after the waters destroy the rest of the people. Deucalion wishes that he could replace the lost people by his father's arts and infuse souls into molded earth.
