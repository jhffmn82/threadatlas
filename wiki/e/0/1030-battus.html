Battus in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Battus lives on a rock, recognizes stolen cattle, accepts Hermes’ rewards for secrecy then disclosure, and is transformed into a rock for being double-tongued.

THE GREAT EOIAE

Battus lived on a rock and recognized the stolen cattle being driven past. Hermes promised him a reward for secrecy, but Battus later accepted a robe and disclosed that he had seen the cattle, after which Hermes changed him into a rock.

Battus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Battus, an old man known in his country, keeps forests, pastures, and Neleus's mares. After witnessing Mercury's theft, he accepts payment for silence but betrays it for more, so Mercury condemns him and changes him into stone.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

opening

Battus is an old man who keeps the forests, grassy pastures, and Neleus's fine-bred mares. He witnesses Mercury's theft and accepts a cow in exchange for silence, but later reveals the cattle's location when Mercury offers him a greater reward. Mercury condemns his betrayal and changes him into a hard stone.
