Battus

person · 2 works · 9 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: an old man, him, the old man

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Battus lives on a rock, recognizes stolen cattle, accepts Hermes’ rewards for secrecy and then disclosure, and is transformed into a rock for being double-tongued. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Battus keeps forests, pastures, and Neleus's mares, betrays Mercury's theft after accepting payment for silence, and is changed into stone.

Abstract written by a model

Battus, an old man known in his country, keeps forests, pastures, and Neleus's mares. After witnessing Mercury's theft, he accepts payment for silence but betrays it for more, so Mercury condemns him and changes him into stone.

opening

Battus is an old man who keeps the forests, grassy pastures, and Neleus's fine-bred mares. He witnesses Mercury's theft and accepts a cow in exchange for silence, but later reveals the cattle's location when Mercury offers him a greater reward. Mercury condemns his betrayal and changes him into a hard stone.
