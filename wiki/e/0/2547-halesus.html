Halesus in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

Halesus, an exile fleeing his crime and his father’s wealth, wanders over land and sea, then prospers by erecting lofty walls and teaching his Falisci the rites of Juno.

ELEGY XIII.

Halesus is said to have fled from both his crime and his father's wealth, wandered over land and sea, and then built lofty walls with prospering hand. He taught the Falisci the rites of Juno.

Halesus in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Halesus, a youthful son of Agamemnon and foe of Troy by birth, arrives in a high chariot and leads a thousand armed men from several regions to Turnus’ aid.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

And now the mighty labour is begun

Halesus comes in a high chariot as an enemy of Troy by birth. Claiming descent from Agamemnon, he leads a thousand men from the Massic, Auruncan, Sidicinian, Vulturnian, Calesian, Oscan, and Saticulan regions, armed with light demi-lances, short crooked swords, and light bucklers.
