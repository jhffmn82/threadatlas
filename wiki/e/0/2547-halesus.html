Halesus

person · 3 works · 10 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Halaesus, the youthful hero

Abstract written by a model

In The Aeneid, Halesus, the youthful hero and a son of Agamemnon, arrives in a high chariot and leads a thousand armed men from several regions to Turnus’ aid. In Heroides and Amores, Halaesus, exiled after Agamemnon’s killing, wanders land and sea, founds Falerii, and teaches its people Juno’s sacred rites. In The Amores, or Amours, Halesus, an exile fleeing his crime and his father’s wealth, wanders over land and sea, then prospers by erecting lofty walls and teaching his Falisci the rites of Juno.

Abstract written by a model

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

And now the mighty labour is begun

Halesus comes in a high chariot as an enemy of Troy by birth. Claiming descent from Agamemnon, he leads a thousand men from the Massic, Auruncan, Sidicinian, Vulturnian, Calesian, Oscan, and Saticulan regions, armed with light demi-lances, short crooked swords, and light bucklers.
