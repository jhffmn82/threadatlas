Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Tyndareus is the son of Oebalus and king who angers Aphrodite, binds Helen’s suitors by oath, and allows Menelaus to prevail. In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Tyndarus is the father of Clytaemnestra and Helen, grandfather and former nurturer of Orestes, and Orestes’ condemner after the matricide. In The Library, Tyndareus is the son of Oebalus and Batia, king of Lacedaemon, husband of Leda, father of Clytaemnestra, and the ruler who restored Agamemnon and Menelaus and arranged Helen’s marriage to Menelaus.

Tyndarus in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Tyndarus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Tyndarus is the father of Clytæmnestra and Helen and grandfather of Orestes, whom he nurtured. After Orestes kills Clytæmnestra, Tyndarus mourns her, condemns him as an unholy matricide, and opposes him on legal and religious grounds. He argues Orestes should have charged her legally or accepted banishment, then urges death sentences for Orestes and Electra.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

MENELAUS, ORESTES, CHORUS.

Tyndarus arrives mourning Clytæmnestra and condemns Orestes as an unholy matricide. He argues that Orestes should have brought a legal charge against Clytæmnestra or accepted banishment, and he plans to urge the Argives to sentence Orestes and Electra to death.

PYLADES, ORESTES, CHORUS.

He is present and argues against Orestes because Orestes killed his daughter. He favors the ties connecting him with Clytemnestra over those connecting him with Orestes' father and privately encourages speeches seeking their death.

Tyndareus in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Tyndareus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Tyndareus, said to be son of Oebalus and Batia, was expelled from Lacedaemon by Hippocoon and his sons. After their deaths, he returned and ruled, married Leda, fathered Clytaemnestra, brought back Agamemnon and Menelaus, chose Menelaus for Helen, bound her suitors by oath, and gave Menelaus Sparta’s kingdom.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Εἰσὶ δὲ οἱ λέγοντες ᾿Αφαρέα μὲν καὶ Λεύκιππον

Tyndareus was a son of Oebalus and the nymph Batia, according to one genealogy. Hippocoon expelled him and Icarius from Lacedaemon, but after Heracles killed Hippocoon and his sons, Tyndareus returned and took the kingdom. He married Leda, chose Menelaus as Helen’s bridegroom, and caused Helen’s suitors to swear an oath.

Tz 15 «Τὸν δ᾽ ᾿Αγαμέμνονα ὃ τροφὸς μετὰ τοῦ Μενελάου

Tyndareus later brought Agamemnon and Menelaus back. They became his sons-in-law, and he gave the kingdom of Sparta to Menelaus.
