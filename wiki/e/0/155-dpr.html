DPR in Generation-Augmented Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering

Yuning Mao, Pengcheng He, Xiaodong Liu, Yelong Shen, Jianfeng Gao, Jiawei Han, Weizhu Chen · 2021

DPR is a state-of-the-art dense passage-retrieval method for open-domain question answering and a comparison baseline. It uses BERT-base for representation learning and generates dense representations for Wikipedia passages. Training with 8 GPUs requires 24 hours, indexing with 8 GPUs 17.3 hours, and retrieval with 1 GPU 30 minutes. DPR is also reused as a retriever in generative QA studies and supplies the reader design and architecture followed by extractive GAR. Across evaluations, DPR generally surpasses unaugmented BM25 at low retrieval cutoffs, but GAR with BM25 can match or exceed it in several settings. GAR and DPR are complementary: combining them consistently improves retrieval and end-to-end QA, though DPR has substantially greater training and indexing computation.

DPR in Robust Retrieval Augmented Generation for Zero-shot Slot Filling

Michael Glass, Gaetano Rossiello, Md Faisal Mahbub Chowdhury, Alﬁo Gliozzo · 2021

Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) is a retrieval method used to retrieve evidence passages for slot filling and before answer generation. It is trained for slot filling without retrieval-specific pre-training; a baseline passage encoder is trained on Natural Questions. DPR trains query and passage encoders, and its zero-shot slot-filling approach extends dense retrieval with hard negatives, including negatives mined from a learned dense index. Models are compared using Natural Questions, BM25 hard negatives, and dense-index hard negatives; further RAG training improves retrieval. KGI uses DPR, trains encoders with KILT provenance ground truth, and during domain adaptation indexes a new corpus with pretrained DPR. Training uses two epochs and takes 24 hours on one NVIDIA V100 GPU. Finally, DPR indexes the TACRED corpus for analysis.

DPR in LI-RAGE: Late Interaction Retrieval Augmented Generation with Explicit Signals for Open-Domain Table Question Answering

Weizhe Lin · 2023

DPR is a retriever model used for table retrieval in open-domain TableQA and as a component in contrastive experiments, DPR-RAGE, and RAG. It represents each question and table with a single vector, computes question-table similarity, and uses 768-dimensional embeddings. Its official pretrained model is fine-tuned on each dataset and on the retrieval task before joint retriever-reader training, using contrastive learning. Training uses four negatives per positive, Adam with learning rate 0.0001, batch size 8 per device, four gradient-accumulation steps, and eight GPUs; it runs 6,000 steps on NQ-TABLES and 600 on E2E-WTQ. DPR is evaluated with Recall@K against BM25, reports 1.10 iterations per second and training times of 60 minutes on NQ and 10 on WTQ, then pre-extracts table embeddings for an index built in 40 seconds.

Narrative, section by section

8 sections; each heading opens the text

Abstract

Variants of it are commonly used for retrieval in open-domain TableQA models, computing similarities between questions and tables from a single representation of each.

Introduction

Dense Passage Retriever is a basis for variants used to build table retrievers. It computes question-table similarity using single vector representations of the question and the table.

Experimental Setup

DPR is used as one of the table retrieval components in contrastive experiments. Its official pretrained model is fine-tuned on each dataset before joint retriever-reader training, and it is used in DPR-RAGE and RAG.

Dataset Statistics

DPR training uses four negative samples per positive sample, a learning rate of 0.0001, the Adam optimizer, a batch size of 8 per device, four gradient accumulation steps, and eight GPUs. Training runs for 6,000 steps on NQ-TABLES and 600 steps on E2E-WTQ.

CLTR and T-RAG Evaluation

DPR is used for retrieval evaluation through Recall@K and is compared with BM25. Its reported training speed is 1.10 iterations per second, with batch size 8 and training times of 60 minutes on NQ and 10 minutes on WTQ.

Technical Details / Hyperparameters

DPR extracts table and question embeddings with dimension d = 768.

Indexing and Dynamic Retrieval
