British Museum

museum · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: British museum, The British Museum

Abstract written by a model

In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the British Museum was described as an organisation containing ancient Greek sculpture, including the Payne-Knight Apollo, remnants from Apollo's temple at Phigales, much of the Parthenon frieze, and Parthenon gable fragments purchased by Lord Elgin. In Musical Instruments, the British Museum was described as a place containing Egyptian pipes, a fresco fragment, cymbals, bronze bells, and a fourteenth-century manuscript, including a seven-holed pipe with two possible reed-like straws.

British Museum in Musical Instruments

Carl Engel · 1875-08 · the work’s page · read it where British Museum first appears

Abstract written by a model

British museum contains Egyptian pipes, a fresco fragment, cymbals, bronze bells, and a fourteenth-century manuscript; one seven-holed pipe had two possible reed-like straws.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

The British museum contains Egyptian pipes, a fragment of a fresco showing two female nofre performers, a pair of cymbals, and several small bronze bells. One Egyptian pipe has seven side holes, and two straws found with it may have served a purpose like a reed.
