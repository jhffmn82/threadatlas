pigs in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Pigs are Eumaeus’s herd in Ithaca, kept in twelve styes with additional boars outside. The herd includes 360 boar pigs, fattens on beechmast and spring water, and is guarded and managed by Eumaeus. At night they are driven into styes; Eumaeus protects them, sends the best boars to the suitors, and sacrifices two young pigs to feed Ulysses.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XIV

Eumaeus keeps hundreds of pigs in twelve styes and has additional boars outside the yards. The suitors continually eat the best boars, so Eumaeus must keep sending them the finest animals. Eumaeus sacrifices two young pigs to feed Ulysses.

“I am by birth a Cretan; my father was a well to do man, who had many

The pigs are driven into their styes at night, and Eumaeus considers feeding the stranger from the labor of raising them. Eumaeus later leaves the hut to sleep near and protect them from possible attacks.
