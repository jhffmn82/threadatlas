Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Ceyx is the glorious king of Trachis, friend of the blessed gods, and horse-driving host whose feast Heracles attends. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Ceyx is the son of Lucifer, husband of Halcyone, and peaceful king of Trachyn; after sailing to Claros, he drowns in a shipwreck and is transformed into a kingfisher. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Ceyx is the peaceful king of Thessaly and devoted husband of Halcyone; he drowns when a storm wrecks his ship, and he and Halcyone are transformed into birds.

Ceyx in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Ceyx first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ceyx is the son of Lucifer, husband of Halcyone, and peaceful king of Trachyn, marked by his sceptre. He kindly receives the exiled Peleus and recounts his brother Dædalion’s transformation after Chione’s death. Seeking the Clarian God’s answer about his brother’s fate, he sails for Claros despite Halcyone’s wishes, promising a swift return. A storm wrecks his ship in the Ægean Sea; thinking of and calling for Halcyone, he drowns. Morpheus tells Halcyone of his death, and after she finds his body on shore, divine pity transforms Ceyx and her into birds, Ceyx becoming a kingfisher.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

FABLES V. AND VI. [XI.221-409]

Ceyx, the son of Lucifer and ruler of Trachyn, receives the exiled Peleus with kindness. He tells Peleus how his brother Dædalion was transformed into a hawk after the death of Chione.

FABLE VII. [XI.410-748]

Ceyx decides to go to Claros to consult the Clarian oracle about his brother’s fate. He refuses to abandon the voyage or take Halcyone with him, promising to return before the moon has twice completed her orb. During the storm he thinks constantly of Halcyone, calls her name while swimming, and is finally submerged beneath a wave.

In the meantime, the daughter of Æolus, ignorant of so great

Ceyx dies when his ship is wrecked in the Ægean Sea. Morpheus appears to Halcyone in his form and tells her that the South wind shattered the ship and that he perished while calling her name. His body is later carried to the shore, where Halcyone finds it, and he is transformed with her into a bird.

Ceyx in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Ceyx first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ceyx, son of Hesperus, is the peaceful king of Thessaly and devoted husband of Halcyone. He sails to Claros in Ionia to consult Apollo's oracle, but a storm wrecks his ship and he drowns while calling for Halcyone. After Morpheus tells her of his death in Ceyx's form, Ceyx and Halcyone are transformed into birds.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CEYX AND HALCYONE: OR, THE HALCYON BIRDS

Ceyx is King of Thessaly and the son of Hesperus, the Day-star. He travels by ship to Claros in Ionia to consult Apollo's oracle, but a storm destroys his vessel and he drowns while repeatedly calling for Halcyone. After his death, Morpheus appears to Halcyone in Ceyx's form to tell her what happened, and Ceyx is transformed into a bird with her.
