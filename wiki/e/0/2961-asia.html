Asia in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Asia is a region, divided by Mount Taurus into northern and southern parts and described through its provinces, rivers, climates, coastal countries, and inland regions. Its cities on the side within the Halys are specifically noted. Its record unfolds chiefly as a contested and repeatedly reorganized theater of rule and war. Coastal peoples, governors, and commanders revolted against Artaxerxes, depriving the Persian king of half his revenues; Mentor governed the Asiatic shore while Bagoas held the higher parts. Philip sent Attalus and Parmenio toward Asia, and rivalry followed between Macedonian and Persian forces. Alexander entered from Europe across the Hellespont, claimed conquest, campaigned along the coast and inland, and after Issus Asia is described as under a conquering sword; Darius offered him the part within the Halys. Disorder also accompanied these campaigns, as discharged mercenaries robbed the country. After Alexander, Asia was divided among commanders and became the object of struggles involving Eumenes, Antigonus, Cassander, Lysimachus, Demetrius, Seleucus, and others. Antigonus sought its provinces, became its undisputed absolute lord, and used beacons and couriers to manage it, though later warfare again spread through its cities and regions. Asia also saw a slave uprising and harsh government under Attalus. Under Roman administration it suffered illegal exactions by publicans, then relief under Quintus Mutius Scaevola; afterward Mithridates's party drove cities to revolt from the Romans. Following Antiochus's overthrow, Roman commissioners redistributed territories in Asia among Eumenes, the Rhodians, and freed cities.

Asia in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Asia is a continent, formerly ruled by native kings whose names and deeds were not recorded. It became a broad field of conquest and empire: Ninus sought the region between the Tanais and Nile; Semiramis held Persia and other dominions, traveled through them, reshaped landscapes, and founded or adorned settlements; and Sesostris invaded and largely conquered Asia, reaching beyond the Ganges and bringing remaining parts and Cyclades islands under his rule. The Amazons also conquered much of it as far as Syria. After the Assyrian empire fell, the Medes ruled all Asia until Cyrus transferred power to the Persians. Under Persian control, its Greek cities sought liberty, and it later became the main theater of Agesilaus's campaign.

Asia in Musical Instruments

Carl Engel · 1875-08

Asia is presented as the likely source of much Greek music and many Greek stringed instruments. Strabo associated a large portion of contemporary music with Asia as far as India, and named instruments with foreign or Asiatic associations. Greek instruments were especially adopted from peoples inhabiting Asia-minor.
