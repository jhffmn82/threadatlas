Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, Machaon is a physician and surgeon who treats Menelaus, is wounded by Alexandrus, and rests in Nestor’s tent needing healing. In Description of Greece, Machaon is the son of Æsculapius who was killed by Eurypylus and whose bones, tomb, temple, sacred place, and bronze statue are found in Gerenia. In The Fall of Troy, Machaon is a warrior and healer who fights Eurypylus, is wounded, and is killed by him with a spear.

Machaon in The Iliad

Homer · 749 to 724 BC · the work’s page · read it where Machaon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Machaon, son of Aesculapius, is a physician and surgeon valued by the army. Agamemnon summons him to treat Menelaus: he removes an arrow and applies soothing drugs given by Chiron to Aesculapius. Later Alexandrus wounds Machaon in the right shoulder with a triple-barbed arrow. Nestor carries him by chariot toward the ships, where Machaon rests in Nestor’s tent needing healing himself.

Machaon in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Machaon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Machaon, son of Æsculapius, was killed by Eurypylus; Nestor recovered his bones, and he has a tomb, temple, sacred place, and bronze statue in Gerenia.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIII.

Machaon, the son of Æsculapius, has a tomb and temple in Gerenia, where his sacred place is called Rhodon and his bronze statue stands upright. The Little Iliad says that Eurypylus killed him, and the tradition says that Nestor recovered his bones.

Machaon in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · about AD 200 to 299 · the work’s page · read it where Machaon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Machaon, a warrior and healer, brother of Podaleirius, attacks Eurypylus over Nireus's death and wounds his shoulder. Eurypylus wounds Machaon, and Danaans fight to rescue and carry him toward the ships. Eurypylus then kills him with a spear; Machaon foretells Eurypylus's imminent death before passing into Hades.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK VI

Machaon attacks Eurypylus in anger over Nireus's death and wounds him in the shoulder. Eurypylus wounds Machaon in the haunch and then kills him by driving a spear through his breast. Machaon foretells Eurypylus's imminent death before his spirit passes into Hades.

By this to Podaleirius tidings came

Machaon is one of the warriors over whom the Danaans fight. The Danaans rescue him and Aglaia's son and carry them toward the ships, though many who attempt the rescue remain surrounded by the battle.
