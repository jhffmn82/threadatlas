the Trojan lament for Hector in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

The Trojan lament is the city’s collective mourning for Hector. It begins when his parents see his body dragged away, with Hecuba and his wife grieving his death, dishonour, and Astyanax’s future. When his body reaches Troy, Andromache, Hecuba, Helen, and the crowd mourn his family, kindness, protection, and importance.

The Trojan lament begins when Hector’s mother and father see his body being dragged away, and it spreads throughout the city. Hecuba and Hector’s wife give extended cries of grief, mourning Hector’s death, his dishonour, and the hardship facing Astyanax.

The lamentation for Hector begins when his body reaches Troy and is led by Andromache, Hecuba, and Helen. The women and the gathered crowd grieve his death, recalling his family relationships, kindness, protection, and importance to the city.
