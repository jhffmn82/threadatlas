Winkies in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04

The Winkies are yellow people living at the Yellow Castle, including tinsmiths and a goldsmith. Enslaved by the Wicked Witch of the West, they must obey her, are sent against Dorothy and her companions, flee the Lion, and are beaten on returning. Dorothy frees them; they celebrate with feasting, dancing, and their holiday. They rescue and repair the Tin Woodman, recover the Scarecrow, offer the Yellow Land of the West to the Tin Woodman, and give gifts to Dorothy and her companions before their departure.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter XII

The Winkies are the slaves of the Wicked Witch of the West and are forced to obey her commands. A dozen Winkies are sent to destroy Dorothy and her companions, but they flee when the Cowardly Lion roars and charges. The Witch beats them when they return and later keeps the castle guarded by yellow Winkies.

Chapter XIII

The Winkies are freed from slavery and celebrate their emancipation with feasting and dancing. They help rescue and repair the Tin Woodman and recover the Scarecrow, then give gifts and good wishes to Dorothy and her companions before they leave.

Winkies in The Road to Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1909

The Winkies inhabit the Country of the Winkies, living among neat yellow houses and scattered farmhouses. Freed by Dorothy after the wicked witch’s defeat, they become skilled tinsmiths, build Nick Chopper’s castle, love him as Emperor, and cheer him with a tin-instrument orchestra.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

15. The Emperor's Tin Castle

The Winkies are described as skilled tinsmiths who made Nick Chopper's castle. They play the non-tin parts of a tin-instrument orchestra, and they gather at the castle gates to cheer their beloved Emperor when he leaves.
