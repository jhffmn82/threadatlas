Arcas

person · 5 works · 26 quoted facts · 9 connections
Also called: Arcas the son of Callisto, Arcas the son of Callisto and Zeus, Arcas, the son of Callisto, Callisto's boy, a youth, her son, his own name, the boy Arcas, the offspring of the daughter of Lycaon, the old Podares, the son of Aphidas, the son of Azan, the son of Callisto
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Arcas is the son of Callisto and Zeus, lives in Lycaeum, is given with his mother to Lycaon, pursues her, and is identified as Bootes, Bear-warden.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Arcas is the son of Calisto and Jupiter who unknowingly nearly spears his transformed mother while hunting, before Jupiter places them among the heavens as the Little Bear.
  • In The Library, Arcas is the spouse of Leanira, Meganira, or Chrysopelia in differing genealogies and the father of Elatus and Aphidas, who divide the land while Elatus holds power.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Arcas is a local hero of Tegea who succeeds Nyctimus as king, teaches agriculture and crafts, divides Arcadia among his legitimate sons, and has his remains brought to Mantinea.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Arcas is Callisto's son who, while hunting, nearly kills his transformed mother before Jupiter rescues them and places them in the heavens; he is the source of the name “Star of Aready.”

Arcas in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Arcas, son of Callisto and a local hero of Tegea, succeeds Nyctimus as king. In his reign he introduces sowing corn and teaches his people bread-making, weaving, and spinning. He has three legitimate sons by the Dryad Nymph Erato, Azan, Aphidas, and Elatus, and an earlier bastard son, Autolaus. When the legitimate sons grow up, he divides Arcadia among them. Afterward, his remains are brought from Mænalus to Mantinea by an oracle at Delphi, where he has a tomb at the altars of the Sun.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IV.

Arcas succeeded Nyctimus as king and introduced sowing corn, breadmaking, weaving, and spinning to his people. He mated with the Dryad Nymph Erato and had Azan, Aphidas, and Elatus by her, as well as an earlier bastard son, Autolaus. When his legitimate sons grew up, he divided the country among them.

CHAPTER IX.

Arcas, the son of Callisto, has a tomb near the altar of Hera at Mantinea. His remains were brought from Mænalus to Mantinea in accordance with an oracle at Delphi, and the place of his tomb is called the altars of the Sun.
