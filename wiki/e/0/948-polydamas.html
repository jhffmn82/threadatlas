Polydamas in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Polydamas is a Trojan warrior, son of Panthous, comrade of Cleitus, and a man of judgment among the Trojans. He commands Trojans and allies with Hector and Cebriones, chiefly acting as Hector’s adviser: he urges an attack on foot rather than across the trench with horses, interprets an eagle and snake as warning of costly failure, and later urges withdrawal and consultation with chieftains. In battle he avenges Satnius by wounding Prothoenor, retreats from Ajax, then sees Cleitus fall, assigns Cleitus’s horses to Astynous, returns to the front, kills Otus, and escapes Meges.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XII.

Polydamas advises the Trojans to leave their horses at the trench and attack the wall on foot. After seeing the eagle drop the snake, he interprets the sign as a warning that the Trojans may break through but will suffer losses and fail to return in order.

Thus then did they fight as it were a flaming fire. But Hector

Polydamas criticizes Hector for believing that excellence in war makes him superior in counsel. He advises Hector to withdraw and gather the chieftains to decide whether to attack the ships or retreat, then gathers the leaders as Hector ordered.

Sleep now departed and went his ways to and fro among mankind,

Polydamas son of Panthous avenged Satnius by wounding Prothoenor and boasting over him. He then tried to retreat from Ajax son of Telamon's attack, causing Ajax's spear to strike Archelochus instead.

Now Patroclus, so long as the Achaeans and Trojans were fighting

Polydamas sees Cleitus fall, gives Cleitus’s horses to Astynous, and returns to the front ranks. He kills Otus of Cyllene, evades Meges, and causes Meges’s spear to strike Croesmus instead.
