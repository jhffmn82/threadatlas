Achilles' withdrawal from the war in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Achilles' withdrawal from the Trojan War follows his quarrel with Agamemnon and the taking of Briseis. He returns with Patroclus and his company to his tents and ships, refuses to fight for the Achaeans, and predicts they will seek him vainly in suffering. He then prepares to sail his provisioned ships toward Phthia, if granted fair passage.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK I.

Achilles declares that he will return to Phthia and later swears that the Achaeans will seek him in vain when they are suffering. After Agamemnon takes Briseis, Achilles goes back to his tents and ships with Patroclus and his company. He refuses to fight for the Achaeans unless they later seek him and cannot find him.

Achilles plans to sacrifice to Jove and the other gods, draw his ships into the water, provision them, and sail toward Phthia if Neptune grants a fair passage. He says he may be there in three days and invites Phoenix to accompany him, though he will not force him.
