toys in The Life and Adventures of Santa Claus

L. Frank Baum · 1902

Toys are Santa Claus's gifts for children, made and distributed as dolls, animal figures, drums, cymbals, whistles, and horns. As the number of children grows, Claus prepares larger yearly loads so no poor or rich child goes without a Christmas gift. He leaves the pretty, wonderful toys by sleeping children's bedsides, bringing happiness to children and their families, though parents wonder how he enters homes. After the Awgwas steal and hide them in a mountain cave, the Fairies recover them in time for Claus to load his sledge and deliver them.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

8. The First Journey with the Reindeer

Claus makes and distributes dolls, animal figures, drums, cymbals, whistles, and horns. He leaves toys for sleeping children during his journeys until his supply is exhausted.

9. "Santa Claus!"

The toys Claus leaves by children's bedsides are described as pretty and wonderful. Parents say that they are the only toys in all the world made by the good Claus. Their appearance causes children and their parents to wonder how Claus entered the homes.

10. Christmas Eve

The Awgwas had stolen Claus's toys and hidden them in a cave beneath rocks in the mountains. The Fairies recover the toys just in time for Claus to load them onto his sledge and give them to children.

2. When the World Grew Old

Santa Claus decides that no child, poor or rich, should go without a Christmas gift if he can manage to supply one. The growing number of children requires him to prepare larger yearly loads of gifts.
