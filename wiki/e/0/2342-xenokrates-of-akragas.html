Xenokrates of Akragas

person · 1 work · 19 quoted facts · 9 connections
Also called: Xenokrates, a light of the people of Akragas, a man of goodly chariot, same winner

Abstract written by a model

Xenokrates of Akragas is a chariot-race victor and celebrated citizen. Son of Ainesidamos, brother of Theron, and father of Thrasyboulos, he wins at the Isthmian Games, receiving Poseidon’s Dorian parsley wreath, and gains glory at Krisa, Athens, and Olympia. An ode builds him a treasure of song in Apollo’s valley, honoring his parental devotion and family ties. He is praised as Akragas’s light, a supporter of horse-racing, a festival worshipper, and an exceptionally generous host.

VI.

Xenokrates is honored as the winner of the chariot-race victory celebrated in the ode. A ready treasure of song is built for him in Apollo's valley, and the poem praises his observance of parental honor and his connection with Thrasyboulos.

Isthmian Ode II

Xenokrates won the Isthmian chariot-race, for which Poseidon gave him a Dorian parsley wreath. He also gained glory at Krisa, Athens, and Olympia, and is praised as a distinguished citizen of Akragas, supporter of horse-racing, worshipper at festivals, and generous host.
