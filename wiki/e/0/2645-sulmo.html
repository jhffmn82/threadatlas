Sulmo in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

Sulmo is the speaker’s native place, a small, salubrious third part of Pelignian land. Watery walls and flowing streams surround its few acres, with fertile fields, vines, olives, and refreshing air. Though a stranger may see it as little, it is called great for producing the speaker, a great Poet.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ELEGY XVI.

Sulmo, described as a small and salubrious place, receives the speaker. It is presented as part of the Pelignian land and is surrounded by flowing streams, fertile fields, vines, olives, and refreshing air.

ELEGY X.

Sulmo is described as watery and occupying only a few acres of land. A stranger is imagined looking at its walls and calling it great because it produced so great a Poet.

Sulmo in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Sulmo is a small, wholesome, fertile town in the Paelignian fields, watered by streams and enclosed by walls. It is Ovid’s hometown and the speaker’s setting; though its waters and fields are praised, it cannot please him while his beloved is distant.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

XV

Sulmo holds the speaker in the third part of the Paelignian fields. Although it is fertile and watered, it does not satisfy him because his beloved is far away.

XII

Sulmo is Ovid's watery hometown, whose walls enclose only a few acres of plain. Ovid imagines a stranger praising the small town for having produced so great a poet.
