the ring in A Study in Scarlet

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1886

A plain gold woman’s wedding-ring is taken by Jefferson Hope from Lucy Ferrier’s dead finger and carried as he pursues Enoch Drebber and Joseph Stangerson. Hope uses it to remind Drebber of Lucy as Drebber dies, but drops it while leaning over the body and later returns to recover it. Found in the roadway near Brixton Road, it becomes an unexplained clue. Holmes uses a facsimile in an advertisement trap; the ring is claimed by Sally Dennis in Mrs. Sawyer’s story.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER III.

A plain gold wedding-ring rolls out when Enoch J. Drebber’s body is lifted. Lestrade identifies it as a woman’s wedding-ring, which Tobias Gregson says complicates the case.

CHAPTER IV.

The woman’s ring was found at the scene and is one of the unexplained facts troubling Doctor Watson. Holmes believes the second suspect returned to the house to recover it and may use it to catch him.

CHAPTER V.

The wedding ring was found near Brixton Road after Enoch J. Drebber’s murder, and Sherlock Holmes believes the murderer dropped it while leaning over the body. Holmes uses a facsimile ring to support the advertisement trap and gives it to the disguised visitor.

CHAPTER VI.

Jefferson Hope takes Lucy Ferrier's marriage ring from her dead finger and carries it while pursuing Enoch Drebber and Joseph Stangerson. He holds it before Drebber's eyes as Drebber dies, but later discovers that it is missing and returns to the house to search for it.
