Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Iolaus drives Arion as Heracles’ charioteer and fellow warrior against Cycnus and Ares. In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Iolaus is the aged guardian of Hercules’s children who seeks Athens’s protection, accepts Macaria’s sacrifice, is renewed by divine aid, and captures Eurystheus. In Hecuba and other plays, Iolaus protects Hercules’s children as an aged exile, accepts Macaria’s sacrifice, regains youthful strength through divine favor, and captures Eurystheus. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Iolaus leads the Thespiadae to Sardinia, conquers and settles the island, establishes its institutions, and later helps command the Heraclid forces. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Iolaüs, son of Iphiclus and brother of Hercules, is restored from old age toward boyhood by Hebe’s divine favor. In The Library, Iolaus drives Hercules to Lerna and helps him defeat the hydra by burning the roots of its severed heads.

Iolaus in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · 437 BC · the work’s page · read it where Iolaus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Iolaus is the aged son of Iphiclus, nephew and companion of Hercules, and elder guardian of Hercules’s children. Exiled and unsafe because his country is gone, he brings the children as suppliants to the gods’ altars in Marathon and urges Demophoon and Athens to protect them through kinship, obligation, and justice. He remains with them in fear of expulsion, proposes a lot among Macaria and her sisters, and accepts Macaria’s voluntary death. When Hyllus arrives for battle against Argos, Iolaus insists on fighting despite his weakened body. Through divine aid after prayers to Juno and Jupiter, he is made young for one day, fights gloriously, captures Eurystheus at the Scironian rocks, binds him, and brings him to Alcmena.

Iolaus in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · about 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Iolaus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Iolaus is Hercules’ companion and comrade, an aged kinsman, friend, and warrior who becomes the surviving guardian of Hercules’ children after Hercules’ death. Though physically weakened and himself exiled from Argos, he travels with and protects the children, bringing them to Jupiter’s altar at Marathon and pleading with Demophoon and the Athenians against the Argive threat. He fears the oracle’s demand for a noble-born maiden, accepts Macaria’s voluntary sacrifice, and refuses to abandon the suppliants. When battle approaches, he arms himself, rides with Hyllus, prays to Hebe and Zeus, regains youthful strength through divine favor, pursues Eurystheus, and captures him alive near Scyron.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

THE CHILDREN OF HERCULES.

Iolaus protects the children of Hercules after their father’s death, sharing their exile despite needing protection himself. At Marathon, he brings them to the altar of Jupiter and appeals to Demophoon and the Athenians to prevent their forcible surrender. He refuses to leave the altar until Demophoon has safely resisted the Argive threat.

DEMOPHOON, IOLAUS, CHORUS.

Iolaus learns that the oracles demand the sacrifice of a noble-born maiden and fears that the suppliants will be surrendered. He proposes that the sisters draw lots, but accepts Macaria’s voluntary choice to die. When Hyllus’s attendant announces the approaching battle, Iolaus obtains armor and insists on going to fight despite his weakened body.

SERVANT, ALCMENA, CHORUS.

Iolaus asks Hyllus to place him in his chariot, takes the reins, and pursues Eurystheus. After praying to Hebe and Zeus, he temporarily regains his youthful strength, captures Eurystheus near the rocks of Scyron, and brings him back alive.

Iolaus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Iolaus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Iolaus is Hercules’s nephew, associate, captain, and companion in expeditions. After Hercules’s tenth labour, he led the Thespiadae colony to Sardinia, defeated its inhabitants, took possession of the island, founded cities, divided fertile land among settlers, and established public schools, temples, and other monuments. The people were named Iolaeians after him, and colonists in Sardinia and Sicily honored him as a father and demi-god. After Hercules’s translation to the gods, Iolaus helped command Heraclid forces with Theseus and Hyllus against Eurystheus, then went with the Heraclidae and allies from Trachina, later returning toward Greece and Sicily.
