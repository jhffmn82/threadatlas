training framework in Retrieval Augmented Generation with Rich Answer Encoding

Wenyu Huang, Mirella Lapata, Pavlos Vougiouklis, Nikos Papasarantopoulos, Jeff Z. Pan · 2023

This training framework for knowledge-intensive generation tasks trains a retriever to seek knowledge-relevant passages rather than only label-relevant ones, aiming for greater robustness and generalization. It uses RAG as a retrieve-generate encoder-decoder backbone and combines generation loss with two knowledge-constrained objectives: Retriever as Answer Classifier (RAC) and Dense Knowledge Similarity (DKS). In multi-task training, it optimizes generation, answer-classification, and knowledge-similarity losses, with a weight vector computed at each step so the losses have the same value. It is evaluated in retrieval, end-to-end generation, combined evaluation, and robustness, against RAG and non-finetuned DPR. The reported methods improve retrieval and generation metrics on MSMARCO and KILT Wizard of Wikipedia, outperform RAG in retrieval, and show lower overfitting, especially on KILT-WoW. Reported KILT-WoW results include 13.3% relative improvement on KILT-RL and 13.2% on KILT-F1 over baseline RAG. By guiding retrieval toward knowledge-relevant passages, it mitigates overfitting and can reduce hallucinations when training retrievers for large language models.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Problem Statement

The training framework focuses on retrieving knowledge-relevant passages rather than only label-relevant passages. It is intended to train the retriever more robustly and improve its generalization ability.

Methodology / Retrieve-Generate Backbone .. Overall Architecture

The proposed model combines the retrieve-generate backbone with Retriever as Answer Classifier and Dense Knowledge Similarity in a multi-task setting. It optimizes the sum of generation, answer-classification, and knowledge-similarity losses, using a weight vector to balance them.

Experimental Setup / Datasets .. Comparison Models

They are evaluated for effectiveness in retrieval and end-to-end generation and for robustness in retrieving relevant passages. They include RAC, DKS, and multi-task variants, which are compared with RAG and DPR.

Results / RQ1: Effectiveness / Generative QA .. Case Studies

The proposed methods consistently improve retrieval metrics over RAG and non-finetuned DPR, with corresponding gains in end-to-end and combined evaluations. They also show lower overfit degrees than RAG and achieve 13.3% relative improvement on KILT-RL and 13.2% on KILT-F1 in the reported KILT-WoW leaderboard results.

Conclusion

The training framework uses the knowledge-constrained objectives RAC and DKS to guide the retriever toward knowledge-relevant passages rather than only label-relevant passages. It also considerably mitigates overfitting caused by focusing on label-relevant passages.
