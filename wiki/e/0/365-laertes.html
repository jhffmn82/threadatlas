Laertes in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Laertes is Ulysses’ aged father, a former ruler of the Cephallenians who lives sorrowfully on the country farm and house he reclaimed through labor. Distressed by Ulysses’ absence and his wife’s death, he prays to die peacefully at home. Ulysses recognizes and reunites with him through a scar and knowledge of the vineyard; despite old age, Laertes joins Ulysses in the Ithacan civil conflict and kills Eupeithes.

Laertes is still living and prays to die peacefully in his own house. He is deeply distressed by Ulysses’ absence and by the death of his wife, and he once gave goods to obtain Eumaeus.

Laertes is found alone, aged and sorrowful, hoeing a vine at his farm. After Ulysses proves his identity by showing his scar and naming the vineyard trees, Laertes embraces him and later fights beside him, killing Eupeithes with a spear.
