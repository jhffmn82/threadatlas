Mortal excellence in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

Mortal excellence, pursued with wealth and toil, earns praise and public recognition when achieved; it comes from Zeus, shifts with fortune and craft, yet noble deeds can preserve reputation beyond mortal life.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Isthmian Ode I

Valour is presented as worthy of high praise when a person pursues it through wealth and toil and succeeds. Those who achieve it deserve generous public recognition in words.

Isthmian Ode III

Mortal excellence comes from Zeus and is subject to changing fortune. The ode says that even the better man may be overtaken by a worse through craft, while noble deeds can preserve a person's reputation beyond mortal life.
