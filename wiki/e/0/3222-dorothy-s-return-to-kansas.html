Dorothy's return to Kansas in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04

Dorothy’s return to Kansas is her central goal: she wants to go home to Aunt Em and Uncle Henry, believing people prefer their own homes to even beautiful foreign countries. She asks the Great Oz to send her back, but Oz first refuses and later delays while considering how to carry her across the desert. Dorothy and her companions then continue toward the Land of the South, crossing fields and a forest, overcoming attacking trees, and reaching a china wall. At last, Dorothy says goodbye to Glinda the Good Witch and her companions, uses the Silver Shoes, and arrives with Toto on the Kansas prairie near Uncle Henry’s new farmhouse.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter IV

Dorothy says that people of flesh and blood would rather live in their own homes than in any other country, however beautiful, because there is no place like home.

Chapter X

Dorothy hopes that the Great Oz will find Kansas and send her there because it is her home. In this unit, she has not yet returned to Kansas.

Chapter XI

Dorothy wants Oz to send her back to Kansas, where Aunt Em and Uncle Henry are. Oz refuses to help her return until she kills the Wicked Witch of the West, so she prepares to travel with her companions to seek out the Witch.

Chapter XV

Dorothy's return to Kansas remains unresolved in this unit. Oz asks for two or three days to consider how to carry her over the desert and promises to try to find a way.

Chapter XIX

Dorothy and her companions continue their journey toward the Land of the South so she can return to Kansas. They travel through fields and a forest, overcome the attacking trees, and reach a white china wall that blocks their way.

Chapter XXIII

Dorothy says goodbye to Glinda the Good Witch and her companions before using the Silver Shoes to return to Kansas. She arrives on the broad Kansas prairie near Uncle Henry’s new farmhouse with Toto.
