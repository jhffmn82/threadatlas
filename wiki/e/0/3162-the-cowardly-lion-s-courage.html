the Cowardly Lion's courage in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04

The Cowardly Lion’s courage is identified as facing danger while afraid, which Oz says he already possesses. Wanting fearlessness, the Lion receives liquid courage from Oz, drinks it, and believes he is full of courage.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter XV

Oz explains that true courage means facing danger while afraid and says the Cowardly Lion already has that kind of courage. Because the Lion still wants confidence that will make him forget fear, Oz promises to give him courage.

Chapter XVI

Oz presents the Lion's courage as liquid from a square green bottle. The Lion drinks it and believes that he is full of courage.
