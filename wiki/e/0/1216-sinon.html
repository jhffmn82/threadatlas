Sinon

person · 4 works · 14 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Far-famed Sinon, a hapless-seeming man, a man far-famed, that man of guileful soul, the miscreant, the villain, Σίνων, Σίνωνα
  • In The Aeneid, Sinon, a Greek captive, deceives the Trojans with tears and a fabricated sacrifice story, persuades them to admit the wooden horse, and opens it at night.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Sinon secretly enters Ilium by pretence and raises the arranged fire-signal, bringing Greek ships from Tenedos and beginning the attack.
  • In The Library, Sinon, a Greek signal-bearer left behind after the Greeks sail away, lights a beacon from Achilles’ tomb to signal their approach from Tenedos.
  • In The Fall of Troy, Sinon remains with the Wooden Horse to sustain the Achaeans’ deception, endures Trojan mutilation and torture while falsely claiming it was an offering and the Achaeans had sailed away, then signals the waiting Danaans with a blazing torch and calls the hidden warriors out.

Sinon in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

A Greek captive who deceives the Trojans with tears and a fabricated sacrifice story, persuades them to admit the wooden horse, then opens it at night.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK II

Sinon allows himself to be captured and falsely claims that the Greeks intended to sacrifice him before fleeing. He tells the Trojans that the wooden horse was built to reconcile Minerva and that Troy will be destroyed if it is not admitted.

Sinon in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

Sinon, an Achaean volunteer and Greek agent, remains with the Wooden Horse to sustain the Achaeans’ deception. He endures Trojan mutilation and torture while falsely claiming the Horse was an offering and the Achaeans had sailed away. After the Trojans sleep, he signals the waiting Danaans with a blazing torch and calls the hidden warriors out.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XII

Sinon volunteers to remain beside the Wooden Horse and endure torture rather than reveal the Achaeans' plan. The Trojans mutilate and torture him, but he repeats the false story that the Achaeans sailed away and left the Horse as an offering to Athena.

BOOK XIII

Sinon raises a blazing torch after the Trojans fall asleep, signaling the Argive forces waiting at sea. He then calls softly to the warriors inside the Trojan Horse.
