  • In Hecuba and other plays, the Grecian companions are eaten at Polyphemus's feast, while the survivors hide and help Ulysses escape.
  • In The Odyssey, Ulysses's men are his ship's companions and crew, sharing his voyage, suffering losses, and ultimately dying after slaughtering the Sun-god's cattle.
  • In The Library, the companions of Odysseus taste lotus, suffer deaths and transformations, are restored by Circe, use wax against the Sirens, and slaughter the Sun's cattle.

Ulysses's men in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Ulysses’s men are the companions and crew of his ship, commanded by Ulysses. Their course is marked by shared labor, repeated losses, and fatal folly. They raid Ismarus and divide booty fairly, but refuse to leave when ordered, suffer losses against the Cicons, and some eat lotus and lose the desire to return. They escape the Cyclops after warning Ulysses against further provocation, mourn lost comrades, and continue sailing. Suspicious that an ox-hide sack holds treasure, they open it while Ulysses sleeps. After six windless days, Laestrygonians kill all men in the harbor except those aboard Ulysses’s ship. On Circe’s island, some are changed into pigs but retain human senses; Circe restores them, and the company remains eating and drinking for a year. They later urge return home, follow orders toward Hades, bury Elpenor, seal their ears with wax, bind Ulysses to the mast, and row past the Sirens. Finally, despite the danger and their oath, they slaughter the Sun-god’s cattle. Jove destroys their ship, and the surviving crew are killed in the shipwreck.

companions of Odysseus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Odysseus’s companions taste lotus and are dragged back; some are eaten by Polyphemus or swept away; twenty-two are transformed and restored by Circe, later use wax and slaughter the Sun’s cattle.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

VII. ‘O δὲ Ὀδυσσεύς, ὡς μὲν ἔνιοι λέγουσιν,

Odysseus's companions taste the lotus and must be dragged back to the ships. Some are eaten by Polyphemus, others are swept away when the winds are released, and twenty-two companions who go to Circe are transformed into animals before being restored.
