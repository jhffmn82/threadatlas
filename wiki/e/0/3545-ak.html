Ak in The Life and Adventures of Santa Claus

L. Frank Baum · 1902

Ak is the immortal Master Woodsman of the World: wise, farseeing, grizzled-bearded, and armed with a gleaming ax. He sees everything, knows more than mortals, makes and enforces the Law, and rules or oversees forests, orchards, and groves throughout the world. He guards Burzee, the Great Black Forest, and the wooded wilderness of Braz, while acknowledging that people will clear forests for settlement. He loves the wood-nymphs as a father loves children and sometimes aids helpless human children. Ak’s central concern becomes Claus. He rescues or protects endangered infants, including the newborn whom Necile takes despite his Law. Discovering her with the child, Ak relaxes the Law for the first and final time, permits the adoption, promises protection, and decrees that beasts and reptiles must not molest Claus. As Claus grows, Ak examines him, finds him brave and innocent, and becomes his master, teacher, guide, and friend. He takes Claus through the world, teaches him about mortals, unequal childhoods, his own babyhood, and the purpose of helpful lives, then returns him safely to Burzee. Ak continues to secure Claus’s work and safety. He wins the favor of the field Ryls, declares Claus protected by the immortals, organizes the immortals against the Awgwas, kills their king with his ax, and announces their defeat. When questions arise about the deer that draw Claus’s sledge, Ak gathers leading immortals, hears confirmation that Glossie and Flossie serve willingly, and persuades the immortals to support Claus. In Claus’s old age, Ak convenes the council after learning he is near death, argues that his love for little children merits immortality, and persuades the council to grant the Mantle of Immortality. He then explains this gift to Santa Claus and provides Kilter, the silent and swift, to help him continue his work.

Narrative, section by section

10 sections; each heading opens the text

2. The Child of the Forest

Ak is the Master Woodsman of the world and is said to see everything and know more than the sons of men. He sometimes rescues helpless human children from misery, including four children saved from freezing and a newborn infant threatened by a lioness. He orders Shiegra to protect the infant and give it milk until he can attend to other matters.

3. The Adoption

Ak discovers Necile holding the sleeping infant and recognizes that she has defied his Law. He permits Necile to keep the infant and promises to protect it, while declaring that this is the final time he will relax the Law.

4. Claus

Ak sanctions the nymphs' adoption of the human infant. His decree forbids beasts and reptiles from molesting Claus, allowing him to walk fearlessly through the forest.

5. The Master Woodsman

Ak returns to Burzee after visiting his many forests throughout the world. He studies Claus's face and is relieved to find his eyes placid and his heart brave and innocent. He calls Claus aside and tells him to accompany him on a journey through the world.

6. Claus Discovers Humanity

Ak takes Claus around the world to show him the haunts and lives of human beings. He explains the differences between mortals and immortals, the unequal conditions of children, Claus's babyhood, and the purpose of helpful lives. He returns Claus safely to the Forest of Burzee.

MANHOOD / 1. The Laughing Valley

Ak has spoken to the Ryls of the Field Flowers about Claus. Although Ak has no command over the field Ryls because he is not of the Forest, they are willing to favor Claus because Ak loves him.

7. The Great Battle Between Good and Evil

Ak tells the Awgwas that Claus is under the protection of the immortals and orders them not to harm him. When the Awgwa King declares war and threatens to kill Claus, Ak organizes the immortals for battle, kills the King Awgwa with his ax, and later tells Claus that the Awgwas have perished.

10. Christmas Eve

Ak hears Claus's account and summons the Prince of the Knooks, the Fairy Queen, and the Prince of the Ryls to consider the deer’s punishment and service. He persuades the immortals to support Claus and calls Glossie and Flossie to confirm that they willingly drew the sledge.

OLD AGE / 1. The Mantle of Immortality

Ak summons a council of the immortals after Necile tells him that Claus is near death. He argues that Claus deserves immortality and persuades the council to bestow the Mantle of Immortality upon him.

2. When the World Grew Old

Ak tells Santa Claus about the Mantle of Immortality and explains that Santa Claus won it through his love for little children. He gives Santa Claus his man Kilter, known as 'the silent and swift,' to help with the work. Ak explains that he guarded the forests until people needed them and hopes that they will not cut down every tree.

Master Woodsman in The Life and Adventures of Santa Claus

L. Frank Baum · 1902

The Master Woodsman of the World holds authority over the Forest’s protection, his word being law. He grants and preserves Claus’s protection and powers, guides him mercifully toward and after his new home among men, promises other beings’ service, and provides support through Nelko, an ax, Fairies, supplies, and clothing.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

7. Claus Leaves the Forest

Ak confirms that Claus has spoken well and declares that Claus will retain the Forest's protection and his existing powers after entering the world of men. Ak also promises that the Nymphs, Ryls, Knooks, and Fairies will serve Claus when he calls upon them, and he guides Claus's steps after he leaves.

MANHOOD / 1. The Laughing Valley

The Master Woodsman sends his servant Nelko with a strong, bright ax for Claus. He also sends the Fairies to provide Claus with household supplies and clothing.
