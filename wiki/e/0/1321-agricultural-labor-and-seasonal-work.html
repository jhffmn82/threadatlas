agricultural labor and seasonal work in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Agricultural labor and seasonal work is a cycle governed by seasonal signs and calendar guidance. It demands timely effort, weather-appropriate clothing, oxen for ploughing, and early rising at harvest, rewarding industry. Across the year it directs ploughing, sowing, pruning, harvesting, winnowing, storage, timber-cutting, vine and animal care, shearing, wine-opening, and work on ships when sailing is unsafe.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

(ll. 383-404) When the Pleiades, daughters of Atlas, are rising 1310,

Agricultural labor must follow seasonal signs, including the rising and setting of the Pleiades, the crane’s call, the cuckoo’s call, and the appearances of Arcturus, the swallow, Orion, and Sirius. The work includes ploughing, sowing, pruning, harvesting, winnowing, storing grain, cutting timber, and tending vines and animals.

(ll. 618-640) But if desire for uncomfortable sea-faring seize you;

When sailing is unsafe, the speaker tells Perses to till the land. He gives seasonal and calendar guidance for sowing, planting, reaping, shearing sheep, cutting timber, building ships, opening wine-jars, and putting yokes on animals.
