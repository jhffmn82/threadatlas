Eleusis

place · 5 works · 5 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Eleusinian territory, city, fragrant Eleusis, rocky hold of Eleusis, stronghold of fragrant Eleusis, ᾿Ελευσῖνα, ᾿Ελευσῖνος

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Eleusis is Celeus’s stronghold with the Maiden Well and Demeter’s temple, where Demeter commands a temple and altar and teaches her rites and mysteries. In The Library, Eleusis is where Demeter was welcomed by Celeus and where Eumolpus fled, later becoming involved in war against the Athenians. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Eleusis is the Attic place dedicated especially to Ceres, where she rested while seeking Proserpine and where Triptolemus established her mysteries and worship. In The Library, Eleusis is where Demeter arrives in human form, sits on the Laughless Rock, and goes to Celeus’s house, and where Hercules visits Eumolpus to be initiated. In Description of Greece, Eleusis is a city with temples, the Well of Callichorus, and the Rharian plain, associated with Athens after a battle while retaining its Mysteries.

Eleusis, ruled by Celeus, is a place with a rocky stronghold, the Maiden Well, and Demeter’s temple. Demeter arrives disguised, its women draw water, and she commands a temple and altar; after fertility returns, she teaches its rites and mysteries.

Eleusis in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Eleusis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Eleusis is a city named for the hero Eleusis, with temples, the Well of Callichorus, and the Rharian plain; after battle it became associated with Athens while retaining its Mysteries.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXVIII.

Eleusis contained temples to Triptolemus, Propylæan Artemis, and Father Poseidon, as well as the Well of Callichorus and the Rharian plain, which tradition called the first land sown and cultivated. Its name was attributed by different traditions to a hero called Eleusis, and it became associated with Athens after the battle with the Athenians while retaining its Mysteries.
