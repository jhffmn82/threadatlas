Palamedes

person · 3 works · 11 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Παλαμήδην, Παλαμήδης

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Palamedes exposes Odysseus’s feigned madness by seizing Telemachus, then is drowned while fishing by Odysseus and Diomedes. In The Library, Palamedes exposes Odysseus’s feigned madness, is framed for treason by Ulysses and Agamemnon, and is stoned to death, leading Nauplius to seek revenge on the Greeks. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Palamedes invents dice, is depicted playing them with Actæon, Salaminian Ajax, and Thersites, and is drowned while fishing by Diomede and Odysseus.

Palamedes in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Palamedes first appears

Abstract written by a model

Palamedes, son of Nauplius and Clymene, exposes Odysseus’s feigned madness by threatening Telemachus, forcing Odysseus to reveal himself. Later, Ulysses and Agamemnon arrange false evidence of treason, a letter from Priam and hidden gold, and Palamedes is handed to the allies and stoned to death. His death leads Nauplius to seek revenge on the Greeks.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Tz 15 «Τὸν δ᾽ ᾿Αγαμέμνονα ὃ τροφὸς μετὰ τοῦ Μενελάου

Palamedes, son of Nauplius, exposed Odysseus’s feigned madness. He followed Odysseus while he pretended to rave and threatened Telemachus with a sword, causing Odysseus to reveal the deception.

\"O7 ᾿Οδυσσεὺς λαβὼν αἰχμάλωτον Φρύγα

Palamedes is falsely implicated in treason when a letter supposedly from Priam and hidden gold are discovered. Agamemnon hands him over to the allies to be stoned as a traitor.

ἔμελλον ἀποπλεῖν πορθήσαντες Τροίαν, ὑ ὑπὸ Κάλ-

Palamedes, the son of Nauplius and Clymene, was stoned to death through the machinations of Ulysses, with Agamemnon as his accomplice. His death prompted Nauplius to seek satisfaction from the Greeks and to take revenge.

Palamedes in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Palamedes first appears

Abstract written by a model

Palamedes invents dice, is depicted playing them with Actæon, Salaminian Ajax, and Thersites without a beard, and is drowned while fishing by Diomede and Odysseus.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXI.

Palamedes is shown playing dice with Actæon, Salaminian Ajax, and Thersites; the text says that he invented dice. The Cyprian Poems state that Diomede and Odysseus drowned him while he was fishing, and he is the only one of this group without a beard.
