the Argive host’s return in The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies

Aeschylus · -0457

The Argive host’s return is the hoped-for safe completion of the Greek expedition after the Trojan War. Though Troy is conquered, the homecoming remains incomplete until the forces reach home: only a remnant survives war and storm, many households receive death, and Menelaus is missing as Agamemnon returns victorious.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

AGAMEMNON

The expected homecoming of Agamemnon is treated as a cause for celebration, but the Chorus warns that the Greek forces must reach home safely before their success is complete. The unit also emphasizes that many households receive only ashes, weapons, or news of death instead of a living warrior.

the voyage from Troy in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

The voyage from Troy is a hazardous return marked by divine demands, delays, storms, and uncertain homecoming. Menelaus is held by gods and wind near Pharos, learns from Proteus to sacrifice in Egypt, then gains a fair wind and returns after nearly eight years through many lands. Ulysses’s party reaches Ismarus, is driven past Cape Malea and Cythera for nine days, and arrives among the Lotus-eaters; his return remains unfinished under Calypso.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK IV

Menelaus says that it took him nearly eight years to return home after traveling through many lands. He contrasts his difficult return with Odysseus’s continued absence and uncertainty about whether Odysseus is alive.

When the child of morning, rosy-fingered Dawn appeared, Menelaus rose

Menelaus’s voyage home is delayed first by the gods and then by a lack of wind near Pharos. After receiving instructions from Proteus, offering sacrifices in Egypt, and appeasing the gods, Menelaus makes a quick passage home with a fair wind. Ulysses’s voyage home remains unfinished because Calypso holds him on an island.

BOOK IX

The voyage from Troy takes Ulysses and his men first to Ismarus, then off course through storms and currents near Cape Malea and Cythera. After nine days of being driven across the sea, they reach the land of the Lotus-eaters.
