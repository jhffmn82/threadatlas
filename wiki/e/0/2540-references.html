  • In Retrieval-Augmented Controllable Review Generation: References are review texts drawn from a training-dataset pool labeled by attributes. Each is a token sequence written by a user or for a product, including other users’ reviews of the same product. They are intended to resemble the generated review syntactically and semantically, supplying context, attribute-related information, frequently discussed aspects such as plot and character, habitual phrases, and rating-related language that identifiers alone may not provide. Because many candidate references can be irrelevant or noisy and can hinder output, REFLECT reduces N candidates through attribute-based coarse-grained matching and fine-grained relevance scoring, selecting an optimal K. Pseudo-supervision uses lexical similarity and rating accuracy, while selection can use attribute or textual features. RL-REFLECT can select or compose documents so different references contribute different aspects, evaluating their aggregate value through generation. GEN encodes selected references with a bidirectional LSTM and attention pooling, contextualizes them with self-attention, aggregates them with attention, and uses them during decoding, including copying words. Retrieved reviews can improve attribute-specific generation, but references lacking rating-semantic relevance may conflict with the target rating. Overall, they guide review generation by enriching inductive biases and context.

Narrative, section by section

9 sections; each heading opens the text

Abstract

References are selected from a large pool of texts labeled with one of the attributes. They provide textual information that enriches attribute inductive biases and are intended to be syntactically and semantically similar to the output text.

Introduction

Reference reviews provide inductive biases for generation, including frequently discussed product aspects such as plot and character and habitual user phrases such as “looking forward to the next book.”

opening .. Augmenting context using references

References provide textual context and introduce attribute-related information that may not be available from identifiers alone. Useful references can have ratings different from the input rating while still containing relevant rating-related language.

AT2T .. SL-REFLECT

References are reviews from the training dataset that are written by the user or written for the product. Because many references can be irrelevant, REFLECT selects an optimal subset of K references.

Relevance Pseudo-Supervision .. Fine-Grained REFLECT

Reference candidates are scored for relevance using lexical similarity and rating accuracy during pseudo-supervision, and using attribute or textual features during REFLECT selection. Coarse-Grained REFLECT reduces the original set of N candidates to M candidates before fine-grained scoring selects K references.

RL-REFLECT .. Stabilizing Training of RL

Reference documents can be selected individually or composed so that different documents contribute different aspects, such as character, plot, or rating-related language. RL-REFLECT samples sets of references from a candidate pool and evaluates their aggregate contribution through generation.

GEN .. Training GEN

References are encoded with a bidirectional LSTM and attention pooling, then further contextualized with self-attention and aggregated with another attention layer. GEN uses their representations during decoding and can copy words from them.

Experiments .. Analysis on Rating Consistency

Reference reviews are retrieved from existing reviews and supplied to GEN. They provide attribute-specific terms, but references selected without sufficient rating-semantic relevance can contain information inconsistent with the target rating.

Conclusion

They guide review generation in the work.
