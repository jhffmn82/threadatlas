  • In The Aeneid, Priam is Troy’s aged former king who sees Pyrrhus kill Polites, condemns Pyrrhus, and is killed at the sacred altar.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Priam urges the restoration of Hesione and the promised horses, aids Iphiclus and Telamon, and becomes king of Troy after Hercules takes the city.
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Priam is the Trojan king whose city and household the Greeks destroy, and whom they kill and leave uninterred.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Priam is Troy’s wealthy king who secretly sends Polydore to Polymestor and is killed at an altar after Troy falls.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Priam flees to the altar of Zeus Herceius during the sack of Ilium, where Neoptolemus kills him.
  • In The Iliad, Priam is the aged king of Troy who ransoms Hector’s body from Achilles and directs his burial rites.
  • In The Library, Priam, formerly called Podarces, becomes king of Ilium, fathers many children, ransoms Hector’s body, and is killed by Neoptolemus during the sack of Troy.
  • In The Library, Podarces, Laomedon’s son, becomes king of Ilium after Heracles captures it and is called Priam.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Priam is Hecuba’s deceased husband and king of Troy who is killed at Jupiter’s altar before seeing Polyxena cut off.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Priam is depicted among the dead after Neoptolemus tears him from Household Zeus’s altar and kills him.
  • In The Fall of Troy, Priam is Troy’s aged king who defends the city, mourns its dead, and is beheaded beside Zeus the Hearth-lord’s altar during the sack.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Priam is the old king of Troy who ransoms Hector’s body and is killed by Pyrrhus at Jupiter’s altar after Troy falls.

Priam in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Priam, Troy’s aged, declining former king and ruler of Asia, is father of Polites. During the Greeks’ assault on his palace, he arms himself, sees Pyrrhus kill Polites, condemns Pyrrhus, and is killed at the sacred altar.

Priam in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Priam, son of Laomedon and brother of Hesione, is characterized by justice and equity. When Laomedon refuses to honor his agreement with Hercules, Priam urges that Hesione and the promised horses be restored. Ignored, he secretly aids Iphiclus and Telamon and exposes Laomedon’s plan. After Hercules takes Troy, he makes Priam king. Priam rules Troy, marries Hecuba, and has many sons, including Hector, noted for valor in the Trojan War.

Priam in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Priam is the old king of Troy, father of Paris, Hector, and Polites. He strengthens Troy through good government and alliances and honors Memnon on his arrival. During the war, he watches his army flee, mourns Hector, and travels with gifts and gold to Achilles's tent to ransom Hector's body. He lives to see Troy fall, then is killed by Pyrrhus at Jupiter's altar after seeing Polites die.

Priam lived to see Troy fall. While seeking refuge at Jupiter's altar, he saw his son Polites die and was then killed by Pyrrhus after throwing a spear at him.
