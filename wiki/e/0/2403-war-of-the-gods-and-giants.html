  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, it is fought by Jupiter against the Giants, who are destroyed with those who fought alongside the gods.
  • In The Extant Odes of Pindar, it is prophesied to take place on Phlegra’s plain, where Herakles will aid the gods and overcome the Giants.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, it is distinguished from the war between Jupiter and the Titans and concerns the Giants’ attempt to reach heaven.
  • In The Library, it is fought by Hercules alongside the gods, who defeat the Giants with his aid.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, it takes place at Bathos according to the Arcadians, rather than at Pallene in Thrace.

war against the giants in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Jupiter fought giants because of their violence, oppression, and refusal to submit to justice. He was said to have killed Mytinus in Crete and Typhon in Phrygia, and later fought giants at Pallene in Macedonia and at Phlegra, identified with the plains near Cumæ; those who fought with the gods were destroyed.
