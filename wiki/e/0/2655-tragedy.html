Tragedy in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

Tragedy is a personified Roman literary genre: ruthless and severe, marked by a scowling brow, flowing locks, royal sceptre, and Lydian buskin. She demands greater works on heroes, reproaches the Poet’s love poetry, and presents an everlasting task. After his entreaties, she grants him brief respite before the serious work begins.

Tragedy in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Tragedy is a Roman, exalted personified genre. She appears in a powerful stride with a royal sceptre and high Lydian buskin, reproaches the poet’s continual love writing, urges heroic poetry as greater work, then grants time for tender love poems.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

LIBER TERTIUS

Tragedy appears in a powerful stride, wearing a royal sceptre and a high Lydian buskin. She reproaches the poet for continually writing about love and urges him to sing of heroic deeds in a greater work. After the poet asks for more time, she grants permission for him to continue writing tender love poems.
