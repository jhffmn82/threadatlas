Philoetius in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Philoetius is a stockman and servant loyal to Ulysses despite the suitors’ abuse of Ulysses’s cattle. He mourns the stranger’s apparent hardship, condemns the suitors for consuming cattle and planning to divide property, and promises to fight if Ulysses returns. After recognizing Ulysses by his scar, he secures the outer court, guards the narrow-passage door, arms himself, fights beside Ulysses, and kills Pisander and Ctesippus.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XX

Philoetius brings a barren heifer and goats and expresses sorrow because the stranger's condition reminds him of Ulysses. He condemns the suitors for consuming Ulysses's cattle and planning to divide his property, and he promises to help if Ulysses returns.

Then the swineherd and the stockman left the cloisters together, and

Philoetius declares that he would fight for Ulysses if Ulysses returned, and he recognizes Ulysses when shown the scar. He secures the gates of the outer court with a ship’s cable and then watches Ulysses with the bow.

BOOK XXII

Philoetius is ordered to guard the narrow-passage door so that only one person can attack through it. He arms himself, fights beside Ulysses, and kills Pisander and Ctesippus.
