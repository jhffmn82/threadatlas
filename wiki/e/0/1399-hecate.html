  • In The Argonautica, Hecate is a dread underworld goddess who teaches Medea magic, is invoked and propitiated by Medea and Jason, and appears at their offering amid serpents, torches, and howling hellhounds.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Hecate is a goddess honored by Zeus and the deathless gods, sharing privileges in earth, heaven, and sea, aiding people in many pursuits, nursing the young, and becoming Persephone’s companion and minister.
  • In Description of Greece, Hecate is a deity honored above other gods by the Aeginetans, with statues attributed to Scopas and Myron.

Hecate in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · -0299/-0200

Hecate is a dread underworld goddess, the only-begotten daughter of Perses, associated with hounds of hell. She teaches Medea to use magic herbs from land and water, enabling aid against Aeetes’ dangers. Medea and Jason invoke and propitiate her through sacrifice, including a ewe and honey. Hearing Jason, Hecate Brimo comes to the offering amid serpents, torches, and howling hellhounds, shaking the meadows and terrifying him.

Hecate in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Hecate is a goddess, born to Phoebe and Coeus, though also recorded as daughter of Persaeus. Zeus honours her above all, gives her splendid gifts and shares in earth and the unfruitful sea, while the deathless gods honour her in heaven. She keeps privileges in earth, heaven, and sea from the first division among the former Titan gods. By her will she aids and advances people, influences judgment, assemblies, battle, games, horsemen, sea catches, and livestock, and Zeus appoints her nurse of the young. Later, she alone hears Persephone’s cries from her cave, tells Demeter what she heard, accompanies her to Helios, and becomes Persephone’s companion and minister.

Hecate is born to Phoebe and Coeus and is honoured by Zeus above all. She retains privilege in earth, heaven, and sea, and aids or advances whom she chooses in judgment, assemblies, battle, games, horsemanship, seafaring, and the keeping of herds. Zeus makes her a nurse of the young who later see the light of Dawn.

II. TO DEMETER

Hecate hears Persephone’s cries from her cave when the other gods and mortals do not. On the tenth day, she meets Demeter, reports what she heard, and accompanies her to Helios.

Hecate in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Hecate, a deity, is honored by Æginetans above other gods through annual rites attributed to Orpheus; her depictions include Scopas's stone statue at Argos and Myron's one-headed, one-bodied wooden statue.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIX.

The people of Ægina honor Hecate above all other gods and celebrate her rites annually, saying that Orpheus the Thracian introduced them. Her temple there contains a wooden statue by Myron with one head and one body.
