Lynceus

person · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Lynkeus, Λυγκεὺς

Abstract written by a model

In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Lynkeus is the keen-eyed son of Aphareus who watches Kastor and Polydeukes, fights Polydeukes, and dies by his spear. In The Library, Lynceus is the keen-eyed son of Aphareus who discovers Castor and is killed by Pollux after wounding him.

Lynkeus in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Abstract written by a model

Lynkeus, Aphareus's son with the keenest eyes among men, watched Kastor and Polydeukes from Taygetos, fought Polydeukes by his father's sepulchre, and died from his bronze spear.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Nemean Ode X

Lynkeus, watching from Taygetos, saw Kastor and Polydeukes sitting within a hollow oak because he had the keenest eyes among men. He fought Polydeukes beside his father's sepulchre and was killed when Polydeukes drove a bronze spear into his side.

Lynceus in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Lynceus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Lynceus, son of Aphareus, has exceptional sight, even underground. While driving Arcadian cattle booty with Idas, Castor, and Pollux, he finds Castor and tells Idas, who kills him; Lynceus wounds Pollux before Pollux kills him with a spear.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Of the sons born to Leda Castor practised the art

Lynceus joined Idas, Castor, and Pollux in driving cattle booty from Arcadia. He spied Castor and revealed his position to Idas, who killed Castor. Pollux pursued Lynceus and killed him by throwing his spear, but Lynceus had wounded Pollux in the head with a stone.
