Persian defeat at Salamis in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

The Persian defeat at Salamis is a naval disaster commemorated in The Persians. In confined waters, Persian ships collide, lose oar-banks, overturn, and leave wrecks and dead men; Greek forces kill survivors. The battle destroys much of Xerxes’ fleet and army, sends him home without an escort, and causes Persia’s mourning and humiliation.

INTRODUCTION

The Battle of Salamis is commemorated in The Persians, produced in 472 B.C., eight years after the sea fight. The play's Messenger gives a vivid and highly praised account of the Persian naval defeat.

At Salamis, Persian ships collide in the confined waters, lose their oar-banks, overturn, and fill the sea with wrecks and dead men. Greek forces continue killing survivors with oars and broken planks, while corpses cover the beaches and reefs.

The Persian defeat at Salamis destroys much of Xerxes’ fleet and army and leaves him returning without an escort. Xerxes and the Chorus identify the disaster at sea as the central cause of Persia’s mourning and humiliation.
