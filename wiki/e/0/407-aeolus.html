Aeolus in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Aeolus is Canace's hard-hearted father and the grandfather of her infant son. As sovereign of the winds, he rules Boreas and controls East-winds in skins for Odysseus. His wrath dominates the account: he exposes Canace's secret, orders her infant abandoned, and sends her a sword as a death sentence.

XI

Aeolus is Canace's hard-hearted father, ruler of the winds, whose wrath exceeds his dominion. He exposes Canace's secret, orders the infant to be abandoned to dogs and birds, and sends Canace a sword so that she will understand and carry out her punishment.

Aeolus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Aeolus, son of Hippotas and descended from Deucalion and Helen, arrived at Lipara with companions, married Cyane, and ruled the island. He helped Liparus recover Italian territory, received demi-god honors, and was credited with teaching sailors about sails and predicting winds. A separate Aeolus descended from Mimas is distinguished from him.

Aeolus in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Aeolus, son of Hippotas, is Jove’s captain of the winds who lives on Aeolian island with his wife, six daughters, and six sons. He entertains Ulysses for a month and gives him a wind sack, but after its winds are released, rejects and sends him away as hated by heaven.

Aeolus, son of Hippotas and captain of the winds, entertains Ulysses for a month and gives him an ox-hide sack containing the winds. When Ulysses returns after the winds have been released, Aeolus refuses to help him again and sends him away, saying that heaven hates him.

Aeolus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Aeolus is Halcyone's father, a monarch and god who rules and guards the winds at Jupiter's command. As a just and pious king he ruled islands in the Tyrrhenian Sea, gives Ulysses winds in a leathern bag, and later restrains winds during Halcyone's calm winter days.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CEYX AND HALCYONE: OR, THE HALCYON BIRDS

Aeolus is Halcyone's father and the god of the winds. He guards the winds after Ceyx and Halcyone become birds, keeping them from disturbing the sea during the seven calm winter days.
