Pythia

person · 3 works · 2 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Pythoness, priest, priestess, prophetess

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Pythia gives responses concerning Homer, identifies him with Ithaca, Telemachus, and Epicasta, warns him about Ios and the riddle of young children, foretells Hesiod's fame, and warns him against the pleasant grove of Nemean Zeus. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Pythia presides over prophecy and divination as the consecrated woman who delivers the oracle's answers. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Pythia is Apollo's priestess at Delphi who prepares by bathing and laurel crowning, inhales chasm vapor on a tripod, and speaks words interpreted by priests.

MYTH OF OSIRIS AND ISIS

The Pythia was the priestess appointed to serve Apollo's oracle at Delphi. After bathing at the fountain of Castalia and being crowned with laurel, she sat on a tripod over the chasm, inhaled its vapor, and spoke words that priests interpreted.
