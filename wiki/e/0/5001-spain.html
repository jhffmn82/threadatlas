Spaniards in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Spaniards, called alongside Balearic Islanders and others, included two thousand sent with Gauls to aid the Lacedemonians; after courageous service and reward, they returned to Sicily, then came under Asdrubal’s authority and granted him full governmental power.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. VIIL

Two thousand Spaniards were sent with the Gauls by Dionysius the tyrant to assist the Lacedemonians. They proved courageous and useful in battle, were rewarded by the Lacedemonians, and returned to Sicily at the end of the summer.

vooKxxiy.

The Spaniards were brought under the authority of Asdrubal, who gained cities in Spain, married the daughter of the king of Spain, and received full governmental power from the Spaniards.

Iberia in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Iberia is a broad, fertile, mineral-rich country of several peoples, with silver, gold, brass, and tin and mountains that sheltered raiders. Ruled by Chrysaor and defended by his sons’ armies, it was gained by Hercules after he defeated them and drove away Gerion’s oxen; he later entrusted its government to leading inhabitants.

Spanish in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

The Spanish possess the Jafarine Isles and the presidios of Melilla, Alhucemas, Penon de Velez, and Ceuta on the Moroccan coast. Their settlements are described as fortified or penal establishments, and their authority is portrayed as a remnant of Spain’s weakened power.

Spanish in Vestiges of the Mayas, or, Facts tending to prove that Communications and Intimate Relations must have existed, in very remote times, between the inhabitants of Mayab and those of Asia and Africa

Augustus Le Plongeon · 1881

The Spanish first arrive in Yucatan in 1517, encountering a population described as mostly Indians. They are surprised by large stone monuments, cities, roads and boats, and meet inhabitants portrayed as civilized, wearing white cotton and having polite manners.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

The population of the country, about 250,000 souls all told, are mostly Indians

The Spanish are said to have been surprised by the stone monuments, cities, roads, boats, and civilized inhabitants they encountered in Yucatan. Their arrival brought them into contact with a people dressed in white cotton and possessing polite manners.
