Abstract written by a model

Spaniards, called alongside Balearic Islanders and others, included two thousand sent with Gauls to aid the Lacedemonians; after courageous service and reward, they returned to Sicily, then came under Asdrubal’s authority and granted him full governmental power.

CHAP. VIIL

Two thousand Spaniards were sent with the Gauls by Dionysius the tyrant to assist the Lacedemonians. They proved courageous and useful in battle, were rewarded by the Lacedemonians, and returned to Sicily at the end of the summer.

Iberia in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Iberia first appears

Abstract written by a model

Iberia is a broad, fertile, mineral-rich country of several peoples, with silver, gold, brass, and tin and mountains that sheltered raiders. Ruled by Chrysaor and defended by his sons’ armies, it was gained by Hercules after he defeated them and drove away Gerion’s oxen; he later entrusted its government to leading inhabitants.
