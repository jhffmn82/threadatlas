Hector's funeral rites in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Hector’s funeral rites are the promised return and burial of Hector’s corpse, despite Achilles’ earlier refusal to allow proper rites. The Achaeans give back the body, enabling Priam’s plan: nine days of mourning and gathering wood, burning and burying Hector on the tenth day, and raising a burial mound on the eleventh. His bones are placed in a golden urn, the grave is covered with stones, and a funeral feast is held in Priam’s house.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XXII.

Hector asks Achilles to accept ransom and return his body so that the Trojans and their wives may burn it and give him his due rites. Achilles refuses and instead declares that dogs and vultures will consume him.

BOOK XXIV.

Hector’s funeral has not yet taken place. Apollo argues that Hector’s body should be returned so that his wife, mother, child, father, and people may give him proper funeral rites.

Hector’s funeral lasts according to Priam’s plan: nine days of mourning and wood gathering, burial on the tenth day, and construction of the barrow on the eleventh. The Trojans burn his body, place his bones in a golden urn, cover the grave with stones, raise a barrow, and hold a feast.
