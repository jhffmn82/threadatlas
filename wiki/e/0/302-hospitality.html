hospitality in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Hospitality is a law recognized by mankind and associated with the gods. Offered to Rhesus by Troy, it is invoked by Ulysses to demand shelter and aid from Polypheme, who rejects it by eating guests; Ulysses warns that such defiance brings ruin.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

MENELAUS, HECUBA, CHORUS.

Menelaus and Hecuba treat Helen’s death as justice for the deaths and suffering caused by the war. Menelaus proposes that her punishment should serve as a warning to all women who betray their husbands.

THE CYCLOPS.

Ulysses appeals to hospitality as a law requiring Polypheme to shelter and assist shipwrecked strangers. Polypheme rejects this obligation and instead treats guests as food, while Ulysses warns that defying such laws brings ruin.

hospitality and its laws in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Hospitality and its laws are presented as sacred duties of protection, justice, and honoring guests and former friends. Polymestor’s murder of a guest after sharing Hecuba’s table violates these duties; Hecuba and Agamemnon demand justice, while the Chorus warns that such impiety brings destructive punishment.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

FEMALE ATTENDANT, CHORUS, HECUBA.

Hecuba argues that Polymestor violated the laws of hospitality by murdering a guest and former friend after sharing her table and receiving what was due for his services. She maintains that neither the gods nor the sacred laws can be honored if such a crime goes unpunished.

POLYMESTOR, HECUBA, CHORUS.

The Chorus says that violating hospitality and justice brings destructive punishment. Hecuba and Agamemnon argue that Polymestor acted impiously and unjustly by killing a guest whom he was supposed to protect.

hospitality in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Hospitality is protected by Jove and shown as a duty toward strangers and suppliants. Alcinous lavishly receives Ulysses with gifts, care, food, entertainment, and safe packing for departure. Eumaeus likewise aids the disguised Ulysses despite doubting him. In contrast, Polyphemus rejects hospitality and Zeus’s protection, refusing mercy and eating his visitors.
