  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Pelias is king of Thessaly and Iolchus, Jason’s uncle, who sends Jason to Colchis hoping he will die and is later killed through Medea’s deception.
  • In The Argonautica, Pelias is king of Iolcos who fears a prophecy about a one-sandaled man, sends Jason to seek the Golden Fleece, and offends Hera by neglecting her sacrifice.
  • In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Pelias is the king of Iolkos who unlawfully holds Jason’s family throne, fears a prophecy about a one-sandaled man, and sends Jason for the Golden Fleece.
  • In The Library, Pelias sends Jason to fetch the Golden Fleece and is later murdered by his daughters after Medea beguiles them.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Pelias is Æson’s brother who usurps his kingdom, sends Jason on a dangerous expedition, and is killed by his daughters after Medea deceives them.
  • In The Library, Pelias is Tyro’s twin son and Neleus’s brother, king of Iolcus, father of Acastus, Alcestis, and other daughters, and Jason’s uncle who sends Jason to seek the Golden Fleece and later dies through Medea’s deception or bull’s blood.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Pelias is Æginetus’s son and the father whose daughters dismember and boil him at Iolcos after Medea targets him.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Pelias is Aeson’s brother and a neighboring Thessalian king who withholds Jason’s kingdom, sends him for the Golden Fleece, and is killed by Medea’s deception.

Pelias in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Pelias is a king of Thessaly, Jason’s homeland, who rules Iolchus and neighboring countries and is Jason’s uncle. Son of Tyro and Neptune, and later called son of Cretheus, he fears Æson and Jason as threats to his kingdom. He sends Jason on the dangerous voyage to Colchis hoping he will die, then, believing Jason and the other heroes have perished, destroys Jason’s family by forcing Jason’s father to drink bull’s blood and murdering the child Promachus. He also conflicts with Neleus and enters the Peloponnese with Melampus and Bias. Medea deceives him with a promise of restored youth, leading Pelias’s daughters, except Alcestis, to kill him.

Pelias in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · -0299/-0200

Pelias is king of Iolcos who receives an oracle that a one-sandaled man will cause his death. When Jason arrives wearing one sandal, Pelias, fearing the prophecy, devises a dangerous voyage to remove him. He commands Jason and a great company of heroes on the expedition to seek the Golden Fleece from Aeetes, becoming Jason’s enemy through this demand. Pelias also neglects Pelasgian Hera at a banquet, leaving her without sacrifice and committing an outrage. Though Hera says he has escaped an evil doom, she intends that he repay this offense.

Pelias in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Pelias, Æson’s brother, dethrones him and usurps his kingdom, becoming king and father of daughters. Warned by an oracle to fear a one-shoed person, he tries to send Æson’s son Jason on a dangerous expedition. In old age, he is deceived by Medea: his daughters, hoping to restore his youth, stab him; Medea cuts his throat and puts him in a boiling cauldron.

Pelias in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Pelias, Tyro’s twin son and Neleus’s brother, was exposed as an infant, marked on the face by a mare, and raised by horse-keepers. After recognizing Tyro, he and Neleus killed their stepmother Sidero. He lived in Thessaly, became king of Iolcus after Cretheus, and was father of Acastus, Alcestis, and other daughters; accounts name Anaxibia or Phylomache as his wife. He offered Alcestis to whoever could yoke a lion and boar, a condition Admetus met. Warned against the one-sandaled man, he recognized Jason and ordered him to seek the Golden Fleece from Colchis. He later despaired of the Argonauts and sought Aeson’s death. Accounts differ: he died from bull’s blood during sacrifice, or Medea deceived his daughters into killing him; Acastus buried him and expelled Jason and Medea.

Pelias was exposed with his twin brother Neleus and was rescued and raised by a horse-keeper after a mare left a livid mark on his face. He and Neleus later recognized Tyro and killed their stepmother Sidero, with Pelias cutting her down at Hera’s altars. Pelias dwelt in Thessaly and had a son, Acastus, and daughters including Alcestis.

Πελίον θυγατέρα Αλκηστιν. ἐκείνου 1 δὲ δώσειν

Pelias promised Alcestis to the man who could yoke a lion and a boar to a car, and Admetus fulfilled the condition. He was warned by an oracle to beware of the one-sandaled man and later recognized Jason as that man. After Jason proposed sending someone for the Golden Fleece, Pelias ordered Jason to fetch it from Colchis.

τῶν ταύρων οὐκ ἐδίδου τὸ δέρας Αἰήτης, ἐβούλετο

Pelias despairs of the Argonauts’ return and wants to kill Aeson. He dies after drinking bull’s blood while offering a sacrifice, though another account in the narrative concerns his later death at the hands of his daughters, whom Medea deceives into cutting him up and boiling him. His son Acastus buries him and expels Jason and Medea from Iolcus.

Pelias in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Pelias, Aeson’s brother and king of a neighboring Thessalian kingdom, held Aeson’s crown during Jason’s minority but kept Jason from his kingdom. He sent Jason to seek the Golden Fleece; after Jason returned with it, Medea killed Pelias by deceiving him with promised rejuvenation.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter XI

Pelias held the crown of Aeson on the condition that he would keep it only during Jason's minority. When Jason came to claim the crown, Pelias encouraged him to seek the Golden Fleece instead. Jason later returned the fleece to Pelias.

MEDEA AND AESON

Pelias is Jason's usurping uncle who kept him out of his kingdom. Medea kills him after pretending that she can rejuvenate him as she did Aeson.
