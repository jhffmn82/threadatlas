Abstract written by a model

In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Theron wins an Olympic crown in the four-horse chariot-race, supports Akragas as its saviour, and is celebrated as righteous, god-fearing, liberal, and exceptionally generous. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Theron becomes king of Agrigentum, defends Himera against Carthage, governs moderately and justly, and dies revered as a divine hero.

Theron in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · 497 BC · the work’s page · read it where Theron first appears

Abstract written by a model

Theron, son of Ainesidamos, brother of Xenokrates of Akragas, and a member of the Emmenidai, traces descent from Polyneikes's son. He wins an Olympian crown in the four-horse chariot-race. Celebrated as a saviour of the state, he supports Akragas and is righteous and god-fearing. Liberal and exceptionally generous toward friends, he gives the Theoxenia for the Dioskouroi, honoring them with hospitality and pious observance, and reaches the furthest bound through fair deeds.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

II.

Theron is celebrated as the winner of the four-horse chariot-race and as a righteous, god-fearing leader who supports Akragas. He is praised as exceptionally well-disposed toward friends and liberal in spirit, and his generosity is said to be too great for anyone to count.

notes

Theron traced his descent from the son of Polyneikes.

III.

Theron is celebrated as the winner of the chariot-race and recipient of an Olympian crown. He gives the Theoxenia feast in the name of the Dioskouroi and is praised for honoring them with hospitality and pious observance.

Theron in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Theron first appears

Abstract written by a model

Theron, prince of the Agrigentines and later king of Agrigentum, feared the Carthaginian advance, built gates, and sought Gelon’s aid to defend Himera. He colonized Himera with Dorians, received Polyzelus, reconciled with Hiero after the Himeraean conspiracy, governed moderately and justly, and died in his sixteenth regnal year, revered as a divine hero.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. U.

Theron, prince of the Agrigentines, was responsible for the defense of Himera but became frightened by the Carthaginian advance. He sent urgently to Gelon at Syracuse for reinforcements and had previously built gates because of fear of the enemy.

CHAP. VII.

Theron, king of Agrigentum, received Polyzelus when he fled from Hiero and was later reconciled with Hiero after the Himeraean conspiracy was revealed. He governed with moderation and justice, died in the sixteenth year of his reign, and was revered as a divine hero.
