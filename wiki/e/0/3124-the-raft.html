the raft in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04

The raft, built by the Tin Woodman from small trees as logs fastened with wooden pins, is proposed to cross the river. Begun before nightfall, it is completed and carries the travelers across, swept downstream until the Lion pulls it to shore.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter VII

The raft is proposed as a way to cross the river. The Tin Woodman begins making it from small trees, but it is not finished when night comes.

Chapter VIII

The raft is completed from logs fastened with wooden pins and carries Dorothy, Toto, the Scarecrow, the Tin Woodman, and the Cowardly Lion across the river. The current sweeps it downstream, and the travelers reach shore after the Lion swims while pulling it.
