  • In The Marvelous Land of Oz: Gump is an animated flying machine made from miscellaneous materials: two sofas form its body and protective rampart, a Gump’s head is its figure-head, palm leaves are its four wings, and a broom is its tail, with clotheslines, ropes, and glass eyes among its parts. Proposed as an aerial escape from Jinjur’s palace roof, it is built by Nick Chopper and brought to life by Tip’s magic. It responds to Tip’s commands, flies through the air, and agrees to carry the party despite having no flying experience and unusable former legs. On its first journey it loses its way, crashes, and breaks two wings, but frightens the Jackdaws, escapes, and is restored to perfect repair by the Woggle-Bug’s wish. It then carries Scarecrow, Woggle-Bug, Tin Woodman, and Glinda toward her palace, reaches Glinda’s country, later flies over the Emerald City’s walls, and lands in its courtyard, helping enable Jinjur’s capture and the war’s end. Ashamed of its monstrous wings and embarrassing, conglomerate body, it asks Ozma to have it dispersed. Its components are returned to former uses, while its head continues to talk from the hall.

Gump in The Marvelous Land of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1904-06

Gump is an animated flying machine made from miscellaneous materials: two sofas form its body and protective rampart, a Gump’s head is its figure-head, palm leaves are its four wings, and a broom is its tail, with clotheslines, ropes, and glass eyes among its parts. Proposed as an aerial escape from Jinjur’s palace roof, it is built by Nick Chopper and brought to life by Tip’s magic. It responds to Tip’s commands, flies through the air, and agrees to carry the party despite having no flying experience and unusable former legs. On its first journey it loses its way, crashes, and breaks two wings, but frightens the Jackdaws, escapes, and is restored to perfect repair by the Woggle-Bug’s wish. It then carries Scarecrow, Woggle-Bug, Tin Woodman, and Glinda toward her palace, reaches Glinda’s country, later flies over the Emerald City’s walls, and lands in its courtyard, helping enable Jinjur’s capture and the war’s end. Ashamed of its monstrous wings and embarrassing, conglomerate body, it asks Ozma to have it dispersed. Its components are returned to former uses, while its head continues to talk from the hall.

Narrative, section by section

7 sections; each heading opens the text

The Marvelous Land of Oz

The Gump appears in the chapter list in connection with an astonishing flight.

The Scarecrow Takes Time to Think

The aerial machine is proposed by the Scarecrow as a way to escape through the air. Nick Chopper is to build it with strong wings on the palace roof, after which Tip will use the Powder of Life to bring it to life.

The Astonishing Flight of the Gump

The Gump’s head is attached to one end of the sofa body and animated with the remaining magic powder. It screeches, turns toward Tip’s voice, and directs the flying Thing back to the roof.

In the Jackdaw’s Nest

The Gump awakens as a Thing with a Gump’s head, four wings, and a broom tail after being brought to life by Tip and the others. It agrees to serve as their aerial transport, but has no experience flying, cannot use its legs, loses its way, and breaks two wings in the crash.

Dr. Nikidik’s Famous Wishing Pills

The Gump’s glass eyes cannot be injured by the Jackdaws. It frightens the birds by waving its remaining wings, breaks free from the rock, and later has its broken wings repaired by the Woggle-Bug’s wish.

The Scarecrow Appeals to Glinda the Good

The Gump carries the party away from the Jackdaws' nest and flies north across cities, villages, plains, and the desert toward Oz. Although the travelers initially believe it is moving in the wrong direction, it eventually reaches Glinda's country and lands in her gardens.

The Riches of Content

The Gump flies over the Emerald City's walls and lands in the courtyard, enabling the capture of Queen Jinjur and ending the war. The Gump asks Princess Ozma to take it apart because it dislikes its conglomerate form and condition of servitude, so its parts are returned to their former uses; its head continues to talk from the hall.
