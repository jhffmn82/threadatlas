ghosts of the dead in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

The ghosts of the dead are feeble, aimless souls of deceased people in Hades, lacking the living’s substance and strength; only Teiresias retains understanding. Drawn from Erebus by Ulysses’ prescribed sacrifice and spilt blood, they crowd around the trench, but must be kept from it until Teiresias answers. After drinking blood, they can speak rationally and recount melancholy stories, including Agamemnon, Achilles, Ajax, Minos, Orion, Tityus, Tantalus, Sisyphus, and Hercules’ phantom.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

“Thus did she speak and we assented. We stayed with Circe for a whole

The dead ghosts are described as poor, feeble, and generally aimless after death, except for Teiresias. They will come to Ulysses when he offers the prescribed sacrifices, but he must keep them away from the blood until Teiresias has answered his questions.

BOOK XI

The ghosts come from Erebus after Ulysses pours blood into the trench. They include brides, bachelors, old men, maids, and warriors, but they cannot speak reasonably until they have tasted the blood.

Here he ended, and the guests sat all of them enthralled and speechless

The ghosts of the dead gather around Ulysses and tell him their melancholy stories. They include Agamemnon, Achilles, Ajax, Minos, Orion, Tityus, Tantalus, Sisyphus, and the phantom of Hercules.
