Elysian hill in The Amores, or Amours

Elysian hill has a grove at its foot, shaded by dark holm oaks and covered with ever-living grass; it is imagined as the abode of innocent birds, drawn by a dead parrot’s words.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ELEGY VI.

At the foot of the Elysian hill there is a grove of dark holm oaks and earth covered with ever-living grass. It is described as the dwelling place of innocent birds, where the dead parrot is imagined to attract other guileless birds with its words.
