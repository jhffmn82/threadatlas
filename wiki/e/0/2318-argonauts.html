Abstract written by a model

In The Extant Odes of Pindar, the Argonauts are godlike heroes who sail with Jason, seek and take the Golden Fleece, face dangers and the Colchians, and return to Iolkos. In Medea, the Argonauts are heroes gathered from across Hellas to accompany Jason aboard the Argo in quest of the Golden Ram, surviving through Medea’s aid and counsel. In The Argonautica, the Minyae are the assembled heroes and Argonauts who sail aboard the Argo to Colchis, aid Phrixus’s sons, escape with Medea and the fleece, and endure the hazardous return voyage. In The Argonautica, the Argonaut gathering is the assembly of heroes formed to join Jason, bringing together numerous chiefs whom Jason leads toward the voyage. In The Argonautica, the Minyan heroes are the exceptionally brave crew of the expedition to Colchis, who launch the Argo, face dangers and conflicts, take Medea and the fleece aboard, and prepare for the return. In The Argonautica, the Argonauts are Jason’s heroic Achaean expeditionary band, crewing and sailing the Argo in quest of the Golden Fleece. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Argonauts are Jason’s chosen group of heroes who sail to Colchis, take the Golden Fleece, fight the Colchians, and travel home with Medea’s help. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the Argonauts are young Greek nobles and adventurers who sail with Jason on the Argo, rescue Phineus, reach Colchis, and celebrate Jason’s success.

In The Library, the Argonauts are the Greek nobles and heroes who crew the Argo on Jason’s expedition, aid him in Colchis, flee with Medea and Apsyrtus, and eventually reach Iolcus. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Argonauts are Greek heroes who follow Phineus’s guidance through the Symplegades, reach Colchis, escape with Jason and Medea after taking the Golden Fleece, and return safely to Thessaly.

Minyae in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · 299 to 200 BC · the work’s page · read it where Minyae first appears

Abstract written by a model

Minyae are the assembled heroes also called the Argonauts: nearby people use the name for the chiefs, many of whom claim descent from the daughters of Minyas. They travel aboard Argo in pursuit of the golden fleece in Colchis. At Lemnos they accept the Lemnian women’s invitation and hospitality, delay their departure, and leave after Heracles reproaches them. They meet and aid Phrixus’ shipwrecked sons, sacrifice with them, and sail to Aea and the Phasis. In Colchis, where they are strangers, Aeetes charges them with insolence and secret designs. During their escape, they make a covenant concerning Medea and the fleece, ambush and kill Apsyrtus, defeat the Colchian force, and continue with Medea’s assistance. Their return leads through islands, seas, Ister, Eridanus, Celtic waterways, the Stoechades, Aethalia and its Argoan harbour, and Aeaea, amid storms and other dangers. At Drepane they prepare to defend Medea against the Colchians, celebrate her marriage to Jason, receive gifts, and depart. Finally, stranded in Libya, they carry Argo across the desert, mourn Canthus and Mopsus, find Heracles’ spring, and resume sailing after Triton reveals the lake’s outlet.

Minyan heroes in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · 299 to 200 BC · the work’s page · read it where Minyan heroes first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Minyan heroes are a band of half-divine, exceptionally brave heroes, also called the Argonauts, who form the crew of the expedition to Colchis to obtain the golden fleece. They prepare and launch Argo from Pagasae, sacrifice to Apollo, choose Jason as leader, assign rowing places, and sail with Orpheus’s song. Their voyage brings both conflict and fellowship: they reach Lemnos, fight the Earthborn, mistakenly battle the Doliones, mourn Cyzicus with rites and games, and sacrifice on Dindymum. Hospitably received by the Mysians, they feast and take supplies, but depart without Heracles and Hylas; the resulting quarrel ends after Glaucus’s explanation and Jason’s reconciliation with Telamon. They support Polydeuces against the Bebrycians, rout them, care for the wounded, sacrifice, feast, and continue. At Colchis they cannot bend Jason’s spear, encourage him during his tasks, and rejoice in hope. They take Medea aboard, carry the golden fleece away, and prepare to defend their return. Their homeward journey is troubled: following Argus’s reading of Phineus’s prophecy, they sail toward Ister, become grief-stricken and fearful, pray after the ship’s warning, and are shaken by Hera’s cry.

Argonauts in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · 299 to 200 BC · the work’s page · read it where Argonauts first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Argonauts are a band of Hellenes, heroes, warriors, and Jason’s comrades: an Achaean expeditionary band that crews and sails on Argo in Jason’s quest for the Golden Fleece. Their voyage begins at Iolcos, from which they launch Argo for the land of Aeetes, passing Magnesia and Thrace before reaching Lemnos. Welcomed by Cyzicus and the Doliones, they defeat the Earthborn, but during a stormy nighttime return accidentally kill Cyzicus and other Doliones; after sacrifices to Rhea, they sail on. They aid the detained Phineus, receive his prophecy, and prepare for the Cyanean rocks on the way to Aea, at the edge of Pontus and the world. Given gifts by the Thynians, they sacrifice to the blessed twelve, carry a trembling dove aboard, pass the rocks, sacrifice to Apollo on Thynia, and swear an oath to help one another with concord of heart. Lycus welcomes them in the land of the Mariandyni and sends Dascylus with them. Near their goal, they wait unobserved in an ambush amid reed-beds. They decide Jason should first speak with Aeetes rather than use force; his challenge discourages them, though some volunteer and Argus proposes Medea’s help. They follow Mopsus’s interpretation of a divine sign, move Argo nearer shore, and depend on Medea for safety and return. Jason’s comrades greet him after his return to the ship, while Idas remains angry; Telamon and Aethalides seek the dragon’s teeth. On the homeward voyage, Thetis warns them. They evade the Sirens, pass the Wandering rocks with Nereids’ help, and go beyond Thrinacia. When wind fails they row night and day; Talos drives them from Crete until Medea devises a plan. After the Pall of Darkness and arrival at Anaphe, they race for water at Aegina and safely reach Pagasae.

Chapter XI

The Argonauts were the band of Greek heroes who accompanied Jason, taking their name from their vessel, the Argo. They received instructions from Phineus, passed through the Symplegades by following a dove and using the favorable rebound, and reached Colchis. They escaped with Jason and Medea after taking the Golden Fleece and returned safely to Thessaly.
