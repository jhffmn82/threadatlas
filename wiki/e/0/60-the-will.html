the will in The Diary of Samuel Pepys

Samuel Pepys · 1660/1669

Uncle Robert’s will is read and reviewed by the narrator, his father, relatives, Dr. John Pepys, Sir Robert Bernard, and Mr. Moore. It gives the narrator little at present, with more after his father’s death, but provides for the family and other kindred. Mr. Moore judges it sufficient despite three witnesses being legatees. Roger Pepys offers guidance and agrees to read the Captain’s will with Pepys’s uncles, while trouble over interpretation and execution is expected.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

The narrator and his father read uncle Robert's will on the morning after his arrival. Although the narrator received little immediately and would receive more only after his father's death, he was pleased that the will provided well for the family and other relatives.

opening

The will is read and reviewed with Dr. John Pepys, Sir Robert Bernard, Mr. Moore, and several family members. Mr. Moore advises that the three witnesses, although legatees, do not invalidate it, but Pepys continues to expect trouble over its interpretation and execution.

opening

Pepys discusses his uncle's will at length with Roger Pepys at Impington. Roger gives him useful information about what to expect and do, warns that the matter will bring considerable trouble, and later agrees to read the Captain's will with Pepys's uncles for their satisfaction.
