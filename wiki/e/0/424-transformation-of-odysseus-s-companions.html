transformation of Odysseus's companions

event · 3 works · 4 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Circe's transformation of the men, changed into swine, changed their shapes, made wolves, she turned them into pigs, suffered the same treatment, the sad fate of our comrades, they became men again, transformation of Ulysses' companions, transformed into swine, τὰς μορφὰς ἠλλοίου
  • In The Odyssey, it was Circe’s transformation of the men into pigs after a drugged mixture and a blow from her wand, later reversed when she anointed them with a second drug.
  • In The Library, it was caused by Circe’s enchantment: she changed Odysseus’s companions into wolves, swine, asses, and lions after they drank her mixture, then restored them.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, it was a transformation into swine caused by Circe’s drugged mixture and wand, which Ulysses reversed with harmless plants, an inverted wand, and counter-charms.

transformation of Ulysses' companions in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

A transformation into swine caused by Circe’s drugged mixture and wand; Ulysses intervenes, and Circe reverses it with harmless plants, an inverted wand, and counter-charms, restoring human forms.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE IV. [XIV.154-247]

Circe’s companions drink her drugged mixture and are transformed into swine, growing bristles, snouts, swollen necks, and cloven feet. After Ulysses intervenes, Circe sprinkles them with harmless plants, strikes them with her inverted wand, and reverses the spell, restoring their human forms.
