  • In Heroides and Amores, the Golden Fleece is the fleece Jason is said to have taken from Phrixus’s ram after it was guarded by an ever-watchful dragon; Hypsipyle questions whether Jason or Medea deserves credit.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Golden Fleece is the gilded ram’s skin hanging in the temple of Mars, which Jason and the Argonauts seize and carry away with Medea’s help.
  • In The Argonautica, the Golden Fleece is the central prize of Jason’s voyage, guarded by a sleepless dragon and taken by Jason after Medea puts the guardian to sleep; it is later spread over Jason and Medea’s bridal couch.
  • In The Extant Odes of Pindar, the Golden Fleece is the imperishable golden covering of the ram that carried Phrixos to safety, guarded by a dragon until Jason takes it with Medea’s assistance.
  • In The Library, the Golden Fleece is demanded by Jason from Aeetes, won with Medea’s aid, carried away with her, and later surrendered to Pelias at Iolcus.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the Golden Fleece is the treasure of Phryxus sought by Jason and the Minyæ, guarded by a dragon, taken with Medea’s aid, and later recovered by the Argonauts.
  • In The Library, the Golden Fleece is the fleece in Colchis that Pelias orders Jason to retrieve; Jason takes it with Medea’s help and returns it to Pelias.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Golden Fleece is the ram’s fleece placed in a grove and guarded by a sleepless dragon; Jason recovers it with Medea’s help and delivers it to Pelias.

golden fleece in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · -0299/-0200

The golden fleece is a golden, immense fleece from the sacrificed golden ram, hanging on an oak in the sacred grove of Ares in the land of Aeetes. It is the central object and prize of Jason’s voyage with the assembled heroes: the expedition travels to Aeetes’s land seeking to take it and return with it. Though possessed by Aeetes, it is guarded by a sleepless dragon or serpent, and Aeetes believes that those seeking it aim to seize his power. He promises it to Jason only if Jason completes the required contest. Hera and Athena arrange for Medea’s love and assistance, and Medea tells Jason that he will carry the fleece away after the contest. In the end, Medea puts the guardian serpent to sleep, allowing Jason to take the fleece from the huge oak. Its brightness is like the rising sun, and aboard the ship Jason covers it with a newly woven mantle. The heroes retain it under a covenant even if it was won by craft rather than openly. Finally, it is spread over the bridal couch in Macris’s sacred cave to honor Jason and Medea’s marriage; its golden tufts shine like flame and inspire longing and awe among the nymphs.

Golden Fleece in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The Golden Fleece is a fictional treasure: a fleece of Phryxus, glittering with shining golden hair, and the prize sought in Colchis. Calaïs and Zethes seek it with the Minyæ, while Jason seeks it from Æetes. Guarded by a dragon, it is taken after the dragon is lulled to sleep; Jason, aided by Medea after conquering fire-breathing bulls and producing armed men from serpent’s teeth, carries it away. It is later recovered by the Argonauts. Notes present it as poetic and suggest links to treasure or Phryxus’s gilt sheep skin.

The Golden Fleece is recovered by Jason with the aid of Medea after he conquers the fire-breathing bulls, produces armed men from serpent’s teeth, and lulls the dragon to sleep.

opening

The Golden Fleece is a fleece that glittered with shining hair of gold. Calaïs and Zethes later seek it with the Minyæ aboard the first ship.

BOOK THE SEVENTH.

The Golden Fleece is the prize that Jason seeks from Æetes in Colchis. After the dragon guarding it is put to sleep, Jason takes it and carries it away.

notes .. notes

The Golden Fleece is presented as part of the poetic version of the Argonauts’ story. The explanation suggests that it may have originated from a misunderstood account of a treasure, or from the gilt skin of the sheep sacrificed by Phryxus.

Golden Fleece in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

The Golden Fleece is a fleece in Colchis, hung on an oak in a grove of Ares and guarded by a sleepless dragon. Pelias orders Jason to bring it back. Jason seeks it, while Aeetes sets tasks involving brazen-footed bulls and dragon’s teeth and later refuses it. Medea leads Jason to the fleece, helps overcome the dragon, and escapes with it to the Argo. Jason returns to Iolcus and gives it to Pelias.

Golden Fleece in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The Golden Fleece came from the ram that carried Phryxus to Colchis, was given to Aeetes, and placed in a consecrated grove guarded by a sleepless dragon. Jason recovered it with Medea's help and delivered it to Pelias, causing rejoicing; its later fate is unknown. Psyche also safely gathered it for Venus.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CUPID AND PSYCHE

Psyche gathers the golden fleece safely from bushes and tree trunks after the sheep have moved into the shade. She returns to Venus with her arms full of it.

Chapter XI

The Golden Fleece originally belonged to a ram that carried Phryxus to Colchis. Phryxus sacrificed the ram and gave its fleece to Aeetes, who placed it in a consecrated grove under the care of a sleepless dragon. Jason obtained it with Medea's help and delivered it to Pelias, after which its fate is unknown.

MEDEA AND AESON

The recovery of the Golden Fleece causes rejoicing and leads Jason to wish that his father Aeson could share in the celebrations.
