Hiero in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Hiero is Gelon’s eldest brother, heir, and successor as king of Syracuse. To retain the kingdom, he relies on mercenaries and strangers and fears Polyzelus’s influence, sending him to command an army. He reveals a plot against Theron and reconciles with him, displaces Cataneans and Naxians, colonizes Catana, aids Cuma against the Etruscans, defeats Thrasideus, and dies at Catana after eleven years, leaving the kingdom to Thrasybulus.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. VII.

Hiero, king of Syracuse, feared the influence of his brother Polyzelus and sought to remove him by sending him to command an army. He later exposed the Himeraeans' plot against Theron, expelled the Cataneans and Naxians, defeated the Etruscans at sea, and defeated Thrasideus in war.

CHAP. XIII.

Hiero, king of Syracuse, advised the sons of Anaxilaus to take control from their tutor Micythus. He died at Catana after reigning eleven years and left the kingdom to his brother Thrasybulus.

Hieron of Syracuse in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

Hieron of Syracuse is presented as a king, chief, captain, and leader of the Syracusans who rules Syracuse, Ortygia, Sicily, and Aitna with a sceptre of justice, righteous counsel, and benevolence. Favored by destiny, he possesses wealth, wisdom, military valor, generosity, and excellence in many forms; he treats citizens and strangers like a father. His rule includes founding Aitna near Etna, leading the Syracusans before Cumae and defeating the Etruscans, and protecting the Epizephyrian Lokrians from war. He is repeatedly celebrated in the Muses’ song for athletic victories: an Olympic horse-race with Pherenikos and Pythian chariot-races, including a four-horse car. The records portray his renown as joining political power, just governance, military protection, religious honor, and victories made enduring through song.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

OLYMPIAN ODES. / I.

Hieron is praised as the ruler of Sicily, where he governs with justice and gathers excellence of many kinds. He is celebrated for winning the Olympic horse-race with Pherenikos, and the speaker hopes to glorify him again in song.

VI.

Hieron ruled Syracuse and Ortygia with a righteous sceptre and true counsels. He honored Demeter, her daughter’s festival, and Aetnaean Zeus, and was known for song and lute.

I.

Hieron is celebrated for winning the chariot-race at the Pythian Games. He is praised as the captain of the Syracusans before Cumae, the founder of Aitna city, and a ruler urged to govern truthfully and honor his people.

II.

Hieron wins the four-horse chariot-race and crowns Ortygia with splendid renown. He is praised as a powerful ruler with wealth, wisdom, military valor, generous character, and the strength to protect the western Lokrians from the troubles of war.

III.

Hieron of Syracuse is praised as a king who rules his citizens benevolently and treats strangers like a father. The speaker wishes to bring him both golden health and a song celebrating Pherenikos's Pythian victory, and says that Destiny has favored him with happiness.

Hiero in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Hiero, Gelon’s brother, becomes tyrant of Syracuse, wins Olympian contests, and is commemorated by offerings made by his son Dinomenes.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XII.

He was tyrant of Syracuse after his brother Gelon and won victories in the Olympian contests. Memorials of his victories included a brazen chariot, race-horses, and boys on the horses, although these offerings were made by Dinomenes the son of Hiero rather than by Hiero himself.
