Telemachus's journey to Pylos and Lacedaemon in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Telemachus’s journey is a secret voyage from Ithaca to Pylos and Lacedaemon to seek news of Ulysses. With a picked crew, he first visits Nestor at Pylos, who welcomes him but knows nothing. Nestor then sends him with Pisistratus by chariot toward Lacedaemon; after a night at Pherae, they reach Menelaus during a wedding feast. Menelaus and Helen speak with them about Ulysses, and Menelaus reports that the old man of the sea saw him grieving in Calypso’s house, unable to return. The suitors plot to murder Telemachus at sea, but he reaches Ithaca safely before they can intercept him.

Telemachus’s voyage in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Telemachus’s voyage is a ship journey from Ithaca to Pylos and then Sparta, undertaken to learn whether Ulysses is alive, where he is, or how he died. Urged by Minerva, Telemachus secretly obtains provisions, a ship, and twenty men. With Minerva and his crew he reaches Pylos, questions Nestor, and is directed to continue to Lacedaemon to question Menelaus; if Ulysses is dead, he will return for funeral rites.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

So saying she bound on her glittering golden sandals, imperishable,

Minerva urges Telemachus to take a good ship and a crew of twenty men to seek news of Ulysses. She directs him first to Pylos to question Nestor and then to Sparta to visit Menelaus.

BOOK II

Telemachus's voyage is planned as a journey to Pylos and Sparta to learn whether Ulysses is alive or dead. Minerva promises to accompany him, and provisions, a ship, and a crew are secretly arranged. If Telemachus learns that Ulysses has died, he will return to perform the funeral rites.

BOOK III

Telemachus’s voyage takes him from Ithaca to Pylos with Minerva and his crew. At Pylos he seeks information about Ulysses and is advised to continue to Lacedaemon to question Menelaus.
