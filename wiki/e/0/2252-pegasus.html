Pegasos in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

Pegasos, a winged horse, is sought and bridled by Bellerophon at Peirene’s spring after Athene’s dream-given golden bit, throws him when he seeks heaven, and lives in Zeus’ Olympus stalls.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

XIII.

Pegasos was the winged horse whom Bellerophon longed to bridle at Peirene’s spring. After Athene provided a golden bit and instructed Bellerophon through a dream, Pegasos was mastered and later found a home in the ancient stalls of Zeus at Olympus.

Pegasus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Pegasus is a winged horse born from Medusa's blood, tamed by Minerva for the Muses; he creates Hippocrene, carries Bellerophon against the Chimaera, then throws him after Jupiter sends a gadfly.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter X

Pegasus was the winged horse produced when Medusa's blood sank into the earth. Minerva tamed him and presented him to the Muses, and Bellerophon captured him with a golden bridle and rode him to defeat the Chimaera; later, Jupiter's gadfly caused Pegasus to throw Bellerophon.
