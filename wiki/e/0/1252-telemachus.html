  • In Heroides and Amores, Telemachus seeks news of Ulysses at Pylos, learns from Nestor about his father’s Trojan War deeds, reports them to Penelope, and survives an attempt on his life.
  • In The Odyssey, Telemachus, Ulysses and Penelope’s son and heir, seeks news of his absent father, challenges the suitors, joins Ulysses in reclaiming their household, and helps kill the suitors.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Telemachus is taken with Ulysses’s body and Penelope to Circe’s island, becomes immortal, and marries Circe.
  • In The Library, Telemachus learns Ulysses’ identity after his disguised arrival, joins Ulysses, Eumaeus, and Philoetius against the suitors, and helps kill them.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Telemachus searches for news of Ulysses, returns to Ithaca, reunites with him, supports his disguise, and helps attack the suitors.

Telemachus in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Telemachus is the son of Ulysses and Penelope, Ulysses’s only son, heir, and eventual master of his Ithacan household and lands. At the beginning, the suitors consume his estate and abuse his home while Ulysses is absent; Telemachus doubts and seeks confirmation of his father’s fate but is urged by Minerva to act. He summons an assembly, publicly demands that the suitors leave, and secretly prepares a ship, crew, and provisions to seek news in Pylos and Sparta. He travels first to Pylos, where he is Nestor’s guest and is directed toward Menelaus, then goes by chariot with Pisistratus to Lacedaemon. There Menelaus and Helen recognize him as Ulysses’s son. Telemachus describes the ruin of his household and insists on truthful news of Ulysses. Though he is received comfortably and given gifts, he departs with Pisistratus, takes Theoclymenus aboard at Pylos, and sails home. During his absence, the suitors plot to kill him before he returns, but he reaches Ithaca safely, avoids their danger, and visits Eumaeus. At the swineherd’s station, Telemachus learns that Ulysses is his father and joins him in planning revenge. He keeps Ulysses’s identity secret, returns to Penelope and the household, and increasingly asserts authority over the suitors. He protects the disguised Ulysses as far as he can, condemns insults and violence, and openly warns the suitors that he will no longer endure the household’s disgrace. With Ulysses he removes and conceals the armour, controls access to Ulysses’s bow, and, though unable to string it himself, yields the contest at Ulysses’s signal. In the final struggle, Telemachus arms himself, kills Amphinomus and other suitors, brings weapons for Ulysses, and helps clear the household afterward. He seeks mercy for Phemius and Medon, keeps faith with Ulysses’s plans, and continues beside him in the Ithacan civil conflict, declaring that he will not disgrace his family. His course is from an aggrieved but hesitant heir to a shrewd, bold ally who helps recover and defend his father’s house.
