  • In The Amores, or Amours, Minos is a lawgiver who wishes for many fruitful years like those of prosperous Crete and for Ceres's love to last.
  • In Heroides and Amores, Minos is identified as Ariadne's father whose realm she betrays by helping Theseus escape.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Minos becomes king and law-maker of Crete, rules justly, conquers islands, demands Athenian tribute, seeks Daedalus, dies in Sicily, and becomes a judge of hell.
  • In The Library, Minos rules Crete, makes laws, confines the Minotaur in the Labyrinth, demands tribute from Athens, imprisons Daedalus and Icarus, and is killed by the daughters of Cocalus.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Minos rules Crete, wars with Nisus and the Athenians, condemns Scylla, encloses the Minotaur in the Labyrinth, imprisons Dædalus, and continues ruling in old age.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Minos rules Crete, conquers nearby islands, wages war against Athens and Ægina, and gives Procris a dog.
  • In The Library, Minos becomes king of Crete, marries Pasiphae, fathers children, gives Procris a magical dog, makes laws, confines the Minotaur, and recovers Glaucus after his restoration.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Minos rules Crete, keeps the Minotaur in the labyrinth, demands Athenian tribute, judges the dead, and condemns Scylla's betrayal.

Minos in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Minos, son of Jupiter and Europa, became king and law-maker of Crete. He claimed Jupiter gave him laws in a cave, ruled the Cretans, built Gnossus, Phaeus, and Sidonia, and was renowned for justice and valor. Husband of Pasiphae and father of several children, he gained dominion of the sea with a great fleet and conquered many islands. After Androgeus was murdered, he demanded justice, fought the Athenians, and required youths and girls for the Minotaur. He later sought Daedalus, invaded Sicily, and died there, suffocated in Cocalus's hot bath. Afterward he was made a judge of hell alongside Rhadamanthus.

Minos in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Minos is a king and ruler of Crete, husband of Pasiphae, father of Phaedra and Androgeus, and owner of Naucrate. He becomes king, passes laws, receives Talos from Hephaestus, and possesses a swift dog. After failing to sacrifice a bull to Poseidon, he shuts the Minotaur in the Labyrinth. Pasiphae bewitches him because he cohabited with many women; when he seeks Procris, her Circaean root prevents the harm to women who sleep with him, and he gives her the dog and a straight-flying dart. Androgeus’s death leads Minos to attack Athens, capture Megara, kill Megareus, drown Scylla, and demand seven youths and seven maidens as food for the Minotaur. He receives Daedalus, but after Theseus and his company escape, imprisons Daedalus and Icarus. Pursuing Daedalus with a spiral shell, he finds him with Cocalus and is killed by the daughters of Cocalus with boiling pitch.

Minos in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Minos is a king of Crete, probably the second prince of that name, son and successor of Lycastus, ruler of a hundred cities. Before the war, he conquers several islands adjoining Crete, gains control of the seas, and possesses notable strength in soldiers and shipping. After his son Androgeus dies, Minos sends for auxiliaries, seeks revenge, and prepares war against Athens and its allies. He crosses the sea with a swift, strong fleet, securing support through treaty or conquest, and seeks Æacus’s alliance. Æacus refuses because of a compact with the people of Cecropia; Minos leaves sadly, choosing threats of war over an early attack on the island. He wages war against Ægeus and leads his fleet against Ægina. Later, after Procris flees to his court, he loves her and gives her a dog.

Minos in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Minos is a king, son of Europa, and a god who rules Crete and receives ordinances attributed to Jupiter. He wars with Nisus and the Athenians, avenging slain sons, leading allies, conquering Nisea and the Athenians, besieging Megara and Alcathoë, and imposing tribute and conditions on defeated cities. He rejects Scylla’s purple lock and condemns her crime. Seeking to hide his family’s disgrace associated with the Minotaur, he encloses it in the Labyrinth. He imprisons Dædalus, who escapes. In grievous old age, Minos continues ruling with diminished dignity.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK THE EIGHTH.

Minos lays waste to the Lelegeian coasts and besieges Alcathoë. He refuses the purple lock Scylla gives him, condemns her crime, imposes conditions on the defeated city, and orders his fleet to depart.

opening

Minos conquers the Athenians and imposes a recurring tribute of youths and virgins from the best families. He seeks to conceal the disgrace of the Minotaur by enclosing it in the Labyrinth.

FABLE III. [VIII.183-259]

Minos controls the land and sea around Crete, but Dædalus observes that he cannot control the air. The explanation says that Minos kept Dædalus in prison after learning that he had assisted Pasiphaë, and that Dædalus escaped by ship.

Minos in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Minos, son of Europa by Zeus, is a prince and later king of Crete. After Poseidon sends a bull in response to his prayer, he sacrifices another instead. He marries Pasiphae, fathers children, gives Procris a magical dog, makes laws, confines the Minotaur, and recovers Glaucus after his restoration.

Minos in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Minos is king of Crete, ruling and commanding its army. He keeps the Minotaur in the labyrinth, demands Athenian youths and maidens as tribute, and judges dead children and falsely accused dead. After war against Megara, he condemns Scylla’s betrayal, sets terms, and departs; Ariadne helps Theseus defeat the monster.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter VIII

Minos, king of Crete, made war upon Megara. After Scylla cut off Nisus's purple lock and surrendered the city to him, he condemned her betrayal, arranged equitable terms for Megara, and sailed away.

Chapter XIII

Minos is the king of Crete who requires Athens to send seven youths and seven maidens each year as tribute to be devoured by the Minotaur. His daughter Ariadne falls in love with Theseus and helps him defeat the monster.
