Abstract written by a model

In The Odyssey, Ulysses's return is his long-sought homecoming to Ithaca after his homeward voyage, restoring him to his household after hardship, concealment, and the suitors' slaughter. In The Library, the return of Ulysses ends in Ithaca after the Phaeacians send him home, leading to his disguise as a beggar, reunion with Telemachus, killing of the suitors, and recognition by Penelope and Laertes. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Ulysses' return to Ithaca brings him ashore aboard a Phaeacian ship, after which Minerva disguises him to assess the palace, reunite with Telemachus, and reclaim his household.

Ulysses's return in The Odyssey

Homer · about 632 to 599 BC · the work’s page · read it where Ulysses's return first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ulysses’s return is his long-sought homecoming to Ithaca after the homeward voyage, a return desired by Ulysses, Eumaeus, and Philoetius and decreed by the gods. It depends on endurance and restraint: Teiresias and Circe warn that harming the Sun’s flocks will cost Ulysses his companions and make the journey difficult, while leaving them unharmed may allow Ithaca after hardship. Earlier progress is repeatedly delayed, including by released winds and losses at Telepylus. Calypso predicts suffering, but Ulysses remains determined to see Penelope, his property, bondsmen, and household again. The Phaeacians ultimately enable the return, preparing a ship and carrying him while he sleeps. He is set down with his gifts near the harbour of Phorcys; Minerva conceals his arrival and directs him to remain unknown. Though reports and predictions of his approach are doubted by Eumaeus and Penelope, Ulysses is already in Ithaca, enters his house disguised, and is recognized only by Argos. After twenty years, he reveals himself to Eumaeus, Philoetius, Telemachus, and finally Laertes, proving his identity. The return restores him to his household, culminates in the suitors’ slaughter, and is followed by an Ithacan civil conflict when the deaths become known.

The Odyssey / BOOK I

The gods decide that Ulysses should return to Ithaca. Mercury is to notify Calypso, while Minerva will help Telemachus seek information that may support Ulysses’ return.

BOOK V

Ulysses’s return home is decreed by Jove, who says that the Phaeacians will help him continue the journey. Ulysses remains determined to return to Penelope despite the suffering Calypso predicts.

BOOK VII

Ulysses asks the Phaeacians to help him return to his own country and says that he will be content to die after seeing his property, bondsmen, and household again. Alcinous promises to arrange his escort, and Ulysses prays that this promise will be fulfilled.

BOOK VIII

Ulysses asks Alcinous and the Phaeacians to further his return home. Alcinous says that the ship is already drawn into the water and the crew found, and the Phaeacians prepare the escort that will carry Ulysses away.

BOOK X

The return voyage brings Ulysses's fleet near its native land after nine days of sailing. The voyage is prolonged when the winds are released, sending the fleet back to the Aeolian island, and it suffers a major loss at Telepylus.

BOOK XI

Ulysses’s return to Ithaca will be difficult and will follow the loss of his companions if the Sun’s flocks are harmed. He will find trouble in his house, kill the suitors, and later undertake the journey with the oar described by Teiresias.

BOOK XII

Circe explains the dangers that Ulysses and his crew must face during their journey home. If they leave the sun-god's herds and flocks unharmed, they may reach Ithaca after much hardship.

BOOK XIII

Ulysses returns to Ithaca asleep on a Phaeacian ship and is set down near the harbour of Phorcys with his gifts. Minerva conceals his arrival with mist, confirms that he is home, and directs him to remain unknown while he prepares to confront the suitors.

BOOK XIV

Ulysses declares that he will return within the same year, at the end of one moon and the beginning of the next. Eumaeus does not believe this prediction, though he wishes that Ulysses could come home.
