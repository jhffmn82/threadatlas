Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Tartarus is an awful primordial gulf deep within Earth that imprisons the defeated Titans and later Typhoeus. In The Aeneid, Tartarus is the deep region of night and punishment where guilty souls, fiends, tyrants, traitors, and the impious remain. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the Infernal Regions are Pluto’s eternally dark, low-lying western kingdom, where Proserpine becomes queen and must spend part of each year. In The Library, Tartarus is a gloomy place in Hades where the Cyclopes were shut away and the defeated Titans were imprisoned under guard. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Tartarus is a deep gulf in Pluto and Proserpine’s underworld, where condemned ghosts suffer punishments and Orpheus enters to seek Eurydice.

Abstract written by a model

Tartarus is an awful primordial place, a great dank gulf deep within Earth. It came into existence near the beginning alongside Chaos, Earth, and Eros. Sealed by shining bronze gates, walls, gloom, and a vast gulf, it imprisons the defeated Titans under warders serving Zeus; later Zeus casts defeated Typhoeus there amid unending clamour and strife.

Infernal Regions in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Infernal Regions first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Infernal Regions are Pluto’s low-lying western kingdom, imagined as eternally dark and associated with subterranean mines. Their threshold is Avernus, and they contain the burning river Phlegethon and Tityus’s punishment. Proserpine is carried there to become queen and spouse of the Infernal King; Jupiter makes her return depend on whether she has eaten there, and she must ultimately spend part of each year with Pluto. They are proposed to derive from Egyptian notions of the future state of man.

notes .. notes

The Infernal Regions are associated with Pluto’s low-lying western kingdom, subterranean mines, and the ancient belief that the region was covered in eternal darkness. Le Clerc’s interpretation instead connects them figuratively with Epirus and its river Acheron.

The Infernal Regions are the domain where Proserpine has been carried and where she is the queen and spouse of the Infernal King. Jupiter’s condition for her return concerns whether she has eaten food there.

notes .. notes

The Infernal Regions are also signified by names such as Avernus and are described as containing Phlegethon, a burning river. Proserpine was required to spend part of each year there with Pluto.

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Tartarus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Tartarus is a deep gulf in Pluto and Proserpine’s underworld, among condemned ghosts and punishments; Orpheus enters through Taenarus, moves its inhabitants with music, and wins Eurydice’s temporary release.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XVII

The underworld was the realm of Pluto and Proserpine, reached by Orpheus through a cave on the promontory of Taenarus. It contained crowds of ghosts and the punishments of Tantalus, Ixion, the vulture, the daughters of Danaus, and Sisyphus. Orpheus's music moved its inhabitants and won Eurydice's temporary release.
