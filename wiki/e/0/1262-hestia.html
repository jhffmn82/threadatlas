Hestia

deity · 2 works · 22 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Daughter of Cronos, a queenly maid, chief of the goddesses, fair goddess, the pure maiden Hestia, the worshipful and dear

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Hestia is a pure maiden and queenly goddess, daughter of Cronos, who refuses marriage with Poseidon and Apollo, receives high honour from Zeus, tends Apollo’s holy house at Pytho, and is first and last offered sweet wine at mortal banquets. In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Hestia is the daughter of Rhea and sister of highest Zeus who shares Hera’s throne and guards city halls, where she is honored with offerings, sacrifices, music, songs, and feasts.

Hestia in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · about 699 BC · the work’s page · read it where Hestia first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hestia is a pure maiden and queenly goddess, daughter of Cronos, described as both first-born and youngest child. She refuses Poseidon and Apollo in marriage, and Zeus grants her high honour instead. She tends Apollo’s holy house at Pytho, shares one mind with Zeus, and has an everlasting abode among deathless gods and people on earth. Her honour, portion, and right are glorious. No mortal banquet occurs without her: sweet wine is offered first and last. Invited to dwell with Hermes, she bestows grace and aids people’s wisdom and strength.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

XXIV. TO HESTIA

Hestia tends the holy house of Apollo at goodly Pytho. She is invited to come into the house with one mind with all-wise Zeus and to bestow grace on the song.

XXIX. TO HESTIA

Hestia has an everlasting abode and highest honour in the dwellings of deathless gods and men. Mortals pour sweet wine to her first and last at banquets, and she is asked to dwell together with Hermes and aid the wisdom and strength of men.

Abstract written by a model

Hestia, daughter of Rhea and sister of highest Zeus, shares Hera’s throne and guards city halls, where senators are welcomed and she is honored through offerings, sacrifices, music, songs, and feasts.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Nemean Ode XI

Hestia is addressed as the daughter of Rhea, sister of highest Zeus, and sharer of Hera's throne. She is asked to welcome Aristagoras and his fellow senators to her sanctuary, and she is honored as the guardian of the city halls through drink-offerings, burnt sacrifices, music, songs, and ritual feasts.
