Central Park in Gallegher and Other Stories

Richard Harding Davis · 1890

Central Park is a place with a secluded box-bush dell, a lake, paths, bushes, an artificial lake, and nearby stables. Van Bibber enters it grieving a breakup and crosses the turf toward the secluded place where he and his fiancée met. Later, in hot weather, he waits for his groom and moves to a shaded bench near the lake.

" A WALK UP THE AVENUE He came down the steps slowly, and pulling mechanically at his gloves.

Central Park contains the secluded place where Van Bibber and his fiancée used to meet away from their friends. Van Bibber enters the Park while grieving the breakup and crosses the turf toward that place.

" VAN BIBBER AND THE SWAN-BOATS It was very hot in the Park, and young Van Bibber, who has a good heart and a great deal more money than good-hearted people generally get, was cross and somnolent.

The Park is very hot, and Van Bibber waits there for his groom before moving to a shaded bench near the lake. Its paths, bushes, artificial lake, and nearby stables provide the setting for the outing.
