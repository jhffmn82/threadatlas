Abstract written by a model

The Aiakids are the heroic descendants of Aiakos, celebrated as Aigina’s national heroes. Their valorous deeds bring inherited glory and fame to Aigina, providing an example for Aristomenes. Their story ranges across far-reaching lands: they twice sack the Trojans’ town, with Herakles and later the sons of Atreus, while Neoptolemos remains at Delphi to oversee justice; they are also honored in Oinone.

Aiakids is in one work on this site, The Extant Odes of Pindar. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

VIII.

The sons of Aiakos are credited with glorious deeds that contribute to Aigina's fame. Aristomenes is said to be following their example through his athletic achievements.

Nemean Ode IV

The Aiakids are connected with Aigina, whose stronghold is praised as a common light and a just aid to strangers. The ode recounts the far-reaching lands and deeds of the sons of Aiakos.

Nemean Ode VII

The sons of Aiakos are associated with Aigina's inherited path to glory and with valorous deeds. One of them, Neoptolemos, is destined to remain at Delphi, where he oversees justice in connection with the heroes and the games.

Isthmian Ode IV

The sons of Aiakos are central to the poet’s song about Aigina. They twice sacked the Trojans’ town, first with Herakles and later with the sons of Atreus, and are honored in Oinone.
