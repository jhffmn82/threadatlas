Abstract written by a model

In Heroides and Amores, it is a portal secured by a guardian and bar that stays closed and deaf to a lover's appeals, with its seeming movement attributed to wind. In The Amores, or Amours, it is a stubborn oak barrier that stays shut against a lover's tears, pleas, and threats, though gentle words later overcome it and its bar.

door in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · about 24 to 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where door first appears

Abstract written by a model

A portal secured by a guardian and bar, braced with hard oak, stays closed and deaf to a lover’s tears, prayers, threats, and appeals. A seeming movement proves to be wind; the lover curses it and bids farewell to door and posts.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

VI

The door remains closed and rigid despite the lover's tears, prayers, threats, and appeals. The lover briefly thinks that it has moved on its hinge, but discovers that the sound came only from the wind; he ends by cursing and bidding farewell to the door and its posts.

door (mistress's door) in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where door (mistress's door) first appears

Abstract written by a model

The mistress's door is a stubborn oak barrier, secured to its post by an oak bar and turning on a hinge. It stays shut against a lover's tears, pleas, and threats; its creak and shaking prove only wind. Later, Naso says gentle words overcame the obdurate door and bar.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ELEGY VI.

The door remains shut despite the lover's tears, entreaties, and threats. He mistakes a creaking hinge and a shaking door for a sign that it is opening, but attributes the movement to the boisterous wind, and he finally bids farewell to the doors as hard-hearted blocks of wood.

BOOK THE SECOND ELEGY I.

The door shut against Naso was more potent to him than Jove’s thunderbolt. Naso says that his gentle words overcame the obdurate door and its oak bar.
