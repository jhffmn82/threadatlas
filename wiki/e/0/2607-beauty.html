beauty in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

Beauty appears in many forms, so no single style is necessary for passion. It has divine sway, winning excessive leniency even after perjury; yet it begets pride and, by the husband’s assertion, cannot be united with chastity.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ELEGY III.

Beauty appears in many forms, including modesty, liveliness, learning, simplicity, graceful movement, singing, skilled playing, height, shortness, adornment, hair color, complexion, youth, and maturity. The poet finds no single style of beauty necessary for passion.

ELEGY III.

Beauty is presented as having divine sway over the Gods. The mistress’s beauty remains unchanged after her perjury, and her beauty is treated as a reason for the Gods’ excessive leniency.

beauty in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Beauty gives confidence, pride, and lasting privilege, even protecting a faithless girl from divine offense. Yet it breeds arrogance, jealous desire, and adultery; its possessors should not scorn lesser partners or let beauty promise rule.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

XVII

Beauty gives people confidence and breeds arrogance. The speaker says that a beautiful face can inspire pride and a promise of rule, but argues that beauty should not lead its possessor to scorn a lesser partner.

III

Beauty gives the faithless girl lasting privilege despite her false oath and makes the gods reluctant to offend her. It also increases the danger of jealousy and adultery because a guarded beautiful woman seems especially desirable.
