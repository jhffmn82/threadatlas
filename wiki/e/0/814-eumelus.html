Eumelus

person · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: king of men, son of Admetus

Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, Eumelus leads a chariot race until Minerva breaks his yoke; injured and last, he receives Achilles’ bronze breastplate. In The Fall of Troy, Eumelus enters and leads a chariot race with his horses, then falls from his car and is treated by Podaleirius.

Eumelus in The Iliad

Abstract written by a model

Eumelus, son of Admetus and an excellent horseman, leads a chariot race until Minerva breaks his yoke; injured and last, he receives Achilles’ bronze breastplate.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

The first prize he offered was for the chariot races—a woman

Eumelus, son of Admetus and an excellent horseman, initially leads the chariot race. Minerva breaks his yoke, throws him from his chariot, and leaves him injured, so he finishes last; Achilles gives him a bronze breastplate in compensation.
