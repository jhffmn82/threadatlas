fighting trees in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04

Fighting trees guard the forest by attacking strangers with bending, twining branches. At the first row, they stop the travelers until the Tin Woodman cuts a branch, allowing passage.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XIX

The first row of trees in the forest attacks strangers by bending and twining its branches around them. The Tin Woodman cuts one tree's branch, after which the travelers can pass; they believe these trees act as policemen of the forest to keep strangers out.
