Calchas in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Calchas is a seer and prophet of the Greek army. He declares that the Greeks can sail to Troy and sack it only if Iphigenia is sacrificed to Diana, and he knows the truth about Agamemnon’s supposed marriage arrangement. His prophecy causes Agamemnon to fear disclosure and seek his counsel.

2 sections; each heading opens the text

IPHIGENIA IN AULIS.

Calchas declares that the Greeks will obtain a voyage and sack Troy only if Iphigenia is sacrificed to Diana. He is one of the few people who knows the truth about Agamemnon’s supposed marriage arrangement for Iphigenia.

[_Enter a Messenger_.[29]]

Calchas has pronounced a prophecy requiring Iphigenia's sacrifice. Agamemnon fears that Calchas will reveal the prophecy to the Greek army, and he says he will consult Calchas about the Goddess's will.

Calchas in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Calchas is a famous seer who foretells the serpent and sparrows omen. Returning from Troy with Amphilochus, he reaches the place near Clarus on foot, is surpassed by Mopsus in determining figs on a wild fig-tree, and dies of vexation.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE MELAMPODIA

Calchas returned from Troy with Amphilochus and came on foot to the place near Clarus. After finding Mopsus to be a greater seer, he asked him to determine the number of figs on a small wild fig-tree; when Mopsus answered correctly, Calchas died of vexation.

Calchas in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Calchas is a diviner who interprets signs and prophecies concerning the Greeks’ war against Troy. At Aulis he reads the serpent and sparrows as Zeus’s sign that Troy will fall after ten years, but says the Greeks cannot sail unless Iphigenia is sacrificed to Artemis. During the war he declares that Achilles and Hercules’ bow and arrows are required to capture Troy, and that Helenus knows the city’s protective oracles. After Troy is destroyed, he delays the Greeks because Athena is angry at Ajax’s impiety. He travels to Colophon, loses a divination contest to Mopsus, dies, and is buried at Notium.

Calchas in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

Calchas is an Achaean seer and prophet, wise in prophecy-lore and guided by Phoebus, birds, stars, and other signs. He foretells Ilium’s fall in the tenth year, warns of danger, identifies Philoctetes as necessary, and declares Aeneas destined to survive and found a city. When Troy cannot be taken by force, he interprets signs in favor of cunning, supports Odysseus, and urges the Wooden Horse. Afterward, foreseeing destruction at the Capherean Rocks, he refuses the Argive return and orders Hecuba’s stone body carried across the Hellespont.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Now had the Argives burst the gates, had breached

Calchas is identified as the prophet whose earlier prophecy is recalled by Neleus' son.

BOOK XII

Calchas, guided by Phoebus, birds, stars, and other signs, tells the Achaean chiefs that Troy cannot be taken by force alone. He interprets the falcon and dove as a sign that cunning stratagem must be used, and later urges the Achaeans to build the Wooden Horse.

BOOK XIV.

Calchas foresees destruction for the Argives near the Capherean Rocks and refuses to sail with them. He orders that Hecuba's transformed stone body be carried across the Hellespont.
