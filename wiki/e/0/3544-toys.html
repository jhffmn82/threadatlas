Abstract written by a model

Toys are playthings made by Claus for children of the world, intended to be loved rather than feared. He first gives toy animals to visiting children; after Mayrie becomes frightened, he favors gentler squirrels, rabbits, deer, lambkins, wooden rabbits, and wooden lambs. As children multiply and spread, Claus makes toys in larger quantities, aided by Kilter, Peter, Nuter, and Wisk. The toys bring happiness and encourage obedience, protecting children from the Awgwas' influence, though the Awgwas steal them until they are destroyed. Claus then carries toys safely to dwellings and villages. Over time his work becomes increasingly beautiful: dolls may say "papa" and "mama," lambs squeak, birds whistle, horses have wheels, and a donkey nods. He yearly loads toys for children, and when chimneys prevent delivery, the little folk, parents, and toy-shops help distribute them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

4. How Little Mayrie Became Frightened

Claus's toy animals spread in fame to cottages on the plain and in the village, and no child who visits him goes away empty-handed. After Mayrie's reaction, he chooses squirrels, rabbits, deer, and lambkins as gentler models, and begins making wooden rabbits and lambs.

6. The Wickedness of the Awgwas

The children's toys made by Claus bring happiness and encourage children to be obedient, reducing the Awgwas' influence over them. The Awgwas repeatedly steal the toys and store them in a lonely cavern, while Claus continues making more until his shelves are filled.

7. The Great Battle Between Good and Evil

Claus continues making toys in the Laughing Valley while the immortals fight to protect him. After the Awgwas are destroyed, Ak tells Claus that he can carry the toys to dwellings and villages without fear.

11. How the First Stockings Were Hung by the Chimneys

Santa Claus's toys become more beautiful and attractive as he works on them throughout the year. Some dolls say "papa" and "mama," lambs squeak, birds whistle, horses have wheels, and one donkey has a nodding head. On Christmas Eve, he loads three sacks and the rest of the sledge with toys for children.

2. When the World Grew Old

Santa Claus fashions new toys and begins making them in larger quantities as more children are born and spread across the world. Kilter, Peter, Nuter, and Wisk help him make the toys and prepare his yearly load.

3. The Deputies of Santa Claus

Santa Claus is unable to give away some toys when narrow chimneys block access to children. He later distributes toys with help from the four little folk, parents, and toy-shops.
