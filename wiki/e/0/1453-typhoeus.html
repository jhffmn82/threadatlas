Typhoeus in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Typhoeus is a marvellous, dreadful monster: youngest child of Earth through Tartarus, and also born to Hera apart from Zeus. Aided by Aphrodite at birth and raised by a dragoness, he becomes a cruel plague to men. With strength, untiring feet, a hundred snake- and dragon-like heads, fire, and many voices, he threatens mortals and immortals. Zeus burns his heads, defeats him, hurls him into Tartarus, and confines him there.

Typhoeus is a hugely powerful monster with untiring feet, a hundred snake heads, flashing fire in his eyes and heads, and voices that imitate gods, bulls, lions, whelps, and hissing snakes. He threatens to rule mortals and immortals, but Zeus burns his heads, defeats him, and casts him into Tartarus.

Typhœus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Typhœus is a Giant who aspires to Heaven and is involved in the war of the Gods with the Giants. Defeated by the Gods, he is buried beneath Trinacria and Ætna, with his limbs pinned under Pelorus, Pachynus, and Lilybœum. He struggles to rise, casts out sand and flame, and his efforts are believed to cause fires and earthquakes.

opening

Typhœus is a giant who once hoped to reach the abodes of Heaven. Confined beneath Trinacria and Ætna, he repeatedly struggles to rise, casts out sand, vomits flame, and tries to throw off the earth and mountains covering him.

notes .. notes

Typhœus was described as a Giant defeated by the Gods and buried beneath Mount Ætna. His attempts to free himself from the mountain’s weight were believed to cause fires and earthquakes.
