Abstract written by a model

In A Study in Scarlet, Lestrade investigates the Lauriston Gardens and Stangerson murders, gathers clues, and helps capture Jefferson Hope. In The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes, Inspector Lestrade investigates the Boscombe Valley case and the St. Simon marriage and disappearance matter for Scotland Yard. In The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes, Inspector Lestrade leads the investigation into the Browner and Fairbairn murders, secures Jim Browner’s arrest, and reports his confession. In The Hound of the Baskervilles, Inspector Lestrade joins Holmes’s ambush at Merripit House, helps rescue Sir Henry, gives him brandy, and remains there. In The Return of Sherlock Holmes, Inspector Lestrade conducts investigations, makes arrests, writes reports, works with Holmes, and takes charge of arrests and police inquiries. In His Last Bow, Lestrade investigates the Bruce-Partington Plans case, reports evidence, and joins the Caulfield Gardens operation.

Lestrade in A Study in Scarlet

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1886 · the work’s page · read it where Lestrade first appears

Abstract written by a model

Lestrade is a quick, energetic Scotland Yard inspector and detective. At 3, Lauriston Gardens, he joins the investigation and finds the blood-red word “RACHE,” mistakenly reading it as the start of “Rachel” rather than “revenge.” While Gregson follows another suspect, Lestrade pursues Joseph Stangerson, traces him to Halliday’s Private Hotel, and discovers his murder. He gathers Stangerson’s pills, Drebber’s purse, a telegram, and other clues, recording an account in shorthand. He reports the escapee’s description and helps capture and restrain Jefferson Hope. Newspapers credit Lestrade and Gregson with the capture, though Holmes says that credit is incorrect.

CHAPTER III.

Lestrade participates in the investigation at 3, Lauriston Gardens. He discovers the blood-red inscription “RACHE” on the wall and interprets it as the beginning of the female name Rachel, but Holmes says it is the German word for “revenge.”

CHAPTER VI.

G. Lestrade is pursuing Joseph Stangerson while Tobias Gregson is pursuing Arthur Charpentier. He arrives at Sherlock Holmes’s rooms troubled and reports that Stangerson was murdered at Halliday’s Private Hotel.

CHAPTER VII.

Inspector Lestrade traces Joseph Stangerson to Halliday’s Private Hotel and discovers his murdered body. He reports the blood-written word “RACHE,” the witness’s description of the escaping man, and the clues found in the room. Lestrade helps capture and restrain Jefferson Hope.

CHAPTER VII.

Inspector Lestrade is named with Inspector Gregson as one of the Scotland Yard officials whom the Echo credits with Jefferson Hope’s capture. Sherlock Holmes expects them to be angry because Hope’s death prevents their anticipated public advertisement.

Inspector Lestrade in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25 · the work’s page · read it where Inspector Lestrade first appears

Abstract written by a model

Inspector Lestrade is a lean, furtive-looking official detective and member of Scotland Yard. In the Boscombe Valley investigation, retained to work in James McCarthy’s interest, he nevertheless sees the charge against McCarthy junior as plain and increasingly clear, believes McCarthy junior killed McCarthy senior, examines the stone weapon without finding marks, and remains skeptical of Sherlock Holmes’s theories. Puzzled by the case, he refers it to Holmes, meets Holmes and Dr. Watson at Ross, objects that Holmes may raise Miss Turner’s hopes, and accompanies Holmes to the prison and crime scene. Later, acting for Scotland Yard in the St. Simon marriage and wife-disappearance matter, he treats it seriously, links Hatty’s sighting with Flora Millar, fears Lady St. Simon’s body may be in the Serpentine, and brings wedding clothes, a note, and a hotel-bill fragment. Holmes identifies those clues as pointing to Francis Hay Moulton rather than Flora Millar.

Abstract written by a model

Inspector Lestrade is a Scotland Yard detective inspector, considered one of its smartest officers, who leads the investigation into the murders of Mary Browner and Alec Fairbairn. He examines the packet, brings Holmes and Watson to Susan Cushing’s house, and initially supports a medical-student theory. Inspector Lestrade in The Return of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1903-09-26 · the work’s page · read it where Inspector Lestrade first appears

Abstract written by a model

Inspector Lestrade is an experienced plain-clothes detective, police inspector, and police official who works for Scotland Yard. He conducts official investigations, makes arrests, writes reports, and often works alongside Sherlock Holmes while retaining responsibility for police action. He arrives with officers when Holmes summons him and takes charge of Colonel Sebastian Moran’s arrest for the shooting of Ronald Adair; Holmes publicly credits Lestrade with the arrest, enhancing Lestrade’s reputation. In the Jonas Oldacre case, Lestrade leads the investigation, arrests John Hector McFarlane for Oldacre’s apparent murder, and initially considers the evidence conclusive despite Holmes’s objections. Once Oldacre’s deception is exposed, Lestrade recognizes that Holmes saved an innocent man and prevented a scandal, learns how the evidence was fabricated, and detains Oldacre for conspiracy or possibly attempted murder. Later, Lestrade investigates the destruction of Napoleon busts, a burglary, and an unidentified murder victim at 131 Pitt Street. He identifies the victim as Pietro Venucci, initially favors a Mafia-pursuit explanation, helps arrest Beppo, supplies Holmes with information, witnesses the recovery of the pearl, and praises Holmes on behalf of Scotland Yard. Finally, he brings Holmes into the room where Lucas was killed, investigates mismatched bloodstains under a drugget, and questions Constable MacPherson about a young woman who entered, fainted, and apparently moved it.

Lestrade in His Last Bow

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1908-04-17 · the work’s page · read it where Lestrade first appears

Abstract written by a model

Lestrade is a thin, austere Scotland Yard detective and police inspector who works for the police. In the Bruce-Partington Plans case, he examines every carriage of the Metropolitan train and argues that West intended to sell the plans, was killed by a foreign agent on the train, and was thrown onto the track. He accompanies Mycroft Holmes, Sherlock Holmes, and Dr. Watson in discussing and investigating the case, reports a passenger’s account of a thud near Aldgate, and later joins the Caulfield Gardens operation, criticizing burglary while accepting that an advertisement may expose the culprit.

The Adventure of the Bruce-Partington Plans

Lestrade accompanies Mycroft Holmes to Sherlock Holmes’s rooms and listens to the discussion of the case. He argues that West took the plans to sell them, was murdered by a foreign agent in a train, and had his body thrown onto the track.

An hour later Holmes, Lestrade and I stood upon the Underground

Inspector Lestrade accompanies Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson to the Underground scene and reports that a passenger heard a heavy thud near Aldgate at about 11:40 on Monday night. He says that the train carriages were carefully examined, though Holmes explains that he wanted to inspect the roof rather than the carriages.
