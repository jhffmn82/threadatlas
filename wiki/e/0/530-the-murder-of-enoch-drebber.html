Abstract written by a model

The murder of Enoch J. Drebber takes place during the night in an empty house at 3, Lauriston Gardens on Brixton Road. Drebber has no wound, but blood is scattered in the room; a woman’s wedding ring lies beneath his body, and an inscription, candle, and ring are present at the scene. Holmes concludes that poison was forcibly administered and that the killing was deliberate, motivated by a romantic feud and private revenge rather than robbery or politics. Evidence suggests Drebber entered with Square-toes, who grew agitated before the death. The case initially remains unsolved: Gregson arrests Arthur Charpentier, while Lestrade discovers that Joseph Stangerson has also been murdered. Holmes investigates the dropped ring and uses a newspaper advertisement to draw back the murderer. The two pills found in Stangerson’s room link the killings, leading Holmes to conclude that Jefferson Hope killed Drebber.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER III.

The murder of Enoch J. Drebber occurs during the night at 3, Lauriston Gardens. There is no wound on Drebber, but blood is scattered around the room, a woman’s wedding-ring is found beneath the body, and Sherlock Holmes concludes that poison was used.

CHAPTER IV.

The murder occurred inside the empty house in Lauriston Gardens after the two suspects entered together. Holmes says that the evidence indicates Square-toes became increasingly agitated while speaking before the tragedy occurred.

CHAPTER V.

The Brixton Road murder remains unsolved in this unit, though Sherlock Holmes develops a theory involving poison, a dropped wedding ring, and a murderer who returned to the scene. The case prompts Holmes to use a newspaper advertisement as a trap.

CHAPTER VI.

The Brixton murder concerns the death of Enoch J. Drebber in an empty house on Brixton Road. Tobias Gregson arrests Arthur Charpentier for the crime, while G. Lestrade’s investigation leads to the separate discovery that Joseph Stangerson has also been murdered.

CHAPTER VII.

The murder of Enoch Drebber is linked to the murder of Joseph Stangerson by the discovery of the two pills in Stangerson’s room. Sherlock Holmes concludes that Jefferson Hope committed the murder.

CHAPTER VII.

The murder of Enoch Drebber was carried out deliberately by poison, which was forced upon him. Holmes concludes that the crime was a private revenge connected with a woman rather than robbery or politics.
