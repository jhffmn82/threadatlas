Abstract written by a model

In The Odyssey, Alcinous is king of the Phaeacians and a generous host who organizes Ulysses’s return home. In The Argonautica, Alcinous is king of the Phaeacians who judges Medea’s status, prevents war, and sends the Argonauts away with gifts. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Alcinous is the wise and just king who welcomes Ulysses and arranges his games, feast, gifts, and transport home.

Alcinous in The Odyssey

Homer · about 632 to 599 BC · the work’s page · read it where Alcinous first appears

Abstract written by a model

Alcinous is king of the Phaeacians, holding the whole power of their state after Nausithous’s death. Husband of Arete and father of Nausicaa, he lives in the town’s finest house and first appears providing Nausicaa with a waggon and mules. His central importance is as a powerful, generous host and organizer of Ulysses’s return. He receives Ulysses from the hearth, seats and feeds him, orders offerings and a bed, and promises either escort home or, should he remain, a house, estate, and marriage to Nausicaa. He leads the Phaeacians in assembly, has a new ship manned, hosts a banquet, and responds to Ulysses’s distress by redirecting the gathering toward sports and dance. He praises Phaeacian skill, requires gifts of clothing and gold for Ulysses, makes Euryalus apologize and give a present, arranges a bath, and adds a golden goblet. Recognizing Ulysses as well disposed rather than deceptive, he asks for his account of those who went to Troy and died, while repeatedly assuring him of gifts and escort. At the end, he adds tripods and cauldrons, secures the gifts aboard ship, sends Ulysses away, and recognizes Neptune’s punishment as fulfillment of an old prophecy.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK VI

Alcinous reigns over the Phaeacians after Nausithous’s death, and the power of the state is vested in him. He provides Nausicaa with a waggon and mules when she asks for them, and his house is described as the finest in the town.

BOOK VII

Alcinous receives Ulysses, raises him from the hearth, gives him a seat, and orders food, drink, and offerings to be provided. He promises to arrange an escort to Ulysses’s country and offers him a house and estate, as well as marriage to Nausicaa if Ulysses wishes to stay. He then has a bed prepared for Ulysses.

BOOK VIII

Alcinous leads Ulysses and the Phaeacians to the assembly and orders that a new ship be manned to escort Ulysses home. He provides a lavish banquet, notices Ulysses’ distress during Demodocus’ song, and directs the company to proceed to athletic sports and dancing.

Alcinous tells Laodamas and Halius to dance alone, praises the Phaeacians' abilities, and orders the Phaeacians to give Ulysses cloaks, shirts, and gold. He also requires Euryalus to apologize and give Ulysses a present, arranges for Ulysses to receive a bath and his gifts, and gives him a golden goblet.

Alcinous praises Ulysses’s language and asks him to continue describing the heroes who went to Troy and died. He promises that the Phaeacians will provide Ulysses with gifts and an escort.

BOOK XIII

Alcinous arranges for the Phaeacians to give Ulysses additional tripods and cauldrons and ensures that his gifts are securely stowed. He sends Ulysses to his ship and later recognizes Neptune’s punishment of the Phaeacians as the fulfillment of an old prophecy.

Alcinous in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · 299 to 200 BC · the work’s page · read it where Alcinous first appears

Abstract written by a model

Alcinous is king and lord of the Phaeacians, married to Arete, and bears a golden staff of justice for righteous judgments. He seeks to avert war between the Argonauts and Colchians by judging Medea’s status, then upholds his ruling after her marriage and sends the Argonauts away with gifts.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Fronting the Ionian gulf there lies an island in the Ceraunian sea,

Alcinous seeks to prevent war between the Argonauts and the Colchians and proposes to judge Medea's case according to her marital status. He rules that a maiden must be returned to her father, but that a woman who shares a husband's bed must remain with her husband; after Jason and Medea's marriage, he upholds this judgment and sends the Argonauts away with gifts.
