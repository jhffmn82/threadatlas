the bow-stringing contest in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

The bow-stringing contest tests the suitors by requiring them to string Ulysses’s bow and shoot an arrow through twelve axes. Telemachus and the suitors attempt it, but fail; Antinous postpones it until after the feast of Apollo. Ulysses then strings the bow and completes the trial, surpassing all suitors.

contest of the bow in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Penelope’s test to choose a husband requires shooting an arrow through twelve rings; the suitors cannot bend Ulysses’ bow, but Ulysses succeeds and immediately begins his attack.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXIII

The contest of the bow is Penelope's test for choosing among the suitors. The winner is to shoot an arrow through twelve rings, but the suitors cannot even bend Ulysses' bow, while Ulysses completes the feat and immediately begins his attack.
