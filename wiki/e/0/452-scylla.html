Abstract written by a model

In The Odyssey, Scylla is an immortal, savage sea monster of the rock who seizes and eats six of Ulysses's men. In The Argonautica, Scylla is a deadly sea monster near a sea passage whose hideous lair and jaws Hera warns Thetis to keep the heroes away from. In The Aeneid, Scylla is a sea monster who hides in the straits, draws vessels into her eddy, and dashes them against the rocks. In The Library, Scylla is a sea monster with a woman's face, six dog heads, and twelve dog feet who seizes and eats six of Ulysses's companions; another account makes her the daughter of Nisus. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Scylla is a maiden and sea nymph who rejects Glaucus, is transformed by Circe into a monster, and deprives Ulysses of his companions. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Scylla begins as a fair virgin and becomes a six-headed, snaky monster who devours sailors and is transformed into rock.

Scylla in The Odyssey

Homer · about 632 to 599 BC · the work’s page · read it where Scylla first appears

Abstract written by a model

Scylla is a dreadful, invincible, immortal, savage monster of the rock in the dangerous straits. She lives deep in a cavern, with twelve misshapen feet, six prodigiously long necks, and six heads, each with three close-set rows of teeth. No ship passes without losing men: she catches one in each mouth. In the recorded encounter, she suddenly seizes six of Ulysses's best men, carries them to her rock, and munches them at her den's mouth.

Scylla in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Scylla first appears

Abstract written by a model

Scylla is a maiden and sea nymph who lives near the sea. She flees and rejects Glaucus despite his desire and pleas. Circe transforms her into a monster, whom Scylla hates; Scylla then deprives Ulysses of his companions in revenge before becoming Scylla rock.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

FABLE VIII. [XIII.898-968]

Scylla returns from the company of Galatea and wanders along the shore or cools herself in enclosed sea waters. She encounters the transformed Glaucus, flees from him to a mountain, and listens as he explains his nature and transformation. She leaves him while he is still speaking, rejecting his advances.

BOOK THE FOURTEENTH.

Scylla rejects Glaucus’s promises, entreaties, caresses, and suit. Circe transforms her into a monster, and Scylla later takes revenge by depriving Ulysses of his companions before becoming a rock.

Scylla in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Scylla first appears

Abstract written by a model

Scylla begins as a fair virgin of Sicily, a favorite of the water-nymphs who rejects suitors, including Glaucus. Circe poisons the bay where she bathes, and Scylla becomes a six-headed, snaky monster with serpents and barking forms around her. Living in a high cliff cave, she guards a strait with Charybdis, devours sailors, and takes six of Ulysses's men. After becoming a terror to mariners, she is transformed into rock.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

PROSERPINE

Scylla is a beautiful maiden and favorite of the water-nymphs who rejects Glaucus. Circe, jealous of her, poisons the bay where Scylla bathes, causing serpents and barking monsters to grow around her. Scylla becomes a monstrous terror who devours sailors, destroys six of Ulysses's companions, and is eventually turned into a rock.

Chapter XIX

Scylla was a fair virgin of Sicily and a favorite of the Sea-Nymphs. She had many suitors but rejected them, and she visited Galatea’s grotto to tell her how she was being pursued. She listened as Galatea explained the events that led to Acis’s death.

Chapter XXII

Scylla is a snaky monster living in a high cliff cave, with six heads and a mouth for each head. As Ulysses's vessel passes, she seizes six of his men and carries them away.
