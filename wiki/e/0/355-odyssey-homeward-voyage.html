Odyssey homeward voyage in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Odyssey homeward voyage is Ulysses’s Phaeacian escort home. Alcinous promises safe passage, and the Phaeacians prepare gifts, festivities, and a ship despite Neptune’s anger at their readiness to escort travelers. In one night, they carry the sleeping Ulysses from Scheria to Ithaca; they return home, and Neptune punishes their ship for helping him.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK VII

Alcinous announces that the Phaeacians will discuss and arrange Ulysses’s escort at a sacrificial banquet the next morning. He promises that Ulysses will come to no harm during the journey and that Phaeacian sailors will carry him over smooth waters.

“Aldermen and town councillors of the Phaeacians, let Demodocus cease

The Phaeacians are preparing an escort for the guest, along with gifts and festivities, all given in his honour. Their readiness to escort people is also the reason Neptune is said to be angry with them.

BOOK XIII

The homeward voyage ends when the Phaeacians carry Ulysses from Scheria to Ithaca in a single night while he sleeps. After landing, the Phaeacians return home, while Neptune punishes their ship for assisting him.
