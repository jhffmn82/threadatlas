  • In "Retrieval Augmented Generation with Rich Answer Encoding," retrieval quality was the focus of a framework seeking diverse, knowledge-relevant passages and was supported by reported improvements on MSMARCO and KILT-WoW.
  • In "BERGEN: A Benchmarking Library for Retrieval-Augmented Generation," retrieval quality was identified as an important but insufficiently analyzed factor affecting response generation, with ranking refinement and reranking improving context relevance.

  • In "RAGAS: Automated Evaluation of Retrieval Augmented Generation," retrieval quality was defined as the ability of a retrieval system to identify relevant and focused context passages, with RAGAS providing metrics to assess it.
  • In "FACTS About Building Retrieval Augmented Generation-based Chatbots," retrieval relevancy was identified as strongly determining LLM response generation quality and as depending on metadata enrichment, chunking, query rephrasal, search, reranking, and structured-document handling.
  • In "Document Segmentation Matters for Retrieval-Augmented Generation," retrieval performance was evaluated across six OpenQA benchmarks using Hits@5 and Hits@20, with PIC achieving the highest average retrieval scores among the listed methods.
  • In "CausalRAG: Integrating Causal Graphs into Retrieval-Augmented Generation," retrieval quality was described as limited by disrupted context, reliance on semantic rather than causal relevance, and inaccurate document selection; CausalRAG was introduced to improve it.
  • In "Ragas: Automated Evaluation of Retrieval Augmented Generation," retrieval was described as a RAG component whose evaluation concerns identifying relevant and focused context passages.
  • In "Question Decomposition for Retrieval-Augmented Generation," retrieval quality was described as strongly affecting RAG performance and as something the proposed approach seeks to improve through question decomposition and reranking.
  • In "Question Decomposition for Retrieval-Augmented Generation," retrieval accuracy was assessed with benchmark-specific retrieval metrics for MultiHop-RAG and answer-centric metrics for HotpotQA to determine whether systems recovered evidence for multi-hop answers.

retrieval quality in Retrieval Augmented Generation with Rich Answer Encoding

Wenyu Huang, Mirella Lapata, Pavlos Vougiouklis, Nikos Papasarantopoulos, Jeff Z. Pan · 2023

Retrieval quality is the focus of a proposed framework that seeks diverse, knowledge-relevant passages. It is positioned against KG-FiD, KGI, RE2G, Hindsight, and RAG, and reports 12.1% Recall@1 improvement on MSMARCO and 8.9% R-Prec improvement on KILT-WoW as evidence of more robust retrieval.

Introduction

The proposed framework improves retrieval quality relative to RAG, with reported gains of 12.1% in Recall@1 on MSMARCO and 8.9% in R-Prec on KILT-WoW. The text presents these gains as evidence that the approach produces more robust retrieval.

Related Works

Retrieval quality is a central concern in the reviewed methods. KG-FiD, KGI, RE2G, and Hindsight seek to improve it through knowledge graphs, joint training, reranking, or a hindsight retriever, while the proposed approach seeks a more diverse set of knowledge-relevant passages.

retrieval quality in Optimizing LLM Based Retrieval Augmented Generation Pipelines in the Financial Domain

Yiyun Zhao, Hanoz Bhathena, Prateek Singh, Saket Sharma, Bernardo Ramos, Aviral Joshi, Swaroop Gadiyaram · 2024

Retrieval quality has a strong influence on generation quality. LLM performance decreases when relevant source information is missing, when more distractor documents are included, or when relevant documents are placed lower in the retrieval list. Retrieval quality affects answer generation through gold-document presence, the number of retrieved documents, and their ordering. The results show that retrieval quality cannot be optimized by maximizing recall alone because models are sensitive to retrieval noise.
