Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Atalanta is a swift-footed maiden who races Hippomenes for marriage and loses when he uses three golden apples supplied by Aphrodite. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Atalanta is a hunter among Meleager's associates who joins the Calydonian boar hunt and receives its skin and glory before losing them to Thestius's sons. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Atalanta is a swift virgin huntress who joins the Calydonian boar hunt, marries Hippomenes after losing a race to him, and is transformed into a lion. In The Library, Atalanta is an Arcadian huntress who joins the Calydonian boar hunt, races and marries Melanion, and is transformed into a lion with him. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Atalanta is a hunter who first wounds the Calydonian boar and is represented among the hunters on a temple gable. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Atalanta is a skilled huntress who wounds the Calydonian boar and races Hippomenes for marriage before Cybele transforms them into lions.

Atalanta in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Atalanta first appears

Abstract written by a model

Atalanta is a beautiful, swift virgin, daughter of Schœneus and identified as a daughter of the king of Arcadia, as well as a Tegeæan. Abandoned by her father in one account, she is nourished by a she-bear and discovered by hunters; hunting becomes her favorite pursuit. Armed with a bow, she joins the Calydonian boar hunt, first wounds the boar, and receives its head and hide from Meleager, provoking envy. She also slays two Centaurs who offer her violence. Warned by an oracle that marriage will destroy her, she remains single and permits marriage only to a man who can outrun her, with unsuccessful suitors dying. Hippomenes wins when she stops for golden apples, and she marries him. After profaning Jupiter’s temple with Melanion, she is transformed into a lion or lioness.

FABLE IV. [VIII.260-546]

Atalanta, the daughter of the king of Arcadia, joins the hunt armed with a bow. Her arrow first draws blood from the boar, and Meleager honors her by giving her the boar’s head and hide, provoking the envy of the others.

FABLE IX. [X.519-707]

Atalanta is renowned for both her beauty and her speed. After an oracle warns that marriage will destroy her, she agrees to marry only a suitor who can outrun her, while the defeated suitors must die; she falls in love with Hippomenes but loses their race after stopping to gather the golden apples.

Atalanta in The Library

Apollodorus · about AD 70 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Atalanta first appears

Abstract written by a model

Atalanta, a daughter of Schoeneus from Arcadia, was exposed because her father wanted male children, suckled by a she-bear, and raised by hunters. A virgin and skilled armed huntress, she killed the centaurs Rhoecus and Hylaeus after they tried to force her, joined the Calydonian boar hunt despite objections, first struck the boar, and received its skin from Meleager after it was taken from her and reclaimed. She also defeated Peleus in wrestling. In her marriage race, she lost to Melanion after stopping for golden apples, married him, and had Parthenopaeus in the main account. She and Melanion became lions after intercourse in Zeus’s precinct. Other accounts name Maenalus as her father and Hippomenes as her spouse.
