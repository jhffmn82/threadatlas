the voyage home

Abstract written by a model

The voyage home is Ulysses’s long return from Troy, marked by repeated dangers, detours, and losses. It passes through the lands of the Cicons, Lotus-eaters, Cyclops, Aeolus, Laestrygonians at Telepylos, Circe on Aeaea, Hades, the Sirens, Scylla and Charybdis, and the island of the sun-god. After the shipwreck, Ulysses alone reaches the Ogygian island, where Calypso keeps him. He later reaches the Phaeacians, who return him to his country with treasure. Teiresias also foretells further inland travel, sacrifices, and a peaceful old age.

Ulysses and his men are trying to continue their voyage home after reaching the Aeaean island. Circe tells them to remain with her until they regain their strength before resuming the journey.

“Here she ended, and dawn enthroned in gold began to show in heaven,

The voyage home is interrupted by the Sirens, Scylla and Charybdis, the island of the sun-god, and the shipwreck. Ulysses alone continues after the wreck and is eventually carried to the Ogygian island.

BOOK XXIII

Ulysses recounts that his journey included the Cicons, the Lotus-eaters, the Cyclops, Aeolus, the Laestrygonians, Circe, Hades, the Sirens, Scylla, Charybdis, the cattle of the sun-god, Calypso, and the Phaeacians. He says that the Phaeacians brought him back to his country with gold, bronze, and clothing. He also reports Teiresias's prophecy that he must travel far inland carrying an oar, perform sacrifices, and eventually die peacefully from the sea when he is old.
