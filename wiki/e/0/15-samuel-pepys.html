Samuel Pepys · 1660/1669

Samuel Pepys is a person and diarist whose records follow his work, family obligations, finances, household, health, and recreations. He serves in command as an office official at the Navy Office, handling business, letters, naval financial difficulties, sealing matters, and reports to the Duke of York alongside Sir W. Batten and Sir W. Pen. Early on, he arranges cloth for my Lord, accompanies him to Deptford, and later writes to him at sea. Narrative, section by section

7 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

The narrator visits Lambeth, works at home, and writes a letter to the Duke. He arranges for £300 of cloth to be purchased for my Lord through Alderman Backwell, accompanies my Lord to Deptford, and takes leave of him aboard the Dutch yacht before returning toward London with Captain Ferrers.

opening

The diarist arranges the purchase and delivery of cloth for my Lord, attends to office, household, and financial matters, and visits family and friends. He recovers from his illness, supervises the painters' work on his stairs, and is pleased when it is completed.

JULY 1661 July 1st.

The diarist bought a chest of drawers for his chamber and an Indian gown, attended to business at Westminster and the office, and took singing lessons with Theodore Goodgroome. He visited Sir William Davenant's Opera and the Theatre, wrote letters to his Lord at sea, and learned of his uncle's illness and Mr. Saml. Crew's death.

opening

Samuel Pepys reviews and manages his uncle’s will, papers, debts, surrenders, and property with advice from Dr. John Pepys, Sir Robert Bernard, Mr. Moore, and others. He travels from Brampton to London, resumes business at the Navy Office, visits friends and theatres, and worries that the estate will yield less than expected.

opening

Samuel Pepys visits Lord Hinchingbroke during his illness, consults with Lady Sandwich, and helps house her three younger sons to protect them from possible smallpox. He reports the Navy’s financial difficulties to the Duke of York with Sir W. Batten and Sir W. Pen, follows news of Lord Sandwich’s health, attends plays and social gatherings, and worries that his fondness for plays is distracting him from business.

opening

The narrator conducts sealing business at Worcester House and with the Lord Privy Seal, walks home after losing his coach, and spends time with Mr. Marsh and others. He organizes papers, visits family and associates, assists with proving his uncle’s will, takes his wife to see "The Witts" at the Opera, and attends to various office and household matters. He is troubled by his parents’ discontent and by losing the company of the children, who his Lady keeps at home. He views a baboon brought from Guiny and believes it may understand English and perhaps be taught to speak or make signs.

opening

The diarist handles family accounts, legal business, household changes, and arrangements concerning Pall and Jane. He attends several theatre performances and social gatherings, but recognizes that his spending and pleasure-seeking are interfering with business and resolves to amend this.
