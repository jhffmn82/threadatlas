The Little Iliad

object · 1 work · 3 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Lesser Iliad, Little Iliad, THE LITTLE ILIAD, _Lesser Iliad_, _Little Iliad_

Abstract written by a model

The Little Iliad is a four-book epic poem ascribed to Lesches of Pyrrha or Mitylene, though another record calls it Homer’s composition. It elaborates material associated with the Sack of Troy: Achilles’ arms are adjudged to Odysseus, Aias becomes mad, Philoctetes and Neoptolemus arrive, the wooden horse is made, the Palladium is stolen, and the sack unfolds.

The Little Iliad is in one work on this site, Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

The Ionic School

The Little Iliad by Lesches or Lescheos elaborated material associated with the Sack of Troy. It included the adjudgment of Achilles’ arms to Odysseus, the madness of Aias, the recovery of Philoctetes, the arrival of Neoptolemus, the making of the wooden horse, and the theft of the Palladium.

THE LITTLE ILIAD

The Little Iliad is a four-book epic by Lesches of Mitylene. It describes the adjudging of Achilles’ arms, the bringing of Philoctetes and Neoptolemus to Troy, the construction of the wooden horse, and events during the sack of Ilium.

THE PHOCAIS

The Lesser Iliad was composed by Homer while he was living with Thestorides.
