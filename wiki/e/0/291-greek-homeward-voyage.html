the sea voyage to Greece in The Trojan Women of Euripides

Euripides · -0414

After Troy’s fall, Helen is sent by ship toward Greece under Menelaus’s orders, while Trojan captive women are carried toward Argos and Greek lands, mourning exile as galleys depart the ruined city.

Greek homeward voyage in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

The Greek homeward voyage is prepared after captives are assigned and the fleet receives plunder. It is to carry Helen from Troy to Greece for death, but Minerva and Neptune plan thunder, winds, waves, and shipwreck, while the Chorus prays lightning destroys the ship.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE TROJAN CAPTIVES.

The Greek homeward voyage is prepared after the captives have been assigned and the fleet has received its plunder. Minerva and Neptune intend to make the voyage disastrous through thunder, violent winds, waves, and shipwreck.

MENELAUS, HECUBA, CHORUS.

Menelaus intends to carry Helen across the sea to Greece, where she is to be killed. The Chorus imagines the voyage as a miserable transport from Troy and prays that lightning will destroy the ship before Helen reaches Sparta.

Achaean homeward voyage in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

The Achaean homeward voyage is disrupted by divine displeasure, quarrels, storms, deaths, and shipwrecks. Nestor sails with Diomed’s group toward Pylos, while Menelaus is driven toward Crete and Egypt and other leaders follow different routes.
