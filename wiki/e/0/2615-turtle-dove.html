turtle-dove in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

The turtle-dove, an affectionate bird, shares lifelong friendship with the parrot and is urged to mourn the parrot above other birds.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ELEGY VI.

The turtle-dove is urged to mourn the parrot above the other birds. The two had maintained a firm, lasting attachment throughout life, and the turtle-dove is compared to the companion of Orestes.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

VI

The turtle-dove is described as the parrot’s faithful companion. The two lived in complete harmony and remained loyal to the end, so the turtle-dove is urged to grieve before all other birds.
