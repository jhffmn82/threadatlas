Augeas

person · 4 works · 7 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Augeias, the guest-betraying king of the Epeans, Αὐγέας, Αὐγείαν, Αὐγείας

Abstract written by a model

In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Augeas, the guest-betraying king of the Epeans, refuses payment demanded by Herakles, loses Elis when his city is destroyed, and ends captive in sudden death. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Augeas, king of the Eleans, defrauds Hercules of his promised reward; Hercules later invades Elis, kills Augeas, takes the city, and restores Phyleus to the kingdom. In The Library, Augeas, king of Elis, has many cattle herds, promises Hercules a tenth of them for cleaning their dung in one day, then denies the promise, expels Hercules and Phyleus, appoints generals against Hercules, and is killed when Hercules captures Elis; Phyleus receives the kingdom. In Description of Greece, Augeas rules Elis jointly, owns exceptionally numerous oxen and goats whose dung blocks cultivation, and after refusing Hercules' promised payment and expelling Phyleus, faces defeat, forgiveness, and Phyleus' restoration.

Augeas in The Library

Apollodorus · about AD 70 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Augeas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Augeas is king of Elis, with many cattle herds. He agrees to give Hercules a tenth of them if Hercules cleans the dung in one day, doubting he can do it. Afterward, he denies the promise, expels Hercules and Phyleus, appoints generals against Hercules, and is killed when Hercules captures Elis; Phyleus receives the kingdom.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

V. When Hercules heard that, he went to Tiryns

Augeias is king of Elis and possesses many herds of cattle. Hercules approaches him and says he can remove the dung in one day if Augeias gives him a tenth of the cattle; Augeias agrees because he does not believe Hercules can accomplish it.

THE LIBRARY, II. v. 4-5

Augeas is king of Elis and possesses many herds of cattle. He promises Hercules a tenth of the cattle for cleansing the yard, then denies the promise after learning that the task was commanded by Eurystheus and expels Hercules and Phyleus.

THE LIBRARY, II. vr. 3-4

Augeas appoints Eurytus and Cteatus as generals against Hercules. Hercules later captures Elis, kills Augeas and his sons, and gives the kingdom to Phyleus.

Augeas in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Augeas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Augeas, son of Eleus, rules Elis with shared administration and owns exceptionally numerous oxen and goats. Their dung blocks cultivation; after refusing Hercules' promised payment and expelling Phyleus, he faces defeat, forgiveness, and Phyleus' restoration.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK V.--ELIS.

Augeas ruled Elis and possessed such numerous oxen and goats that their dung hindered cultivation. He hired Hercules to clear it, refused to pay the promised reward, expelled his son Phyleus for defending Hercules, and prepared for war; Hercules eventually captured Elis, forgave Augeas, and restored Phyleus.
