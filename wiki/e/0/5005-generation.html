  • In Enhancing Retrieval-Augmented Large Language Models with Iterative Retrieval-Generation Synergy, generation provides informative context for retrieving additional knowledge and can use both parametric and non-parametric knowledge.

  • In DRAGIN: Dynamic Retrieval Augmented Generation based on the Information Needs of Large Language Models, generation proceeds token by token, resumes from the truncation point after retrieved knowledge is integrated, and may trigger further retrieval.

  • In Optimizing LLM Based Retrieval Augmented Generation Pipelines in the Financial Domain, answer generation is evaluated across six LLMs and is strongly affected by the quality and ordering of retrieved information.
  • In The Good and The Bad: Exploring Privacy Issues in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), generation is the second phase of RAG, in which an LLM uses pre-trained knowledge and retrieved data to produce a response.
  • In Generate-then-Ground in Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Multi-hop Question Answering, generation is evaluated with and without retrieval; retrieval-based methods combine language-model answering with retrieved knowledge, while others rely on parametric knowledge.
  • In M-RAG: Reinforcing Large Language Model Performance through Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Multiple Partitions, generation combines retrieved context with a query and provides it to large language models as a standard Naive RAG stage.
  • In VideoRAG: Retrieval-Augmented Generation over Video Corpus, generation produces responses from a query and retrieved videos or documents by combining video frames, subtitles or other text, and the query as multimodal input to a large video language model.
  • In VisDoM: Multi-Document QA with Visually Rich Elements Using Multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation, answer generation retrieves relevant evidence, applies Chain-of-Thought reasoning and targeted prompts, and produces precise text in a question-appropriate format.
  • In Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Estimation of Source Reliability, answer generation can be harmed by unreliable retrieved information, while Reliability-Aware RAG uses cross-source information and Weighted Majority Voting to produce more robust answers.
  • In VISA: Retrieval Augmented Generation with Visual Source Attribution, answer generation is evaluated in single-oracle and multi-candidate settings, producing answers with corresponding bounding boxes or “No answer” when no relevant document is present.
  • In Retrieval augmented generation for large language models in healthcare: A systematic review, generation produces the final response from the user query and retrieved context, and post-retrieval methods select, combine, and reorder information before it is supplied to the LLM.
  • In A Survey of Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Customized Large Language Models, generation uses retrieved and inherent knowledge to produce responses through a large language model, with efforts focused on improving accuracy and efficiency.
