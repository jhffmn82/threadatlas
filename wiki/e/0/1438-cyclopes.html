Cyclopes

Also called: Cyclops, Orb-eyed, same persons, the Cyclopes, Κύκλωπας, Κύκλωπες, Κύκλωπι

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the Cyclopes are a divine race who give Zeus thunder and make the thunderbolt, later being represented as destroyed by Apollo. In The Library, the Cyclopes are a people who forge Zeus’s thunderbolt; Apollo slays them after Zeus uses it against Aesculapius, and those near Polyphemus’s cave later leave after misunderstanding his cry. In The Library, the Cyclopes are one-eyed beings who give Zeus thunder, lightning, and a thunderbolt, arm Pluto and Poseidon, and fortify Tiryns for Proetus.

Cyclopes in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · about 699 BC · the work’s page · read it where Cyclopes first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Cyclopes are a divine race, offspring of Earth and Heaven, including Brontes, Steropes, and Arges. They are one-eyed, godlike beings who gave Zeus thunder and made the thunderbolt; later, they are represented as destroyed by Apollo.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE THEOGONY

The Cyclopes Brontes, Steropes, and Arges were born from Earth and Heaven. They had one eye in the middle of each forehead and later gave Zeus the thunder and made the thunderbolt.

Fragment #64—For how does he say that the same persons (the Cyclopes)

The Cyclopes are described as being like the gods, yet they are also represented as being destroyed by Apollo.

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Cyclopes first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Cyclopes are a people inhabiting their land who forge thunderbolts for Zeus. After they make Zeus’s thunderbolt, Apollo slays them when Zeus uses it against Aesculapius; later those near Polyphemus’s cave leave after misunderstanding his cry.

The Cyclopes had made the thunderbolt for Zeus. Apollo slew them after Zeus used the thunderbolt to strike Aesculapius.

VII. ‘O δὲ Ὀδυσσεύς, ὡς μὲν ἔνιοι λέγουσιν,

The Cyclopes near Polyphemus's cave come when he calls for help. They misunderstand his statement that 'Nobody' is hurting him and depart without assisting him.

Cyclopes in The Library

Apollodorus · about AD 70 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Cyclopes first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cyclopes are a group whose members have one forehead eye; after Zeus frees them from bonds by killing Campe, they give Zeus thunder, lightning, and a thunderbolt, arm Pluto and Poseidon, and fortify Tiryns for Proetus.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

II. ᾿Ἐ ππειδὴ δὲ Ζεὺς ἐγενήθη τέλειος, λαμβάνει

Zeus frees the Cyclopes from their bonds after killing Campe, who guarded them. The Cyclopes give Zeus thunder, lightning, and a thunderbolt; they give Pluto a helmet and Poseidon a trident.
