the Eleusinian Mysteries in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

The Eleusinian Mysteries are Demeter’s sacred rites, promised to the people of Eleusis and taught to Triptolemus, Diocles, Eumolpus, Celeus, and others. They must be performed reverently and kept secret, neither transgressed, investigated, nor spoken of. Initiates who see them are blessed after death; the uninitiated lack comparable good.

II. TO DEMETER

Demeter promises to teach her rites to the people of Eleusis. She says that performing them reverently will win the favour of her heart.

Demeter teaches the Eleusinian Mysteries and the conduct of her rites to Triptolemus, Diocles, Eumolpus, Celeus, and others. The mysteries are described as sacred and not to be transgressed, investigated, or spoken of; those who have seen them are called blessed, while the uninitiated lack comparable good after death.

Eleusinian mysteries in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The Eleusinian mysteries were rites of Ceres, brought from Egypt and instituted at Eleusis by Triptolemus during his reign. They may have accompanied the introduction of Ceres’s mysterious worship and had already been introduced into Sicily, supporting the tradition of Ceres coming from Sicily to Athens.

Eleusinian mysteries in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The Eleusinian Mysteries are privately managed by Eleusinians as rites of Demeter and Proserpine, performed by Celeus’s daughters; after Eleusis’s battle with Athens, their contents remained forbidden to uninitiated people.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXVIII.

After the battle between Eleusis and Athens, the people of Eleusis remained Athenians in other matters but kept the private management of the Eleusinian Mysteries. The narrator refused to disclose what was inside the sacred wall, saying that the uninitiated were forbidden both to see and to hear about the Mysteries.

the Eleusinian Mysteries in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The Eleusinian Mysteries were religious services regarded by the older Greeks as greatly superior to all others. Their manner at Eleusis was celebrated at Pheneus, having been said to be brought there by Naus, and served as the model for worship of the Great Goddesses at Megalopolis.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XV.

The mysteries of Eleusinian Demeter were said by the people of Pheneus to have been brought there by Naus, the great-grandson of Eumolpus. They are celebrated at Pheneus in the same manner as at Eleusis.

CHAPTER XXXI.

The Eleusinian Mysteries are presented as the model imitated by the worship of the Great Goddesses at Megalopolis.

CHAPTER XXXI.

The painting includes figures who may be people making a mock of the Eleusinian mysteries, although this is only a conjecture. The older Greeks regarded those mysteries as greatly superior to all other religious services.
