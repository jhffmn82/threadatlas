Bagous in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

Bagous is a eunuch slave charged with watching his mistress, a watchful keeper who obstructs the speaker-poet at her house. Unable to experience love’s mutual transports and unskilled in war, he is urged to become the poet’s confidant: overlook letters, visitors, absences, and outings, conceal the affair, and keep silent for honour, savings, and possible freedom. He is also urged to serve his mistress with favors, though she may deceive him despite his vigilance.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ELEGY II.

Bagous is the eunuch charged with watching his mistress. Ovid urges him to become a confidant by overlooking letters, male visitors, unexplained absences, and visits to temples or theatres. If Bagous helps conceal the affair and keeps silent, Ovid says he may gain honour, increase his savings, and eventually become free.

ELEGY III.

Bagous watches his mistress but, being neither man nor woman, cannot experience the mutual transports of love. The poet urges him to abandon manly aspirations, serve his mistress, and overwhelm her with favors, warning that she may deceive him despite his watchfulness.
