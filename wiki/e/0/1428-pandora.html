Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Pandora is fashioned from earth by Hephaestus at Zeus’s command, adorned and gifted by the Olympians, and opens a jar releasing countless ills among men while Hope remains inside. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Pandora is made in heaven, gifted beauty, persuasion, and music, and opens a container from curiosity, releasing plagues or blessings while hope remains inside.

Pandora in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · about 699 BC · the work’s page · read it where Pandora first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pandora is a woman fashioned from earth by Hephaestus at Zeus’s command, adorned and gifted by the Olympians, and named for those gifts. Presented as a lovely but deceitful, irresistible evil, she is linked to the price of fire and the source of the race of women. She opens a jar, releasing countless ills among men while Hope remains inside, explaining evil and the need for work.

Pandora is fashioned as a beautiful woman and given a deceitful nature, crafty words, and a shameless mind. She opens the jar and releases countless evils among men, while Hope remains inside.

Pandora in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Pandora first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pandora, the first woman, is made in heaven and gifted beauty, persuasion, and music. Presented to Epimetheus on earth, she opens a container from curiosity, releasing plagues or blessings while hope remains inside.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II

Pandora was made in heaven with gifts from the gods, including beauty from Venus, persuasion from Mercury, and music from Apollo. She was sent to earth and accepted by Epimetheus, then opened a container out of curiosity, releasing either plagues or blessings while hope remained inside.
