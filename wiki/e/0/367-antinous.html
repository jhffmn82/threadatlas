Antinous in The Odyssey

Narrative, section by section

7 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK II

Antinous answers Telemachus by blaming Penelope for misleading the suitors with her weaving trick. He insists that Telemachus send her back to Icarius and later mocks him while pretending to offer help with his voyage. Telemachus rejects Antinous's friendliness and says he will do him and the other suitors harm if he can.

Antinous is one of the suitors’ ringleaders and becomes enraged when he learns that Telemachus has sailed away. He orders that a ship and twenty men be prepared so that he can lie in wait for Telemachus between Ithaca and Samos.

Antinous urges the suitors to kill Telemachus before he can address the Achaeans in assembly. He proposes seizing Telemachus on his farm or on the road and dividing Ulysses's property among the suitors, while sending Penelope and the man who marries her to the house.

Antinous abuses Eumaeus for bringing the stranger to the house and refuses to give Ulysses anything. He throws a footstool at the disguised Ulysses, striking him on the right shoulder blade, and threatens him when Ulysses protests.

BOOK XVIII

Antinous encourages the fight between Ulysses and Irus and promises to reward the victor with food and freedom from other beggars at the table. He threatens to send Irus to King Echetus if Irus loses, and later gives Ulysses a goat's paunch filled with blood and fat.

BOOK XXI

Antinous scolds Eumaeus and the stockman for weeping and directs the suitors to take turns at the bow. After the suitors fail, he orders Melanthius to light a fire and bring lard so the bow can be warmed and greased.

BOOK XXII

Antinous is struck in the throat by an arrow from Ulysses while raising a cup to drink. He falls dead, and Eurymachus identifies him as the chief offender who wanted to kill Telemachus and become the chief man in Ithaca.
