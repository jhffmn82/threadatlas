Polydorus

person · 2 works · 9 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Polydorus son of Priam, fosterchild, noble Polydorus, thy father, Polydorus

Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, Polydorus, Priam’s youngest and most loved son and fastest runner, disobeys his father’s prohibition against fighting, runs among the front ranks, and is killed by Achilles. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Polydorus, Priam and Hecuba’s youngest surviving male child, is secretly raised by Polymnestor and Ilione, then murdered by Polymnestor, who casts his body into the waters until Hecuba finds it on the shore.

Polydorus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Polydorus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Polydorus, youngest surviving male child of Priam and Hecuba, is entrusted to Polymnestor for secret private upbringing and protected by Ilione, who raises him as her son. After Phrygian fortunes are ruined, Polymnestor murders him with Thracian weapons and throws his body into the waters; Hecuba finds his wounded corpse on the shore.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

The conqueror, Ulysses, set sail for the country of Hypsipyle,[48] and

Polydorus was entrusted by his father to Polymnestor to be raised secretly away from the Phrygian arms. When the Phrygian fortunes were ruined, Polymnestor murdered him and threw his lifeless body from a rock into the waters below.

FABLES III. AND IV. [XIII.439-622]

Polydorus, Hecuba's youngest surviving male child, had been entrusted to the Ismarian king. Hecuba finds his corpse on the shore, bearing great wounds inflicted by Thracian weapons.
