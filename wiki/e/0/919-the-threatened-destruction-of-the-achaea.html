the threatened destruction of the Achaean fleet in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

The threatened destruction of the Achaean fleet is an extreme danger caused by Hector’s threatened attack. Trojans light watchfires near the ships, plan a daybreak assault, and Hector vows to destroy the sterns and burn the hulls; Achaean leaders keep watch, seek counsel, and send Ulysses and Diomed for information.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK IX.

The Trojans have lit watchfires beside the ships and threaten to attack them at daybreak. Hector vows to cut apart their sterns and burn their hulls, and Ulysses warns Achilles that the Achaeans may perish unless he intervenes.

BOOK X.

The danger to the Achaeans is described as extreme because Hector has devastated them and the Trojans are camped near the ships. Agamemnon, Nestor, and the other leaders seek counsel, maintain the watches, and send Ulysses and Diomed to gather information.
