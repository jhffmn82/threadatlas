Jane in The Diary of Samuel Pepys

Samuel Pepys · 1660/1669

Jane, the diarist's maidservant for three years, leaves for her mother's country home after receiving wages and an extra 2s. 6d. Though judged lazy and spoilt after Pall's arrival, she is harmless, pleasing, and deeply missed.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

opening

Jane completes three years as the diarist's maid and leaves to go into the country to her mother. Although the diarist considers her lazy and spoiled by Pall's arrival, he regards her as harmless and unusually pleasing, pays her wages, gives her an additional 2s. 6d., and is deeply troubled by her departure.
