Mopsus

person · 3 works · 7 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Mopsus, the son of Manto, Mopsus, the son of Rhacius and Manto, Μόψον, Μόψος, Μόψου

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Mopsus is Manto's son, a seer greater than Calchas who correctly counts a wild fig-tree's figs. In The Library, Mopsus is a diviner who defeats Calchas in a divination contest and later dies fighting Amphilochus for the kingdom. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Mopsus, the son of Rhacius and Manto, drives all the Carians out of the country.

Mopsus in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Mopsus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Mopsus is a diviner, son of Apollo and Manto. At Colophon he received Calchas and companions, prevailed in a dispute about divination by correctly predicting figs and piglets, and later received storm-driven Amphilochus. Fighting Amphilochus in single combat for the kingdom, they killed each other according to one account.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ἔμελλον ἀποπλεῖν πορθήσαντες Τροίαν, ὑ ὑπὸ Κάλ-

Mopsus, the son of Apollo and Manto, received Calchas and his companions at Colophon and disputed with Calchas about divination. He correctly said that a wild fig tree bore ten thousand figs, a bushel, and one extra, and that a pregnant sow carried nine male piglets to be born the next day at the sixth hour.

ΕἸ [Ὅτι πλανηθέντες “Ελληνες ἄλλοι ἀλλαχοῦ

Mopsus received Amphilochus son of Alcmaeon after the latter was driven to him by a storm. According to one account, Mopsus and Amphilochus killed each other while fighting for the kingdom.
