treacherous speech and slander in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

Treacherous speech and slander are an overpowering, hateful evil: secret, fox-like, deceitful speech that harms both its target and listener, twists stories, injures what shines, and elevates the unworthy. It is rejected for directness and simplicity, culminating in an appeal to Zeus to avoid such paths.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

II.

Slander is described as a powerful evil that harms both the person slandered and the listener. Its secret whispers are compared to foxes, and those who use such crooked speech are rejected in favor of directness.

Nemean Ode VIII

Treacherous speech and slander are described as hateful, deceitful forces that twist stories, injure what shines, and raise the fame of obscure or unworthy people. The speaker rejects such paths and asks Zeus to let him follow simplicity.
