Medes in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Medes supplied forces under Arbaces, joined Persians and other allies in revolt against Sardanapalus, invaded and overthrew the Assyrian empire. They then conquered Assyrians and ruled an expanding empire until Cyrus conquered Astyages. Later, as Persian troops at Thermopylae, they led the attack but were defeated by Leonidas's Greek formation with heavy losses.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. IL

The Medes provided the annually summoned forces commanded by Arbaces. They joined the revolt against Sardanapalus, invaded the Assyrian empire with the Persians and other allies, and ultimately overthrew the Assyrian empire.

CHAP. III.

The Medes conquered the Assyrians and later ruled an empire that was enlarged by successive kings. Their history is presented through differing accounts by Herodotus and Ctesias, ending with the conquest of Astyages by Cyrus.

LAST TEN BOOKS / BOOK XI.

The Medes were placed at the front of the Persian attack at Thermopylae. They were defeated by the Greek formation, suffering heavy losses before the Cissians and Sacians continued the assault.

Medes in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Those who fell fighting against the Medes are commemorated by a tomb near the entrance to Platæa. The Medes are associated with the Persian invasion and with the spoils that Themistocles brought to Delphi as a proposed offering to Apollo. One explanation for Apollo’s refusal was that Themistocles would later become a suppliant of the Persians, and acceptance might have brought him the undying hatred of the Medes.
