Eurymachus in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Eurymachus, son of Polybus, is a leading suitor of Penelope and ringleader among the suitors: an understanding man who dismisses bird omens and has an improper relationship with Melantho. He deceptively claims friendship with Telemachus while plotting against him, proposes pursuing his ship, falsely reassures Penelope, praises and gifts her, then mocks the disguised Ulysses and violently throws a footstool.

Eurymachus first proposes sending a ship after Telemachus, before learning that Telemachus's ship has already returned. He falsely reassures Penelope that Telemachus has nothing to fear from the suitors while secretly plotting against him.

Eurymachus praises Penelope’s beauty and understanding, then brings her a magnificent chain of gold and amber beads. He mocks the disguised Ulysses by questioning his ability and willingness to work, becomes furious at Ulysses’s reply, and throws a footstool that hits the cupbearer.
