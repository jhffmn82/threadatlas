  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Oedipus was the exposed son of Laius who unknowingly killed him, solved the Sphinx’s riddle, became king of Thebes, and married Jocasta.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Oedipus is a blind former king confined in the palace who curses his sons, is banished after their deaths, and leaves with Antigone for Colonus.
  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Oedipus is an aged, blind, exiled former king who curses his sons, receives Theseus’s protection, and disappears peacefully at his appointed death near Colonus.
  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Oedipus investigates Laius’s murder, discovers that he is Laius’s son and Jocasta’s husband, blinds himself after Jocasta’s death, and asks to be exiled from Thebes.
  • In Four Plays of Aeschylus, Oedipus is the blinded former king of Thebes whose curse on Eteocles and Polynices is fulfilled through their deaths.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Oedipus is the father of sons by Euryganeia who cursed Polyneices and his brother to kill one another after being angered by their gifts and treatment.
  • In The Library, Oedipus is the legendary man who unknowingly killed his father, married his mother, was expelled from Thebes, and was received by Theseus among the Athenians.
  • In The Library, Oedipus is the exposed and Corinth-raised son of Laius who killed Laius, solved the Sphinx’s riddle, ruled Thebes, married Jocasta, blinded himself, and died at Colonus.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Œdipus is the son of Laius who was exposed, raised in Corinth, slew the Sphinx and Laius, and suffered the consequences of patricide and incest.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, OEdipus unknowingly killed his father and married his mother, became king of Thebes, blinded himself, was exiled, and died while wandering with Antigone.
  • In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, Oedipus rules Thebes, discovers that he has killed his father and married his mother, blinds and exiles himself, and later dies peacefully at Colonus under Theseus’s protection.

Oedipus in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Oedipus is the biological son of Laius and Jocasta, exposed as an infant and raised by Polybus. He later solves the Sphinx’s riddle, unknowingly kills Laius as foretold by Apollo, and marries Jocasta without knowing she is his mother. When he discovers the truth, he blinds himself; he is also described as blinded by the gods as punishment and warning. Former king of Thebes, he becomes an aged, blind man confined in the palace and grieving his family. Father of Antigone, Eteocles, and Polynices, he curses his children after his sons dishonor him and try to conceal their family troubles. The curse is fulfilled in the deaths of Eteocles and Polynices. Learning that Jocasta and both sons are dead, Oedipus laments, is banished from Thebes by Creon, and leaves with Antigone guiding him toward Colonus, where Apollo’s oracle says he is destined to die.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

THE PHŒNICIAN VIRGINS.

Oedipus is the son of Laius and Jocasta, though he was raised by Polybus after being exposed as an infant. He unknowingly killed Laius, married Jocasta, discovered that she was his mother, and blinded himself after learning the truth.

POLYNICES, CHORUS.

Œdipus is described as blind and shut away in the palace, grieving the separation of his sons. He has cursed his children, and the brothers fear that their conflict fulfills or is intensified by that curse.

ETEOCLES, CHORUS, MESSENGER.

Oedipus is described as the son of Jocasta who was exposed as an infant and later became the husband of his mother. After learning the truth, he blinded himself, and his sons' attempts to conceal their family troubles and their dishonor toward him led him to curse them.

MESSENGER, JOCASTA, CHORUS.

Oedipus is referred to as the aged, blind man in the house and as the father whose misfortunes affect Creon. His curse is said to have been fulfilled through the deaths of Eteocles and Polynices.

ANTIGONE, CREON, CHORUS.

Oedipus learns that Jocasta and both of his sons are dead and laments the fate of his family. He is banished from Thebes and departs with Antigone, who guides his blind steps toward Colonus near Athens.

Oedipus in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Oedipus is a former king of Thebes, adopted son of Polybus and believed son of Merope, who becomes Jocasta’s husband and the father of Antigone, Ismene, Polynices, and Eteocles. As an infant, he is abandoned at Cithaeron with pierced feet, saved by the Corinthian Shepherd, and named for those feet. Believing Polybus and Merope to be his parents, he receives an oracle at Delphi that he will kill his father and marry his mother, so he flees Corinth. On the road, during a quarrel at a crossroads, he kills Laius and Laius’s attendants without knowing Laius’s identity. He later solves the Sphinx’s riddle, wins Thebes’s approval, marries Laius’s widow Jocasta, and rules as king. When Thebes suffers a plague, Oedipus, firmly allegiant to Apollo and to Laius as a dead sovereign, investigates Laius’s murder to save and purge the city. He vows to find and punish the killer, curses the murderer and any concealers, and initially accuses Tiresias and Creon of conspiracy when Tiresias names him as the polluter. Evidence from Jocasta, the Corinthian Shepherd, and the surviving shepherd reveals that Oedipus is the biological son of Jocasta and Laius. His unwitting killing of Laius and marriage to Jocasta fulfill the prophecies: he is both parricide and husband-son of his mother. After Jocasta’s death, Oedipus blinds himself with her pins, becomes a cursed and blind former ruler, and asks to be exiled from Thebes. He requests that Creon care for his daughters and is taken into the palace.

Oedipus is an aged, blind former king of Thebes, son of Laius and descendant of Labdacus. Native to Thebes, he becomes an exile after unknowingly killing his father and marrying his mother, acts for which he is accused despite insisting they were involuntary. Banished and forbidden from Thebes, he wanders with the devoted support of his daughters Antigone and Ismene. At Colonos near Athens, led by Antigone, he enters the sacred grove of the Eumenides as a suppliant and says he brings blessing to those who shelter him. He learns that his sons Eteocles and Polynices are in conflict and seek him because an oracle makes his fate important; he refuses to help them and curses them, especially Polynices for driving him into exile. He seeks Theseus's protection, offers his body and burial as a benefit to Athens, and resists Creon's attempt to seize his daughters. Theseus rescues Antigone and Ismene, earning Oedipus's gratitude. Protected by the gods and guided toward an appointed death, Oedipus recognizes thunder and lightning as its approach. He leads his daughters and Theseus toward a secret resting-place, entrusts Theseus with knowledge that will protect Athens, asks him to guard the daughters, and disappears without visible cause. His death is peaceful and wondrous; his hidden burial is a boon to Athens and inauspicious to his enemies.

CHORUS.

Oedipus understands the thunder and lightning as signs that his divinely promised death is near. He leads his daughters to his appointed resting-place, entrusts Theseus with a secret meant to protect Athens, and disappears mysteriously after asking Theseus to protect Antigone and Ismene.

Oedipus in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Oedipus is a king of Thebes whose life is governed by hidden lineage, prophecy, unwitting acts, suffering, and eventual exile. Raised as the adopted son of Polybus and Merope, he believes them to be his parents. He later saves Thebes by solving the riddle and becomes its ruler and Jocasta’s husband. When Thebes seeks relief from the murder of Laius, Oedipus vows to find and punish the killer, even cursing the unknown criminal and those who conceal him. Teiresias accuses him, but Oedipus initially rejects the charge and suspects Teiresias and Creon of conspiracy. Evidence from Jocasta, a messenger, and a herdsman reveals that he is the biological son of Laius and Jocasta, that he killed Laius at a crossroads, and that he has married his mother and fathered her children. He recognizes himself as both parricide and incestuous offender, blinds himself after finding Jocasta dead, and asks to be driven from Thebes while entrusting Antigone and Ismene to Creon. Blind, weak, and exiled, Oedipus wanders with the support of his daughters and reaches Colonus. He describes his crimes as unwitting, says the killing occurred in self-defense, and argues that he is innocent in the law’s eye, though he continues to suffer their consequences. Rejected by his sons, Polyneices and Eteocles, he refuses to aid them and curses them with mutual destruction. At Colonus he becomes a suppliant and guest under Theseus’s protection, promises that his death and burial will benefit Athens, and resists Creon’s effort to bring him back to Thebes. After Theseus rescues his daughters, divine signs announce Oedipus’s end. He prepares at a sacred place, entrusts his daughters to Theseus, and disappears from sight without pain. His secret burial place remains a protective gift for Athens.

Narrative, section by section

OEDIPUS THE KING

Oedipus receives Creon’s report of the oracle and vows to discover and punish the murderers of Laius. He curses the unknown murderer and anyone who protects him, then angrily accuses Teiresias of plotting with Creon before Teiresias declares that Oedipus himself is the murderer.

CHORUS.

Oedipus angrily insults Teiresias and orders him to leave. Teiresias declares that Oedipus does not know his lineage and prophesies that he will be exposed as the murderer of Laius, the husband and son of his mother, and the brother and father of his children.

CHORUS.

A crime is laid against Oedipus, but the Chorus cannot assent to the accusation without proof. It recalls that he saved the State when the winged songstress came.

CREON.

Oedipus accuses Creon of plotting against him and of using the seer as a mouthpiece. After hearing Jocasta’s account of Laius’s death and comparing it with his own encounter at the crossroads, he fears that he killed Laius and asks that the surviving herdsman be brought to him.

[Enter JOCASTA.]

Oedipus hears that Polybus has died and briefly believes the prophecies about killing his father have failed. He learns from the Corinthian Messenger and Laius’s Herdsman that he is not Polybus’s biological son but the child of Laius and Jocasta. He recognizes that he has killed his father and married his mother, then leaves in horror.

[Enter SECOND MESSENGER.]

Oedipus blinds himself after finding Jocasta dead, saying that his hands dealt the blows because sight brought him no delight. He accepts responsibility for murdering Laius and marrying Jocasta, begs to be driven from Thebes, and asks Creon to care for Antigone and Ismene.

OEDIPUS AT COLONUS

Oedipus arrives at Colonus as a blind, exiled wanderer led by Antigone. He rests in the sacred grove of the Eumenides, identifies himself to the Chorus as the son of Laius and descendant of Labdacus, and pleads not to be driven away. He says that his suffering has taught him endurance and that his coming can bring comfort to the land.

[Enter ISMENE]

Oedipus receives Ismene and learns that his sons are fighting over sovereignty. He condemns them for abandoning him during his banishment and refuses to aid either one, declaring that the prophecies concerning him have come to pass. He is unable to perform the required ritual himself because he is weak and blind, so he sends Ismene to do it.

CHORUS.

Oedipus recounts that he unknowingly entered an impious marriage with the woman who was both his wife and mother. He says that he killed a man who would otherwise have killed him, but argues that the killing was without intent and that he is innocent in the law's eyes.

[Enter THESEUS]

Oedipus asks Theseus to shelter his woe-worn body and promises that its value will become clear after his death and burial. He explains that his sons expelled him from his country and now seek to bring him back, but he chooses to remain where he can overcome those who cast him out.

[Enter CREON with attendants]

Oedipus refuses Creon's request that he return to Thebes and accuses him of disguising hostile intentions in soft words. When Creon seizes his daughters, Oedipus appeals to the people of Athens and curses Creon, then defends himself by arguing that his parricide and incest were committed unknowingly and through the gods' design.

[Enter ANTIGONE and ISMENE with THESEUS]

Oedipus welcomes Antigone and Ismene after Theseus brings them back safely, and he thanks Theseus for their rescue. He refuses to receive Polyneices favorably, condemns him for having driven him into exile, and curses Polyneices and Eteocles. When thunder and lightning signal his appointed end, he asks for Theseus and prepares to lead him to the place of his death.

[Enter MESSENGER]

Oedipus reaches a sacred place, prepares himself with water and offerings, and tells Antigone and Ismene that he will die that day. He entrusts them to Theseus, then orders them to leave before his final passage. He is taken from sight without wailing, disease, or pain.

ANTIGONE

Oedipus is identified as the father of Antigone, Ismene, Polyneices, and Eteocles. Antigone and Ismene recall his dishonor, self-inflicted blindness, and death after he recognized his sin.
