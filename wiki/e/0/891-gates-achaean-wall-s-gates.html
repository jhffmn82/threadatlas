gates (Achaean wall's gates)

object · 1 work · 6 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Achaean gates, all the gates, gates, the gates, the gates with their doors

Abstract written by a model

The Achaean gates are high double doors in the wall, barred yet kept open to admit fleeing Achaeans and provide chariot access. They initially block Trojan attackers as defenders repel Asius’s men. Hector then strikes them with a great stone, breaking them open; Trojans enter through the gates and over the wall.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

BOOK XII.

The Achaean gates are opened and their great bar is lowered so that fleeing Achaeans can enter. Asius attacks them with his horses, but Polypoetes and Leonteus defend the gates and kill several of his men.

Ajax and Teucer then both of them attacked him. Teucer hit him
