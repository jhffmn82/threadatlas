Abstract written by a model

Children are little and helpless, living in villages, including those Claus visits. They travel to Claus’s house for his wonderful toys, creating a path from the plain to his cottage, while he also brings gifts to sick or crippled children. The Awgwas influence them to quarrel, disobey, and behave badly, though Claus’s toys make them happier and more obedient; after he stops visiting villages, the Awgwas keep them from finding the Laughing Valley. Claus later brings toys to children everywhere, including those who have never owned one, delivering playthings to sleeping children in villages and a city. Children find the toys by their beds, learn that Santa Claus brought them, and are taught to pray for forgiveness when naughty. They receive his toys with love and begin hanging stockings by fireplaces on Christmas Eve after Margot, Dick, Ned, and Sara find toys in theirs.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

4. How Little Mayrie Became Frightened

Children travel to Claus's house to obtain his wonderful toys, while Claus carries gifts to children who are sick or crippled. Their visits create a path from the plain to his cottage, and their pleasure makes Claus happy.

6. The Wickedness of the Awgwas

The children are influenced by the Awgwas to quarrel, disobey, and behave badly, but Claus's toys make them happier and more obedient. The Awgwas prevent them from finding the Laughing Valley after Claus stops traveling to the villages.

8. The First Journey with the Reindeer

Children everywhere receive toys from Claus, including children who have never owned a toy. During the first journey with the reindeer, sleeping children in villages and a city receive dolls, drums, horns, elephants, and other playthings.

9. "Santa Claus!"

Children find pretty toys by their bedsides and ask their parents where they came from. They are told that the good Claus brought them, and mothers teach them to pray to Santa Claus for forgiveness when they are naughty or disobedient. The text says that the best children are sometimes naughty and naughty children are often good.

11. How the First Stockings Were Hung by the Chimneys

Children receive Santa Claus's toys and speak of him with love because of his unselfish deeds. Margot, Dick, Ned, and Sara discover toys in their stockings and tell their friends about it. Other children then decide to hang their stockings by the fireplace on Christmas Eve.
