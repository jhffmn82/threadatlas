Callisto

person · 4 works · 23 quoted facts · 10 connections
Also called: Callisto the daughter of Lycaon, a bear, another maiden, the bear, Καλλιστοῦς, Καλλιστὼ
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Callisto is Lycaon’s daughter, a huntress with Artemis who becomes a bear, gives birth to Arcas, and is placed among the stars.
  • In The Library, Callisto is a nymph or daughter of Lycaon, Nycteus, or Ceteus, an unwilling companion of Zeus, and later a bear placed among the stars.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Callisto is Lycaon’s only daughter and a local hero of Tegea who becomes a bear and is associated with the Great Bear.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Callisto is a maiden huntress and mother of Arcas who becomes a bear and is placed in the heavens as the Great Bear.

Callisto in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Callisto, Lycaon’s daughter in Arcadia, hunted with Artemis among wild beasts. After Zeus seduced her and Artemis discovered her pregnancy, Artemis transformed her into a bear; she gave birth to Arcas and Zeus placed her among the stars.

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE ASTRONOMY

Callisto was the daughter of Lycaon and lived in Arcadia, occupying herself with wild beasts in the mountains with Artemis. Zeus seduced her, and after Artemis discovered that she was pregnant, Artemis changed her into a bear; Callisto later gave birth to Arcas and was placed among the stars by Zeus.

Callisto in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Callisto is described as a nymph or daughter of Lycaon, Nycteus, or Ceteus. Artemis’s companion, she wears Artemis’s garb and vows virginity. Zeus sleeps with her against her will; to escape Hera’s notice, he transforms her into a bear and later places her among the stars.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

VIII. ᾿Επανάγωμεν δὲ νῦν πάλιν ἐπὶ τὸν Πε-

Callisto is said by Eumelus and others to be Lycaon’s daughter, though other accounts describe her as a nymph or as the daughter of Nycteus or Ceteus. She is a companion of Artemis who swears to remain a virgin, but Zeus sleeps with her against her will; she is transformed into a bear, and Zeus later places her among the stars.

Callisto in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Callisto, Lycaon’s only daughter and a local hero of Tegea, had an intrigue with Zeus and became pregnant. Hera transformed her into a she-bear, and Artemis shot her to please Hera. She was associated with the Great Bear, though Arcadians showed her grave; a painting shows her with a bear-skin coverlet, feet on Nomia’s knees.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER III.

Callisto was Lycaon’s only daughter. After Zeus had an intrigue with her, Hera turned her into a she-bear, and Artemis shot her to please Hera; Callisto was then associated with the constellation called the Great Bear, although the Arcadians showed her grave.

CHAPTER XXXI.

Callisto, the daughter of Lycaon, is shown with a bear-skin coverlet and her feet resting on the knees of Nomia.

Callisto in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Callisto, a maiden huntress and mother of Arcas, is changed into a fearful bear by Juno’s jealousy; after recognizing her hunting son, Jupiter saves them and places them in the heavens as the Great and Little Bear.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter III

Callisto excites Juno's jealousy, and Juno changes her into a bear. Though her form changes, her former disposition remains, and she wanders fearfully through the woods. Callisto recognizes her grown son while hunting, but Jupiter saves them both and places them in the heavens as the Great and Little Bear.
