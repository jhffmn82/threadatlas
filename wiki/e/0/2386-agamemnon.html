  • In Heroides and Amores, Agamemnon demands Briseis, later regrets offending Achilles and seeks reconciliation, and is attracted to Cassandra.
  • In Iphigenia in Tauris, Agamemnon is the king who leads the Trojan expedition after falsely summoning Iphigenia and is later killed by Clytemnestra.
  • In The Trojan Women of Euripides, Agamemnon is the Greek general and king who chooses Cassandra as his bride despite her madness and prophecies.
  • In The Electra of Euripides, Agamemnon is the victorious king of Argos who sacrifices Iphigenia, is murdered by Clytemnestra and Aegisthus, and whose children avenge him.
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Agamemnon commands the Greeks, permits Polyxena’s sacrifice, favors Cassandra, and supports Hecuba’s revenge and judgment against Polymestor.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Agamemnon is the Greek commander who sacrifices Iphigenia for the expedition, supports Hecuba after Troy, and is murdered by Clytemnestra.
  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Agamemnon leads the Achaean host, sacrifices his daughter at Aulis, is murdered after Troy, and permits Aias’s burial after Odysseus persuades him.
  • In The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies, Agamemnon is the victorious monarch returning from Troy who is murdered by Clytemnestra and Aegisthus.
  • In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Agamemnon returns from Troy after destroying the Trojans’ dwellings and is murdered with Cassandra by Clytaemnestra.
  • In The Odyssey, Agamemnon leads the Achaeans home from Troy but is murdered by Aegisthus and Clytemnestra, later recounting the betrayal among the dead.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Agamemnon helps organize the expedition, causes Artemis to demand Iphigeneia’s sacrifice, and is murdered by Aegisthus and Clytaemnestra.
  • In The Iliad, Agamemnon is the chief ruler and greatest king of the Achaeans, commanding their forces against Troy until his quarrel with Achilles and later reconciliation.
  • In The Library, Agamemnon commands the Greek army against Troy, engineers Iphigenia’s summons and Palamedes’s death, and is murdered by Aegisthus and Clytaemnestra after returning home.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Agamemnon chooses public duty over affection by permitting Iphigenia’s sacrifice, organizes the contest for Achilles’ armor, and receives Cassandra as a captive.
  • In The Fall of Troy, Agamemnon is an Achaean king who mourns Achilles, apologizes to Philoctetes, takes Cassandra as a prize, and departs Troy with the Argives.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Agamemnon commands the Greeks, consents to Iphigenia’s sacrifice, quarrels with Achilles over Briseis, and is murdered by Clytemnestra and Aegisthus.

Agamemnon in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Agamemnon is a Greek king and commander with chief authority over the Greek army, renowned throughout Greece and described as a ruler who was not a tyrant. He is son of Atreus, brother of Menelaus, husband of Clytemnestra/Clytæmnestra, consort of Cassandra, and father of Iphigenia, Orestes, Electra, and Chrysothemis. As leader of the expedition against Troy, he is torn between family love and the demands of command. Bound by Calchas's prophecy that Iphigenia must be offered to Diana for the Greeks to sail, he first tries to prevent her arrival, then uses a supposed marriage to Achilles to bring her to Aulis, and finally admits that fear of the army, prophecy, and the expedition has made the sacrifice unavoidable. He openly grieves for Iphigenia, serves at the altar, and later tells Clytemnestra to return home with Orestes; Iphigenia is reported to have been rescued by Diana. After Troy, Agamemnon supports Hecuba, seeks to protect Polyxena, orders her release when the army assents, and hears Hecuba's case against Polymestor. Though cautious of Greek opposition and motivated in part by Cassandra, he judges Polymestor guilty of killing his guest for gold and orders him removed, favoring punishment for injustice and impiety. On returning from war, Agamemnon is murdered inside the palace by Clytemnestra. His death becomes central to his children's fate: Orestes, supported by Electra and Pylades, invokes his memory and avenges him by killing Clytemnestra, while others honor Orestes for defending Agamemnon's household and memory.

Agamemnon is a king of men, son of Atreus, elder and more powerful brother of Menelaus, husband of Clytemnestra, and father of Chrysothemis, Laodice, Iphianassa, and Orestes. As chief ruler and greatest king of the Achaeans, he commands their largest force, including one hundred ships from Mycenae and associated lands, and leads them in the war against Troy. He is depicted as imposing among heroes and as a commander who honors Nestor, favors Idomeneus, addresses assemblies, directs sacrifices and oaths, organizes battle, and at times personally fights fiercely. The central conflict of his record begins when he refuses Chryses’s ransom and keeps Chryseis, bringing Apollo’s plague on the Achaeans. Though he returns her, he demands compensation and takes Briseis from Achilles. This dishonors Achilles, causes their quarrel, and harms the Achaeans. He receives a lying dream, tests the army by proposing departure, later despairs and urges retreat as the Trojans gain strength, and loses sleep over danger to the Achaeans and their ships. Agamemnon apologized to Philoctetes and attributed his abandonment to the will of Heaven and the Immortals. He promised rich gifts and gave Philoctetes seven handmaids, forty swift horses, and twelve tripods, while granting him royal honor at the feast.

Agamemnon takes Cassandra as his prize and is named as the chief recipient of Achilles' command to bring Polyxeina to Achilles' tomb. He joins the Argives in their departure from Troy.

Agamemnon in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Agamemnon is king of Mycenae, Menelaus's brother, and commander-in-chief of the Greeks. After offending Diana, he consents to Iphigenia's sacrifice. During the war, he keeps Chryseis until plague forces her release, seizes Briseis from Achilles, later offers her return with gifts, and leads councils amid Greek defeats. Returning from Troy expecting celebration, he is murdered at a banquet by Clytemnestra and AEgisthus.

Chapter XX

Agamemnon, king of Mycenae and brother of Menelaus, is chosen commander-in-chief of the Greek forces. After killing a stag sacred to Diana, he consents to the proposed sacrifice of his daughter Iphigenia.

THE ILIAD

Agamemnon possesses Chryseis and refuses to release her until Apollo's pestilence afflicts the Greeks. He takes Briseis from Achilles, later agrees to return her with additional gifts, and calls a council when the Trojans defeat the Greeks.

Chapter XXI

Agamemnon, the Greek general-in-chief and Menelaus's brother, returned from Troy expecting to celebrate his victory. His wife Clytemnestra and her paramour AEgisthus murdered him at the welcoming banquet.
