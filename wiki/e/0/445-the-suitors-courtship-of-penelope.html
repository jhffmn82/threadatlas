Abstract written by a model

The suitors’ courtship of Penelope is a three-year pursuit at Ulysses’ house. Claiming to seek marriage, chiefs from Dulichium, Same, Zacynthus, and Ithaca remain there, offer wedding presents, demand that Penelope marry, rule the household, and consume Ulysses’ estate. Penelope neither plainly refuses nor concludes the matter, sending encouraging messages while secretly meaning otherwise. The unwanted pressure leaves her broken-hearted and helps motivate Ulysses’ planned revenge.

The suitors claim to be courting Penelope, who has neither refused them plainly nor completed arrangements to marry. Their courtship serves as a pretext for remaining in Ulysses’ house and consuming his estate.

BOOK XIII

The suitors have courted Penelope for three years and have offered wedding presents while ruling in Ulysses’ house. Penelope sends them encouraging messages while secretly meaning the opposite, and Minerva identifies their conduct as a reason for Ulysses’ planned revenge.

BOOK XVI

The suitors from Dulichium, Same, Zacynthus, and Ithaca court Penelope while refusing to leave Ulysses's household. Penelope neither openly rejects them nor brings the matter to a conclusion.

BOOK XIX

The chiefs from Dulichium, Same, Zacynthus, and Ithaca woo Penelope against her will. They demand that she marry again while consuming her estate, and Penelope says that their pressure has left her broken-hearted.
