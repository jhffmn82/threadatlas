Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Philoctetes is an Achaean leader abandoned on Lemnos, then brought back and healed before killing Alexandrus and later Admetus. In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Philoctetes is abandoned on Lemnos, endures years of suffering and isolation, and ultimately sails to Troy with Neoptolemus to help conquer it. In The Library, Philoctetes is abandoned on Lemnos, later brought to Troy and healed, kills Alexander, and eventually settles at Crimissa, where he founds a sanctuary and dedicates Hercules’ bow. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Philoctetes lights Hercules’s funeral pile, receives his bow and arrows, lives in suffering exile on Lemnos, and is brought by Ulysses to Troy. In The Fall of Troy, Philoctetes is returned from exile, healed, forgives the Achaeans, fights for them, and kills Paris with Hercules’ arrows.

Philoctetes in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · 449 to 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Philoctetes first appears

Abstract written by a model

Philoctetes is a Trachinian Greek warrior, son of Poeas, hero, and participant in the Trojan War. He possesses Heracles' sacred bow and arrows, a gift gained through kindness; the bow is necessary for Troy's capture and sustains him by letting him hunt. After a grievous, chronic foot wound causes cries and a smell that disrupt the army's rites, the Achaeans abandon and reject him on Lemnos. For ten years he lives companionless in a two-entranced rocky cave, enduring recurrent pain, hunger, and isolation, hunting wild creatures and using healing plants or a leaf without curing the wound. When Neoptolemus arrives, Philoctetes recounts his abandonment and begs to be taken home; Neoptolemus agrees. During a violent attack, Philoctetes entrusts him with the bow, but feels betrayed when the plan to take him to Troy is disclosed. He demands the bow, refuses to sail, and curses Odysseus and Atreus' sons; Odysseus seeks to bind him but releases him once the bow seems sufficient. Still lamenting exile, Philoctetes finally accepts Heracles' command: he will sail with Neoptolemus, be healed, and help conquer Troy.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

PHILOCTETES

Philoctetes has been abandoned on Lemnos because his foot wound caused loud cries that disturbed the army's rites and sacrifices. He lives alone in a cave, obtains food by shooting wild creatures, and is heard approaching while crying out in severe pain.

_Enter_ PHILOCTETES.

Philoctetes meets Neoptolemus and recounts his abandonment on Lemnos, his ten years of suffering, and the means by which he has survived. He begs Neoptolemus to take him home, and Neoptolemus agrees before the arrival of news that the Argives are pursuing him.

CHORUS.

Philoctetes suffers a violent attack of his wound, begs Neoptolemus not to abandon him, and entrusts him with the bow. When Neoptolemus reveals the plan to take him to Troy, Philoctetes denounces the betrayal, demands his bow, refuses to sail, and curses Odysseus and the Atreus' sons. Odysseus orders him bound, but later releases him when he decides the bow is sufficient.

MONODY.

Philoctetes laments his isolation, hunger, wound, and exile in his cave on Lemnos. He initially curses the Achaeans and refuses to return to Troy, but after Heracles commands him, he agrees to sail with Neoptolemus, be healed, and help conquer Troy.

Abstract written by a model

Philoctetes, son of Poeas, leads Olizonians against Troy and possesses the bow of Heracles. Bitten by a water-snake during a sacrifice to Apollo at Tenedos, he is abandoned by the Greeks on Lemnos because of his incurable, foul-smelling wound. He survives by shooting birds with the bow. During the ten-year war, Odysseus persuades him to sail to Troy after obtaining the bow and arrows by craft; Podalirius heals him, and he kills Alexander with them. After later wanderings, he reaches Italy, fights near Croton and Thurium, settles at Crimissa, founds a sanctuary of Apollo Alaios, and dedicates his bow there.

Philoctetes in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · about AD 200 to 299 · the work’s page · read it where Philoctetes first appears

Abstract written by a model

Philoctetes is a Greek warrior, Poeas’ son, renowned as a great archer with Hercules’ bow, arrows, and arms. Abandoned by the Achaeans on Lemnos after a venomous water-snake left him with a festering, agonizing wound, he lives alone in a cave, surviving by hunting birds for food and clothing. After a long exile, Odysseus and Diomede return him to the Achaean host on Calchas’ counsel, and Podaleirius heals him. He freely forgives Atreus’ sons, urges the Achaeans into war, and fights prominently, killing Deioneus, Acamas, and others. Wearing Hercules’ arms, he wounds Paris with two arrows; the second causes Paris’ death.
