Epic Cycle in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Epic Cycle is a collection of epic poems, arranged chronologically in the Alexandrian Age, apparently by Zenodotus of Ephesus. Together its poems form an epic history of the Greek world down to the death of Odysseus. It begins with the union of Heaven and Earth and the birth of three hundred-handed sons and three Cyclopes. The surviving presentation selects only fragments judged important or interesting; these come from various authors, chiefly through Kinkel’s collection and the fifth volume of the Oxford Homer (1912), alongside the Müller collection of Epic Cycle fragments.

PREFACE

The volume includes only fragments of the Epic Cycle that seemed to possess distinct importance or interest. Hugh G. Evelyn-White relied mostly on Kinkel's collection and the fifth volume of the Oxford Homer from 1912.

The Ionic School

The Cycle was a collection of epic poems arranged in chronological order in the Alexandrian Age, apparently by Zenodotus of Ephesus. Its poems formed an epic history of the Greek world down to the death of Odysseus.

BIBLIOGRAPHY

The fragments of the Epic Cycle derive from a variety of authors, so no single manuscript list is given. The unit names collections and editions by Muller, Dindorff, Kinkel, and Allen, and cites discussions by Welcker, Monro, and Croiset.
