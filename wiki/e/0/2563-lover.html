anxious lover in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

An anxious lover is portrayed as a soldier in Love. He endures sleepless nights and the ground, follows and watches his mistress, watches a rival as an enemy, besieges her threshold when she resists, exploits a sleeping husband, and evades sentinels and patrols.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ELEGY IX.

The lover is compared to a soldier, enduring sleepless nights, sleeping on the ground, following a distant beloved, facing harsh weather, watching a rival, and besieging an obdurate mistress. Lovers also take advantage of sleeping husbands and evade sentinels and patrols.

lover in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

The lover is a soldier in love: serving and guarding a mistress, watching rivals as enemies, and besieging her threshold. Former idleness becomes action under love. Suited to a spirit experienced in trials, the lover endures night, cold, rain, snow, mountains, rivers, and sea travel.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

IX

The lover is compared to a soldier because he keeps watch through the night, endures cold, rain, snow, mountains, rivers, and sea travel, watches a rival as though he were an enemy, and besieges a beloved's threshold. Love drives the formerly idle speaker into action, and the lover is said to be suited to anyone ready for trials.
