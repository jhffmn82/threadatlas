the second suspect (Square-toes) in A Study in Scarlet

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1886

Square-toes is the second suspect in the Lauriston Gardens murder: a tall man, over six feet, in the prime of life, with small feet in coarse square-toed boots, a florid or red face, and remarkably long right-hand fingernails. He smokes Trichinopoly cigars, wears a brown overcoat, and is described as desperate, with an accomplice and friends willing to risk something for him. He came with Enoch J. Drebber in a four-wheeled cab, paced and hopped across a puddle, and was likely the tall red-faced man seen by John Rance. Holmes believes he lost a wedding ring at the scene and returned for it; when an advertisement is placed, a disguised accomplice appears instead of him.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER III.

Sherlock Holmes states that the murderer was a man over six feet tall and in the prime of life, with small feet, coarse square-toed boots, probably a florid face, and unusually long fingernails on his right hand. Holmes says the man smoked a Trichinopoly cigar and came to 3, Lauriston Gardens with Enoch J. Drebber in a four-wheeled cab drawn by a horse with three old shoes and one new shoe on its off fore leg.

CHAPTER IV.

Square-toes is identified as the taller man, whose stride measured about four and a half feet and whose writing was placed just over six feet high. He crossed a puddle by hopping over it, paced excitedly while talking, and is believed to be the tall, red-faced drunken man seen by John Rance; Holmes thinks he returned for the ring.

CHAPTER V.

The murderer in the brown coat is suspected by Sherlock Holmes of having lost the wedding ring at the murder scene and of returning to the house after discovering it was missing. Holmes expects him to answer the advertisement, but the person who appears is a disguised accomplice.
