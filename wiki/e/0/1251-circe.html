  • In The Argonautica, Circe is the sister of Aeetes who purifies Jason and Medea after Apsyrtus’s death and sends them away.
  • In The Odyssey, Circe is a cunning Aeaean goddess and enchantress who transforms Ulysses’s men into pigs, restores them, and advises and assists Ulysses on his journey.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Circe is a witch who bears children with Odysseus and makes Odysseus, Penelope, and Telemachus immortal.
  • In The Library, Circe is an enchantress who transforms and restores Odysseus’s companions, advises him about his voyage, and later receives his body and Penelope.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Circe is a goddess and sorceress who transforms Scylla, Ulysses’s companions, Picus, and Picus’s attendants through magic.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Circe is an enchantress and magician who turns Ulysses’s companions into swine, restores them, and guides Ulysses past future dangers.

Circe in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Circe is a great, cunning Aeaean goddess and enchantress, child of the sun by Perse, sister of Mercury and Aeetes, who lives on Aeaean island in her own house with four maid servants. She practices magic with drugs and a wand, transforms people, and weaves an exceptionally fine web. She drugs Ulysses’s men and turns them into pigs, then restores them and gives the company food, clothing, washing, shelter, and time to recover. She becomes an adviser and helper to Ulysses: she teaches him to fasten a bond, directs him to visit Hades and Proserpine’s house, supplies sacrificial animals, and sends the crew onward with a fair wind. After their return, she feeds them and explains the voyage ahead, warning of the Sirens, Wanderers, Scylla, Charybdis, and especially the sun-god’s island, where the crew faces its worst danger.

Circe in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Circe is an enchantress, daughter of Sun and Perse and sister of Aeetes, who lives on the Aeaean isle and knows all enchantments. She transforms Odysseus’s companions into animals, restores them after he resists her magic, and sends him onward with advice for passing the Sirens and avoiding the Wandering Rocks. Mother of Telegonus and married to Telemachus, she later receives Odysseus’s body and Penelope from Telegonus and sends Telegonus and Penelope to the Islands of the Blest.

Circe in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Circe is a goddess, daughter of Titan and the Sun, who rules an island and inhabits halls in Italy. Skilled in herbs, poisons, enchantments, spells, and an enchanted wand, she uses magic chiefly when desire is rejected. After Glaucus refuses her advances, she transforms Scylla with herbs and incantations; she later drugs Ulysses’ companions and turns them into swine, but restores them when Ulysses resists and compels her. She loves Picus, separates him from his companions, transforms him into a woodpecker when he refuses her, and changes his attendants into wild beasts. Ulysses remains with her for a year.

Circe is identified as the daughter of Titan. After Scylla rejects him, the enraged Glaucus goes to Circe's remarkable court.

BOOK THE FOURTEENTH.

Circe, daughter of the Sun, is asked by Glaucus to make Scylla share his passion. When Glaucus rejects her own advances, she attacks Scylla with herbs and incantations, although she does not harm Glaucus because she loves him.

FABLE IV. [XIV.154-247]

Circe receives Macareus and Ulysses’ companions at her palace and secretly mixes drugs into a drink of barley, honey, wine, and milk. She touches the companions with her wand and transforms them into swine, but Ulysses resists her potion with moly and forces her to restore them. Ulysses remains with her for a year.

FABLE VI. [XIV.320-440]

Circe, the daughter of the Sun, falls in love with Picus and uses magic to separate him from his companions. When Picus refuses her, she transforms him into a woodpecker and changes his attendants into various wild beasts.

Circe in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Circe, daughter of the Sun, is an enchantress and powerful magician skilled in herbs and spells. She turns Ulysses’s companions into swine, but after Ulysses resists her enchantments, restores them, promises safety, and guides him past future dangers.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXII

Circe is the daughter of the sun and a powerful magician who changes Ulysses's companions into swine. Mercury gives Ulysses the plant Moly to resist her enchantments, after which Ulysses forces her to restore the men and promise them safety; she later instructs him about the Sirens and the dangers ahead.
