marriage and household in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Marriage is presented as a household choice to make near age thirty. A man should choose a nearby grown maiden, marry her so he can teach her careful ways, and value a good wife as the greatest household gain, while avoiding a bad, greedy wife who brings misery and old age.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

(ll. 618-640) But if desire for uncomfortable sea-faring seize you;

A man should marry when he is near thirty, choosing a grown woman who has been raised for four years and lives nearby. He should marry a maiden so that he can teach her careful ways, since a good wife is the greatest household gain and a bad, greedy wife brings misery and old age.
