In The Wonderful Wizard of Oz, Emerald City is Dorothy’s destination and the green-walled seat of Oz, later ruled by the Scarecrow. In The Marvelous Land of Oz, Emerald City is the central city whose rule passes from the Scarecrow to Jinjur and ultimately to Ozma. In Ozma of Oz: The Emerald City, Ozma's royal city at the exact center of the four kingdoms of the Land of Oz, has jewel-studded gates and Ozma's palace. In Dorothy and the Wizard in Oz, Emerald City is the green royal city that welcomes the Wizard and celebrates Ozma’s holiday. In The Road to Oz, Emerald City is Ozma’s central royal city and the destination of the travelers’ journey and Ozma’s birthday celebrations. In The Emerald City of Oz, Emerald City is Ozma’s capital and the first target of the Nome King’s invading armies. In Little Wizard Stories of Oz, Emerald City is the central fairy city containing Ozma’s Royal Palace and the homes of several principal characters. In The Patchwork Girl of Oz, Emerald City is Ozma’s royal city, where Ojo is tried, forgiven, and later receives help for Unc Nunkie.

Emerald City in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04 · the work’s page · read it where Emerald City first appears

Abstract written by a model

Emerald City is a beautiful, wondrous city at the exact center of the Land of Oz, reached at the end of the yellow brick road and by air. Far from the Munchkin country, it is first presented as Dorothy’s destination: she travels there to ask Oz for help returning to Kansas, joined by the Scarecrow, Tin Woodman, and later their companions, who each seek Oz’s aid. The journey toward it is long and dangerous, but the country grows more beautiful near the city. The city is enclosed by a high, thick, bright green wall. Within are green marble buildings, green pavement, green glass window panes, emerald-like streets and rooms, green goods, and the Palace of Oz at its center. Its inhabitants wear green and seem happy, contented, and prosperous. The travelers enter through its gate with the Guardian of the Gates. Although it appears entirely green, Oz later explains that it is not naturally emerald-colored: the ordered green spectacles make it look green to its inhabitants, and Dorothy’s dress and Toto’s ribbon become white once they leave. After leaving for the west, the travelers seek the city again to meet Oz and claim his promise. Lost on the way, they are carried back by the Winged Monkeys and arrive before its gate. They return after Dorothy melts the Wicked Witch of the West, and the people gather to hear of it. Oz eventually leaves. The Scarecrow is then left as ruler, and the city and its Palace belong to him; its people are proud of him. Finally, Dorothy and her companions depart, looking back at its green walls, towers, steeples, and the Palace’s spires and dome, while the Scarecrow is expected to return to rule.

Emerald City in The Marvelous Land of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1904-06 · the work’s page · read it where Emerald City first appears

Abstract written by a model

Emerald City is a city in and part of the Land of Oz, serving as its center and largest town. It is defined by green: everything there is green or appears so, and it has high walls of green stone or green marble, gateways, streets, buildings, towers, turrets, and pavement decorated with green gems. It contains abundant beautiful gems, a royal treasury, and a royal palace. At first it is ruled by the Scarecrow as His Majesty after the Wizard's departure. Tip, Jack Pumpkinhead, and the Saw-Horse travel there, enter through its emerald-studded wall, and visit the Scarecrow at the palace. Its wealth then makes it the target of General Jinjur's Army of Revolt. The army enters its gateway and captures the city without bloodshed, drives the Scarecrow from his throne, searches its walls and paving for emeralds, and robs its treasury and public places of jewels. Jinjur, later called Queen Jinjur, controls the city while the men are enslaved and the Scarecrow and his companions flee and plan to return. The Scarecrow's party reaches the barred gates and enters the city during the revolution, while Glinda determines that the throne rightfully belongs to Ozma, daughter of Pastoria. Glinda's army surrounds the city and enters after its gates are opened; Mombi is sought, Jinjur is captured, and the stolen jewels are restored. Princess Ozma resumes her proper form, is recognized as the rightful ruler, and the people celebrate her accession. Thus the city moves from the Scarecrow's rule, through Jinjur's conquest and plunder, to Ozma's rightful rule.

Abstract written by a model

Emerald City is the sparkling capital of Oz, with emerald-twinkling streets, shining towers, and Ozma's towered green castle. It is home to Princess Dorothy, Sir Hokus, courtiers, servants, animals, and other residents. At first, the city prepares to accompany Sir Hokus on his quest. Later it becomes the destination of Tuzzle's recovery party, which reaches its outskirts and proceeds through jewelled streets to Ozma's castle. Sir Hokus plans to bring Speedy and Marygolden there, and Speedy and the Comfortable Camel expect to return after the Yellow Knight is restored.
