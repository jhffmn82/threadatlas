Abstract written by a model

In "Active Retrieval Augmented Generation," FLARE is presented as an active, forward-looking retrieval-augmented generation framework that retrieves information when needed during generation. In "DRAGIN: Dynamic Retrieval Augmented Generation based on the Information Needs of Large Language Models," FLARE is described as a dynamic RAG framework that triggers retrieval when next-token probability falls below a threshold. In "Unified Active Retrieval for Retrieval Augmented Generation," FLARE is discussed as a forward-looking active retrieval-augmented generation method that uses model confidence to decide whether retrieval is needed. In "DioR: Adaptive Cognitive Detection and Contextual Retrieval Optimization for Dynamic Retrieval-Augmented Generation," FLARE is described as triggering retrieval when uncertain tokens are encountered, specifically when a token's probability falls below a threshold. In "SEAKR: Self-aware Knowledge Retrieval for Adaptive Retrieval Augmented Generation," FLARE is evaluated as an adaptive retrieval method that triggers search on low-probability tokens, retrieves knowledge, and regenerates answers.

FLARE in Active Retrieval Augmented Generation

Zhengbao Jiang · 2023 · the work’s page · read it where FLARE first appears

Abstract written by a model

FLARE, Forward-Looking Active REtrieval augmented generation, is an active, forward-looking retrieval augmented generation framework for existing language models at inference time, without additional training. It generates a temporary upcoming sentence to anticipate future content, checks it for low-probability or low-confidence tokens, and, when needed, uses that predicted sentence as a query to retrieve relevant documents. It then regenerates the sentence using the retrieved information and continues this process throughout generation. Its central aim is to retrieve only when needed while making retrieval queries reflect the intent of future generations. FLARE includes FLAREinstruct and FLAREdirect; FLAREdirect is used when FLARE is not otherwise specified. Built on text-based retrievers, it differs from iterative retrieval and refinement in its generation and retrieval granularity and strategies, and may be combined with a browser. It was evaluated with few-shot in-context learning on 2WikiMultihopQA, StrategyQA, ASQA, and WikiAsp, where records report superior or competitive results versus single-time and multi-time retrieval baselines, including strong FLAREdirect results on 2WikiMultihopQA. Selective retrieval is reported as most effective. The records also report no significant gains on Wizard of Wikipedia or ELI5, despite another record stating significant gains over no retrieval on Wizard of Wikipedia. Its interleaved approach can increase engineering and generation overhead under a naive implementation.

Abstract

Forward-Looking Active REtrieval augmented generation (FLARE) iteratively predicts the upcoming sentence and uses that prediction as a query to retrieve relevant documents. It regenerates the sentence when it contains low-confidence tokens and achieves superior or competitive performance across four long-form knowledge-intensive generation tasks/datasets.

Introduction

Forward-Looking Active REtrieval augmented generation (FLARE) iteratively generates a temporary next sentence, checks whether it contains low-probability tokens, retrieves relevant documents when needed, and regenerates the sentence using those documents. It applies at inference time to existing language models without additional training and is evaluated on multihop question answering, commonsense reasoning, long-form question answering, and open-domain summarization.

3 .. 3.3

Forward-Looking Active REtrieval Augmented Generation (FLARE) is proposed as a framework that retrieves information only when needed and makes retrieval queries reflect the intents of future generations. It includes FLAREinstruct and FLAREdirect.

5 .. Open-domain summarization

FLARE is evaluated on diverse knowledge-intensive tasks using few-shot in-context learning, with at most 500 examples sampled from each dataset. Its hyperparameters are selected on development sets, and FLAREdirect is used when FLARE is not otherwise specified.

6 .. 6.2

FLARE outperforms all evaluated baselines across the reported tasks and datasets. Its direct variant reaches 51.0 EM and 59.7 F1 on 2WikiMultihopQA, and the method performs best overall on StrategyQA, ASQA, ASQA-hint, and WikiAsp. Its retrieval is most effective when activated selectively, and both implicit and explicit query formulation methods perform similarly.

7

FLARE differs from iterative retrieval and refinement methods in the granularity of generation and retrieval strategies. It focuses on long-form generation requiring active information access and is built on text-based retrievers, though it can potentially be combined with a browser.

9

FLARE did not provide significant gains on Wizard of Wikipedia or ELI5. Its interleaved generation-and-retrieval approach also increases engineering and generation overhead under a naive implementation.

FLARE in DRAGIN: Dynamic Retrieval Augmented Generation based on the Information Needs of Large Language Models

Weihang Su, Yichen Tang, Qingyao Ai, Zhijing Wu, Yiqun Liu · 2024 · the work’s page · read it where FLARE first appears

Abstract written by a model

FLARE is a dynamic RAG framework that triggers retrieval when next-token probability falls below a threshold. It forms queries from the last generated sentence, excluding uncertain tokens. Across datasets, it makes the fewest retrieval calls and is most efficient by that measure, though performance varies substantially by dataset and it remains below DRAGIN in reported timing and query-formulation comparisons.

FLARE in Unified Active Retrieval for Retrieval Augmented Generation

Qinyuan Cheng · 2024 · the work’s page · read it where FLARE first appears

Abstract written by a model

FLARE is a forward-looking active retrieval augmented generation method that uses model confidence to decide whether retrieval is needed, retrieving information only when uncertainty about generated predictions is high. It is reported to perform worse than UAR on the Active Retrieval Benchmark, especially in the self-aware scenario, possibly due to calibration-dependent uncertainty estimation.
