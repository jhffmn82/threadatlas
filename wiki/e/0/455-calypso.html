Calypso in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Calypso is an immortal, great and powerful goddess and nymph, daughter of Atlas, who dwells in a cave on the Ogygian island. After Ulysses is stranded there, she receives and cares for him, but becomes his unwilling lover, wants to marry him, and keeps him prisoner for seven years, trying to make him forget home. Though she can make him immortal and ageless, his lack of ships and sailors also prevents his return. Mercury brings her Jove’s judgment that Ulysses must leave. Calypso releases him, helps him build a raft, supplies clothing and provisions, sends a favorable wind, and warns that he will suffer greatly on the journey.

Calypso in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Calypso, Atlas’s daughter and a Nereid, receives Ulysses on Ogygia, bears Latinus by him, and is left after five years when he sails away.

Calypso in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Calypso, a sea-nymph, hospitably receives Ulysses, loves him and offers immortality; ordered by Jupiter to release him, she supplies his raft, provisions, and favorable wind for departure.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXII

Calypso is a sea-nymph who hospitably receives Ulysses, falls in love with him, and offers him immortality so that he will remain with her. Jupiter orders her to release him, and she provides materials, provisions, and a favorable wind for his journey.
