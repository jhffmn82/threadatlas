Phineus

person · 3 works · 21 quoted facts · 11 connections
Also called: Agenor’s son, aged man, aged sire, old man, seer, son of Agenor, unerring seer, Φινέα, Φινεὺς, Φινεῖ
  • In The Argonautica, Phineus is a blind, aged prophet and former ruler of the Thracians who is tormented by Harpies, welcomes Jason and the Argonauts, and foretells their route and dangers after the sons of Boreas restrain the Harpies.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Phineus, son of Phoenix and Cassiopea, tells Phrixus the way and is blinded either for this or for choosing long life over sight; he fathers Thynus and Mariandynus and is brought by Harpies to milk-feeders.
  • In The Library, Phineus is a blind seer at Salmydessus, said by one tradition to be Poseidon’s child, whom the Argonauts free from Harpies so that he can guide their voyage past the Clashing Rocks.

Phineus in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · -0299/-0200

Phineus, son of Agenor, is a prophet who lives by the sea and formerly rules the Thracians. Though married to Cleopatra, he is wholly irreverent toward Zeus, who punishes him with irreversible blindness; in old age he is also weak and continually tormented by Harpies that steal his food. Neighbors bring food while seeking his prophetic knowledge. Jason and the Argonauts visit him, and he welcomes them. The sons of Boreas, strengthened and protected by Zeus, restrain the Harpies and help him. In return, Phineus foretells the Argonauts’ route, dangers, later easier toils, and return with the aid of the Cyprian goddess. His earlier prophecies are fulfilled by the arrival of the sons of Phrixus, and his instructions support interpretation of a divine sign.

Phineus in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Phineus, son of Phoenix and Cassiopea in Hesiodic traditions, tells Phrixus the way and is blinded, either for this or for choosing long life over sight; father of Thynus and Mariandynus, he is brought by Harpies to milk-feeders.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE CATALOGUES OF WOMEN AND EOIAE1701

Phineus is described in different Hesiodic traditions as the son of Phoenix and Cassiopea, or as having been blinded either for revealing the road to Phrixus or for choosing long life over sight. He has two sons, Thynus and Mariandynus, and is brought by the Harpies to the land of the milk-feeders.
