Abstract written by a model

Accusations are unwelcome to a mistress’s husband: indifference leaves him unmoved, while devotion makes him suffer. Ovid portrays informing as dangerous, bringing punishment, chains, imprisonment, premature death, and loss of the woman’s character.

ELEGY II.

Ovid says accusations please no husband: an indifferent husband will not care, while a devoted husband will be made miserable. Informers may be chained or imprisoned, and the example of Tantalus shows the danger of a blabbing tongue; Ovid also mentions a keeper of Io who died before his time and another informer who suffered fetters while the woman lost her character.
