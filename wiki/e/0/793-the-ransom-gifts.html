In The Iliad, the ransom gifts are Priam’s treasure offered to Achilles for Hector’s body; Achilles accepts them, and Hector’s body is released. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the ransom for Hector's body consists of rich garments, cloths, gold, tripods, and a golden cup offered to Achilles in exchange for Hector’s body.

the ransom gifts in The Iliad

Homer · 749 to 724 BC · the work’s page · read it where the ransom gifts first appears

Abstract written by a model

The ransom gifts are Priam’s treasure offered to Achilles for Hector’s body: garments, gold, tripods, cauldrons, and a precious cup. Priam gathers and loads them onto a waggon, and Mercury brings them into Achilles’ dwelling. Achilles accepts the ransom, judges it not unworthy, and says it will be shared equitably with Patroclus; Hector’s body is released and wrapped in mantles and a shirt.
