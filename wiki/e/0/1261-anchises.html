  • In The Aeneid, Anchises is Aeneas’s aged father, paternal guide, prophetic interpreter, and posthumous revealer of Rome’s future.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Anchises is a mortal herdsman of Mount Ida who welcomes Aphrodite, fathers Aeneas with her, and is warned to conceal their union.
  • In The Library, Anchises is the son of Capys and Themiste who joins Aphrodite in love and fathers Aeneas and Lyrus.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Anchises escapes Troy on Aeneas’s shoulders, travels to Delos, receives a sceptre from Anius, and is later visited by Aeneas as an aged ghost in the underworld.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Anchises is Aeneas’s father, who dies and is buried at the foot of Mount Anchisia, giving the mountain its name.
  • In The Fall of Troy, Anchises is Aeneas’s aged father, carried by him through the flames and battle as they escape Troy.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Anchises resides in the Elysian fields, where he meets Aeneas, explains souls, reveals his descendants, and foretells Rome’s future.

Anchises in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Anchises is an aged Trojan, husband of Venus and father of Aeneas, honored by divine love and important as both paternal guide and prophetic interpreter. At Troy he first refuses flight, choosing death among the ruins, but the flame around Ascanius and Jupiter’s sign persuade him that the gods command escape; Aeneas carries him from the burning city. During the voyage Anchises leads and advises the Trojans, responds to Polydore’s murder, misreads Apollo’s oracle as pointing to Crete, then recognizes that their future lies in Italy. He receives aid from Helenus and Andromache, reads omens, warns of Charybdis, prays, and dies at Drepanum after many hardships. His ashes and tomb remain in Sicily, where Aeneas honors his anniversary with sacrifices and games. After death, Anchises appears in dreams, directs Aeneas to leave behind the old and fearful, and tells him to seek the underworld. In Elysium he welcomes Aeneas, explains souls, purification, and Lethe, reveals the future Italian race and Rome’s leaders, mourns Marcellus, and sends Aeneas out through the ivory gate.

“Then, not before, I felt my curdled blood

Anchises initially refuses to leave Troy, choosing death and burial among its ruins rather than exile and old age. After witnessing the omen of flame around Ascanius and a celestial sign from Jupiter, he accepts that the gods command him to flee. Aeneas carries him from the burning city.

BOOK III

Anchises leads the Trojans to sea, advises them after the discovery of Polydore’s murder, and interprets Apollo’s oracle as directing them to Crete. After the household gods correct this interpretation, he recognizes the connection between the prophecy of Cassandra and the Trojans’ future in Italy.

“The prophet first with sacrifice adores

Anchises receives gifts and recruits from Helenus and Andromache before departing. He interprets the omen of the four white steeds, identifies Charybdis, offers prayers during the voyage, and dies at Drepanum after enduring many hardships.

BOOK V

Anchises’s ashes are kept in Sicily, and Aeneas marks the anniversary of his death with sacrifices and games. Aeneas hopes that Anchises’s name will later be honored in Trojan towns and temples in Italy.

Thus far the sacred sports they celebrate:

Anchises’s shade visits Aeneas in a dream and confirms that Aeneas should leave the old and fearful behind and take the chosen youths to Italy. He also instructs Aeneas to visit him in the realms below with the Sibylla.

BOOK VI

Anchises is Aeneas’s dead father, whose image has appeared to Aeneas at night. Aeneas seeks to descend to the underworld to see Anchises’s holy shade.

Ye realms, yet unreveal’d to human sight,

Anchises is the dead father whom Aeneas is sent to seek in the Elysian grove. Aeneas invokes his dead sire when Palinurus asks for help.

Where long extended plains of pleasure lay:

Anchises reviews the spirits awaiting future bodies and warmly welcomes Aeneas. He explains the nature of souls, purification, and Lethe; identifies the future Italian race; foretells Rome’s leaders and greatness; mourns the younger Marcellus; and guides Aeneas through the ivory gate.

Anchises in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Anchises is a handsome mortal herdsman on Mount Ida, descended from Dardanus. He welcomes and sleeps with Aphrodite believing her mortal, fears her revealed divinity, and learns he will father Aeneas if he conceals their union.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

V. TO APHRODITE

Anchises is a handsome herdsman tending cattle on Mount Ida and is described as resembling the immortal gods. He welcomes Aphrodite while believing her to be mortal, sleeps with her, and then becomes afraid when he sees her divine form. Aphrodite tells him that he will father Aeneas, but warns him to conceal their union.
