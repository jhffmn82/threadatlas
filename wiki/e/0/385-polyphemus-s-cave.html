Abstract written by a model

In The Odyssey, Polyphemus’s cave is a sheep and goat station near the sea, containing cheese-racks and milk vessels and sealed by a stone at its entrance. In Hecuba and other plays, Polyphemus’s cave is the Cyclops’s dwelling on Mount Ætna, where Silenus and the Satyrs serve and Ulysses and his men encounter danger and prepare their escape. In The Aeneid, the Cyclops’ cave is the dark cave where Polyphemus keeps captives and Achaemenides is left behind when the Greeks flee. In The Library, Polyphemus's cave is near the sea and holds his flocks, trapping Odysseus and his companions until they blind Polyphemus and escape.

Polyphemus’s cave in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · about 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Polyphemus’s cave first appears

Abstract written by a model

Polyphemus’s cave is a rocky cave on Mount Ætna and the Cyclops’s dwelling, where Silenus and the Satyrs serve. It holds provisions, furnishings, cooking implements, wine, and an olive pole. Ulysses and his men enter for food, face threats and feasting on companions, then prepare escape and an attack.

THE CYCLOPS.

Polyphemus’s cave is the Cyclops’s dwelling and the place where Silenus and the Satyrs are forced to serve. Ulysses and his men enter it for food and are threatened with being roasted or boiled there.

ULYSSES, CHORUS.

The Cyclops’ cave contains Polyphemus’s hearth, couch, milk, wine vessel, cooking implements, and the olive pole used in the attack. It is the place where Polyphemus feasts on the companions and where Ulysses and his allies prepare their escape.
