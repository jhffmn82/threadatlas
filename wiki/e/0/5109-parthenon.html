Parthenon

place · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: the Parthenon, the temple of the Parthenon, the temple which they call the Parthenon

Abstract written by a model

In Description of Greece, the Parthenon was described as a place whose gables depict Athene’s birth and contest with Poseidon, and whose principal statue shows armed Athene with Victory. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Parthenon was described as a building in Athens built to honor Athene and extensively sculpted under Pheidias.

the Parthenon in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where the Parthenon first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Parthenon is a place whose front gables depict Athene’s birth and whose rear depicts her contest with Poseidon for Attica; its principal ivory-and-gold statue shows armed Athene with Victory, while its base depicts Pandora’s birth.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIV.

The Parthenon depicts Athene’s birth on its front gables and the contest between Poseidon and Athene for the soil of Attica behind them. Its principal statue is made of ivory and gold and shows Athene with a helmet, tunic, shield, spear, Victory, and other associated figures; the base depicts the birth of Pandora.

Parthenon in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Parthenon first appears

Abstract written by a model

A building in Athens, built to honor Athene, was extensively sculpted under Pheidias, its pediments, frieze, and metopes depicting divine, mythological, civic subjects, including Pan-Athenaic procession.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

SKETCH OF THE HISTORY OF GREEK SCULPTURE

The Parthenon was built in honor of Athene and decorated with extensive sculpture under the direction of Pheidias. Its eastern and western pediments, frieze, and metopes presented divine, mythological, and civic subjects.
