Dionysus's birth

event · 3 works · 1 quoted fact · 1 connection
Also called: Dionysus sprang from Dian seed, birth, born, born by Semele to Zeus, born by Semelê to Zeus, born perfect from the thigh, gave you birth, re-conceived
  • In The Bacchae of Euripides, Dionysus's birth follows Semele's death in lightning fire: Zeus conceals the unborn child in his flesh, and Dionysus is born perfect; another account says Zeus fashioned a likeness from fire.
  • In Hippolytus and The Bacchae, Dionysus's birth is attributed to Zeus and Semelê, despite Semelê's sisters' denial; Dionysus says he has come to prove the truth of his birth. (Hippolytus; The Bacchae)
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Dionysus's birth is said to have occurred secretly and far from men, at Nysa, after Zeus gave birth to him; other locations are rejected as false.

Dionysus's birth in The Bacchae of Euripides

Euripides · -0406/-0405

Dionysus's birth follows Semele's death in lightning fire: Zeus saves the unborn child by concealing it in his flesh, then Dionysus is born perfect; another account says Zeus fashioned a likeness from fire.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

The Bacchae

Dionysus is said to be the son of Zeus and Semele, who was consumed by heavenly fire. The Chorus recounts that Zeus saved the unborn child and that Dionysus was later born perfect, while Teiresias offers another account in which Zeus fashioned a likeness of the child from heavenly fire.

Dionysus's birth in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Different accounts place Dionysus's birth at Dracanum, windy Icarus, Naxos, the river Alpheus, and Thebes, but the hymn says these accounts are false. It states that Zeus gave birth to Dionysus secretly and far from men, at Nysa.
