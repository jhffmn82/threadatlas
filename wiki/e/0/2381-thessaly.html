Thessaly in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Thessaly is a region and province repeatedly contested by rulers and armies. It was associated with Jason’s growing power and ambition to lead it toward Greek sovereignty, while its forces supported the Thebans. Polydorus and then his brother Alexander ruled it tyrannically and oppressively; Alexander of Macedonia later promised to restore its cities but garrisoned and controlled them. It became the setting for Pelopidas’s imprisonment and release, Philip’s war with the Phocians, and major fighting involving Onomarchus, Leonatus, Antipater, Craterus, and Greek forces. Its cavalry shifted from Antipater to Leosthenes and the Athenians, and most Thessalians joined the Greek confederacy. The Aetolians recruited local support there before Polyperchon recovered the province. Finally, Demetrius entered through Larissa, captured or recovered places, and faced Cassander’s forces, whose soldiers later deserted to Antigonus.

Thessaly in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Thessaly is a chalky, mountainous region containing Peneus, near horsemen and associated with magicians. During Deucalion’s reign, stopped Peneus and extraordinary rain flooded it; it was generally identified with the deluge, though one narrative made the flood universal.

Thessaly is identified as the region that the deluge of Deucalion was generally said to have overflowed, although Ovid’s narrative follows the tradition of a universal flood.
