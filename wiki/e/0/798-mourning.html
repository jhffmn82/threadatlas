They mourn Troy’s ruin, Priam, children, families, lost homes and status, and the dead. grief and mourning in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Grief and mourning are a relief for sufferers, expressed through tears and lamentation over the dead, burning city, lost family, and separated mothers and children; they dominate Hecuba, Andromache, and the Chorus, then extend to Helen, Greek wives, and war’s suffering.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHOR. Attendants of the aged Hecuba,

Grief and mourning dominate the speeches and songs of Hecuba, Andromache, and the Chorus. They mourn the dead, the burning city, the loss of family, and the separation of mothers from children; tears and lamentation are described as a relief to sufferers.

CHORUS.

Helen mourns Menelaus by cutting her hair, wearing black, shedding tears, and claiming that she cannot forget him. The Chorus also describes the grief of Greek wives and the broader suffering caused by war.

grief for the dead in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

A mortal burden shared by all people, grief for the dead appears as Nestor, Achilles, Eos, and warring peoples mourn fallen companions, then intensifies in Greek losses, Podaleirius’s despair, and Deidameia’s continuing mourning for Achilles.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Then to the side of Aeacus' mighty son

Nestor grieves for Antilochus, Achilles grieves for his fallen friend, and Eos grieves intensely for Memnon. The Trojans, Argives, Aethiopians, Gods, and transformed birds also mourn the dead amid the continuing war.

BOOK VII

The Greeks mourn Machaon, Nireus, and Peneleos, while Podaleirius nearly takes his own life beside Machaon's tomb. Deidameia's grief over Achilles and fear for her son are likewise overwhelming, and she continues mourning after his departure.
