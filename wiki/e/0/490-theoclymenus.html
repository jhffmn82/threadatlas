Theoclymenus

person · 1 work · 8 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: a prophet, a stranger, another fellow, this stranger

Abstract written by a model

Theoclymenus is an exiled Argive seer and descendant of Melampus who seeks Telemachus’s protection after killing one of his own people. Telemachus takes him from Pylos to Ithaca and entrusts him to Piraeus. As Telemachus’s guest, he interprets a hawk omen as foretelling Telemachus’s enduring royal power and later sees darkness, blood, ghosts, and disaster around the suitors. Mocked and expelled by Eurymachus, he leaves their house and returns to Piraeus.

Theoclymenus is in one work on this site, The Odyssey. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Theoclymenus in The Odyssey

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XV

Theoclymenus, an exiled seer from Argos, approaches Telemachus at Pylos and asks for protection after killing a man of his own race. Telemachus accepts him as a suppliant and brings him aboard the ship.

Meanwhile Ulysses and the swineherd were eating their supper in the

Theoclymenus asks Telemachus where he should stay in Ithaca. After seeing a hawk carrying a dove, he interprets the sight as a sign that Telemachus will remain powerful and that no house in Ithaca will be more royal than his.

BOOK XX

Theoclymenus sees darkness, blood, ghosts, and other signs of disaster surrounding the suitors. When Eurymachus mocks him and orders him expelled, he leaves the house and returns to Piraeus, who welcomes him.
