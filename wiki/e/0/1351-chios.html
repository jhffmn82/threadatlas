Chios in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Chios joined the Athenian confederacy after defecting from Lacedaemonian rule, aided Theban fleet preparations, came under Thebes, and later defended against an Athenian siege with allies.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. IX,

Chios was asked to assist the Thebans in preparing their fleet and docks. Epaminondas later brought Chios under the obedience of Thebes.

CHAP. 11.

Chios was besieged by Athenian land and naval forces during the Social War. The Chians defended it with assistance from the Byzantines, Rhodians, Coians, and Mausolus.

Chios in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Rocky island Chios is home of a blind singer, claimed as Homer, whose songs are declared supreme and sweetest by the poet-narrator.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

III. TO DELIAN APOLLO

Chios is identified as the home of a blind man whose songs are declared supreme. The poet-narrator instructs the maidens of Delos to name this blind singer as the sweetest singer who comes there.

Chios in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Chios is an Ionian city opposite Mimas, associated with Poseidon and rulers Œnopion, Amphiclus, and Hector, and inhabited by Carians and Abantes from Eubœa.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IV.

Chios was an Ionian city opposite Mimas. Its traditions included the story that Poseidon fathered Chios there, and its rulers Œnopion, Amphiclus, and Hector were associated with Cretan, Eubœan, Carian, and Abantes populations.
