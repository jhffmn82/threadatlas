winds

group · 3 works · 4 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: East wind, The Winds, West wind, breeze, east wind, favouring breeze, fierce winds, side-wind, the Winds, the cloudy South wind, the wind, the winds, violent tempest, wind
  • In The Odyssey, the winds are controlled by Aeolus, who shuts them inside a sack while the West wind carries Ulysses’s fleet toward home; when Ulysses’s men open the sack, the winds raise a storm and drive the fleet back toward the Aeolian island.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the winds remain confined in a leather bag while Ulysses’s ship makes a favorable voyage; when his sailors cut its fastenings, the released winds drive the ship back over its former course to Æolia.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Winds are Memnon’s mythic brothers, carry him after death to the Esepus, and are personified by directional figures; Boreas and Zephyrus chiefly embody rudeness and gentleness.

the Winds in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The Winds, Memnon’s mythic brothers, carry him after death to the Esepus and are personified by directional figures; Boreas and Zephyrus chiefly embody rudeness and gentleness.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XV

The winds are personified as Boreas or Aquilo from the north, Zephyrus or Favonius from the west, Notus or Auster from the south, and Eurus from the east. Boreas and Zephyrus receive the most attention, as types of rudeness and gentleness respectively.
