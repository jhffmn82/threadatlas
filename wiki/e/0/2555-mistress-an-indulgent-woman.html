Abstract written by a model

The mistress is an indulgent, cruel woman whose choices shape the speaker-poet’s love and art. She rejects him while favoring and embracing the wealthy Knight despite his violent past, valuing wealth over poetry or tenderness. Yet when he tries to send her away, she defeats him with tears, embraces, kisses, and her presence, drawing him from Tragedy back to Love.

ELEGY XVIII.

The speaker's mistress defeats the speaker-poet's attempts to send her away by sitting in his lap, weeping, embracing him, and giving him many overpowering kisses. Her influence draws him away from tragedy and back to Love.

ELEGY VIII.

The mistress praises the speaker-poet’s poems but refuses to receive him and instead prefers the wealthy rival Knight. She is willing to embrace the soldier despite his former violence and bloodstained hand. Her conduct is presented as governed by wealth and presents rather than by poetry or tenderness.
