night watch

event · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 2 connections

  • In Hecuba and other plays, the night watch is organized by the Trojans in successive divisions, with different groups guarding the ramparts and Lycia’s warriors due to be roused.
  • In The Iliad, the Trojan night watch is a military watch maintained from night until dawn, with youths, grey-bearded men, women, and warriors keeping fires and guarding the walls.

the Trojan night watch in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

The Trojan night watch is a military watch maintained from night until dawn. Youths, grey-bearded men, women, and warriors keep fires and guard the walls to stop Achaean escape or surprise entry. Hector sends Dolon to assess Achaean watches; Dolon reports Trojan vigilance while allied forces sleep.

The Trojan night watch includes youths and grey-bearded men camping on the walls, women keeping fires in their houses, and warriors stationed by numerous watchfires. Its purpose is to prevent the Achaeans from escaping by sea or entering the city by surprise.

Neither again did Hector let the Trojans sleep; for he too called

Hector wants Dolon to determine whether the Achaeans are still maintaining their watches. Dolon reports that the Trojans keep watch through fires and sentinels, while the allied forces sleep.
