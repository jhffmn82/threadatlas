Rhesus’s horses

object · 3 works · 3 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Rhesus's horses, Rhesus’s white horses, Thracian coursers, bridles, coursers, harnessed steeds, his horses, horse, horses, steeds, such horses as these are, team, the horses, these horses
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Rhesus’s white horses are described as having coats like swan feathers and as unmatched in beauty; Minerva directs Ulysses and Diomede to carry them away as a glorious prize, and the charioteer says that the raiders escaped with them.
  • In The Rhesus of Euripides, Rhesus’s horses are trained by the Thracian Charioteer for war and chariot service, left without their yoke and harness while the Thracians sleep, and seized by attackers who flee with their bridles.
  • In The Iliad, Rhesus’s horses are described as exceptionally fine, strong, whiter than snow, and fleeter than any wind; after Dolon describes them, Ulysses unties, fastens, and drives them back to the Achaeans’ ships.
