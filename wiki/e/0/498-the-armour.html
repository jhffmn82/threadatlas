the armour in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

The armour in Ulysses’ house consists of shields, spears, and brass helmets. After Ulysses’ absence, it is said to be soiled with soot. Ulysses orders Telemachus to remove it from the hall to the strong store-room, citing smoke damage and the risk that weapons could encourage quarrels among the suitors. Later it arms Ulysses’ party, while Melanthius also supplies the suitors with armour from the store-room.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XVI

Ulysses orders Telemachus to collect the arms in the house and hide them in the strong store room. Telemachus is to explain that they are being moved because smoke has damaged them and because arms might encourage quarrels during the suitors' drinking.

BOOK XIX

The armour consists of helmets, shields, and spears left behind by Ulysses. Telemachus and Ulysses remove it from the hall and place it in the store-room, pretending that smoke has soiled it and that weapons might encourage quarrels among the suitors.

BOOK XXII

Telemachus fetches shields, spears, and helmets from the store-room so that he, Ulysses, Eumaeus, and Philoetius can arm themselves. Melanthius later brings shields, helmets, and spears to the suitors, enabling them to renew their attack.
