Europa

person · 5 works · 30 quoted facts · 12 connections
Also called: Europa herself, Europe, The virgin of royal birth, genealogy, genealogy of Europa, her, lost lady, sister, the daughter of Agenor, the daughter of the great king, the virgin, Εὐρώπην

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Europa is the daughter of Phoenix, carried to Crete by Zeus as a bull and mother of Minos, Rhadamanthys, and Sarpedon. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Europa is the daughter of Agenor, mother of Minos and Rhadamanthus by Jupiter, and reportedly carried to Crete on a bull. In The Library, Europa is the daughter of Agenor or Phoenix, carried to Crete by Zeus, mother of Minos, Sarpedon, and Rhadamanthys, and wife of Asterius. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the genealogy of Europa records her as a child of Agenor and Telephassa, or alternatively as the daughter of Phoenix and granddaughter of Agenor. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Europa is the royal-born daughter of Agenor who is carried to Crete by Jupiter disguised as a bull and later honored as a divinity. In The Library, Europa is the daughter of Agenor and Telephassa, or possibly Phoenix, carried to Crete by Zeus, mother of Minos, Sarpedon, and Rhadamanthys, and wife of Asterius.

Europa in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Europa first appears

Abstract written by a model

Europa is a royal-born daughter of Agenor, King of Tyre, renowned for beauty and attended by Tyrian virgins. Some writers instead make her the daughter of Phœnix and Agenor’s granddaughter; her name may refer to her fair complexion. While with the virgins, she encounters Jupiter disguised as a bull, overcomes her fear, touches and decorates him, and sits on his back. Jupiter carries her across the sea to Crete. Agenor then sends Cadmus to seek and return her. After her death, Europa is honored as a divinity and called Hellotis.

Europa in The Library

Apollodorus · about AD 70 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Europa first appears

Abstract written by a model

Europa, daughter of Agenor and Telephassa in one account, though possibly Phoenix’s daughter, is loved and carried by Zeus as a tame bull across the sea to Crete. Zeus fathers Minos, Sarpedon, and Rhadamanthys with her. Her disappearance prompts her brothers’ search; Asterius later marries her and raises her children.

Europa was described as a daughter of Agenor and Telephassa, although some said she was a daughter of Phoenix. Zeus desired her, carried her to Crete in the form of a tame bull, and fathered Minos, Sarpedon, and Rhadamanthys with her. After she disappeared, Agenor sent her brothers to search for her.
