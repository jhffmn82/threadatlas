Selene in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Selene is the moon, an eternal god in the lowest orb, moist and cold, receiving borrowed light and eclipsed by earth’s shadow. Egyptians call the moon Isis, honor its crescent-like horns, and sacrifice an ox to her. Selene was born to Basilea and Hyperion, loved Helios, and, after learning of his death, threw herself from a housetop; her name was later transferred to the moon.

Moon in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

The Moon, long-winged celestial body also called Selene, shines from heaven, embraces earth, brightens air, and reaches brightest fullness mid-month as a sign to mortals.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

XXXII. TO SELENE

The Moon is long-winged and shines with radiance from heaven, embracing the earth and making the previously unlit air glow. At mid-month her orbit is full and her beams shine brightest, making her a sure token and sign to mortal men.

Selene in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Selene is a white-armed, bright, mild, bright-tressed goddess, child of Hyperion and Euryphaessa and daughter of Pallas. Radiant beneath a golden crown, she drives her long-maned horses in a mid-month orbit, her increasing light a sure sign to mortals. At eventime she bathes in Ocean, harnesses her team, and, after joining Zeus in love, bears Pandia.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

XXXII. TO SELENE

Selene bathes her lovely body in the waters of Ocean, dons her far-gleaming team, and drives her long-maned horses at full speed at eventime in the mid-month. She conceived and bore Pandia after the Son of Cronos joined her in love, and she is hailed as a white-armed, bright, mild, bright-tressed queen.
