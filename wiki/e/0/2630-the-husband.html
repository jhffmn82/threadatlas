Corinna's husband in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

Corinna's husband is a jealous husband who guards and restrains his wife through a keeper and strong door, seeking chastity. Feared by the mistress, he is urged to abandon surveillance, soften, and treat her friends courteously because her mind cannot be confined.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ELEGY IV.

The jealous husband watches and guards his wife, but the speaker tells him that these precautions cannot constrain her mind. He is urged to show indulgence, soften his manner, and be courteous to his wife's friends.

the husband in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

The husband is Corinna’s jealous judge in accusations of adultery, yet is influenced by his beloved mistress and fails to guard her from the speaker. Criticized as slow and overly tolerant, he is urged to shut doors, investigate visitors and messages, and recognize her estrangement; his indulgence enables the affair and threatens the speaker’s possession of her.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

XVII

The husband is criticized for being too slow and tolerant of what no husband should endure. The speaker urges him to close the door at night, investigate visitors and messages, and notice why his wife sleeps apart from him. His indulgence is said to spoil the speaker’s pleasure and may cause the woman to cease being the speaker’s.
