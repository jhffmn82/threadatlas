Haemon in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Haemon, Creon’s son and Antigone’s promised bridegroom, values her burial of her brother and pleads with Creon to show reason and mercy because Thebes honors her act. After Creon refuses, Haemon leaves in anger. Found with Antigone dead in the burial vault, he attacks Creon, then dies by his own sword while holding her.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS.

Haemon comes to Creon to plead for Antigone, saying that the people of Thebes mourn her and consider her burial of her brother glorious. He urges Creon to listen, change his mind, and avoid stubbornness, but leaves in deep anger after Creon refuses.

CHORUS.

Haemon is found kneeling beside the maiden’s body in the burial vault. He attacks Creon, then turns his sword against himself and dies while still holding the maiden.

Haemon in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912

Haemon, Creon and Eurydice’s youngest son and Antigone’s betrothed lover, advocates for Antigone and justice. Angry at her condemnation, he tells Creon that the people mourn her, urges him to listen and yield, rejects one-man rule, and warns that her death will cause another. After breaking with Creon, Haemon finds Antigone dead in her tomb, attacks Creon unsuccessfully, then dies by his sword while embracing her.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

[Enter GUARD bringing ANTIGONE]

Haemon is identified as Creon’s son and Antigone’s plighted bridegroom. The Chorus announces that Haemon is approaching in an angry mood, apparently distressed over Antigone or their marriage.

[Enter HAEMON]

Haemon tells Creon that the people mourn Antigone and that wisdom requires listening to others and yielding when necessary. He argues that a State ruled by one man is no State at all, warns that Antigone’s death will bring another death, and leaves after breaking with Creon.

[Enter TEIRESIAS and BOY]

Haemon is found in Antigone’s tomb beside her strangled body. He attacks Creon with a sword, misses, and then kills himself with the sword, dying while embracing Antigone.
