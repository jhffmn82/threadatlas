Arete in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · -0299/-0200

Arete, Alcinous's wife and a queen, hears Medea's pleas, argues that Medea acted from fear, invokes Jason's marriage oaths, and urges protection from the Colchians by arranging Jason and Medea's marriage before judgment.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Fronting the Ionian gulf there lies an island in the Ceraunian sea,

Arete hears Medea's pleas and urges Alcinous to protect her from the Colchians. She argues that Medea acted under fear, reminds Alcinous of Jason's oaths to marry her, and sends a herald to urge Jason to marry Medea before Alcinous gives judgment.

Arete in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Arete is the greatly respected queen and wife of Alcinous among the Phaeacians, valued for goodness, judgment, and influence. When Ulysses arrives as her guest, she recognizes clothes made by her and her maids, questions his identity and journey, and orders a bed prepared for him.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK VII

Arete is presented as the respected wife of Alcinous and a woman whose judgment and influence extend throughout the Phaeacian community. She recognizes that Ulysses’s clothes were made by herself and her maids and asks him who he is, where he came from, and how he obtained them. She orders her maids to prepare Ulysses’s bed.
