Knooks in The Life and Adventures of Santa Claus

L. Frank Baum · 1902

The Knooks are queer, crooked, ill-natured immortal forest beings who watch over gentle and wild beasts, keep animal order with Claus’s help, and enforce laws obeyed by many beasts. Skillful and silent, they build Claus’s house while he sleeps, then select, cut, and stack soft wood for his work and fireplace because they understand human needs. After Claus gives their secret sign, the Knooks of the Forest rescue him from the jungle of Ethop, feed him, and return him to the Laughing Valley. They guard the reindeer Flossie and Glossie, require permission for their journey with Claus, and threaten punishment if they do not return to Burzee by daybreak. Though reluctant to grant favors, their Prince permits selected deer to serve Claus on Christmas Eve.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

MANHOOD / 1. The Laughing Valley

The Knooks silently and skillfully build Claus's house while he sleeps. They later cut and stack wood for his fireplace, and Claus thanks them for their work and knowledge of human needs.

6. The Wickedness of the Awgwas

The Knooks of the Forest rescue Claus after the Awgwas leave him bound in the jungle of Ethop. Recognizing the secret sign and signal of their order, they free him, give him food and drink, and transport him back to the Laughing Valley.

8. The First Journey with the Reindeer

The Knooks are the guardians of Flossie and Glossie. Their permission is required for the deer to make the journey with Claus, and they would punish the deer if they failed to return to Burzee by daybreak.

10. Christmas Eve

The Knooks guard the deer in the Forest and are described as unaccustomed to granting favors. Their Prince ultimately allows selected deer to serve Claus on Christmas Eve.
