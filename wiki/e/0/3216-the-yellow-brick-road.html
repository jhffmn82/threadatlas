the road of yellow brick in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04

The road of yellow brick is a yellow-brick-paved route to the Emerald City, made so travelers cannot miss it. Dorothy finds it near the Munchkin homes and follows it with Toto, later the Scarecrow and other companions. It passes through country that may be pleasant, dark, or terrible, then becomes rough and uneven, with broken or missing bricks and holes, before entering a great forest where dried branches and dead leaves cover it. The Tin Woodman clears a blocked section. It crosses the second gulf, continues through beautiful country, and is lost when a river carries the travelers’ raft away; they walk the riverbank to find it again.

the road of yellow bricks in The Patchwork Girl of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1913-07-01

The road of yellow bricks is a broad, winding road paved with smooth, bright yellow bricks, the only yellow thing in the blue country. It follows an easy route over hills and dales and leads west, directly toward the Emerald City through the Munchkin Country. Ojo and his companions seek it, find it, and travel on it. On one section, the landscape and road slide backward while the bricks remain still, forcing them to walk backward before continuing forward.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Eight

The yellow brick road is reached by continuing along the path the travelers are following. The Wise Donkey says it is the only yellow thing in the blue country and that it leads directly to the Emerald City.

Chapter Nine

Ojo and his companions are seeking the road of yellow bricks while traveling through the dismal blue country. They have not yet found it in this unit.

Chapter Ten

The yellow brick road is broad and winding, with smooth, bright yellow bricks that follow the easiest route over hills and dales. Ojo and his companions find it and travel west toward the Emerald City.

Chapter Twelve

The road of yellow bricks leads Ojo and his companions toward the Emerald City. In one section, the surrounding landscape and the road move backward while the yellow bricks remain still, requiring the travelers to walk backward before they can continue forward.
