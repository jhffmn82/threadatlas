Idæi Dactyli

group · 2 works · 7 quoted facts · 9 connections
Also called: Dactyli, Id&ci Dactyli, Idaean Dactyls, Idsei Dactyli, the Idaean Dactyls, those who are called the Idaean Dactyls

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the Idaean Dactyls are said to have taught iron smelting and tempering in Crete, beginning with Celmis and Damnameneus. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Idæi Dactyli are regarded as the first recorded inhabitants of Crete and are credited with magic, sacred rites, fire, and iron and brass working.

Idæi Dactyli in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Idæi Dactyli first appears

Abstract written by a model

Idæi Dactyli are regarded as the first recorded inhabitants of Crete, associated with Ida, Samothracia, and traditions of origin from Mount Ida in Phrygia. Credited with magic, sacred rites, fire, iron and brass working, they include Hercules, who instituted the Olympic games.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAP. IV.

The Idæi Dactyli were regarded as the first recorded inhabitants of Crete, dwelling on Mount Ida; some traditions instead derive them from Mount Ida in Phrygia. They were credited with magic, sacred rites, the use of fire, and the discovery and working of iron and brass, and one of them was called Hercules, who instituted the Olympic games.
