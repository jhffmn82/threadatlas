In Retrieval-augmented Generation across Heterogeneous Knowledge, a document is an element of a reference set that can be scored for query relevance and selected among the top-K retrieved documents. In BlendFilter: Advancing Retrieval-Augmented Large Language Models via Query Generation Blending and Knowledge Filtering, knowledge documents are retrieved from a knowledge base for LLM generation and filtered to remove irrelevant or misleading documents before answer generation. In Evidence-Driven Retrieval Augmented Response Generation for Online Misinformation, relevant documents are collected to support the reasoning and generation of responses. In R2AG: Incorporating Retrieval Information into Retrieval Augmented Generation, relevant documents were provided in five evaluation datasets, while cases with no relevant documents remain to be addressed. In RE-RAG: Improving Open-Domain QA Performance and Interpretability with Relevance Estimator in Retrieval-Augmented Generation, context is retrieved information paired with a question, encoded independently, and used by the generator, with relevance determining its effect on answer generation. In Retrieval-Augmented Generation Approach: Document Question Answering using Large Language Model, documents could be processed in real time in future work. In RetrievalQA: Assessing Adaptive Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Short-form Open-Domain Question Answering, retrieved documents may be noisy and lack answers or necessary information, limiting their usefulness. In Searching for Best Practices in Retrieval-Augmented Generation, contexts are varied during training and evaluation and may contain relevant or random documents, duplicate relevant documents, or no retrieved context.

context in RE-RAG: Improving Open-Domain QA Performance and Interpretability with Relevance Estimator in Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Kiseung Kim and Jay-Yoon Lee · 2024 · the work’s page · read it where context first appears

Abstract written by a model

A context is information retrieved from an external knowledge base and paired with a question for retrieval-augmented QA. It is encoded as an embedding independently of the question and used by the model generator to produce answers. Its relevance is crucial: irrelevant or low-confidence contexts can distract the language model, cause it to parrot misleading material, and reduce answer accuracy. In RE-RAG, a relevance estimator reranks retrieved contexts so top-ranked ones are used for generation. Training uses top 20 contexts, while development and testing provide 100 and inference selects 25. Context sets may be answerable or unanswerable, shaping reranking and whether contextual or parametric knowledge is used.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Abstract

Contexts can be relatively relevant or irrelevant to a question. Irrelevant contexts can reduce the performance of the Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) framework, while the relevance estimator (RE) assesses whether contexts are useful for answering the question.

opening

A retrieved context that is irrelevant to the query can distract the language model and negatively affect answer accuracy. A low-confidence retrieved context set may lead to wrong answers when the model parrots the context.

2 / 2.1 .. 2.3

A context is retrieved from an external knowledge base and paired with a question. In RE-RAG, contexts are reranked by relevance and only the top-ranked contexts are used before answer generation.

3 .. 3.3

The dataset contains the top 20 retrieved contexts for training and the top 100 retrieved contexts for development and testing. The experiments train with 20 contexts and use the top 25 contexts selected by the relevance evaluator at inference.

5 / 5.1 .. 5.3

Context sets are divided into answerable sets, containing at least one gold answer, and unanswerable sets, containing none. Their relevance and confidence affect reranking, answer generation, and the choice between contextual and parametric knowledge.
