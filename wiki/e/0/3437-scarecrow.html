In The Wonderful Wizard of Oz, Scarecrow is a straw-stuffed figure who joins Dorothy, seeks brains from the Great Oz, proves resourceful, receives brains, and becomes ruler of the Emerald City. In The Marvelous Land of Oz, His Majesty the Scarecrow is the former king who reunites with the Tin Woodman and joins the effort to recapture the Emerald City. In The Marvelous Land of Oz, Scarecrow is the former king of the Emerald City who helps reclaim it, supports Ozma's succession, and becomes the Tin Woodman's Royal Treasurer. In Ozma of Oz, Scarecrow joins Ozma's rescue mission, advises the party, is transformed by the Nome King, is restored by Billina, and helps defeat the Nome King. In Dorothy and the Wizard in Oz: The Scarecrow is an important personage in the Land of Oz. Oz gave him brains, which he proudly says work day and night and are the best in the world. In The Road to Oz, Scarecrow joins Dorothy's procession on the Saw-Horse, helps receive guests, performs sword-swallowing, and attends the dinner. In The Emerald City of Oz, Scarecrow settles on his farm, joins Ozma's defense of Oz, devises a secret plan, and helps defeat the Nome King at the Forbidden Fountain. In Little Wizard Stories of Oz, Scarecrow steers his corncob boat, repeatedly causes mishaps, saves the Tin Woodman, and is freed by the Wizard.

Scarecrow in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04 · the work’s page · read it where Scarecrow first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Scarecrow is a speaking, moving figure made and stuffed with straw, placed on a pole above a cornfield to frighten birds. He wears faded blue clothes and an old pointed blue hat, has painted features, padded hands, and a head full of straw. He does not eat, sleep, tire, or become hungry, and he can see in darkness; fire, especially a lighted match, is his only fear. Because he believes straw has left him without brains and unable to think, his central wish is to obtain brains from the Great Oz. Dorothy removes him from his pole, and he joins her journey toward the Emerald City. Though he calls himself brainless, he repeatedly offers practical ideas and aid: he helps free and repair the Tin Woodman, gathers food and warmth for Dorothy, devises ways across dangers, proposes rescuing the Cowardly Lion with field mice, guards his companions, defeats wild crows, and protects the group from bees. He is repeatedly knocked down, swept away, stripped of straw by the Winged Monkeys, flattened, or thrown, but can be restored by being patted or restuffed. After his clothes are recovered and filled with clean straw, the Winkies give him a gold-headed walking stick. Oz observes that he has been learning through experience, then replaces the straw in his head with bran, pins, needles, and straw. The Scarecrow feels wise and has wonderful thoughts. Oz appoints him ruler of the Emerald City, where the people are proud to be ruled by a stuffed man. Yet he leaves the city to help Dorothy get home, in gratitude for her help in gaining brains and good fortune. At the end, he intends to return to the Emerald City, with Glinda promising that the Winged Monkeys will carry him over the hill blocking his way.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

20 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter III

The Scarecrow is a stuffed figure placed on a pole to scare birds from a cornfield. He can speak and move, but he says he has no brains and wants to ask the Great Oz for some. Dorothy removes him from the pole and takes him along toward the Emerald City; he can carry her basket without getting tired and is afraid only of a lighted match.

Chapter IV

The Scarecrow often falls into holes in the uneven road, but Dorothy helps him up and he laughs at his mishaps. He explains that he was made only two days earlier, wants brains, and believes the Great Oz will give them to him. He leads Dorothy to a cottage in the forest and waits there through the night.

Chapter V

The Scarecrow helps Dorothy free the Tin Woodman by gently moving his head and arms after they are oiled. He continues toward the Emerald City to ask the Great Oz for brains and argues that brains are preferable to a heart.

Chapter VI

The Lion knocks the Scarecrow spinning to the edge of the road, but Dorothy picks him up and pats him back into shape. The Scarecrow says he is going to ask Oz for brains because his head is stuffed with straw. He oils the Tin Woodman's jaws after they rust shut.

Chapter VII

The Scarecrow fills Dorothy’s basket with nuts, helps keep her warm with dry leaves, and proposes that the Cowardly Lion carry them across the first ditch. He suggests using a fallen tree to cross the second gulf and thinks of cutting away the tree bridge when the Kalidahs attack.

Chapter VIII

The Scarecrow is swept away from the raft when his pole sticks in the river mud, leaving him clinging to it in the middle of the water. A Stork carries him back to his friends, after which he helps carry Dorothy out of the poppy field.

Chapter IX

The Scarecrow suggests that the field mice rescue the Cowardly Lion from the poppy bed. He asks the Queen to summon thousands of mice with strings and helps the Tin Woodman move the Lion onto the truck.

Chapter X

The Scarecrow travels with Dorothy toward the Emerald City and wants the Great Oz to give him brains. He argues that their errand is important and is fitted with locked green spectacles before entering the city.

Chapter XI

The Scarecrow is lodged in a Palace room but stands awake all night watching a spider because he cannot lie down or close his eyes. Oz promises to give him many brains and make him the wisest man in the Land of Oz if he helps kill the Wicked Witch of the West. He agrees to accompany Dorothy despite believing he will be of little help.

Chapter XII

The Scarecrow keeps watch while Dorothy, Toto, and the Cowardly Lion sleep, and he defeats forty wild crows by twisting their necks. He protects Dorothy and the others from the bees by having his straw spread over them. The Winged Monkeys pull all the straw from him and throw his clothes, hat, and boots into a tall tree.

Chapter XIII

The Scarecrow's clothes are found in the branches of a tall tree after the Winged Monkeys have tossed them there. Dorothy has the clothes stuffed with clean straw, restoring the Scarecrow, who rejoins the group and plans to return to Oz to obtain his brains. The Winkies give him a gold-headed walking stick.

Chapter XIV

The Scarecrow travels with Dorothy and the others through the fields and worries that he will never reach the Emerald City to get his brains. He suggests calling the field mice and is carried by the Winged Monkeys to the city.

Chapter XV

The Scarecrow tells the Guardian of the Gates that the Wicked Witch of the West has been melted and later demands that Oz give him brains. Oz says the Scarecrow is already learning through experience but agrees to stuff his head with brains.

Chapter XVI

The Scarecrow goes to Oz to receive brains. Oz removes his head, replaces the straw with a mixture of bran, pins, needles, and straw, and tells him he now has bran-new brains. The Scarecrow feels wise and returns proudly to his friends.

Chapter XVII

The Scarecrow says he has wonderful thoughts in his head but will not explain them. Oz appoints him to rule the people of the Emerald City during Oz's absence.

Chapter XVIII

The Scarecrow becomes the ruler of the Emerald City, and its people are proud that a stuffed man rules them. He suggests calling the Winged Monkeys and later recommends that Dorothy travel south to ask Glinda for help. He decides to accompany Dorothy because she helped him gain his brains and good fortune.

Chapter XIX

The Scarecrow is told that he is now the ruler of the Emerald City, but he leaves with Dorothy to help her get home. In the forest, the fighting trees seize him with their branches and throw him, though he is not hurt.

Chapter XX

The Scarecrow watches the Tin Woodman make the ladder and climbs it first. He falls from the wall so the others can jump onto him, flattening his body, but they pat his straw back into shape. He is thankful that being made of straw keeps him from being easily damaged.

Chapter XXII

Abstract written by a model

Scarecrow is a straw-made, lightweight character, friend of Dorothy and the Tin Woodman, famed as a hero and especially proud of brains given by the Great Wizard. He regards education and thought highly, calls his brains unusually fine, and repeatedly proves resourceful in crisis. He begins as king and ruler of the Emerald City, seated on its throne, but General Jinjur’s Army of Revolt conquers the city. Calm despite the danger that the invaders intend to destroy him, he escapes with Tip, Jack Pumpkinhead, the Soldier, and the Saw-Horse, even floating through a river like a cork. Traveling with his companions, Scarecrow helps navigate altered country, discusses education with the Woggle-Bug, and proposes practical repairs. Seeking to regain his throne, he hides twelve Field Mice in his straw body. Captured after returning to the city, he releases the mice from his jacket; the rebels flee, and the palace is secured. Yet Jinjur remains Queen and the group is imprisoned, so Scarecrow thinks out an aerial escape using the Gump. He provides ropes and clothes-lines, insists the machine needs the Gump’s head for direction, and later helps lead the party toward Glinda. During the journey, Jackdaws strip him of straw and clothing. He is restuffed with money, becoming the party’s most valuable member while retaining his old brains. He loses the pepper-box and its remaining Wishing Pills, but continues to guide the party. When Glinda is asked to restore the Emerald City, Scarecrow accepts that Ozma is the rightful heir rather than himself or Jinjur, supports the search for Ozma, and offers to rule temporarily if needed. He helps pursue Mombi and proposes using the Gump rather than besieging the city. By the end, Glinda praises his brains, and he becomes the Tin Woodman’s Royal Treasurer, maintaining that brains are worth more than money because they can enable comfortable living without wealth.

Abstract written by a model

Scarecrow is a living, straw-stuffed figure from Oz with artificial brains given by the Wizard of Oz, a very active conscience, and a body vulnerable to fire. Formerly king of the Land of Oz before General Jinjur deposed him, he is a popular favorite among Oz's people and a friend and counsellor of Dorothy, as well as an old comrade of the Tin Woodman. He does not sleep or need food. He travels with Dorothy to the great Wizard's city to obtain brains, though Dorothy believes he thought well even beforehand; afterward, he says the brains work splendidly. He arrives with Ozma on the Saw-Horse when Dorothy needs help, warmly reunites with her, and joins Ozma's small band to rescue Ev's royal family despite fearing the Nome King's furnaces. He advises Ozma, carries Billina's eggs, devises a way past a giant's hammer, and saves a private by cushioning his jump, though the hammer smashes his left foot. During the Nome King's challenge, Scarecrow fears becoming bric-a-brac, grieves when the Tin Woodman is enchanted, and is himself changed into a solid-gold card-receiver and ornament after failing to guess an enchantment. Billina restores him. Though afraid of the dungeons and fires, he returns to confront the Nome King and, acting on Billina's advice, throws her eggs into the King's eyes and blinds him. He presses the King to restore the Tin Woodman, later commands the army in the Tin Woodman's absence, and joyfully embraces his restored comrade. Finally, he returns with Ozma's party to the Emerald City, where the people greet him joyfully and he sits at the royal feast without eating.

Abstract written by a model

Scarecrow is a famous, living straw figure and straw man in the Land of Oz: very light, made and stuffed with straw except at the top of his head, where the Wizard of Oz placed sawdust mixed with needles and pins to sharpen his wits. Greatly wise because of his brains, he rides the Sawhorse, wears a blue Munchkin dress, and is a trusted old friend of Ozma of Oz and the dearest friend of the Tin Woodman. He is also a traveler and explorer of strange places. He cannot swim, since water would make his straw soggy and cause him to sink. He first meets Ojo's travelers while riding the Sawhorse, has the Shaggy Man pat him into shape, and learns of Ojo's attempt to restore Unc Nunkie. He identifies the Crooked Magician's unlicensed magic as lawbreaking, advises seeking Dorothy's help, admires the Patchwork Girl, and visits Jinjur to repaint his left ear. On returning to the Emerald City, his freshly painted ear gives him perfect hearing, and he eagerly seeks another look at the Patchwork Girl. Permitted to accompany Dorothy and Ojo in their search for a dark well, Scarecrow carries Dorothy's knapsack, helps explain and guide the journey, and keeps watch at night with the Patchwork Girl. He endures being tossed by the Tottenhots without harm, leads the party through a mountain rift, and lets Mister Yoop seize him so the others may escape. He enters a tunnel first, seeks a peaceful way to prevent war with the Horners, and is thrown onto a marble fence. A sharp picket traps him through the back, damaging his straw body and ripping his leg, until Ojo and Dorothy free and repair him. Continuing onward, he proposes reaching the Tin Woodman, supplies Square Meal Tablets, helps build and steer a raft, and recognizes the Tin Woodman's castle while warning the travelers not to crush the lilies.

Abstract written by a model

Scarecrow is a living straw man and fairy man from the Land of Oz, famous and popular, who lives in the Emerald City and at times in Ozma's palace or his corncob castle in Winkie Country. He wears blue Munchkin clothes, has a painted, jolly sack face, and is stuffed with straw. Though awkward and unsymmetrical, he is kind and considerate. The Wizard of Oz supplied his magic, carefully-assorted brains, described as the Wizard's best; Scarecrow does not sleep and has no nerves, so ordinary weapons and blows cannot hurt him. Fire is his greatest danger, while water ruins his straw, although he can still speak after soaking and can be restored with fresh straw. Sent by Glinda the Good to protect Trot, Button-Bright, and Cap'n Bill, Scarecrow crosses the mountains and Great Gulf into Jinxland. He helps Trot and Pon after Blinkie removes his straw, then is restuffed. He leads a council of war, proposes conquering King Krewl and placing Gloria on the throne, and goes alone to demand Krewl's surrender. Bound and nearly burned, he is rescued by the Orks. After they conquer Krewl, Scarecrow sits on the throne in the golden crown and is hailed as King and master of Jinxland. Using Glinda's magic boxes, he compels Blinkie to undo her wrongs: restore Cap'n Bill, heal Gloria's heart, and surrender her powers. He then rejects the people's offer to keep him as King, crowns Gloria, and returns with the others toward the Land of Oz. On a later journey, he guides the group toward Glinda's castle but takes a wrong path and falls through a river and waterfall. Trot, Button-Bright, and Cap'n Bill rescue him, dry him, and restuff him. He again leads them to Glinda's palace, tells of Oz, and travels on to the Emerald City. He remains Ozma's friend and humble servant.

Abstract written by a model

Scarecrow is a manufactured straw man: a suit of clothes filled with straw, made in Munchkin Country by a Munchkin farmer. He has painted eyes, two legs, and remarkable manufactured brains focused on facts. Though he needs neither sleep nor food, he can be affected by humiliation, rain, worn stuffing, and changes to his body. Intelligent, thoughtful, and curious, he values civil questions, kindness, learning, and careful reasoning. He can read when his eyes are freshly painted and recites poetry learned from Professor Wogglebug. His closest and chosen friend is the Tin Woodman, also called Nick Chopper. He travels with Nick and Woot the Wanderer, carries the Emperor's oil-can for Nick's joints, and joins the search for Nimmie Amee. Along the way he advises others, explains events involving the Wicked Witch, Captain Fyter, and Nimmie Amee, and assigns the Tin twins the duty of seeking Nimmie Amee. He is also friends with Jinjur, Polychrome, and Captain Fyter. During the journey, Mrs. Yoop transforms him into a small, poorly stuffed Brown Bear. He objects that such magic is forbidden, seeks restoration, and is eventually restored by Ozma because he remains stuffed with straw. Later, threatened by the Hip-po-gy-raf, he gives up his straw so the party can cross a ditch. Restuffed first with hay, he becomes heavy and lumpy; Woot and Polychrome later replace it with crisp golden straw. He avoids rain to protect his stuffing, helps the party reason through obstacles, and finally travels on with the Tin Woodman toward the Tin Woodman's castle, where the friends expect to talk over their adventures.

Scarecrow in The Royal Book of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1921 · the work’s page · read it where Scarecrow first appears

Abstract written by a model

Scarecrow is a straw-stuffed man and citizen of Oz, formerly its ruler and later ruler of the Silver Islands. He has very good magic brains given by the Wizard of Oz, needs neither food nor sleep, and can work with his head rather than his hands. Though he has no heart, he is troubled when Professor Wogglebug says he has no ancestors or family, only a bean pole as a family tree. Embarrassed and unhappy at being alone in Oz, he leaves the Emerald City to seek his family tree. At the Munchkin farm he investigates the bean pole, falls through it, pays the Middlings with an emerald, and lands in a silver palace. There he is hailed as Emperor Chang Wang Woe, receives imperial dress, fan, and parasol, and learns that he is considered to have a wife, three sons, and fifteen grandsons. He accepts the emperorship, appoints Happy Toko, befriends Tappy Oko, and faces demanding customs, a dragon-kite fall that requires straw and safety pins, and danger from the Golden Islands. He reforms the Silver Island and improves its prosperity, but misses Oz deeply. His sons are cold, his grandsons disbelieve in Oz, and he discovers his sons intend to remove him; after an attempted escape with Happy Toko, they capture him and bind him to the magic beanpole. He ultimately abandons the descent plan, decides to abdicate, gives Dorothy the magic fan and parasol, installs Happy Toko as Emperor, promises his grandsons the Books of Oz, and travels upward through the passage with Dorothy and the others toward Oz.

CHAPTER 1

The Scarecrow is embarrassed when Professor Wogglebug says that he has no ancestors or family and only a bean pole for a family tree. He explains that he descended from a bean pole, asks whether his brains and former rule of Oz count, and becomes increasingly distressed. After the party, he decides to find his family tree and leaves the palace to search for it.

CHAPTER 2

The Scarecrow becomes unhappy because he believes he is the only person in Oz without a family. He travels from the Emerald City to the Munchkin farm, meets the A-B-Sea Serpent and the Rattlesnake, crosses the river, and falls down the bean pole while investigating his past.

CHAPTER 3

The Scarecrow of Oz slides down the bean pole, pays the Middlings' toll with an emerald, and is released to continue falling. He lands in a silver palace, where the inhabitants mistake him for their Emperor and welcome him as an ancestor and father.

CHAPTER 4

The Scarecrow is missing after the party, and Dorothy believes he may be distressed about Professor Wogglebug's remarks concerning his family tree. His home is empty when Dorothy and the Cowardly Lion arrive, and Blink does not know where he is.

CHAPTER 5

The Scarecrow is not present in this unit. Dorothy and the Cowardly Lion miss him because he never grows sleepy and usually knows what to do, while Sir Hokus joins them in seeking him.

CHAPTER 7

The Scarecrow is hailed as Emperor of the Silver Islands after touching the magic beanstalk and is identified as the spirit of Chang Wang Woe. He learns that he supposedly has a deceased wife, sons, and grandsons, and that he is considered eighty-five years old. He accepts royal robes, forbids the imperial drum from being sounded near him, appoints Happy Toko as Imperial Punster, and begins inspecting the island.

CHAPTER 8

The Scarecrow finds ruling the Silver Islands difficult because of their formal customs, quarrelsome subjects, and demanding court duties. He befriends Tappy Oko, disguises himself to attend a kite-flying exhibition, is carried into the air by a dragon kite, and survives a fall by having his straw stuffing replaced and his clothing pinned. From the air, he sees a fleet entering the harbor and learns that the King of the Golden Islands threatens the Silver Islands.

CHAPTER 10

The Scarecrow is hosting Dorothy. The A-B-Sea Serpent and Rattlesnakes followed his advice to spend their vacation in the Emerald City, but they never mentioned him or his plan to hunt his family tree.

CHAPTER 14

The Scarecrow institutes reforms on Silver Island and makes its people more prosperous, but he remains melancholy because he misses Oz. He is disappointed by his sons' coldness and his grandsons' solemn disbelief in Oz. After overhearing his sons discussing how to remove him, he laments that his family has brought him unhappiness.

CHAPTER 15

The Scarecrow suspects that his sons are plotting against him and pretends to cooperate while planning his own response. He learns that the Grand Gheewizard has supposedly found a way to restore him to his original form, but he fears becoming eighty-five years old and being unable to return to Oz. He tries to escape that night with Happy Toko, but the eldest Prince has him captured and tied to the magic beanpole.

CHAPTER 19

The Scarecrow abandons the plan to climb down the beanstalk after returning for the Cowardly Lion. He proposes to abdicate, make a farewell speech, and return with the others to Oz, while planning to make a dash for it if necessary. He gives Dorothy the magic fan and parasol.

CHAPTER 21

The Scarecrow agrees that Happy Toko should rule the Silver Island and gives him the Imperial hat. He abdicates, promises to send his grandsons the Books of Oz, installs Happy Toko as Emperor, and prepares to return to Oz with an easy mind.

CHAPTER 22

The Scarecrow hangs just below Dorothy while the party flies through the passage and warns her that the journey will take hours. He worries that the Middlings might hear them and later says they are flying upward faster than he fell, suggesting that they are near the top.

Abstract written by a model

Scarecrow is in an excellent body on Silver Island, where he is treated as Emperor and temporarily speaks Silver Islandish. Though he has the Emperor’s sons, grandsons, and bride, and is identified there as Chang Wang Woe, he prefers being himself and considers Oz his true home. After recognizing his friends, he explains that he is better suited to being a Scarecrow, gives Happy Toko a language pill, and proposes using the family-tree bean pole to reach Oz. He announces his abdication in favor of his eldest son, keeps his current body, confronts the Grand Gheewizard’s magic, orders the Grand Gheewizard seized, and dismisses the Silver Islanders and Princess Orange Blossom. Back in Oz, he helps cover the bean-pole opening, vows never to leave Oz, and follows Dorothy upward to help Sir Hokus before being carried toward the sky.

Abstract written by a model

Scarecrow is a clever, gallant, and valiant straw man who is recognized by the silver palace’s inhabitants as Emperor and ruler of Silver Island. He has straw stuffing, excellent brains, a royal robe, and a little fan kept for Dorothy; through the fan’s magic, he wins a Silver Island victory despite having no plan. When the Imperial Army retreats, he urges the Silver Islanders to fight the King of the Golden Islands and helps defeat the Gnome King by outwitting him. Fireworks scorch his robe and stuffing, and after Happy Toko extinguishes him he is damp, dull-brained, lonely, and longing for Oz, Dorothy, and friends. Later he goes away to investigate his family tree, prompting Dorothy, Sir Hokus, and the Cowardly Lion to seek him.

Abstract written by a model

Scarecrow is a cheerful, jolly, living straw-stuffed straw man and tolerated inhabitant of the fairy Kingdom of Oz. Created to frighten crows from a cornfield, he has hay in his shirt front, a cotton finger, magic brains in his head, and an unusual freedom from discomfort when his neck is wrung. Though recorded as not a real person, he advises Benny to act like one; he is also described as a Munchkin, a former real person, a celebrity, a traveler, Ozma of Oz's subject, and formerly Emperor of the Emerald City. He is now an emperor of the Land of Oz, has a castle in the Winkie Country, and lives and spends most of his time in the Emerald City. He meets Benny in Oz, names a statue Benny, explains Oz's monarchy, offers an introduction to the Queen, recognizes Trot, and recounts his creation, journey with Dorothy, and receipt of brains from the Wizard of Oz. Traveling with Trot and Benny toward the Emerald City, he shows fear of Akbad, Quiberon's fire, and High Boy's appetite, yet repeatedly helps the group: devising entry to Cave City, questioning and threatening its rulers, warning King Silly the Second of Ozma's response, and surviving an explosion, soaking, and a shadow transformation attempt. He then leads the companions through the Roundabout Way, escapes the Round-abouties, and reaches the Emerald City. There he introduces the arrivals, praises Benny's courage, and joins the journey to the Ozure Isles.
