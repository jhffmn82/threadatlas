  • In “Active Retrieval Augmented Generation,” retrieved documents are retrieved from a document corpus and prepended before user input, while previously retrieved documents are discarded so only the current set conditions generation.
  • In “TRACE the Evidence: Constructing Knowledge-Grounded Reasoning Chains for Retrieval-Augmented Generation,” retrieved documents are supplied as potential evidence for answering questions and are converted into knowledge triples and reasoning chains to identify a useful subset.
  • In “Unsupervised Information Refinement Training of Large Language Models for Retrieval-Augmented Generation,” retrieved texts are contextual passages supplied to language models and are transformed into concise, accurate, and complete text.
  • In “Unified Active Retrieval for Retrieval Augmented Generation,” reference documents are varied in number during evaluation to measure how they affect model performance across datasets.
  • In “DRAGIN: Dynamic Retrieval Augmented Generation based on the Information Needs of Large Language Models,” a retrieved document is inserted into the prompt as an external knowledge reference and may later be replaced by a newly retrieved set.
  • In “REANO: Optimising Retrieval-Augmented Reader Models through Knowledge Graph Generation,” passages provide information and contextualized embeddings for generating entities, relations, triples, and knowledge graphs that support answer prediction.
  • In “GEAR: Graph-enhanced Agent for Retrieval-augmented Generation,” a passage supports multiple triples, supplies supporting triples, links expanded and memorized triples back, and is ranked for final output.

documents in TRACE the Evidence: Constructing Knowledge-Grounded Reasoning Chains for Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Jinyuan Fang · 2024

The entity is retrieved documents supplied as potential evidence for answering questions. They are obtained with a retriever model, often from Wikipedia, and each has a title and text. Datasets provide relevant and irrelevant documents, randomly shuffled in their original order, with annotations of relevance; using all of them as context can therefore introduce noise. TRACE converts the documents into knowledge triples, extracts reasoning chains, and uses those chains to identify a useful subset. The selected documents contain much less noise than the originals: in the HotPotQA test set, their error rate is 21.06%, versus 79.66% for the original documents.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Abstract

Retrieved documents may contain irrelevant information because of imperfections in retrievers. TRACE processes them to create a knowledge graph and extract reasoning chains, and it outperforms using all retrieved documents as context.

Introduction

Documents are retrieved as potentially relevant sources for answering questions, but they may contain irrelevant information. TRACE transforms retrieved documents into knowledge triples and can use reasoning chains to identify a subset of useful documents.

Problem Formulation

The documents are obtained with a retriever model and are often retrieved from Wikipedia. Each document comprises a title and a text.

Datasets

Retrieved documents are provided with each question and come from Wikipedia. They include relevant and irrelevant documents, are randomly shuffled in the original datasets, and have annotations indicating which documents are relevant.

Baselines

Documents are used as evidence for answering questions. The datasets provide documents that are randomly shuffled, and the baseline using all documents concatenates them in their original order.

Statistics and Analyses of the Generated

The documents selected through reasoning chains contain significantly less noise than the original documents. In the HotPotQA test set, the selected documents have a 21.06% error rate, compared with 79.66% for the original documents.

retrieved texts in Unsupervised Information Refinement Training of Large Language Models for Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Association for Computational Linguistics · 2024

Retrieved texts are contextual passages supplied to language models in retrieval-augmented generation. Their quality varies: they can contain correct, incomplete, incorrect, noisy, misleading, semantically related, complex, or lengthy information, ranging from all knowledge needed for a question to no directly usable answer. Such noise can hinder or mislead models, which may also ignore useful material. The Information Refiner perspective seeks to transform them into concise, accurate, and complete text. In experiments, systems share the same retrieved documents: top-five ColBERTv2 Wikipedia passages for several tasks, or dataset-provided contextual passages for others.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Abstract

Retrieved texts vary in correctness, completeness, and usefulness, and language models may ignore or be misled by them. The proposed Information Refiner perspective aims to transform them into text that is more concise, accurate, and complete.

Related Work / Retrieval Augmented Generation .. Our INFO-RAG

Retrieved texts supply contextual information for language models in RAG. Noise in these texts can interfere with the performance of language models or mislead them.

Positive Information Gain in RAG

Retrieved texts may contain all the knowledge needed for a question, a mixture of usable, incomplete, and incorrect knowledge, or no directly usable answer. They may also contain complex, lengthy, noisy, misleading, or semantically related content.

Experiments / Datasets and Evaluation Metrics .. Enhancing Previous SOTA in Open-Retrieval

For Open-domain Question Answering, Soft Filling, and Language Modeling, the experiments provide the top five passages retrieved by ColBERTv2 from Wikipedia. For Long-Form Question Answering, Dialogue, and Multi-Hop Question Answering, the retrieved list consists of the contextual passages supplied by the datasets.

passages in REANO: Optimising Retrieval-Augmented Reader Models through Knowledge Graph Generation

Jinyuan Fang · 2024-08-11/2024-08-16

Passages are retrieved, question-relevant source texts, often obtained by a retriever such as DPR and associated as a set with a question. They may contain marked entity mentions and are independently encoded with the question by Fusion-in-Decoder before their representations are concatenated for answer generation. In REANO, passages supply the information and contextualized embeddings used by the knowledge-graph generator: it identifies entities, infers relations, and produces triples and graphs that model relationships across passages. These generated graphs, triples, and the passages themselves are then given to the answer predictor or T5 decoder to support answers. Passage quantity matters: reducing it generally lowers performance, although triples from all 50 passages can postpone the decline until only 20 passages remain.
