Narrative, section by section

22 sections; each heading opens the text

YOUTH / 1. Burzee

The Forest of Burzee is a vast, ancient forest with closely packed trees, dense foliage, mosses, lichens, and dried leaves. It is described as mighty, grand, and filled with peaceful natural sounds and changing shadows. Nature originally populated Burzee with Fairies, Knooks, Ryls, and Nymphs, who live there as sweet immortals. Civilization has never reached the forest, though it is uncertain whether it ever will.

2. The Child of the Forest

Necile, a wood-nymph of the Forest of Burzee, becomes restless with her uneventful duties and longs for meaningful adventure. Ak tells the nymphs that he found a human infant abandoned near the forest and ordered the lioness Shiegra to protect it and feed it. Defying the Law of the Forest, Necile leaves the forest’s depths, finds the sleeping infant, and carries it away into the forest after it reaches for her.

3. The Adoption

Ak, the Master Woodsman, discovers that Necile has brought a sleeping human infant into the Forest of Burzee. Necile asks to keep and care for the infant, arguing that Ak can permit her to do so. Ak agrees to the adoption and grants the infant his protection, but declares that no mortal will ever again be adopted by an immortal. Necile rejoices and takes the infant to her bower.

4. Claus

Necile and the forest nymphs care for the human infant, naming him Claus or Neclaus after the Queen suggests the name. Claus grows up in Necile's bower, fed by milk from bell-udders, does, and Shiegra, while Necile teaches him to speak, walk, and play. Ak's decree protects Claus from the beasts, and the Ryls, Knooks, and Fairies visit him and become fond of him. Claus learns their laws and believes the Forest of Burzee is the whole world, unaware that other human beings exist.

5. The Master Woodsman

Claus grows from infancy to a stalwart youth while living among Necile and the immortals of Burzee. He learns the language and lore of the forest and helps the Ryls and Knooks with their work. When Ak returns to Burzee, he observes Claus closely and decides to take him on a journey through the world. Necile grieves at the separation and clings to Claus as Ak watches them thoughtfully.

6. Claus Discovers Humanity

Ak takes Claus on a journey around the world so he can see the many human beings from whom he is descended. Claus learns that humanity is numerous, unequal in wealth and circumstances, and subject to mortality, while the immortals are few. He becomes especially interested in the children and longs to help them, remembering how Necile rescued him as a baby. When Claus returns to the Forest of Burzee, he is thoughtful and no longer as carefree as before.

7. Claus Leaves the Forest

Claus tells Queen Zurline and the Forest people that, as a son of humanity, he must leave the Forest and accept the brief, difficult life of mankind. He chooses to devote himself to caring for the children of mankind, hoping to make them happy as Necile made him happy. Ak grants Claus continued protection and the assistance of the Nymphs, Ryls, Knooks, and Fairies. Claus leaves Burzee and chooses the Laughing Valley of Hohaho as his home.

MANHOOD / 1. The Laughing Valley

Claus settles in the Laughing Valley and begins building a home without harming living trees or flowers. The Knooks build his house, while the Fairies provide household tools, food-preparation items, clothing, blankets, and other comforts. The Spirit of Happiness removes Claus's fears and cares, and the Ryls of the Field Flowers promise to supply his food so he will not need to cultivate the flower-filled ground. Claus therefore begins a peaceful life supported by the immortals.

2. How Claus Made the First Toy

Claus befriends children throughout the countryside and brings comfort and happiness to those who welcome him, though he is refused entry to the palace of the Lord of Lerd and the castle of Baron Braun. When winter arrives, Claus worries that the cold and snow will keep him from seeing the children. While indoors, he carves a wooden cat modeled after Blinkie, creating his first toy. Blinkie's reactions amuse Claus and confirm his pleasure in the finished work.

3. How the Ryls Colored the Toys

Claus rescues Weekum from a snowdrift during a storm and gives him a wooden cat to keep. The Ryls help Claus color a second wooden cat to resemble a real cat, and the toy brings comfort to a sick girl. Inspired by Shiegra, Claus then carves and paints a wooden lioness. These events begin Claus's great work of making toys for children.

4. How Little Mayrie Became Frightened

In spring, Mayrie and her younger brother visit Claus in the Laughing Valley to ask for toy cats. When Claus shows Mayrie an image of the lioness Shiegra, both children become frightened and flee. Claus makes Mayrie a wooden cat and decides to model future toys on mild animals instead of fierce creatures. His toys become popular, and the immortals help supply him with wood, paints, brushes, and tools.

5. How Bessie Blithesome Came to the Laughing Valley

Claus gives in to Bessie Blithesome’s request for a toy and seeks advice from Necile and the Queen of the Fairies about whether rich children should receive his toys. The Queen declares that all children’s wishes and sufferings matter equally, so Claus decides to make toys for rich and poor alike. Claus creates the first dolly from clay in the likeness of Necile, then makes a baby dolly and a dressed image of Bessie. He gives the Bessie image dolly to Mayrie after she breaks her toy cat, making her very happy.

6. The Wickedness of the Awgwas

The Awgwas are invisible, evil creatures who influence people toward wickedness and decide to destroy Claus because his toys make children happier and more obedient. They abduct Claus to a jungle in Ethop and later imprison him in a mountain cave, but the Knooks and Fairies rescue him, while Queen Zurline and other immortals protect his home. The Awgwas then steal Claus's toys and prevent children from reaching the Laughing Valley, causing Claus to stop traveling and fill his home with toys. Finally, Claus asks the great Ak for help against the Awgwas' persecution.

7. The Great Battle Between Good and Evil

Ak confronts the Awgwas after they persecute Claus, but their King refuses to stop and declares that Claus will be killed within three days. Ak gathers the immortals, who prepare for war against the Awgwas and the evil creatures summoned to support them. In the battle at Laughing Valley, the immortals defeat the Awgwas, Dragons, Goblins, and most of the Giants, allowing Claus to carry his toys safely to children. Ak explains that the Awgwas have perished and that the powers of Good cannot be overthrown when opposed to Evil.

8. The First Journey with the Reindeer

Claus makes several journeys to distribute toys, then creates new dolls, animals, and musical instruments after studying children's tastes. During a deep winter, Flossie and Glossie visit him and help him build a sledge and harness so they can pull him to distant villages. On their first journey together, Claus travels by night, enters houses through chimneys, and leaves toys for sleeping children in a village and a large city. He empties his pack and barely returns to the Laughing Valley before daybreak, allowing the deer to reach the Forest before the Knooks' deadline.

9. "Santa Claus!"

Claus's gifts become widely known, and people tell children stories about his wonderful toys. Parents explain that the toys came from the good Claus, whom mothers begin to regard as a Saint because of his mysterious ability to enter homes. Santa Claus brings toys because children are little and helpless and because he loves them, not because they are always well behaved. His good deeds cause people to enshrine him as a Saint in their hearts.

10. Christmas Eve

Santa Claus asks Ak and other immortals to save Glossie and Flossie from punishment and secure permission for deer to draw his sledge on Christmas Eve. The Prince of the Knooks grants permission for up to ten deer to accompany Claus once a year, provided they return to the Forest by daybreak, and names them reindeer. Fairies recover the toys stolen by the Awgwas just before Christmas Eve, allowing Claus to make his first Christmas Eve journey and deliver gifts to children. He returns safely before daybreak, beginning a mission that brings happiness to hundreds of homes.

11. How the First Stockings Were Hung by the Chimneys

Santa Claus builds a larger sledge and prepares a team of ten reindeer so he can carry more toys to children on Christmas Eve. With help from the Gnome King and Peter Knook, he obtains steel runners, sleigh-bells, and strong leather harness materials. On one Christmas Eve, Margot, Dick, Ned, and Sara hang their wet stockings by the chimney, and Santa Claus fills them with toys. The children tell others, beginning the tradition of hanging stockings for Santa Claus, which helps him deliver presents more quickly.

12. The First Christmas Tree

Claus continues traveling around the world each Christmas Eve, bringing toys, fruit, candies, and other gifts to children. He discovers the idea of decorating a tree when he visits three neglected children living beneath a tent on a treeless plain. The children and their parents are filled with wonder, and Claus later carries Christmas trees to poor homes and decorates them with candles and toys. Years later, the new Baron Braun respectfully asks Claus to visit his children, and Claus agrees.

OLD AGE / 1. The Mantle of Immortality

Santa Claus grows old and weak after many years of making and delivering toys to children, and the Forest of Burzee and Laughing Valley mourn his approaching death. Necile asks Ak for help, so he summons a council of the immortals to decide whether Claus deserves the Mantle of Immortality. Every immortal votes yes, and the Mantle is brought from its crypt and bestowed upon Claus. The Mantle becomes part of Claus's being, driving away the Spirit of Death and making him immortal.

2. When the World Grew Old

Santa Claus regains his youthful strength after winning the Mantle of Immortality through his love for little children. As the world’s population grows and spreads across the earth, Santa Claus expands his toy-making and gift-giving work with help from several immortals. Ak, the Knook Prince, the Ryl Prince, and the Fairy Queen each provide an assistant to help Santa Claus. Santa Claus’s reindeer carry him across land and sea so that he can visit children around the world each Christmas Eve.

3. The Deputies of Santa Claus

Santa Claus discovers that new houses increasingly have narrow chimneys connected to stoves rather than fireplaces, preventing him from reaching the children inside. Kilter, Wisk, Peter, and Nuter propose entering through walls, and the four little folk accompany Santa Claus to distribute toys. Santa Claus later makes loving parents his deputies by asking them to prepare trees and stockings, while toy-shops supply additional gifts. Despite the growing number of children, Santa Claus remains committed to making them happy.
