Abstract written by a model

In Heroides and Amores, the ring is a gift for the speaker’s beloved that carries his love, circles her finger, stays with her in bathing, seals secret letters, and becomes his imagined means of touching her lips and bosom. In The Amores, or Amours, the ring is a pleasing gift from the speaker to his mistress, fitting her finger and accompanying her in bathing, touching her bosom, sealing her secret tablets, and conveying his constancy and affection.

ring in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · about 24 to 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where ring first appears

Abstract written by a model

A gift for the speaker’s fair beloved, the ring carries the giver’s love rather than material value. Sent to circle her finger, stay with her even in bathing, and seal secret letters, it becomes the speaker’s envied imagined means of touching her lips and bosom as a man.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

XV

The ring is sent to circle the finger of the speaker's fair beloved, carrying the giver's love rather than material value. It is wished to fit her finger well, remain with her during bathing, and help seal her secret letters. The speaker envies the gift and imagines becoming it so that it could touch her lips, fall into her bosom, and act as a man.

ring in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where ring first appears

Abstract written by a model

A pleasing gift from the speaker to his mistress, the light ring fits her finger and is happily handled. It may accompany her in bathing, touch her bosom, seal her secret tablets, and convey his constancy and affection.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

ELEGY XV.

The ring is presented as a pleasing gift to the mistress and is asked to fit closely around her finger. It is imagined touching her bosom, sealing her secret tablets, and being worn while she bathes; it is also told to convey the speaker’s constancy.
