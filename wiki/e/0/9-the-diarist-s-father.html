the diarist's father

person · 1 work · 12 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: My father, Pepys's father, Samuel Pepys's father, father, father (speaker's father), my father, my father and I, my father's, poor man

Abstract written by a model

The diarist’s father is a central family and business figure, assisting Samuel Pepys with the cloth business and helping choose scarlet, purple, and black cloth and linen. When a letter reports that Robert is seriously ill and needs help with his business, he prepares to go to him and considers advice about a proposed land purchase. After Robert’s death, he and the diarist work at Brampton through Robert’s will, property, and papers, trying to put the estate in order: letting the crop, arranging for Stankes to manage the business, and discussing the will with the Trices and others. He remains alone at Brampton after the diarist returns to London, while there is concern that he may profit from his London house rather than leave it to Tom. Later he is found well, travels between Brampton and London, and joins the diarist in dealings with Thomas Trice, Dr. Williams, and Mrs. Whately’s household, including examination and proof of the uncle’s will and discussion of a possible marriage for Tom. He notices the diarist drinking, an unfamiliar sight to him. In family matters, he helps confront Pall and agrees she should go into the country with the diarist’s mother. His finances appear precarious: £45 is owed to him, about £45 is owed by him, and this is said to be all his money due in the world.

My father advises and assists with the cloth business. He visits the diarist, drinks his morning draft with him, discusses the cloth, and helps resolve on two pieces each of scarlet, purple, and black, with £50 in linen.

opening

The diarist's father learns from the letter from the diarist's aunt that Robert is seriously unwell and needs help with his business. He plans to travel to Robert, and the diarist advises him on how to discuss the proposed land purchase.

opening

Pepys finds his father well at Brampton and later returns with him to Gorum's to speak with Thomas Trice. His father notices that Pepys has been drinking, something he had never seen or heard of before, and later returns from Brampton to London in good health.

opening

The narrator’s father accompanies him to Dr. Williams and to Mrs. Whately’s household while discussing a possible marriage for Tom. He helps examine and prove his uncle’s will with Tom Trice and Dr. Williams. He is present with the narrator and uncle Wight at an overheated gathering at his brother’s house, and later he and the narrator find the narrator’s mother discontented and unquiet.
