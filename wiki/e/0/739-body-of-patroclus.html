body of Patroclus in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

The body of Patroclus, after his death, becomes the center of a day-long struggle. Menelaus and Ajax guard it as Trojans seek to carry it to Ilius and Achaeans pull it toward their ships. Hector attacks and tries three times to drag it away, but the two Ajaxes repel him. After Achilles frightens the Trojans, the Achaeans recover, wash, anoint, and place the mangled body on a bier. In Achilles’s tent, comrades mourn while Thetis preserves it from decay.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XVII.

Menelaus and Ajax stand over the body of Patroclus to prevent the Trojans from taking it. Trojans and Achaeans drag it back and forth throughout the day, with the Trojans trying to carry it into Ilius and the Achaeans trying to take it to their ships.

BOOK XVIII.

Patroclus’s body lies exposed amid a fierce fight between the Achaeans and Trojans. Hector tries three times to drag it away, but the two Ajaxes drive him back; after Achilles frightens the Trojans, the Achaeans recover the body, wash it, anoint it, and place it on a bier.

Patroclus's body lies mangled in Achilles's tent with his feet toward the door, while his comrades mourn around him. Thetis treats the wounds with ambrosia and red nectar so that the body will not decay.
