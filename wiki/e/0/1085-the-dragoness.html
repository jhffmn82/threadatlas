the dragoness

creature · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: The dragon that was slain by Apollo, bloody plague, dragon, great she-dragon, monster, she-dragon, the dragon
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the dragoness plagues mortals and sheep, raises Typhaon, and is killed by Apollo near a spring, where her sun-rotting body gives Pytho its name.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the dragon guards Earth's oracle, is slain by Apollo with arrows, and its rotting is said to give Pytho its name.

the dragoness in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

A bloated, great, fierce she-dragon plagues mortals and sheep, raising Typhaon; Apollo kills her near a spring, where her sun-rotting body gives Pytho its name.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

TO PYTHIAN APOLLO—

The dragoness raises Typhaon and causes great harm to men and their sheep. Apollo shoots and kills her near the spring, after which her body rots in the sunlight and the place is called Pytho.

dragon in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Earth placed dragon to guard her oracle; Apollo slew it with arrows, and its rotting was said to give Pytho its name.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

The dragon was said by the poets to have been placed there by Earth to guard her oracle. Apollo slew it with his arrows, and its rotting was traditionally given as the reason the town was called Pytho.
