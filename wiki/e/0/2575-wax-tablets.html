tablets in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

Vermilion maple wax tablets, filled with writing, carry the speaker’s persuasive letter to Corinna. They return with her refusal; the speaker curses them as fatal and useless, imagines their ominous origin, yet conditionally dedicates them to Venus if they succeed.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ELEGY XI.

The tablets carry the speaker's persuasive writing to Corinna. They return with a letter of refusal, after which the speaker curses them, calls them fatal and useless, and imagines that their wood came from a tree associated with execution and birds of ill omen.

wax tablets in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

The wax tablets are double writing tablets with wax surfaces, inscribed by Naso and carried by Nape to Corinna as love correspondence. Naso asks Corinna for a reply, saying “Come!” would suffice and promising to dedicate them to Venus. They return bearing her refusal to come that day, provoking curses and wishes for their destruction.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

XI

The wax tablets are inscribed by Naso and carried by Nape to Corinna. Naso wants Corinna to fill them with a reply, but says that a single word, “Come!”, would be enough; he would then dedicate the tablets to Venus.

XII

The tablets return with gloomy news that the beloved woman cannot come that day. The speaker calls them ill-natured, funereal, and double in their dealings, throws curses upon them, and wishes them to decay or be crushed.
