Epigoni

group · 4 works · 8 quoted facts · 9 connections
Also called: Epigoni (the victorious descendants), Epigonians, The Epigoni, sons of the fallen, the Epigoni, those whom the Greeks call Epigoni, ἐπίγονοι, ἐπιγόνων

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the Epigoni sent Manto to Delphi as a first fruit of their spoil. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Epigoni, led by Alcmaeon, defeated the Thebans, razed Thebes, and returned home with spoil. In The Library, the Epigoni, sons of the Argive dead, marched against Thebes ten years later to avenge their fathers, choosing Alcmaeon as leader. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the Epigoni were a Greek army led by Thersander that marched against and defeated the Thebans at Glisas.

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Epigoni first appears

Abstract written by a model

Epigoni was a Greek army led by Thersander. Not many years after the war involving Adrastus, it marched against Thebes, strengthened by recruits from Argolis, Messenia, Arcadia, Corinth, and Megara, and defeated the Thebans at Glisas.
