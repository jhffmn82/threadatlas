Epigoni

group · 4 works · 8 quoted facts · 9 connections
Also called: Epigoni (the victorious descendants), Epigonians, The Epigoni, sons of the fallen, the Epigoni, those whom the Greeks call Epigoni, ἐπίγονοι, ἐπιγόνων
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Epigoni, led by Alcmaeon, defeated the Thebans, razed Thebes, and returned home with spoil.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the Epigoni sent Manto to Delphi as a first fruit of their spoil.
  • In The Library, the Epigoni, sons of the Argive dead, marched against Thebes ten years later to avenge their fathers, choosing Alcmaeon as leader.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the Epigoni were a Greek army led by Thersander that marched against and defeated the Thebans at Glisas.

Epigoni in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Epigoni was a Greek army led by Thersander. Not many years after the war involving Adrastus, it marched against Thebes, strengthened by recruits from Argolis, Messenia, Arcadia, Corinth, and Megara, and defeated the Thebans at Glisas.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IX.

The Epigoni marched against Thebes not many years after the war involving Adrastus. Their army included forces from Argolis, Messenia, Arcadia, Corinth, and Megara, and they defeated the Thebans at Glisas.
