subqueries in Question Decomposition for Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Paul J. L. Ammann · 2025-07-28/2025-07-29

Subqueries are simpler, focused queries produced by question decomposition from a complex original question. They address distinct aspects independently, retrieve documents for each part, and aggregate results into a broader candidate pool, improving coverage. They are intended for broad coverage rather than reproducing reasoning steps or gold evidence; one decomposition module is limited to five and produces five for most listed queries. Their quality depends on prompt design and the large language model. Although they can target different aspects of a query, sequential resolution can add latency and cascading errors, while independent retrieval can introduce noise. For already-specific queries they may distract from the original, and in rare cases retrieve no stronger evidence than the original query alone.

question decomposition (QD) in Question Decomposition for Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Paul J. L. Ammann · 2025-07-28/2025-07-29

Question decomposition (QD) is a RAG pipeline component and process that uses a large language model to break an original complex query into simpler, fact-seeking subqueries. Each subquery targets a distinct aspect or reasoning step, allowing passage retrieval to seek evidence from different, self-contained sources. QD then supports the assembly and aggregation of complementary documents, especially for multi-hop questions, where broader evidence coverage can improve retrieval and answer correctness. It can be used as a drop-in enhancement with an off-the-shelf cross-encoder reranker; together, decomposition and reranking improve document coverage, ranking quality, and the surfacing of multi-hop evidence without fine-tuning or task-specific training. The number of generated subqueries is generally small and may vary with the query’s aspects or reasoning steps, although one reported setup generated a fixed five. QD adds computational overhead and depends on LLM quality. It may also introduce noise, distract from the original intent, and be counterproductive when the query is already precise. Therefore, it should be applied selectively, when multi-hop reasoning is predicted to be necessary.
