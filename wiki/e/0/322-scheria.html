Scheria in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Scheria is the fertile, inhabited land and home of the Phaeacians, settled far from other people. It contains a walled town or city with houses, temples, divided lands, harbours, and seafaring associations. Assigned by Jove and aided by Minerva as Ulysses’s safe destination after his perilous voyage, it first appears to him as mountainous, rocky, spray-covered coast without an evident safe harbour. After his raft is wrecked, he reaches Scheria and is guided to the palace. Its Phaeacians escort him to Ithaca with gifts; afterward Neptune turns their returning ship to stone and threatens the city with burial under a mountain.

The land of the Phaeacians is the destination toward which Minerva guides Ulysses and the place where he is expected to be safe. When Ulysses sees land, he finds a coast covered in spray, with low rocks, headlands, mountain tops, and no safe harbour at first.

BOOK VI

Scheria is the land where Nausithous settled the Phaeacians, far from other people. It has a walled city, houses, temples, divided lands, harbours, and a town associated with seafaring.

BOOK VII

Scheria is the land of the Phaeacians, where Ulysses arrives after swimming from the wreck of his raft. Minerva leaves Scheria for Marathon and Athens after guiding Ulysses to the palace.

BOOK XIII

Scheria is the home of the Phaeacians, who escort Ulysses to Ithaca and provide him with gifts. Neptune later turns the returning ship to stone near Scheria and threatens the city with burial under a mountain.
