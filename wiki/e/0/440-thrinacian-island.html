Abstract written by a model

In The Odyssey, the Thrinacian island is where the Sun’s sacred livestock graze, and Ulysses and his crew remain there because unfavorable winds prevent departure. In The Library, Thrinacia is the island of the Sun where cattle graze; Ulysses is detained there by lack of wind, and his companions slaughter some cattle for food.

Thrinacian island in The Odyssey

Homer · about 632 to 599 BC · the work’s page · read it where Thrinacian island first appears

Abstract written by a model

Thrinacian island is the place where the Sun’s sacred livestock graze: seven herds of cattle and seven flocks of sheep, fifty animals in each, belonging to the sun-god Hyperion and tended by Phaethusa and Lampetie. The animals neither breed nor diminish. Teiresias warns Ulysses that harming them will destroy his ship and men. Though Ulysses warns his exhausted crew away, they land on the island and remain for a month because unfavorable winds prevent departure.

The Thrinacian island contains the sheep and cattle belonging to the Sun. Teiresias warns Ulysses to leave these flocks unharmed, since harming them will bring destruction to his ship and men.
