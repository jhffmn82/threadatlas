Also called: Swift lightning, thunder, and deadly thunderbolt, Zeus's weapons, all mine armoury, blazing thunderbolt, glowing thunderbolt, lightning, lurid thunderbolt, the thunderbolt, thunder-stone, κεραυνὸν

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the thunderbolt was given to Zeus by the released sons of Heaven and became his formidable weapon against the Titans' armies, and he later cast it at Hera's urging to frighten Frogs and Mice. In The Library, the thunderbolt was made by the Cyclopes for Zeus, who used it to strike Aesculapius. In The Fall of Troy, Zeus gave Athena his lightning, thunder, and deadly thunderbolt, which no god but Zeus could lift; she used them to unleash the storm and shatter Aias's ship.

Abstract written by a model

Given to Zeus by released sons of Heaven after he releases them, the thunderbolt becomes his formidable weapon. He uses its fire, heat, and noise to overwhelm the Titans' armies, then casts it at Hera's urging, frightening Frogs and Mice without ending the Mice's hope.

The brothers of Zeus's father give him the thunderbolt, thunder, and lightning after he releases them. Zeus uses the thunderbolt against the Titans, whose armies are overwhelmed by its fire, heat, and noise.

THE BATTLE OF FROGS AND MICE

The thunderbolt is Zeus's formidable weapon. He casts it after Hera urges him to intervene, and it frightens both the Frogs and the Mice, though it does not stop the Mice from continuing to hope for victory.
