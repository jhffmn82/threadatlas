Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, Teucer fights as an Achaean archer beside Ajax, kills several Trojans, wounds Glaucus, strikes Hector, and wins an archery contest against Meriones. In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Teucer defends Aias’s worth, protects Eurysakes, and organizes the cleansing, preparation, and burial of Aias’s corpse. In Hecuba and other plays, Teucer reaches Egypt after Troy’s fall and Ajax’s death, seeks Theonoe’s prophecy, encounters Helen, and is destined to settle in Salamis. In The Library, Teucer rules the country to which Dardanus comes, welcomes him, gives him land and Batia as a wife, and after Teucer’s death Dardanus names the whole country Dardania. In The Fall of Troy, Teucer competes in the footrace and archery-test, fights to rescue Machaon, and mourns his brother Aias after his death.

Teucer in The Iliad

Homer · 749 to 724 BC · the work’s page · read it where Teucer first appears

Abstract written by a model

Teucer is an Achaean archer, king, son of Telamon, and half-brother of Ajax son of Telamon. Known for a mighty bow given by Apollo, he also carries a four-hide shield and bronze-shod spear and can fight hand-to-hand. Fighting beside and behind Ajax’s shield, he kills several Trojans, including Gorgythion, Archeptolemus, and Cleitus, while repeatedly aiming at Hector. He wounds Glaucus at the wall and strikes Hector’s shield-band, but Hector later disables his arm with a stone, forcing him to drop his bow; Jove also breaks its string. Teucer then takes spear and shield to fight with Ajax. In an archery contest against Meriones, he cuts the pigeon’s tether and wins the single-edged axes.

BOOK VIII.

Teucer kills several Trojans with his bow while taking cover behind Ajax son of Telamon’s shield. He twice misses Hector, kills Gorgythion and Archeptolemus instead, and is then struck by Hector’s stone, which breaks the sinew of his arm and makes him drop his bow.

BOOK XII.

Teucer accompanies Ajax son of Telamon to reinforce Menestheus and the threatened section of the wall. He wounds Glaucus in the exposed shoulder with an arrow, forcing Glaucus to withdraw.

Ajax and Teucer then both of them attacked him. Teucer hit him

Teucer attacks Hector and strikes him with an arrow on the band bearing the shield that covers his body.

Now Patroclus, so long as the Achaeans and Trojans were fighting

Teucer uses his bow to kill Cleitus and then aims at Hector, but Jove breaks the bowstring and prevents the shot. He puts aside the bow, takes up a spear and shield, and joins Ajax in the fighting.

Thereon the son of Peleus, when he had listened to all the thanks

Teucer competes against Meriones in the archery contest and shoots first. Although he misses the pigeon, he cuts the string holding it, so he receives the single-edged axes.

Teucer in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · 449 to 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Teucer first appears

Abstract written by a model

Teucer is Telamon’s son, Aias’s brother and defender, an Achaean warrior and skilled archer. While absent in pursuit of enemies, he comes from the Mysian heights with Calchas’s warning that Aias must not go abroad, but arrives after Aias’s death. He mourns, fears Telamon’s anger and exile, and makes Aias’s burial his chief concern. Though Menelaus and Agamemnon forbid burial and insult him over his mother’s captive status, Teucer defends Aias’s worth, recalls his rescue of the army and combat with Hector, and vows resistance. He summons Eurysakes, protects him as guardian, and organizes the cleansing, preparation, and burial of Aias’s corpse.

Of Aias, and his fortunes as they are. [AIAS _is discovered_

Teucer arrives from the Mysian heights and is attacked by Argives because he is Aias's brother. He has ordered that Aias be kept indoors and is coming to explain Calchas's warning that Aias will die if he roams abroad.

_The scene changes to a lonely wooded spot._

Teucer arrives after learning of Aias's death, mourns his brother, and fears Telamon's anger and exile because he has returned without Aias. He argues with Menelaus, rejects the order to leave Aias unburied, summons Eurysakes, and prepares to defend and bury Aias despite opposition.

_Enter_ TEUCER.

Teucer defends Aias against Agamemnon’s insults and declares that he will bury his brother despite the kings’ opposition. He recalls Aias’s rescue of the army and his single combat with Hector, rejects Agamemnon’s attack on his birth, and threatens resistance if Aias is harmed. Teucer later begins organizing the cleansing, preparation, and burial of Aias’s corpse.

Abstract written by a model

Teucer, son of Telamon and a Greek warrior from Salamis, fights in the Trojan War. After Troy’s fall and Ajax’s death, he reaches Egypt seeking Theonoe’s prophecy about a voyage to Cyprus, encounters Helen, and is destined to settle in Salamis there.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

HELEN.

Teucer, son of Telamon and a Greek from Salamis, arrives in Egypt while seeking Theonoe’s prophecy about his voyage to Cyprus. He reports that Ajax died by his own sword, Troy was destroyed, and Menelaus and his consort are presumed dead. He mistakes Helen for the woman whose reputation ruined the Greeks, then leaves after she warns him to escape Theoclymenus.

Teucer in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · about AD 200 to 299 · the work’s page · read it where Teucer first appears

Abstract written by a model

Teucer, son of Telamon, is a Danaan and Argive warrior, helper of Atreus’ sons, and a skilled archer. In a footrace he injures his ankle on a tamarisk root and loses to Aias of the Locrians; despite the pain, he excels in the archery-test, shearing a plume from a helm and receiving Troilus’ arms. In battle he calls the Argives to rescue fallen Machaon. After his brother Aias dies, Teucer mourns, embraces him, considers suicide, and remains alive as Agamemnon promises protection to Tecmessa and her son.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK IV

Teucer competes in the footrace against Aias of the Locrians. Near the finish, an unearthly power causes his foot to strike a tamarisk root, wrenching his ankle and allowing Aias of the Locrians to win.

Then for the archery-test Oileus' son

Teucer competes with Aias in the archery-test and shears the plume from the helm with his arrow. The people praise his accuracy despite the pain that makes his foot halt, and he receives the arms of Troilus from Thetis.

Then Aias saw those sheep upon the earth

Teucer embraces Aias after his death and considers killing himself, but the others restrain him. He mourns Aias as his mighty-souled brother and says that he has no desire to return home without him. Agamemnon invokes Teucer's continued life when promising protection to Tecmessa and her son.
