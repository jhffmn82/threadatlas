  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Patroclus carries Lycaon away to Lemnos and sells him as a slave.
  • In The Iliad, Patroclus is Achilles’ dearest companion, a Myrmidon warrior and squire who leads the Myrmidons, fights the Trojans, and is killed by Hector.
  • In The Library, Patroclus is Menoetius’s son, Achilles’s companion and beloved, a Myrmidon and suitor of Helen who is killed by Hector and honored with funeral games.
  • In Description of Greece, Patroclus is identified as the person who killed Las and is said to have wooed Helen rather than Achilles.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Patroclus is Achilles’s companion and dearest friend who leads the Myrmidons in Achilles’s armor, kills Sarpedon, and is mortally wounded by Hector.

Patroclus in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Patroclus is a son of Menoetius, an Achaean and Myrmidon warrior, and Achilles’ dearest, trusted companion, attendant, and squire. Though older and less nobly born than Achilles, he serves him closely: he helps prepare a feast, obeys him, and is guided by his father’s charge. His compassion is central as well as his courage. Sent to learn of the wounded, he carries Eurypylus into a tent, removes and dresses his wound, and cares for him until the worsening battle compels him to seek Achilles. Moved by the Achaeans’ suffering, Patroclus weeps and asks to lead the Myrmidons while wearing Achilles’ armour. He drives back the Trojans, helps save the ships, commands the Danaans, and kills many fighters, including Pyraechmes, Pronous, Thestor, Erylaus, and Sarpedon in single combat. Pursuing the retreating Trojans and Lycians, he tries to reach Hector, attacks Troy’s wall, kills Cebriones, and fights over his body. His success ends when Phoebus Apollo overpowers and strikes him, Euphorbus wounds him from behind, and Hector gives the fatal blow. Dying, Patroclus foretells Hector’s death by Achilles. Afterward, his body and Achilles’ armour become the focus of fierce fighting. His death causes profound grief, above all for Achilles, whose vengeance against Hector is driven by the loss of his comrade. Patroclus is mourned by the Myrmidons and Achaeans; his body is preserved, burned on a richly supplied pyre, and his bones are placed in a golden urn beneath a burial mound. Funeral contests honor him. Even after Hector’s body is returned to his father, Achilles speaks to Patroclus and continues to share his grief and honor with him. Patroclus is ultimately buried with Achilles in a single urn.

Patroclus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Patroclus is a person, chiefly described as Menoetius’s son, Achilles’s companion and beloved, a Myrmidon, and a suitor of Helen; another record lists him as Hercules’s son by Pyrippe. After killing Cleitonymus in a knucklebones quarrel, he flees with his father to Peleus. At Troy, sent in Achilles’ armor to defend burning ships, he kills Sarpedon and others, is wounded by Euphorbus, killed by Hector, and honored with funeral games.

Patroclus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Patroclus is Achilles’s companion, dearest friend, and guide, charged by their fathers. As the Greeks’ position worsens, he persuades Achilles to let him lead the Myrmidons in Achilles’s armor, repels the Trojans, and kills Sarpedon. Apollo disarms him and Hector mortally wounds him; his death brings Achilles grief and revenge, followed by burial and funeral games.
