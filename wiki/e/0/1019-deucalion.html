Deucalion

person · 5 works · 38 quoted facts · 19 connections
Also called: he, son of Prometheus, the man, Δευκαλίων, Δευκαλίωνος
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Deucalion is the king and father of Hellen, Pandora, and Thyia, and ancestor through his daughters of Graecus, Magnes, and Macedon.
  • In The Library, Deucalion reigns at Lycorea, flees to Cranaus for safety, sacrifices to Zeus, and witnesses the flood during Cranaus's reign.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Deucalion is the just king of Thessaly who survives the Deluge with Pyrrha and helps renew the human race.
  • In The Library, Deucalion is the son of Prometheus who rules near Phthia, marries Pyrrha, survives the Flood in a chest, and creates men from stones.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Deucalion is the just man who survives the flood with Pyrrha, interprets an oracle, and renews mankind by casting stones behind him.

Deucalion in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Deucalion is a man, son of Prometheus, king of Thessaly, and husband of Pyrrha. Amid universal corruption, he is credited as at least one just man: the most upright among men and their greatest lover of justice. Saved with Pyrrha for virtuous conduct, he survives the Deluge, described as the only man surviving it, reaching Mount Parnassus in a little ship; records also say he flees there with some subjects. When the waters recede, he mourns the empty world and, with Pyrrha, seeks guidance to repair the loss of their race. He interprets Themis's response as an instruction to throw stones behind them, helping renew the human race. Afterward he raises an altar to Jupiter the Liberator, goes to Athens, builds a temple to Jupiter, and institutes sacrifices in Jupiter's honor. He lives before worship of Apollo at Delphi, becomes ancestor of Athamas, and after death receives a temple and worship as a divinity.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

front matter

Deucalion survives the Deluge with Pyrrha. After the waters abate, he helps renew the human race by throwing stones behind him.

notes .. notes

Deucalion is credited by tradition with being at least one just man amid the universal corruption. The explanation says that he possesses the merit attributed to Noah in Scripture.

notes .. notes

Deucalion is presented as the survivor of a great deluge, together with Pyrrha, because of his virtuous conduct. After being saved, he raised an altar to Jupiter the Liberator, and his story is compared with that of Noah.

opening

Deucalion is the only man saved from the deluge and reaches Parnassus in a little ship with Pyrrha. He is described as the most upright and greatest lover of justice, and he worships the Corycian Nymphs, the mountain deities, and Themis. After the waters recede, he mourns the empty world and tells Pyrrha that they alone remain.

opening

Deucalion prays with Pyrrha for guidance on repairing the loss of their race. He interprets Themis's obscure response to mean that they should throw stones, which he and Pyrrha then do.

notes .. notes

Deucalion was king of Thessaly and fled with some of his subjects to Mount Parnassus during the inundation. He later went to Athens, built a temple to Jupiter, and instituted sacrifices in his honor. He was the son of Prometheus and was worshipped as a Divinity after his death.

Deucalion in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Deucalion, a just man of the race of Prometheus, survives the flood with Pyrrha on Parnassus, seeks divine guidance, interprets an oracle, and casts earth’s stones behind him to renew mankind.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II

Deucalion, a just man of the race of Prometheus, survived the flood with Pyrrha on Parnassus. He sought guidance from the gods, interpreted the oracle's reference to the bones of their mother as stones from the earth, and cast them behind him to renew mankind.
