Cupid's triumph in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

Cupid's triumph is an imagined splendid procession obtained with great glory from men free from Love and damsels. Cupid rides with myrtle, doves, attendants, captives, and the recently captured narrator, wounded and chained. Soundness of Understanding and Shame, enemies of Love, are bound and displayed as the crowd acclaims Cupid.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ELEGY II.

Cupid's triumph is imagined as a splendid procession with myrtle, his mother's doves, a chariot, captive youths and fair women, and the narrator bearing his recent wound and chains. Soundness of understanding, shame, and other enemies of Love are led bound, while the crowd and Cupid's attendants acclaim him.

Cupid's triumph in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Cupid's triumph is imagined as a sacred public victory procession over lovers: Cupid rides a golden dove-drawn chariot while captives, attendants, roses, and jewels follow, ending with the speaker's submission as newly captured prey.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

P. OVIDI NASONIS AMORES / LIBER PRIMUS

Cupid's triumph is imagined as a public victory procession in which Modesty and other obstacles to Love are led captive. Cupid rides in a golden chariot drawn by his mother's doves, while roses, jewels, attendants, captive youths, captive girls, and the speaker himself form part of the spectacle.
