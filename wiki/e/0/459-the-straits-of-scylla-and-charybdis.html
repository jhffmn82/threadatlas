the Straits of Scylla and Charybdis

place · 3 works · 4 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Sicilian Strait, the Strait, the Straits, the Straits in great fear of mind, the passage, the straits, the straits of Messina, this strait

Abstract written by a model

In The Odyssey, the Straits contain Scylla and Charybdis; Ulysses's ship passes through in terror, avoids the whirlpool, and loses six men to Scylla. In The Aeneid, the straits separate Italy from Sicily; Helenus warns Aeneas about Scylla and Charybdis there and advises him to keep out to sea rather than approach Scylla. In Description of Greece, the Sicilian Strait is where the Sicilian and Tyrrhenian seas meet, creating strong ebb and flow and tremendous swells; it is described as stormy, full of sea-monsters, and dangerous for shipwrecked mariners.
