human mortality

topic · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 0 connections
Also called: Death is a debt all mortal men must pay, Life is not life, but just unhappiness, death and mortality, life will last him yet a single day, men are vanished away, mortal souls, mortality, race of man is as nought, the things that are in our path
  • In Alcestis, death and mortality is presented as an unavoidable human condition that every mortal must face, shaping Heracles's warning about death and the conflicting attitudes of Admetus and Pheres toward life.
  • In The Extant Odes of Pindar, human mortality is presented as an unavoidable condition of mortals, marked by illness, death, and changing fortunes, teaching mortals to recognize the limits of their allotted path rather than seek what belongs to the gods.

death and mortality in Alcestis

Euripides · -0437

is_a unavoidable human condition [every mortal must face it] Heracles tells the Servant that every mortal must pay the debt of death and cannot know whether life will last another day. The quarrel between Admetus and Pheres shows their conflicting attitudes toward mortality: Admetus condemns Pheres for clinging to life, while Pheres insists that his own life is as precious to him as Admetus's is to Admetus.
