OpenQA in Generation-Augmented Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering

Yuning Mao, Pengcheng He, Xiaodong Liu, Yelong Shen, Jianfeng Gao, Jiawei Han, Weizhu Chen · 2021 · the work’s page · read it where OpenQA first appears

Abstract written by a model

OpenQA is an open-domain factoid question-answering task that answers questions without a pre-specified domain, commonly using Wikipedia as a large document collection. A typical approach uses a retriever-reader architecture: retrieve relevant documents or passages, then extract or generate an answer. It serves as the evaluation setting for retrieval and reading methods on Natural Questions and TriviaQA. The records present Generation-Augmented Retrieval as an efficient OpenQA approach that uses query-context generation, relevant-passage retrieval, and passage reading; it improves sparse retrieval, reports strong retrieval and end-to-end results, and can be competitive with dense and generative approaches. They also note limits of some reformulation resources and naive pseudo-relevance feedback, and suggest applicability beyond OpenQA.

Abstract written by a model

Open-domain QA is a knowledge-intensive NLP task used to evaluate retrieval-augmented generation and large-scale pre-trained language models. It progresses from Wikipedia-based text methods to retrieval of text, tables, and knowledge-graph triples; RAG methods significantly outperform large-scale PLMs on three tasks.

Abstract written by a model

ODQA is an open-domain question answering task that aims to answer questions using knowledge from an external corpus, such as Wikipedia. It is commonly handled by two-stage retrieval-augmented systems: a retriever obtains relevant information, and a reader model interprets retrieved passages to extract or generate answers. Work on ODQA emphasizes improving retrievers, readers, and their joint optimization, including the reader’s processing of retrieved information. Systems are evaluated on five datasets spanning single-hop and multi-hop settings, using Exact Match with greedy decoding. Fusion-in-Decoder and REANO are designed to address this task.
