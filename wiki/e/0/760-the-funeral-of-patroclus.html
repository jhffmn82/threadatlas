Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, the funeral of Patroclus proceeds through communal mourning, a feast, hair-cutting, construction and burning of a pyre with extensive sacrifices and offerings, placement of his bones in a golden urn, raising of a burial mound, and funeral games with prizes. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the funeral of Patroclus involves burning his body on a pile with due solemnity, contests in chariot racing, wrestling, boxing, and archery, and a funeral banquet.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK XXIII.

The funeral includes communal mourning, a great feast, the cutting of hair, the building and burning of a pyre, and offerings of animals, horses, dogs, weapons, honey, and unguents. After the cremation, Patroclus's bones are placed in a golden urn and a burial mound is raised.
