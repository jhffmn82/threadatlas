the funeral of Patroclus in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Patroclus’s funeral proceeds through communal mourning, a feast, hair-cutting, construction and burning of a pyre with extensive sacrifices and offerings, then placement of his bones in a golden urn, raising of a burial mound, and funeral games with prizes.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK XXIII.

The funeral includes communal mourning, a great feast, the cutting of hair, the building and burning of a pyre, and offerings of animals, horses, dogs, weapons, honey, and unguents. After the cremation, Patroclus's bones are placed in a golden urn and a burial mound is raised.
