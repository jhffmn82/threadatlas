marriage to Penelope in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Marriage to Penelope is a contested prospect shaped by the suitors, Telemachus, and Penelope’s delay. The suitors demand that she choose, while Penelope postpones remarriage by promising to finish Laertes’s burial pall. Telemachus refuses to send her to Icarius but says she may marry again if Ulysses is known dead. Antinous proposes separate offers, with the greatest offer winning her. Penelope ultimately declares that the man who strings Ulysses’s bow and shoots through twelve axes will marry her and receive her departure from her lawful husband’s house.

BOOK II

Penelope's suitors demand that she choose one of them, but she delays them through her promise to finish Laertes's burial pall. Telemachus refuses to send her to Icarius, while stating that he will allow her to marry again if he learns that Ulysses is dead.

Antinous proposes that, if Telemachus is killed, Penelope and the man who marries her should receive the house. He also suggests that, if Telemachus is allowed to live, the suitors should make separate offers to Penelope and let her marry the man who offers the most.

Penelope says that Odysseus told her to marry again when their son grew a beard if Odysseus did not return. She says that this prediction is coming true and that she will eventually have to submit to a marriage she detests because Zeus has taken away her hope of happiness.

BOOK XXI

Penelope offers marriage to the suitor who can most easily string Ulysses’s bow and shoot through the twelve axes. She says that she will leave the house of her lawful husband for the winner.
