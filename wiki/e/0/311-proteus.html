Proteus in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Proteus is an old immortal Egyptian of the sea who serves Neptune, knows its depths and distant events, and changes into many forms. Menelaus and three companions seize him among his seals and withstand his transformations until he resumes his true form, then he reveals how Menelaus may finish his voyage and the Achaeans’ fates.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

When the child of morning, rosy-fingered Dawn appeared, Menelaus rose

Proteus is an old immortal Egyptian who lives under the sea and knows the sea and the events taking place in distant lands. Menelaus and three companions seize him while he sleeps among his seals, despite his transformations into various creatures, fire, water, and a tree. After being forced to resume his true form, Proteus tells Menelaus how to complete his voyage and reports the fates of the Achaeans.

Proteus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Proteus is a shape-shifting sea deity and Carpathian prophet, inhabiting the sea surrounding the earth. Seen in many forms, he foretells that Thetis’s son will surpass his father and advises Peleus to bind the transforming Thetis until she resumes her former shape.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

FABLE V. [VIII.547-610]

Proteus is described as an inhabitant of the sea that surrounds the earth. Acheloüs says that people have seen him take many different forms.

FABLES V. AND VI. [XI.221-409]

Proteus foretells that Thetis will bear a son who surpasses his father in gallant deeds and is greater than him. He advises Peleus to bind Thetis while she sleeps and hold her through her transformations until she resumes her former shape.

Proteus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Proteus is an old prophet favored by Neptune, tending sea-calves and knowing past, present, and future. Seized by Aristaeus, he shifts into fire, flood, and beasts before resuming his form, explaining the bees’ destruction and prescribing sacrifices, altars, and honors for Orpheus and Eurydice.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XVII

Proteus was an old prophet favored by Neptune who knew all things past, present, and future and tended Neptune's sea-calves. When Aristaeus seized him, he changed into fire, a flood, and various beasts, but eventually resumed his own form and explained that the nymphs had destroyed the bees to avenge Eurydice. He instructed Aristaeus to sacrifice four bulls and four cows, build four altars, and honor Orpheus and Eurydice.
