New York in Gallegher and Other Stories

Richard Harding Davis · 1890 · the work’s page · read it where New York first appears

Abstract written by a model

New York is a crowded, harsh place linked by trains and marked by the Bowery, Broadway, Thirty-fifth Street, pawnshops, loan offices, drug-store and cigar-store post-offices, vice, crime, poverty, and green-goods operations. Its scattered private post-offices are said to make vice and crime easy. It is repeatedly a destination and setting for danger: Richard F. Burrbank is murdered there, Hade is to be taken there after a planned arrest, and secrecy depends on newspapers there learning nothing. Snipes knows the city but refuses to exchange it for the country. After Mrs. Catherwaight's death, Miss Catherwaight's father and daughter live and entertain there while she searches for medals. At dawn, Van Bibber returns from Jersey City through nearly deserted streets; a reporter calls the city exciting yet brutal and commonplace, shaped by recurring crime and poverty. Van Bibber then helps a burglar leave by train.

THE TRAILER FOR ROOM NO.

New York is portrayed as a crowded, harsh city associated with crime, poverty, and the green-goods operation. Snipes knows the city well but rejects the farmer's offer to leave it for the country.

THE CYNICAL MISS CATHERWAIGHT Miss Catherwaight's collection of orders and decorations and medals was her chief offence in the eyes of those of her dear friends who thought her clever but cynical.

New York is where Miss Catherwaight's father and daughter later live together and entertain at dinners after Mrs. Catherwaight's death. The city contains the pawnshops and loan offices where Miss Catherwaight searches for medals, as well as Lewis Lockwood's law office on Broadway and the former Lockwood residence on Thirty-fifth Street.
