the Ithacans in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

The Ithacans assemble at Telemachus’s call, hear his complaint about the suitors, pity him, but disperse without acting. After the suitors’ deaths, they bury them and divide over vengeance: some heed Halitherses, while others follow Eupeithes into civil conflict until Minerva stops them.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK II

The people of Ithaca gather when Telemachus calls the assembly and listen to his complaint against the suitors. They feel pity when he weeps but remain inactive, and they later disperse without stopping the suitors.

Thus did they converse in the house of Hades deep down within the

The Ithacans gather after hearing of the suitors' deaths, bury the dead, and debate whether to attack Ulysses. Some accept Halitherses's warning and leave the assembly, while others follow Eupeithes into battle before Minerva forces them to stop.
