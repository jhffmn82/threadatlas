Also called: Deiphobus son of Priam, Priam's son, Priam's strong son, Priam’s son, Teucer’s race, that hero war-renowned, that slain man, the Trojan chief, thee, Δηίφοβον, Δηίφοβος, Δηιφόβου

Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, Deiphobus is a Trojan warrior and leader who seeks to avenge Asius, mistakenly kills Hypsenor, calls Aeneas, wounds Ascalaphus, is wounded and carried away by Polites, and later remains inside the wall while Minerva impersonates him before Hector. In The Aeneid, Deiphobus is a son of Priam and Trojan warrior who is betrayed and murdered by Helen during Troy’s fall, then tells Aeneas how Helen enabled the Greeks’ entry before withdrawing into shadows for penance. In The Library, Deiphobus quarrelled with Helenus over marriage to Helen and was preferred over him. In The Fall of Troy, Deiphobus is a battle-staunch Trojan warrior who courageously defends Troy, rallies Trojans, kills and drives back Achaeans, faces Neoptolemus, and is carried into Troy by Apollo after rescue from a spear.

Deiphobus in The Iliad

Homer · 749 to 724 BC · the work’s page · read it where Deiphobus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Deiphobus, son of Priam and Hecuba, is a Trojan warrior and leader, brother of Polites. He seeks to avenge Asius, mistakenly kills Hypsenor, calls Aeneas, wounds Ascalaphus, and is wounded and carried away by Polites. Later he remains inside the wall while Minerva impersonates him before Hector.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

And now Idomeneus, though his hair was already flecked with grey,

Deiphobus comes to avenge Asius, but his spear misses Idomeneus and kills Hypsenor instead. He summons Aeneas to help him, wounds Ascalaphus while retreating, and is then wounded in the arm by Meriones and carried from the battle by Polites.

BOOK XXII.

Hector believes Deiphobus has come outside the wall to help him confront Achilles. In reality, Minerva has taken Deiphobus's form, while the actual Deiphobus remains inside the wall.

Deiphobus in The Aeneid

Virgil · 28 to 18 BC · the work’s page · read it where Deiphobus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Deiphobus, a son of Priam and Trojan warrior, is betrayed and murdered by Helen during Troy’s fall, leaving his face and limbs mutilated. He later tells Aeneas how Helen enabled the Greeks’ entry, then withdraws into shadows for penance.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Ye realms, yet unreveal’d to human sight,

Deiphobus appears with his face and limbs mutilated after being betrayed and murdered by his wife and the Greeks. He tells Aeneas how Helen enabled the Greeks to enter Troy and then withdraws into the secret shadows to endure his penance.
