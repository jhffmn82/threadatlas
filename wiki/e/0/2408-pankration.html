Also called: PANKRATION, Pankration, pankratiast victory, pankratiast's, trial of strength

Abstract written by a model

Pankration is a combat sport, game, and forceful contest combining wrestling and boxing or striking, apparently with wide license of rules. Its central matter is stubborn prowess in a struggle of hands that can bring opponents low and involve wearying blows. Competitors enter it at places including Nemea, Isthmos, and Epidauros; they contend through wrestling and striking; and a winner gains a victory or prize. Timodemos, Aristokleides, Pytheas, Themistios, Melissos, Phylakidas, and Kleandros are recorded as successful competitors. Victories may be praised in song, credited to guidance, counted for a family, or honored with a wreath of tender myrtle.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Nemean Ode II

The pankration is the contest in which Timodemos won at Nemea. His success is attributed to his stubborn prowess in the event.

Nemean Ode III

The pankration is the forceful contest in which Aristokleides competed. He did not disgrace the ancient meeting-place of the Myrmidons and won a victory described as a remedy for wearying blows.

Nemean Ode V

Pankration is the contest Pytheas won at Nemea. Themistios also won a double prize in boxing and pankration at Epidauros.

Isthmian Ode III

The pankration is the contest in which Melissos wins his Isthmian victory. His success in it becomes the subject of a song comparing his methods with those of Herakles against Antaios.

Isthmian Ode IV

The pankration is the contest in which Phylakidas won. Pytheas is praised for guiding Phylakidas in this struggle of hands that brings opponents low.

Isthmian Ode V

Phylakidas won the pankration at the Isthmian Games. The family is also said to have gained three victories in the pankration at Isthmos, along with victories at Nemea.

Isthmian Ode VII

The pankration is the contest in which Kleandros won his victory. Kleandros’ pankratiast victory is honored with a proposed wreath of tender myrtle.
