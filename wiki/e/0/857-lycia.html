Lycia

place · 3 works · 5 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Lycian, fertile Lycia, land, native land of Sarpedon, the broad lands of Lycia, the country of Lycia, the rich land of Lycia
  • In The Iliad, Lycia is Sarpedon’s rich homeland, where Jove orders Apollo, Death, and Sleep to take him after his death, and where Sarpedon’s brothers and kinsmen are expected to bury him with a mound and pillar.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Lycia is a fertile province in Asia Minor on the Mediterranean Sea, marked by heat, the Chimæra, Latona’s insult, and a miraculous lake.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Lycia contained the pond where Latona was refused water and where the rustics were transformed into frogs; the account was witnessed by a traveler who had gone there to drive oxen.
