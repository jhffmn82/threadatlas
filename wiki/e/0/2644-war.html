warfare in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

Warfare begins as the poet’s intended serious epic subject, including battles of the heavens, but Cupid and Love redirect him to love poetry. It remains important as the central comparison for lovers’ endurance, hardship, vigilance, sieges, and attacks.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

ELEGY I.

Warfare is the poet’s original subject, intended for serious verse. The poet bids farewell to warfare after Cupid turns him toward love.

ELEGY IX.

Warfare is used as the central comparison for love: lovers and soldiers both endure sleeplessness, hardship, marches, watchfulness, sieges, and attacks.

BOOK THE SECOND ELEGY I.

The wars, specifically the battles of the heavens, were the subject Naso had begun to sing about before Love redirected him to love poetry.
