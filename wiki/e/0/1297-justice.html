Justice in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

Justice is a maiden-child and daughter of Zeus, queen of suppliants, and a personified force of right dealing. The Danaids invoke her as Pelasgus weighs justice against his city's safety. Later, she is depicted on Polynices's shield promising restoration, but Eteocles rejects her support for his impious attack.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Enter the KING OF ARGOS.

Justice is called the daughter of right-dealing Zeus and the queen of suppliants. The Danaids ask Pelasgus to stand beside Justice, while Pelasgus seeks counsel that will preserve justice without destroying the city.

Enter on one side THE SPY; on the other ETEOCLES and the SIX

Justice appears as a woman on Polynices's shield, leading a golden warrior and promising to restore him to his city and ancestral hall. Eteocles denies that Justice supports Polynices's impious attack on his home and says she would be misnamed if she aided it.

Justice in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Justice, Zeus’s honored virgin daughter, reports human wrongdoing, leaves cities that reject her, prospers those with straight judgements, protects cities, and eventually defeats Outrage.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

HESIOD / HESIOD’S WORKS AND DAYS

Justice is the daughter of Zeus and is honored among the gods. She reports human wrongdoing to Zeus, leaves cities that reject her, and brings prosperity to those who give straight judgements.

Astræa in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Astræa is a virgin heavenly deity and Goddess of Justice, variously said to be daughter of Astræus and Aurora or of Jupiter and Themis. Last of the heavenly deities to abandon Earth, she leaves only when it is drenched in slaughter, then takes her place among the stars as the Constellation of the Virgin.
