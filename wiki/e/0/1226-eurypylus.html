Eurypylus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Eurypylus denotes several figures: son of Evaemon, who sought Helen, led the Ormenians against Troy, and was wounded; son of Hercules by Eubote; son of Poseidon, king of Cos, slain by Hercules; son of Telephus, who commanded Mysians for the Trojans and was slain by Neoptolemus; and son of Temenus, who hired murderers against his father.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

E 8 |’ ‘Hoy δὲ ὄντος τοῦ πολέμου δεκαετοῦς ἀθυμοῦσι

Eurypylus, son of Telephus, arrived to aid the Trojans with a large force of Mysians. After performing great deeds, he was killed by Neoptolemus.

Erysichthon, son of Cecrops by

Several figures named Eurypylus are recorded. Eurypylus son of Evaemon sues for Helen, leads the Ormenians against Troy, and is wounded there; Eurypylus son of Hercules by Eubote is also listed, as is Eurypylus son of Poseidon, king of Cos, who is slain by Hercules. Eurypylus son of Telephus fights for the Trojans and is slain by Neoptolemus, while Eurypylus son of Temenus hires men to murder his father.

Eurypylus in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

Eurypylus is the son of Telephus, grandson of Hercules, king of the Ceteians, and a giant, godlike warrior of great strength and dauntless spirit. He arrives at Troy from the region of the Caicus with a great host, is welcomed by Paris and Hector, and swears to defend Troy until victory or death. Honored by the Trojans as highly as Priam's sons, he leads Trojan and Ceteian assaults, rallies their forces, and causes panic among the Danaans. He kills Nireus, Machaon, Peneleos, Bucolion, Nesus, Chromion, Antiphus, and many other Achaeans, driving the Argives behind the ships' rampart and attacking their wall with a huge stone. After Machaon foretells his death, Eurypylus continues fighting fiercely, but Neoptolemus arrives, halts his advance, and finally kills him with the Pelian lance after Eurypylus challenges him.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

The Fall of Troy

Eurypylus is identified in the contents as Hercules' grandson who came to help Troy. Book VIII concerns his death in battle against the son of Achilles.

BOOK VI

Eurypylus, the son of Telephus and grandson of Hercules, arrives at Troy with a great host from the region of the Caicus. He is welcomed by Paris and Hector, promises to defend Troy, and leads the Trojan forces in battle. He kills Nireus and Machaon, though Machaon foretells that Eurypylus will soon die on the plain of Troy.

By this to Podaleirius tidings came

Eurypylus turns from chasing the Danaans and attacks the Atreus' sons and Oileus' seed. He rallies the Trojans, drives the Danaans into panic, and kills Bucolion, Nesus, Chromion, Antiphus, and many unnamed men before withdrawing with the Trojans.

BOOK VII

Eurypylus fights with immense strength and kills many Argives, including Peneleos. He drives the Argives behind the rampart of the ships and continues to press the war after the agreed burial truce.

So came they to the heavy-plunging sea,

Eurypylus leads the Trojan and Ceteian assault against the Achaean wall and urges his men to continue fighting. He throws a huge stone against the wall and taunts the Achaeans, but Neoptolemus's arrival prevents his victory and forces his host to withdraw somewhat.

BOOK VIII

Eurypylus, the son of Telephus, fights fiercely for the Trojans and kills many Achaeans. He challenges Neoptolemus, but Neoptolemus withstands his attack and kills him with the Pelian lance.
