Love in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

Love is a sweet evil, tender yet cruel, and a species of warfare belonging to a discerning mind rather than slothfulness. It demands endurance, vigilance, and service: it commands the speaker into its camp, keeps him active by night, disturbs his slumbers, leaves his bones bare, and repeatedly carries him back to familiar weapons when passion fades. It also directs poetry, requiring Naso to compose this work and overcoming the speaker-poet whenever he attempts serious or tragic subjects, smiling at his tragic pall and coloured buskins. Though found in Peace and able to evade Argus, Love remains a heat difficult to escape. It is initially resisted, subdued, and trodden under foot, yet continues to torment and overpower the Poet. In the final struggle, Love contends with Hatred for the speaker's heart, prevails, and compels him to keep loving.

ELEGY IX.

Love is presented as a species of warfare that demands endurance, vigilance, and discernment rather than mere slothfulness. Love commands the speaker to serve in its camp and makes him active by night.

BOOK THE SECOND ELEGY I.

Love demands that Naso compose this work rather than continue with his earlier subject of heavenly battles.

ELEGY IX.

Love has left the speaker’s bones quite bare and causes him to be bewildered when passion fades from his mind. The speaker describes Love as an uncertain force that repeatedly carries him away and makes him resume his familiar weapons.

ELEGY X.

Love continually disturbs the speaker's sleep and causes him to endure passionate struggles. The speaker prefers suffering from Love to living a serious or solitary life and wishes to die while serving Love.

ELEGY XVIII.

Love attacks the speaker-poet whenever he attempts a serious subject and triumphs over him as a tragic poet. Love also appears amid warfare, and the speaker-poet encourages Macer to sing of it.

---- BOOK THE THIRD. / ELEGY I.

Love is presented as the subject of Elegy's playful poetry, with Cupid described as Elegy's care and companion. The Poet is allowed to sketch tender Love briefly before turning to a more weighty undertaking.

ELEGY V.

The heat beneath the trees is difficult for the narrator to escape. The interpreter explains that it represents the heat of Love.

ELEGY XI.

Love is described as having been resisted, subdued, and trodden under foot, but it continues to overpower the speaker. It struggles against Hatred and ultimately appears to prevail, forcing the speaker to keep loving.
