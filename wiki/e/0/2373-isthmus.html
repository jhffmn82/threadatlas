Isthmus

place · 4 works · 8 quoted facts · 9 connections
Also called: Bridge, Corinthian Isthmus, Corinthian isthmus, Dorian Isthmus, Isthmian, Isthmos, Isthmus (of Corinth), Isthmus of Corinth, famous Dorian Isthmus, the, the Corinthian isthmus, the Isthmus, the Isthmus of Corinth

Abstract written by a model

In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Isthmos is a sea-prisoning, sea-bridging place before the valley of Corinth, sacredly praised with Poseidon, whose games and contests grant crowns and victories, including chariot-racing and pankration prizes. In The Library, the Isthmus of Corinth is traversed by the Cretan bull; Sinis forced passers-by to bend pine trees there until Theseus killed him and cleared it of malefactors, and the Argo is dedicated to Poseidon there, which is also linked to an oracle and chariot race. In Description of Greece, Isthmus extends from the sea near Cenchreae to the sea near Lechaeum, making the Peloponnese a peninsula; failed canal-cutting attempts left visible excavation remains. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Corinthian isthmus hosted chariot races and musical competitions that attracted people of Grecian lineage, and Ibycus was traveling there when he was murdered.

Isthmos in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · 497 BC · the work’s page · read it where Isthmos first appears

Abstract written by a model

Isthmos is a sea-prisoning, sea-bridging place before the valley of Corinth, sacredly praised with Poseidon. Its games and contests are its chief importance: they grant crowns and victories to the people of Kadmos, Ainesidamos, Herodotos, Melissos, Strepsiades, and Ergoteles, including chariot-racing and pankration prizes.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

XII.

Isthmos was the site of a victory by Ergoteles.

Isthmian Ode I

Isthmus is praised as the place that has given the people of Kadmos six crowns in its games. Pindar invokes Poseidon and holy Isthmus while celebrating Herodotos’ victory.

Isthmian Ode III

The Isthmus is described as the bridge between the seas before the valley of Corinth. Its games provide Melissos with one of the two crowns celebrated in the ode.

Isthmian Ode VI

At Isthmos, Strepsiades carries away the pankratiast’s prize.

The Argo is dedicated to Poseidon at the Isthmus of Corinth. The Cretan bull traverses it, Theseus clears it of malefactors, and Sinis is associated with it. It is also the subject of an oracle and is the goal of the chariot race for the suitors of Hippodamia.
