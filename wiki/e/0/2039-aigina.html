Aigina in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · 497 BC · the work’s page · read it where Aigina first appears

Abstract written by a model

Aigina is a beloved island and city, praised as a commonwealth of equal laws and a place of long-honored valour. Through union with Zeus she is mother of Aiakos, and his sons form her heroic lineage and subjects for song. Her seamen helped deliver Salamis. The records move from her divine and familial association to civic praise, martial service, and poetic honor.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Nemean Ode VIII

Aigina is the beloved city and island associated with the ode. She was the mother of Aiakos through her union with Zeus, and the speaker seeks favor for her citizens.

Isthmian Ode IV

Aigina is praised as the commonwealth of equal laws and as an island whose high-adventuring valour has been honored from long ago. Its sons, especially the sons of Aiakos, provide many subjects for song, and its seamen helped deliver Salamis.

Isthmian Ode V

Aigina is honored through the song and is described as a city loved by the gods. The poet makes a libation of honey-speaking song in her honor.

Aigina in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · 497 BC · the work’s page · read it where Aigina first appears

Abstract written by a model

Aigina is a sea-defended, righteous island-commonwealth: a Dorian, stranger-thronged city and Oinopian isle, free among the free. Its central character is hospitality joined to justice. It honors Themis as judge and protector of strangers, aids strangers justly, and is called a pillar of support and a friendly field for people from every clime. It is guarded by Zeus, Aiakos, and Achilles, and its identity is bound to the Aiakids and Aiakidai: their stronghold of goodly towers, the ancient meeting-place of the Myrmidons, and a nurse of heroes. Aigina is also renowned for ships, song, athletic zeal, games, and violent fights; its mortal men are pre-eminent, and its children follow an inherited path to glory descended from Zeus. Across the odes, it is praised through Alkimedon, Aristokleides, Timasarchos, Pytheas, Alkimidas, Kleandros, and the house of Telesarchos. It sends Theoroi to the Pythian god, receives glory from victories and Theban honor, and finally is offered the choicest gift of the Graces, with its fame joined to Aiakos, Achilles, and their race.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

8 sections; each heading opens the text

VIII.

Aigina is described as a sea-defended land and a pillar of support for strangers from every region. It is praised as the Dorian homeland of Alkimedon and for honoring Themis, who presides over justice and the protection of strangers.

VIII.

Aigina is praised as a righteous island-commonwealth famed for the deeds of the sons of Aiakos. She is described as a nurse of heroes and as pre-eminent in games and violent fights, and the poet asks her to guard the city.

Nemean Ode III

Aigina is described as a Dorian isle crowded with strangers during the sacred festival of the Nemean games. It is identified as the ancient meeting-place of the Myrmidons and receives new glory through Aristokleides’ victory.

Nemean Ode IV

Aigina is called the Aiakids' stronghold of goodly towers and the common light of all, aiding the stranger with justice. Timasarchos came from Aigina, and the Thebans honored his victory for Aigina's sake.

Nemean Ode V

Aigina is Pytheas's mother-city and the place from which the ode's song is sent abroad. It is praised as a friendly field to strangers, famous for its ships, and the home of good men.

Nemean Ode VI

Aigina is the place associated with Alkimidas's victory and with the Aiakidai. The ode may have been sung there at a feast of the Bassid clan in honor of the victory.

Nemean Ode VII

Aigina is portrayed as a city of the spear-clashing sons of Aiakos that loves song and nurtures zeal for athletic competition. Pindar invokes Aigina while proclaiming the inherited path to glory of its children descended from Zeus.

Isthmian Ode VII

Aigina is the island associated with Kleandros and the house of Telesarchos. The poet offers Aigina the choicest gift of the Graces and connects its glory with Aiakos, Achilles, and the Aiakid race.
