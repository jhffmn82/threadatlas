Claus in The Life and Adventures of Santa Claus

L. Frank Baum · 1902 · the work’s page · read it where Claus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Claus is a human child, later known as Santa Claus, whose life is devoted to making children happy. Found newborn, abandoned, helpless, and crying near the Forest of Burzee, he is rescued by Necile, fed and protected by Shiegra and the forest people, and adopted as Necile's foster child. Raised among nymphs, Ryls, Knooks, Fairies, and Ak, he grows gentle, brave, innocent, and happy, learning their ways and believing the Forest is his whole world. As a strong youth, Claus helps the Forest people and journeys through the world with Ak. There he discovers that he is human and sees the suffering of humanity's children. Thoughtful after this journey, he leaves the Forest to live among people, while retaining the immortals' protection and aid. In the Laughing Valley, he builds a house from fallen trees, refuses to harm living trees, befriends children, and seeks to comfort those who are poor, sick, grieving, or helpless. During winter he makes a wooden cat, his first toy. With materials, colors, tools, and protection from his immortal friends, he becomes a skilled toy-maker, creating animals, dolls, and musical toys and giving them to children. Even attacks by the Awgwas do not turn him from this purpose. To reach more children, he builds a sledge and harness, guides reindeer, enters homes through chimneys, and delivers toys at night, returning to the Laughing Valley by daybreak. His yearly Christmas Eve journeys expand: he fills stockings, brings trees and gifts, and becomes beloved by children and honored as a Saint. In old age, Claus remains jolly and kind despite failing strength. Because of his love for little children, the immortals grant him the Mantle of Immortality, renewing his strength and giving him everlasting life. With helpers, deputies, toy-shops, reindeer, and his sledge, he continues making and distributing gifts so that children, poor or rich, may receive happiness.

2. The Child of the Forest

The human infant is found newborn, naked, helpless, and crying on the grass near the Forest of Burzee. Shiegra quiets its hunger with her milk under Ak’s command, after which the infant sleeps, smiles at Necile, reaches toward her, and is carried into the forest by her.

3. The Adoption

The infant is sleeping, weak, and helpless when Necile presents it to Ak and the nymphs. Ak allows Necile to keep it and gives it his protection.

4. Claus

Claus is raised by Necile and the forest nymphs in Necile's bower, where he grows big and sturdy. He is gentle, fearless, playful, and loved by the Ryls, Knooks, and Fairies; he learns their laws and believes the forest is the entire world.

5. The Master Woodsman

Claus grows to manhood, becoming a broad-shouldered and stalwart youth. He learns the language of the beasts, the songs of the bees, the poetry of the wood-flowers, and the history of Burzee's owls, while helping the Ryls and Knooks. Ak later asks him to accompany him on a journey through the world.

6. Claus Discovers Humanity

Claus travels with Ak through the air and visits many human settlements while remaining invisible by holding the Master's girdle. He learns that he is descended from humanity and that he was abandoned at the forest's edge before Necile rescued him. After seeing the lives of people and children, he longs to become helpful to his fellows and returns to Burzee grave and thoughtful.

7. Claus Leaves the Forest

Claus recognizes that he belongs to humanity and decides to leave the Forest to live according to mankind's lot. He chooses to devote his life to making children happy, and Ak grants him continued protection and the aid of the Forest people.

MANHOOD / 1. The Laughing Valley

Claus comes to the empty Laughing Valley, builds a house from fallen trees, and chooses not to cut living trees or destroy flowers. He receives help and supplies from the Knooks, Fairies, and Ryls of the Field Flowers, and begins a happy life devoted to doing good.

2. How Claus Made the First Toy

Claus travels through the Valley and the surrounding plain to visit villages and befriend children. He comforts poor, abused, crippled, ailing, and grieving people, but he is refused admission to the palace of the Lord of Lerd and the castle of Baron Braun. During the winter, he carves a wooden cat from a stick of wood and makes his first toy.

3. How the Ryls Colored the Toys

Claus rescues Weekum from a snowdrift, warms and feeds him, and carries him home to his mother. He makes and colors wooden cats with help from the Ryls, gives one to a sick girl, and then carves and paints a wooden lioness after Shiegra's suggestion.

4. How Little Mayrie Became Frightened

Claus welcomes Mayrie and her younger brother, feeds them, and gives the boy his last ready-made toy cat. After frightening Mayrie with Shiegra's image, he makes her a wooden cat and decides to use mild animals as models for future toys. His toy-making becomes so popular that he turns his living room into a workshop, while the Knooks, Ryls, and Fairies supply materials and tools.

5. How Bessie Blithesome Came to the Laughing Valley

Claus promises Bessie Blithesome that he will make her a toy, then consults Necile and the Queen of the Fairies about whether he should give toys to rich children. He creates several clay dolls, including a toy nymph and an image of Bessie, and gives the Bessie image dolly to Mayrie.

6. The Wickedness of the Awgwas

Claus makes toys to bring happiness to children and thereby keeps them from the Awgwas' influence. After being abducted and repeatedly attacked, he continues making toys, but eventually asks the great Ak for help when the Awgwas prevent him from delivering them.

7. The Great Battle Between Good and Evil

Claus is protected by the immortals after the Awgwas interfere with his work. While the battle is fought on his account, he remains in the Laughing Valley making toys, and afterward Ak tells him that he may carry them to children without fear.

8. The First Journey with the Reindeer

Claus carries toys to children in hamlets, farmhouses, villages, and a large city, receiving grateful welcomes. He makes a sledge and harness, then rides with Flossie and Glossie on his first long journey with the reindeer, entering houses through chimneys to leave toys for sleeping children. He returns to the Laughing Valley just before daybreak.

9. "Santa Claus!"

Santa Claus becomes famous for giving wonderful toys to children. Mothers come to regard him as a Saint who can enter homes without unlocking doors, but he does not approve of using his gifts to threaten naughty children. He gives toys because children are little and helpless and because he loves them.

10. Christmas Eve

Santa Claus consults Ak after Will Knook threatens to punish Glossie and Flossie for returning one minute after daybreak. With the recovered toys, he makes his first Christmas Eve journey, delivering presents to children and returning home before daybreak.

11. How the First Stockings Were Hung by the Chimneys

Santa Claus decides to spend the year making toys and to deliver them to children on Christmas Eve. He builds a larger sledge, obtains a harness for ten reindeer, and improves his toys with suggestions from the immortals. He fills the stockings of Margot, Dick, Ned, and Sara when he sees them hanging by the chimney.

12. The First Christmas Tree

Claus travels over the world on his sledge and returns to the Laughing Valley by daybreak. He brings children toys, fruit, candies, and decorated trees, and his kindness wins the hearts of everyone. He visits the children of the new Baron Braun after the Baron humbly asks him to do so.

OLD AGE / 1. The Mantle of Immortality

Santa Claus grows old, with white hair and beard, wrinkles, and a stout, waddling form, but he remains lively, jolly, and kind. After his strength is exhausted and he stops making toys, the immortals bestow the Mantle of Immortality upon him, making it part of his being.

2. When the World Grew Old

Santa Claus awakens with his old strength renewed and feels as vigorous and merry as a boy of sixteen, although his beard remains white and wrinkles remain around his eyes. After learning about the Mantle of Immortality, he prepares more toys than ever and resolves that no child, poor or rich, should go without a Christmas gift if he can provide one. As the number of children grows, he receives help from Kilter, Peter, Nuter, and Wisk and continues his yearly work across the world.

3. The Deputies of Santa Claus

Santa Claus struggles to deliver toys when new-fashioned chimneys lead to stoves instead of fireplaces. He brings Kilter, Wisk, Peter, and Nuter with him so they can enter houses through the walls, and later asks loving parents to help distribute presents as his deputies. He also sends large supplies of toys to toy-shops so that children who are missed, ill, or celebrating birthdays can receive gifts.

Santa Claus in The Road to Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1909 · the work’s page · read it where Santa Claus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Santa Claus is an immortal saint, a mighty and loyal friend of children, with a round body and a red ermine-trimmed cloak. He keeps reindeer at home, owns and loves the Ryls and Knooks, and visits children on Christmas Eve. At Princess Ozma's birthday celebration, he arrives with the Ryls and Knooks, brings Ozma presents, recognizes Dorothy and Button-Bright, and promises to tell Polychrome's father where she is. Because the weather is too warm for his reindeer, he rides the Saw-Horse around the Emerald City. At Ozma's banquet he is the jolliest and most important guest, sits in the seat of honor, and offers a verse toast to Ozma. In the procession he tosses toys to children, directs guests' bubble journeys, and rides home with his companions in soap bubbles.

23. The Grand Banquet

Santa Claus attends Ozma's royal banquet and is given the seat of honor because he is considered both the jolliest and the most important person present. He makes a speech in verse congratulating Ozma on her birthday and asks the guests to drink to her health and happiness.

24. The Birthday Celebration

Santa Claus rides the Saw-Horse in the birthday procession and tosses small toys to children. He helps direct the guests' bubble journeys because he knows where everyone lives, then rides home in a giant soap bubble with smaller bubbles around his Ryls and Knooks.
