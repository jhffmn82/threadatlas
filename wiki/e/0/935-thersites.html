Thersites

person · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: dastard soul, knaves, niddering Thersites, railer, rascalry, this fellow, thy dog's soul, vile niddering

Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, Thersites attacks Agamemnon and urges departure until Ulysses rebukes, beats, and silences him. In The Fall of Troy, Thersites mocks Achilles for pitying Penthesileia; Achilles kills him with a strike beneath the ear, and he is buried separately.

Thersites in The Iliad

Homer · 749 to 724 BC · the work’s page · read it where Thersites first appears

Abstract written by a model

Thersites, the ugliest soldier before Troy and a habitual critic of authority, attacks Agamemnon and urges departure until Ulysses rebukes, beats, and silences him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK II.

Thersites openly insults Agamemnon, accuses him of taking prizes, and urges the Achaeans to sail home. Ulysses rebukes him, beats him with the staff, and drives him from speaking further in the assembly.

Thersites in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · about AD 200 to 299 · the work’s page · read it where Thersites first appears

Abstract written by a model

Thersites, a venomous railer and son of Agrius, loudly mocks Achilles for pitying Penthesileia; Achilles kills him with a strike beneath the ear, and he is buried separately.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Then did the warrior sons of Argos strip

Thersites loudly mocks Achilles for pitying Penthesileia and accuses him of being ruled by desire for women. Achilles strikes him beneath the ear, killing him, and Thersites is buried separately in a pit.
