Abstract written by a model

In Heroides and Amores, poetry is valued above great wealth, gives women a name, sings only the speaker’s mistress, and is redirected by love toward love poetry and letters for legendary figures. In Heroides and Amores, poetry and poetic fame survive the pyre, preserve Troy, and keep Tibullus, Nemesis, and Delia known. In Heroides and Amores, Ovid's poetry, stirred by Corinna, publicizes her beauty and creates or transmits marvelous myths beyond historical truth. In The Amores, or Amours, poetry is the speaker’s verses and chief endowment, praising deserving women, especially Corinna, and seeking enduring fame beyond death, though its celebration of Corinna also harms his happiness.

poetry in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where poetry first appears

Abstract written by a model

Poetry is presented as the speaker’s verses and chief endowment: it praises deserving women, especially Corinna, and gives them fame. Its greatest power is endurance, seeking everlasting renown beyond death and outlasting monarchs, triumphs, wealth, flint-stones, and the plough. The speaker offers cheerful poems to Corinna instead of wealth and makes her his sole subject. Yet poetry’s fame has a cost: by celebrating Corinna’s charms and attracting rivals, it harms the speaker’s happiness. Its merit is uncertain in the present age, and poets are not reliable witnesses.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

ELEGY X.

The speaker says that his endowments lie in his lines and that he can sing the praises of fair women who deserve them. Through his poetic skill, the woman he chooses becomes celebrated.

ELEGY XV.

Poetry seeks everlasting fame and is exempt from death. It is presented as capable of outlasting monarchs, their triumphs, wealth, flint-stones, and the enduring plough.

ELEGY XVII.

The poems are the speaker’s cheerful lines, which he offers Corinna in place of great wealth. They will celebrate no woman but Corinna, who alone will provide subject matter for his genius.

ELEGY XII.

The poems have made Corinna celebrated and have brought the speaker many rivals. They praise her charms, introduce lovers to her, and open her door, but the speaker says they have injured his happiness.
