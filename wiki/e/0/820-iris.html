Iris in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Iris, Juno’s rainbow goddess and messenger, descends unseen disguised as Beroe to deceive and urge Trojan matrons to burn ships, then returns to heaven on wings; she also releases Dido’s soul from dying life.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Thus far the sacred sports they celebrate:

Iris descends unseen to the Trojan shore, disguises herself as Beroe, and urges the Trojan matrons to destroy the ships. After the matrons begin the attack, she returns to heaven on her wings.

Iris in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Iris is a winged, golden-winged, fleet messenger goddess who serves Jove and the gods, moving as swiftly as the wind. She carries commands that shape the Trojan war: warning the Trojans while disguised as Polites, directing Hector’s response to Agamemnon, ordering Neptune to withdraw, secretly urging Achilles to appear and drive back the Trojans, and conveying Jove’s instructions to Thetis and Priam about Hector’s ransom. Though usually Jove’s messenger, she also secretly delivers Juno’s message without Jove’s knowledge. Her work ends with Priam’s ransom mission, which she escorts by Jove’s command.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

And now, O Muses, dwellers in the mansions of Olympus, tell

Iris is sent by Jove to bring the Trojans bad news about the advancing Achaeans. Disguised with the voice and likeness of Polites, she urges Hector to have each allied chief organize and lead his own people.

BOOK XI.

Iris carries Jove’s message from Mount Ida to Hector. She tells him to hold back while Agamemnon is dominant and to attack once Agamemnon has been wounded.

BOOK XV.

Iris goes swiftly from Olympus to Neptune and delivers Jove’s command that he stop fighting and withdraw. She urges Neptune to reconsider his defiance, and he finally agrees to leave the field while declaring his resentment if Jove spares Ilius.

BOOK XVIII.

Iris secretly carries Juno’s message to Achilles, telling him to show himself at the trench even though he lacks armour. She warns him that Hector may outrage Patroclus’s body, and her message leads Achilles to frighten the Trojans into retreat.

BOOK XXIV.

Iris carries Jove’s messages to Thetis and Priam. She tells Priam to travel alone with one honored servant and ransom Hector’s body from Achilles.

Iris in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Iris is Juno’s goddess and messenger, wearing a garment of a thousand colours and travelling by rainbow. At Juno’s command, she visits Sleep to order a vision for Halcyone, departing as sleepiness overtakes her; later she descends from heaven, instructs Hersilia, and leads her to Quirinus’s grove.

Iris goes to the abode of Sleep at Juno’s command. She orders Sleep to send Halcyone a vision in the likeness of Ceyx and then departs along the rainbow after the vapour of Sleep begins to overcome her.

FABLES XII. AND XIII. [XIV.698-851]

Iris descends from heaven along her rainbow at Juno's command. She tells Hersilia to go to the grove on the hill of Quirinus, then leads her there.
