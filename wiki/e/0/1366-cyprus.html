Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Cyprus is a sea-girt, sea-set island and Aphrodite’s sweet-smelling home, birthplace, and dominion, with walled cities including Paphos and her precinct, temple, and fragrant altar. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Cyprus is an island with nine major cities and kings and a principal theater in wars involving Artaxerxes, Evagoras, Ptolemy, and Antigonus; Demetrius captures and controls it before Nicomedes rules it. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Cyprus is an island and contested theater of power where Cimon and the Athenians defeat a Persian fleet, capture cities, and bring most of the island under Athenian control; later, Evagoras’s expansion makes it the scene of war. In The Library, Cyprus is where Agapenor, Phidippus with the Coans, and Demophon with his followers settled, with Demophon going there after leaving Phyllis and his followers settling there after his death. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Cyprus is an island sacred to Venus, containing Amathus, nearby mines, and Venus’s cities; it is also called Ophiusia and Cerastis and includes places linked to the Cerastae and Propoetides, while Paphos gives his name to a place there. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Cyprus is associated with claims that Homer was born there and with Agapenor’s founding of Paphos after a storm brought him there following Ilium’s capture.

Cyprus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Cyprus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cyprus, an island with nine major cities and kings, was a principal theater in war between Artaxerxes and Evagoras: Persian forces invaded, defeated Evagoras’s fleet, and besieged Salamis. An eunuch then murdered Evagoras and became king of Salamis. Its kings formed a confederacy against Persian authority, while the island was divided between supporters of Ptolemy and Antigonus. Demetrius invaded, captured towns and cities, defeated Ptolemy’s forces, and controlled Cyprus; after Ptolemy’s campaigns, Nicomedes ruled it.

Cyprus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Cyprus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cyprus is an island and a contested theater of power. Cimon and the Athenians defeat a Persian fleet, capture several cities, bring most of the island under Athenian control, and leave Salamis under a Persian garrison as Cimon dies there. Later, Evagoras, king of Salamis, expands his authority, prompting other cities to seek Artaxerxes’ aid and making Cyprus the scene of war.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. XIII.

The Persian fleet was stationed at Cyprus when Cimon sailed there and joined battle. After the Athenian victory, the Persian soldiers fled ashore and the remaining ships came into the hands of the enemy.

DIODORUS SICULUS. / BOOK XIL

Cyprus was invaded by Cimon and the Athenians, who captured several cities and brought most of the island under Athenian power. Salamis remained under a strong Persian garrison until peace ended the campaign, and Cimon died on the island.

CHAP. XL

Evagoras expanded his authority in Cyprus after becoming king of Salamis. The Amathusians, Solians, and Cittans appealed to Artaxerxes, who ordered preparations to aid them and to make war against Evagoras.

CHAP. XIII.

Cyprus became the scene of the war between Artaxerxes and Euagoras. Euagoras raised forces across the island before Artaxerxes turned his attention to the conflict.

Cyprus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Cyprus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cyprus is an island sacred to Venus, containing Amathus, mines near it, and Venus’s cities. Called Ophiusia for many serpents and Cerastis for hornlike promontories, it includes places linked to the Cerastæ and Propœtides; Paphos gives his name to a place there.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE VI. [X.220-242]

Cyprus is sacred to Venus and contains her cities, the lands called Ophiusian, and the places associated with the Cerastæ and Propœtides. It is described as being surrounded by pointed promontories that resemble horns and as having been known as Cerastis; Paphos is said to give his name to a place on the island.

Cyprus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Cyprus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cyprus is a place associated with Homer through local claims that Themisto was his mother and that he was born there, sailed away, and sang of Hellas’s woes. After Ilium’s capture, a storm brought Agapenor there; he founded Paphos, and Laodice sent Tegea a sacred robe.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER V.

A storm carried Agapenor and the Arcadian fleet to Cyprus after the capture of Ilium. Agapenor became the founder of Paphos there, and his daughter Laodice later sent a robe from sacred Cyprus to Tegea.

CHAPTER XXIV.

Cyprus is another place that claims Homer as its own. Its people say that Themisto was Homer’s mother and cite prophetic verses of Euclus that describe Homer’s birth in Cyprus, his departure by sea, and his singing of the woes of Hellas.
