  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the sons of Oedipus dishonor their father and deny him proper liberty, provoking his curses; their conflict is expected to kill them and cause widespread slaughter around Thebes.
  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Oedipus's sons, Eteocles and Polynices, reject their exiled father, fight over the Theban throne, and later seek to compel his return, while he refuses their help and prays for their conflict to end as he wishes.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the sons of Oedipus are cursed after actions involving their father, assigned war and fighting, killed by one another, and sent into Hades.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the sons of Œdipus fight in single combat, prompting war between Argives and Thebans; their tombs near Thebes divide flame and smoke during sacrifices.
  • In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, Oedipus's sons are cursed rivals for kingship who fail to aid their banished father, expel him, and later seek to return him to Thebes despite an oracle's warning.

Oedipus's sons in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Oedipus's sons, Eteocles and Polynices, reject their father in exile, choosing ambition and sovereignty. They fight over the Theban throne and later seek to compel his return to Thebes because of foretold consequences, but Oedipus refuses help and prays their conflict end as he wishes.

_Enter_ ISMENE.

Oedipus's sons, Eteocles and Polynices, choose ambition and sovereignty over concern for their father. They fight over the Theban throne, and Oedipus refuses to help either of them, praying that their conflict end according to his wish.

_Enter_ THESEUS.

Oedipus's sons are said to be compelling his removal to Thebes because of the foretold consequences of his presence elsewhere. Oedipus declares that they rejected him when he wanted exile and now seek him when he refuses to return.

the sons of Oedipus in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

The sons of Oedipus, born of Euryganeia, are cursed after actions involving their father, then assigned war and fighting, fall by one another’s hands, and go into Hades.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE THEBAID

The sons of Oedipus were cursed by their father after Polyneices presented him with Cadmus's treasures and again after they sent him a haunch. Oedipus prayed that they would not divide their father's goods peacefully and that each would die by the other's hand.

sons of Œdipus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The sons of Œdipus fight single combat; Argives and Thebans wage war over them. They have tombs near Thebes, where, during sacrifices, flame and smoke divide.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IX.

The war between the Argives and the Thebans was fought over the sons of Œdipus.

CHAPTER XVIII.

The children of Œdipus have tombs near the road from Thebes to Chalcis. The Thebans offer funeral sacrifices to them, during which the flame and smoke are said to divide.

Oedipus's sons in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912

Oedipus's sons, cursed members of Oedipus's race, first meant to leave the throne to Creon but turn to fierce rivalry for kingship. They fail to aid Oedipus after his banishment, expel him from his country, then seek to return him to Thebes despite an oracle's warning of destruction in Colonus.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

[Enter ISMENE]

Oedipus's sons initially intended to leave the throne to Creon to remove the old curse on their race, but they have instead entered a fierce rivalry for kingship. Oedipus accuses both of them of failing to help him when he was banished and prays that neither will retain or regain the throne.

[Enter THESEUS]

Oedipus's sons expelled him from his country and later seek to convey him back to Thebes. An oracle has warned them of destruction awaiting them in the land where Oedipus is sheltered.
