Abstract written by a model

The Pelignian fields are fertile land containing Sulmo, with flowing streams, grass, corn, abundant grapes, vines, olive trees, meadows, and cool air. They foster the poet and receive his appeal for a golden standard, yet their beauty cannot console him during his mistress’s absence.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ELEGY XVI.

The Pelignian land is praised for its flowing streams, verdant grass, fertile cornfields, fruitful vines, and olive trees. Its meadows, vines, and cool air cannot make the speaker happy while his mistress is absent.

ELEGY X.

The Pelignian fields are identified as the place of which the poet is a foster-child. The poet asks the beauteous boy and the Amathusian parent of the beauteous boy to raise their golden standard from his fields.
