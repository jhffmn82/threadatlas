Colophon

place · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Colophonia, Colophonians
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Colophon is associated with Homer as the place where he began composing; Manto came there after marrying Rhacius, cast down and weeping over the destruction of her country.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Colophon is associated with Cretan, Ionian, and other Greek settlement after Mopsus drove out the Carians; its colonists at Ephesus alone fought Lysimachus and the Macedonians, and Lysimachus later dispeopled it, with its fallen buried in tombs on the road to Claros.

CHAPTER III.

Colophon was associated with Cretan, Ionian, and other Greek settlement after Mopsus drove out the Carians. It was later dispeopled by Lysimachus, and the tombs of its people who fell in battle with those from Smyrna stood on the road to Claros.
