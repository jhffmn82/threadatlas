field of poppies in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04

A meadow covered with poppies in great clusters, whose poisonous scent endangers travelers. After other flowers become fewer, Dorothy, Toto, and the Cowardly Lion fall asleep there; the Scarecrow and Tin Woodman carry them out, and the Lion is later taken to green fields.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter VIII

The travelers enter a great meadow of poppies after the other flowers become fewer. The field puts Dorothy, Toto, and the Cowardly Lion to sleep, while the Scarecrow and the Tin Woodman carry Dorothy and Toto beyond its dangerous scent.

Chapter IX

The poppy bed is where the Cowardly Lion lies asleep, and its poisonous scent threatens to keep him from waking. The Lion is carried out of it to the green fields.
