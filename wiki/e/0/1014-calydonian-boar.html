  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Calydonian boar is sent by Diana as punishment for Oeneus’s failed sacrifice, ravages Calydonia and its cattle, and is pursued by Meleager and other hunters.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the Calydonian boar is a fierce, destructive wild boar with gleaming tusks that Meleager slays in high Calydon.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the Calydonian boar is sent by Diana to avenge neglected worship, ravages Calydon and Œneus’s dominions, kills country people, harms hunters, and is wounded by Atalanta before Meleager kills it.
  • In The Library, Artemis’s boar is sent by Artemis because Oeneus omitted her from a sacrifice; it devastates Calydon and kills livestock and people until assembled heroes hunt it.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the Calydonian boar is first struck by Atalanta’s arrow and attacked by wounded Ancæus; its hunt is depicted on Athene Alea’s temple, and its tusks are carried away or displayed in gardens.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Calydonian boar is an enormous monster sent by Diana to ravage Calydon, killing or wounding hunters before Atalanta wounds it and Meleager kills it.

Calydonian boar in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

The Calydonian boar is a huge wild boar, sent by Diana as avenger for neglected worship. It ravages Calydon and part of Œneus’s dominions, especially a vineyard, killing country people and harming hunters. After devastating the land, it is wounded by Atalanta and finally killed when Meleager strikes it with his spear.

Calydonian boar in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The Calydonian boar was first struck by Atalanta’s arrow and attacked by wounded Ancæus; its hunt appeared on Athene Alea’s temple. Augustus carried away its tusks, while a broken remaining tusk stood in Cæsar’s gardens.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XLV.

The Calydonian boar was attacked by Ancæus, who sustained its attack despite being wounded, while Atalanta was the first to hit it with an arrow. Its hunting was represented on one gable of the temple of Athene Alea.
