  • In Hecuba and other plays, the Dioscuri are Castor and Pollux, divine brothers who protect Helen, prevent Theoclymenus from killing Theonoe, explain Helen's fate, guide Helen and Menelaus, and announce future honors.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the Dioscuri are Castor and Polydeuces, sons of Leda and Zeus who ride swift horses, aid men and ships in storms, and become signs of deliverance for sailors.
  • In The Library, the Dioscuri are Castor and Pollux, twin heroic gods who fight, raid, rescue, and restore rulers before Zeus grants them alternating existence among gods and mortals.
  • In Description of Greece, Castor and Pollux are deities believed to have become hostile to the Messenians before later being appeased and receiving sacrifices during the restoration.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Castor and Pollux are twin offspring of Leda and Jupiter who join the Argonauts, become patrons of seamen, rescue Simonides, and share alternating life after Castor's death.

Dioscuri in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

The Dioscuri are the Tyndaridae, Castor and Polydeuces, children of Leda and Zeus. Born beneath Taygetus, they ride swift horses and aid men and ships in storms: appearing on tawny wings, they calm winds and waves, becoming signs of deliverance for shipmen.

The Tyndaridae are Castor and Polydeuces. They sprang from Olympian Zeus and were borne by Leda beneath the heights of Taygetus.

XXXIII. TO THE DIOSCURI

The Dioscuri are identified with the Tyndaridae, Castor and Polydeuces, the children of Leda and Zeus. They deliver men and ships from storms by appearing on tawny wings, calming the winds, and stilling the waves.

Dioscuri in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Dioscuri are Castor and Pollux, a pair of twin heroic gods named for their bravery and manliness: Castor practiced warfare and Pollux boxing. They fought in Messene over cattle booty and other spoil, carried off and married Hilaira and Phoebe, captured Athens with the Lacedaemonians and Arcadians, carried away Helen and Aethra, and restored Menestheus to sovereignty. After Castor’s death, Zeus granted the pair alternating existence among gods and mortals.

Castor and Pollux in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Castor and Pollux are Leda and Jupiter's twin offspring and Helen's brothers, also called Dioscuri. Castor tames horses and Pollux boxes. Together they join the Argonautic expedition; stars above them in a storm lead them to become patrons of seamen and voyagers. They rescue Simonides from a collapsing banquet hall. After Castor dies, Pollux shares his life, alternating days under the earth and in heaven; in one story they become Gemini.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter XIII

Castor and Pollux are the twin offspring of Leda and Jupiter, and Helen is their sister. Castor is skilled at taming horses, while Pollux is skilled in boxing; they join the Argonautic expedition and become patrons of sailors after stars appear above them during a storm. After Castor's death, Pollux shares his life with him by spending alternate days under the earth and in heaven, and they are also said to have been placed among the stars as Gemini.

Chapter XVIII

Castor and Pollux were the sons of Leda whose exploits Simonides introduced into his poem. According to Simonides, they repaid the poet by sending two young men to draw him from the banquet just before the hall collapsed.
