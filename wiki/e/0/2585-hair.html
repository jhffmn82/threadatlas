hair in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

The mistress’s former hair was abundant, fine, soft, naturally black-and-golden, and hung in many harmless ringlets down her sides. It resembled Seres veils in fineness and stripped cedar of Ida in colour. Though it endured combing, bodkins, iron, and fire, repeated noxious dyeing tortured and lost it.

ELEGY XIV.

The mistress's former hair was abundant, fine, soft, naturally mixed in colour, easy to manage, and long enough to reach down her sides. It endured combing, bodkins, iron, and fire until the tortured hair was lost; the speaker compares it to hair desired by Apollo, Bacchus, and Dione.

the hair in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

is_a fine and abundant bodily hair [formerly belonging to the beloved woman] The beloved woman’s former hair was abundant, delicate, easily arranged, and a mixture of black and golden color. It endured irons and fire but was ultimately ruined by her own dyes and treatments; the speaker says that replacement hair from captive German women will be sent.
