Aurora in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

Aurora is a fair, yellow- and saffron-locked mother of Memnon, with rosy hands and a frosty chariot. Married to aged Tithonus yet avoiding him, she once loved Cephalus. She brings day, first sees field workers, summons oxen, wakes boys, and recalls workers to labor, becoming hated by those whose sleep or pleasure she ends. Reproached for waking lovers and fleeing Tithonus, she blushes but still brings daylight.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ELEGY XIII.

Aurora comes from her aged husband Tithonus in a frosty chariot and brings on the day. The speaker urges her to restrain her dewy reins and delay, reproaching her for disturbing lovers and waking people to their labors. He accuses her of avoiding Tithonus because of his age and recalls her former passion for Cephalus; she blushes at his words, but does not delay the day.

Aurora in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Aurora is a goddess dwelling in the eastern quarter, opening purple doors in the ruddy east at dawn and possessing the Sun's chariot. She becomes enamoured of Cephalus while he hunts and carries him away against his will. When he rejects her for Procris, she angrily returns him, alters his appearance to test Procris, and fosters his suspicions of her fidelity.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Aurora becomes enamoured of Cephalus while he is hunting and carries him off against his will. After he rejects her in favor of Procris, she sends him back angrily and encourages his suspicions of his wife’s fidelity, changing his shape for his test.

notes

According to Ovid and other writers, Aurora carried off Cephalus, the son of Deïoneus. Cephalus subsequently left her and returned to Procris.

notes .. notes

Aurora is associated with the story of Cephalus's love for her, which the explanation says originated in his early rising for the chase.

Aurora in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Aurora is the goddess of the Dawn. She loves Cephalus, carries him away despite his resistance and warns him about Procris; she loves Tithonus most, gains immortality but not youth for him, and bears Memnon. She anxiously watches Memnon fight at Troy, mourns his death, and her tears become morning dew.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

PYTHON

Aurora saw Cephalus at dawn, fell in love with him, and carried him away. When he resisted her because he loved Procris, she warned that he would eventually regret having his wife.

Chapter XIX

Aurora, the goddess of the Dawn, loved Tithonus and obtained immortality for him, though she failed to secure perpetual youth. She was the mother of Memnon and watched anxiously as he fought at Troy; after his death, she mourned him inconsolably. Her tears are said to appear as dew-drops on the grass at early morning.
