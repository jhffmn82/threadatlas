the Theatre in The Diary of Samuel Pepys

Samuel Pepys · 1660/1669

The Theatre is a house and performance venue. It presents Bartholomew Faire, admired as well acted though excessively profane and abusive, and Claracilla, also well acted. Once thronged, it becomes unusually empty after the Opera begins, ending with the diarist’s first viewing of Claracilla.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

The diarist goes to the Theatre with Mr. Shepley and Mr. Creed and sees Bartholomew Faire. He considers the play admirable and well acted, though excessively profane and abusive.

JULY 1661 July 1st.

The Theatre presented "Claracilla" on July 4th, which the diarist saw for the first time and considered well acted. The house, formerly very crowded, was unusually empty because the Opera had begun.
