Abstract written by a model

Utah, also called the Land of the Saints, is a broad-valley Mormon settlement and promised destination after a westward journey. Its valley prospers agriculturally and includes Lucy Ferrier, praised across the Pacific slope. Later, religious dissent becomes dangerous; resistance is secretly punished, prompting John Ferrier, Lucy, and fugitives to attempt escape through surrounding mountains.

Utah in A Study in Scarlet

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

Utah is the promised land where the immigrant Mormons settle after their westward journey. Its valley becomes a prosperous agricultural region, and Lucy Ferrier is described as the fairest specimen of American girlhood in the whole Pacific slope.

CHAPTER III.

Utah is depicted as a place where religious dissent is dangerous and secret powers punish resistance. John Ferrier proposes leaving Utah with Lucy before the order concerning her marriage is enforced.

CHAPTER IV.

Jefferson Hope says that before harm comes to Lucy there will be one less member of the Hope family in Utah. The fugitives attempt to leave the settlement and travel through the surrounding mountains.
