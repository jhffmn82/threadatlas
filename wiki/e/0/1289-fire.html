fire

object · 4 works · 5 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Jove's authentic fire, far-seen gleam of unwearying fire, far-seen ray of fire, fount of fire, gift, plastic fire, radiant fire, spits, the stolen fire, unwearying fire, πυρὸς, πῦρ
  • In Four Plays of Aeschylus, the stolen fire is given by Prometheus to mortal men, enabling them to learn many arts and causing Zeus to punish Prometheus.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, fire is the gleam Zeus hides from mortals, which Prometheus steals and gives to them, prompting Zeus to punish them by creating Pandora.
  • In The Library, fire is hidden in a fennel stalk, stolen by Prometheus, and given to human beings, for which Zeus punishes Prometheus.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, fire is brought by Prometheus to humanity and enables weapons, tools, cultivation, warmth, and resistance to cold.
