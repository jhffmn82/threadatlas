love poetry in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Love poetry is a light poetic form, suited to youthful subjects and the alternating six- and five-foot elegiac couplet, including The Amores. It begins when the speaker abandons violent epic after Cupid changes his meter, drawing its subject and lasting fame from the beloved woman. In the later account, Elegy and Cupid defend its power against Tragedy, and the poet continues it temporarily before a greater task.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

P. OVIDI NASONIS AMORES / LIBER PRIMUS

The speaker abandons the preparation of violent epic poetry for lighter elegiac verse when Cupid alters his meter. Love poetry is presented as deriving its subject from the beloved woman, whose beauty and relationship with the speaker can make their names famous throughout the world.

LIBER TERTIUS

Love poetry is presented as the subject of the poet's earlier work and as a light form associated with Elegy and Cupid. Tragedy considers it too narrow for the poet's talent, while Elegy defends its power and influence. The poet is allowed to continue writing it temporarily before undertaking a greater task.
