Procris

person · 4 works · 24 quoted facts · 13 connections
Also called: Cephalus's wife, daughter of Erectheus, her, his wife, my attached wife, my wife, rival, she, wife, wife Procris, Πρόκρις

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Procris is the wife of Cephalus, who accidentally kills her and is subsequently purified of the homicide by the Cadmeans. In The Library, Procris is a hunter and wife of Cephalus who uses Circaean root against Minos, reconciles with Cephalus, and is accidentally killed by him while hunting. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Procris is the daughter of Erectheus, sister of Orithyïa, and wife of Cephalus, whose jealousy leads to her accidental death by his javelin. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Procris is Cephalus's wife and Diana's favorite, giving him an unfailing javelin and swift dog before being accidentally killed by the javelin.

Procris married Cephalus. After being bribed with a golden crown, she slept with Pteleon and fled to Minos when Cephalus discovered her; she later returned to Athens and reconciled with Cephalus. While hunting, she was unknowingly killed by Cephalus with a dart, and Cephalus was condemned to perpetual banishment.

Procris in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Procris first appears

Abstract written by a model

Procris is the daughter of Erectheus, king of Athens, sister of Orithyïa, and wife of Cephalus. She loves Cephalus until death, though their marriage is marked by separation, disguise, jealousy, and reconciliation. When the disguised Cephalus tests her, she resists him as faithful to her absent husband, then flees in shame after recognizing him; he seeks forgiveness, and they reconcile. In accounts mentioning Pteleon, she has an amour with him, a report of her infidelity follows, and she flees to Minos’s court in Crete. She later lives at Thoricus, returns to Cephalus, and gives him Lælaps and a celebrated javelin. Believing he loves the Nymph Aura, she hides in a forest to watch him and is fatally wounded in the breast by the javelin, asking him not to share their marriage with Aura.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Procris is Erectheus’s daughter and Cephalus’s wife. She resists the disguised Cephalus for a long time, declaring that she is reserved for her absent husband, but flees in shame when he reveals himself; after he asks forgiveness, she is reconciled with him and gives him Lælaps and a javelin.

notes

She was the daughter of Erectheus, king of Athens. She married Cephalus, the son of Deïoneus, and received him back after he left Aurora, according to Ovid and other writers.

opening

Procris became jealous after hearing that Cephalus repeatedly called upon the breeze in affectionate words, believing that he loved a Nymph. She followed him into the forest to discover the supposed infidelity, concealed herself in the thicket, and was fatally struck by the javelin. As she died, she asked Cephalus not to let the Nymph Aura share their marriage ties.

notes .. notes

Procris carried on an amour with Pteleon, according to Apollodorus, and probably spread a report of her own infidelity to divert attention from it. She fled to the court of Minos in Crete, later returned to Thoricus, was reconciled with Cephalus, and received the celebrated dog and javelin.
