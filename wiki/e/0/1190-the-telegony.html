The Telegony

work · 1 work · 6 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: THE TELEGONY, Telegony, _Telegony_

Abstract written by a model

The Telegony is a two-book poem by Eugammon of Cyrene, following the Odyssey in the epic sequence. It continues Odysseus’s story through adventures in Thesprotis and his return to Ithaca, ending with his death at Telegonus’s hands, Circe’s grant of immortality, and marriages among surviving characters.

The Telegony is in one work on this site, Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

The Ionic School

The Telegony, a two-book poem by Eugammon of Cyrene, continued the story after the Odyssey. It described Odysseus’s adventures in Thesprotis, his return to Ithaca, his death at the hands of Telegonus, and the marriages of the surviving characters.

THE TELEGONY

The Telegony is a poem in two books by Eugammon of Cyrene. It concerns the events following the Returns and the Odyssey, including Odysseus’s travels, his death at the hands of Telegonus, and the immortality granted by Circe.
