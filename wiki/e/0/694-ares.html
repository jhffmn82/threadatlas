  • In The Amores, or Amours, Mars is a soldier's god and Cupid's stepfather who is caught in forge-wrought chains and later girds a sword.
  • In Heroides and Amores, Mars is a war god who hinders Dipsas's young woman through a contrary star and wields a sword against men.
  • In The Argonautica, Ares has a temple and sacred grove near Aea where the golden fleece hangs guarded by a serpent.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Mars is the god of war and bloodshed, father of Lycaon and Cycnus, and a force in Thebes's violence.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Ares is a war god and warrior who battles Heracles after Cycnus's death and is wounded before being carried to Olympus.
  • In The Iliad, Mars is a war god fighting for the Trojans who is wounded by Diomed and Minerva and driven from battle.
  • In The Library, Ares kills Halirrhothius, is tried and acquitted by the twelve gods, opposes Odysseus, and is bound by Otus and Ephialtes.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Mars guards a dragon, fathers it in one interpretation, and is Venus's lover and Hermione's father.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Mavors asks Jupiter to raise Romulus and the Roman state to heaven.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Mars recognizes Jupiter's signal, drives to the Palatium, and carries Romulus from earth to heaven.
  • In The Library, Ares is said to father the dragon guarding the spring near Thebes, and Cadmus serves him in atonement for killing it.
  • In The Fall of Troy, Ares is Penthesileia's divine father, gives her arms, grieves her death, aids Troy, and withdraws to Thrace.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Mars is the god of war, son of Jupiter and Juno, who loves violence and favors the Trojans.

Ares in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Ares is a god and warrior defined by war: deadly, insatiable, mighty with spear and shield, golden-helmed, bronze-harnessed, and chariot-borne by blazing horses. He possesses a brazen spear, chariot, and team, and is called saviour of cities, defender of Olympus, helper of men, giver of dauntless youth, father of warlike Victory, and leader of righteous men. He is also father to Panic, Fear, Harmonia, and Cycnus. Though associated with strength, boldness, and peace maintained through courage, he is urged to restrain fury, avoid strife and hatred, and oppose cowardice. He loves war, city-sacking, shouts, and battle. With Cycnus he arms for battle against Heracles; grieving Cycnus’ death, he attacks Heracles, but Athene diverts his spear and Heracles wounds his thigh. Panic and Dread then carry him by chariot to Olympus.

THE SHIELD OF HERACLES

Ares appears with Cycnus, armed and eager for war, and urges his footmen into battle. Athena tells Heracles to watch Ares closely and wound him when he is exposed below his shield, but not to take his horses or armor.

Ares attacks Heracles in grief over Cycnus’ death, but Athene turns aside his spear. Heracles wounds Ares deeply in the thigh, after which Panic and Dread carry him by chariot to Olympus.

VIII. TO ARES

Ares is praised as strong, chariot-riding, golden-helmed, bronze-harnessed, and skilled with the spear. He is described as the Saviour of cities, defender of Olympus, father of warlike Victory, ally of Themis, and leader of righteous men. The speaker asks him for courage and strength, and asks him to restrain the fury that provokes blood-curdling strife.

XI. TO ATHENA

Ares loves deeds of war, the sack of cities, shouting, and battle with Pallas Athene.

Mars in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Mars is an immortal, warlike god of war, son of Juno and fathered by Jove, a divine warrior, piercer of shields, and father of Ascalaphus. He possesses gold-bedizened steeds and a sword, and is associated with causing the war over Helen and with battle’s destructive force. Fighting for the Trojans, he destroys many Achaeans, rallies the Trojans in the likeness of Acamas, and covers them with darkness. Minerva enables Diomed to wound him; he cries out, flees to Olympus, complains to Jove, and Paeeon heals him. He later attacks Minerva, but she knocks him down with a stone. Venus leads him away, and Minerva draws him from battle, allowing the Danaans to drive back the Trojans.
