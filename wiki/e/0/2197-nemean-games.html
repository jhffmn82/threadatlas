Nemean Games in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

Nemean Games are sacred games and a sacred festival held at Nemea, commemorated for Aigina and long managed by Kleonai. They award winners a crown and a branch of sacred palm, with victory bringing song, praise, and a libation to Zeus. The records begin with Pytheas’s first mentioned victory and the victories of Psalychidai, then show clans testing themselves: Timasarchos wins boys’ wrestling, Alkimidas wins, and Praxidamas takes three crowns. Timodemos establishes his first victory there, while Timodemidai ultimately obtain seven crowns. Pindar attended in winter.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Nemean Ode II

The Timodemidai had obtained seven crowns at Nemea. Timodemos also established his first foundation of victory in the sacred games there.

Nemean Ode III

The Nemean games are the sacred festival commemorated by the ode. Victory there is presented as deserving song, crowns, and praise.

Nemean Ode IV

The Nemean games are among the contests at which the Theandrid clan tested itself. Timasarchos won the boys' wrestling-match at Nemea.

Nemean Ode VI

The Nemean Games are represented through the contests and crowns won at Nemea. Alkimidas wins there, while Praxidamas is said to have taken three Nemean crowns.

Isthmian Ode V

The family won victories at leafy Nemea, and the poet recalls that the first libation was made to Zeus when a Nemean crown was won.

notes

The Nemean games were an occasion when a winner received a branch of the sacred palm along with a crown. Pindar had attended them in winter.

Nemean Games in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

After Archemorus’s burial, the champions establish the Nemean Games in honor of Opheltes, called Archemorus; Adrastus, Eteoclus, Tydeus, Amphiaraus, Laodocus, Polynices, and Parthenopaeus win seven contests.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

VI. ᾿Ἑτεοκλῆς δὲ καὶ ἸΠολυνείκης περὶ τῆς

The champions establish the Nemean Games in honor of Opheltes, whom they call Archemorus. Adrastus wins the horse race, Eteoclus the foot race, Tydeus boxing, Amphiaraus leaping and quoit-throwing, Laodocus the javelin, Polynices wrestling, and Parthenopaeus archery.

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

is_a Greek athletic games [held at Nemea] The Nemean games are mentioned in comparisons of athletes' records. Nicasylus of Rhodes was proclaimed victor there, Tisander had victories there according to the account's context, and the Argives were said not to have kept records of Nemean victors at an earlier time.
