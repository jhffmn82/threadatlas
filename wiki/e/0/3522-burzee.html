Burzee in The Life and Adventures of Santa Claus

L. Frank Baum · 1902

Burzee is a mighty, grand, ancient forest, flourishing for hundreds of years and largely unreached by civilization or the human race. Its close-standing trees have big trunks, rough bark, gnarled limbs, bushy foliage roofing the forest, and intertwining roots and branches; sunbeams enter in little spots, while silence is generally unbroken. Nature peopled it in the beginning, and it is inhabited by nymphs and wood-nymphs, Fairies, Knooks, Ryls, animals, and other immortals. It is home to Fairies, Nymphs, and Necile, refuge for Knooks, and playground for Ryls, with the Laughing Valley of Hohaho at its eastern edge. Governed by the Law of the Forest, it has old, sturdy trees no longer threatened by weeds and Gadgols. Necile brings an infant there to raise safely; that child, Claus, grows up among its immortals and regards Burzee as the whole world. He explores its heart, learns natural lore, and helps maintain its plants and animals. After traveling around the world with Ak, Claus returns to the circle of nymphs, then leaves Burzee through its eastern edge to live among men. In his old age, when Santa Claus seems near death, Burzee becomes sad and hosts the council of immortal rulers who decide his fate.

Narrative, section by section

8 sections; each heading opens the text

YOUTH / 1. Burzee

The Forest of Burzee is a mighty, grand, and ancient forest whose trees have intertwined roots and branches. It provides a home, refuge, and playground for its inhabitants and remains largely untouched by civilization.

2. The Child of the Forest

The Forest of Burzee is a great forest inhabited by wood-nymphs and governed by the Law of the Forest. Its trees have become old and sturdy, and weeds and Gadgols no longer threaten them as they once did.

3. The Adoption

The Forest of Burzee is the place where Necile wants to raise the infant, although the human race has never penetrated it. Necile considers it her home and believes the infant cannot harm it.

4. Claus

The Forest of Burzee is where Claus is raised and where the nymphs, Ryls, Knooks, and Fairies visit him. Claus comes to regard it as the whole world because he does not know that other human beings exist.

5. The Master Woodsman

Burzee remains a vast forest inhabited by nymphs, Ryls, Knooks, and many animals. Claus explores its heart, learns its natural lore, and helps maintain its plants and animals.

6. Claus Discovers Humanity

The Forest of Burzee is Claus's home, where he was reared among the immortals after Necile rescued him. Ak and Claus return there after traveling around the world, and Claus is set down among the circle of nymphs.

7. Claus Leaves the Forest

Burzee remains the Forest that Claus leaves when he begins his life among men. Claus passes through Burzee to reach its eastern edge and the Laughing Valley of Hohaho.

OLD AGE / 1. The Mantle of Immortality

The Forest of Burzee becomes sad when Santa Claus grows old and appears near death. It later hosts the council where the rulers of the immortals gather to decide Claus's fate.

Forest in The Life and Adventures of Santa Claus

L. Frank Baum · 1902

The Forest in Burzee contains nymphs who guard it and is home to deer guarded by the Knooks. It teaches Claus that living trees are sacred and feeling, leading him to use fallen trees. Claus knows Flossie and Glossie there; after journeys, the deer return by daybreak and may bathe in its Pool of Nares.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

MANHOOD / 1. The Laughing Valley

The Forest has taught Claus that living trees are sacred and endowed with feeling. Claus applies this lesson by using only fallen trees for his house.

8. The First Journey with the Reindeer

The Forest is where Claus had known Flossie and Glossie, and where the deer normally live under the guardianship of the Knooks. The deer must return to the Forest by daybreak after their journey.

10. Christmas Eve

The Forest is where the Knooks guard the deer and where Glossie and Flossie must return before daybreak. The deer serving Claus may bathe in its Pool of Nares.
