Cottus

person · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: blameless Cottus, Κόττον

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Cottus is a deathless god with a hundred arms who is bound beneath the earth by his father, restored by Zeus and the gods, pledges aid, and hurls three hundred rocks against the Titans. In The Library, Cottus is a Hundred-handed Hecatoncheire born to Uranus and Earth, extraordinarily large and powerful, with one hundred hands and fifty heads.
