Amphiaraus

person · 6 works · 24 quoted facts · 12 connections
Also called: Amphiaraos, Amphiaraus the son of Œcles, Amphiaraus the soothsayer, Amphiarus, prophet, seer Amphiaraos, son of Oikleus, the prophet, the prophet, holding up his targe, the seer Amphiaraus, the son of Oikleus, ᾿Αμφιάραος, ᾿Αμφιαράου

Abstract written by a model

In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Amphiaraos is a prophetic figure at Thebes, son of Oikleus, rival of Adrastos, and a participant in the expedition against Thebes who is buried with his steeds when Zeus opens the earth. In Four Plays of Aeschylus, Amphiaraus is a seer and prophet regarded as the wisest and bravest warrior, who rebukes Tydeus, guards the Homoloid gate, and accepts his foreknown death. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Amphiaraus is a soothsayer who foresees his death in the war against Thebes, joins the campaign because of Eriphyle’s judgment and gifts, and is swallowed by the earth with his chariot. In The Library, Amphiaraus is a seer who foresees the expedition’s deaths, joins the campaign despite opposing it, interprets omens, orders his sons to kill their mother, and is swallowed by the earth and made immortal. In Description of Greece, Amphiaraus is worshipped as a god at Oropus, where he has a temple and dream oracle, after being swallowed by the earth with his chariot and Bato. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Amphiaraus is identified as the son of Œcles and Poseidon and is said to have been swallowed by the earth in an enclosure between Potniæ and Thebes.

Amphiaraos in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · 497 BC · the work’s page · read it where Amphiaraos first appears

Abstract written by a model

Amphiaraos is a prophetic figure at Thebes, son of the prophet Oikleus and rival of Adrastos. After fleeing Argos with Adrastos before civil strife, he joins the expedition against seven-gated Thebes, foretells Alkmaion’s nobility and Adrastos’s safe return, and is buried with his steeds when Zeus opens the earth by thunderbolt.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

VIII.

Amphiaraos speaks prophetically during the story of the second enterprise against Thebes. He recognizes the noble nature of Alkmaion, foresees better tidings for Adrastos, and predicts that Adrastos alone of the Danaans will return safely with his people.

Nemean Ode IX

Amphiaraos fled Argos with Adrastos before civil strife and later joined the expedition against seven-gated Thebes. Zeus opened the earth with a thunderbolt and buried Amphiaraos with his horses before Periklymenos could strike him from behind.

Amphiaraus in The Library

Apollodorus · about AD 70 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Amphiaraus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Amphiaraus, son of Oicles, is a seer who foresees that all expedition participants but Adrastus will die. Opposing the march to Thebes, he is persuaded by Eriphyle to join, hates Tydeus for urging the campaign, and at Nemea interprets Opheltes’s death as an omen while winning leaping and quoit-throwing contests. He orders his sons to kill their mother and march against Thebes, kills Melanippus, then flees until Zeus opens the earth, swallowing him with his chariot and charioteer and making him immortal.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

VI. ᾿Ἑτεοκλῆς δὲ καὶ ἸΠολυνείκης περὶ τῆς

Amphiaraus, son of Oicles, is a seer who foresees that all the expedition's participants except Adrastus will die. He initially opposes the war, but Eriphyle persuades him to join after accepting the necklace; he then orders his sons to kill their mother and march against Thebes when they are grown. At Nemea, he explains that Opheltes's death foretells the future and wins the leaping and quoit-throwing contests.

Amphiaraus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Amphiaraus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Amphiaraus is a figure worshipped as a god at Oropus, where he has a temple, a white-stone statue, and an oracle giving direction through dreams. During the rout from Thebes, the earth swallowed him, his chariot, and Bato, reportedly at Harma rather than Oropus. After becoming a god, he instituted dream divination for worshippers who sacrifice and sleep in a ram’s skin.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXI.

Amphiaraus was said to have been swallowed by the earth with his chariot while fleeing from Thebes, though this was said to have happened at Harma rather than Oropus. At Oropus he is worshipped as a god, has a temple and a white-stone statue, and gives divine direction through dreams after worshippers perform sacrifices and sleep wrapped in a ram’s skin.

CHAPTER XXIII.

Amphiaraus has a temple at some distance from the house of Adrastus, and the tomb of Eriphyle is beyond it. When the rout from Thebes occurred, the earth opened and swallowed Amphiaraus, his chariot, and Bato together.

CHAPTER VIII.

Amphiaraus was believed to have been swallowed by the earth in a small enclosure marked by pillars on the road between Potniæ and Thebes. It was said that neither birds sat on the pillars nor tame or wild animals fed on the grass there.
