Aunt Em

Also called: Aunt, Em, aunt, good lady, her aunt, her aunt and uncle, his wife, ma'am, my aunt, the elder people, the lady, the poor woman

Abstract written by a model

In The Wonderful Wizard of Oz, Aunt Em is Dorothy’s thin, gaunt farmer’s-wife aunt and guardian who shelters in the cellar during the cyclone and later welcomes Dorothy home. In Dorothy and the Wizard in Oz, Aunt Em has returned to the Kansas farm and wears mourning because she believes Dorothy was killed by the earthquake. In The Emerald City of Oz, Aunt Em is Dorothy Gale’s aunt, Uncle Henry’s wife, and Dorothy’s guardian who is brought to the Land of Oz with Uncle Henry and given a permanent palace home.

Aunt Em in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04 · the work’s page · read it where Aunt Em first appears

Abstract written by a model

Aunt Em is a thin, gaunt farmer’s wife, aunt, and guardian who lives with Dorothy and Uncle Henry in the house. Life on the prairies has made her gray and seldom cheerful. When a cyclone comes, she goes to the cellar and tells Dorothy to follow. While Dorothy is away, Aunt Em waits in Kansas; Dorothy returns, and Aunt Em embraces and kisses her.

Aunt Em in The Emerald City of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1910 · the work’s page · read it where Aunt Em first appears

Abstract written by a model

Aunt Em is Dorothy Gale’s aunt, Uncle Henry’s wife, and, with him, Dorothy’s guardian. An aging, feeble country woman and family housekeeper, she begins worried by poverty, the possible loss of the Kansas farm, and the prospect that Dorothy may need to work. Though heartbroken at possibly losing Dorothy, she accepts that Dorothy might be better off with her fairy friends. When Dorothy explains that Aunt Em and Uncle Henry are too old for hard work, Princess Ozma brings them to the Land of Oz and gives them a permanent palace home. Transported abruptly from housework in a calico dress and apron, Aunt Em is frightened and confused, then welcomed as an honored subject. Palace rooms, royal costumes, gold toilet articles, and leisure are more grandeur than she wants: she misses useful work and is troubled by having none. She nevertheless joins Dorothy’s travels, is impressed by Billina’s talking chicken family, and fiercely frightens the Lion with her gaze. She compares School Pills to medicine, doubts the value of matching the Fuddles, but helps find Lord High Chigglewitz’s pieces and shares the group’s amusement. She thinks the Love Magnet might have aided her Kansas family, dislikes the Rigmaroles’ excessive speech, and urges the travelers not to delay in Rigmarole Town. At the end, she is relieved by Dorothy’s return, though she thinks Dorothy is crazy for claiming to have eaten a wheelbarrow.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

2. How Uncle Henry Got Into Trouble

Aunt Em does the housework with Dorothy's help and worries about the family's poverty. She tells Dorothy that she and Uncle Henry may have to leave the farm and that Dorothy might need to work. Although she is heartbroken at the thought of losing Dorothy, she agrees that Dorothy may be better off living with her fairy friends.

3. How Ozma Granted Dorothy's Request

Aunt Em is facing the loss of her Kansas home with Uncle Henry. Dorothy says that she and Uncle Henry are too old to do much hard work, so Ozma agrees to bring Aunt Em to the Land of Oz and provide a comfortable place for her.

5. How Dorothy Became a Princess

Aunt Em is transported from her household work in Kansas to the Throne Room while wearing a calico dress, apron, and Uncle Henry's slippers. She is frightened and confused by the sudden change, but Princess Ozma welcomes her as an honored subject and Dorothy tells her she will live comfortably in Oz.

7. How Aunt Em Conquered the Lion

Aunt Em is given luxurious rooms, fine clothing, and gold toilet articles in the palace, although she feels the finery is too grand for her. She visits Billina's chicken family with Dorothy and is impressed by the talking chickens. When she and Uncle Henry encounter the Lion, she tries to conquer him with her fierce gaze and succeeds in frightening him.

9. How the Wogglebug Taught Athletics

Aunt Em is unhappy that she cannot do household work and finds constant finery and leisure difficult. She travels with Dorothy and the others, enjoys the impressive departure, and says that the Love Magnet might have helped her family in Kansas. At the Royal Athletic College of Oz, she compares the School Pills to taking medicine.

12. How they Matched the Fuddles

Aunt Em doubts the usefulness of visiting the Fuddles and later calls matching them together a fool business. She helps find Lord High Chigglewitz's pieces and joins in the travelers' amusement.

22. How the Wizard Found Dorothy

Aunt Em is relieved when Dorothy returns but thinks Dorothy is crazy when she says she ate a wheelbarrow. She finds the Rigmaroles' lengthy speech frustrating and urges the group not to wait in Rigmarole Town.
