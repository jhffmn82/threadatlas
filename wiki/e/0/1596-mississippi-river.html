Mississippi River in Pen Pictures of Eventful Scenes and Struggles of Life

B. F. Craig · 1880 · the work’s page · read it where Mississippi River first appears

Abstract written by a model

Mississippi River is a great river with tributaries, central to steamboat navigation, the wood trade, and travel. In youth, a narrator crosses it at night in a heavily laden canoe through bank-full, turbulent water and driftwood, guided by a cabin light. Along its course, wood-cutters at Carlo wood yard and Shirt-Tail Bend fuel passing boats amid disputes over steam-navigation monopolies. The Brandywine burns after grounding on a deep sand bar, killing passengers. The river region then extends through plantations, cotton cultivation, and slavery.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Craig Kansas City, Missouri 1880 [Ill cover] [Ill 0020] SCENE FIRST--INTRODUCTION.

The narrator traveled on the Mississippi River and its tributaries in his youth. He crossed the river at night in a heavily laden canoe amid turbulent, bank-full water and driftwood, using the light of a cabin as his guide.

SCENE SECOND.

The Mississippi River is central to the expansion of steamboat navigation and the wood trade. The Carlo wood yard and Shirt-Tail Bend are located along it, and early wood-cutters supply passing boats with fuel. The text also describes the legal struggle against exclusive monopolist claims to navigate its western waters by steam.

SCENE FOURTH--ROXIE DAYMON AND ROSE SIMON.

The Brandywine burns on the Mississippi River after being driven onto a deep sand bar, causing many passengers to drown or be killed by the fire.

SCENE SIXTH.

The Mississippi River region is associated with the expansion of plantations, cotton cultivation, and slavery. S. S. Simon's plantation work extends along the Mississippi, where an early morning bell summons the slaves to labor.
