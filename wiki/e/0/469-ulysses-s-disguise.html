Ulysses's disguise in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Ulysses's disguise is a poor old beggar's appearance. Minerva makes him wrinkled, gray-eyed, ragged, and repulsive so the suitors, Penelope, and his son will not recognize him. He then appears with a staff, begs food from the suitors, and hides his identity behind a false history.

BOOK XIII

Minerva disguises Ulysses by covering him with wrinkles, removing the appearance of his yellow hair, clothing him in a repulsive garment, and making his eyes bleary. The disguise is intended to prevent the suitors, Penelope, and his son from recognizing him.
