Boeotian School

group · 1 work · 10 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: continental school of epic poetry

Abstract written by a model

The Boeotian School is a Boeotian form of epic, unanimously attributed to Hesiod down to Alexandrian criticism. It replaces Ionian romance and pathos with practical, matter-of-fact moral maxims, technical agriculture, astronomy and augury, religion, and genealogies. Its loosely connected subjects lack unity of action, making works artificial collocations of facts; later it increasingly reverts to Homeric tradition, as in the Shield of Heracles.

INTRODUCTION

The Boeotian School developed a practical and matter-of-fact form of epic concerned with moral maxims, technical information, religion, and genealogies. Because such subjects lacked the unity of action needed for epic, its works were often artificial collocations of facts and gradually returned toward Homeric romance, as in the Shield of Heracles.
