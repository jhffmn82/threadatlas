Jack Pumpkinhead in The Marvelous Land of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1904-06

Jack Pumpkinhead is a tall, awkward animated wooden man constructed by Tip: he has jointed wooden limbs, a wooden neck, and a large carved pumpkin head with a perpetual smile. Tip clothes him in Mombi’s clothing and Tip’s shoes, names him, and is regarded by Jack as his creator and father. Mombi brings the dummy to life with the Powder of Life, after which Jack can speak and walk, though he cannot eat or sleep and has no literal heart. Mombi confines him in an empty cow-stable stall, but he escapes with Tip. Jack’s wooden joints are weak and stiff, making walking and sitting difficult, so he commonly rides the Saw-Horse. He accompanies Tip toward the Emerald City, reaches it after Tip is left behind, meets the Scarecrow, and becomes his friend despite being teased and misunderstood because his fixed grin and rigid body do not reflect his feelings. He stays cheerful and calm, but his chief recurring fear is that his pumpkin head will spoil through water, sunshine, insects, fire, or other harm. He repeatedly loses or risks losing the head, including when he falls into a river and when the Saw-Horse throws him, but Tip or others restore it safely. On later travels with Tip, the Scarecrow, the Saw-Horse, and Nick Chopper, Jack has his limbs strengthened, gives up his left leg for the Saw-Horse’s repair, and later receives a carved mahogany table leg that lets him walk and assist an escape. He contributes a broom for the flying machine’s tail and, when Jackdaws threaten, permits his head to be removed and hidden beneath straw. A poor student of arts and sciences, he remains an unusual but loyal friend of Tip and ultimately stays with Princess Ozma for the rest of his days.

Narrative, section by section

The Marvelous Land of Oz

Pumpkinhead appears in the chapter list as a figure manufactured by Tip.

Tip Manufactures a Pumpkinhead

Jack Pumpkinhead is the name Tip gives to the tall wooden man he constructs with a carved, smiling pumpkin for a head. The figure has jointed arms and legs, a wooden neck, and clothing taken from Mombi’s belongings and Tip’s shoes.

The Marvelous Powder of Life

Jack Pumpkinhead is positioned by Tip at the bend in the road as a dummy. After Mombi sprinkles the Powder of Life over him and performs an incantation, he comes to life and speaks, walks, and considers whether he is wise or foolish. Mombi takes him home and locks him in an empty stall in the cow stable.

The Flight of the Fugitives

Jack Pumpkinhead leaves Mombi’s cow-stable with Tip and travels south with him. He learns that Tip made him and accepts Tip as his creator and father, owing him obedience while expecting support.

Tip Makes an Experiment in Magic

Jack Pumpkinhead’s wooden joints give way when he tries to sit down, so Tip tells him to remain standing. He suggests riding the saw-horse if it can be brought to life, and watches as Tip’s experiment succeeds.

The Awakening of the Saw-horse

Jack Pumpkinhead is knocked down by the newly awakened Saw-Horse but takes an active interest in the creature. He suggests making ears for the Saw-Horse, helps hold its head while Tip attaches them, and rides on its back during the journey. At camp, Tip helps Jack lie on the ground to rest.

Jack Pumpkinhead’s Ride to the Emerald City

Jack Pumpkinhead crosses the river by holding the Saw-Horse’s tail and reaches the Emerald City after Tip is left behind. He tells the Guardian of the Gates that he does not know his business and is taken to the Scarecrow. Jack wears green spectacles and remains calm and cheerful throughout.

His Majesty the Scarecrow

Jack Pumpkinhead meets His Majesty the Scarecrow after coming from the Country of the Gillikins. Because his carved face is permanently smiling and his wooden body cannot bend, he is repeatedly misunderstood and teased, but he becomes friends with the Scarecrow and joins him in playing quoits.

The Scarecrow Plans an escape

Jack Pumpkinhead tells Tip that the Saw-Horse carried him safely to the palace and worries that he may spoil if held prisoner. He rides behind Tip on the Saw-Horse, holding his pumpkin head firmly in place during the violent journey. He enters the wide river with the others and floats after being plunged beneath the current.

The Journey to the Tin Woodman

Jack Pumpkinhead falls into the river, loses his pumpkin head, and is restored when Tip retrieves it from the water. He worries repeatedly that water, sunshine, insects, or other dangers might spoil his pumpkin, although his head is not cracked. He continues traveling with Tip, the Scarecrow, and the Saw-Horse.

A Nickel-Plated Emperor

Jack Pumpkinhead meets Nick Chopper and is invited to join the Emperor’s select society because he is unusual. He fears that his head will spoil, but Nick suggests that it can be canned and preserved. Tip strengthens Jack’s wooden limbs, and Jack rides the Saw-Horse on the return journey.

Mr. H. M. Woggle-Bug, T. E.

Jack rides the Saw-Horse through the sunflower illusion and announces when the way is clear. He is thrown into the air when the Saw-Horse falls, but the Tin Woodman catches his pumpkin, and Tip replaces it on his neck.

A Highly Magnified History

Pumpkinhead asks whether seeds could be considered brains and is told by Tip to keep quiet. Because his joints are stiff, he must ride the Saw-Horse; he gives up his left leg so it can be reshaped into a replacement leg for the horse, and he quarrels with the Saw-Horse afterward.

The Scarecrow Takes Time to Think

Jack Pumpkinhead fears that Jinjur will make tarts of him and worries that he will decay. After asking to be released from the Saw-Horse, he receives a carved mahogany table leg from Nick Chopper and is able to walk and help with the escape effort.

The Astonishing Flight of the Gump

Jack Pumpkinhead brings a broom to the roof and suggests fastening it to the machine as a tail. Nick Chopper attaches the broom to the back end of the sofa body.

In the Jackdaw’s Nest

Jack Pumpkinhead worries that flying might spoil his pumpkin head and wishes it could never spoil. The Scarecrow has Tip remove his head and hide it beneath the straw when the Jackdaws approach.

At the Goody Shop, he chooses good advice but rejects the instruction to keep his mouth shut because his mouth is carved open.

CHAPTER 3

Jack Pumpkinhead travels with Peter and is pursued by the Christmas Tree, which tries to take his pumpkin head. He picks up the Red Djinn's dinner bell, uses it to summon dinner, and continues with Peter toward Scare City.

CHAPTER 4

Jack Pumpkinhead trembles before the Scares and turns his head around so he will not have to see their frightening faces. He remembers the magic dinner bell and rings it repeatedly so Peter can throw food and dishes at the attackers. Afterward, he helps search for a way out and worries that the pirate sack may swallow them.

CHAPTER 5

Jack questions whether Snif is carnivorous before Peter releases him and later rides in front of Peter on Snif's back. He helps carry the dinner bell and is warned to hold onto his head during the flight.

CHAPTER 6

Jack Pumpkinhead travels with Peter on Snif's back and suggests that Belfaygor throw his beard over his shoulder so it will grow away from his feet. Belfaygor appoints Jack the Royal Bearer of the beard, but Jack says he cannot take the beard with him to the Emerald City.

CHAPTER 7

Jack Pumpkinhead praises Peter's past victories and joins the plan to rescue Shirley Sunshine. He uses the magic dinner bell to summon meals and later cuts Belfaygor's growing beard while Peter comforts the shrunken Snif.

CHAPTER 8

Jack Pumpkinhead helps tend Belfaygor's beard by snipping it and later offers to stand guard while the others sleep. He worries about crossing the chasm and about the Baffleburghers impaling the group.

CHAPTER 10

Jack Pumpkinhead cuts Belfaygor's beard and rakes the clippings into a huge mound. He reluctantly crosses the chasm and follows the others through the dangerous tunnel, where his head comes off and rolls into a hollow before Peter replaces it. He helps climb into the Baffleburg tower but complains about the many steps.

CHAPTER 11

Jack Pumpkinhead confronts Mogodore and claims that Peter and Snif are powerful magicians. He rings the Red Jinn's bell, causing the little slave, Biggen, and Little to disappear, but Mogodore's men capture him. Jack is thrown across the table and beheaded, and his pumpkin head rolls through a curtained doorway.

CHAPTER 12

Jack's head is recovered in a cellar after rolling down a spiraling passage. Jack tells Peter, Snif, and Belfaygor about the guarded forbidden flagon and remains behind while they enter the lost labyrinth.

CHAPTER 13

Jack's Pumpkinhead waits above the cellarway and welcomes Peter, Snif, and Belfaygor when they return with the Forbidden Flagon. Peter places Jack's head back on his body, and Jack dances before preparing to leave with the group. He carries the dinner bell and rides on Snif's back.

CHAPTER 14

Jack Pumpkinhead travels with Peter, Belfaygor, and Snif and jokes about opening the sack and using the forbidden objects against Mogodore. In Swing City, the acrobats toss his head around like a ball, and he later needs help placing it back on his body. He escapes the net and continues toward the Emerald City.

CHAPTER 15

Jack Pumpkinhead escapes the falling pirate's sack by diving to earth with the flagon, but his wooden left leg breaks off at the knee. Believing Peter, Belfaygor, and Snif are trapped in the sack, he retrieves it and resolves to take it to the Wizard of Oz. He summons the Red Jinn's slave with the silver bell, but vanishes when the slave disappears.

CHAPTER 16

Jack Pumpkinhead arrives at the Palace of the Red Jinn carrying his broken leg under his arm and is struck on the head by visitors as part of their greeting custom. He asks the Red Jinn for help rescuing his friends and saving Oz, and the Red Jinn magically restores his leg. Jack then suddenly disappears from the palace before he can be sent back to Oz.

CHAPTER 18

Jack Pumpkinhead arrives through a window, carried to the castle by the magic belt while holding the forbidden flagon. He removes his head, sets it on a chair, and throws the flagon at Mogodore, breaking it against the baron's nose.

CHAPTER 19

Jack Pumpkinhead returns in time to use the forbidden flagon against Mogodore the Mighty and his forces. He explains that he must tell a long story, then prepares to release three brave comrades from enchantment by following the Red Jinn's advice.

CHAPTER 20

Jack Pumpkinhead shakes the pirate sack to release its prisoners, accidentally filling the banquet room with Scares before shaking it again and producing Peter, Snif, and Belfaygor. He explains that the Red Jinn advised him to remove his head before throwing the forbidden flagon and to turn the sack inside out and shake it three times.
