Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, Briseis is taken from Achilles by Agamemnon, later returned among his gifts, and grieves for Patroclus while recalling his promise to marry her. In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Briseis is taken among the spoils of cities attacked by Achilles and awarded to him as a prize. In Heroides and Amores, Briseis writes to Achilles in grief, accusing him of not reclaiming her and begging him to take her back or let her die. In The Amores, or Amours, Briseis is identified as the captive who inflamed Achilles. In The Library, Briseis is Chryses’ daughter, captured at Lyrnessus and made Achilles’ concubine, whose seizure causes Achilles to refuse to fight until she is returned. In The Fall of Troy, Briseis mourns Achilles, saying he treated her like a wife rather than a captive, and lays her shorn hair on his corpse as a final gift.

Briseis in The Iliad

Homer · 749 to 724 BC · the work’s page · read it where Briseis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Briseis is a captive woman, daughter of Briseus, taken after Achilles sacked Lyrnessus and awarded to Achilles as his prize. Achilles cherishes her, but Agamemnon takes her from his tent through heralds as compensation for surrendering Chryseis, an injury Achilles refuses to forgive. Agamemnon later offers her return and swears he has not had relations with her. Returned among his gifts, Briseis finds Patroclus dead, grieves for him, and recalls his promise that Achilles would marry her and take her to Phthia.

Achilles allows Patroclus to hand her over, while declaring that the act will have consequences if the Achaeans later need his help.

Briseis in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · about 24 to 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where Briseis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Briseis, a captive woman from Lyrnesus and Achilles’ beloved slave, loses her husband and three brothers when her homeland is destroyed. Taken captive by Achilles, she is later removed from him. In grief she writes to accuse him of not reclaiming her, recounts her losses, and begs him to take her back or let her die, offering humble service in Phthia.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

III

Briseis writes to Achilles in grief, accusing him of failing to reclaim her and of allowing her to remain separated from him. She recalls the destruction of Lyrnesus, the deaths of her three brothers and husband, and her captivity, then begs Achilles either to take her back or to let her die. She offers to follow him as a captive or serve him humbly if he returns to Phthia.
