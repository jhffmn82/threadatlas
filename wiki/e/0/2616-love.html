love in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

Love is a lasting affection that begins suddenly, painfully, and irresistibly, binding the narrator to one beloved. It cannot be ruled and may be awakened by many kinds of beauty or character. Resistance, denial, fear, hope, and prolonged frustration strengthen it, while easy indulgence can make it weary. The narrator accepts love’s servitude, seeks safe meetings with his mistress, rejects other damsels, and ends fearing that poems about Corinna have made his once-exclusive love shared with many others.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

ELEGY II.

The narrator's love is sudden, painful, and constant. He says that resisting it would intensify the flame, so he accepts its servitude and declares that a thousand other damsels have no charm for him compared with the beloved mistress.

ELEGY II.

Ovid asks that Bagous enable him and the mistress to love and meet safely. He insists that their request is harmless because they are not mixing poisons or carrying drawn swords.

ELEGY III.

Love is presented as a force that the poet cannot rule or resist. It can be awakened by any of a hundred forms of beauty, character, talent, age, or behavior.

ELEGY XIX.

Love is said to become lasting through resistance, fear, hope, and prolonged experience. Too much indulgence and easy access make love weary, like sweet food that cloys the appetite.

ELEGY XII.

The speaker began by loving Corinna alone. He now fears that his love must be shared with many others because his poems have publicized her.
