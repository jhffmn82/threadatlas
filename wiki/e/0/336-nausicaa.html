Nausicaa in The Odyssey

Homer · about 632 to 599 BC · the work’s page · read it where Nausicaa first appears

Abstract written by a model

Nausicaa is a lovely Phaeacian princess, daughter of Alcinous, protected by Minerva and admired by the best young men among her people. Following Minerva’s instruction, she travels with her maids by waggon to wash clothes. When Ulysses approaches, she stands firm rather than fleeing, gives him clothes, oil, food, and aid, and directs him to her father’s house, saving him from danger. Though worried that Phaeacians will gossip if she is seen with him, she returns home with her brothers. Ulysses credits her rescue, defends her before Alcinous, and calls her his guardian angel; she admires him and asks not to be forgotten.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK VI

Nausicaa, daughter of Alcinous, follows Minerva’s instruction and asks for a waggon and mules to carry clothes to the washing cisterns. She stands firm when Ulysses approaches, gives him clothes, oil, food, and aid, and explains how he can reach her father’s house. She is concerned that the Phaeacians may gossip if they see her traveling with a strange man.

BOOK VII

Nausicaa returns to her father’s house with her brothers after washing clothes, and her servants prepare her supper. Ulysses says that she found him on the shore, gave him bread and wine, had him washed, and provided the clothes he is wearing. Alcinous reproves her for not bringing Ulysses directly to the palace, but Ulysses defends her.

Alcinous’s daughter Nausicaa washes family clothes, finds shipwrecked Ulysses, gives food and clothing, directs him to the palace, and secures her parents’ reception.
