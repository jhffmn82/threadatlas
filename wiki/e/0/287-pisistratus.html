Pisistratus

person · 1 work · 6 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Nestor’s son Pisistratus, my friend, the only unmarried son now left him

Abstract written by a model

Pisistratus, Nestor’s only remaining unmarried son, serves as Telemachus’s companion and escort on his journey to Lacedaemon. He helps prepare a heifer for sacrifice, travels and sleeps alongside Telemachus, explains his plight to Menelaus, and recalls grief for Antilochus. He later helps Telemachus depart, drives him to Pylos, leaves him at his ship, and returns to report to Nestor.

Pisistratus is in one work on this site, The Odyssey. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Pisistratus in The Odyssey

Homer · about 632 to 599 BC · the work’s page · read it where Pisistratus first appears

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

As he spoke the sun set and it came on dark, whereon Minerva said,

Pisistratus sleeps in the room over the gateway with Telemachus. He helps prepare the heifer for sacrifice, drives the chariot carrying Telemachus, and spends the night with him at Pherae before continuing the journey.

BOOK IV

Pisistratus accompanies Telemachus to Lacedaemon as Nestor’s escort. He explains Telemachus’s difficult situation and urges Menelaus to end the weeping during supper, drawing on his own grief for his brother Antilochus.

BOOK XV

Pisistratus helps Telemachus prepare to leave Lacedaemon and drives him to Pylos. At Telemachus’s request, he leaves him at the ship and returns to tell his father, Nestor, what has happened.
