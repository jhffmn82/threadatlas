Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, Idomeneus is a grey-haired Achaean warrior, captain and ruler of the Cretans, and leads them in battle. In The Library, Idomeneus is a king of Crete who leads his army at the siege of Troy, later being exiled and settling in Sallentine land. In The Fall of Troy, Idomeneus is an elder-born, cunning Danaan warrior aiding Atreus' sons; Thetis entrusts him with a chariot and swift horses.

Idomeneus in The Iliad

Homer · 749 to 724 BC · the work’s page · read it where Idomeneus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Idomeneus is a grey-haired Achaean warrior, captain and ruler of the Cretans, son of Deucalion, and trusted comrade of Meriones. He leads the Cretans in battle with his ox-hide and bronze round shield. After seeing Machaon wounded, he tells Nestor to carry him away because a physician is highly valuable. Urged by Neptune, he returns to battle, joins Meriones, attacks the weak left wing, rallies the Danaans, panics the Trojans, kills Erymas, Othryoneus, Asius, and Alcathous, and fights on despite fatigue and missiles.

Idomeneus in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Idomeneus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Idomeneus is a king of Crete who rules there and leads his army at the siege of Troy. Returning from Troy, he vows to Neptune to sacrifice whoever first meets him. Accounts differ on whether he sacrifices or only attempts to sacrifice his son, and on his expulsion: he is banished after a pestilence or expelled by Leucus. Exiled from Crete, he settles in Sallentine land. His legends are cited as possible evidence of sacrificial-vow custom.
