Death in Alcestis

Euripides · -0437

Death is the headsman of the pit, a black, winged lord of corpses and Lord of the Dead who governs mortality and claims the dead. He comes to the Castle of Admetus to claim Alcestis as his destined prey, rejects Apollo's request to delay her death, and enters to place his drawn sword upon her. He holds Alcestis among the dead and binds Admetus to sorrow, but Heracles fights him, seizes him as he flees, and recovers Alcestis from his power.

Death in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Death is a masculine priest, king, and prince of the dead, serving Pluto as bearer of the dead to the underworld. Sable-vested and armed with a sword used in funerary rites, he claims Alcestis at Admetus’s palace, saying her death is owed to him. He cuts and consecrates her hair, dedicates her to the underworld gods, and takes her from Admetus toward Pluto’s house. At Alcestis’s tomb, Heracles ambushes and fights Death, defeats him, and compels Alcestis’s release so that she may return to Admetus.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

ALCESTIS.

Death comes to Admetus's palace to claim Alcestis, arguing with Apollo that she is the second person whose death he is owed. He says that he will perform the rites for Alcestis and consecrate her hair with his sword before taking her to the house of Pluto.

CHOR. Desist, for the present calamity is sufficient; and do not, O son,

Death is described as the sable-vested king of the departed, whom Hercules intends to seize near Alcestis's tomb. Hercules believes that Death may be drinking libations there and plans to compel him to release Alcestis.

ADMETUS, CHORUS.

Death has taken Alcestis from Admetus and deprived him of his wife. Heracles says that he seized Death by ambush and fought him at Alcestis's tomb in order to bring her back.

notes

Death is treated as the masculine personification corresponding to Θάνατος, for which Orcus was used as a Latin equivalent. Death cuts Alcestis’ hair and consecrates her to the dead, requiring a contrary ceremony before she can return to Admetus’ society.
