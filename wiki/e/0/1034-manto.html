Manto

person · 2 works · 13 quoted facts · 7 connections
Also called: Manto the daughter of Teiresias, Manto’s
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Manto is sent by the Epigoni to Delphi as spoil, follows Apollo's oracle to meet and marry Rhacius, comes with him to Colophon, and weeps over Thebes's destruction.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Manto is captured after Thebes falls, taken to Apollo at Delphi, sent to Asia Minor to found a colony, marries Rhacius at Claros, and becomes Mopsus's parent.

Manto in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Manto, daughter of Teiresias, is sent by the Epigoni to Delphi as spoil. Following Apollo’s oracle, she meets and marries Rhacius, comes with him to Colophon, and weeps there over Thebes’s destruction.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE EPIGONI

Manto, the daughter of Teiresias, was sent to Delphi by the Epigoni as a first fruit of their spoil. Following an oracle of Apollo, she met and married Rhacius, then came to Colophon and wept over the destruction of her country.

Manto in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Manto, daughter of Tiresias, is captured after Thebes falls and taken to Apollo at Delphi. Sent to Asia Minor to found a colony, she reaches Claros in Colophonia, explains the captives' purpose to Rhacius, marries him, and becomes Mopsus's parent.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER III.

Manto was among the captives taken to Apollo at Delphi after the capture of Thebes. At Claros, Rhacius learned from her who the captives were and why they had come, then married her.
