Joseph Stangerson in A Study in Scarlet

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1886

Joseph Stangerson is a young Mormon, son of an Elder, formerly of the Chosen People and associated with the Latter Day Saints. He has four wives and is described as cunning, guarded, and quietly reserved. In the pursuit of the Ferriers, he shoots John Ferrier; Lucy is ultimately assigned to Enoch J. Drebber rather than him. Stangerson later becomes Drebber’s private secretary, paid servant, and travel companion, boards with him at Charpentier’s establishment, and is linked to him through a letter, a book bearing his name, and Drebber’s purse. After Drebber’s death, Stangerson’s whereabouts are initially unknown, but he remains indoors at Halliday’s Private Hotel, apparently fearing danger. Jefferson Hope confronts him there and offers poisoned pills. Stangerson attacks Hope and is stabbed to the heart in self-defence. He is found dead in his locked room in nightdress, with a deep wound, “RACHE” written in blood, and the purse, telegram, novel, pipe, water, and pills nearby. His death provides the pills sought in the investigation.

Narrative, section by section

7 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER III.

Joseph Stangerson’s name is found on the fly-leaf of a pocket edition of Boccaccio’s Decameron in Enoch J. Drebber’s possession. A letter addressed to Stangerson is also found among Drebber’s belongings, and Tobias Gregson has sent inquiries about him.

CHAPTER VI.

Joseph Stangerson is described as Enoch J. Drebber’s private secretary and travel companion. G. Lestrade reports that Stangerson was murdered at Halliday’s Private Hotel about six o’clock in the morning.

CHAPTER VII.

Joseph Stangerson is found dead in his locked hotel room, wearing his nightdress, with a deep stab wound in his left side. The word “RACHE” is written in blood above him, and his room contains Drebber’s purse, a telegram, a novel, a pipe, a glass of water, and two pills.

CHAPTER V.

Stangerson is part of the party that pursues the Ferriers and shoots John Ferrier. He loses Lucy to Drebber in the marriage decision, later becomes Drebber's private secretary, and is pursued with him by Jefferson Hope.

CHAPTER VI.

Joseph Stangerson travels with Enoch Drebber and is described as Drebber's secretary and paid servant. Jefferson Hope says that Stangerson also perished by Hope's hand, though this unit gives no detailed account of that killing.

“That was how Enoch Drebber came to his end. All I had to do then was

Stangerson stayed indoors at Halliday’s Private Hotel after Enoch Drebber disappeared, apparently suspecting danger. When Jefferson Hope confronted him and offered him poisoned pills, Stangerson attacked Hope and was stabbed to the heart.

CHAPTER VII.

Joseph Stangerson is murdered by Jefferson Hope in an unexpected incident during the investigation. His death enables Holmes to obtain the pills whose existence he had already surmised.
