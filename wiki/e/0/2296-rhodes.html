Abstract written by a model

In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Rhodes is described as a sea-girt island, the mythical child of Aphrodite and bride of Helios, divided among Kameiros, Ialysos, and Lindos. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Rhodes is described as a maritime city that endured floods and attacks, engaged in shifting alliances, and defended its walls and harbour. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Rhodes is described as an island that later became a city and was involved in colonization, political revolts, and a siege. In The Library, Rhodes is described as the place where Althaemenes and Apemosyne arrived after leaving Crete and where Althaemenes unknowingly killed Catreus. In Description of Greece, Rhodes is described as the home of Diagoras and his family and as a place that revolted from the Lacedæmonians and joined the Athenians and the great king.

Rhodes in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · 497 BC · the work’s page · read it where Rhodes first appears

Abstract written by a model

Rhodes is a sea-girt island, mythically Aphrodite’s child and Helios’s bride, emerging from the sea under Helios’s rule and receiving gold and handicraft. It is divided among Kameiros, Ialysos, and Lindos, whose confederacy was founded by Argive Dorians.

Rhodes in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Rhodes first appears

Abstract written by a model

Rhodes is a maritime city, built in the form of a theatre, with Digma, temples of Bacchus and Esculapius, and statues of Ptolemy, Cassander, and Lysimachus. Its shape worsened a flood: water ran to lower parts, more than five hundred died, and citizens fled upward until a broken wall spared the city from total destruction. Politically, it joined the Athenian confederacy after defecting from Lacedaemonian rule, later revolted from Athens, and was brought under Theban obedience by Epaminondas. Wealthy, well governed, and sustained by trade with Egypt, it favored Ptolemy and resisted Demetrius when he demanded hostages and harbour access. Citizens, inhabitants, and foreigners defended its walls and harbour through prolonged attacks, aided by Ptolemy, Cassander, and Lysimachus. Peace left it under its own laws without a garrison; it then honored Ptolemy and rebuilt its theatre and walls more gloriously.

Rhodes in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Rhodes first appears

Abstract written by a model

Rhodes is an island, later also a city formed by incorporating inhabitants from Lindus, Ialysus, and Camirus. It was first inhabited by the Telchines, then associated with Rhode, and consecrated to Sol after Sol dried its primordial moisture and cleared the Telchines’ flood. Before Tlepolemus arrived, Hellenes inhabited it; a colony led by Triopas also settled there. Tlepolemus divided the island into three parts and founded Lindus, Ialysus, and Camirus, while remaining sons of Sol built Achaia and dwelt at Ialysia. Later, Rhodes refused the Peloponnesian fleet, revolted from the Lacedaemonians, received Conon, and helped seize grain ships. It then defected from Athens amid factional conflict, expulsions, and a siege involving Rhodian exiles.
