Thebais

work · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: THE THEBAID, The Thebaid, The epic poem called the Thebais, Thebaid

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, The Thebaid is an epic of seven thousand verses that begins by singing of parched Argos, unfolds through Oedipus's curses, Adrastus's flight, battle and Parthenopaeus's death, and ends with seven dead buried. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Thebais is an epic poem about the war between the Argives and Thebans, specifically the Epigoni’s campaign against Thebes; considered later than the Iliad and Odyssey, it includes Periclymenus killing Parthenopæus.

THE THEBAID

The Thebaid was described as an epic of seven thousand verses, beginning with a song of parched Argos. It tells of Oedipus's curses against his sons, Adrastus's flight from Thebes, the burial of seven dead, and the battle in which Parthenopaeus was killed.
