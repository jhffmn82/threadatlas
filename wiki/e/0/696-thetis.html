Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, Thetis is the silver-footed sea-goddess and grieving mother of Achilles who seeks Jove’s aid, obtains armor from Vulcan, preserves Patroclus’s body, and persuades Achilles to accept Hector’s ransom. In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Thetis is Nereus’s daughter and Achilles’s mother who marries Peleus, tests their children in a cauldron, warns Achilles about Memnon, and carries his body to White Island. In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Thetis is Nereus’s daughter and sea-goddess who marries Peleus after a prophecy about her son, becomes the mother of Achilles and Neoptolemos, and rules over Phthia. In Hecuba and other plays, Thetis is a goddess, wife of Peleus, and mother of Achilles who counsels Peleus, orders Achilles’s son buried at Delphi, sends Andromache and her son to Molossia, and makes Peleus immortal. In The Argonautica, Thetis is a Nereid, wife of Peleus, and mother of Achilles who tries to immortalize Achilles, promises Hera aid, summons the Nereids, and guides the Argo through the Wandering Rocks. In The Library, Thetis is a Nereid and sea-goddess, daughter of Nereus, wife of Peleus, and mother of Achilles and Neoptolemus who attempts to make Achilles immortal, protects him, and aids the Argonauts. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Thetis is a beautiful Nereid, wife of Peleus, and mother of Achilles who resists Peleus through shape-shifting, marries him after capture, and intercedes for him. In The Fall of Troy, Thetis is a sea-queen, chief of the Nereids, Peleus’s unwilling bride, and Achilles’s grieving mother who provides funeral prizes, armor, and divine protection for his descendants.

Thetis in The Iliad

Homer · 749 to 724 BC · the work’s page · read it where Thetis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Thetis is an immortal, silver-footed marine goddess and sea-nymph, daughter of Nereus, the old sea-god or old merman. She is reared by Juno, saved by Vulcan in his childhood, and married to Peleus against her will. Above all, she is the loving, grieving mother of the mortal Achilles. When Achilles suffers, she rises from the sea to comfort him, seeks Jove’s aid, and persuades him to favor the Trojans until Achilles receives his due. After Patroclus dies, she foretells that Achilles’ death will follow Hector’s, obtains splendid armour from Vulcan, brings it to Achilles, and preserves Patroclus’s body with ambrosia and nectar. Finally, summoned by Jove, she tells Achilles to accept ransom for Hector’s body, and he obeys.

Above all she is Achilles’ mother: splendid, honored among the immortals, yet defined by love, grief, and care for her son and his descendants. Achilles recalls that she sheltered Dionysus, welcomed Hephaestus after his fall, and released Zeus from bonds. The gods prayed that she would bear a son to Peleus, and she tells Achilles that Apollo’s shafts will kill him before the Scaean Gates. After Achilles’ death, she mourns with the Nereids, throws herself on his body, and protests to Zeus that Achilles received glory but only a brief life. She comes from the sea to provide and distribute prizes for his funeral games, sighing when Aias reminds her of Achilles. She lays out Achilles’ divinely wrought arms for the warrior who recovered his body, later gives the armor to Odysseus, and sends sea wind to consume Aias’s body. As grandmother and divine protector of Neoptolemus, she removes his weariness by enchantment, aids the Achaeans’ voyage, and watches his victories with joy tempered by continuing grief for Achilles.

Thetis mourns Achilles beside the Nereids and later casts herself upon his body. She complains to Zeus that he made Achilles glorious but gave him only a brief life, and she intends to remind Zeus of her suffering and accuse him of failing her son.

Thetis comes forth from the sea to the Argives and sets down prizes for Achilles’ funeral games. She rewards the competitors, giving four handmaids to Aias son of Telamon and Diomedes, and presenting silver bowls to Acamas and Epeius.

Then for the archery-test Oileus' son

Thetis distributes prizes throughout the athletic contests. She gives Teucer the arms of Troilus, Aias the arms of Memnon, Agapenor the battle-gear of Cycnus, Euryalus a silver oil-flask, Menelaus a golden cup, and other prizes to the racers; Aias's stature makes her think of her dear son, and she sighs.

Thetis lays Achilles' divinely wrought arms before the Argives and offers them to the mightiest warrior who rescued Achilles' dead body. She later plunges into the sea with the Nereids, grieving for Achilles.

Thetis in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Thetis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Thetis is an immortal sea-nymph, daughter of Nereus and Doris, dwelling in the ocean. Her beauty leads Jupiter to seek her, but a prophecy that her son will surpass his father causes him to decree her marriage to Peleus. She becomes Achilles’ mother and later answers his grief, securing Vulcan’s armor for him.

Chapter XV

Thetis is the daughter of Nereus and Doris and is so beautiful that Jupiter seeks to marry her. After learning that her son will be greater than his father, Jupiter decrees that she marry a mortal; she becomes Peleus’s wife and the mother of Achilles.
