Eurytus

person · 2 works · 6 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Εὐρύτῳ, Εὔρυτον, Εὔρυτος

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Eurytus is the father of Iole and two or four sons and is linked to Heracles sacking Oechalia for Iole’s sake. In The Library, Eurytus is prince and king of Oechalia and a general of Elis with Cteatus; he offers Iole to an archery victor but refuses her to Hercules, suspects Hercules of cattle theft, rejects compensation for Iphitus’s murder, and is later killed by Hercules, who takes Oechalia.

Eurytus in The Library

Apollodorus · about AD 70 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Eurytus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Eurytus, prince and king of Oechalia and a general of Elis with Cteatus, offers Iole to an archery victor but refuses her to Hercules, fearing for his children. He suspects Hercules of cattle theft and rejects compensation for Iphitus’s murder. Hercules later attacks, kills Eurytus and his sons, and takes Oechalia.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE LIBRARY, IIT. v. r1-12

Eurytus, prince of Oechalia, offers his daughter Iole as a prize to whoever can defeat him and his sons in archery. Although Hercules proves superior in archery, Eurytus refuses to give him Iole because he fears Hercules may again kill his children. Eurytus suspects Hercules of stealing cattle from Euboea, and later refuses to accept compensation for Iphitus’s murder.

THE LIBRARY, II. vu. 6-7

Eurytus is the ruler of Oechalia. Hercules attacks him, kills him and his sons, and takes Oechalia.
