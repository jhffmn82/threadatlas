Saw-Horse in The Marvelous Land of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1904-06

Saw-Horse begins as a wooden structure used for sawing logs: a tree-trunk body with four straight wooden legs, a branch tail, knotty eyes, a chopped-out mouth, and no neck. Tip brings it to life with the Powder of Life and Mombi’s spell. At first it is stiff, awkward, earless, and unable to understand much; after Tip fastens bark ears to its head and teaches it commands, it hears, obeys, and carries Jack Pumpkinhead. It never sleeps and is left to watch the camp. It becomes a fast, living wooden horse with bulging eyes, green spectacles, and a pacing gait in which the legs on each side move together. It floats and paddles on water, carries Jack to the Emerald City, and is later kept in the royal treasury, fed sawdust, and used to carry Tip, Jack, the Scarecrow, and the Soldier during an escape. Its buoyant wooden body lets it cross rivers, and it continues traveling with the group despite quarrelling with Jack after being called hoarse. The Saw-Horse is brave, fearless, useful, quick to learn, and possessed of untiring energy: its wooden limbs can run for days. It is also considered by the party to be especially stupid. It has no pain when a leg breaks in a rabbit hole, but its pride is injured; it cannot continue until it receives a reshaped wooden replacement leg, initially too long and disliked by it. It helps carry sofas, fights Jackdaws by kicking, and offers a simple counting solution. In the end, it carries Glinda while pursuing Mombi’s Griffin form, running until the Griffin is exhausted while it remains vigorous. It is Princess Ozma’s property and wears golden shoes to protect its wooden legs from wear.

Narrative, section by section

12 sections; each heading opens the text

The Marvelous Land of Oz

The Saw-horse appears in the chapter list as a character or object that is awakened.

Tip Makes an Experiment in Magic

The saw-horse is a wooden structure made from a tree trunk, branches, knots, and four wooden legs, used for sawing logs. After Tip sprinkles it with the Powder of Life and repeats Mombi’s spell, it moves, stretches its legs, yawns, and shakes powder from its back.

The Awakening of the Saw-horse

The Saw-Horse awakens alive but has stiff, awkward legs, no ears, and difficulty understanding its surroundings. After Tip gives it ears and teaches it commands, it agrees to obey and carries Jack Pumpkinhead while walking in a rocking, side-to-side manner. It cannot sleep, so Tip leaves it standing to watch for anyone who might disturb the camp.

Jack Pumpkinhead’s Ride to the Emerald City

The Saw-Horse floats across the river and moves its legs like oars. After Tip commands it to trot, it races along the road and carries Jack Pumpkinhead to the Emerald City. It obeys Jack’s command to stop, receives green spectacles, kicks a barking green dog, and is led around to the rear of the palace.

The Scarecrow Plans an escape

The Saw-Horse is brought from the royal treasury after being fed a heaping peck of sawdust. It carries Tip, Jack Pumpkinhead, the Scarecrow, and the Soldier through the palace gateway and charges through the invading girls, a fruit cart, several men, and the new Guardian of the Gate. It then races west and leaps into a wide river with its riders.

The Journey to the Tin Woodman

The Saw-Horse floats because its wooden body is buoyant, paddles toward the opposite bank, and carries the travelers onward. It becomes offended by Jack’s remark that it sounds hoarse and quarrels with him, but continues the journey. It does not sleep and carries the group until Tip stops it for the night.

A Nickel-Plated Emperor

The Saw-Horse carries Jack Pumpkinhead and accompanies Tip and the Scarecrow to the Winkies. Nick Chopper accepts it as a comrade after learning that it is alive and useful, and the Saw-Horse claims that it learns quickly. It is inspected before the group sets out toward the Emerald City.

Mr. H. M. Woggle-Bug, T. E.

The Saw-Horse falls into a rabbit hole, sending Jack into the air, and one of its legs breaks when it is pulled free. It feels no pain but says that its pride is injured because its anatomy is brittle.

A Highly Magnified History

The Saw-Horse has a broken leg and cannot travel until it is repaired. It receives Pumpkinhead’s reshaped wooden leg, though it complains that the new leg is a disgrace and insults Pumpkinhead.

The Astonishing Flight of the Gump

The Saw-Horse helps Tip carry the sofas to the roof. When the Thing comes to life, the Saw-Horse topples onto his back with his legs waving helplessly.

Dr. Nikidik’s Famous Wishing Pills

The Saw-Horse fights the Jackdaws by kicking them with its wooden legs. It suggests beginning the count at one-half, providing the simple solution for counting seventeen by twos.

The Transformation of Old Mombi

The Saw-Horse carries Glinda in pursuit of Mombi’s Griffin form. It runs with untiring energy for so long that the Griffin becomes exhausted, while the Saw-Horse remains vigorous.
