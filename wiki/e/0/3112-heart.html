heart in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04

The heart is sought by the Tin Woodman, who lost his original heart when his body became tin and believes a heart brings love and happiness. Though Oz says hearts can cause unhappiness, the Tin Woodman insists on having one. Oz gives him a very kind heart, made of silk and stuffed with sawdust, and places it in his breast.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter V

The Tin Woodman lost his heart when his body was replaced with tin, and he consequently lost his love for the Munchkin girl. He believes a heart is the key to happiness and plans to ask the Great Oz for one.

Chapter VI

The Tin Woodman believes he has no heart and plans to ask Oz for one. He suggests that having no heart might prevent someone from being a coward, while his concern over killing a beetle shows his care for living creatures.

Chapter XV

Oz claims that having a heart often makes people unhappy and suggests that the Tin Woodman is fortunate not to have one. The Tin Woodman rejects this view, and Oz agrees to give him a heart.

Chapter XVI

Oz's heart for the Tin Woodman is made entirely of silk and stuffed with sawdust. Oz places it in the Tin Woodman's breast and assures him that it is a kind heart.
