Abstract written by a model

The ship belongs to Telemachus and serves his voyage with a crew of twenty men, first for travel to Pylos and then in search of Ulysses. Mentes says it lies in the harbour Rheithron beneath Neritum, while Minerva advises Telemachus to obtain a ship and later obtains one from Noemon son of Phronius. After sunset she brings it into the water, equips it with usual tackle, and stations it at the harbour’s end. It waits at Pylos with the crew and Menelaus’s gifts aboard; Minerva sends a favorable wind, and it sails toward Ithaca. It carries the crew, armour, and presents from Pylos to Ithaca, reaches Ithaca, is drawn onto land, and is unloaded after Telemachus remains in the country.

Mentes says that his ship lies off the town in the harbour Rheithron, under the wooded mountain Neritum. Minerva later advises Telemachus to obtain the best ship he can find for his search for Ulysses.

BOOK II

Minerva obtains a ship from Noemon son of Phronius and brings it into the water after sunset. She equips it with the tackle ships generally carry and stations it at the end of the harbor for Telemachus's voyage.

As he spoke the sun set and it came on dark, whereon Minerva said,

Minerva and Telemachus initially plan to return to the ship, but Nestor persuades Telemachus to stay at his house. Minerva returns to the ship to keep its young crew in good heart, and two men remain in charge while the crew attends the sacrifice.

Noemon lent Telemachus his ship so that Telemachus could travel to Pylos. The suitors use another ship for their pursuit and ambush.

BOOK XV

Telemachus’s ship waits at Pylos with the crew and Menelaus’s gifts aboard. Minerva sends a strong favorable wind, and the ship sails past Crouni and Chalcis toward Pheae, Elis, and the flying islands.

Telemachus's ship reaches Ithaca, is drawn onto the land, and has its armour and presents removed. A servant is sent to tell Penelope that the ship has returned while Telemachus remains in the country.
