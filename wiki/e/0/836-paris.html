  • In Heroides and Amores, Paris is a Phrygian prince and judge of the goddesses who chooses Venus, pursues and carries off the married Helen, and violates Menelaus's hospitality.
  • In The Trojan Women of Euripides, Paris is the dead Trojan prince whose love for Helen causes Troy's destruction.
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Paris is the Phrygian prince who chooses Venus in the judgment, takes Helen to Troy, causes the Trojan War, and kills Achilles.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Paris is the herdsman who judges the goddesses, crosses the sea to Helen, and takes her while Menelaus is away, causing the Trojan War.
  • In The Rhesus of Euripides, Paris is Hector's brother and Troy's general who trusts Athena, brings her to Troy, and returns to his post after seeking Hector.
  • In The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies, Paris is the Trojan prince who robs Helen from the house of Atreus and brings war and destruction upon Troy.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Alexandrus judges Aphrodite fairest, sails to Sparta, carries Helen and treasures away, and returns to celebrate his marriage.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Paris kills Achilles together with Apollo.
  • In The Iliad, Alexandrus is Helen's Trojan husband and an archer who retreats from Menelaus, is rescued by Venus, and fights the Danaans.
  • In The Library, Alexander is the later name of Paris, who judges the goddesses, chooses Aphrodite, takes Helen to Troy, and dies after being wounded by Philoctetes.
  • In The Library, Paris is Priam and Hecuba's son who was exposed on Ida, raised after defending flocks, and later carried a phantom Helen to Troy.
  • In The Library, infant Paris is Priam and Hecuba's exposed son who is nursed by a bear and raised by Agelaus.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Paris is the cowardly ravisher who brings Helen to Troy, causes the Trojan War, and kills Achilles with an arrow guided by Apollo.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Paris is depicted in a painting as a beardless youth clapping his hands like a rustic to attract Penthesilea's attention.
  • In The Fall of Troy, Paris is a Trojan archer and warrior who fights for Troy, is mortally wounded by Philoctetes, and dies after Oenone refuses to heal him.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Paris is the beautiful shepherd who chooses Venus, elopes with Helen, causes the Trojan War, kills Achilles, and dies from his wounds.

Paris in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Paris is a Phrygian and Priamid, son and descendant of Priam, of the royal line and descended through ancestry from Jupiter and Laomedon. He recalls an earlier life as a poor slave and shepherd on Ida, husband to Oenone; after his recognition as Priam’s son, he abandons her and later has a new wife. He presents himself as noble, wealthy, handsome, youthful, and skilled with weapons, archery, horses, and contests, possessing ships, men, and the strength for war. His defining divine role is judge of Venus, Juno, and Minerva. He prefers Helen to the gifts of Juno and Pallas, receives Helen as the alleged prize of the judgment, and is characterized as better suited to Venus than Mars. Paris then writes to Helen and undertakes a voyage to Lacedaemon. As a Trojan guest and stranger in Menelaus’s household, he openly and secretly pursues Helen while she is married: looking at and signaling to her, sharing her drinking place, sending declarations of love, offering gifts, and urging a secret meeting during Menelaus’s absence. Helen finds him handsome, persuasive, and daring, but also rash, immodest, late, and possibly untrustworthy. He is accused of violating hospitality and entering as a guest but acting as an enemy. He commands a prepared Trojan fleet, travels from Lacedaemon, and carries Helen away. His passion for her is steadfast, yet Oenone accuses him of choosing a stolen wife over his homeland and former love, while others call him a beautiful but faithless guest and a lover who took Menelaus’s wife.

Paris in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Paris is a Phrygian prince, son of Priam and Hecuba and brother of Hector, portrayed as a royal swain and wanderer. At Ida he judges the contest among Juno, Minerva, and Venus, choosing Venus as most beautiful and receiving Helen as his reward. He sails to Sparta, where accounts say he takes Helen, or Helen’s phantom, to Troy; he is also described as her youthful paramour. This act causes the Trojan War and is repeatedly linked to Troy’s ruin, the sufferings of the Phrygian women, and calamities in Greece. In the conflict, Venus protects him, while Minerva, disguised as Venus, persuades him to return to his station after he seeks Hector. Paris also kills Achilles with a missile shaft. His story thus moves from the judgment and voyage for Helen to a war whose disasters are attributed to his choice and abduction.

Paris in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

Paris is a Trojan prince and warrior, Hector’s brother, Eurypylus’s kinsman, Helen’s husband, and Oenone’s former husband. An archer fighting for Troy, he is associated in differing accounts with Achilles’s death. He urges the Trojans to seize Achilles’s corpse, is wounded by Aias, and is carried into Troy. He refuses to abandon the war, welcomes and encourages Eurypylus, and later fights beside Iris and Aeneas: wounded by Thoas, he nevertheless kills Phorcys, Mosynus, and Cleolaus and wounds Eetion. In his final battle, Philoctetes’ poisoned arrows strike his wrist and pierce his flank and groin. Paris goes to Mount Ida seeking Oenone’s healing, but she refuses, and he dies before Helen can see him again. Shepherds and others mourn their former comrade and king at his pyre, while the Nymphs condemn his abandonment of Oenone for another bride.
