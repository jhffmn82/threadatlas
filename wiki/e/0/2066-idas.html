Idas

person · 3 works · 9 quoted facts · 7 connections
Also called: Apharcian Idas, Aphareus’ son, impetuous spear, Ἴδας

Abstract written by a model

In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Idas, angered over oxen, strikes Kastor with a bronze spear, faces Polydeukes beside his father’s sepulchre, and is killed by Zeus’s thunderbolt. In The Argonautica, Idas wounds a white-tusked boar, rejects aid from Cypris and omens from doves and hawks, and urges reliance on Enyalius, war, and mighty deeds. In The Library, Idas divides cattle booty by a trick, drives it to Messene, kills Castor, wounds Pollux, and is killed by Zeus’s thunderbolt.

Idas in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Idas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Idas, called a son of Aphareus and in many accounts a child of Poseidon, divides cattle booty by a trick, eating his and Lynceus’s shares to claim the cattle. He drives them to Messene, kills Castor after Lynceus reveals him, wounds Pollux, and is killed by Zeus’s thunderbolt.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Of the sons born to Leda Castor practised the art

Idas divided the cattle booty into four parts and declared that the first to eat his share would receive half the booty and the second the remainder. He consumed his own share and his brother’s before the others could act, then drove the cattle to Messene with Lynceus. After Lynceus revealed Castor’s position, Idas killed Castor; while being pursued by Pollux, he struck Pollux on the head with a stone, and Zeus killed Idas with a thunderbolt.
