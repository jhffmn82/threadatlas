Hector's body in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Hector’s body is a dead body whose treatment after Hector’s death centers on Achilles’ dishonour and the gods’ protection. Achilles strips it of armor; Achaeans gather around and spear it. Its feet are pierced and tied to Achilles’ chariot, which drags it toward the ships with its head in the dust. Achilles lays it beside Patroclus’s bier, threatens it with dogs, drags it around Patroclus’s tomb, and leaves it near his tent. Venus prevents dogs from tearing it, while Apollo protects it from heat and disfigurement; after twelve days, the immortals keep it fresh, unwasted, and free from worms. Priam ransoms it, and Achilles releases it. The body is washed, anointed, clothed, carried on a bier by mules to Troy, displayed for mourning, burned, placed in a golden urn, and buried beneath a barrow.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XXII.

After Hector dies, Achilles strips the blood-stained armor from his shoulders. The Achaeans gather around and repeatedly thrust their spears into the body.

When Achilles had done spoiling Hector of his armour, he stood

Hector’s body is stripped of its armour, pierced through the sinews behind both feet, and fastened to Achilles’ chariot with ox-hide thongs. It is dragged over the ground toward the ships, with its head trailing in the dust.

BOOK XXIII.

Achilles lays Hector's body in the dust beside Patroclus's bier and drags it about, threatening to have dogs devour it. Venus preserves the body from being torn by the dogs, while Apollo protects it from the sun's heat with a dark cloud.

BOOK XXIV.

Achilles drags Hector’s corpse around Patroclus’s tomb and leaves it near his tent. Apollo shields it with his golden aegis so that it is not disfigured, and Jove arranges for Priam to ransom it.

So did he pray, and Jove the lord of counsel heard his prayer.

Hector's body lies at Achilles' tent after twelve days, but it remains fresh, unwasted, and free from worms because the gods have cared for it. Priam asks Achilles to accept the ransom and let him take the body, and Achilles agrees to give it up.

The old man feared him and obeyed. Then the son of Peleus sprang

Hector’s body is washed, anointed, clothed, placed on a bier, and transported by mules from the Achaean camp to Troy. It is displayed for mourning, then burned on a pyre, while its bones are gathered into a golden urn and buried beneath a barrow.

Hector's body in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Hector's body, dragged by Achilles before Troy and around Patroclus's tomb but protected by Apollo, is returned to Priam and cremated, its bones buried in a golden urn.

Achilles fastens Hector's body to his chariot and drags it before Troy and around Patroclus's tomb. Apollo prevents it from being torn or defiled. Achilles later places it on a litter and returns it to Priam.
