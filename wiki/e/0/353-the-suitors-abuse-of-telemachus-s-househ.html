Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

The Odyssey / BOOK I

The suitors abuse Telemachus’s household by persistently eating his sheep and oxen. Minerva intends for Telemachus to confront them before the Achaeans in assembly.

BOOK II

The suitors sacrifice and consume the household's livestock, drink its wine, and repeatedly feast without paying. They refuse to leave until Penelope chooses a husband and mock Telemachus when he announces his intention to seek Ulysses.

BOOK XVI

The suitors waste Ulysses's bread and wine, consume his estate, and behave insolently in his house. They also mistreat strangers and drag the women servants about the house in an unseemly way.

The suitors remain at the house day after day, consuming its oxen, sheep, goats, grain, and wine. Penelope says they give no thought to the waste and that the household lacks Ulysses's protection.

BOOK XVIII

Ulysses tells Amphinomus that the suitors are wasting the estate and dishonoring the wife of a man who is certain to return. He warns that the suitors and Ulysses will not part without bloodshed once Ulysses comes back.

BOOK XXII

Ulysses accuses the suitors of wasting his substance, forcing his women servants to lie with them, courting Penelope while he was alive, and showing no fear of gods or men. Eurymachus admits that they have done great wrong on Ulysses’ lands and in his house.
