Toto in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04

Toto is Dorothy’s little black dog, with long silky hair, small lively black eyes, and a white ribbon around his neck. He plays all day, sees very well in darkness, does not bite Dorothy, and belongs closely with her, becoming unhappy when she is unhappy. During the cyclone he hides under a bed, falls through an open trap door, and Dorothy pulls him back. He accompanies her on her journey, drinking from a brook, barking at birds, alarming the Munchkins, and first growling at the Scarecrow. He stays near Dorothy through dangers, is protected from the Lion and the bees, and later becomes friends with the Cowardly Lion. He remains with Dorothy during captivity, travels by Winged Monkey, and reaches the Emerald City. After tiring in the fields, chasing a kitten makes Dorothy miss the balloon. He crosses the china country, receives a golden collar from the Winkies, and finally returns with Dorothy from the Land of Oz.

Narrative, section by section

8 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter I

Toto is Dorothy's small black dog with long silky hair and lively eyes. He hides under the bed during the cyclone, later falls through the open trap door, and is pulled back into the room by Dorothy.

Chapter III

Toto accompanies Dorothy, drinks from the brook, barks at birds, and attracts the curiosity of the Munchkins, who have never seen a dog. He initially growls at the Scarecrow and suspects there may be rats in the straw, but Dorothy says he never bites.

Chapter VI

Toto stays close to Dorothy when the forest animals growl and runs barking toward the Lion when he becomes an enemy. Dorothy protects him from being bitten, and he later becomes good friends with the Cowardly Lion.

Chapter XII

Toto travels with Dorothy and is protected from the bees by the Scarecrow’s straw. The Winged Monkeys do not harm him while carrying Dorothy to the Witch’s castle. He stays with Dorothy during her captivity and becomes unhappy when she is unhappy.

Chapter XIV

Toto becomes too tired to chase a butterfly while the travelers are lost in the fields. A Winged Monkey carries him, although Toto tries to bite it, and he arrives safely at the Emerald City.

Chapter XVII

Toto runs into the crowd to bark at a kitten just before the balloon launch. Dorothy finds him, picks him up, and runs toward the balloon, but they arrive too late.

Chapter XX

Toto climbs over the wall after the Scarecrow and begins to bark at the strange country, but Dorothy makes him be quiet. He crosses the china country with the others.

Chapter XXIV

Toto returns with Dorothy from the Land of Oz.

Toto in The Road to Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1909

Toto is Dorothy’s little black dog and companion: curious, lively, loyal, and often guided or restrained by her. He belongs to and is fond of Dorothy, communicates by wagging his tail, and travels with her, later returning with her to Kansas by the Magic Belt. He chases butterflies, birds, bees, and chickens, for which Uncle Henry disciplines him; he has more sense than Button-Bright, is known by Button-Bright as a former friend, and is friendly with the Cowardly Lion and Hungry Tiger. At the animals’ table he wears a bib and eats from a silver platter. Toto’s journey begins with the Shaggy Man putting him in a pocket after Toto attacks him. Released, Toto tries unsuccessfully to lead Dorothy home, then follows her and the Shaggy Man. He digs in Button-Bright’s hole, barks and tries to bite a fox-captain, befriends Polychrome, and growls at Allegro da Capo. Invisible among rocks, he barks at the Scoodlers but becomes frightened and crouches between Dorothy’s feet. He crosses the Deadly Desert in Dorothy’s arms, bites a donkey ear after a crash, and finally stays with Dorothy, barking at departing bubbles before returning to Kansas.

Narrative, section by section

8 sections; each heading opens the text

1. The Way to Butterfield

Toto attacks the Shaggy Man, who puts him in a pocket with the apples. After being released, Toto tries several roads in an attempt to lead Dorothy home but cannot find the farm-house. He eventually gives up and follows Dorothy and the Shaggy Man down the seventh road.

2. Dorothy Meets Button-Bright

Toto watches Button-Bright dig and becomes excited, perhaps thinking the boy is looking for a wild animal. He jumps into the hole and digs with his paws until Dorothy stops him.

3. A Queer Village

Toto barks angrily at the fox-captain and tries to bite his leg before Dorothy restrains him. He later becomes excited by the foxes but quiets when Dorothy holds him, and he barks when she pounds the drum for him.

5. The Rainbow's Daughter

Toto is allowed to run freely, barking at birds and chasing butterflies. He makes friends with Polychrome, who pats his head, and Dorothy says he has more sense than Button-Bright.

8. The Musicker

Toto dislikes Allegro da Capo and tries to grab his chubby leg. Dorothy catches the growling dog, and Toto later runs away to chase a bumble-bee.

9. Facing the Scoodlers

Toto does not like apples and disappears from view because he is invisible, although the travelers can hear him barking furiously among the rocks. He later runs between Dorothy's feet and crouches there in fear when surrounded by the Scoodlers, then whines as they threaten to make the travelers soup.

12. The Deadly Desert Crossed

Toto rides in Dorothy's arms during the crossing. After the crash, he bites and shakes one of the shaggy man's donkey ears until the shaggy man makes him let go.

24. The Birthday Celebration

Toto remains with Dorothy after the visitors depart and barks at the bubbles as they sail away. He does not want to ride in a bubble, and Princess Ozma plans to send him with Dorothy to Kansas by the Magic Belt.

Toto in The Emerald City of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1910

Toto is a small little black dog with curly black hair and big brown eyes, Dorothy Gale’s companion and friend, and the only dog in the Land of Oz. He is dearly loved by Dorothy and respected by the people of the Emerald City. After first wanting to chase Billina, he becomes friendly with her. He accompanies Dorothy and Billina into the forest, barks at the Spoon Brigade, is captured and later released, and travels with them to Bunbury. There he eats Mr. Muffin’s wheelbarrow, Crumpets, and a Salt-rising Biscuit, prompting the inhabitants to pelt him. In Bunnybury he must remain outside with Billina. Dorothy’s note says he shares lasting love for the writer and children who love them.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

15. How Dorothy Happened to Get Lost

Toto has become friendly with Billina after previously wanting to chase her. He accompanies Dorothy and Billina into the forest, barks at the Spoon Brigade, and is included among Dorothy's captured companions.

16. How Dorothy Visited Utensia

Toto travels in the center of the Spoon Brigade with Dorothy and Billina. After knocking over a spoon with his tail, he is warned to be careful, and he is later released with his companions.

17. How They Came to Bunbury

Toto accompanies Dorothy and eats some of Mr. Muffin's wheelbarrow. He later eats three Crumpets and begins devouring a Salt-rising Biscuit, causing the Bunbury inhabitants to pelt him with hard-tack, crackers, and baked furniture.

19. How Bunnybury Welcomed the Strangers

Toto follows Dorothy and Billina to Bunnybury but is not permitted to enter because the rabbit people dislike dogs and the letter from Ozma does not mention him. He remains outside with Billina.

30. How the Story of Oz Came to an End

Toto is mentioned in Dorothy Gale's note as sharing her lasting love for the writer and the children who love them.

Toto in The Patchwork Girl of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1913-07-01

Toto is a little, black, shaggy-coated, non-fairy dog, Dorothy Gale's pet, usually well behaved, and able to understand her without speaking. He travels beside Dorothy, sleeps near her, drives off Tottenhots, and watches for trouble. Frightened in a canyon, he returns to Dorothy, who carries him during escape. He later barks at and attacks Hip Hopper, causing him to fall. On a raft he cannot swim well enough to help reach shore, then is welcomed by the Tin Woodman at his castle.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Nineteen

Toto travels beside Dorothy and sleeps near her at Jack Pumpkinhead's house. He helps Dorothy drive off the Tottenhots by barking and snapping at their bare legs, and later watches for trouble while the travelers sleep.

Chapter Twenty

Toto runs ahead in the canyon, becomes frightened, and returns with his tail between his legs. Dorothy carries him while the group escapes, and he lands ahead after Yoop throws the Scarecrow.

Chapter Twenty-One

Toto climbs with the group, enters the tunnel after Scarecrow, and barks at Hip Hopper. He attacks Hip Hopper's leg and causes him to fall, becoming the first living thing Hip Hopper says has conquered him.

Chapter Twenty-Six

Toto travels on the raft and can swim a little, though not well enough to help the group reach shore. At the Tin Woodman's castle, he runs ahead and barks at the front door. The Tin Woodman recognizes him as an old friend and welcomes him.

Toto in The Lost Princess of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1917

Toto is Dorothy’s little, shaggy black dog and a member of the search party. Though he can speak, he seldom says anything; he is often silent and sober, can bark, and is associated with a growl. After Dorothy leaves the Emerald City without him, Toto follows the party, hides until the shepherd’s hut, and rejoins Dorothy the next morning. Later he realizes his growl is missing, worries that its thief also stole Ozma, and finally finds it when a mouse runs from a corner.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 6

Toto follows the search party out of the Emerald City after realizing that Dorothy has left without him. He hides until the party reaches the shepherd’s hut, then reveals himself to Dorothy the next morning and is allowed to continue with them.

CHAPTER 10

Toto realizes that he has lost his growl and worries about what has become of it. He discusses the loss with the other animals and suspects that the person who stole his growl also stole Ozma.

CHAPTER 25

Toto discovers his growl after a mouse runs out of a corner, and the Lion judges it respectable for a small dog.

Toto in Grampa in Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1924

Toto is Dorothy’s small shaggy dog from Kansas and her traveling companion. He prefers his own language to the language of dogs and most strongly loathes the sloppy, messy wash women called the Tubbies. At first he chases a baconfly, sending himself and Dorothy off course, then is carried during escapes. He wakes seeking breakfast, hides from Percy Vere’s poetry and the wash women, but growls at them and tells them to stop shouting. Captured and tied to Dorothy’s tub, he escapes, slides down a mountain in hot soapy water, and rests beneath a tulip tree.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 9

Toto chases a baconfly across a daisy field, causing him and Dorothy to lose their intended direction. He accompanies Dorothy when she meets Percy Vere, is carried by her during the runaway road's attack, and is later carried in Percy's blouse while Dorothy and Percy escape.

CHAPTER 14

Toto wakes early because he wants breakfast and travels with Dorothy and Percy Vere. He hides from Percy’s strange poetry and from the approaching wash women, but later growls fiercely at them and unexpectedly tells them to stop shouting. He follows Dorothy and Percy when the Tubbies carry them away.

CHAPTER 16

Toto is tied to Dorothy's wash tub while they are prisoners and growls at the wash women, whom he loathes. He escapes with Dorothy and Percy, slides down the mountain in the hot soapy water, and later rests beneath a tulip tree trying to catch his breath.
