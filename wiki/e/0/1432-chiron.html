Chiron

mythic being · 4 works · 36 quoted facts · 14 connections
Also called: Centaur Chiron, Centaur, Chiron, Chiron the Centaur, dear father, father, her father, my father, one of these, the Centaur, the half-beast, the sage, the two-formed Chiron, Χείρωνα
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Chiron tends Achilles as a boy and addresses him in The Precepts of Chiron.
  • In The Library, Chiron is a centaur, healer, teacher of Aesculapius, protector and adviser of Peleus, and raiser of Achilles.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Chiron is a learned Centaur, teacher, healer, musician, astronomer, and formerly immortal being made subject to death.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Chiron is the wisest and justest Centaur, a teacher of heroes who becomes Sagittarius after death.

Chiron in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Chiron is a centaur, offspring of Cronus and Philyra, and a healer who teaches Aesculapius. He advises and repeatedly aids Peleus—helping him seize Thetis, saving him, restoring his sword, and giving an ash spear—then raises Achilles. He also restores Phoenix’s sight, unfolding as a protector, teacher, and healer.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

XIII. Peleus fled to Phthia to the court of Eurytion,

Chiron advised Peleus to seize and hold Thetis despite her transformations. He saved Peleus from the centaurs on Pelion, found and returned Peleus’s sword, gave Peleus an ashen spear, and later received and raised Peleus’s child, feeding him the entrails of lions and wild swine and the marrow of bears.

Chiron in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Chiron is a two-formed Centaur, son of Philyra and Saturn, living in Thessaly’s mountains. Renowned for knowledge when learning was little cultivated, he is regarded as an early promulgator of medicines, an excellent musician, and a good astronomer. He teaches Æsculapius, Hercules, and Jason, marks out constellations to direct the Argonauts, and is proud of his divine pupil. Initially immortal, he is wounded in the foot by Hercules’ arrow while examining a baneful serpent; serpent blood makes him mortal. Ocyrrhoe foretells that he will wish to die, and the Gods make him subject to death.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Chiron is proud of his pupil of divine origin and rejoices in the responsibility. Ocyrrhoe prophesies that although he is immortal, he will later wish to die after receiving the blood of a baneful serpent in his wounded limbs, and that the Gods will make him subject to death.

notes

Chiron’s foot was pierced by one of Hercules’ arrows while he was examining a baneful serpent.

Chiron was a Centaur, the son of Philyra and Saturn. He was renowned for his knowledge of medicine, music, and astronomy, taught Æsculapius and heroes such as Hercules and Jason, and was generally believed to have marked out the constellations to guide the Argonauts.

Chiron in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Chiron, the wisest and justest Centaur, learned hunting, medicine, music, and prophecy from Apollo and Diana, taught heroic pupils including Aesculapius, and became Sagittarius after death.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter X

Chiron was the wisest and justest of the Centaurs and was instructed by Apollo and Diana in hunting, medicine, music, and prophecy. He taught many Greek heroes, including Aesculapius, and Jupiter placed him among the stars as Sagittarius after his death.
