Isthmian Games in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

The Isthmian Games are contests held at Isthmos, where victories bring crowns, Dorian parsley wreaths, glory, and renewed fame. They include a chariot-race, pankration, and boxing. Xenophon won twice and received parsley wreaths before the people; the Oligaithidai won many times. The people of Kadmos won six crowns, while Timodemidai won eight, and Timodemos is predicted to win repeatedly. Xenokrates’ chariot-race victory is credited to Poseidon. Melissos, Phylakidas, Kleandros, and Nikokles also win there, chiefly in pankration or boxing, with Phylakidas’s double glory and family victories forming part of the honor celebrated in the odes.

Narrative, section by section

8 sections; each heading opens the text

XIII.

Xenophon had two victories at the Isthmian games, where parsley wreaths were placed on his head before the people. The Oligaithidai also achieved many victories at the games of Poseidon between the seas.

Nemean Ode II

The ode predicts that Timodemos will repeatedly win the noble flower of the Isthmian games.

Isthmian Ode I

The Isthmian Games are associated with six crowns won by the people of Kadmos, bringing glory to Thebes. Their victories are treated as a fair distinction for Herodotos’ native country.

Isthmian Ode II

The Isthmian victory celebrated in the ode was won by Xenokrates in a chariot-race. Poseidon is said to have granted him this victory and its Dorian parsley wreath.

Isthmian Ode III

At the Isthmian games Melissos wins a crown in the pankration. His victory gives the poet a means to celebrate the excellence and renewed fame of the Kleonymid clan.

Isthmian Ode IV

The Isthmian Games are represented through Phylakidas’s double glory stored at Isthmos. His success there forms part of the honor celebrated in the ode.

Isthmian Ode V

The Isthmian Games provided the occasion for Phylakidas’s victory in the pankration. The ode also records three pankration victories at Isthmos by the noble sons and their mother’s brother.

Isthmian Ode VII

The Isthmian Games provided Kleandros with a victory in the pankration. The Isthmian vale also witnessed Nikokles’ boxing victory, for which he won the Dorian parsley.

Isthmian games in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The Isthmian games were Greek athletic games held at the Isthmus and established by Sisyphus in honour of Melicerta, also called Palæmon, after his burial. They endured Corinth’s destruction by Mummius, moved to Sicyon while Corinth was desolate, and returned when Corinth was rebuilt. They occasioned a truce, were avoided by Elis according to disputed traditions, and featured recorded victories by Nicasylus, Epitherses, and Lycus.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK II.--CORINTH.

Sisyphus established the Isthmian games in honour of Melicerta after burying him at the Isthmus. The games continued after Corinth was destroyed by Mummius, being held at Sicyon while Corinth lay desolate, and returned to Corinth when the city was rebuilt.

BOOK V.--ELIS.

The Isthmian games were the occasion of a truce during which the sons of Actor left to attend and were ambushed by Hercules. The people of Elis traditionally abstained from the games because of curses attributed to Moline, although the text gives competing explanations for this practice.

CHAPTER XIII.

The Isthmian games are listed with the Olympic, Pythian, and Nemean games in accounts of an athlete's complete record. Nicasylus was proclaimed victor there, Epitherses won two boxing victories, and Lycus won once with the horse of Phidolas.
