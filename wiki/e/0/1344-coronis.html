Coronis

person · 3 works · 16 quoted facts · 8 connections
Also called: mistress, Κορωνίδος

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Coronis is the daughter of King Phlegyas who bears Asclepius in the Dotian plain. In The Library, Coronis is Phlegyas’s Thessalian daughter who is loved by Apollo, chooses Ischys, is killed by Apollo, and bears Asclepius, whom Apollo rescues from her burning pyre. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Coronis is a beautiful Nymph of Larissa who becomes Apollo’s mistress, is unfaithful, and is transformed into a crow after Apollo kills her.

Coronis in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Coronis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Coronis is a Nymph from Larissa in Hæmonia, famed as its most beautiful woman. She pleases Apollo while chaste, becomes his mistress, then is unfaithful with a youth of Hæmonia. The bird of Phoebus reports her, and Apollo pierces her with an arrow. Dying, she laments that punishment could have waited until after childbirth, since she and her child will die together. She is transformed into a crow for her overly faithful report.

Coronis, a woman from Larissa in Hæmonia, is described as exceptionally beautiful and as having pleased the Delphian God while she remained chaste. Her infidelity is discovered and reported by the bird of Phoebus.

opening

Coronis is reported to have lain with a youth of Hæmonia. Apollo wounds her with an arrow; before dying, she says that she might have been punished after first giving birth, and that she and the child will die together.

notes .. notes

Coronis is described as a Nymph whose name was also used for a crow. The story says that she was transformed into a crow for making too faithful a report.
