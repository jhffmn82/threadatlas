Aegisthus in The Electra of Euripides

Euripides · -0419/-0409

Aegisthus is the usurping ruler of Argos and Mycenae and Clytemnestra’s husband and chosen partner after Agamemnon’s death. With her, he plots and benefits from Agamemnon’s murder, then rules harshly: he persecutes Electra, restricts her marriage to prevent an avenger, and mocks Agamemnon’s grave. He fears Orestes, travels guarded, and is encountered outside the city while sacrificing a wreathed ox to the wild-wood nymphs. Believing Orestes and Pylades are travelers, he welcomes them and examines ominous signs. Orestes kills him with a sword, driving him into the house for an ignominious death; he dies alongside Clytemnestra and is to be buried by the people of Argos in a high green grave.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Introduction[1]

Aegisthus is Agamemnon's paramour and one of the guilty figures killed by Orestes and Electra. In Sophocles' treatment, he is driven into the house to receive an especially ignominious death.

ELECTRA

Aegisthus is described as the usurping king who married Clytemnestra and helped murder Agamemnon. He kept Electra from a powerful marriage, gave her to a poor husband to prevent an avenger from arising, and mocks Agamemnon's grave.

[ORESTES _and_ PYLADES _go in, following the_ SERVANTS.

Aegisthus is said to live in fear of Orestes and to travel with watchers and armed pikemen. He is found outside the city at a feast involving a wreathed ox and worship of the wild-wood nymphs, and Orestes plans to approach him there and kill him.

CHORUS.

Aegisthus welcomes Orestes and Pylades, whom he believes to be Thessalian travelers, to a sacrifice. While examining ominous sacrificial signs, he is struck and killed by Orestes, who cleaves his neck with a sword.

LEADER.

Aegisthus is described as Clytemnestra’s chosen partner and as harsh toward Electra. He is killed along with Clytemnestra, and Castor says that the people of Argos will bury him in a high green grave.

Aegisthus in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Aegisthus is the usurping king of Mycenae and a murderer who helped Clytemnestra kill Agamemnon, then took the throne. He is feared by Electra and Chrysothemis, who regard him as powerful, careful, and responsible for their father’s death; Electra expects punishment or imprisonment when he returns. Away during disputes in the household, he later rejoices at false news of Orestes’ death. After finding Clytemnestra dead and recognizing Orestes, he is taken into the palace and killed where Agamemnon was murdered.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

ELECTRA

Aegisthus helped Clytemnestra murder Agamemnon and usurped the Mycenaean throne. He is absent when Electra speaks with the Chorus but is expected to punish or imprison her when he returns.

_Enter_ CLYTEMNESTRA.

Aegisthus is away from home during the quarrel between Clytemnestra and Electra. His absence allows Electra to speak more freely, though Clytemnestra threatens that Electra will regret her boldness when Aegisthus returns.

_Enter_ CHRYSOTHEMIS.

Electra identifies Aegisthus as the guilty author of their father's death and proposes killing him. Chrysothemis says that Aegisthus is powerful and careful enough to prevent danger to himself.

_Enter_ ORESTES _and_ PYLADES, _with the urn_.

Aegisthus rejoices at the report of Orestes' death and demands to see the supposed evidence. He discovers Clytemnestra's body and recognizes Orestes, then is forced into the palace to die in the place where Agamemnon was killed.

Aegisthus in The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies

Aeschylus · -0457

Aegisthus, youngest son of Thyestes, becomes Clytemnestra’s lover and partner in a plot against Agamemnon. Claiming vengeance for Atreus’s crime against Thyestes, he helps murder Agamemnon and rejoices in the death. He then rules Argos, Agamemnon’s palace, and the house of Atreus with Clytemnestra, threatening the Chorus with subjection, hunger, and imprisonment. Identified as Clytemnestra’s new lord, an adulterer, slayer, and usurper, he hears falsely that Orestes is dead, enters the palace unguarded to question disguised strangers, and is killed by Orestes.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

[_The scene opens, disclosing Clytemnestra, who comes forward. The body

Aegisthus enters and rejoices over Agamemnon’s death, claiming that he planned the murder to avenge Thyestes. He recounts Atreus’s crime against his father and threatens the Chorus with subjection, hunger, and imprisonment as he prepares to rule with Clytemnestra.

THE LIBATION-BEARERS

Aegisthus is identified as Clytemnestra’s new lord and as the man who joined her in Agamemnon’s death. Electra prays that the slayers, including him, may be slain.

ORESTES (_approaching suddenly_)

Aegisthus is described as ruling in Agamemnon’s place with Clytemnestra. Orestes plans to confront and kill him inside the palace.

[_Enter Orestes and Pylades, in guise of travellers_.

Aegisthus comes to question the strangers after hearing that Orestes is dead. He enters without the guard Clytemnestra had originally requested and is killed by Orestes.

Aegisthus in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Aegisthus is a murderer and ruler of Mycenae whose central acts are seducing Clytemnestra and killing her husband Agamemnon. Despite Mercury’s warning, he removes the bard guarding Agamemnon’s wife, wins Clytemnestra by persistent flattery, and learns of Agamemnon’s return. He welcomes Agamemnon with a feast or banquet, ambushes and kills him and his companions, then rules in Mycene for seven years. Orestes, Agamemnon’s son, eventually returns and kills Aegisthus in revenge, bringing the consequences of Aegisthus’s actions upon him.
