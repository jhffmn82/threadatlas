Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, Ithaca is held by the Cephallenians, who bring twelve ships under Ulysses along with forces from neighboring islands and the mainland. In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Ithaca is ravaged by Telegonus after Odysseus returns there following Callidice’s death. In The Odyssey, Ithaca is Ulysses’s rugged homeland and estate, the home of his family, and the island to which he returns to reclaim his household and kill the suitors. In The Library, Ithaca is Ulysses’s native island, where suitors waste his household, he kills them, and Telegonus later fatally encounters him while driving away cattle. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Ithaca is Ulysses’s native island and kingdom, which he initially fails to recognize before regaining its palace, kingdom, and his wife.

Ithaca in The Odyssey

Homer · about 632 to 599 BC · the work’s page · read it where Ithaca first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ithaca is a rugged, productive island and country: Ulysses’s homeland, the home of Telemachus, Penelope, and their household, and the setting of Ulysses’ estate, house, city, harbour, Neritum, Eumaeus’s hut and pig yards, and Laertes’s farm. Though it lacks open fields and racecourses and is better for goats than horses, it has corn, wine, cattle, goats, timber, rain, dew, and unfailing watering places. Its greatest importance is as the home to which Ulysses longs to return and as the property endangered during his absence. While he is away, suitors consume his estate and household while courting Penelope; Telemachus leaves from Ithaca seeking news, then returns despite their attempted interception. Ulysses repeatedly names Ithaca as his dearest country and intended destination, though prophecy makes his return dependent on suffering and the loss of his men. He finally reaches the island by ship, initially fails to recognize it in mist, and moves through it in disguise, staying with Eumaeus before approaching his household. After twenty years, he returns openly enough to recover his home and kill the suitors. Their deaths divide Ithaca’s people, but Minerva brings peace.
