Phaeacians in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

The Phaeacians are a people of Scheria, living apart from other people at the sea’s end. Near of kin to the gods and blessed with abundance, they are ruled by Alcinous and are renowned as the world’s best seafarers, proud of their ships, masts, and oars. They also excel in running, other athletic contests, dancing, and appreciation of Demodocus’ songs. Though described as disliking strangers, they hold that strangers in distress are protected by Jove and become conspicuous hosts to Ulysses. They had formerly lived near the lawless Cyclopes, who plundered them, until Nausithous moved them to Scheria. When Ulysses arrives, their leaders make offerings and agree to send him home. At Alcinous’ summons they gather, feast, compete, dance, hear Ulysses’ account, and supply him with cloaks, shirts, gold, and other valuable gifts. Their ships need neither pilots nor rudders and know cities and countries throughout the world. Finally, they carry the sleeping Ulysses safely from Scheria to Ithaca; on the return, Neptune turns their ship to stone, leaving them afraid that their city may be buried under a mountain.

BOOK VI

The Phaeacians once lived near the lawless Cyclopes, who plundered them, before Nausithous moved them to Scheria. They are a seafaring people who pride themselves on ships, masts, and oars, and they believe strangers in distress are under Jove’s protection.

BOOK VII

The Phaeacians are described as a seafaring people who dislike strangers and are ruled by Alcinous. Their leading men witness Ulysses’s arrival, make drink offerings, and agree that he should receive an escort home. They are portrayed as wealthy and closely connected with the gods.

The Phaeacians gather at Alcinous’ summons to arrange Ulysses’ escort and fill the assembly and the king’s house. They compete in races, wrestling, jumping, discus throwing, and boxing, and are charmed by Demodocus’ songs and the dancers.

The Phaeacians give Ulysses valuable gifts and carry him safely from Scheria to Ithaca while he sleeps. Neptune transforms their returning ship into stone, and they fear that he will also bury their city under a mountain.

Phaeacians in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The Phaeacians, godlike people who migrated from near the Cyclopes to Scheria, excel at navigation with pilotless intelligent ships; they welcome Ulysses, equip him, and carry him safely to Ithaca.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXIII

The Phaeacians are skilled navigators whose intelligent ships need no pilot. They welcome Ulysses, provide him with food, clothing, gifts, and a ship, and carry him safely to Ithaca.
