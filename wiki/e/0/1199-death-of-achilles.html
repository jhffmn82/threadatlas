  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, it occurs when Paris and Apollo kill Achilles after he routs the Trojans and rushes into the city with them.
  • In The Library, it occurs when Alexander and Apollo shoot Achilles in the ankle; Achilles is buried with Patroclus in the White Isle, where games are held in his honour.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, it occurs when Apollo directs Paris's arrow into Achilles's vulnerable heel; his body is burned with little ash remaining, while his enduring glory fills the whole world.
  • In The Fall of Troy, it is foretold by Thetis at the Scaean Gates and caused by Apollo alone, bringing lasting grief to Achilles's loved ones and inspiring Neoptolemus to avenge him.

Achilles' death in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

Achilles' death is an event foretold by Thetis at the Scaean Gates and caused by Apollo alone. Apollo secretly strikes Achilles' ankle with a shaft, draining his strength. Though wounded, Achilles continues killing before falling among the dead; even dead, he terrifies the Trojans. His loss makes the Achaeans fear defeat, delights the Trojans, and brings lasting grief to Peleus, Phoenix, Thetis, Briseis, his companions, and Neoptolemus. Phoenix is continually consumed by grief, while Neoptolemus mourns at Achilles' tomb, is driven to avenge him, and asserts that Achilles' spear and son still make enemies fear.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

The Fall of Troy

The death of Achilles is the subject of Book III. The introduction states that Quintus Smyrnaeus has Achilles slain by Apollo alone, differing from traditions that associate Paris with the killing.

BOOK III

Achilles' death comes through Apollo's concealed shaft, which wounds his ankle and drains his strength. He continues killing after the wound but finally falls among the dead, terrifying the Trojans even after he is dead.

So the kings drew him from that stricken field

Achilles' death is caused by a god's arrow that strikes him unexpectedly rather than in direct combat. His death causes the Achaeans to fear defeat, gives joy to the Trojans, and brings grief to Peleus, Phoenix, Thetis, Briseis, and his companions.

So came they to the heavy-plunging sea,

Achilles' death is remembered by Phoenix, who says that grief for him continually wastes him away. Neoptolemus is driven to avenge his dead father and later grieves when he sees Achilles' empty tent, armor, weapons, and handmaids.

BOOK IX

Achilles' death is recalled through Neoptolemus' mourning at his tomb and Deiphobus' statement that the fire consumed him. Neoptolemus declares that Achilles' spear and son have nevertheless made his enemies fear.
