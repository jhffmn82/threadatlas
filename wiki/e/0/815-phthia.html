Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, Phthia is Achilles’s homeland, where his father grieves and grows old and to which Achilles imagines returning with Briseis and Patroclus’s son. In Hecuba and other plays, Phthia is Peleus’s Thessalian realm and city, where Andromache lives captive and Peleus defends its succession.

Phthia in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · about 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Phthia first appears

Abstract written by a model

Phthia is Peleus’s Thessalian realm and city, ruled by him with cavalry and infantry, though Neoptolemus is associated with its rule. Andromache arrives at its coast and lives there captive among Hector’s killers. Peleus vows to protect her and educate Molossus, defending the realm’s succession against Menelaus’s challenge. Hermione, living in Achilles’ son’s household, fears Phthia hates and will expel her after her attempted violence against Andromache. It ends with Peleus mourning its empty mansions and lost descendants.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

ANDROMACHE.

Phthia is the Thessalian land where Andromache now lives as a captive. Neoptolemus is associated with its rule, while Peleus continues to rule because of his age.

MENELAUS, MOLOSSUS, ANDROMACHE, CHORUS.

Phthia is the land whose prince is said by Andromache to direct Menelaus’s servants. Andromache says she reached the coast of Phthia and lived there among those who killed Hector.

PELEUS, MENELAUS, ANDROMACHE, MOLOSSUS, CHORUS.

Phthia is ruled by Peleus, who promises to protect Andromache and educate Molossus there. Menelaus questions whether sons of a barbarian woman should rule Phthia, while Peleus threatens to defend its succession.

THE NURSE OF HERMIONE, CHORUS.

Phthia is the land where Hermione lives in Achilles’ son’s household. Hermione believes that Phthia hates her because of her attempted violence against Andromache and fears being expelled from it.

PELEUS, CHORUS.

Phthia is Peleus’s city and the location of his house. Peleus laments that he has no children or descendants left to occupy its mansions.
