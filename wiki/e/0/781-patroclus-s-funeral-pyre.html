Abstract written by a model

A hundred-foot-square wooden pyre holds Patroclus, animals, captives, honey, and unguents; after Achilles prays to Boreas and Zephyrus, winds kindle and sustain its flames through the night.

BOOK XXIII.

The Achaeans build a large pyre and place Patroclus's body upon it with animals, horses, dogs, Trojan captives, honey, and unguents. When it will not kindle, Achilles prays to Boreas and Zephyrus; the winds then fan the flames through the night.
