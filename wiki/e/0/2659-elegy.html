Elegy in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · about 24 to 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where Elegy first appears

Abstract written by a model

Elegy is a feminine, love-associated poetic genre personified as a graceful woman with uneven feet, thin robe, loving expression, and a myrtle branch. She inspires the poet, enables Corinna’s secret love, and defends her light Cupid-suited style, winning the poet’s continuation. She gives the poet’s love lasting renown, then mourns Tibullus with loosened hair and tears.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

LIBER TERTIUS

Elegy appears with one foot longer than the other, a graceful form, a thin robe, and a loving expression. She claims that she inspired the poet's work and taught Corinna to evade her guard and meet her lover. She argues that her light style is suited to Cupid and love, and she persuades the poet to continue writing in her manner.

IX

Elegy is told to mourn Tibullus, loosen her hair, and weep because the singer and glory of her poetic strain has died. Her name is said to be all too truthful in this bereavement.

Elegy in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where Elegy first appears

Abstract written by a model

Elegy is a beauteous personified literary genre with wreathed perfumed hair, a humble robe, the garb of love, and one longer foot. A trifler devoted to Cupid and allied with his mother, she defends her power against Tragedy: her caressing words open barred doors. She helps Corinna escape her keeper, receive messages, and makes the Poet's genius prosper.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

---- BOOK THE THIRD. / ELEGY I.

Elegy appears with perfumed hair, a humble robe, and one foot seemingly longer than the other. She defends herself against Tragedy, saying that her caressing words can open doors that Tragedy's rigid buskin cannot. She claims to have helped Corinna deceive her keeper, leave her couch at night, receive messages, and inspire the Poet's genius.
