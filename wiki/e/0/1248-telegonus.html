Telegonus in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Telegonus, son of Odysseus and Calypso, searches for his father, lands on and ravages Ithaca, and unwittingly kills Odysseus. After learning his mistake, he transports Odysseus’s body, Penelope, and Telemachus to Calypso’s island, then marries Penelope.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE TELEGONY

Telegonus is the son Odysseus has by Calypso. While searching for his father, he lands on Ithaca and ravages it, then unwittingly kills Odysseus; after learning his mistake, he transports Odysseus’s body, Penelope, and Telemachus to his mother’s island and later marries Penelope.

Telegonus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Telegonus, son of Odysseus and Circe, learns his parentage from Circe and sails to Ithaca seeking Odysseus. After driving away cattle, he wounds Odysseus with a sting-ray-spined spear, then recognizes him, laments, takes Odysseus’s body and Penelope to Circe, marries Penelope, and is sent with her to the Islands of the Blest.

80 Τηλέγονος δὲ παρὰ Κίρκης μαθὼν ὅ ὅτι παῖς Oévo-

Telegonus learned from Circe that he was a son of Odysseus and sailed to Ithaca in search of him. After driving away cattle, he wounded Odysseus with a spear fitted with a sting-ray spine. Recognizing Odysseus afterward, he lamented, took his body and Penelope to Circe, and married Penelope.
