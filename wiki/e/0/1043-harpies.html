Harpies

group · 5 works · 14 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Harpy, half-human birds, hounds of mighty Zeus, pests, the Harpies, the foul Harpies, the hellish nation, the hungry Harpies, the monsters, the rav’nous birds, the winged warriors, those monstrous birds the Harpies, ἁρπυίαις, ἁρπυίας
  • In The Aeneid, the Harpies are foul, winged monsters who inhabit the Strophades, defile the Trojans' meals, resist their weapons, and escape.
  • In The Argonautica, the Harpies are winged tormentors and hounds of Zeus who punish Phineus by snatching his food and leaving a stench, then are chased away by Zetes and Calais.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the Harpies pursue or flee Zetes and his companions across many lands while tormenting and transporting Phineus; one falls into the Tigris and another reaches the Echinades.
  • In The Library, the Harpies are winged female creatures sent by the gods to Phineus, whose food they repeatedly steal and defile before being pursued by Zetes and Calais.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Harpies are monstrous female birdlike beings who torment Phineus by taking his meals and later seize the Trojans' feast before foretelling suffering.

Harpies in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · -0299/-0200

Harpies are winged tormentors and hounds of Zeus who punish Phineus by snatching his food and leaving stench; chased away by Zetes and Calais, they cannot return and later inhabit Minoan Crete.

The Harpies swoop from the clouds to snatch food from Phineus’s mouth and hands, leaving behind an intolerable stench. Zetes and Calais chase them away, and Iris’s oath ensures that they will not return to Phineus.

Harpies in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Harpies pursue or flee Zetes and his companions across many lands, tormenting and transporting Phineus; one falls into the Tigris, another reaches the Echinades, and Hesiod says they survive.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE CATALOGUES OF WOMEN AND EOIAE1701

The Harpies pursue or flee from the sons of Boreas across many lands and peoples. One Harpy falls into the river Tigris, while another reaches the Echinades, and the Harpies are not killed in the Hesiodic account; they also torment and transport Phineus.

Harpies in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

The Harpies are winged female creatures sent by the gods to Phineus. They repeatedly steal most food from his table and leave a stench-filled remainder. Zetes and Calais pursue them through the air; one falls into the river Tigres, while the other reaches the Echinadian Islands. They include several differently named Harpies.

Harpies in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The Harpies are dreadful daughters of earth and sea, monstrous female birdlike beings sent to torment Phineus by taking his meals. Driven to an island, they seize the Trojans’ feast, resist weapons, and foretell suffering before the Trojans depart.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXIV

The Harpies are dreadful creatures sent by the gods to torment Phineus by snatching away his food. When the Trojans slaughter cattle and prepare a feast on their island, the Harpies seize the meat, resist the Trojans’ weapons, and predict future suffering before the Trojans leave.
