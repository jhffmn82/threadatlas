sea-faring and sailing in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Sea-faring is a livelihood when poverty leaves insufficient livelihood. It should be avoided as the Pleiades descend and winter gales begin; its favourable season comes fifty days after the solstice, while spring sailing is risky, and sailors must return before new wine, autumn rain, and storms of Notus.

(ll. 618-640) But if desire for uncomfortable sea-faring seize you;

Sailing should be avoided when the Pleiades descend and winter gales begin. The best stated time for sailing is fifty days after the solstice, while spring sailing is possible but dangerous; sailors should return before the new wine, autumn rain, and storms of Notus.
