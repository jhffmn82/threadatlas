  • In Hecuba and other plays, Amphitryon is an aged refugee who defends Hercules, grieves after Hercules kills Megara and the children, undertakes their burial, and receives Hercules’ promise to bury him.
  • In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Amphitryon is a charioteer and sword-armed captain who fathers Herakles’ father and rushes to defend the children from serpents.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Amphitryon kills Electryon over oxen, comes to Thebes as a suppliant, leads a campaign against the Taphians and Teleboans, and fathers Iphiclus.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Amphitryon accidentally kills Electryon, is expelled from Argos, goes to Thebes, marries Alcmena, and is associated as the father of Hercules and father of Iphiclus.
  • In The Library, Amphitryon accidentally kills Electryon, is banished, goes to Thebes with Alcmena, conquers the Tafian islands and Teleboans, and dies fighting the Minyans.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Amphitryon flees Tiryns after Electryon’s death, fights the Eubœans and Chalcodon, kills Chalcodon, and dedicates tripods to Apollo.

Amphitryon in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Amphitryon is an aged, physically weakened man from Argos, descended from Alcaeus through Perseus’ son, and the father of Hercules and Megara. Once a doer of glorious deeds, he is reduced in one day from former glory to a refugee with Megara and Hercules’ children at Jupiter’s altar, threatened with death by Lycus. He defends Hercules’ courage, recounts his lineage and labours, appeals unsuccessfully to Zeus for rescue, and bitterly reproaches Jove for failing Hercules’ family. When Hercules returns, Amphitryon explains the danger and urges him to hide, expecting Lycus’ punishment. After Hercules, driven by madness, kills Megara and the children, Amphitryon grieves, fears for his own life, and carefully tells Hercules what happened. He urges Hercules to restrain himself, undertakes the children’s burial because Hercules may not perform it, and receives Hercules’ promise to bury him afterward.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

HERCULES DISTRACTED.

Amphitryon identifies himself as Hercules’ father and describes Hercules’ lineage, labours, and descent to the Underworld. He shelters with Megara and the children at the altar of Jupiter, defends Hercules’ courage against Lycus, and begs that he and Megara be killed before the children. He finally reproaches Jove for sharing his nuptial couch yet failing to save Hercules’ family.

MEGARA, AMPHITRYON, CHORUS.

Amphitryon raises his hands to the sky and appeals to Zeus to save the deserted children, but he believes that his prayers have failed and that they must die. He tells the Chorus that Fortune has taken away his former glory in a single day and bids them make life as pleasant as possible while it lasts.

HERCULES, MEGARA, AMPHITRYON, CHORUS.

Amphitryon welcomes Hercules and explains the danger posed by Lycus and his supporters. He urges Hercules to remain hidden in the palace, then leaves expecting Lycus to be killed and rejoicing in the tyrant’s deserved punishment.

IRIS, A FIEND, CHORUS.

Amphitryon witnesses the devastation and grieves over the murdered children and Megara. He fears that Hercules may kill him as well, but after Hercules wakes he cautiously explains that Hercules, driven by madness, committed the murders.

THESEUS, AMPHITRYON, HERCULES, CHORUS.

Amphitryon tells Theseus that Hercules, seized by frenzy, killed his own children and wife. He urges Hercules to restrain himself and later agrees to bury the children. He asks who will bury him, and Hercules promises to do so after the burial.

Amphitryon in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Amphitryon is a heroic figure, son of Alceus and cousin and husband of Alcmena. After accidentally killing Electryon with a club thrown at a cow, he is expelled from Argos, goes to Thebes, and is absolved by Creon. He accepts Alcmena’s condition to wage war against the Teleboans, marries her, and is associated as father or putative father of Hercules and father of Iphiclus.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

EXPLANATION.

Amphitryon, son of Alceus and grandson of Perseus, accidentally killed Electryon by throwing a club at a cow and was expelled from Argos by Sthenelus. He went to Thebes, was absolved by Creon, and accepted Alcmena’s condition that he wage war against the Teleboans before marrying her; during that war, Hercules was born.

Amphitryon in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Amphitryon, son of Alcaeus in one version, receives a kingdom and Alcmena from Electryon under an oath to preserve her virginity until Electryon returns. While recovering cattle, he accidentally kills Electryon with a rebounding club and is banished by Sthenelus. He goes with Alcmena and Licymnius to Thebes, where Creon purifies him. To marry Alcmena and avenge her brothers, he gathers allies, attempts to rid the Cadmea of the vixen, and conquers the Tafian islands and Teleboans, killing Comaetho and distributing the islands and booty. Husband of Alcmena and father of Iphicles and, in one account, Hercules, he dies bravely fighting the Minyans.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

THE LIBRARY, 1]. 1v. 2-3

Amphitryon received the kingdom and Alcmena from Electryon, with an oath to keep her a virgin until Electryon returned. While recovering the cattle, he threw a club at a charging cow, and its rebound killed Electryon; Sthenelus then banished him.

THE LIBRARY, II. tv. 6-7

Amphitryon went with Alcmena and Licymnius to Thebes and was purified by Creon. He undertook to avenge Alcmena’s brothers, tried to rid the Cadmea of the vixen, and then led an expedition against the Teleboans. He later conquered the Tafian islands and killed Comaetho.

THE LIBRARY, II. tv. 7-8

Amphitryon dies fighting bravely in the battle against the Minyans. Hercules later receives Megara from Creon after this conflict.

Amphitryon in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Amphitryon, son of Alcæus by Laonome, fled Tiryns after Electryon’s death and lived in a house at Electris. He armed himself by nearby statues of Athene Zosteria, fought the Eubœans and Chalcodon, slew Chalcodon, and dedicated notable tripods to Apollo. The Thebans say Hercules later sought to kill him but slept.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XI.

Amphitryon is said to have lived in the house whose ruins stand left of the Electris gate after fleeing Tiryns because of the death of Electryon. The Thebans say that Hercules, in madness, wished to kill him, but sleep overcame Hercules after Athene threw the stone called Composer.
