Arctinus of Miletus

person · 1 work · 1 quoted fact · 4 connections
Also called: Arctinus

Abstract written by a model

Arctinus of Miletus was an author credited with the Aethiopis and the Sack of Ilium. Said to have flourished in the first Olympiad, he continued the Trojan story after the Iliad through deaths of Penthesilea, Memnon, and Achilles, ending with Ilium’s fall and aftermath.

Arctinus of Miletus is in one work on this site, Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

The Ionic School

Arctinus of Miletus, said to have flourished in the first Olympiad, was credited with the Aethiopis and the Sack of Ilium. He continued the Trojan story after the close of the Iliad, including the deaths of Penthesilea, Memnon, and Achilles, and he treated the sack of Troy.

THE SACK OF ILIUM

Arctinus of Miletus is identified as the author of the Sack of Ilium. His account includes the fall of Ilium, the deaths and captures that follow it, and details about the Palladium and the physicians Machaon and Podaleirius.
