Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, Achilles’ anger is the rage caused by Agamemnon’s seizure of Briseis, leading Achilles to withdraw from battle and bringing suffering to the Achaeans until he renounces it after Patroclus’s death. In Heroides and Amores, Achilles’ anger began with Agamemnon’s taking of Briseis and keeps Achilles from fighting for the Danaans, while Briseis asks him to overcome it.

Achilles’ anger in The Iliad

Homer · 749 to 724 BC · the work’s page · read it where Achilles’ anger first appears

Abstract written by a model

Achilles’ anger is rage caused by Agamemnon’s seizure of Briseis, which Achilles sees as dishonor and insult. Directed chiefly against Agamemnon, it nearly drives Achilles to kill him before Minerva restrains him. Achilles withdraws from assembly and battle, keeps his people by the ships and seashore, and brings countless ills upon the Achaeans, who are to regret losing him from the fighting. Agamemnon’s embassy offers gifts, promises, and appeals, but neither these nor Phoenix and Ajax can move Achilles while the anger remains fixed. After Patroclus’s death, Achilles says his anger against Agamemnon is over and renounces it, compelled to return to battle.

Achilles' anger in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · about 24 to 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where Achilles' anger first appears

Abstract written by a model

Achilles' anger began with Agamemnon's taking of Briseis and has kept him from fighting for the Danaans. Briseis asks him to conquer his anger, since Agamemnon now regrets his own wrath and Greece lies distressed before him. She wants her return to be both the cause and the end of his anger.
