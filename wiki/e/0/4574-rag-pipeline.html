RAG pipelines in FACTS About Building Retrieval Augmented Generation-based Chatbots

Rama Akkiraju, Anbang Xu, Deepak Bora, Tan Yu, Lu An, Vishal Seth, Aaditya Shukla, Pritam Gundecha, Hridhay Mehta, Ashwin Jha, Prithvi Raj, Abhinav Balasubramanian, Murali Maram, Guru Muthusamy, Shivakesh Reddy Annepally, Sidney Knowles, Min Du, Nick Burnett, Sean Javiya, Ashok Marannan, Mamta Kumari, Surbhi Jha, Ethan Dereszenski, Anupam Chakraborty, Subhash Ranjan, Amina Terfai, Anoop Surya, Tracey Mercer, Vinodh Kumar Thanigachalam, Tamar Bar, Sanjana Krishnan, Jasmine Jaksic, Nave Algarici, Jacob Liberman, Joey Conway, Sonu Nayyar and Justin Boitano · 2024-07-10

RAG pipelines combine vector-search-based information retrieval systems, Large Language Models, vector databases, and LangChain-like orchestration frameworks. They unfold through metadata enrichment and chunking, document extraction, query rephrasing, retrieval, reranking, prompt design, and response generation, with memory and agents orchestrating activities. Their fifteen critical control points support optimization at each stage. Effective engineering includes semantic-embedding and LLM fine-tuning, access controls, concise responses with pertinent references, and personal-information safeguards. The central concern is improving relevance, accuracy, quality, speed, and cost while managing large- versus small-LLM accuracy-latency tradeoffs; poorly tuned stages can produce hallucinations or irrelevant chatbot responses.

ABSTRACT

RAG pipelines require meticulous engineering, including embedding and LLM fine-tuning, document extraction from vector databases, query rephrasing, reranking, prompt design, access controls, concise responses, references, privacy protection, and agent orchestration. The unit presents fifteen RAG pipeline control points and techniques for optimizing chatbot performance at each stage.

INTRODUCTION

A RAG pipeline combines vector-search-based information retrieval systems, LLMs, and LangChain-like orchestration frameworks. Building an effective pipeline requires engineering for relevance, accuracy, access control, references, privacy, quality, speed, and cost, and the paper presents fifteen control points for optimizing it.

A RAG pipeline contains multiple stages and control points, including metadata enrichment, chunking, query rephrasal, retrieval, reranking, and response generation. Poorly tuned stages can reduce accuracy and cause hallucinations or irrelevant chatbot responses.

Xun Liang, Simin Niu, Zhiyu Li, Sensen Zhang, Hanyu Wang, Feiyu Xiong, Jason Zhaoxin Fan, Bo Tang, Jihao Zhao, Jiawei Yang, Shichao Song, Mengwei Wang · 2025

RAG pipeline is a pipeline for integrating external knowledge with LLM generation. It sequentially retrieves, filters, and generates, using a retriever, filter, and generator to exploit query-relevant knowledge-base contexts. Its main value is mitigating hallucinations while improving answer accuracy and interpretability. It begins with indexing, then retrieval, filtering, and generation; its composite mapping is retrieve → filter → generate. In evaluation, it is vulnerable to attack tasks across all classified tasks: attackers can inject noise, conflict, toxicity, or Denial-of-Service material into the knowledge base, retrieved context, or filtered context. Such attacks are most effective in filtered context, then retrieved context, then the knowledge base. Fourteen representative components are evaluated, with default top K of 2 and injection ratio 1/2 except for the silver noise task. Coverage is mainly single-modal unstructured text, not images, structured graphs, or audio.
