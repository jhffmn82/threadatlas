Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, Eurypylus, son of Euaemon, is a brave Achaean warrior and prince who helps Ajax by killing Apisaon, is wounded by Alexandrus, and receives treatment from Patroclus after limping to Ulysses' ships. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Eurypylus, son of Euæmon, receives a chest containing a statue of Dionysus, goes mad after opening it, recovers after following an oracle's instructions, and is thereafter honored annually at Patras; he also kills Axion.

Eurypylus in The Iliad

Homer · 749 to 724 BC · the work’s page · read it where Eurypylus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Eurypylus, son of Euaemon, is a brave Achaean warrior and prince. During fighting, he helps Ajax by killing Apisaon and taking his armour. Alexandrus wounds his right thigh with an arrow, forcing him from battle; he urges defense of Ajax, limps to Ulysses’ ships, and receives treatment from Patroclus.

Eurypylus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Eurypylus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Eurypylus, son of Euæmon, receives a chest containing a statue of Dionysus after Ilium’s capture. Opening it drives him mad, though he sometimes recovers. Seeking Delphi’s oracle, he is told to dedicate the chest at a strange sacrifice. At Aroe the sacrifice stops and he recovers; afterward Patras annually sacrifices to him during Dionysus’s festival. He also kills Axion.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIX.

After the capture of Ilium, Eurypylus son of Euæmon received a chest containing a statue of Dionysus. He became mentally ill after opening the chest, consulted the oracle at Delphi, and was directed to dedicate it where a strange sacrifice was being offered. He arrived at Aroe, recovered when the sacrifice was stopped, and thereafter received annual sacrifices from the people of Patras during the festival of Dionysus.
