Abstract written by a model

No. 3, Lauriston Gardens is an empty house off the Brixton Road, its owner refusing drain repairs. In its bare, dusty front dining-room, Enoch J. Drebber’s body is found beside a red wax candle and a blood-red inscription. Holmes and Watson examine the murder scene and draw deductions from its evidence. Earlier, Jefferson Hope entered with a duplicate key, brought in the drunk Drebber, forced him to choose a pill, and left him dead.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

CHAPTER III.

3, Lauriston Gardens is an empty house off the Brixton Road where Enoch J. Drebber’s body is found. The house has a bare, dusty dining-room with a dirty window, a red wax candle, and a blood-red inscription on a section of exposed plaster.

CHAPTER IV.

Sherlock Holmes and Doctor Watson leave No. 3, Lauriston Gardens after examining the scene of the murder. Holmes bases several deductions about the suspects and their movements on evidence found there.

CHAPTER VI.

Jefferson Hope has a duplicate key to the house in the Brixton Road and brings Enoch Drebber inside while he is drunk. There, Hope reveals his identity, forces Drebber to choose a pill, and leaves him dead on the floor.
