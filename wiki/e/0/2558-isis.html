Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Isis is a goddess, queen, former ruler of Egypt, and mother of Orus who avenges Osiris, reconstructs and buries his body, establishes his worship, and later governs Egypt with Orus. In Heroides and Amores, Isis is a goddess invoked to attend to Corinna and grant her and the speaker life by sparing her. In The Amores, or Amours, Isis is a goddess associated with the Nile and invoked to show mercy to Corinna and Naso. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Isis is the Egyptian divinity who is the restored and worshipped identity of Io, with a cult associated with agriculture and spread to foreign countries. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Isis is a protective goddess who appears to Telethusa in a dream and transforms Iphis so Iphis can marry Ianthe. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Isis is a deity worshipped at Bura and near Tithorea who controls shrine access through dream invitations and receives festivals and restricted animal sacrifices. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Isis is a goddess and wife of Osiris who teaches agriculture, governs Egypt during his absence, and searches for and recovers parts of his body after his murder. In Vestiges of the Mayas, or, Facts tending to prove that Communications and Intimate Relations must have existed, in very remote times, between the inhabitants of Mayab and those of Asia and Africa, Isis is the goddess, wife and sister of Osiris, queen of the Amenti, and personification of Egypt's irrigated portion who searches for and recovers Osiris's body.

Isis in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Isis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Isis is a goddess, queen, and former ruler of Egypt, described as the sister and wife of Osiris, daughter of Saturn, and mother of Orus. She signifies ancientness, has horns associated with a sickle and increase and decrease, and is identified in various accounts with the moon, Ceres, Thesmophorus, Luna, Juno, and Io. She discovers bread from wheat and barley, establishes laws for human society, invents medicines, heals the sick, and receives harvest offerings. During Osiris's expedition she rules Egypt; after Typhon murders him, she helps avenge his death with Orus, is said to kill Typhon, searches for Osiris, reconstructs and buries his body, assigns priests to honor him, and establishes his worship. She does not remarry after his death. She raises Orus from the dead and makes him immortal, then governs Egypt justly with him. She is later worshipped as a goddess and is said to be buried and to have a shrine at Memphis, though other burial places are reported.

Isis is identified with the moon and with Ceres. She is said to have discovered the preparation of bread from wheat and barley, established laws for human society, ruled Egypt during Osiris's expedition, and received harvest offerings in memory of her discoveries.

CHAP. 11.

Isis, the sister and wife of Osiris, helps avenge his death with Orus and takes possession of Egypt. She reassembles Osiris's body, assigns priests to bury and honor it, vows not to remarry, and is later worshipped as a goddess.

From Osiris and Isis, to the reign of Alexander the Great, who

Isis is described as the wife and sister of Osiris, the mother of Orus, and a former ruler of Egypt who governed justly after avenging Osiris's death. She is credited with discovering grain, inventing medicines, healing the sick, raising Orus from the dead, and making him immortal. She is identified with Ceres, Thesmophorus, Luna, Juno, and Io, and is said to have been buried at Memphis, although other burial places are reported.

Tlie adoration and worshipping of beasts among the Egyptians,

Isis is said to have killed Typhon for murdering Osiris. According to one account, she cast the members of Osiris into a wooden ox covered with ox hides, and dogs are said to have guided her while she searched for Osiris.

Isis in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Isis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Isis is a goddess worshipped at Abydos, wife of Osiris, whose soul was believed to reside in Sothis. Represented as a woman crowned with a sun-disc and throne, she taught earth's inhabitants wheat and barley and governed Egypt during Osiris's absence. After Osiris was murdered, she mourned, searched with Anubis, recovered his chest at Byblos, found most scattered pieces, and replaced a missing piece with a sycamore-wood imitation.

Chapter XXVII

Isis was a goddess worshipped at Abydos and was represented as a woman crowned with the sun-disc surmounted by a throne, sometimes between horns. Her soul was said to reside in Sothis on the Dog-star, and she formed part of the Abydos triad with Osiris and Horus.

MYTH OF OSIRIS AND ISIS

Isis taught people the use of wheat and barley and governed while Osiris was away. After Typhon murdered Osiris, she mourned, searched for his body with Anubis's help, recovered the coffin, and later found most of Osiris's scattered pieces. She was represented with a veiled head as a symbol of mystery.
