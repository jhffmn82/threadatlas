  • In The Aeneid, Ida is a mountain sacred and beloved by the Trojans; Anchises considers seeking refuge there, a celestial sign vanishes toward its grove, and its wood later becomes Trojan ships transformed into sea nymphs.
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Ida is where Hera, Aphrodite, and Pallas competed for the prize of beauty and asked Paris to judge them; Helen says Paris later came from Ida to Sparta.
  • In The Rhesus of Euripides, Mount Ida is the direction from which Rhesus and his forces approach Troy, and a shepherd from the mountain reports hearing the Thracian army moving through its dark, rocky terrain.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Ida is a many-fountained, forested, furrowed, windy mountain rich in wild creatures and Nymphs, where Anchises tends cattle, Aphrodite finds him, and Nymphs raise Aeneas.
  • In The Iliad, Ida is a many-fountained mountain, mother of wild beasts, containing Mount Olympus and feeding several rivers; it is visible from Samothrace and serves as Jove’s vantage for watching Troy and the battle, meeting Juno, awakening, and dispatching Apollo and Iris.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Mount Ida is the region connected with the second Herophile through her mother, one of its Nymphs, and its thin, porous, red, dry soil explains why the river Aidoneus disappears into the ground.
  • In The Fall of Troy, Ida is Oenone’s home, where wounded Paris dies after leaving her, and where Oenone mourns him and Troy’s destruction apart from Troy’s people.

Ida in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Ida is a mountain, sacred and beloved by the Trojans. Anchises means to seek refuge there before fleeing with Aeneas; a celestial sign vanishes toward its grove, and its wood becomes Trojan ships later transformed into sea nymphs.

Ida is the grove toward which the celestial sign travels and disappears. Anchises had intended to take refuge there before deciding to flee with Aeneas.

BOOK X

Ida is the mountain beloved by the wandering Trojans. Aeneas’s ship bears carved lions representing Ida, and the Trojan ships were made from wood cut there before they became sea nymphs.

Ida in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Ida is a many-fountained mountain, mother of wild beasts, containing Mount Olympus and feeding the Rhesus, Heptaporus, Caresus, Rhodius, Grenicus, Aesopus, Scamander, and Simois Rivers. Visible from Samothrace with Troy and the Achaean ships, it becomes Jove’s vantage: he reaches Gargarus, watches Troy and battle, meets Juno, awakens, and dispatches Apollo and Iris.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK VIII.

Jove travels to Ida, reaches Gargarus, and watches the city of Troy and the Achaean ships from its heights.

BOOK XIV.

Mount Ida is where Jupiter watches the battle and where Juno meets him. Juno and Sleep reach its forests, Sleep hides in a pine tree, and Jupiter and Juno lie together on its crest while a golden cloud conceals them.

BOOK XV.

Ida is where Jove awakens and where he later sits on Gargarus surrounded by fragrant cloud. Apollo and Iris go there to receive and carry out Jove’s commands.
