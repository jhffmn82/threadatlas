  • In The Amores, or Amours, Ceres’s daughter is found again by Ceres and rules realms ranked below Juno’s.
  • In The Aeneid, Proserpine is queen of the underworld and requires a golden bough and the sacrifice of a barren heifer before Aeneas enters the nether skies.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Proserpine is carried away by Pluto, associated with Sicilian festivals and spring, and permits the returns of Orpheus’s wife and Theseus and Pirithous.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Proserpina is Ceres’s daughter whose abduction and recovery cause Ceres’s grief, reconciliation with Jupiter, and distribution of seed-corn through Triptolemus.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Proserpine jointly possesses the land with Ceres as one of two goddesses.
  • In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Persephone possesses a dark-walled home, receives Sicily from Zeus, and sends souls back to sunlight in the ninth year.
  • In The Odyssey, Proserpine is a dread goddess who rules Hades and preserves Teiresias’s understanding after death.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Persephone is abducted by Hades and spends one-third of each year in the underworld because she ate a pomegranate seed.
  • In The Library, Persephone is carried off by Pluto, receives a pomegranate seed, keeps Adonis for part of the year, and detains Pirithous in Hades.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Proserpine is abducted by Pluto, becomes queen of the shades, and divides her time between the upper and lower worlds.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Persephone is Pluto’s royal consort who rules the Infernal Regions, changes Mentha into mint, and permits Eurydice’s conditional return.
  • In The Library, Persephone is Demeter’s daughter and Pluto’s wife who is taken to the underworld and thereafter divides each year between Pluto and the gods.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Proserpine is Demeter’s daughter, worshipped in temples, and involved in the origin of the river Hercyna.
  • In Description of Greece, Proserpine is a deity worshipped with Demeter and associated with traditions of her abduction by Pluto.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Despœna is the Arcadians’ most-worshipped god, privately named Persephone, whose mystic rites are withheld from the uninitiated.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Proserpine is abducted by Pluto, becomes queen of Erebus, and divides each year between Pluto and Ceres.

Proserpine in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Proserpine, also called Persephone, is Ceres's daughter and Pluto's wife, an infernal deity who rules as queen of Erebus and the underworld. While gathering lilies and violets in the vale of Enna, she is abducted by Pluto; after eating pomegranate seeds below, she must divide each year between Pluto and Ceres. She symbolizes seed-corn. As underworld queen, she gives Psyche a closed box of beauty containing supernatural sleep, receives Aeneas's golden branch, and, moved by Orpheus's grief, joins Pluto in allowing Eurydice to leave under an unmet condition.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter I

Proserpine, or Persephone, was the daughter of Ceres. She became the wife of Pluto and queen of the realms of the dead.

PROSERPINE

Proserpine gathers lilies and violets with her companions in the vale of Enna when Pluto sees and abducts her. She drops the flowers from her apron during the abduction, later becomes the queen of Erebus, and eats some pomegranate seeds in the underworld. Because she has eaten, she must spend part of each year with Pluto and the rest with Ceres.

CUPID AND PSYCHE

Proserpine receives Psyche in the underworld and gives her a closed box containing the beauty requested by Venus. Psyche is instructed not to open or inspect it.

Chapter XVII

Proserpine heard Orpheus sing about his grief and could not resist his plea for Eurydice. Together with Pluto, she allowed Eurydice to leave the underworld under a condition that Orpheus failed to keep.
