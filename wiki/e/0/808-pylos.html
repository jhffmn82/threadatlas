Pylos

place · 7 works · 19 quoted facts · 15 connections
Also called: Pjlos, Pyios, Pylian, Pylos the city of Neleus, Pylus, distant Pylos, men of Pylos, sandy Pylos, the high citadel of Pylos, the land of sheep, Πύλον
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Pylos is a naturally fortified Messenian place that Demosthenes walled and the Athenians defended before its Messenian garrison surrendered to the Lacedaemonians.
  • In The Odyssey, Pylos is Nestor’s city and a chief destination in Telemachus’s search for Ulysses, where sacrifices are held, Telemachus is welcomed, and from which he travels to and returns from Lacedaemon.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Pylos is the destination toward which Hermes drives Apollo’s cattle and where Hermes and Apollo later find them in a high-roofed byre.
  • In The Iliad, Pylos is led by Nestor and sends forces from Pylos, Arene, and neighboring places, contributing ninety ships.
  • In The Library, Pylus is where Hermes brings Apollo’s cattle, hides the rest, and is later visited by Apollo, who searches for them and questions its inhabitants.
  • In The Library, Pylus is a city founded by Neleus in Messene that Heracles ravages, killing Neleus’s sons except Nestor.
  • In Description of Greece, Pylos is a mostly sandy place associated with Neleus, founded by Pylos son of Cleson, later made Neleus’s seat of government, and home to temples, houses, tombs, and a sacred cave.

Pylos in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Pylos is a naturally fortified place in Messenia, four hundred stadia from Messene, founded by Neleus after bordering inhabitants gave him territory. Demosthenes walled it in twenty days, and the Athenians defended it against a large Lacedaemonian land and sea force, using it to threaten Laconia and block supplies to Sphacterian island. After fifteen years, its Messenian garrison surrendered under siege, losses, and lack of provisions, and it passed to the Lacedaemonians.

Pylos in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Pylos is a city, ruled by Nestor, containing Nestor’s house, and described as a land of sheep. It is a chief destination in Telemachus’s search for Ulysses: Minerva directs him to go there, and Antinous notes his intended departure. In Pylos, the people gather by the shore to sacrifice black bulls to Neptune, and Nestor and his people welcome Telemachus and Minerva. Telemachus later leaves its high citadel with Pisistratus for Lacedaemon, returns there to part from Pisistratus, takes aboard Theoclymenus, and finally sails back from Pylos with his crew.

Pylos in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Pylos is a mostly sandy place, associated with Neleus and called the city of Neleus. It was founded by Pylos, son of Cleson, who brought Leleges from Megaris, before Neleus and Pelasgians from Iolcus expelled him. Aphareus gave it to Neleus, who made it his seat of government; Nestor and his descendants later kept their royal court there. It has Athene Coryphasia’s temple, Nestor’s house and painting, Nestor’s tomb, and a cave said to be an ox-stall. Its men aided Elis and Pisa against Hercules, who later punished them.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK IV.--MESSENIA.

Pylos was among the maritime cities given by Aphareus to Neleus, who made it his seat of government. During the reign of Nestor and his descendants, the royal court was located there.

CHAPTER XXXIV.

Pylos was founded by Pylos, the son of Cleson, who brought Leleges from Megaris, but he was expelled by Neleus and the Pelasgians of Iolcus. It contains a temple of Athene Coryphasia, a house called Nestor’s house, a painting of Nestor, Nestor’s tomb, and a cave said to have been the stall of the oxen of Nestor and earlier of Neleus. The district is mostly sandy and is associated with the epithet “sandy Pylos.”

BOOK V.--ELIS.

The men of Pylos in Elis assisted the people of Elis and Pisa against Hercules. Hercules later punished the men of Pylos.
