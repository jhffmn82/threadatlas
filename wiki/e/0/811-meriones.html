Meriones in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Meriones is a brave, valorous Achaean warrior and the stalwart squire of Idomeneus, serving him in battle and commanding Cretan rear battalions. He fights prominently, breaks his spear on Deiphobus’s shield, rearms, and leads with Idomeneus toward the left wing. He wounds Deiphobus, kills Acamas, Adamas, and Harpalion, and proves skilled beyond battle: he finishes fourth in chariot racing, wins the archery contest and ten axes, and receives a spear in the javelin contest.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XIII.

Meriones breaks his spear against Deiphobus’s shield and returns to his tent to fetch another. After arming himself, he joins Idomeneus and leads the way toward the left wing of the battle.

And now Idomeneus, though his hair was already flecked with grey,

Meriones wounds Deiphobus in the arm and forces him to drop his helmet. He kills Adamas by stabbing him between the private parts and the navel, and later kills Harpalion with an arrow through the buttock.

Thereon the son of Peleus, when he had listened to all the thanks

Meriones wins the archery contest by shooting the pigeon in the wing after promising a sacrifice to Apollo, and he receives the ten double-edged axes. He also competes in the javelin contest and receives the spear after Achilles gives Agamemnon the cauldron.
