Abstract written by a model

In The Odyssey, the suitors occupy Ulysses’s house while courting Penelope, consume his wealth, plot against Telemachus, and are ultimately killed by Ulysses and his allies. In Heroides and Amores, the suitors crowd into Ulysses's hall, seek Penelope, and consume and spoil his possessions during his absence. In The Library, Odysseus’s suitors from Ithaca and Same court Penelope, consume Ulysses’s herds, fail to bend his bow, and are killed by Ulysses and his allies. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the suitors occupy Ulysses's palace, consume his wealth, court Penelope, plot against Telemachus, and are killed by Ulysses and his allies, except Phemius and Medon.

the suitors in The Odyssey

Homer · about 632 to 599 BC · the work’s page · read it where the suitors first appears

Abstract written by a model

The suitors are a group of 108 chiefly men from Ithaca and neighboring islands, led especially by Antinous and Eurymachus, who occupy Ulysses’ house while seeking to marry Penelope against her will. For three years they consume Ulysses’s and Telemachus’s livestock, wine, food, and household wealth without payment, hold lavish banquets, abuse hospitality, mistreat servants and guests, and dishonor Penelope and Telemachus through pride and insolence. Though Telemachus summons them to leave, they refuse until Penelope chooses a husband. They mock and threaten him, then plot to kill him as he returns from Pylos and Lacedaemon, waiting at sea to ambush him. Their conduct increasingly draws warnings and omens: Halitherses predicts Ulysses’ return, Teiresias foretells their deaths, Penelope dreams of geese killed by an eagle, and other signs announce destruction. While Ulysses is disguised as a stranger, they insult and attack him, though some give him food. They continue courting Penelope, give her gifts, expose her weaving ruse, and fail collectively to string Ulysses’s bow. After Ulysses strings it and shoots through the axe handles, he kills Antinous. The suitors try to escape and fight with armour supplied by Melanthius, but Minerva turns aside most of their weapons, and Ulysses and his allies kill them all. Their bodies are removed or buried, the house is purified, and their deaths lead Eupeithes and others to seek revenge.
