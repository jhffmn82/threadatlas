the murder of Agamemnon in The Electra of Euripides

Euripides · -0419/-0409

Agamemnon's murder is a betrayal carried out after his return from Troy by Clytemnestra and Aegisthus, who is also described as its instigator and as killing by craft. Clytemnestra defends it as response to Agamemnon's sacrifice of Iphigenia, marital betrayal, and bringing another woman home, but Electra rejects that defense. The killing causes Electra's grief and drives Orestes' planned vengeance: he is urged to kill Aegisthus and Clytemnestra, with Aegisthus's death treated as payment for the blood-debt.

Agamemnon's murder in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Agamemnon's murder is a hideous, bloody crime committed after his return from Troy by Clytemnestra with Aegisthus, using a bronzen-griding axe. Clytemnestra admits the deed and presents it as vengeance for her daughter's sacrifice, while Electra says it sprang from her relationship with Aegisthus and lacked rightful authority. The killing causes Electra's mourning and drives her, Chrysothemis, and Orestes toward vengeance: Electra seeks Aegisthus's death, and Orestes plans to kill him where Agamemnon was cut down.

the murder of Agamemnon in The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies

Aeschylus · -0457

Agamemnon’s murder is the killing of Agamemnon in the palace after his return from Troy. Clytemnestra, assisted by another hand and joined with Aegisthus, uses treachery and an entangling robe or net to trap and kill him. Cassandra foresees it and his cries confirm the attack. The murder brings uncleansable blood guilt, curses the household, and leaves the dead angry.

the killing of Orestes's father in The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies

Aeschylus · -0457

The killing of Orestes's father is an unlawful, treacherous bloodshed committed by Clytemnestra, with Aegisthus as planner. After welcoming him home from war, Clytemnestra traps him in a robe or net while he bathes and strikes him three times. Aegisthus presents the act as revenge for Atreus’s treatment of Thyestes. His dishonourable burial and lack of mourning lead Orestes and Electra toward vengeance; the Chorus foresees another death. The killing is ultimately avenged by the killing of Orestes's mother under Apollo’s oracle.

the killing of Agamemnon in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

The killing of Agamemnon occurs after his return home from Troy. Aegisthus allies with Clytemnestra, removes Agamemnon’s bard, invites Agamemnon to a banquet, and kills him after the feast; both sides’ followers die, and Menelaus later raises a barrow.
