Metis

person · 2 works · 8 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Athena’s mother, Thought, Μήτιδι, Μῆτιν

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Metis is Zeus’s first wife, a goddess called wisest among gods and mortal men, and Athene’s mother; Zeus deceives and swallows her when she is about to bear Athene and a son destined to rule, and she remains linked to wisdom and devising good and evil within his belly. In The Library, Metis is Ocean’s daughter and Zeus’s consort who helps Zeus defeat Cronus by administering a drug that makes him disgorge the stone and swallowed children; she changes shape to avoid Zeus, becomes pregnant by him, and is swallowed by Zeus because he fears her child’s future rule.

Metis in The Library

Apollodorus · about AD 70 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Metis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Metis, Ocean’s daughter and Zeus’s consort, helps Zeus defeat Cronus by administering a drug that makes him disgorge the stone and swallowed children. She changes shape to avoid Zeus, becomes pregnant by him, and is swallowed by Zeus because he fears her child’s future rule.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

II. ᾿Ἐ ππειδὴ δὲ Ζεὺς ἐγενήθη τέλειος, λαμβάνει

Metis, the daughter of Ocean, helps Zeus by giving Cronus a drug to swallow. The drug forces Cronus to disgorge the stone and the children he had swallowed.

III. Ζεὺς δὲ γαμεῖ μὲν “Ἥραν, καὶ τεκνοῖ

Metis had intercourse with Zeus and changed into many forms to avoid him. After she became pregnant, Zeus swallowed her because he feared that her future child would become ruler of heaven.
