Metis in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Metis is Zeus’s first wife, a goddess called wisest among gods and mortal men, and Athene’s mother. When about to bear Athene and a son destined to rule, Zeus deceives and swallows her. Hidden in his belly, she remains linked to wisdom and devising good and evil.

Metis was Zeus’s first wife and was described as the wisest among gods and mortal men. When she was about to bear Athene and a son destined to rule, Zeus deceived and swallowed her, placing her in his belly. She remained hidden within Zeus and continued to be associated with wisdom and the devising of good and evil.

Metis in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Metis, Ocean’s daughter and Zeus’s consort, helps Zeus defeat Cronus by administering a drug that makes him disgorge the stone and swallowed children. She changes shape to avoid Zeus, becomes pregnant by him, and is swallowed by Zeus because he fears her child’s future rule.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

II. ᾿Ἐ ππειδὴ δὲ Ζεὺς ἐγενήθη τέλειος, λαμβάνει

Metis, the daughter of Ocean, helps Zeus by giving Cronus a drug to swallow. The drug forces Cronus to disgorge the stone and the children he had swallowed.

III. Ζεὺς δὲ γαμεῖ μὲν “Ἥραν, καὶ τεκνοῖ

Metis had intercourse with Zeus and changed into many forms to avoid him. After she became pregnant, Zeus swallowed her because he feared that her future child would become ruler of heaven.
