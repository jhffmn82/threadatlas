Circe’s sorcery

topic · 2 works · 0 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Circe's magic, charms, drugged into subjection, enchanted potions, enchantments, her art, incantations, magic and enchantment, magic lips, magical power, spells, the bad drug, witchcraft
  • In The Odyssey, magic and enchantment enable Circe to transform Ulysses’s men into pigs and attempt to charm Ulysses, while moly and a second drug prevent or reverse the enchantment.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Circe’s sorcery uses herbs, juices, incantations, a wand, and counter-charms to transform Scylla and men into monsters or swine and restore the men to human form.

Ovid · 0008~

Circe’s sorcery combines potent herbs, noxious juices, and spoken incantations. She mutters a mysterious charm over the polluted bay and uses this magic to change Scylla into a monster. Circe’s magic combines secret drugs, a wand, and spoken charms to transform men into swine.
