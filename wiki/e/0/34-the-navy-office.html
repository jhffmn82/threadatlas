the Navy Office in The Diary of Samuel Pepys

Samuel Pepys · 1660/1669

The Navy Office is a quiet workplace where Samuel Pepys returns to official business, takes pleasure in authority, and considers Tangier matters and adding Sir R. Ford’s house. With clerks at Tom Whitton’s burial, officials report severe financial losses to the Duke of York. Lack of money leaves the house addition uncertain because of division and rent-guarantee difficulties.

3 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Samuel Pepys returns to work at the Navy Office and takes pleasure in speaking with persons of quality and exercising authority. He attends to official business there, including matters concerning Tangier, and helps consider whether Sir Robert Ford’s house could be added to the office.

opening

At the Navy Office, Pepys and the other officials have little business because clerks are attending the burial of Tom Whitton. The officials report the Navy’s financial condition and the severe losses on the Office’s bills to the Duke of York.

opening

The Office is quiet because of a lack of money. It may add Sir R. Ford's house, though difficulties over dividing the house and personally guaranteeing the rent may prevent the arrangement.

the Navy Office in The Diary of Samuel Pepys

Samuel Pepys · 1660/1669

The Navy Office is an office that occupies a house under an agreed arrangement. In June–August 1661, the diarist repeatedly attends it for official business while it considers nearby lodgings. Facing a sad lack of money that prevents its men from serving further, it agrees to write to the Duke of York. It also secures Sir R. Ford’s house for its quarters at £200 per year.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

JUNE, JULY & AUGUST 1661 June 1st.

The diarist attended the Navy Office on several days to conduct and sit over official business. The office was also involved in arrangements concerning nearby lodgings, though the houses considered by the Comptroller and the diarist did not suit them.

opening

The office agrees to write to the Duke of York about its sad condition and lack of money. Its men are unable to serve it further without money.

opening

The office reaches an agreement with Sir R. Ford to add his house to its quarters, completing the arrangement for £200 per year. The narrator spends the morning of the 24th at the office conducting business.
