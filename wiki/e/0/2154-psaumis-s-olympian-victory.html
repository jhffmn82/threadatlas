Psaumis's Olympian victory in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

Psaumis’s Olympian victory, won in 452 in the mule-chariot race at Olympia, is aided by the Graces and celebrated in song to Zeus. After his return to Kamarina, it brings honor to Psaumis, his father, and the city as a lasting witness to valorous deeds.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

IV.

Psaumis's Olympian victory is presented as a lasting light and witness to far-prevailing valorous deeds, achieved with the aid of the Graces. It is celebrated through a song offered to Zeus.

V.

The mule-chariot race victory is the occasion of the ode. It is celebrated after Psaumis’s return to Kamarina and is presented as a source of honor for him, his father, and the city.
