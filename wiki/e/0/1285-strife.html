Strife

personification · 1 work · 5 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Eris, This Strife, abhorred Strife, blood-curdling strife, harsh Strife, strife, the one, the other

Abstract written by a model

Strife is a goddess of two kinds: a blameworthy form fostering cruel war and battle, and a wholesome form promoting work by driving neighbours, craftsmen, beggars, and minstrels to compete for wealth. At Peleus and Thetis’s marriage feast, she begins the dispute over whether Hera, Athena, or Aphrodite is fairest.

Strife is in one work on this site, Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

HESIOD / HESIOD’S WORKS AND DAYS

Strife has two forms: one promotes cruel war and battle, while the other is wholesome and drives people to work through rivalry. The beneficial Strife makes neighbors, craftsmen, beggars, and minstrels compete with one another.

THE CYPRIA

Strife arrives while the gods are feasting at the marriage of Peleus and Thetis and starts the dispute over which of Hera, Athena, and Aphrodite is fairest.
