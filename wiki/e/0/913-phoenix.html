Phoenix

person · 3 works · 16 quoted facts · 8 connections
Also called: Ancient, Phoenix the old, ancient Phoenix, father’s follower Phoenix, old friend and father, old knight Phoenix, umpire, Φοίνικα, Φοίνικος, Φοῖνιξ
  • In The Iliad, Phoenix is Achilles’ tutor, companion, father figure, and trusted umpire who leads the embassy to urge Achilles to end his anger.
  • In The Library, Phoenix is the son of Amyntor, a man restored from blindness by Chiron, king of the Dolopians, father of Adonis, and companion of Achilles at Troy.
  • In The Fall of Troy, Phoenix is Achilles’ former nurse, foster-father, and spiritual father who mourns Achilles, recognizes him in Neoptolemus, and urges revenge.

Phoenix in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Phoenix is an old knight, Achilles’ tutor, companion, and father figure, sent by Peleus to train him in speech and action. He became ruler of the Dolopians, commands Myrmidons, and is trusted as an umpire. Appointed by Nestor to lead the embassy, he travels to Achilles’ tent and urges him to set aside anger and save the Achaean ships, offering his history and Meleager’s story.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK IX.

Phoenix is appointed by Nestor to lead the embassy to Achilles, with Ajax and Ulysses following him. He travels with them to Achilles’ ships and tent.

Achilles answered, “Ulysses, noble son of Laertes, I should give

Phoenix urges Achilles to overcome his anger and help save the Achaean ships, using his own history and the story of Meleager as examples. Achilles asks him to stay and sleep comfortably, but Phoenix remains with the embassy and plans to return with Achilles if he chooses to go home.

Phoenix in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Phoenix, son of Amyntor, was blinded after a concubine’s false accusation, restored by Chiron, made king of the Dolopians by Peleus, fathered Adonis according to Hesiod, and accompanied Achilles to Troy.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Os δὲ ἐγένετο ἐνναετὴς ᾿Αχιλλεύς, Κάλχαντος

Phoenix, son of Amyntor, accompanied Achilles to Troy. His father had blinded him after a concubine falsely accused him of seduction; Peleus brought him to Chiron, who restored his sight, and Peleus then made him king of the Dolopians.

Phoenix in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

Phoenix is an old man, former nurse, foster-father, and father-figure to Achilles. He raises Achilles from infancy and regards him as a spiritual son, hoping for his care in old age. After Achilles dies, Phoenix mourns him deeply; later he recognizes Achilles in Neoptolemus, embraces him, and urges him to avenge his father.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

So the kings drew him from that stricken field

Phoenix mourns Achilles as the child whom Peleus entrusted to his care. He recalls holding Achilles as a baby and hoping that Achilles would support him in old age, then grieves that both he and Peleus must live with sorrow for his death.

So came they to the heavy-plunging sea,

Phoenix comes to Neoptolemus after the battle and recognizes him as the image of Achilles. He embraces and kisses him, recalls having raised Achilles, mourns Achilles' death, and urges Neoptolemus to avenge his father.
