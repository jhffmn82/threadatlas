Chryseis

person · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: daughter of Chryses, the child, the daughter, the girl, the woman
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Chryseis is taken among the spoils and awarded to Agamemnon as a prize; the Cypria reportedly says she had taken refuge at, or gone to, Hypoplacian Thebes to sacrifice to Artemis.
  • In The Iliad, Chryseis is awarded to Agamemnon as spoil from Thebe, held after he rejects her father’s ransom, and released to appease Apollo before Ulysses returns her to Chryse.

Chryseis in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Chryseis, daughter of Chryses, is awarded to Agamemnon as spoil from Thebe and held after he rejects her father’s ransom. To appease Apollo, Agamemnon releases her without payment; Ulysses returns her to Chryse, where Chryses gladly receives her.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK I.

Chryseis is held by Agamemnon after he refuses the ransom offered by her father, Chryses. To appease Apollo, Agamemnon sends her back to Chryse with a hecatomb and places Ulysses in command of the voyage.
