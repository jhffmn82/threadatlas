adultery in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Adultery is a secret, difficult love pursued despite fear, scrutiny, locks, and guards. Helen feels invited to wrongdoing yet delays, fearing condemnation even from Paris, who shares responsibility. Its safety depends on the mistress’s guardian: cooperation, silence, false explanations, concealed messages, and invented visits protect the affair. Though doors and guards restrain bodies, desire remains unconfined, and adultery ultimately appears as a stain on the breast.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Prima mea est igitur Veneri plaeuisse voluptas ;

Helen calls the path of adultery difficult and fears that all eyes are fixed on her. She says that everything invites her to wrongdoing, yet fear still delays her, and she imagines that Paris may later condemn her as an adulteress despite sharing responsibility for the crime.

LIBER SECUNDUS

Ovid seeks the guardian’s help so that he and the mistress can love safely and discreetly. The proposed affair relies on false explanations, concealed messages, invented visits, and the guardian’s silence rather than violence or poison.

III

Adultery can occur despite locked doors and appointed guards because the mind and desire cannot be confined. In the dream interpretation, the dark mark on the heifer's breast indicates that her breast is not free from the stain of adultery.
