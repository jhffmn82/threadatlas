revenge upon the suitors in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Revenge upon the suitors is Ulysses’s planned punishment of the suitors for occupying his house, consuming his substance, and mistreating Penelope and Telemachus. Teiresias first foretells that, once home, Ulysses will kill them by force or fraud. Ulysses and Minerva then consult about their destruction; Minerva conceals his identity so he can investigate. Ulysses and Telemachus prepare together: Ulysses returns disguised as a beggar, Telemachus behaves normally among the suitors, and they remove weapons while relying on Minerva and Jove. As the time approaches, Ulysses considers how to overcome the many suitors and receives Minerva’s assurance. He ultimately slaughters them with Telemachus, aided by Eumaeus and Philoetius, after which the house is purified. The killings prompt fear of retaliation, and Eupeithes seeks vengeance for Antinous but dies after Minerva intervenes.

BOOK XI

Teiresias foretells that Ulysses will take revenge on the suitors after returning home. Ulysses will kill them in his own house, using force or fraud.

BOOK XIII

Ulysses and Minerva consult about destroying the suitors, who have occupied his house and consumed his substance. Minerva promises to support him and begins by concealing his identity so that he can gather information before taking revenge.

BOOK XIV

Ulysses says that, after returning, he will take vengeance on all those who mistreat Penelope and Telemachus. The suitors are the people he specifically threatens with this vengeance.

BOOK XVI

Ulysses and Telemachus plan to return to the house, conceal Ulysses's identity, remove most of the arms, and confront the suitors with help from Minerva and Jove. Telemachus will appear to act normally among the suitors while Ulysses returns disguised as a beggar.

BOOK XVII

Telemachus asks Penelope to promise offerings to the gods if they grant revenge upon the suitors. Theoclymenus says that Ulysses is investigating the suitors’ wrongdoing and preparing a day of reckoning.

BOOK XX

Ulysses lies awake considering how he can kill the numerous suitors despite being alone. He waits for the proper moment, receives Minerva's assurance of help, and watches for signs that the attack and his revenge are near.

BOOK XXIII

Ulysses has slaughtered the suitors with the assistance of Telemachus, while Eumaeus and Philoetius also support him. The bodies lie piled in the gatehouse, and the house is purified with sulphur. Ulysses fears that the deaths will provoke the people of the town and plans to leave for the countryside.

the battle against the suitors in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

The battle against the suitors is Ulysses’s revenge and punishment for the suitors’ insolence and crimes, carried out with Telemachus in the cloisters of Ulysses’ house. It begins when Ulysses, after proving mastery of the bow, kills Antinous. The suitors obtain armour and attack from the court, but Ulysses, Telemachus, Eumaeus, and Philoetius fight them with Minerva’s help. The battle ends with every suitor dead. Afterwards, townspeople mourn and bury them, and Eupeithes calls for an armed attack on Ulysses.

The slaughter begins when Ulysses shoots Antinous after proving his mastery of the bow. Ulysses and Telemachus then attack the suitors throughout the cloisters, killing them with divine assistance.
