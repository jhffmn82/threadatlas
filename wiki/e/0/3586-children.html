children in The Life and Adventures of Santa Claus

L. Frank Baum · 1902

Children are tender, helpless beings found in the city of men across sharply different conditions of wealth and poverty, though equally fair and sweet as babes. Often neglected by their parents, they welcome Claus as a playfellow, finding happiness in his attention and fearing winter will keep him away. Claus sees ragged children playing in dust and richly dressed children eating sugar-plums; their shared sweetness awakens his love, pity, and wish to help, because childhood seems the most contented time before humanity's cares arrive. They love toys and Santa Claus, receive his gifts in long stockings, and eagerly anticipate his yearly visits, including the children of Baron Braun. As their numbers grow worldwide, they become the purpose of Santa Claus's lifelong work and a reason he is granted immortality: they would grieve if he were lost. When obstacles such as new houses block his visits, he and his helpers seek other ways—through parents and toy-shops—to ensure no child longs for toys in vain.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

6. Claus Discovers Humanity

Claus observes children living in poverty and wealth, including ragged children playing in dust and richly dressed children fed with sugar-plums. He sees them as equally fair and sweet and believes childhood is the time of greatest content, before the cares of humanity overtake them. Their condition awakens his love, pity, and desire to help.

2. How Claus Made the First Toy

Children in the villages welcome Claus as a playfellow, riding on his shoulders, nesting in his arms, and clinging to his knees. They have been much neglected by their parents, so Claus's attention makes them very happy. Claus worries that winter will prevent him from seeing them.

12. The First Christmas Tree

Children receive toys, oranges, bananas, candies, and bonbons from Claus in their long stockings. They look forward to his yearly visit and gain happiness by wondering what will happen when he arrives. The children of the new Baron Braun long for a visit from Santa Claus, and Claus promises to bring them presents.

OLD AGE / 1. The Mantle of Immortality

Children are the recipients of Santa Claus's toys and the beneficiaries of his lifelong work. Ak argues that Claus should live on because children would miss and grieve over his loss.

2. When the World Grew Old

Little children are loved by Santa Claus, whose devotion to them earns him the Mantle of Immortality. Their growing numbers increase Santa Claus’s work, and children throughout the world come to love him for the gifts and happiness he provides.

3. The Deputies of Santa Claus

Children suffer disappointment when new houses prevent Santa Claus from reaching them. Santa Claus and his helpers work to ensure that children receive toys, including through parents and toy-shops, and Santa Claus says that no child should long for toys in vain if he can help it.
