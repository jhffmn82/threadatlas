Mormons

Abstract written by a model

The Mormons are an immigrant religious community, described as persecuted while traveling west under Brigham Young, their leader, Prophet, and religious head. Admission requires belief in the Mormon creed. After a difficult pilgrimage, they migrate to Utah, establish a settlement with farms, trades, streets, squares, and a central temple, and control Utah through religious and coercive authority. They rescue John Ferrier, give him land and protection, but require conformity, subject members to the Elders, practice polygamy, and are portrayed as using fear and secret violence. Their authority culminates when Stangerson and Drebber seek Lucy Ferrier’s marriage and threaten punishment from the Prophet and Council of Four after Ferrier refuses.

Mormons is in one work on this site, A Study in Scarlet. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

PART II. / CHAPTER I.

The Mormons are a persecuted religious people traveling west in search of refuge under the leadership of their Prophet. They require John Ferrier and Lucy Ferrier to become believers in their creed before admitting them to the group.

CHAPTER II.

The Mormons complete their migration to the valley of Utah after enduring severe hardships. They establish farms, trades, streets, and squares, and construct a great temple in the centre of their city.

CHAPTER III.

The Mormons rescued John Ferrier from starvation, brought him to the Chosen Valley, gave him land, and protected him. Their society is described as enforcing religious conformity through fear and secret violence.

CHAPTER IV.

Joseph Stangerson and young Drebber, identified as young Mormons, visit John Ferrier to solicit Lucy’s hand in marriage. When Ferrier orders them out, they threaten him with punishment from the Prophet and the Council of Four.
