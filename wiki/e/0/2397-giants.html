Giants

group · 6 works · 24 quoted facts · 14 connections
Also called: These giants, giants, human giants, men of vast bodies, one of the giants, sons, superhuman giants, that proud race, the giants, the giants of Phlegraea, the giants of Phlegrsea, this ruthless race, Γίγαντας, τῶν Γιγάντων

Abstract written by a model

In The Extant Odes of Pindar, the giants are destined to fight the gods at Phlegra's plain and be defeated by Herakles' arrows. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the giants are vast-bodied men who fought Jupiter and Osiris and were destroyed for warring against the gods. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the Giants attempt to dispossess Jupiter and conquer Heaven. In The Library, Giants are described as children of Earth and Sky who war with the gods. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the giants are presented as mortal beings who aided and protected Rhea against Cronos. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the giants are monsters who warred against the gods and tried to reach heaven.

The Giants are a group that attempts to dispossess Jupiter and conquer Heaven. Aspiring to rule there, they heap Olympus, Pelion, and Ossa into a mountain structure to reach the stars. Jupiter strikes them with lightning, and they are buried beneath the mountains they piled.

Giants in The Library

Apollodorus · about AD 70 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Giants first appears

Abstract written by a model

Giants are children of Earth and Sky, born in Phlegra or Pallene. They have matchless bulk, invincible might, terrible forms, long locks, and dragon-scaled feet; they hurl rocks and burning oaks at the sky. Porphyrion and Alcyoneus excel among them, with Alcyoneus immortal in his native land. In war with the gods, Zeus strikes many with thunderbolts and Heracles shoots the dying Giants.

The Giants are born from Earth and Sky, possessing enormous bodies, great strength, terrifying appearances, dragon scales for feet, and the ability to hurl rocks and burning oaks at the sky. Porphyrion and Alcyoneus surpass the others; Alcyoneus is immortal while fighting in his native land and has driven away the Sun’s cows from Erythia. The Giants are defeated by the gods with the help of Heracles, who shoots the dying Giants with arrows, while Zeus destroys many of them with thunderbolts.

giants in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where giants first appears

Abstract written by a model

Giants are presented as mortal, not a divine race; oversized bones were stored near Asclepius’s child-form temple. They aided and protected Rhea against Cronos, and the account ends by rejecting dragon-footed fables.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIX.

The text presents the giants as mortal rather than divine and says that Homer described the Læstrygones and Phæacians in connection with giants. It rejects the fable that the giants had dragons instead of feet.

the giants in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where the giants first appears

Abstract written by a model

The giants are monsters differing from men chiefly in size. They warred against the gods, sometimes forcing them to flee and hide; Briareus had a hundred arms and Typhon breathed fire. They piled Ossa on Pelion to reach heaven before thunderbolts subdued them.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter X

The giants warred against the gods and at times forced them to flee and hide under various forms. They included Briareus, who had a hundred arms, and Typhon, who breathed fire; they also tried to reach heaven by piling Ossa upon Pelion before being subdued by thunderbolts.
