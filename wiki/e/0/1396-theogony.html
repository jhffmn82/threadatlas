Theogony

topic · 1 work · 2 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Hesiodic _Theogony_, _Theogony_, divine genealogy, holy race, offspring, race of the blessed gods, race of the deathless gods, the _Theogony_
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the divine genealogy traces the immortals from Chaos, Earth, Tartarus, and Eros through the births of the Titans, Olympians, rivers, goddesses, and monsters, and records how the gods divided honors and how Zeus came to rule.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Theogony is a divine genealogy presented as a song of the blessed gods’ race; it begins with their origins and continues through the gods’ births, honors, divisions of power, and taking of Olympus, preceding the Catalogues.

Theogony in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Theogony is a divine genealogy presented as a song of the blessed gods’ race. It begins with their origins, asks the Muses which beings first came to be, and continues through the gods’ births, honors, divisions of power, and taking of Olympus. In the edition, it precedes the Catalogues.

PREFACE

The Theogony precedes the Catalogues in the arrangement adopted for the edition.

BIBLIOGRAPHY

The Theogony is represented by papyri from the first century B.C. or first century A.D. through the fifth century and by medieval manuscripts. Rudolf Rzach divides the listed manuscripts into two main families, with the groups Ωa, Ωb, Ωc, and Ψ.

THE THEOGONY

The theogony is presented as a song about the race of the blessed gods, beginning with their origins and continuing through their births, honors, and divisions of power. The Muses are asked to tell which beings first came to be and how the gods took Olympus.
