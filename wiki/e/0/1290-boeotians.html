Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the Boeotians are presented as practical and unromantic people whose resistance to conventional Homeric poetry fostered the Boeotian School. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, the Boeotians formed alliances, fought wars, intervened militarily, supported the temple and Amphictyonic decrees against Phocis, aided Philip, joined Athens at Chaeronea, possessed Theban territory after Thebes’ destruction, and later left their league with Cassander. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Boeotians were a hardy Greek people who fought Athens and Lacedaemon, defeated the Athenians at Delium, defeated Lysander, joined the anti-Lacedaemonian confederacy, fought at Aricas, Nemea, and Coronea, and helped rebuild Athens’ walls.

Boeotians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Boeotians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Boeotians are a people defined in these records by shifting alliances, wars, and military interventions. They first appear allied with the Phocians and Locrians under Epaminondas and Pelopidas, invading Laconia, helping rebuild Messene, and unsuccessfully attacking Corinth. They fought the Lacedemonians and their allies, endured attack and retreat in Thessaly, uncovered a conspiracy involving Orchomenian horsemen, and sent Pelopidas with seven thousand men to aid the Thessalians. Under Epaminondas they aided the Tegeans and defeated the Lacedaemonians at Mantinea, where he was mortally wounded; they also intervened in Euboea before returning home and disarming. Their central later conflict was the war against the Phocians. In support of the temple, oracle, and Amphictyonic decrees, they allied with Locrians and Amphictyons, raised armies, won several battles, invaded and plundered Phocis, but also suffered defeat at Coronea. Exhausted, they sought Philip's aid and supported him with the Thessalians at the war's end. They then joined Athens at Chaeronea after Demosthenes' appeal, suffered heavy losses, and made peace with Philip. After Thebes' destruction, they possessed its territory and opposed Athens, but were defeated near Phlitsa. Finally, after holding a garrison among the Chalcidians, fear of Demetrius led them to leave their league with Cassander.

CHAP. vn.

They joined the Phocians and Locrians in the expedition commanded by Epaminondas and Pelopidas. Their forces invaded Laconia, helped rebuild Messene, and later broke through the wall between Cenchreae and Lechaeum but failed to take Corinth.

CHAP. VIIL

The Boeotians fought the Lacedemonians and their allies and were also attacked by Alexander’s cavalry in Thessaly. When their allies deserted them and provisions ran short, Epaminondas rescued their army during its retreat.

CHAP. IX,

The Boeotians learned of the conspiracy involving the Orchomenian horsemen after some conspirators betrayed their fellows. They later held a general council and ordered Pelopidas to assist the Thessalians with seven thousand men.

CHAP. X.

The Boeotians appointed Epaminondas general and sent him with a strong army to aid the Tegeans. Their forces defeated the Lacedaemonians at Mantinea, although Epaminondas was mortally wounded in the victory.

CHAP. 11.

The Boeotians supported one party in the civil war in Euboea. After the conflict ended, they returned home and laid down their arms.

CHAP. VI.

The Boeotians resolved to relieve the temple and raised an army against Philomelus. They joined the Locrians and others in opposing the Phocians in defense of the oracle.

CHAP. VII.

The Boeotians joined the war against the Phocians out of religious concern and support for the Amphictyonic decrees. They defeated the Phocians in a battle in rough country and later defeated Onomarchus near Chaeronea. They also sent Pammenes with an army to assist Artabazus in Asia.

CHAP. Vlll.

The Boeotians repeatedly defeated the Phocians, including at Orchomenum, the river Cephisus, and Coronea, and killed or captured many of them. They surprised the Phocians at Abas, invaded Phocis, and plundered its countryside, while also fighting against the Lacedaemonians and their allies in the Peloponnesus.

CHAP. X.

The Boeotians raided Phocian territory and defeated the Phocians at Hyampolis, but were later defeated at Coronea. They also attacked Phocian towns in Boeotia, sought Philip's aid when the war exhausted them, and joined the Thessalians in supporting him at the war's end.

CHAP. XIV.

The Boeotians joined the Athenians after Demosthenes persuaded them to defend their common liberty. They fought beside the Athenians at Chaeronea, where many were killed or captured, and afterward made peace with Philip.

Boeotians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Boeotians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Boeotians were a Greek people of Boeotia, regarded as among the stoutest and hardiest in Greece. After many shook off Theban authority, they assembled armies in conflicts with Athens and Lacedaemonian forces. They were defeated by Athenians after a vineyard battle, but later under Pantoedas defeated Athenians at Delium, captured it, and used spoils for public works and games. They also defeated Lysander, joined the anti-Lacedaemonian confederacy, fought at Aricas, Nemea, and Coronea, and helped rebuild Athenian walls.

CHAP. XXIV.

Many Boeotians had shaken off Theban authority, prompting the Thebans to seek Lacedaemonian support. The Boeotians later assembled a large army against the Athenians, but were defeated after a day-long battle among their vineyards.

The Boeotians gathered a large army under Pantoedas and defeated the Athenians at Delium. They pursued the Athenians, killed many of them, captured Delium after further fighting, and used the spoils to build and adorn public works and establish games.

CHAP. vm.

The Boeotians drew out their forces and were joined by the Athenians when Haliartus was besieged. They defeated Lysander and the Lacedaemonians, though two hundred Thebans later died after falling down steep precipices.

CHAP. IX,

The Boeotians joined the anti-Lacedaemonian confederacy and fought at Aricas, the river Nemea, and Coronea. They later assisted the Corinthian faction opposed by the Lacedaemonians and sent five hundred carpenters and masons to help rebuild the Athenian walls.
