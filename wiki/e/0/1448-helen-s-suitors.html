Helen’s suitors

group · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: all who came for the maid, heroes, suitors, suitors of Helen, the suitors, the wooers

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the suitors of Helen swore Helen’s suitor oath at Tyndareus’ command and were ultimately defeated by Menelaus in the suit. In The Library, Helen’s suitors were Greek kings and heroes who sought Helen’s hand and swore to defend the chosen bridegroom against wrong concerning the marriage.

Helen’s suitors in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Helen’s suitors first appears

Abstract written by a model

Helen’s suitors were Greek kings and heroes who came to Sparta seeking her hand. Because Tyndareus feared their rivalry, he made them swear to defend whichever bridegroom was chosen against wrong concerning the marriage.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Εἰσὶ δὲ οἱ λέγοντες ᾿Αφαρέα μὲν καὶ Λεύκιππον

Helen’s suitors included many kings and heroes, among them Odysseus, Diomedes, Antilochus, Agamemnon, Menelaus, Ajax, and others named in the unit. Tyndareus feared that choosing one suitor would cause the others to quarrel, so he had them swear to defend the chosen bridegroom.
