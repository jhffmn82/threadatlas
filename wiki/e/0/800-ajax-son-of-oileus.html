Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, Ajax son of Oileus commands the Locrians and fights as a swift Achaean warrior, killing several Trojans and competing in the footrace. In The Library, Ajax son of Oileus violates Cassandra while she clings to Athena's wooden image. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Ajax the son of Oileus appears near an altar bound by an oath concerning Cassandra's violation, and later watches dice players while hating Odysseus.

Ajax son of Oileus in The Iliad

Homer · 749 to 724 BC · the work’s page · read it where Ajax son of Oileus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ajax son of Oileus is a swift, impetuous Argive and Achaean warrior who commands the Locrians and their forty ships. Smaller than Ajax son of Telamon and wearing a linen breastplate, he nevertheless excels Hellenes and Achaeans in spear use. In battle he stands continually beside Ajax son of Telamon, while his bow- and sling-armed Locrians remain behind. Recognizing Neptune in Calchas’s likeness, he is stirred to fight, throws at Hector, helps recover Imbrius, and beheads him for Amphimachus. He kills Satnius and Cleobulus, and, when the Trojans flee in panic, kills more than any other through swift pursuit. His speed also leads the running contest until he slips near the finish, loses to Ulysses, and says Minerva favored Ulysses. He is Medon’s brother.

Ajax the son of Oileus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Ajax the son of Oileus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ajax the son of Oileus stands near an altar holding a shield and is bound by an oath concerning the violation of Cassandra, administered by the sons of Atreus. Later he appears wet with sea foam, like a shipwrecked man, watching others play dice and hating Odysseus for urging the Greeks to stone him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXVI.

Ajax the son of Oileus stands near the altar with a shield in his hand and takes an oath concerning the violation of Cassandra. The sons of Atreus administer the oath to him.

CHAPTER XXXI.

Ajax the son of Oileus is shown looking at the others playing dice, with the appearance of a shipwrecked man and a body wet with sea foam. He hated Odysseus because Odysseus urged the Greeks to stone him for his rape of Cassandra.
