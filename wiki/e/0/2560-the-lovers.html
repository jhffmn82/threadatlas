the lovers in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

The lovers are sources of profit in the courtesan’s trade, required to give presents and payments for favor. They are attracted, deceived, kept uncertain, and made jealous through feigned affection, refusal, and rivalry, so their love and spending continue.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ELEGY VII.

The lovers are treated as sources of profit who should be attracted, deceived, kept uncertain, and made jealous. Dipsas advises admitting those who bring gifts, refusing those who merely plead, pretending anger, and maintaining rivalry so that their love does not fade.
