Throne Room in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04

The Throne Room is a large round chamber with a high arched roof, emerald-covered walls, ceiling, and floor, a bright roof light, and a green marble throne. Oz receives visitors there in changing forms until the travelers find it empty and the Lion topples a screen, exposing the little man concealed behind it.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter XI

The Throne Room is a large round chamber whose walls, ceiling, and floor are covered with emeralds. A bright light in the roof makes the emeralds sparkle, and a green marble throne stands in the center. Oz receives each visitor there while taking a different form.

Chapter XV

The travelers enter the Throne Room expecting to see one of Oz's previous forms but initially find it empty. The Lion's roar knocks over a screen there, revealing the little man who has been pretending to be Oz.

Throne Room in Little Wizard Stories of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1913

The Throne Room, a great royal audience room in the palace in Emerald City, is where Princess Ozma daily hears her people’s troubles, guarded by the Cowardly Lion and Hungry Tiger.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE COWARDLY LION AND THE HUNGRY TIGER

The Throne Room is where Princess Ozma listens to the troubles of her people for an hour each day. The Cowardly Lion and Hungry Tiger serve as guardians there, although they usually have nothing to do except look fierce and remain quiet.
