Polydeuces

person · 3 works · 21 quoted facts · 8 connections
Also called: POLYDEUCES, prize-winning Polydeuces, son of Tyndareus, strong Polydeuces, the hero, the son of Tyndareus

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Polydeuces is a Tyndarid and Dioscurus, son of Zeus and also called son of Tyndareus, who rides swift horses, joins Castor in their exploits, avenges Castor’s death, and receives shared immortality from Zeus. In The Electra of Euripides, Polydeuces is a god and Clytemnestra’s maternal brother who appears with Castor as a divine Horseman, reveals murder’s consequences, promises Orestes protection, and guards holy, truthful strivers. In The Argonautica, Polydeuces is a son of Leda, Tyndareus, and Zeus who excels at horsemanship and boxing, kills Amycus, Itymoneus, and Mimas, and is welcomed and worshiped as a god by the Mariandyni.

Polydeuces in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · about 699 BC · the work’s page · read it where Polydeuces first appears

Abstract written by a model

Polydeuces is a blameless, prize-winning member of the Tyndaridae and Dioscuri, named with his brother Castor. He is presented as son of Zeus, born to Leda beneath Taygetus, yet also called son and child of Tyndareus. A rider of swift horses, he joins Castor in trying to make Philoctetes their brother and in stealing Idas and Lynceus’s cattle. After Castor is killed, Polydeuces kills Lynceus and Idas; Zeus grants the brothers immortality every other day.

Polydeuces in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · 299 to 200 BC · the work’s page · read it where Polydeuces first appears

Abstract written by a model

Polydeuces is a son of Leda, Tyndareus, and Zeus, skilled in guiding swift-footed steeds and boxing. As an Argonaut champion, he accepts Amycus’s challenge, withstands his attacks, and kills him with a blow above the ear. During fighting with the Bebrycians he kills Itymoneus and Mimas; the Mariandyni then welcome and worship him as a god, and song celebrates him.
