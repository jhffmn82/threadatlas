Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, Automedon is Patroclus's trusted charioteer and comrade, drives Achilles' horses, and fights alongside Alcimedon after Patroclus's death while grieving him. In The Fall of Troy, Automedon drives Neoptolemus' immortal horses toward the fighting and identifies Deiphobus and the Trojan chief for his lord.

Automedon in The Iliad

Homer · 749 to 724 BC · the work’s page · read it where Automedon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Automedon is Patroclus's trusted charioteer and comrade, son of Diores, who drives the chariot drawn by Achilles' horses. After Patroclus's death, he struggles to fight and control the horses alone; with Alcimedon holding the reins, he kills Aretus, yet remains grief-stricken for Patroclus.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Such fearful turmoil of men and horses did Jove on that day

Automedon tries to drive Achilles' horses and fight the Trojans at the same time, but he cannot wield his spear effectively while controlling the chariot alone. After Alcimedon takes the reins, Automedon kills Aretus and strips off his armour, though he says the victory does little to ease his grief for Patroclus.
