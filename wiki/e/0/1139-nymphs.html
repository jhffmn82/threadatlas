Nymphs

Also called: Naiads of the ocean, Nymphs of the ocean, charming Nymphs, clear-voiced nymphs, nymphs, ocean Nymphs, sea Nymphs, the ocean Nymphs
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, nymphs wander wooded glades and sheer cliff edges, dancing and singing with Pan beside dark water, and celebrate blessed gods, high Olympus, and Hermes.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Nymphs receive garlands from Dryope, inhabit a cave before Pan, flee after being insulted by an Apulian shepherd, recover, and dance as he grotesquely imitates them.

nymphs in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Nymphs wander wooded glades and sheer cliff edges, dancing and singing with Pan beside dark water; clear-voiced and nimble-footed, they celebrate blessed gods, high Olympus, and Hermes.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

XIX. TO PAN

The nymphs wander and dance with Pan on sheer cliff edges and sing with him beside a spring of dark water. They have clear voices and nimble feet, and they join Pan in songs of the blessed gods, high Olympus, and Hermes.

Nymphs in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Nymphs receive garlands from Dryope and inhabit a cave before Pan; insulted by an Apulian shepherd, they flee, recover, and dance as he grotesquely imitates them.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLES VII. AND VIII. [XIV.441-526]

Nymphs once possessed the cavern later occupied by Pan. When an Apulian shepherd insults them, they initially flee, then regain their composure and dance while he imitates them with grotesque movements.
