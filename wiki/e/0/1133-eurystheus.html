  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Eurystheus is king of Argos and Mycenae who commands Hercules’ twelve labours, banishes him and the Heraclidae, leads an army against them, and is killed by Hyllus.
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Eurystheus is the tyrant of Argos and Mycenae who pursues Hercules’ children, leads an army against Athens, is defeated and captured, and grants that his buried corpse will guard Athens.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Eurystheus is king of Mycenae and Argos, commands the Argive forces, persecutes Hercules and his children, invades Athens, is captured, foretells that his tomb will protect Athens, and is ordered executed by Alcmena.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Eurystheus is a king who commands Heracles to travel over land and sea and perform his labors.
  • In The Library, Eurystheus is the son of Sthenelus who reigns over Mycenae and Tiryns, assigns Hercules’ labours, demands his sons, wages war with the Athenians, and is killed along with his sons.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Eurystheus is Sthenelus’s son and king of Mycenae who persecutes Hercules and, at Juno’s command, orders his labours.

  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Eurystheus is Hercules’s cousin who, through Juno’s influence, commands the twelve Labors and receives the treasures Hercules brings back.

Eurystheus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Eurystheus is king of Argos and king of Mycenae, brought forth before his full time through Juno's intervention. He commands Hercules to perform twelve labours, including killing the Nemean lion; he imposes the tenth task, driving away Gerion's oxen, because he thinks it too great for Hercules, and later orders Hercules to bring Cerberus from hell. Terrified by Hercules' return with the Erymanthian boar, he later banishes Hercules and others from the kingdom. Fearing the Heraclidae will expel him, he orders their banishment from Greece and leads an army against them. They rout his forces, and Hyllus kills him after his chariot breaks during flight; his sons also fall.

Eurystheus in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Eurystheus is a king and tyrant of Argos and Mycenae, a foe of Alcmene, Hercules, and Hercules’ sons. He commands Hercules to bring captive horses, then, after Hercules’ death, pursues the children from city to city, sends Copreus to seize them, and seeks their murder because they may avenge their father. Waiting near Alcathous’ realm, he threatens Athens and leads an Argive army against it. He refuses Hyllus’s challenge, is defeated, captured, chained, and brought before Alcmene. As a suppliant, he accepts Athens’ protection and gives Phoebus’s oracle that his buried corpse will guard Athens against Hercules’ descendants, becoming its guardian after death.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

THE CHILDREN OF HERCULES.

Eurystheus has pursued Iolaus and the children of Hercules from city to city and sent Copreus to bring them back. He waits near Alcathous’ realm and threatens Athens through the promised arrival of an Argive army under his command.

DEMOPHOON, IOLAUS, CHORUS.

Eurystheus leads the Argive forces toward Athens and observes the routes by which his troops may attack. Macaria says that he seeks the death of the children because they are the offspring of a noble race and remember their father’s wrongs. Iolaus also describes him as unwilling to face the dangers of the battlefield.

SERVANT, ALCMENA, CHORUS.

Eurystheus refuses Hyllus's challenge and is defeated, captured, chained, and brought before Alcmene. He defends his actions as those of a threatened enemy, accepts that Athens spared him under its laws, and reveals an oracle stating that his buried corpse will protect Athens against the descendants of the race he opposed.

Eurystheus in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Eurystheus is king of Mycenæ and Argos, commander of the Argive forces, and a deliberate enemy of Hercules, Iolaus, and Hercules' children. Though Alcmena's cousin by birth, he drives the children of Hercules from their land, repeatedly demands their surrender, and seeks their deaths to prevent vengeance. He leads an Argive invasion against Athens, positioning his army while hoping to capture and insult Iolaus. After battle he is captured alive and brought before Alcmena. He says Juno compelled his actions and claims Athens cannot lawfully execute him. He reveals that his tomb at Pallene will make him a protector of Athens after death while harming its descendants' enemies, and foretells offerings with evil consequences. Alcmena orders his execution.

Eurystheus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Eurystheus, son of Sthenelus and born seven months before the expected birth of Heracles, is the commander who orders and imposes Hercules’s labours. He begins by demanding the Nemean lion’s hide and, frightened by Hercules’s prowess, bars him from Mycenae and requires displays outside its gates, hiding in a bronze jar. He uses Copreus as herald, assigns successive labours, and controls their count: he rejects the hydra because Iolaus helped and rejects tasks done for hire, while receiving the Cretan Bull and mares. He receives and sacrifices Geryon’s red kine to Hera, then orders an eleventh labour for golden apples and receives them. Later he demands the surrender of Hercules’s sons and threatens war; after his own sons are killed, he flees but is pursued and killed by Hyllus, and his head is given to Alcmena.

7 sections; each heading opens the text

THE LIBRARY, II. tv. 7-8

Eurystheus commands Hercules to perform the labours. His first command is that Hercules bring back the hide of the Nemean lion.

V. When Hercules heard that, he went to Tiryns

Eurystheus orders Hercules to perform the labours. After seeing the Nemean lion, he is so afraid of Hercules’ strength that he forbids him to enter the city and orders him to display the labours before the gates; he also refuses to count the hydra among the ten because Iolaus helped.

THE LIBRARY, II. v. 4-5

Eurystheus assigns the fifth through ninth labours to Hercules. He refuses to count the cleansing of Augeas's cattle-yard and Hercules's killing of Eurytion among the ten because they were performed for hire, but he receives the Cretan Bull and the man-eating mares.

THE LIBRARY, II. v. 9

Eurystheus receives the kine brought back by Hercules and sacrifices them to Hera.

When the labours had been performed in eight

After Hercules had completed his labours in eight years and a month, Eurystheus ordered him to fetch the golden apples as an eleventh labour.

THE LIBRARY, 11. v. 11

Eurystheus receives the golden apples from Hercules.

THE LIBRARY, IIL. vir. 7

Eurystheus demanded that the sons of Hercules be surrendered and threatened war when they were sheltered by Ceyx. After his sons were killed by the Athenians, Eurystheus fled in a chariot, but Hyllus pursued and killed him; his head was given to Alcmena.
