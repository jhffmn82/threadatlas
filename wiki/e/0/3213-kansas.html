Abstract written by a model

In Scientific American Supplement No. 360, November 25, 1882, Kansas is reported to have 138,561 farms, compared with 38,202 in 1870. In The Wonderful Wizard of Oz, Kansas is Dorothy’s dry, gray home country and the place she longs to return to. In Dorothy and the Wizard in Oz, Kansas is Dorothy’s intended destination, where she is expected to reunite with Aunt Em and Uncle Henry. In The Road to Oz, Kansas is an unromantic state containing a farmhouse and Uncle Henry’s farm. In The Emerald City of Oz, Kansas is the place where Dorothy Gale, Aunt Em, and Uncle Henry’s farm is threatened by poverty and foreclosure.

Kansas in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04 · the work’s page · read it where Kansas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Kansas is Dorothy’s dry, gray home country, a prairie place with dreary homes and people of flesh and blood. Though less beautiful than the Land of Oz, it matters most to Dorothy because it is home: she believes there is no place like it and wants to live there with Aunt Em and Uncle Henry. From the beginning, Dorothy seeks the Great Oz’s help to return to Kansas with Toto. She repeatedly longs for it, grows sad when captivity seems to make return harder, and continues traveling toward the Land of the South after Oz cannot identify its direction or carry her across the desert. The Winged Monkeys also cannot take her there. At the end, the Silver Shoes carry Dorothy to Kansas, where she arrives on the prairie near Uncle Henry’s new farmhouse.

Kansas in The Emerald City of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1910 · the work’s page · read it where Kansas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Kansas is the place of Dorothy Gale, Aunt Em, and Uncle Henry’s farm. Poor crops and too little rain leave the family in poverty; the farm cannot support them, Uncle Henry cannot repay borrowed money, and it is going to be taken, so Dorothy concludes they cannot remain there safely.
