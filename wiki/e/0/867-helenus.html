Helenus in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Helenus is a royal Trojan prophet and priest serving Phoebus who rules Buthrotus in Chaonia, reviving the Trojan name in Greece. Married to Andromache after Pyrrhus relinquishes her, he receives Aeneas, gives guidance on Italy’s perils and route, and equips the Trojans for their journey.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK III

Helenus rules at Buthrotus in Chaonia and is described as having revived the Trojan name in Greece. He receives Aeneas and his companions, and Aeneas asks him for prophetic guidance about the dangers and route to Italy.

“The prophet first with sacrifice adores

Helenus gives Aeneas a prophecy about the route to Italy, the sow omen, hostile shores, Scylla and Charybdis, Juno, and the Sibyl at Cumae. He also supplies the Trojans with treasure, arms, ships' crews, pilots, horses, and other warlike stores.

Helenus in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Helenus, Priam’s son and wisest augur, is a leading Trojan and Lycian in battle and counsel. Wounded in the hand but alive, he tells Hector and Aeneas to rally Trojans and seek Minerva’s protection through offerings.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK VI.

Helenus, described as the wisest of augurs, tells Hector and Aeneas to rally the Trojans. He directs Hector to ask the women of Ilius to offer a robe and twelve yearling heifers to Minerva for protection.

Helenus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Helenus is a seer who knew oracles protecting Troy. After Deiphobus was preferred over him in marriage to Helen, he left Troy for Ida, was captured by Odysseus, and under compulsion revealed how Ilium could be taken. In one account he received Hecuba, crossed to Chersonese, and buried her. He later traveled with Neoptolemus to Molossia, married Deidamia, and founded and inhabited a city there.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

E 8 |’ ‘Hoy δὲ ὄντος τοῦ πολέμου δεκαετοῦς ἀθυμοῦσι

After Deiphobus was preferred over him in marriage to Helen, Helenus left Troy and stayed on Ida. Odysseus captured him and brought him to the Greek camp, where he was forced to reveal how Ilium could be taken.

EPITOME, v. 22-23

Some accounts say that Helenus received Hecuba, crossed with her to the Chersonese, and buried her after she became a bitch.
