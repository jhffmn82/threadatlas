Christmas Eve in The Life and Adventures of Santa Claus

L. Frank Baum · 1902

Christmas Eve is the annual night of Claus's journey. It is the time when he may use up to ten deer to draw his sledge and travels with toys or gifts to sleeping children, farmhouses, a village, and children around the world before daybreak. On his first journey, he leaves toys; on this night, stockings are first hung by chimneys and filled during his hurried visit. In later years, children hang stockings expecting him, while four little folk help complete the yearly work.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

10. Christmas Eve

Christmas Eve is established as the one night each year when Claus may use up to ten deer to draw his sledge. Claus's first journey on Christmas Eve takes him to farmhouses and a village, where he leaves toys for sleeping children.

11. How the First Stockings Were Hung by the Chimneys

On Christmas Eve, Santa Claus travels with his ten reindeer and a large load of toys to visit children around the world. The first stockings are hung by the chimney on this night, and Santa Claus fills them during his hurried visit. In later years, children hang stockings in anticipation of his arrival.

3. The Deputies of Santa Claus

On Christmas Eve, Santa Claus travels in his sledge to deliver gifts. The four little folk join him on this yearly journey and help him finish his work before daybreak.
