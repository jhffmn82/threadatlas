Amphinomus

Also called: Amphinomus of Dulichium, Amphinomus the son of Nisus, the son of Nisus

Abstract written by a model

Amphinomus, son of Nisus of Dulichium, is a foremost suitor of Penelope, favored by her in conversation and judged by Ulysses to have good understanding and a good natural disposition. He notices Telemachus’s return, opposes killing him without Jove’s approval, and aids Ulysses with food and wine. Warned of Ulysses’s return, he grows foreboding, but Minerva has doomed him to die by Telemachus.

Amphinomus is in one work on this site, The Odyssey. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Thus did they converse, and meanwhile the ship which had brought

Amphinomus notices that Telemachus's ship has entered the harbour and tells the suitors that they need not send a message. He opposes killing Telemachus without first consulting the gods, though he says he will help if the oracles of Jove approve.

BOOK XVIII

Amphinomus brings Ulysses two loaves and a golden goblet of wine, expressing hope that the stranger's fortunes will improve. Ulysses praises his understanding and warns him about the suitors and Ulysses's imminent return. Amphinomus becomes serious and foreboding, but Minerva has doomed him to be killed by Telemachus.
