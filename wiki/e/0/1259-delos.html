Abstract written by a model

Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica describes Delos as a sea-girt, rocky island that receives Apollo after Leto promises it an altar, precinct, and oracle, and that becomes the site of the Delian festival. The Extant Odes of Pindar describes Delos as a rocky, sacred island of Apollo and Artemis, once drifting before becoming immovable when Leto arrived, and as the Apollonian island for which Pindar intended a song. The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books describes Delos as the location selected for a common treasury holding money collected for the public service of the allied cities, whose contributions under Aristides reached 560 talents. The Aeneid describes Delos as an Aegean island containing Apollo's oracle that welcomes Aeneas as he seeks guidance on a permanent Trojan home. Heroides and Amores describes Delos as a sacred island containing Diana's temple, other holy sites, offerings, statues, and the tree beside which the goddess gave birth, visited by Cydippe. The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV describes Delos as Apollo's city and island, ruled by the priest-king Anius and visited by Æneas and his companions for worship, oracle guidance, and an exchange of gifts. The Library says that Delos, formerly called Asteria, was where Leto fled Hera and gave birth first to Artemis and later to Apollo, and where Artemis killed Orion and Dawn brought Orion. Description of Greece describes Delos as a former Greek emporium attacked and destroyed by Menophanes, who killed inhabitants, robbed merchants and offerings, enslaved women and children, and cast Apollo's statue into the sea.

and 1 more work

Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II describes Delos as an emporium of all Greece with Athenian temple guards and Apollo's games, instituted by Theseus after his return from Crete.

Delos in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · 497 BC · the work’s page · read it where Delos first appears

Abstract written by a model

Delos is a rocky island, sacred to Apollo and native to Artemis, also called Asteria. It is a god-reared daughter of the sea, an earth-shoot dear to Leto’s children, and a marvel of wide earth; mortals call it Delos, while the gods call it a far-seen star of the dark earth. Once it drifted before waves and winds. When Leto arrived, four pillars with adamantine bases supported it, making it immoveable. It is also the Apollonian island for which Pindar intended a song.
