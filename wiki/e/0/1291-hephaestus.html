  • In The Argonautica, Hephaestus is a craftsman-god associated with fire and metalworking who influences the Lemnian women, stops his forge, and watches the Argonauts pass.
  • In Four Plays of Aeschylus, Hephaestus is Prometheus's kin and a god of fire and the forge who reluctantly binds Prometheus to a rock at Zeus's command.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Hephaestus is a famed craftsman and inventor who teaches crafts, is husband of Aglaea, guides Orion through Cedalion, and makes armor and a shield for Heracles.
  • In The Library, Hephaestus is a deity, craftsman, and maker who is involved in numerous divine and heroic events, including binding Prometheus and making weapons and other objects.
  • In The Library, Hephaestus is Hera's son, also said to be Zeus's son, whom Zeus casts from heaven and who falls on Lemnos before Thetis saves him.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Hephæstus is a maker, called a son of Talos, credited with several notable objects and with representing Linus's song.

Hephaestus in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · -0299/-0200

Hephaestus is a god associated with bronze anvils, fire, iron hammers, and smoke-grimed bellows. Through Cypris’s intervention he causes the Lemnian women’s alliance with the Argonauts, then stops his forge at Iris’s message and watches Argo pass.

But they were not unmarked by the spouse of Zeus, son of Cronos; but

At Iris’s message, Hephaestus ceases the work of his iron hammers and stops the blasts of his smoke-grimed bellows. He later watches from a rock as the Nereids carry Argo through the Wandering rocks.

Hephaestus in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

Hephaestus, a god and Prometheus’s kin, possesses fire as craft and forge power; compelled by Zeus, he reluctantly binds the pitied Prometheus to a rock.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

PROMETHEUS BOUND

Hephaestus binds Prometheus to the rock with adamantine chains, wedges, and shackles under Zeus’s command. He pities Prometheus and regrets the pain he must inflict, but says that necessity and fear of Zeus compel him to act.

Hephaestus in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Hephaestus is a craftsman and famed worker, more skilled in crafts than all the sons of Heaven. He is famed for inventions, teaches men glorious gifts and crafts with Athene, and is husband of Aglaea. He gives Cedalion to guide Orion and, at Zeus’s command, makes Heracles’ armor and an intricate shield.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE SHIELD OF HERACLES

Hephaestus makes Heracles’ bronze greaves and fashions the adamantine helmet and the great shield. His work on the shield includes intricate images of warfare, gods, death, cities, agriculture, contests, and Ocean.

XX. TO HEPHAESTUS

Hephaestus is famed for inventions and as a worker. Together with Athene, he taught men glorious gifts and crafts, and the speaker asks him to grant success and prosperity.

Hephaestus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Hephaestus is a deity, born to Hera without intercourse and also described as son of Zeus and Hera. Lame, he is hurled from heaven by Zeus and falls to Lemnos. He shares a workshop with Athena, desires her, and is named father of Erichthonius, Palaemon, and Periphetes. He helps at Athena’s birth, kills Mimas, nails Prometheus to Caucasus, and reproaches him for stealing fire. A maker and giver, he makes weapons, Harmonia’s necklace, Achilles’ armour, gives Talos to Minos, dries Scamander, and is abandoned by Aphrodite.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Κέκροπος δὲ ἀποθανόντος Κραναὸς «ἐβασί-

Hephaestus is named as the father of Erichthonius in both accounts: with Atthis in one account and with Athena in another. In the account involving Athena, she comes to him because she wants weapons to be made.

IIJ.—Myrus oF THE ORIGIN OF FIRE

Hephaestus shares a workshop with Athena, from which Prometheus steals fire according to Plato. Lucian represents Hephaestus as reproaching Prometheus for taking the fire and leaving his forge cold; Cicero’s reference to the “Lemnian theft” connects the fire with Hephaestus’s forge in Lemnos.

Hephæstus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Hephæstus, called a son of Talos, is a maker credited with Zeus’s sceptre, Agamemnon’s sceptre, a brazen bowl, and a Troy-brought chest; Agamemnon’s sceptre is probably his only extant work. He also represents Linus’s song.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XLI.

Hephæstus is credited with making the sceptre of Agamemnon, a brazen bowl shown at Patara, and a chest brought from Troy by Eurypylus. The text concludes that the sceptre is probably the only work of Hephæstus still extant.
