mountain Nymphs in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Mountain Nymphs are divine, long-lived beings born from daughters of Hecaterus, neither mortal nor immortal. Living on Mount Ida, they raise Aeneas, eat heavenly food and dance among immortals; their lives begin and end linked to trees.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

V. TO APHRODITE

The mountain Nymphs who inhabit Mount Ida are to bring up and rear Aeneas. They are described as neither mortal nor immortal, living for a long time, eating heavenly food, and dancing among the immortals. Their lives are linked to trees that spring up with them and die when their own fates approach.

Nymphs in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Nymphs throw Rhea’s afterbirth into Lymax, inspire prophetic people, and are linked to Mount Ida through the second Herophile’s mother and to Herophile’s tomb.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XII.

The second Herophile said that her mother was one of the Nymphs of Mount Ida and that she was connected with Mount Ida through her mother. Nymphs were also associated with her tomb, and other prophetic figures were said to have been inspired by Nymphs.
