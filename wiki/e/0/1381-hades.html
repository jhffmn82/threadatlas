Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, Hades is both the god who rules the dead and the dark house and realm of the dead containing Patroclus’s spirit. In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Hades is a god who rules the dead beneath the earth, abducts Persephone with Zeus’s permission, and secretly gives her a pomegranate seed. In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Erebus is a misty, gloomy underworld region from which Persephone returns to the light. In The Odyssey, Hades is the dark, inaccessible house and realm of the dead that Ulysses must visit before returning home. In The Odyssey, the house of Hades is a place of the dead from which Ulysses and his crew return alive. In The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies, Hades is a realm of death where the dead face judgment and punishment. In Alcestis, Hades is the house of the dead beneath the earth to which Thanatos takes the dead and toward whose palaces Alcestis travels. In Alcestis, Sunless Houses is the dark underworld of tears and hungry dead, where shadows are carried across the Tears that Well.

In The Library, Hades is an abode ruled by Pluto where Theseus and Pirithous are detained and where punishments and the stone of Ascalaphus are found. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the Infernal Regions are the underworld ruled by Pluto, containing Styx and the shades, which Orpheus enters to seek Eurydice. In The Library, Hades is the place to which Hercules descends to fetch Cerberus and where he finds Theseus and Pirithous bound. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Hades is the setting for scenes of the dead and their punishments, including the demon Eurynomus. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Erebus is the underworld ruled by Pluto and Proserpine, which Psyche visits to deliver Venus’s request. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Hades is the infernal regions entered through a cave near Lake Avernus, containing the dead, Tartarus, Elysium, and places of purification.

Hades in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · about 699 BC · the work’s page · read it where Hades first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hades is a god, brother of Demeter and Zeus, born to Cronos, who swallows him and later brings him forth. Assigned the underworld, he rules the dead from beneath the earth, where his house with Persephone is guarded by a fearful hound. Called Aidoneus and Host of Many, he abducts Persephone in his golden chariot with Zeus’s permission and becomes her husband. When Zeus orders her release, Hades obeys but secretly gives her a pomegranate seed, promising her rule and honors among the deathless gods.
