Mrs. Stapleton in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901/1902

Mrs. Stapleton is the woman living at Merripit House on the moor with Jack Stapleton, publicly presented as his sister but later revealed to be his wife. Beautiful, fascinating, tropical, and exotic in appearance, she is devoted to Nature and shares books and intimate knowledge of Sir Charles with Stapleton. Despite this household connection, she fears Stapleton and becomes a victim of his deception and ill-treatment. She first approaches Dr. Watson on the moor to urge him to leave for London and to take Sir Henry away. When Stapleton appears, she hides what she has done, and later qualifies her warning as fear connected with the Baskerville curse, explaining that Stapleton would be angry if he knew. She also earnestly warns Sir Henry himself to leave the dangerous moor, though Stapleton restrains the development of Sir Henry’s attraction to her. She believes in the hound, unlike Watson. Her apparent role as Stapleton’s sister is exposed as a device: she is his wife, kept seemingly free so that she will be more useful to him. Near the end, she is discovered at Merripit House bound to a beam, gagged, bruised, and tortured. She tells Holmes of Stapleton’s intended refuge, then eagerly guides Holmes and Watson along Stapleton’s track through the bog, her relief revealing the horror of her life with him.

Beryl Stapleton lives with her brother and says that she is devoted to Nature and is never dull. She first warns Dr. Watson to take Sir Henry away from the moor, then follows Watson to retract or qualify the warning, saying it arose from fears connected with the Baskerville curse. She says her brother would be angry if he knew she had tried to make Sir Henry leave.

Chapter 8.

Miss Stapleton is described as beautiful, fascinating, tropical, and exotic, in contrast to her cool and unemotional brother. She appears to return Sir Henry Baskerville’s attraction, while Jack Stapleton seems intent on preventing their intimacy from developing.

Chapter 9.

Miss Stapleton meets Sir Henry on the moor and speaks earnestly about the danger of the place, urging him to leave. She appears reluctant when Sir Henry embraces her and accepts Stapleton’s intervention without protest.

Chapter 12.

Miss Stapleton is revealed by Sherlock Holmes to be Jack Stapleton’s wife rather than his sister. Stapleton has concealed the marriage because she would be more useful to him while appearing to be a free woman.

Chapter 13.

Mrs. Stapleton is revealed to be Mr. Stapleton's wife, although she has been presented as his sister. Holmes shows Mrs. Laura Lyons a photograph and descriptions identifying the couple as Mr. and Mrs. Vandeleur.

Chapter 14.

Mrs. Stapleton is found bound, gagged, and badly bruised in a room at Merripit House. She says Stapleton has tortured and deceived her, and she tells Holmes where he may have escaped.

Mrs. Stapleton guides Holmes and Watson to the pathway through the bog. She shows eagerness and joy in leading them onto her husband’s track, revealing the horror of her life with him.

Mrs. Stapleton was presented as Stapleton’s sister although she was his wife. She refused to lure Sir Charles, secretly tried to warn Sir Henry, and finally turned against Stapleton after learning of his intended crime and the hound’s presence.
