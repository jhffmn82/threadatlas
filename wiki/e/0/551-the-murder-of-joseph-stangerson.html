the murder of Joseph Stangerson in A Study in Scarlet

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1886

The murder of Joseph Stangerson, unexpectedly linked to the Drebber investigation, is reported by G. Lestrade after Stangerson is killed in his room at Halliday’s Private Hotel about six in the morning. He is stabbed through the heart; the murderer writes “RACHE” in blood, washes, wipes the knife, and escapes through a window. It is not robbery-motivated and gives Sherlock Holmes the poison pills.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

The murder of Joseph Stangerson is reported by G. Lestrade, who states that Stangerson was killed at Halliday’s Private Hotel about six o’clock in the morning.

CHAPTER VII.

The murder of Joseph Stangerson occurs in his room at Halliday’s Private Hotel. He is stabbed through the heart, while the murderer leaves “RACHE” written in blood and escapes through the window after washing his hands and wiping his knife.

CHAPTER VII.

The murder of Joseph Stangerson occurs after the Drebber investigation has begun and is described as an incident that Holmes could hardly have prevented. It gives Holmes possession of the pills connected with the case.
