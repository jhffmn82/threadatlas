Typhon in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

Typhon, a hundred-headed foe of the gods bred in a Kilikian cave, is bound beneath Aitna near Cumae, where he produces fire, smoke, and lava.

I.

Typhon is described as a foe of the gods with a hundred heads, bred in the Kilikian cave. He lies bound beneath Etna and the cliffs beyond Cumae, producing fire, smoke, and lava from beneath the mountain.

Typhon in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Typhon is a vast hybrid of man and beast, born in Cilicia from Earth and Tartarus after the Giants’ defeat. Larger and stronger than the Giants, he has a human shape to the thighs, wings, dragon heads, serpent coils, and fire-flashing eyes. Zeus wounds and pursues him, but Typhon overpowers Zeus and hides his severed hand and foot sinews in the Corycian cave. After Hermes and Aegipan restore Zeus, Zeus pursues Typhon until defeating and burying him under Mount Etna.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

VI. Περὶ μὲν οὖν Δήμητρος ταῦτα λέγεται"

Typhon is born in Cilicia from Earth and Tartarus after the defeat of the Giants. He is a vast hybrid of man and beast, with dragon heads, serpent coils, wings, fire-filled eyes, and great destructive power. Zeus wounds and pursues him, but Typhon severs Zeus’s hand and foot sinews and hides them in the Corycian cave; Hermes and Aegipan recover and restore them, after which Zeus pursues Typhon through several regions and finally buries him under Mount Etna.

VII. Προμηθεὺς δὲ ἐξ ὕδατος καὶ γῆς ἀνθρώ-

Typhon is pursued by Zeus after Zeus recovers his strength. He flees through Thrace and the Sicilian sea, and Zeus buries him under Mount Etna.
