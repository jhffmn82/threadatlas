person · 4 works · 39 quoted facts · 9 connections

  • In The Wonderful Wizard of Oz, Uncle Henry is a gray, stern Kansas farmer married to Aunt Em who works hard and protects the farm animals during a cyclone.

Uncle Henry in Ozma of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1907

Uncle Henry is a Kansas farmer weakened by overwork. He travels with his niece Dorothy Gale to Australia to rest and visit previously unseen relatives, then lives in Sydney. Ill, thin, sad, lonely, and worried about Dorothy, he is reunited with her when she returns to care for him. Her presence cheers him: he smiles, cuddles her, and says he already feels better.

Uncle Henry in The Emerald City of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1910

Uncle Henry is Dorothy Gale's old, poor Kansas uncle, Aunt Em's husband, and, with her, Dorothy's guardian. A farmer whose mortgaged farm produces too little, he works as hard as he can but is too feeble and unhealthy to continue easily. After a cyclone destroys his house, he cannot repay the loan and faces losing his home. He doubts Dorothy's earlier adventures, taking them for dreams, and worries that she may be mistreated in the fairyland she describes. Ozma brings him from his barn to the Emerald City's Throne Room, ending his financial danger and offering him a permanent palace home in Oz, where he need not work unless he wishes. Startled at first, he accepts the palace and Munchkin-style clothes, selects a blue satin suit, and independently dresses himself. He explores with Aunt Em, recognizes Dorothy's friend the Cowardly Lion, and later finds Oz preferable to Kansas. Yet idleness makes him restless; feeding chickens and joining Dorothy's journey give him activity. On that journey he marvels at the speaking Sawhorse, offers practical suggestions about the Fuddles, helps match Lord High Chigglewitz, welcomes Dorothy back, and criticizes needless delay in Rigmarole Town. He later travels with the Wizard, has traveled to California and Australia, and becomes Keeper of the Jewels in the Royal Storehouses.

Narrative, section by section

7 sections; each heading opens the text

2. How Uncle Henry Got Into Trouble

Uncle Henry is a poor, aging farmer whose health has become too weak for him to work easily. He mortgages his farm after a cyclone destroys his house and later faces losing the farm because he cannot repay the loan. He doubts that Dorothy's fairyland is real and worries that she might be mistreated there.

3. How Ozma Granted Dorothy's Request

Uncle Henry is poor because his Kansas farm does not produce enough, and he borrowed money against it. Since he cannot repay the loan, his farm is going to be taken, leaving him without a home. Ozma agrees to bring him to the Land of Oz, where he can live comfortably and need not work more than he chooses.

5. How Dorothy Became a Princess

Uncle Henry is transported from his barn in Kansas to the Throne Room in the Emerald City while wearing work clothes. He is startled by the palace and the animals, but Dorothy reassures him that he will live comfortably and happily in Oz without having to work until his back aches.

7. How Aunt Em Conquered the Lion

Uncle Henry accepts his new palace accommodations and clothing calmly, choosing a blue satin suit and refusing assistance from a servant. He explores the grounds with Aunt Em and helps recognize the Lion as Dorothy's friend. He explains that the Lion is the Cowardly Lion because he speaks meekly and looks ashamed.

9. How the Wogglebug Taught Athletics

Uncle Henry feels restless because he has no work to do in Oz, and Billina scolds him when he feeds the chickens between meals. He joins Dorothy's journey through the Land of Oz and is astonished that the wooden Sawhorse can think and speak. He says that he likes Oz better than Kansas and admires the Sawhorse.

12. How they Matched the Fuddles

Uncle Henry suggests that the Wizard might know about the Fuddles and later helps match Lord High Chigglewitz. He says that the Fuddles are more fun than playing solitaire or mumbletypeg.

22. How the Wizard Found Dorothy

Uncle Henry helps welcome Dorothy back and listens to her account of the day's adventures. In Rigmarole Town, he observes that the boy could have answered the Wizard's question with 'yes' or 'no' and later agrees that the group is wasting time.
