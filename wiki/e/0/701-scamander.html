Scamander in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Scamander is a fair river and river god at Troy, descended from Immortal Jove, brother to Simois, with warm and cold springs and a channel to the sea. Enraged by corpses, it floods Achilles and seeks Simois’s aid, but Vulcan scalds it into submission.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK XXI.

Xanthus is described as a river descended from Jove that becomes enraged by the corpses filling its waters. It attacks Achilles with a great flood and calls on Simois to help overwhelm him, but Juno sends Vulcan against it. Scalded by Vulcan's flames, Xanthus submits and promises not to help the Trojans again.

Xanthus in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

A divine, immortal river flowing from Ida; in Book IX, Deiphobus drives fleeing Achaeans into it, reddening and choking its streams with dead.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK IX

The Xanthus flows from Ida by the Dardanian Gate. Deiphobus drives fleeing Achaeans into it, and its streams are described as reddened and choked with the dead.
