observation and deduction

topic · 4 works · 3 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Holmes's deductive method, Holmes's method of deduction, You see, but you do not observe, analytical reasoning, deduce, deduction, deductions and the inferences, faculty of observation, habits of observation and inference, intuition, logical faculty, my method, observation, observation and analysis
and 12 moreobservation and inference, peculiar facility for deduction, read my train of thoughts, reading and re-reading, reading it off, the art of the detective, the distinction, the observation of trifles, the train of reasoning, theories, theories and fancies, thought reading
  • In A Study in Scarlet, it is treated by Sherlock Holmes as a practical skill essential to his livelihood, used to conclude that Dr. John Watson had come from Afghanistan.
  • In The Sign of the Four, it is presented as a pair of methods of reasoning: observation identifies facts and deduction infers conclusions.
  • In The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes, it is presented as Holmes's deductive method, based on observing trifles such as stride length, boot impressions, tobacco ash, and cigar remnants.
  • In The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes, it is described as a method of inference in which unspoken thoughts are inferred from observed actions, facial expressions, and eyes.
  • In The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes, it is demonstrated by Sherlock Holmes and Mycroft Holmes, with Mycroft described as possessing the abilities to a greater degree than Sherlock.

observation and deduction in A Study in Scarlet

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1886

Sherlock Holmes treats observation and deduction as practical skills essential to his livelihood. He explains that his reasoning about Dr. John Watson’s military and medical appearance, injuries, complexion, and recent hardships led him to conclude that Watson had come from Afghanistan.

observation and deduction in The Sign of the Four

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1890-02

is_a methods of reasoning [observation identifies facts and deduction infers conclusions] Holmes distinguishes observation from deduction: observation reveals details such as reddish earth on Watson’s instep, while deduction connects those details to conclusions such as a visit to the Wigmore Street Post-Office and the sending of a telegram. He says that the ideal detective needs observation, deduction, and knowledge.

Holmes's method of deduction in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

is_based_on observation of trifles Holmes bases his deductions on observation of trifles, including stride length, boot impressions, the direction of the fatal blow, tobacco ash, a cigar stump, and the cut end of a cigar. thought reading in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1892~/1893~

is_a method of inference Thought reading is described as the process of inferring another person’s unspoken thoughts from observed actions, facial expressions, and eyes. Sherlock Holmes demonstrates it by tracing Watson’s thoughts from a picture of General Gordon through Henry Ward Beecher and the Civil War to the idea that war is a preposterous way to settle disputes.
