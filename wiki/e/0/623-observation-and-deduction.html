observation and deduction

topic · 4 works · 3 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Holmes's deductive method, Holmes's method of deduction, You see, but you do not observe, analytical reasoning, deduce, deduction, deductions and the inferences, faculty of observation, habits of observation and inference, intuition, logical faculty, my method, observation, observation and analysis

Abstract written by a model

In A Study in Scarlet, it is treated by Sherlock Holmes as a practical skill essential to his livelihood, used to conclude that Dr. John Watson had come from Afghanistan. In The Sign of the Four, it is presented as a pair of methods of reasoning: observation identifies facts and deduction infers conclusions. In The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes, it is presented as Holmes's deductive method, based on observing trifles such as stride length, boot impressions, tobacco ash, and cigar remnants. In The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes, it is described as a method of inference in which unspoken thoughts are inferred from observed actions, facial expressions, and eyes. In The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes, it is demonstrated by Sherlock Holmes and Mycroft Holmes, with Mycroft described as possessing the abilities to a greater degree than Sherlock.

Abstract written by a model

is_a methods of reasoning [observation identifies facts and deduction infers conclusions] Holmes distinguishes observation from deduction: observation reveals details such as reddish earth on Watson’s instep, while deduction connects those details to conclusions such as a visit to the Wigmore Street Post-Office and the sending of a telegram. He says that the ideal detective needs observation, deduction, and knowledge.
