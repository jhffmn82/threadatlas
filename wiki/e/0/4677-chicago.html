Chicago in Pen Pictures of Eventful Scenes and Struggles of Life

B. F. Craig · 1880

Chicago is a city named for wild onion, called Chikago by Indians. For a quarter of a century it has fewer than one hundred inhabitants. After Daymon moves there, works as a day laborer, and accepts fifteen acres for a debt, its unparalleled growth lets him sell land and become wealthy. Roxie lives among its moneyed aristocracy, where the pleasure party forms and Simon returns before his death. During the war, loyal Chicago is where Roxie discusses the Simon estate with Governor Morock.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

SCENE FOURTH--ROXIE DAYMON AND ROSE SIMON.

Daymon and Roxie move to Chicago, Illinois, where Daymon works as a day laborer before accepting fifteen acres of land for a debt owed to him. Chicago's rapid growth allows him to sell land at increasing prices and become wealthy.

Chicago is where Roxie lives among the city’s moneyed aristocracy, where the pleasure party is formed, and where Simon returns before his death. It is also the place where Roxie and Simon are expected to marry.

SCENE SEVENTH--WAR BETWEEN THE STATES.

Chicago is described as a loyal city where Roxie Daymon lives during the first year of the war. Roxie meets Governor Morock at his office on Strait street to discuss the Simon estate, the Simon will, and the possible recovery of cotton.

Chicago in The Valley of Fear

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1914-09/1915-05

Chicago is a city tied to McMurdo’s past: he came from Lodge 29 there, fled after trouble connected with Jonas Pinto’s shooting and counterfeit money, though no clear case was made against him. It is also where Edwards became a Freemen, later married Ettie, survived two attempts on his life, and departed under a changed name for California.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

PART II / Chapter I

Chicago is the place McMurdo says he last came from. He belongs to Lodge 29 there, and he left the city because of serious trouble that caused him to flee from justice.

Chapter III

Chicago is Jack McMurdo’s former home and the place where Captain Marvin served as a police officer. McMurdo is associated there with the shooting of Jonas Pinto and with passing counterfeit money, though Marvin says that no clear case could be made against him and that Chicago is open to him.

Chapter VII

Chicago is where Edwards was made a Freemen before traveling to the coal valleys, and where he later marries Ettie. After surviving two attempts on his life there, he leaves under a changed name for California.
