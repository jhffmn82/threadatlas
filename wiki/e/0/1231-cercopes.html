group · 2 works · 18 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: These Cercopes, the Cercopes, the fraudulent race, these men, two brothers, two notorious robbers
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the Cercopes are two brothers, Passalus and Acmon, accomplished knaves, liars, and cheats who roam the world deceiving men until Zeus turns them to stone.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the Cercopes are a fraudulent race condemned for fraud, perjury, and other crimes whom Jupiter transforms into ape-like creatures.

Cercopes in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

The Cercopes are two brothers, Passalus and Acmon, children of Theia and Ocean. Accomplished knaves, liars, and cheats, they live on earth and continually roam the world deceiving men, until they are turned to stone for trying to deceive Zeus.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE CERCOPES

The Cercopes were two brothers, Passalus and Acmon, known for their cunning, lies, cheating, and knavery. They roamed far over the world deceiving people, and they were said to have been turned to stone for trying to deceive Zeus.

the Cercopes in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

The Cercopes are a fraudulent race condemned for fraud, perjury, and other crimes; Jupiter transforms them into ape-like creatures, contracting their limbs, altering their faces, covering them with long yellow hair, and removing language.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK THE FOURTEENTH.

The Cercopes are condemned for fraud, perjury, and other crimes. Jupiter contracts their limbs, flattens and bends back their noses, furrows their faces with wrinkles, covers their bodies with long yellow hair, and changes them into ape-like creatures.
