KGI in Robust Retrieval Augmented Generation for Zero-shot Slot Filling

Michael Glass, Gaetano Rossiello, Md Faisal Mahbub Chowdhury, Alﬁo Gliozzo · 2021

KGI (Knowledge Graph Induction) is a novel slot filling system and approach for zero-shot slot filling, based on Retrieval Augmented Generation and combining Dense Passage Retrieval with RAG. It uses BERTBASE query and passage encoders and a BARTLARGE generator, with 620 million parameters, to provide generated answers and provenance. KGI unfolds through two training phases: it first trains DPR using KILT provenance ground truth, improving retrieval with hard negatives from the dense index; it then trains sequence-to-sequence generation and further trains the shared query encoder using the target tail entity. Trained on KILT data, it was evaluated on T-REx and zsRE, ranked top-1 on the KILT leaderboard, and improved KILT-F1 by 38.24 and 21.25 percentage points, respectively. It also proposed and released a TACRED-based zero/few-shot slot filling benchmark, achieved better zero-shot results than baselines there, and supports rapid few-shot fine-tuning and domain adaptation.

Narrative, section by section

opening

KGI (Knowledge Graph Induction) is a slot filling system based on advanced training strategies for Dense Passage Retrieval and Retrieval Augmented Generation. It improves KILT-F1 by 38.24% on T-REx and 21.25% on zsRE compared with previously submitted systems, and it supports domain adaptation and zero/few-shot slot filling.

Related Work .. DPR for Slot Filling

Knowledge Graph Induction is presented as an approach to zero-shot slot filling that combines a DPR model and a RAG model, both trained for slot filling. It uses a two-phase training procedure: first training DPR with KILT provenance ground truth, then training sequence-to-sequence generation and further training the query encoder using the target tail entity.

KILT Experiments .. Slot Filling Evaluation

KGI is based on RAG and contains BERTBASE query and passage encoders plus a BARTLARGE generator, totaling 620 million parameters. Its models are trained using the KILT data and provide both generated answers and provenance.

Conclusion

KGI is presented as a novel approach to zero-shot slot filling that improves Dense Passage Retrieval using hard negatives from the dense index and applies robust training procedures for Retrieval Augmented Generation. It ranked first on the KILT leaderboard for T-REx and zsRE, with net KILT-F1 improvements of 38.24 and 21.25 percentage points, respectively. KGI also achieved much better zero-shot results than the baselines on the TACRED-based benchmark and improved significantly with rapid few-shot fine-tuning.
