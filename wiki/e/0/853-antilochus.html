Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, Antilochus is Nestor’s son, an Achaean warrior and swift runner who fights prominently, kills several Trojans, helps defend Hypsenor’s body, captures horses, carries news of Patroclus’s death to Achilles, and competes in the chariot race and footrace. In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Antilochus is slain by Memnon in battle and buried by the Achaeans. In The Fall of Troy, Antilochus is Nestor’s son and a Pylian prince who attacks Memnon, wounds him, and is slain by him in battle; the Pylians bury him beside the Hellespont, and his death drives Achilles’ vengeance.

Antilochus in The Iliad

Homer · 749 to 724 BC · the work’s page · read it where Antilochus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Antilochus is Nestor’s son, an Achaean warrior, and a notably swift young runner, protected by Neptune in battle. He fights prominently in the foremost ranks, killing Echepolus, Atymnius, Asius’s charioteer, Thoon, and Melanippus; he also helps defend Hypsenor’s body, captures horses from the Trojans, and withdraws when Hector attacks. After Menelaus tells him that Patroclus has fallen and Achilles’ armour has been taken, grief first silences him, but he gives his armour to Laodocus and runs to the ships with the news for Achilles. In the chariot race, he dangerously passes Menelaus with his slow Pylos-bred horses, admits his fault, and offers Menelaus the mare before receiving it back. He later places last in the footrace, jokes about seniority, and gains extra gold from Achilles.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

And now Idomeneus, though his hair was already flecked with grey,

Antilochus kills the charioteer of Asius and drives the charioteer’s horses from the Trojans to the Achaeans. He helps protect Hypsenor’s body, later kills Thoon, and is protected from Adamas’s spear by Neptune.

Now Patroclus, so long as the Achaeans and Trojans were fighting

Antilochus responds to Menelaus’s urging by attacking the Trojans and killing Melanippus. He then flees when Hector comes against him.

Such fearful turmoil of men and horses did Jove on that day

Antilochus receives the news from Menelaus that Patroclus has fallen and that the Trojans have taken Achilles' armour. Though horror and grief leave him speechless for a time, he gives his armour to Laodocus and runs to the ships to inform Achilles.

The first prize he offered was for the chariot races—a woman

Antilochus drives slow horses but uses a risky maneuver to pass Menelaus at a narrow part of the course. When Menelaus protests, Antilochus admits that his youth and poor judgment caused the offense, offers to give up the mare, and later receives it back from Menelaus.

Thereon the son of Peleus, when he had listened to all the thanks

Antilochus competes in the running contest and finishes behind Ajax son of Oileus and Ulysses, receiving the last prize. He jokes that the gods have shown respect for seniority, and Achilles rewards his compliment with an additional half talent of gold.

Antilochus in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · about AD 200 to 299 · the work’s page · read it where Antilochus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Antilochus, Nestor's son and a Pylian prince, is a Greek warrior slain in battle by Memnon. After Memnon kills Pheron and Ereuthus, Antilochus attacks him, misses with a spear, kills an Aethiopian comrade, and wounds Memnon with a stone before being pierced above the breast. Pylians bury him beside the Hellespont; his death drives Achilles' vengeance.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK II

Antilochus confronts Memnon after Memnon kills Pheron and Ereuthus. He throws a spear that misses Memnon but kills an Aethiopian comrade, then strikes Memnon with a stone before Memnon pierces him above the breast and kills him.

Then to the side of Aeacus' mighty son

Antilochus is identified as Nestor's slain son. Achilles promises to avenge his death by killing Memnon.

BOOK III

Antilochus's corpse is carried to the ships by the Pylians and buried beside the Hellespont. His death causes Achilles to seek terrible vengeance against the Trojans.
