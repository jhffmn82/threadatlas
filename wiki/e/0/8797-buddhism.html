Buddhism in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Buddhism is a religious sect and rival system to Brahmanism. It rejects the Vedas, caste distinctions, and bloody animal sacrifices, permits animal food, and chooses priests from all classes. Tolerated for centuries, it was persecuted and ultimately expelled from India after a long struggle, while spreading to Ceylon, China, Corea, Japan, Java, and the eastern peninsula.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXX

Buddhism is described as a rival system to Brahmanism in the eastern nations. The struggle between Buddhism and Brahmanism lasted from about A.D. 500 to A.D. 1400 and ended, according to the text, with the expulsion of Buddhism from India.

BUDDHA

Buddhism rejects the Vedas, caste distinctions, and bloody sacrifices, while allowing animal food and choosing priests from all classes. After being tolerated for centuries, it was persecuted in India and spread to Ceylon, the eastern peninsula, China, Corea, Japan, and Java.
