Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, the Nabataeans were an Arabian people who valued liberty, lived as solitary nomads in the desert, protected their cattle, families, wealth, and trade goods, pursued and destroyed Athenaeus’s army, and resisted Antigonus and Demetrius before securing peace with gifts and hostages. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Nabatheans inhabited a partly desert, waterless eastern country, roamed for incursions and robberies, used secret wells as refuges, remained free without a foreign prince, and were never conquered by the Assyrians, Medes, Persians, or Macedonians.

Nabataeans in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Nabataeans first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Nabataeans are an Arabian people who value liberty and live as solitary nomads in the desert. They protect cattle, families, wealth, and trade goods by using separate desert refuges, watch-towers, scouts, and a strong rock. After Athenaeus captures their rock and takes myrrh, frankincense, and silver, they pursue and destroy his army. They then complain to Antigonus, resist Demetrius’s invasion, and secure peace by giving gifts and hostages.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

In Asia, Sclcucus after the routing of Demetrius at Gaza in Syria,

The Nabataeans left their wealth, older men, women, and children at a strong rock while attending a public meeting. Athenaeus captured the rock and carried away much of their myrrh, frankincense, and silver, but the Nabataeans returned, pursued his army, and destroyed it.

The Nabataeans complain to Antigonus about Athenseus, remain cautious despite Antigonus’s reassuring reply, and use scouts and fortified positions to detect and resist Demetrius’s invasion. They drive away their cattle, defend their rock successfully, and persuade Demetrius to accept gifts and hostages and end the war.

Nabatheans in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Nabatheans first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Nabatheans inhabit a partly desert, waterless eastern country where little land bears fruit. They roam to conduct incursions and robberies, use secret wells for refuge, remain free without a foreign prince, and were never conquered by Assyrians, Medes, Persians, or Macedonians.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAP. IV.

The Nabatheans inhabit a partly desert and waterless region and live by raiding and robbery because little of their land bears fruit. They use secret wells for refuge, remain free from foreign rulers, and have resisted the Assyrians, Medes, Persians, and Macedonians.
