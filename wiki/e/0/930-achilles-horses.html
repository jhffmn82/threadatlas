Euripides · -0429~

Achilles’ immortal, divinely descended horses, given by Neptune to Peleus, are promised by Hector to Dolon as payment for spying.

horses of Achilles in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Achilles’ immortal horses, given by the gods to Peleus and descended from Aeacus, carry Achilles and are difficult for other mortals to drive. They mourn Patroclus after his death, standing apart and weeping. Jove pities and strengthens them; they then carry Automedon into battle, with Alcimedon later controlling them.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Neither again did Hector let the Trojans sleep; for he too called

Hector swears that Dolon will receive the horses of Achilles. Ulysses tells Dolon that these horses are difficult for any mortal other than Achilles to keep in hand or drive.

Such fearful turmoil of men and horses did Jove on that day

Achilles' immortal horses stand apart from the fighting and weep when they learn that their driver, Patroclus, has been killed. Jove pities them, gives them strength, and they carry Automedon swiftly into the battle; Alcimedon later takes control of them.

Achilles' immortal horses in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

Podarge's begotten horses serve Achilles, mourn his slain king and yearn to leave; gods keep them by ships until Neoptolemus arrives to receive their service.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

So the kings drew him from that stricken field

Achilles' immortal horses mourn their slain king and long to leave the mortal men and Argive horses. The gods keep them beside the ships until Neoptolemus arrives, after which they are destined to serve him.
