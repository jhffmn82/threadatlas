  • In Heroides and Amores, Iole is Eurytus’s captive daughter, publicly brought through the city after Oechalia’s fall, and the woman loved by Hercules whom Deianira fears will replace her.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Iole is Eurytus’s daughter whom Hercules carries away to the promontory of Cenaeum after defeating her brothers for refusing to give her to him in marriage.
  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Iole is Eurytus’s daughter whose beauty and Heracles’s love lead to Oechalia’s conquest; she is brought to Trachis as a captive, espoused to Heracles, and commanded after his death to marry Hyllus.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Iole, also called Ioleia, is Eurytus’s daughter for whose sake Heracles sacks Oechalia.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Iole is Eurytus’s daughter, is named as Hercules’s beloved and Deïanira’s rival, tells Alcmena of Dryope’s transformation, and becomes Hyllus’s wife and Alcmena’s daughter-in-law.
  • In The Library, Iole is Eurytus’s daughter, captured by Hercules at Oechalia, whom he later charges Hyllus to marry in adulthood.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Iole accompanies her sister Dryope, witnesses Dryope’s transformation into a lotus tree, embraces the trunk, and cannot save her.

Iole in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Iole, daughter of Eurytus of Oechalia, is a woman of royal lineage whose beauty and Heracles’s love lead to her father’s city being conquered. Formerly free, she is brought silently to Trachis as a captive woman and is pitied as an innocent accomplice in her land’s ruin. Though espoused to Heracles as his bride, she is ultimately commanded by him to marry Hyllus after his death.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

THE TRACHINIAN MAIDENS

Iole is brought to Trachis among the captive women after the fall of Oechalia. The Messenger identifies her as Iole, daughter of Eurytus, and says that Heracles conquered her father's city because he loved her. She does not speak in this unit.

DÊ. Nay, I entreat thee by His name, whose fire

The maiden's love for Heracles is identified as the sole cause of the conquest of Oechalia. DÊ pities her, believing that her beauty ruined her life and that she unknowingly ravaged and enslaved her own bright land.

CHORUS.

Iole is identified as the daughter of Eurytus and the maiden who came from Heracles's conquest of Oechalia. Heracles commands Hyllus to marry her after his death.

Iole in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Iole is Eurytus’s daughter from Œchalia and Dryope’s sister. Rumour names her as loved by Hercules, making Deïanira see her as a rival. She later tells Alcmena of Dryope’s transformation, becomes Hyllus’s wife and Alcmena’s daughter-in-law, and is received into Hyllus’s affections.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

FABLE II. [IX.101-272]

Iole is the maiden whom Rumour reports Hercules loves after his return from Œchalia. Deïanira regards her as a rival and sends Hercules the tunic of Nessus in hopes of restoring his love.

FABLE IV. [IX.324-425]

Iole relates to Alcmena the story of her sister Dryope's transformation. She witnesses the bloody drops and trembling branches of the lotus, tries to delay Dryope's transformation by embracing her, and later sees Iolaüs restored to youth.
