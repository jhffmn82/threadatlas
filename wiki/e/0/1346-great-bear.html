Great Bear

constellation · 3 works · 5 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Bear, The Great Bear, the Bear, the Constellation of the Bear, the seven Triones
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the Great Bear is Callisto, placed among the stars by Zeus and named Bear because of her misfortune.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the Great Bear is the constellation formed when Jupiter places Calisto among the stars; its never setting may have inspired Juno’s request that it not be received into the Ocean.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Great Bear, also called Charles’ wain by the Greeks, is a constellation linked to Callisto’s transformation by Zeus and mentioned during Odysseus’s voyage from Calypso.

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER III.

The Great Bear was the constellation into which Zeus turned the child of Callisto. Homer mentioned it in the account of Odysseus’s voyage from Calypso, and it may have received its name in honor of Callisto.
