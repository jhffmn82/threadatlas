Pisa in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

Pisa is a place, a colony of Lydian Pelops and an abode of Zeus, containing Altis and a famous valley for games. There Pherenikos wins the horse-race and Pelops’ Olympian contests occur; it bids divinely assigned song for victors. After victories, Herakles gathered host and booty there and established grove and games’ arrangements.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

OLYMPIAN ODES. / I.

Pisa is the place where Pherenikos wins the horse-race and where the Olympian contests associated with Pelops are held.

III.

Pisa is said to bid the poet speak aloud, since songs of divine assignment come from her when the judge of the games places the olive glory upon a victor.

XI.

Herakles gathered his host and all the booty at Pisa after his victories. There he established the sacred grove and the surrounding arrangements for the games.

Pisa in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Pisa, founded by Pisus, first assists Elis against Hercules. It controls territory near Olympia, then loses it to Elis and repeatedly contests the Olympic games, joining neighboring communities. After conflict over control of the games, Elis defeats and razes Pisa and its supporting towns.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK V.--ELIS.

Pisa assisted Elis during Hercules’s campaign against Augeas. Hercules intended to attack Pisa but was stopped by an oracle from Delphi, and Elis later had frequent disputes with Pisa over the restoration of the games at Olympia.

CHAPTER XXI.

Pisa formerly controlled the boundaries and district near Olympia but later lost them to Elis. Its people repeatedly challenged Elis over the Olympian games, allied with neighboring communities, and were eventually defeated when Elis razed Pisa and the towns that supported it.
