the two Ajaxes in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

The two Ajaxes are an Achaean fighting pair and defensive force, leading foot soldiers, guarding the gates and walls, and urging the Danaans to resist. They act as chief defenders, fighting side by side in a compact formation of spears, shields, helmets, and men. As the Trojan attack intensifies, they rebuke the remiss, rally the defenders, receive Neptune’s encouragement, and check Hector’s advance. They then force through the crowd to rescue Automedon and defend Patroclus's body, protecting Menelaus and Meriones as they carry it away while holding back the pursuing Trojans.

The two Ajaxes move along the wall, encouraging the Achaeans to stand firm and rebuking those who are remiss. They urge the defenders to press forward and keep one another’s courage up.

BOOK XIII.

The two Ajaxes are encouraged directly by Neptune and gather strong bands of Achaeans around them. Their troops form a tightly packed wall of spears, shields, helmets, and men that checks Hector’s advance.
