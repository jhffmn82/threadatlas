Cretans

group · 5 works · 13 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Cretan couriers, Cretans (in Panchsea), The Cretans, a nation proud of having nurtured Jove, men, strangers, the Cretans, the master of the Cretans

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the Cretans sail from Cnossos for trade, are carried by Apollo to Crisa, and obey him by establishing an altar and becoming temple ministers who guard his oracle and righteousness. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, the Cretans serve as couriers for Agis, capture and plunder Siphnos by deception, are linked by the senate to Cretan pirates, send ambassadors to Rome, gain and lose favorable judgment, and face demands for ships, hostages, leaders, and silver, causing sedition. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Cretans are among the strangers dwelling in Panchsea, and the priests claim that they originally came from Crete and preserve words of the Cretan speech as evidence of their ancient connection. In The Amores, or Amours, the Cretans are a nation proud of nurturing Jove as a child and are presented as witnesses that Ceres was not devoid of love. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the Cretans come under Miletus to the country near Miletus, share the territory with the earlier-settled Carians, and are said to have been the first Greeks at Claros, arriving under Rhacius and attacking and capturing Apollo’s colonists.

Cretans in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Cretans first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Cretans are a people who serve as couriers for Agis, capture and plunder Siphnos by deception, and are linked by the senate to Cretan pirates. They send ambassadors to Rome, gain then lose favorable judgment, and face demands for ships, hostages, leaders, and silver, causing sedition.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

23. Ambassadors came to Rome both from the yoonger and the

The Cretans send thirty eminent ambassadors to Rome to defend themselves against accusations and seek restoration of their former amity and confederacy. The senate initially acquits them and declares them friends and confederates, but Lentulus Spinther reverses the decree; later the senate orders them to surrender their ships, three hundred hostages, Lasthenes, Panares, and four thousand talents of silver, leading to internal disagreement and sedition.

Cretans in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Cretans first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cretans, a Greek group, came under Miletus to the country near Miletus when it was called Anactoria and were admitted by earlier-settled Carians to share the territory. Later, they are said to have been the first Greeks at Claros, arriving powerfully under Rhacius, attacking and capturing Apollo’s colonists.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

Cretans under Miletus came to the country near Miletus while it was still called Anactoria. The Carians who had settled there earlier admitted them to a joint share of the territory.

CHAPTER III.

The Cretans were the first Greeks said to have reached the country around Claros, arriving in a large force under Rhacius. They attacked the colonists sent by Apollo and took them before Rhacius.
