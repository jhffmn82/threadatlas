Cretans in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Cretans are a people who serve as couriers for Agis, capture and plunder Siphnos by deception, and are linked by the senate to Cretan pirates. They send ambassadors to Rome, gain then lose favorable judgment, and face demands for ships, hostages, leaders, and silver, causing sedition.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

23. Ambassadors came to Rome both from the yoonger and the

The Cretans send thirty eminent ambassadors to Rome to defend themselves against accusations and seek restoration of their former amity and confederacy. The senate initially acquits them and declares them friends and confederates, but Lentulus Spinther reverses the decree; later the senate orders them to surrender their ships, three hundred hostages, Lasthenes, Panares, and four thousand talents of silver, leading to internal disagreement and sedition.

the Cretans in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

The Cretans sail from Cnossos for trade, but Apollo carries them to Crisa; obeying him, they establish an altar and become temple ministers, guarding his oracle and righteousness.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

TO PYTHIAN APOLLO—

The Cretans, sailing from Cnossos for trade, are carried against their will to Crisa by Apollo. They obey his commands, establish an altar, follow him while singing the Ie Paean, and become ministers of his temple.

Cretans in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Cretans, a Greek group, came under Miletus to the country near Miletus when it was called Anactoria and were admitted by earlier-settled Carians to share the territory. Later, they are said to have been the first Greeks at Claros, arriving powerfully under Rhacius, attacking and capturing Apollo’s colonists.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

Cretans under Miletus came to the country near Miletus while it was still called Anactoria. The Carians who had settled there earlier admitted them to a joint share of the territory.

CHAPTER III.

The Cretans were the first Greeks said to have reached the country around Claros, arriving in a large force under Rhacius. They attacked the colonists sent by Apollo and took them before Rhacius.
