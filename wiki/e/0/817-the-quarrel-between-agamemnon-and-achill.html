Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, it is a conflict between Agamemnon and Achilles that begins over prizes, leads Achilles to withdraw from fighting, and is later set aside when Agamemnon offers amends. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, it is a dispute between the Greek leaders that begins over Chryseis and Briseis and leads Achilles to withdraw his forces and refuse to fight.

the quarrel between Agamemnon and Achilles in The Iliad

Homer · 749 to 724 BC · the work’s page · read it where the quarrel between Agamemnon and Achilles first appears

Abstract written by a model

The quarrel is a conflict between Agamemnon and Achilles. It begins over prizes, as Agamemnon refuses to release Chryseis without compensation and threatens, then takes, Briseis. Minerva prevents violence; Achilles withdraws from fighting. Later Achilles sets aside anger, while Agamemnon blames divine Folly and offers amends.

BOOK I.

The quarrel begins when Agamemnon refuses to release Chryseis unless he receives another prize. Achilles accuses him of greed and injustice, while Agamemnon threatens to take Briseis from Achilles. Minerva prevents the conflict from becoming physical, but the quarrel leads Achilles to refuse further fighting.
