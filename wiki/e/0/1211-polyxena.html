Polyxena in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Polyxena is Hecuba’s daughter, sister of Polydore and Hector, a royal-born princess of Troy, and an unmarried virgin. After Troy’s fall, the Greeks take her captive and vote to sacrifice her at Achilles’ tomb as a gift to the dead. Rather than face purchase into slavery or dishonor, she chooses death. Led away by Ulysses and dragged to the tomb, she refuses restraint, asks to die as one born free, and exposes her breast and neck for the fatal blow. After she is slain, Andromache shrouds and mourns her, the Greeks provide foliage, a pyre, and offerings, and her remains await washing and interment. Hecuba seeks to delay burial so Polyxena may share a fire and grave with her dead son.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

HECUBA.

Polyxena learns that the Greeks have voted to sacrifice her at Achilles’ tomb. She accepts death rather than risk being purchased as a slave or suffering dishonor, and she is led away by Ulysses.

CHORUS.

Polyxena is sacrificed at Achilles’ tomb by the Greeks. She willingly faces death, refuses to be restrained, asks to be killed as one born free, and exposes her breast and neck for the fatal blow; afterward, the Greeks honor her with foliage, a pyre, and funeral offerings.

AGAMEMNON, HECUBA, CHORUS.

Polyxena has been newly slain, and a servant was sent to fetch water to wash her remains. Hecuba asks Agamemnon to delay her interment so that she can be buried in the same fire and grave as Hecuba’s dead son.

CHOR. Attendants of the aged Hecuba,

Polyxena is dragged to the tomb of Achilles and killed as a gift to the deceased. Andromache saw her bleed, covered her body with a shroud, and mourned her; Hecuba considers death preferable to the suffering endured by the living.

Polyxena in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Polyxena is a Trojan princess, daughter of Hecuba and Priam, and sister of Hector, Cassandra, and Polydore. After the fall of Troy, Achilles demands her as a victim for his tomb, and the Greek army chooses her for sacrifice. Learning her fate, she refuses to plead for life or accept slavery and dishonor. Led to Achilles’ tomb, she meets death courageously, rejects restraint, and asks to die free, preserving her modesty. The Greeks sacrifice her, and Hecuba is to bury her; her ashes are to be interred beside Polydore’s, at Polyxena’s tomb.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

HECUBA.

Polyxena is Hecuba’s daughter and Polydore’s sister. Achilles has demanded her as a victim, and the Greeks will take her to be sacrificed as an honor to Achilles’ tomb.

HECUBA. CHORUS.

Polyxena learns that the Greek army has voted for her death. She refuses to plead for life, choosing death over enslavement and possible abuse, and bids farewell to Hecuba before being led away.

TALTHYBIUS, HECUBA, CHORUS.

Polyxena is taken to Achilles' tomb and faces death with courage and dignity. She refuses to be restrained, asks to die free, exposes her neck or breast to the sword, and maintains modesty even as she dies.

FEMALE ATTENDANT, CHORUS, HECUBA.

Polyxena has been sacrificed by the Greeks, and Hecuba is expected to bury her. Agamemnon agrees to delay the raising of her tomb so that her ashes can be interred beside those of Polydore.
