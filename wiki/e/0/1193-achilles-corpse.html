Achilles' corpse

object · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Achilles' body, Achilles' corse, body, corpse, corse, dead face, dead man, mighty dead, mighty fallen, slain Achilles, the body of Achilles, the corpse of strong-heart Aeacus' son, the mighty dead
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the body of Achilles is carried to the ships by Aias, laid out by the Achaeans, and later taken from the pyre by Thetis and transported to the White Island.
  • In The Fall of Troy, Achilles' corpse becomes the focus of a fierce Trojan-Argive struggle, is guarded by Aias and recovered by the Argives, then washed, dressed, preserved by Athena, and burned on Achilles' funeral pyre.

Achilles' corpse in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

Achilles' corpse, a dead warrior's body, becomes the focus of a fierce Trojan-Argive struggle. Aias guards it, and the Argives recover it. They wash and dress it, while Athena preserves its lifelike appearance with ambrosia, before burning it on Achilles' funeral pyre.

BOOK III

Achilles' corpse becomes the object of a fierce struggle between the Trojans and the Argives. Aias guards it, and the Argives eventually retain it, intending to place it on the pyre.

So the kings drew him from that stricken field

Achilles' body is washed of blood, dressed in sea-purple clothing, and laid on a couch. Athena preserves it with ambrosia and makes it appear like a living, sleeping warrior before it is burned on the pyre.
