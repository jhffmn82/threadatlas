Aeetes in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · -0299/-0200

Aeetes is the powerful king and ruler of Colchis, the Colchians, Aea, and Cytaean Aea. He is a lord, father of Chalciope, Medea, Apsyrtus, and other offspring, and host to Phrixus. His palace holds the golden fleece, the object sought by Jason and his companions. He also possesses a prophecy received from Helios, dragon’s teeth given by the Tritonian goddess, a corslet from Ares, a golden helmet, and a chariot drawn by steeds gifted by Helios. As the heroes approach his land, Aeetes is expected to resist any effort to take the fleece. He receives Jason, the sons of Phrixus, Telamon, and Augeias, but becomes enraged when their purpose is known. He convenes the Colchians, denounces the Minyae, the sons of Phrixus, and the strangers, and invokes Helios’s warning of treachery by his offspring. He threatens punishment and calamity against the intruders and their allies. Aeetes promises the fleece if Jason fulfills a deadly contest, yet expects him to fail. He provides dragon’s teeth for the trial and watches it armed in his corslet, helmet, shield, and spear. Though he marvels at Jason’s strength, the destruction of the Earthborn men leaves him bitterly grieved. He returns to plan opposition and commands his captains to devise treachery against the heroes. After learning of Medea’s deeds, Aeetes plots against the heroes and pursues Medea and their ship with a large Colchian force, seeking to recover her and exact vengeance. He calls Helios and Zeus as witnesses to the theft and threatens his people unless they recover Medea. After Apsyrtus’s death, remaining Colchians fear his fierce anger and do not return home; his distant anger and authority are feared as a possible cause of war upon Hellas.

Æetes in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Æetes, son of Sun, is king of Colchis, father of Medea, and kinsman of Phryxus. After causing Phryxus’s assassination, he seizes Athamas’s treasures and, fearing an oracle, establishes stranger sacrifice. When Jason seeks the Golden Fleece, Æetes demands dangerous labors before granting it.

Aeetes in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Aeetes is king of the Colchians and ruler of Colchis, child of Sun by Perseis, sibling of Circe and Pasiphae, and father of Medea and Chalciope. He receives Phrixus, gives Chalciope to him, and receives and safeguards the Golden Fleece, nailing it to an oak in a grove of Ares. When Jason seeks the fleece, Aeetes sets tasks involving brazen-footed bulls and dragon’s teeth, then refuses the fleece and seeks to destroy the Argo and its crew. He pursues Medea and Jason, falls behind while recovering and burying Apsyrtus’ limbs, and sends searchers after them. Perses deposes him, but Medea kills Perses and restores his kingdom.

Aeetes refuses to give Jason the fleece because he wants to burn the Argo and kill its crew. After Medea and Jason escape, he pursues them, but Medea kills Apsyrtus and throws his dismembered limbs into the sea; while gathering them, Aeetes falls behind. He buries the recovered limbs, names the place Tomi, and sends Colchians to search for the Argo and Medea.

Aeetes in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Aeetes, king of Colchis and custodian of the Golden Fleece, receives Phryxus, fathers Absyrtus, and promises Jason the fleece for dangerous tasks; after Jason succeeds, Aeetes plans attack, but Jason and Medea escape with it.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XI

Aeetes, the king of Colchis, hospitably received Phryxus and took possession of the Golden Fleece. He promised Jason the fleece if Jason could yoke two fire-breathing, brazen-footed bulls and sow the dragon's teeth. After Jason completed these tasks, Aeetes planned to attack the Argonauts, but Jason and Medea escaped with the fleece before he could stop them.
