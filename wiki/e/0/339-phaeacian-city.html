Phaeacian city

Abstract written by a model

Phaeacian city is a walled coastal place with two narrow-entranced harbours, roadside ships, assemblies, a market, Neptune’s temple, and Alcinous’s palace. Its people make and use ship’s gear. Ulysses crosses it unseen under Minerva’s concealment; later, Neptune threatens to bury it beneath a high mountain.

Phaeacian city is in one work on this site, The Odyssey. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Phaeacian city in The Odyssey

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK VI

The Phaeacian town has a high wall, two harbours with narrow entrances, ships drawn up by the roadside, and a market place with a temple of Neptune. Its people work with ship’s gear, sails, cables, oars, and ships.

BOOK VII

The Phaeacian city has harbours, ships, places of assembly, and lofty walls topped by a palisade. Ulysses passes through it under Minerva’s concealment without being seen by its inhabitants.
