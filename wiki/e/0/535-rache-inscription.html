Abstract written by a model

“RACHE” is a blood-red, single-word inscription on exposed wall plaster, written with Jefferson Hope’s blood after Enoch Drebber’s death. Though Lestrade reads it as an unfinished “Rachel,” it means “revenge” in German. Its non-German-style lettering and false German appearance make it a blind, inspired by a German New York murder case, to divert police toward Socialism, secret societies, or a German suspect.

RACHE inscription is in one work on this site, A Study in Scarlet. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

CHAPTER III.

The blood-red inscription consists of the single word “RACHE,” written on exposed plaster where the wallpaper has peeled away. Lestrade believes it is the unfinished name Rachel, while Sherlock Holmes says that “Rache” is German for “revenge.”

CHAPTER IV.

Holmes says that “RACHE” was a false clue intended to divert the police toward Socialism, secret societies, or a German suspect. He argues that the lettering was made by a clumsy imitator rather than a real German.
