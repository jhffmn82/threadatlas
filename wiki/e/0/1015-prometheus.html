  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Prometheus discovers how to strike fire from flint or stone and is said by some to have stolen fire from the gods for mankind.
  • In Four Plays of Aeschylus, Prometheus is a Titan and benefactor of humanity who gives mortals fire, knowledge, and the arts, defies Zeus, and is punished and buried beneath a mountain.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Prometheus deceives Zeus, steals fire for men, and is bound while an eagle consumes his liver until Heracles releases him.
  • In The Library, Prometheus takes fire and mechanical arts from Athena and Hephaestus's workshop, gives them to men, and is nailed to Caucasus.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Prometheus fashions Man from earth and water and is associated with teaching humanity fire and metalworking.
  • In The Library, Prometheus creates human beings, gives them fire, and is chained to the Caucasus until Hercules releases him.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Prometheus is said to have fashioned the human race from clay.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Prometheus helps make humanity, brings fire from heaven, teaches and protects mankind, and endures Jupiter's punishment.

Prometheus in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

Prometheus is a Titan, god, son of Iapetos, and benefactor of humanity. He aids Zeus during the gods’ strife, yet later champions mortal men against Zeus’s will. By stealth he gives fire to perishable men and provides hope, arts, knowledge, civilization, practical skills, medicine, divination, sacrifice, metals, letters, and handicrafts, transforming mortals from unseeing and unreasoning creatures into conscious, thinking beings. Zeus punishes him for these gifts: Hephaestus fastens, chains, and nails him to a mountain rock in the remote Scythian steppe, at the earth’s limit. Prometheus knowingly accepts this lasting torment but hates the gods’ repayment of kindness with wrong and defies Zeus without yielding. Possessing foreknowledge, he predicts Zeus’s future fall through a destined stratagem and knows the threatened marriage and how it may be prevented. He tells Io of her future, Zeus’s overthrow, and his own eventual release through a saviour descended from her. Although Hermes and Zeus threaten him, he refuses to disclose the secret until his bonds are loosed. The account ends with the rocky ravine shattered by storm and earthquake, burying him beneath the collapsing rocks as he declares Zeus unjust.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

INTRODUCTION

Prometheus is a Titan who aids oppressed humanity by providing benevolence, art, science, letters, and handicrafts. Zeus punishes him by chaining and impaling him on a remote Scythian precipice, but Prometheus refuses to reveal the secret that could threaten Zeus's rule and ends by declaring that Zeus is unjust.

PROMETHEUS BOUND

Prometheus is chained and nailed to a mountain in the Scythian steppe for giving fire, arts, and other benefits to mortal men. He says that he helped Zeus defeat Cronos, saved mortal men from destruction, and knowingly accepted his punishment. He refuses to yield the secret of the future stratagem that will threaten Zeus’s rule.

[_Exit OCEANUS._]

Prometheus explains that he gave mortals the knowledge and arts that changed them from unseeing, unhearing, and unreasoning creatures into beings capable of consciousness and thought. He taught them practical skills, medicine, divination, sacrifice, and the use of metals, but remains unable to escape his own suffering.

Enter IO, horned like a cow.

Prometheus tells Io that Zeus ordered him bound and that Hephaestus fastened him to the rock. He recounts Io’s past and prophesies her future, Zeus’s overthrow, his own eventual release, and the descent of his saviour from Io.

[_Exit IO._]

Prometheus declares that Zeus’s power is temporary and that he knows how Zeus’s prophesied fall may be prevented, but he refuses to reveal the secret. He mocks Hermes and Zeus, rejects any plea for release, and insists that no torture or force will make him yield. He is ultimately buried when the rocks are rent and collapse around him.

Prometheus is the crafty, cunning son of Iapetus and parent of Deucalion with Pronoia. He deceives Zeus by dividing an ox at Mecone, then steals fire for men in a hollow fennel stalk. Zeus plans sorrow for mankind and punishes him with inextricable bonds, cruel chains, and an eagle eating his liver daily until Heracles releases him.

Prometheus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Prometheus is a figure in heathen Mythology and son of Iapetus. He tempers earth with water and fashions Man in the image of the Gods, then is associated with teaching mankind to make fire and with metalworking. He is father of Deucalion and is identified by some writers with Noah or Magog.

opening

Prometheus, the son of Iapetus, fashions Man from earth tempered with water, shaping him after the image of the Gods.

Prometheus is described as tempering the earth in the making of man. Some writers identify him with a Scriptural character, while the unit also notes traditions that associate him with teaching mankind to produce fire and with the invention or improvement of metalworking.

Prometheus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Prometheus, creator of human beings and father of Deucalion, moulds people from water and earth and secretly gives them fire. Punished by Zeus, he is nailed or chained to the Caucasus, where an eagle daily devours his regrowing liver. Hercules shoots the eagle and releases him, with Chiron dying as a substitute.

Prometheus moulds human beings from water and earth and secretly gives them fire hidden in a fennel stalk. Zeus has him nailed to Mount Caucasus, where an eagle eats his liver each day as it grows back during the night. Hercules later releases him.

Prometheus is chained on the Caucasus while an eagle, offspring of Echidna and Typhon, eats his liver. Hercules shoots the eagle, releases Prometheus, and provides Chiron as a willing immortal substitute who will die in his place.

Prometheus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Prometheus is a Titan, son of Iapetus and Themis, who helped make man and other animals. After Epimetheus gave animals their gifts, he brought fire from heaven with Minerva's aid, taught and protected mankind, and favored humanity. Jupiter bound him to Mount Caucasus, where a vulture preyed on him; he refused to reveal Jupiter's fatal marriage and became a symbol of endurance and resistance.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II

Prometheus, a Titan and son of Iapetus and Themis, helped make man and brought fire from heaven with Minerva's aid after Epimetheus had given other animals their gifts. He taught and protected mankind, but Jupiter condemned him to perpetual imprisonment on Mount Caucasus, where a vulture preyed on his liver. Prometheus refused to reveal the fatal marriage that would bring about Jupiter's ruin and became a symbol of endurance and resistance to oppression.
