  • In The Aeneid, the Trojan horse is a huge wooden steed concealing Greek soldiers who emerge at night and help destroy Troy.
  • In The Trojan Women of Euripides, the Trojan Horse is a towering wooden offering concealing Greek soldiers who destroy Troy after the Trojans bring it inside the gates.
  • In Hecuba and other plays, the Trojan horse is a wooden vehicle mistaken for a sacred peace pledge that conceals armed Greeks and admits the force that destroys Troy.
  • In The Odyssey, the Trojan Horse conceals Greek warriors and enables them to attack and destroy Troy.
  • In The Library, the Wooden Horse is a hollow sacred offering containing Ulysses and fifty others, who emerge in Troy and destroy the city.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the Wooden Horse is depicted by its head alone above the walls of Troy near figures associated with the city's fall.
  • In The Fall of Troy, the Wooden Horse is a deceptive offering to Athena that conceals Achaean warriors and enables the night-time taking of Troy.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Wooden Horse is a propitiatory offering filled with armed men that is brought into Troy so they can open the gates at night.

the Trojan horse in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

A huge pine-plank fabric shaped as a monstrous steed, built by Epeus and the Greeks with Minerva’s aid, conceals armed Greek soldiers. Intended as a deceptive offering to enter Troy, it is hauled into the city despite Laocoon’s warning. At night its hidden forces emerge within the gates and help destroy Troy.

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

The wooden horse, made by Epeus with Minerva’s assistance, conceals the bravest Argives and Achaeans, including Ulysses. While they wait inside, Odysseus prevents Anticlus from answering Helen’s imitated voices. Wooden Horse in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

The Wooden Horse is a wooden horse and deceptive offering to Athena, proposed by Odysseus, fashioned by Epeius with Athena's aid, and filled with concealed Achaean and Argive warriors. The Trojan people decorate and bring it into Troy while celebrating, unaware that it hides ruin. Cassandra attempts to destroy it, but is stopped. When Sinon signals, Odysseus, the chiefs, and other warriors descend by Epeius' ladders and pour into Troy, leading to the night-time taking of the city.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

The Fall of Troy

The Wooden Horse is the device described in the contents as being fashioned and brought into Troy by its people. Its construction and entry are treated in Book XII, and the horse is connected with the night-time taking of Troy in Book XIII.

BOOK XII

The Wooden Horse is proposed by Odysseus as a deceptive offering to Athena, with Achaean warriors concealed inside it. Epeius fashions it with Athena's aid, and the Trojans decorate it and drag it into Troy.

The Argive Horse stands in Troy while the Trojans celebrate, unaware that ruin is hidden inside it. Cassandra tries to break or burn it, but the Trojans stop her; the Achaeans within hear the uproar and rejoice at her rejection.

BOOK XIII

The Trojan Horse conceals the Argive chiefs and warriors until Sinon gives the signal. Odysseus and the others descend by ladders made by Epeius and pour into Troy.
