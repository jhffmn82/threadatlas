Poets in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

Poets are called hallowed and cared for by the Deities; believed inspired, their verses preserve lasting fame from Trojan deeds to Tibullus's poetry.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ELEGY IX.

Poets are called hallowed and are believed by some to possess divine inspiration. Their verses can preserve lasting fame, as shown by the enduring accounts of Trojan deeds and by Tibullus's surviving poetry.

IV.

Lentulus assents to praise of celebrated poets with visible reserve, as though he knows that they are merely versifiers. He knows little of particular poets but believes that poetry should use true ideas and that the world has no notion what poetry will become; the narrator concludes that Lentulus cannot eclipse or fundamentally revise the poets.
