Tyndareus's oath

event · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Helen’s suitor oath, oath, oath of Helen’s suitors, oath of the suitors, oaths, oaths of Helen’s suitors, sure oaths, swear and vow, the oaths which they had sworn, these oaths, unmixed libations, ὅρκων

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Helen’s suitor oath, sworn before her marriage, bound all suitors not to act independently and to unite in punishing anyone who seized her by force. In The Library, Tyndareus’s oath bound Helen’s suitors to defend the bridegroom chosen for her against anyone who wronged him over the marriage; Odysseus proposed it to prevent quarrels, Tyndareus imposed it, and later Agamemnon invoked the kings’ oaths after Helen’s abduction.

The oath required all of Helen’s suitors to help the chosen bridegroom if another person wronged him concerning the marriage. Tyndareus imposed the oath after Odysseus proposed it as a way to prevent quarrels among the suitors.

Tz 15 «Τὸν δ᾽ ᾿Αγαμέμνονα ὃ τροφὸς μετὰ τοῦ Μενελάου

Menelaus and the other kings had sworn oaths concerning Helen. Agamemnon reminded the kings of these oaths and urged each to defend his own wife after Helen was abducted.
