Helen’s suitor oath in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Helen’s suitor oath, sworn before her marriage, bound all suitors not to act independently and to unite in punishing anyone who seized her by force.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Fragment #67—Scholiast on Euripides, Orestes 249: Steischorus says that

Helen’s suitor oath required all the suitors to swear, with unmixed libations, that none would act independently concerning Helen’s marriage. If anyone took her by force, all the others were to follow and punish him.

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Tyndareus’s oath bound Helen’s suitors to defend the bridegroom chosen for her against anyone who wronged him over the marriage. Odysseus proposed it to prevent quarrels, Tyndareus imposed it, and later Agamemnon invoked the kings’ oaths after Helen’s abduction.

Εἰσὶ δὲ οἱ λέγοντες ᾿Αφαρέα μὲν καὶ Λεύκιππον

The oath required all of Helen’s suitors to help the chosen bridegroom if another person wronged him concerning the marriage. Tyndareus imposed the oath after Odysseus proposed it as a way to prevent quarrels among the suitors.

Tz 15 «Τὸν δ᾽ ᾿Αγαμέμνονα ὃ τροφὸς μετὰ τοῦ Μενελάου

Menelaus and the other kings had sworn oaths concerning Helen. Agamemnon reminded the kings of these oaths and urged each to defend his own wife after Helen was abducted.
