mistress in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

The speaker’s new mistress watches a chariot race beside him, is jostled by the crowd, favors the winning charioteer, inspires his passion, and promises him something more.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ELEGY II.

The mistress watches the chariot race beside the speaker and is briefly pressed by the people around her. She favors one charioteer, whose victory fulfills her wishes, and she gives the speaker an encouraging sign and a promise of something more.

woman at the races in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

A woman attending circus races favors a charioteer while a poet beside her desires her, praises her appearance, seeks Venus’s aid, and reads her smile and eyes as promise of further favor.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

LIBER TERTIUS

The woman at the races favors one of the charioteers, and the poet tries to win her favor while sitting beside her. He praises her legs and appearance, asks Venus to make her receptive to love, and interprets her smile and expressive eyes as a promise to grant him more elsewhere.
