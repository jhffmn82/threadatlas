Abstract written by a model

In Heroides and Amores, it presents excessive guardianship toward a mistress as provoking hatred and making illicit love more desirable, while silent guardianship benefits the guardian. In The Amores, or Amours, it depicts jealous surveillance and restraint as tactics that preserve a lover’s interest by creating anxiety and obstacles around Corinna, but fail to secure chastity.

jealous surveillance and restraint in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where jealous surveillance and restraint first appears

Abstract written by a model

Jealous surveillance and restraint are depicted as tactics that preserve a lover’s interest by creating anxiety and obstacles around Corinna, but they fail to secure chastity: confinement cannot govern the mind and instead provokes temptation and criminality, whereas indulgence restrains more effectively.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ELEGY XIX.

Jealous surveillance is presented as a way to create anxiety and obstacles that sustain the lover's interest. The husband is urged to investigate nighttime footsteps, barking dogs, the handmaid's tablets, and Corinna's habit of lying apart.

ELEGY IV.

Guarding and confinement do not control the mind and may instigate criminality by constraint. The speaker compares restraint to reins resisted by a horse and says that indulgence can restrain more effectively.
