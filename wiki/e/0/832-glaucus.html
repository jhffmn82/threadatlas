Glaucus in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Glaucus is a Lycian warrior and captain of noble lineage, son of Hippolochus and descended from Bellerophon. He leads the Lycians with his brother Sarpedon, accepting Sarpedon’s call to fight at the front. He tells Diomed his lineage, recognizes old ties of friendship, and exchanges his golden armor for Diomed’s less valuable bronze armor. Wounded by Teucer’s arrow, he withdraws, but after Sarpedon’s death he prays to Apollo, who heals and strengthens him. Glaucus then rallies Lycian and Trojan leaders to defend Sarpedon’s body, rebukes Hector for failing Sarpedon, and threatens that the Lycians will leave Troy unless the Trojans recover Patroclus’s body.

Glaucus leads the Lycians together with Sarpedon. The Lycian contingent comes from the distant land by the eddying waters of the Xanthus.

Glaucus tells Diomed the history of his descent from Sisyphus, Bellerophon, and Hippolochus. He agrees to friendship with Diomed and exchanges his golden armor for Diomed’s bronze armor, which is described as greatly less valuable.

BOOK XII.

Glaucus leads the Lycians alongside Sarpedon and accepts Sarpedon’s call to fight at the front. Teucer wounds him in the shoulder with an arrow, causing him to withdraw covertly from the fighting.

Now when Sarpedon saw his comrades, men who wore ungirdled

Glaucus grieves when Sarpedon is killed and prays to Apollo for healing so that he can fight for Sarpedon. Apollo heals his wound and restores his strength, after which Glaucus rallies the Lycian captains, the Trojans, Aeneas, and Hector to defend Sarpedon’s body.

BOOK XVII.

Glaucus sharply rebukes Hector for failing to save Sarpedon and for retreating before Ajax. He proposes that the Lycians leave Troy unless the Trojans recover Patroclus’s body.

Glaucus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Glaucus, son of Minos and Hippolochus, Lycian leader and Trojan ally, is resurrected by Polyidus and exchanges arms with Diomedes.

1 section; each heading opens the text
