Retrieval-Augmented Multilingual Keyphrase Generation with Retriever-Generator Iterative Training

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

This paper introduces multilingual keyphrase generation: using one model to predict keyphrases in the same language as an input long text across German, Spanish, Italian, French, Korean, and Chinese. It aims to reduce annotation, training, and deployment costs and alleviate low-resource scarcity through joint multilingual training. The study contributes EcommerceMKP, with 73,000 e-commerce examples in Spanish, German, Italian, and French, and AcademicMKP, with 2,693 Chinese and Korean academic papers. Its central method is the Retrieval-Augmented Multilingual Keyphrase Generator (RAMKG). It retrieves related English passages through Cross-Lingual Dense Passage Retrieval, then uses mBART to generate keyphrases from the source passage and retrieved English keyphrases. English passage-keyphrase resources supply external knowledge across e-commerce and academic topics, addressing multilingual retrieval difficulties caused by vocabulary gaps. Because bilingual data is limited, retriever-generator iterative training (RGIT) mines pseudo-parallel passage pairs to improve the retriever and, in turn, generation. The paper details dataset collection and cleaning, English retrieval resources, generation and retrieval metrics, and comparisons with statistical, graph-based, feature-based, neural, Transformer, Copy-RNN, mBART, and RAMKG variants. Results show supervised methods beating unsupervised ones; Transformer surpassing Copy-RNN; and jointly trained mBART exceeding monolingual mBART. RAMKG achieves higher average precision, recall, and F1 than the listed mBART variants on both datasets, while RGIT further improves retrieval and generation. However, external English data has no positive effect on AcademicMKP. A German EcommerceMKP case study finds that retrieved English keyphrases offer useful information despite some noise. The paper concludes that its datasets and retrieval-augmented framework outperform a broad range of baselines.

Abstract

The paper introduces multilingual keyphrase generation, a setting for automatically predicting keyphrases in languages beyond English. It contributes two datasets, EcommerceMKP and AcademicMKP, covering six languages. Its retrieval-augmented method uses a cross-lingual dense passage retrieval module to find relevant English passages whose keyphrase annotations support generation in low-resource languages. A retriever-generator iterative training algorithm mines pseudo parallel passage pairs to strengthen the cross-lingual passage retriever, and experiments show that the approach outperforms all baselines.

Introduction

The introduction concerns multilingual keyphrase generation, a task that predicts keyphrases for long texts and has been studied primarily on English datasets. It presents two new datasets: EcommerceMKP, with 73,000 examples in Spanish, German, Italian, and French, and AcademicMKP, with 2,693 Chinese and Korean academic papers. The authors propose a retrieval-augmented method that uses related English passages and keyphrases to support generation in lower-resource languages. They also introduce retriever-generator iterative training to mine pseudo-parallel data and report improvements across experiments on both datasets.

Related Work

This unit reviews neural keyphrase generation and Retrieval Augmented Text Generation (RAG). It describes CopyRNN and subsequent work using attention, extra structure, reinforcement learning, GANs, hierarchical decoding, dynamic alignment, and pre-trained language models. It explains that English passage-keyphrase training data can serve as external knowledge for keyphrase generation, while prior retrieval methods do not generalize well to multilingual settings because of vocabulary gaps. The RAMKG framework uses Cross-Lingual Dense Passage Retrieval to find relevant English passages and their keyphrases for multilingual keyphrase generation with code-mixed inputs.

Task Definition

The paper addresses the multilingual keyphrase generation task, in which one model generates keyphrases for texts in multiple languages. A single multilingual keyphrase generation model could reduce the cost of collecting annotations, the effort of training and deploying separate models, and the effects of resource scarcity through joint training with monolingual data. Given a text in one language, the task is to predict its corresponding keyphrases in that same language. The study covers German, Spanish, Italian, French, Korean, and Chinese.

The unit presents a retrieval-augmented approach for multilingual keyphrase generation that uses English text-keyphrase resources to support generation in other languages. Its Retrieval-Augmented Multilingual Keyphrase Generator combines a cross-lingual dense passage retrieval step with an mBART generation step that consumes source passages and retrieved English keyphrases. The unit also introduces retriever-generator iterative training to mine pseudo-parallel passage pairs when only limited bilingual data is available. It describes the AcademicMKP Dataset and EcommerceMKP Dataset and reports that English resources cover broad topics and concepts in both academic and e-commerce domains.

Datasets .. Baselines and Ablations

The unit describes the EcommerceMKP and AcademicMKP datasets, their language coverage, collection and cleaning procedures, and the English resources used for retrieval-augmented training. It defines evaluation metrics for keyphrase generation and passage retrieval, then lists statistical, graph-based, feature-based, neural, mBART, and RAMKG baselines and ablations. RAMKG achieves higher average precision, recall, and F1 than the listed mBART variants on both datasets, with further improvements from RGIT.

Results and Analyses / Main Results .. Case Study

This unit reports results and analyses for multilingual keyphrase generation on EcommerceMKP and AcademicMKP. Supervised approaches outperform unsupervised methods, with Transformer outperforming Copy-RNN in the multilingual setting and jointly trained mBART outperforming monolingual mBART. RAMKG and retriever-generator iterative training improve retrieval and generation by using mined pseudo parallel passage pairs, although external English data provides no positive effect on AcademicMKP. A German EcommerceMKP case study shows that retrieved English keyphrases provide useful information despite introducing some noise.

Conclusion

The study investigates the novel task of multilingual keyphrase generation. It contributes two new multilingual keyphrase generation datasets covering multiple domains and languages. It also proposes a retrieval-augmented multilingual keyphrase generation framework with retriever-generator iterative training, which outperforms a wide range of baselines.
