Thracians in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · -0299/-0200

The Thracians were the people raided by the Lemnian men and were feared by the Lemnian women as possible attackers. The Lemnian women watched the sea anxiously for their arrival, and Hypsipyle falsely told Jason that the Lemnian men were living among the Thracians.

the Thracians in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

The Thracians are Rhesus's immense army and Trojan allies in the Trojan War. After campaigning against the Scythians, they march by night around Ida to aid Troy, bringing horses, shield-bearers, archers, and infantry. Hector assigns Apollo as their emergency watchword. That night, Rhesus and much of the force are destroyed; his wounded charioteer reports it.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

RHESUS.

The Thracian army arrives under Rhesus and is described as immense, with many horses, shield-bearing troops, archers, and infantry in Thracian dress. It has marched by night through the area around Ida to aid Troy.

RHESUS, HECTOR, CHORUS.

The Thracian host accompanies Rhesus to Troy after his campaign against the Scythians. Hector orders that Apollo be used as the watchword for the Thracian host in an emergency.

ULYSSES, DIOMEDE.

The Thracians arrive as allies of Troy, but their king and much of their force are destroyed during the night. Their charioteer survives with a wound and reports the disaster.

Thracians in The Rhesus of Euripides

Euripides · -0454/-0439

The Thracians are Rhesus’s army, arriving from the Thracian lands through Bithynia to aid Troy. A large mixed force of horsemen, targeteers, archers, and lightly armed mountaineers, they impress observers with their numbers and equipment. Rhesus says they defeated the Scythians and will fight the Greeks without other Trojans. Before battle, however, they are surprised asleep: many are killed or wounded, their horses taken, and survivors pursue the attackers and report the disaster.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

RHESUS

The Thracian army arrives with Rhesus to support Troy. It includes horsemen, targeteers, archers, and lightly armed troops, and its large numbers and equipment impress the shepherd who sees it approaching Mount Ida.

_Enter_ RHESUS _in dazzling white armour, followed by his_ [vv. 379-395]

Rhesus arrives with Thracians who have marched from the Thracian lands through Bithynia to Troy. He claims that they fought and defeated the Scythians before continuing their journey. He intends for these late-coming soldiers to fight the Greeks without requiring the rest of the Trojans to join them.

_Enter_ PARIS.

The Thracians are surprised while sleeping, and many are killed or wounded before the attackers escape with the horses. Their surviving men pursue Odysseus and Diomede and later report the disaster to the Trojan guards.

NOTES

The Thracians arrive under Rhesus as a large, noisy, mixed force of mountaineers. They are portrayed as boasting before battle, using brutal proposed punishments, and possessing distinctive northern traditions of fighting, music, and burial.

Thracians in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

The Thracians are a Trojan-allied people led by Rhesus son of Eioneus, camped apart at the far end with armour and horses nearby; while sleeping, Diomed kills Rhesus and twelve soldiers.
