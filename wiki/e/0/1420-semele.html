Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Semele, also called Thyone, is Cadmus’s daughter and Dionysus’s mortal mother by Zeus. In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Semele is Kadmos’s immortal daughter, cherished on Olympus and honored in spring by flute-accompanied hymns despite dying in thunder. In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Semele is Cadmus’s daughter who bears Bacchus by Jove, dies from Jove’s lightning, and is defended in Thebes by Bacchus. In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, Semele is the Theban princess, bride of Zeus, and mother of Dionysus who dies when heavenly fire consumes her; her sacred tomb is in Pentheus’s Castle. In The Bacchae of Euripides, Semele is the Theban princess and Dionysus’s mother who dies consumed by heavenly fire, prompting Dionysus to prove she bore a god to Zeus. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Semele is Cadmus’s daughter who is loved by Jupiter, becomes pregnant by him, and is consumed by lightning before giving birth. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Semele is Jupiter’s mortal lover, Bacchus’s mother, and a woman consumed by Jupiter’s lightning who is later ranked among the goddesses as Thyone. In The Library, Semele is Cadmus and Harmonia’s daughter, secretly loved by Zeus and later named Thyone by Dionysus after being brought from Hades.

and 2 more works

In Pausanias’ Description of Greece, Volume II, Semele is the intended spouse of Actæon in one account and Dionysus’s mother by Zeus; the Thebans say her statue and sepulchre stand in the temple of Lysian Dionysus. (Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II) In Bulfinch’s Mythology: The Age of Fable, Semele is a mortal persuaded by Juno, disguised as Beroe, to seek Jupiter’s full splendor, which destroys her while Bacchus is saved. (Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable)

Semele in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Semele first appears

Abstract written by a model

Semele is the daughter of Cadmus, identified also as Cadmus and Hermione’s daughter. She is loved by Jupiter and becomes pregnant by him. One account says she conceives Osiris or Dionysius, while another calls her the mother of the third Bacchus, Jupiter’s son. After asking Jupiter to appear in divine majesty, she is consumed by lightning before giving birth; Jupiter removes the infant and entrusts him to Mercury. An Egyptian-derived account instead portrays the story as concealing her shame after a child resembling Osiris was born.

Semele in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Semele first appears

Abstract written by a model

Semele is a mortal female, daughter of Cadmus, and Jupiter’s lover. Pregnant by Jupiter with Bacchus, she accepts Juno’s deceptive advice, given while disguised as Beroë, and asks Jupiter to appear as he is received by Juno. When Jupiter visits as promised, his lightning overwhelms her mortal body; she burns to ashes among her nuptial presents while pregnant. She is Bacchus’s mother, with one account saying she delivered him in the seventh month and Jupiter carried him for the remaining period. She is buried at Oreate. After death she is ranked among the Goddesses as Thyone, is brought from hell to heaven by Bacchus, and there converses and eats with gods.

After her death, she was ranked among the Goddesses as Thyone, and Bacchus brought her from hell to heaven.
