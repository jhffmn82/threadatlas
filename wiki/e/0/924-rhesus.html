Rhesus in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Rhesus is a Thracian king, son of Strymon and the Muse, who rules Thrace and Pæonia, receives tribute from Scythians, and commands a Thracian host. A kinsman of Hector and raised to the throne by him, he comes late in the war’s tenth year to aid Troy after conflict with the Scythians. He arrives in a car drawn by famed white horses, with golden armor and shield, promising to destroy the Greeks and return home the next day. Ulysses and Diomede kill him in a night attack while he sleeps. The Muse later declares that he will be restored as a god and priest of Bacchus.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

RHESUS.

Rhesus, son of Strymon, is reported to have entered the fields of Troy with a large Thracian force. He travels in a chariot drawn by exceptionally white horses, wears resplendent golden armor, and comes to aid Troy.

RHESUS, HECTOR, CHORUS.

Rhesus arrives at Troy with Thracian forces after being delayed by war with the Scythians. He promises to storm the Greek fortifications, burn their fleet, kill the Greeks, and return home the next day, and he claims he will seize Ulysses if he encounters him.

ULYSSES, DIOMEDE.

Rhesus arrives at Troy with numerous Thracian troops to aid the Trojans. Ulysses and Diomede kill him while he sleeps, and the Muse later declares that he will be restored to life as a god and priest of Bacchus.

Rhesus in The Rhesus of Euripides

Euripides · -0454/-0439

Rhesus is a Thracian king, son of Strymon River and a Muse, and commander of a Thracian army. He comes to Troy/Ilion as an ally with military aid after defeating Scythians and crossing the Pontus. He is portrayed as magnificent and formidable: he arrives in dazzling white armour on a golden chariot, with white horses, a golden shield, richly decorated equipment, and attendants. Delayed by Scythian attacks, he tells Hector that he will break the Greek wall, burn the ships, and kill the Greek princes before departing on the second day; he also intends to seize Odysseus alive and impale him at the Gate. His importance lies in the threat his survival poses to the Greek camp. Athena therefore orders Odysseus and Diomede to kill him. He is slain asleep in the Thracian camp and his horses are taken. The Muse mourns him, saying he will become a spirit in a hidden chamber beneath the battlefield, awaiting his people's need.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

RHESUS

Rhesus arrives from Thrace with a large force to aid Troy. A shepherd describes him as a magnificent figure on a golden chariot with white horses, a golden shield, and richly decorated equipment.

_Enter_ RHESUS _in dazzling white armour, followed by his_ [vv. 379-395]

Rhesus arrives at Troy in dazzling white armour with his charioteer and attendants, bringing a golden shield and a Thracian force. He explains to Hector that Scythian attacks delayed his march, then vows to break the Greek wall, burn the ships, and kill the Greek princes before leaving on the second day. Athena identifies him as the man whose survival until morning could enable Troy's destruction of the Greek camp, and orders Odysseus and Diomede to kill him.

_Enter_ PARIS.

Rhesus is killed while sleeping in the Thracian camp, and his horses are taken. The Muse mourns him as her son and says that he will not be placed in an ordinary grave but will live as a spirit in a hidden place.

NOTES

Rhesus is presented as a powerful Thracian king whose horses, dazzling chariot, boasts, and heroic courage reflect northern and barbaric traditions. He is killed during the night attack, and the Muse says that he will lie in a hidden chamber beneath the earth, waiting to rise in his people's hour of greatest need.
