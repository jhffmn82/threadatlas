group · 5 works · 18 quoted facts · 22 connections

  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, the Ionians moved their Panionion festival to another location, sought sacred images from Helice and Bura, and sacrificed at Neptune’s altar under an Athenian edict.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Ionians sought Greek assistance to free the Asian cities, served with the Persians while considering revolt, and ultimately chose to remain in Asia as Greek confederates.
  • In Four Plays of Aeschylus, the Ionians fought the Persians at sea and helped bring about Persia’s ruin.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the Ionians gathered on Delos to honor Apollo with boxing, dancing, and song.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the Ionians were a people who were dispossessed from their territory and migrated to Attica, then settled cities in Asia Minor and formed the Pan-Ionic confederacy.

The Ionians moved their Panionion festival from Mycale because of wars and consulted Delphi about obtaining sacred images. After the people of Helice and Bura refused their request, the Ionians sacrificed at Neptune's altar under an Athenian public edict.

Ionians in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The Ionians were a people formerly called the Ægialians, a name changed after Ion ruled them. They inhabited territory later held by the Achæans, rejected the Achæans’ proposal to become colonists, fought them, endured siege at Helice, were dispossessed of territory and rule, and migrated to Attica. From Athens they formed the largest part of the expedition led by Nileus and the other sons of Codrus. They settled cities in Asia Minor, conquered Miletus, inhabited Myus and Priene, and lived or settled at Colophon, Lebedus, Teos, Erythræ, Clazomenæ, and Phocæa; at Phocæa they required kings descended from Codrus before its admission to the Pan-Ionic confederacy. They became equal fellow-citizens with Colophonian Greeks, colonized Samos, inhabited Chios, took Smyrna from the Æolians, and admitted Smyrna to the Pan-Ionic league. They possessed fertile Ionia, rich in produce and temples, and continued worship of Poseidon of Helice. Earlier, the Ionians of Aroe, Anthea, and Mesatis shared Artemis Triclaria’s temple and an all-night annual festival, but famine and sickness followed its violation until Eurypylus arrived.

Narrative, section by section

7 sections; each heading opens the text

Description of Greece, Volume II / BOOK VII.--ACHAIA. / CHAPTER I.

The Ionians were the former Ægialians, whose name was added after Ion ruled them. They opposed the Achæans' proposal to become their colonists, fought them, were besieged in Helice, and later migrated to Attica.

CHAPTER II.

The Ionians formed the largest part of the contingent led by Nileus and the other sons of Codrus. They settled several cities in Asia Minor, conquered Miletus, and inhabited Myus and Priene.

CHAPTER III.

The Ionians became fellow-citizens with the Colophonian Greeks on mutual and equal terms. They settled at places including Colophon, Lebedus, Teos, Erythræ, Clazomenæ, and Phocæa, and later required Phocæa to accept kings descended from Codrus before admitting it to the Pan-Ionic confederacy.

CHAPTER IV.

The Ionians inhabited Samos and Chios among the islands. They colonized Samos under Procles, and the community of the Ionians later honored Hector of Chios with a tripod for his prowess.

CHAPTER V.

The Ionians took Smyrna from the Æolians and later admitted its inhabitants to the Pan-Ionic league. They possessed a fertile country with magnificent temples and many notable natural and cultural sites.

CHAPTER VI.

The Ionians departed from the land that the Achæans then divided among twelve towns. Those towns had formerly been inhabited by the Ionians.

CHAPTER XIX.

The Ionians of Aroe, Anthea, and Mesatis shared a grove and temple of Artemis Triclaria and kept her festival annually throughout the night. They suffered famine, sickness, and increased mortality after Comætho and Melanippus violated the temple, and they practiced the prescribed annual human sacrifice until Eurypylus arrived.
