Maia in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Maia is a rich-tressed, shy nymph and goddess, daughter of Atlas, who avoids the deathless gods and lives in a deep shadowy cave on Cyllene. Zeus visits her there at night; she bears Hermes on the fourth day of the month, then rebukes him when he returns at night, warning that Apollo may punish his actions.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

IV. TO HERMES

Maia is a rich-tressed nymph and a shy goddess who lives in a deep, shady cave on Cyllene. Zeus visits her there, and she gives birth to Hermes on the fourth day of the month. She rebukes Hermes when he returns at night, warning that Apollo may punish him for his actions.

XVIII. TO HERMES

Maia is the daughter of Atlas and the mother of Hermes. She is described as a shy goddess who avoided the throng of the blessed gods and lived in a shadowy cave, where Zeus visited her at dead of night.
