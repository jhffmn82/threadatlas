Abstract written by a model

In Heroides and Amores, pregnancy is a hidden bodily burden that endangers Corinna, as she struggles to deliver and a speaker fearing responsibility begs Isis and Ilithyia to save her. In The Amores, or Amours, pregnancy and childbirth concern Corinna’s dangerous labor and unborn child, emphasizing the danger of premature interference and urging that pregnancy continue to birth.

pregnancy and childbirth in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where pregnancy and childbirth first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pregnancy and childbirth concern Corinna’s dangerous labor and her unborn child. Corinna struggles to disengage the burden in her womb, becoming exhausted and imperiled; Naso urges that she never face such combat again. The account then stresses allowing pregnancy to continue to birth, warning premature interference may kill the woman.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ELEGY XIII.

Corinna's pregnant womb carries a burden that she is trying to disengage, leaving her exhausted and in peril. Naso treats the labor as a struggle and asks that Corinna not undergo such a combat again.

ELEGY XIV.

Pregnancy is presented as a condition that should be allowed to continue until the child is born, rather than being ended prematurely. The speaker says that life is a substantial reward for waiting and warns that interfering with pregnancy can bring death to the woman herself.
