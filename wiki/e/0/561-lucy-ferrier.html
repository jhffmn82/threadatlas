Lucy Ferrier in A Study in Scarlet

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1886

Lucy Ferrier is John Ferrier’s adopted daughter, a child rescued with him after her mother dies during a desert journey and brought into a Mormon caravan. Cheerful and trusting at five, she grows into a skilled rider and an admired young woman, regarded by Brigham Young as “the flower of Utah.” She loves Jefferson Hope, becomes engaged to him with John Ferrier’s conditional consent, and waits for his return. Brigham Young requires her to choose between Stangerson’s son and Drebber’s son, but Lucy and her father refuse, fearing punishment while remaining devoted to Hope. She escapes the farm at night with John Ferrier and Hope, but pursuers overtake their camp and capture her. Mormon authorities force her to marry Drebber. She pines away and dies within a month, said by Hope to have died of a broken heart. Hope removes and keeps her wedding ring, and her memory remains central to his vengeance.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

PART II. / CHAPTER I.

Lucy Ferrier is a five-year-old girl who survives the desert journey with John Ferrier after her mother dies. She remains cheerful and trusting, comforts herself with the thought of rejoining her mother, and is rescued and taken into the Mormon caravan with Ferrier.

CHAPTER II.

Lucy Ferrier is adopted by John Ferrier and travels with the Mormons in Elder Stangerson’s waggon. She grows into a skilled rider and admired young woman, falls in love with Jefferson Hope, and promises to be ready to leave with him when he returns.

CHAPTER III.

Lucy learns that Brigham Young expects her to choose between Stangerson's son and Drebber's son. She remains devoted to Jefferson Hope and fears what may happen to her and her father if they oppose the Prophet.

CHAPTER IV.

Lucy supports her father’s refusal of the two young Mormons and trusts that Jefferson Hope will return. She leaves the farm with John Ferrier and Jefferson Hope at night, riding a mule during their escape toward the mountains.

CHAPTER V.

Lucy Ferrier flees with her father and Jefferson Hope, but she is captured when their pursuers overtake the camp. She is forced to marry Drebber, pines away, and dies within a month; Jefferson Hope removes her wedding ring before her burial.

CHAPTER VI.

Lucy Ferrier was engaged to marry Jefferson Hope but was forced to marry Enoch Drebber, and Hope says that she died with a broken heart. Hope repeatedly sees her memory during his vengeance and carries her marriage ring as a reminder of her.
