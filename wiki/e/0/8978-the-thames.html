the Thames in The Sign of the Four

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1890-02

The Thames is a river with many wharves and landing-places on both sides, especially below the bridge. Toby’s trail brings Holmes and Watson to its edge, where fugitives are thought to have boarded a boat. Searches on both sides fail, and Holmes goes downstream in disguise. It becomes the route of the Aurora’s rapid pursuit past bridges, docks, and marshes; the Islander falls into its dark ooze, and Jonathan Small throws away the treasure-box key.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter VIII

Toby’s trail leads Holmes and Watson to the river’s edge at a small wharf. Holmes believes the fugitives boarded a boat there and notes that the many landing-places below the bridge would make a systematic search difficult.

Chapter IX

The river is searched on both sides for the missing launch, without success. Holmes goes down the river in disguise after considering that the launch may have gone upstream.

Chapter X

The Thames is the setting for the pursuit of the Aurora, which travels rapidly downstream past bridges, docks, and marshes. The Islander falls into the river, and his bones are said to lie somewhere in the dark ooze at its bottom.

Chapter XI

The Thames is the river down which the Aurora is pursued. Jonathan Small says that he threw the key to the iron treasure box into the Thames.
