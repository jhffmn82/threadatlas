Demophon

person · 2 works · 6 quoted facts · 8 connections
Also called: Demophoon, boy, child, dear son, infant son of Celeus, late-born son, nursling, Δημοφῶν, Δημοφῶντα

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Demophoon is the late-born son of Celeus and Metaneira, nursed by Demeter and growing like an immortal until Metaneira’s interruption leaves him mortal. In The Library, the infant son of Celeus is the child whom Demeter placed on the fire in an attempt to make him immortal; the sources disagree over whether he was Demophon or Triptolemus, who interrupted the rite, and whether he died. In The Library, Demophon is the child of Celeus and younger brother of Triptolemus whom Demeter tried to make immortal on the fire; he later married Phyllis, received her kingdom, left promising to return, settled in Cyprus, and died after opening Mother Rhea’s casket and falling on his sword when his horse stumbled.

Demophoon is the late-born son of Metaneira and Celeus, whom Demeter nurses in their palace. He grows like an immortal being while Demeter tends him, but he remains mortal after Metaneira interrupts the goddess’s work.

Abstract written by a model

Demophon, child of Celeus and younger brother of Triptolemus, was the infant Demeter tried to make immortal on the fire. He later married Phyllis, received her kingdom, left her promising to return, settled in Cyprus, and died after opening Mother Rhea’s casket and falling on his sword when his horse stumbled.

Demophon arrived with a few ships among the Thracian Bisaltians and married Phyllis, receiving the kingdom as her dowry. He left for his homeland after promising to return, went to Cyprus, and died after opening the casket of Mother Rhea, falling on his sword when his horse stumbled.

Demophon is identified by Apollodorus and the Homeric hymn-writer as the infant whom Demeter tried to make immortal by placing him on the fire. He is distinguished from his elder brother Triptolemus.
