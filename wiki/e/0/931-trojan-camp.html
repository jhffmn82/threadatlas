Trojan camp

place · 3 works · 3 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: camp, lines, the Trojan camp, the camp, the far end of the camp, trenches

Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, the Trojan camp lies behind Dolon as he runs toward the ships, is the place near which Hector and the councillors confer, and is thrown into an uproar after Rhesus’s horses are gone and soldiers are dying. In The Rhesus of Euripides, the Trojan camp lies near the Greeks, with watch-fires among sleeping and watching allies, and hosts night councils, Guards’ movements, Rhesus’s attack, and the discovery of disaster. In Hecuba and other plays, the Trojan camp is threatened by Rhesus’s Thracian army, entered by Ulysses and Diomede at night, and occupied by Rhesus’s troops while its guards fail to prevent the raid.

the Trojan camp in The Rhesus of Euripides

Euripides · 454 to 439 BC · the work’s page · read it where the Trojan camp first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Trojan camp lies near the Greeks on the battlefield, with watch-fires among sleeping and watching allies; it hosts night councils, Guards’ movements, Rhesus’s attack, and discovery of disaster.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

NOTES

The Trojan camp lies on the battlefield close to the Greek lines, with Hector's tent or hut near the front and watch fires around the camp. It is the setting for the night councils, the movement of the Guards, the attack on Rhesus, and the discovery of the disaster.
