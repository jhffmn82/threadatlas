the bow of Ulysses in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Ulysses’ bow is a weapon, given to him by Iphitus son of Eurytus and kept as a valued keepsake. It has two horns and a bow-string that sings when drawn. After Ulysses’ absence, Penelope makes it central to a tournament: the suitors must string it and shoot through twelve axes. They fail, even after warming and greasing it. Ulysses examines the bow, strings it as easily as a skilled bard strings a lyre, and sends an arrow through the axe handles. He then uses it from near the doorway to kill Antinous and many other suitors, before setting it against the wall when his arrows are gone.

Ulysses uses his bow to kill Antinous and many other suitors while standing near the doorway. When his arrows are exhausted, he sets the bow against the wall and takes up a shield and two spears.

bow of Ulysses in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Received from Iphitus, Ulysses’ bow tests Penelope’s suitors: none can bend it; Ulysses takes it and shoots them.

Penelope presents the bow of Ulysses to the suitors and declares that she will live with the man who can bend it. None of the suitors can bend it, but Ulysses takes it and uses it to shoot them.
