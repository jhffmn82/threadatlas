Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Io is changed into a white cow by Zeus after Hera discovers his seduction of her, and Hera sets Argus to watch her. In Four Plays of Aeschylus, Io is transformed into a horned heifer, driven from Argos by Hera’s gadfly through many regions, restored by Zeus, and made the mother of Epaphus. In Four Plays of Aeschylus, Io is transformed into an ox-horned heifer by Hera, driven into frenzy across lands and seas, restored in Egypt, and made ancestress of the Danaids. In Four Plays of Aeschylus, Io is harassed by Hera’s gadfly, wanders through many lands, is restored by Zeus in Egypt, and bears Epaphus. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Io is transformed into a cow, driven to wander by Juno, restored to human form in Egypt, and worshipped there as Isis. In The Library, Io is changed into a white cow, guarded by Argus, tormented by Hera’s gadfly, restored in Egypt, and identified with Isis. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Io is changed into a heifer by Jupiter, guarded by Argus, driven around the world by Juno’s gadfly, and restored to her family.

Io in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · 471 BC · the work’s page · read it where Io first appears

Abstract written by a model

Io is a partly human daughter of Inachus, priestess of Hera in Argos, and beloved of Zeus. Hera’s jealousy transforms her into an ox-horned heifer and drives her into frenzy; Argus watches her as she wanders over lands and seas, suffering a divinely imposed affliction comparable to Prometheus’s punishment. Prometheus recounts her past and future wanderings before she departs again. Io eventually reaches Egypt, where Zeus restores her to sanity and human form by touch. She then gives birth to Epaphus and becomes ancestor of the daughters of Danaus, who claim descent through him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

INTRODUCTION

Io is a partly human figure who wanders past Prometheus after suffering from the lust of Zeus and the jealousy of Hera. Prometheus recounts her past and future wanderings to her before she departs again into the unknown.

THE SUPPLIANT MAIDENS / DEDICATION

Io, the daughter of Inachus and beloved of Zeus, is driven into frenzy by Hera’s jealousy and takes the form of a heifer. After being watched by Argus and pursued across lands and seas, she reaches Egypt, where Zeus restores her and she bears Epaphus.

The Suppliant Maidens

Io is described as the ox-horned maiden and ancestress of the suppliants. After long wandering and suffering, she was touched by Zeus and became the mother of Epaphus, through whom the daughters claim descent.

Enter the KING OF ARGOS.

Io is described as an Argive priestess of Hera who became a heifer through Hera’s will after Zeus loved her. She was guarded by Argus, driven by a gadfly through many lands, and eventually restored to human form by Zeus.

Io in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · 471 BC · the work’s page · read it where Io first appears

Abstract written by a model

Io, daughter of Inachus and ox-horned maiden in animal form, is loved by Zeus and harassed by Hera. Stung continually by a gadfly and driven mad, she reaches Prometheus’s prison seeking her fate. Her divinely inflicted transformation and wandering continue through many lands, including Bosporos and the Ionian Sea, until Egypt, where Zeus restores her reason; she bears Epaphus, founds a new abode, and becomes ancestress of the Danaids and ancestor of her future saviour.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Enter IO, horned like a cow.

Io arrives at Prometheus’s prison, horned and driven mad by the gadfly, and asks him to reveal the causes and future course of her suffering. She learns that her wandering will continue through many lands before she reaches Egypt, where Zeus will restore her reason and she will bear Epaphus.

Io in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Io first appears

Abstract written by a model

Io is a nymph, daughter of Inachus and granddaughter of Phoroneus, whom Greek authors identify with Isis. After Jupiter invites her into the groves, she flees, but he overtakes and ravishes her under darkness. Jupiter changes her into a cow and entrusts her to Argus, under Juno’s control. In captivity she cannot use human speech; she tells Inachus who she is by tracing letters in dust with her foot. Juno then drives her, through Fury and invisible stings, to wander across Earth. At the Nile, the limit of her wanderings, she begs in groans, tears, and lowing for release. She regains human form, bears Epaphus through Jupiter, and is worshipped in Egypt as Isis, adored by priests and Isis’s worshippers in linen vestments. She accompanies Epaphus to Egypt; Greek accounts also say she marries Apis or Osiris.

Abstract written by a model

Io, a priestess of Hera, was seduced by Zeus and changed into a white cow. Guarded by Argus and tormented by Hera’s gadfly, she wandered through Europe and Asia. In Egypt she regained human form, bore Epaphus, married Telegonus after finding him in Syria, and was identified with Isis.

Io in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Io first appears

Abstract written by a model

Io, Inachus’s daughter, is a nymph of mortal mould, allegorically representing the moon. Jupiter changes her into a heifer; guarded by Argus and driven worldwide by Juno’s gadfly, she identifies herself, then is restored and reunited with her family.

Chapter III

Io, the daughter of Inachus, is changed into a heifer by Jupiter and guarded by Argus. She reveals her identity to her father by writing her name in the sand with her hoof, then flees across the world from Juno's gadfly. Jupiter's intercession leads Juno to restore Io's human form, and she is returned to her father and sisters.
