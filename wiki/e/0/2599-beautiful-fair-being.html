beautiful fair being in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

A beauteous fair, constant to the speaker, is sung by the Poet and presents herself to Naso as reward for his verse. She has delicate fingers, is imagined receiving and handling a ring, and bathes her limbs in a tepid stream.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK THE SECOND ELEGY I.

The beautiful fair being is often sung by the Poet, and she presents herself to him as the reward for his verse.

ELEGY XV.

The mistress is expected to receive the ring joyfully, place it on her finger, and handle it. The speaker imagines touching her bosom, sealing her tablets, and seeing her naked while she bathes.
