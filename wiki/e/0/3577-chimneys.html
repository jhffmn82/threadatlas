chimneys in The Life and Adventures of Santa Claus

L. Frank Baum · 1902

Chimneys are entrances that provide access to children in locked houses. Traditional chimneys have fireplaces and let Claus climb down to leave toys for sleeping children, then climb back up to his sledge. New-fashioned chimneys are narrow, may end in cellars, serve stoves, nearly trap Santa Claus, and are abandoned.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

8. The First Journey with the Reindeer

Claus uses chimneys to enter houses whose doors are locked at night. He climbs down them to leave toys for sleeping children and climbs back up to return to the sledge.

3. The Deputies of Santa Claus

New-fashioned chimneys are built narrow and may end in a cellar without a fireplace. Santa Claus initially tries to use them but nearly becomes stuck, and he later abandons them when he learns that they serve stoves.
