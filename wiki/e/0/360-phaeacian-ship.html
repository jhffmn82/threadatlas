Phaeacian ship in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

A new Phaeacian ship, manned by fifty-two sailors, is fitted and launched to escort Ulysses home. It swiftly carries him asleep from Scheria to Ithaca and reaches the harbour of Phorcys. Returning, it is transformed by Neptune into stone and rooted in the ground near Scheria.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK VIII

A new Phaeacian ship is drawn into the sea and prepared by fifty-two young sailors. Its mast and sails are fitted, its oars are secured, and it is moored a little way from land for Ulysses’ journey home.

BOOK XIII

The Phaeacian ship carries Ulysses swiftly from Scheria to Ithaca while he sleeps and runs partway onto the shore at the harbour of Phorcys. On its return journey, Neptune transforms it into stone and roots it in the ground.

Phaeacian ship in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

A Phaeacian ship carries sleeping Ulysses and his gifts from Scheria to Ithaca, then Neptune transforms the returning vessel into a rock opposite the harbor.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXIII

A Phaeacian ship carries Ulysses and his gifts from Scheria to Ithaca while he sleeps. Neptune, angered that the Phaeacians rescued him, transforms the returning vessel into a rock opposite the harbor.
