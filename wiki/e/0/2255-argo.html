Abstract written by a model

In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Argo is the fifty-oared ship carrying Jason and the Argonauts on their dangerous voyage to Phasis in Colchis. In Medea, Argo is the first ship, built for Jason’s expedition to Colchis, associated with his heroic past, and later left to rot. In The Argonautica, Argo is the swift, excellent ship that carries Jason and the Argonauts to Colchis and back, surviving their voyages and returning to Pagasae. In The Argonautica, Argo is the hollow ship built for the Argonauts, carrying them to Colchis, speaking through its Dodonian beam, and being carried across the desert. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Argo is the unusually large ship built for Jason’s expedition, carrying the Argonauts to Colchis and back before being dedicated at the Isthmus. In Heroides and Amores, the Argo is the first ship to open sea routes, carrying Jason and his companions through the clashing rocks with the Golden Fleece. In The Library, Argo is Jason’s ship and flagship, carrying him and the Argonauts to the Phasis, through the dangerous rocks, and back before being dedicated to Poseidon. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the Pagasæan ship is built for Jason and the Argonauts, with its name explained by several possible origins.

Argo in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · 299 to 200 BC · the work’s page · read it where Argo first appears

Abstract written by a model

Argo is a swift, excellent, well-benched ship: the heroes’ vessel, rowed and sailed by the Minyae and carrying Jason, his comrades, and the Argonauts in pursuit of the golden fleece. Athena fashions and guides its construction, with Argus helping by her counsels; it is the finest ship tested with oars, stronger than the ships of the Colchians, firm in its bolts, and able to endure blasts. A divine beam is fitted into the middle of its stem. Tiphys son of Hagnias commands and guides it at the helm. Prepared at Pagasae, Argo is dragged over rollers, launched, and furnished with oars, mast, sails, stores, anchors, ropes, and the divine beam. It first sails along the coast to Lemnos, then continues through the Hellespont, reaches Fair Haven and Chytus, is driven back to the Doliones, and waits through a twelve-day storm before departing again. It leaves the Mysian coast without Heracles and Hylas, passes the Bebrycians, and survives the Bosporus storm and great wave. Warned about the Cyanean rocks, it passes between them when Athena pushes it through; its stern-ornament is damaged, but the ship escapes. Argo proceeds past rivers, headlands, islands, and peoples to the island of Ares, then reaches the river Phasis. It carries dragon’s teeth after Aeetes gives them for the contest, receives Jason and his comrades after their tasks, and later carries Medea during the flight from Colchis. With the golden fleece aboard, it flees through Ister, the Cronian sea, and surrounding islands. The Nereids lift and guide it through the Wandering rocks, and it reaches Drepane, where Alcinous and the Phaeacians receive it. Its hardest later passage comes when it is thrust onto the shore of Syrtis. The heroes carry it across the Libyan desert for twelve days and nights, set it in the Tritonian lake, and Triton guides it back into the sea. It then sails past Carpathus to Crete, continues by Anaphe and Aegina, and returns at last to Pagasae.

Argo in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · 299 to 200 BC · the work’s page · read it where Argo first appears

Abstract written by a model

Argo is a hollow ship, built by Athena with Argus’s cooperation from timbers near Pelion, for the Argonauts’ voyage to Colchis. It has a Dodonian-oak beam in its stem, a stern-ornament, and a crew aboard; it bears the Argonauts in her womb. Jason offers it to the shipwrecked sons of Phrixus, and it carries the company past the Caucasus coast into the Phasis. A hawk is impaled on its stern-ornament before the Argonauts anchor it by the shore. Aeetes threatens to burn Argo and its crew. Driven by storms to Electra, Argo speaks through its beam, directing the heroes to Circe, then carries them through waterways to Aeaea. Finally, suffering labor in the desert, it is lifted and carried by the heroes.

Abstract written by a model

The Argo is a ship built by Argus for Jason’s expedition, carrying fifty men from Thessaly through Lemnos, Mysia, Thrace, and Colchis before returning; Jason then dedicates it to Neptune.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XI

The Argo was a vessel built by Argus to carry fifty men, an undertaking considered gigantic because Greek navigation then used small boats or canoes hollowed from tree trunks. It carried Jason and the Argonauts from Thessaly through Lemnos, Mysia, and Thrace to Colchis and back. After the return, Jason dedicated the Argo to Neptune.
