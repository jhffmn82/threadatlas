Castor

person · 5 works · 22 quoted facts · 8 connections
Also called: CASTOR, God's Horsemen, horse-taming Castor, my kinsmen, sons of Tyndareus, Κάστορος, Κάστωρ

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Castor is a horse-taming rider and one of the Dioscuri who is killed by Idas and granted alternating immortality with Polydeuces. In Hecuba and other plays, Castor appears with Pollux, stops Theoclymenus's attack, confirms Helen's return to Menelaus, and promises guidance at sea. In The Electra of Euripides, Castor appears with Polydeuces after the murders, judges Clytemnestra's death righteous, directs Orestes and Electra's futures, and attributes the bloodshed to Apollo and fate. In The Library, Castor is a Dioscurus warrior, son of Leda and Tyndareus, who helps recover Helen and raid Messene before Idas kills him. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Castor and Pollux are understood to be the two young men whose intervention saves Simonides from the banquet's collapse.

Castor in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · about 699 BC · the work’s page · read it where Castor first appears

Abstract written by a model

Castor is a horse-taming rider, one of the Tyndaridae and Dioscuri, born to Leda beneath Taygetus and called both a son of Zeus and a child or son of Tyndareus. He is brother to Polydeuces; together they are linked to Philoctetes and receive messages from Menelaus during the suit for Helen. Castor and Polydeuces steal cattle from Idas and Lynceus, after which Idas kills Castor. Because Castor is mortal and Polydeuces immortal, Zeus grants the brothers immortality on alternate days.

Castor is presented by Hesiod as a son of Zeus.

Fragment #67—Scholiast on Euripides, Orestes 249: Steischorus says that

Castor, called horse-taming, was a son of Tyndareus and brother of Polydeuces. He and Polydeuces would have made Philoctetes their brother by force, and Menelaus sent messages to them during his suit for Helen.

XVII. TO THE DIOSCURI

Castor is named with Polydeuces as one of the Tyndaridae. He sprang from Olympian Zeus, was borne by Leda beneath the heights of Taygetus, and is hailed as a child of Tyndareus and a rider upon swift horses.

XXXIII. TO THE DIOSCURI

Castor is named as one of the Tyndaridae and as a tamer of horses.

THE CYPRIA

Castor is caught stealing cattle with Polydeuces from Idas and Lynceus and is killed by Idas. He is mortal, while Polydeuces is immortal, and Zeus grants the two brothers immortality on alternate days.

Castor in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Castor first appears

Abstract written by a model

Castor, a son of Leda, was said to have been born to Tyndareus and Leda. A warrior, he and his brother Pollux were called the Dioscuri, recovered Helen from Aphidnae, and took cattle and booty to Messene. He married a daughter of Leucippus and had Anogon by Hilaira. Found hiding by Lynceus, he was killed by Idas, and is alternately among gods and mortals.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Εἰσὶ δὲ οἱ λέγοντες ᾿Αφαρέα μὲν καὶ Λεύκιππον

Castor was said to have been born to Tyndareus and Leda. He practiced warfare, while Pollux practiced boxing, and their bravery led both brothers to be called the Dioscuri. He and Pollux recovered Helen from Aphidnae after Theseus carried her away.

Of the sons born to Leda Castor practised the art

Castor practised the art of war and, with Pollux, was called one of the Dioscuri. He carried off and married a daughter of Leucippus, had Anogon by Hilaira, and joined Pollux in taking cattle and other booty to Messene. Lynceus discovered Castor in hiding and revealed his position to Idas, who killed him.
