Tibullus in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

Tibullus is a poet and the glory of Elegy IX. Though dead and burned on a funeral pile, his verse survives; he is imagined, if anything remains beyond name and spirit, in the Elysian vales.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ELEGY IX.

Tibullus is identified as the poet and glory of Elegy. After his death, his body is burned on a funeral pile, but his verse survives and he is imagined among the blessed in the Elysian vales.

Tibullus in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Tibullus is a sacred bard and the glory of Elegy. He dies, is burned on a high funeral pyre, and is buried as ashes in an urn while his mother and sister mourn; Delia and Nemesis loved him. His fame endures, and he is imagined received in Elysium.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

IX

Tibullus is described as a sacred poet and a singer of Elegy. He dies and is burned on a high-reared funeral pyre, leaving only ashes for a small urn, but his poetic fame is expected to endure and he is imagined in Elysium.
