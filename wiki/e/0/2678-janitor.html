janitor

Also called: Ianitor, Porter, porter, you, your

Abstract written by a model

In Heroides and Amores, the janitor was an enslaved, chained door guardian who repeatedly refused the lover's pleas to open his mistress's threshold. In The Amores, or Amours, the porter was a slave guarding his mistress's threshold in chains who remained unmoved by the lover's repeated pleas to open the door.

janitor in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · about 24 to 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where janitor first appears

Abstract written by a model

The janitor, a door guardian at the mistress's threshold, is enslaved and chained. Iron-hearted toward the lover's pleas, he repeatedly refuses to open the door despite appeals to gratitude and threats, seeming suited to guard a gloomy prison.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

VI

The janitor is bound by a hard chain and refuses to open the door for the lover despite repeated pleas, appeals to gratitude, and threats. The lover calls him harder than the door and says he is more suited to guarding a gloomy prison than the mistress's threshold.

porter in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where porter first appears

Abstract written by a model

Porter, a slave guarding his mistress's threshold in long cruel chains, refuses a lover's repeated pleas to open the door, remaining unmoved despite reminders that the lover once defended him from punishment.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

ELEGY VI.

The porter is fastened with a cruel fetter and refuses to open the door despite the lover's repeated appeals. The lover reminds him that he once defended him before his mistress when the porter was trembling before a whip, but the porter remains unmoved and is called hard-hearted, deaf, sluggish, and worthless.
