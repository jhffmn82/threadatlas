  • In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Memnon is the chieftain and leader of the Ethiops and Helenos’ fiery cousin who attacks Nestor while Nestor’s chariot is halted by a wounded horse, then fights Achilles hand to hand and is destined to die without returning home.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Memnon is Ethiopia’s brazen-crested king and son of Eos who aids the Trojans in Hephaestus-made armour, kills Antilochus, falls to Achilles, and receives immortality through Eos and Zeus.
  • In The Library, Memnon is the son of Tithonus and Eos who leads Ethiopians to Troy, kills many Achaeans including Antilochus, and is killed by Achilles.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Memnon is the son of Tithonus and Aurora and a warrior allied with Troy who fights for his uncle on the Phrygian plains, is slain by Achilles, and is honoured through a funeral whose pyre creates the Memnonides.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Memnon is a bearded king of the Ethiopians who came to Ilium from Susa in Persia and the river Choaspes; in a painting he sits beside Hector near Sarpedon, touching Sarpedon’s shoulder, cloaked in Memnonides birds that annually tend his tomb near the Hellespont.
  • In The Fall of Troy, Memnon is the Dawn’s son, an Aethiopian warrior-king and Troy’s newly summoned champion who arrives with a countless host, leads battle against the Argives, kills Antilochus, takes his armour, drives the Achaeans toward the Hellespont, slaughters many, and is killed by Achilles; winds carry his body to the grove beside Aesopus.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Memnon is the son of Aurora and Tithonus and an Ethiopian prince and king who comes to Troy to aid Priam’s warriors, kills Antilochus, fights Achilles, and is killed by him; Aurora mourns as Jupiter transforms his pyre’s sparks and cinders into annually fighting birds.

Memnon in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Memnon, a bearded king of the Ethiopians, came to Ilium from Susa in Persia and the river Choaspes. In a painting he sits beside Hector near Sarpedon, touching Sarpedon’s shoulder, cloaked in Memnonides birds that annually tend his tomb near the Hellespont.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXI.

Memnon is seated on a stone beside Hector and near Sarpedon, with one hand on Sarpedon’s shoulder. Birds called Memnonides are painted on his cloak; according to local belief, they annually visit his tomb near the Hellespont and clear and sprinkle the area around it. Memnon was king of the Ethiopians, but the text says he came to Ilium from Susa in Persia and the river Choaspes.

Memnon in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

Memnon is the Dawn’s son, an Aethiopian warrior-king and Troy’s newly summoned champion. Revered by Priam for his power and respectful of Nestor’s age, he arrives with a countless host, leads battle against the Argives, kills Antilochus, takes his armour, drives the Achaeans toward the Hellespont, and slaughters many. He then fights Achilles with great strength, but Achilles pierces and kills him; winds carry his body to the grove beside Aesopus.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

The Fall of Troy

Memnon is identified as the son of the Dawn. Book II concerns how he fell in battle for Troy.

BOOK II

Memnon, son of the Dawn-goddess and warrior-king, arrives at Troy with a countless host of Aethiopians. He leads the battle against the Argives, kills Antilochus, drives the Achaeans toward the Hellespont, and slaughters many of them.

Then to the side of Aeacus' mighty son

Memnon has slain Antilochus and taken the armour of the dead. He fights Achilles with great strength, but Achilles eventually pierces him with a sword and kills him; his body is then carried by the winds to the grove beside Aesopus.

Memnon in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Memnon, son of Aurora and Tithonus and Ethiopian prince and king, comes to Troy to aid Priam's warriors, kills Antilochus, fights Achilles, and is killed by him; Aurora mourns as Jupiter transforms his pyre's sparks and cinders into annually fighting birds.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XIX

Memnon was the son of Aurora and Tithonus and the king of the Ethiopians. He came to Troy to aid his father’s kindred, killed Antilochus, and fought Achilles before Achilles killed him. Aurora mourned him, and Jupiter changed the sparks and cinders of his funeral-pyre into birds that reenacted a yearly fight.
