Prometheus’s punishment in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

Prometheus’s punishment is an age-long ordeal imposed by Zeus for giving fire to men. Bound and immured in a Caucasus-like rocky ravine, he endures exposure, solitude, helplessness, anguish, and countless woes. He remains in fetters, believing art cannot overcome destiny and that release depends on Fate, the Furies, and Zeus’s will. The ordeal begins with his bondage on the rock; Hermes then foretells that collapsing rocks will bury him and that Zeus’s eagle will repeatedly tear and consume his flesh. It lasts without a stated natural limit, ending only when Zeus allows it or another deity bears the doom.

PROMETHEUS BOUND

Prometheus’s punishment is an unending ordeal of pain, exposure, solitude, and helplessness on a mountain. He is condemned to endure it until Zeus himself decides that it may end.

[_Exit OCEANUS._]

Prometheus remains in fetters and expects age-long pains and countless woes before his shackles fall. He says that art cannot overcome destiny and that his release depends on the powers of Fate and the unforgiving Furies.

Enter IO, horned like a cow.

Prometheus’s punishment is his prolonged bondage on the rock for giving fire to humanity. He says that he cannot win the release of death and that his suffering will have no limit until Zeus is overthrown.

[_Exit IO._]

Hermes foretells that Zeus will first bury Prometheus in the rocks, then expose him to a ravenous eagle that will tear and consume his flesh day after day. The punishment will not end until another deity willingly enters Hades and bears the doom for him; in this unit, Prometheus is buried by the collapsing rocks.

binding of Prometheus in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

Prometheus is bound by Hephaestus with permanent adamantine restraints and a wedge through his body, while Strength oversees and demands merciless, unwavering completion.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

PROMETHEUS BOUND

The binding of Prometheus is carried out by Hephaestus with adamantine armlets, chains, shackles, and a wedge driven through his body. Strength supervises the work and insists that it be done without pity or hesitation.

the punishment of Prometheus in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Zeus binds Prometheus with inextricable bonds and drives a shaft through his middle. A long-winged eagle eats his immortal liver each day, though it grows again at night, until Heracles kills the eagle and releases him with Zeus's consent.
