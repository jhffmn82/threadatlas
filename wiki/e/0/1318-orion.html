Orion

person · 4 works · 40 quoted facts · 12 connections
Also called: strong Orion, the blinded hero, ᾿Ωρίων, ᾿Ωρίωνος

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Orion is a strong hero and hunter who is blinded, healed, killed by Earth’s scorpion, and placed among the stars by Zeus. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Orion is the greatest and strongest of heroes, a hunter and builder whose deeds earn him representation among the stars. In The Library, Orion is a gigantic, earth-born figure who crosses the sea, is blinded and restored to sight, and is ultimately killed by Artemis. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Orion is a handsome giant and mighty hunter who is blinded, regains his sight, is unintentionally killed by Diana, and becomes a constellation.

Orion in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · about 699 BC · the work’s page · read it where Orion first appears

Abstract written by a model

Orion, son of Euryale and Poseidon, had rude strength and could walk on waves. He outraged Merope at Chios while drunk, was blinded by Oenopion, guided east by Cedalion, and healed by Sun. He piled up Peloris, founded the close of Poseidon, and settled in Euboea. Hunting with Artemis and Leto in Crete, he was killed by Earth’s scorpion, died from its sting, and Zeus placed him among the stars.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE ASTRONOMY

Orion was the son of Euryale, daughter of Minos, and Poseidon, and could walk on the waves as on land. After outraging Merope, being blinded by Oenopion, and being healed in the east, he hunted with Artemis and Leto in Crete, was killed by a scorpion sent by Earth, and was placed among the stars by Zeus; Hesiod also says that he settled in Euboea and was remembered among the stars.

Orion in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Orion first appears

Abstract written by a model

Orion is the biggest and strongest of all heroes, much given to hunting. Seeking vain glory, he performs great actions: he builds Acte for Zanclus, raises Pelorus in the open sea, and builds a temple to Neptune there. He then sails to and resides in Euboea, and is represented by Orion constellation among the stars for his actions.

Orion in The Library

Apollodorus · about AD 70 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Orion first appears

Abstract written by a model

Orion is a gigantic, earth-born figure, though Pherecydes calls him son of Poseidon and Euryale. He marries Side, crosses the sea by Poseidon's gift, is blinded by Oenopion, regains sight through the sun, is carried to Delos by Dawn, and is killed by Artemis.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

IV. Τῶν δὲ Κοίου θυγατέρων ᾿Αστερία μὲν

Orion was described as gigantic and earth-born, though Pherecydes said he was the son of Poseidon and Euryale. Poseidon gave him the power to stride across the sea; Oenopion blinded him, but he regained his sight through the sun's rays. Orion was killed by Artemis according to differing accounts, either for challenging her to a contest at quoits or for offering violence to Opis.

Orion in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Orion first appears

Abstract written by a model

Orion is a handsome giant, son of Neptune and a mighty hunter, favored by Diana. Able to cross the sea, he loves Merope and seeks marriage but, after attempting to seize her, is blinded by her father. Guided by Kedalion, he regains sight from the sun-god, then Diana unintentionally kills him. He becomes a starry giant near Sirius and the Pleiads.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XIX

Orion was the handsome giant son of Neptune and a mighty hunter who could wade through or walk on the sea. After attempting to seize Merope, he was blinded by her father, but Vulcan gave him Kedalion as a guide, and the sun-god restored his sight. Diana later killed him unintentionally, and he was placed among the stars with his dog Sirius and the Pleiads nearby.
