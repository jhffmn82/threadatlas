Abstract written by a model

In The Odyssey, marriage is Nausicaa’s anticipated future, prompted by Minerva’s advice to prepare clothes; though she avoids naming it to Alcinous, Ulysses praises harmonious spouses as creating a peaceful home. In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, marriage is described through distinct terms for husband and wife; it frames Medea’s abandonment by Jason, his alliance with princes, the children’s wedding gifts, and mothers carrying torches at weddings. In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, marriage, tied to Phædra’s union with Theseus after her journey from Crete to Athens, brings bridal and household obligations but turns bitter through love for another man. Phædra condemns marital pollution and fears discovery and dishonor, while the Nurse urges concealment. In Medea, marriage is shown as a structure in which the new bride becomes more valued and Medea is displaced and hunted away. The Chorus argues that women may become powerful figures in story, while men may become fearful and low, reversing familiar accounts of gender. In Heroides and Amores, marriage is promised to Phyllis as a lasting bond and assurance with Demophoon, but she regrets it and fears he has another wife. In An Astronomer’s Wife, marriage is presented as potentially happy even when the partners belong to different religious traditions or when one partner belongs to no religious sect. The unit says that shared moral purpose, mutual encouragement, and willingness to work may matter more than denominational agreement. (An Astronomer's Wife)

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

PHÆDRA, NURSE, CHORUS.

Phædra condemns the pollution of the marriage-bed with other men and fears being discovered to have injured Theseus. The Nurse treats concealment of marital wrongdoing as a common practice among mortals.

CHORUS, PHÆDRA.

Marriage is associated with Venus, bridal chambers, dowries, household burdens, and Phædra's fear of dishonoring her family. The Chorus recalls Phædra's journey from Crete to Athens for her marriage to Theseus.
