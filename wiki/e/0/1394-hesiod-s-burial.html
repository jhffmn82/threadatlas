INTRODUCTION

Hesiod was traditionally buried at Oenoe after his body was recovered from the sea, although Plutarch placed his burial at Ascra. His bones were later said to have been removed to Orchomenus, and the tradition that he was murdered and buried at Oenoe was regarded as at least as old as the time of Thucydides.
