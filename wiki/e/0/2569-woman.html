woman

person · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: female, mulier
  • In The Amores, or Amours, the woman is portrayed as selling sexual pleasure, gaining property through embraces, and prostituting beauty for profit; gifts from wealthy men are permitted, but bargaining payment for favors is condemned.
  • In Heroides and Amores, the woman is criticized for charging for mutual sexual pleasure, hiring out her nights, and profiting from what both partners desire, though she may properly request gifts from a wealthy person and a poor lover should give whatever he has, including service, devotion, faithfulness, and poetry.

woman in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

A woman is portrayed as selling sexual pleasure, gaining property through embraces, and prostituting beauty for profit; gifts from wealthy men are permitted, but bargaining payment for favors is condemned.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ELEGY X.

The text says that woman alone among the animals takes pleasure in spoils taken from man, lets out her nights, and sells sexual pleasure for a price. It also says that a woman may appropriately receive a gift from a wealthy man, but should not bargain for payment for her favors.
