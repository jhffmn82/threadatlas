Penelope's deception of the suitors

Abstract written by a model

In The Odyssey, Penelope's deception of the suitors delays her remarriage through her ruse of weaving Laertes's burial cloth by day and secretly unpicking it at night until a maid exposes it. In The Library, Penelope's weaving ruse deceives the suitors for three years by having her weave Laertes's shroud by day and undo it by night, until they discover what she is doing.

Penelope's deception of the suitors in The Odyssey

Homer · about 632 to 599 BC · the work’s page · read it where Penelope's deception of the suitors first appears

Abstract written by a model

Penelope's deception of the suitors delays her remarriage: she says she must finish Laertes's burial cloth, weaving the pall by day and secretly unpicking it by torchlight at night. The ruse lasts three years until one of her maids exposes it.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XIX

Penelope told the suitors that she would not remarry until she had completed Laertes's burial cloth. She deceived them by weaving during the day and unpicking the stitches by torchlight at night for three years.

BOOK XXIV

Penelope’s weaving ruse consists of making a funeral pall for Laertes by day and undoing its stitches by night. It delays her remarriage for three years, until one of her maids exposes what she is doing.
