Parliament in The Diary of Samuel Pepys

Samuel Pepys · 1660/1669

Parliament begins in disorder, with a delayed expected adjournment and a dispute between its Houses over searches, pamphlets, and seditious books. It faces City of London pressure over the Church settlement, while young men oppose serious men’s measures, raising fears of spoiled affairs and renewed war.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Parliament is in disorder and is expected to be adjourned for several months, though the adjournment is delayed. The two Houses dispute the Lords’ privilege not to have their houses searched, preventing agreement on the Commons’ bill concerning pamphlets and seditious books.

opening

Roger Pepys reports that young men in Parliament have opposed measures advanced by serious men. He considers their profane conduct a danger that could spoil affairs and bring the country into war again.
