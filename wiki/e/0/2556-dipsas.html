Dipsas in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · about 24 to 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where Dipsas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Dipsas, whose thirst accords with her name, is an old bawd and practitioner of magic. Never sober in relation to Aurora, she knows Aeaean incantations and magic that can control clouds and day and summon dead ancestors. As a profaner of chaste union, she advises a young woman as a procurer, urging wealthy lovers, deception, rivalry, feigned feeling, and demands for gifts; the narrator overhears her.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

VIII

Dipsas is an old bawd whose name reflects her thirst, and she is also portrayed as a practitioner of magic. She knows incantations, herbs, magical devices, and poisons, can summon the dead, alter the weather, change form, and fly at night. She counsels the young woman to pursue wealthy lovers through deception, rivalry, feigned emotion, and demands for gifts, and the narrator overhears her.

Dipsas in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where Dipsas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Dipsas is an old, often drunk procuress skilled in magic and Ææa’s charms. She advises a damsel in courtesan’s arts: attract wealthy lovers, deceive and provoke them, feign affection, demand gifts, and extract money. The speaker overhears and curses her.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

ELEGY VII.

Dipsas is described as an old hag skilled in magic, incantations, and other occult practices. She advises the damsel to attract wealthy lovers, deceive them, provoke jealousy, pretend affection, demand gifts, and use servants and relatives to extract money. The speaker overhears her and curses her.
