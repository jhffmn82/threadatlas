Augustus Le Plongeon · 1881 · the work’s page · read it where Maya civilization first appears

Abstract written by a model

Maya civilization is an ancient civilization associated with the earlier inhabitants of Yucatan. It reached a high degree of development through cities, extensive settlements, monuments, artificial mounds, reservoirs, Katun columns, architecture, art, sciences, language, writing, mathematics, and astronomy. The account places some isolated Katun columns at least 6,000 years ago. Its ultimate achievements remain uncertain because books are hidden or unavailable and surviving evidence has been damaged or lost through time, deterioration, and conflict.

Mayas in Vestiges of the Mayas, or, Facts tending to prove that Communications and Intimate Relations must have existed, in very remote times, between the inhabitants of Mayab and those of Asia and Africa

Augustus Le Plongeon · 1881 · the work’s page · read it where Mayas first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Mayas are presented as an autochthonous people of MAYAB and Yucatan, ancient, great, powerful, and highly civilized. They are credited with cities, roads, causeways, architecturally beautiful monuments, sacred edifices, sculpture, painting, mathematics, astronomy, grammar, and writing that used phonetic, symbolic, and ideographic signs. Their Maya language remained vernacular among many inhabitants of Yucatan despite invasions, wars, and attempts to suppress their traditions. Their religious and social life is described as polytheistic and centered in part on sun worship, symbolic blue, sacrifice, divination, prophecy, astrology, and the posthumous worship of kings and heroes. They believed in the soul's immortality, judgment, punishment or reward, and return to material life. Funerary rites included cremation, statues of the dead, an empty vase signifying a righteous judgment, and tomb deposits of books, weapons, or tools; houses could be abandoned after a death. Other reported practices include circumcision, a gesture of reverence, the sistrum and war drum in rites, and the use of vegetable colors, ochres, and cinnabar. The records repeatedly compare Maya monuments, writing, rites, customs, and scientific attainments with those attributed to Egypt, Chaldea, Hindostan, Nubia, Equatorial Africa, and Asia Minor. They report claims that Maya descendants preserved ceremonies in Central America and that Maya tribes emigrated beyond MAYAB, established colonies, and influenced distant peoples. Yet the concluding account rejects Egypt, Asia, and Africa as the source of Maya civilization: it portrays the Mayas as originating in the western continent and carrying some of their customs abroad. Their surviving language, descendants, ceremonies, and monuments are presented as incomplete but enduring evidence of that past.
