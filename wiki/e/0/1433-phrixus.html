Phrixus

person · 3 works · 24 quoted facts · 10 connections
Also called: Aeolid Phrixus, Minyan son of Athamas, Phrixus the Minyan, father Phrixus, he, noble Phrixus, that one Phrixus an Aeolid, the Aeolid Phrixus, Φρίξον, Φρίξος, Φρίξου

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Phrixus travels with Helle on Nephele’s immortal golden-fleeced ram, is pursued by Harpy, reaches Aeetes holding the fleece, and is received because of it. In The Argonautica, Phrixus is an Aeolid son of Athamas who escapes a planned sacrifice on a golden ram, reaches Aeetes in Aea, sacrifices the ram to Zeus, marries Chalciope, has sons, and dies in Aeetes’s home after directing his sons to seek Athamas’s possessions. In The Library, Phrixus is rescued by Nephele from sacrifice to Zeus, crosses to the Colchians on a golden-fleeced ram, marries Chalciope, has four sons, sacrifices the ram to Zeus, and gives its fleece to Aeetes.

Phrixus in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · 299 to 200 BC · the work’s page · read it where Phrixus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Phrixus is an Aeolid, son of Athamas, who fled Hellas on a golden ram after escaping his stepmother’s wiles and a sacrifice prepared by his father. Zeus sent Hermes to guide him to a friendly host, and Aeetes received and honored him in Aea, valuing his exceptional gentleness and fear of the gods. At the Ram’s couch, by Hermes’ command, Phrixus sacrificed the golden wonder and established an altar to Zeus the Alder of Fugitives. Aeetes arranged his marriage to Chalciope, and they had sons. Phrixus grew old and died in Aeetes’ home, instructing his sons to seek Athamas’s possessions in Orchomenus. His escape, the intended sacrifice, and the golden fleece remain central to Jason’s voyage and Zeus’s wrath against the sons of Aeolus.

Phrixus in The Library

Apollodorus · about AD 70 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Phrixus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Phrixus, son of Athamas and Nephele and brother of Helle, is brought to an altar for sacrifice to Zeus. Nephele rescues him; he crosses land and sea by a golden-fleeced ram to the Colchians, marries Chalciope, has four sons, sacrifices the ram to Zeus, and gives its fleece to Aeetes.
