Abstract written by a model

Multilingual keyphrase generation is a new, under-explored task setting that predicts keyphrases in the source language of an input text. It covers German, Spanish, Italian, French, Korean, and Chinese, and is constrained by limited non-English text, domain-specific annotations, and datasets. The study introduces two datasets and evaluates supervised methods, joint multilingual training, and a retrieval-augmented framework across six languages and two domains. The framework retrieves English keyphrases as external knowledge and can tolerate retrieval noise; in a German case study, it produces mostly correct German keyphrases despite noisy retrieved English keyphrases. The work concludes by establishing and investigating this novel setting.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Abstract

Multilingual keyphrase generation is introduced as a new setting for generating keyphrases across languages. The paper studies it with two new datasets and a retrieval-augmented method.

Introduction

Multilingual keyphrase generation is presented as an important but under-explored problem, partly because search engines serve users in different languages. The unit identifies limited datasets and scarce non-English resources as major challenges and evaluates a retrieval-based approach on six languages across two domains.

Task Definition

The multilingual keyphrase generation task predicts the keyphrases for a text in its source language. It covers German, Spanish, Italian, French, Korean, and Chinese.

Model .. 4.3

Multilingual keyphrase generation is described as a resource-scarce task, especially because non-English text and domain-specific keyphrase annotations are limited. The proposed framework uses retrieved English keyphrases as external knowledge when generating keyphrases in another language.

Results and Analyses / Main Results .. Case Study

Multilingual keyphrase generation benefits from supervised approaches, joint multilingual training, and retrieval augmentation. In the German case study, the model uses retrieved English keyphrases to produce mostly correct German keyphrases despite some noisy retrievals.

Conclusion

The study investigates multilingual keyphrase generation as a novel task setting.
