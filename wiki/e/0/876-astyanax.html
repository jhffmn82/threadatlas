  • In The Trojan Women of Euripides, Astyanax is Hector’s son and prince of Troy who is hurled from Ilion’s tower by the Greeks and buried on Hector’s shield.
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Astyanax is Hector and Andromache’s son who is condemned by the Greeks, thrown from Troy’s walls, killed, and buried in Hector’s shield.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Astyanax is Hector’s son who is killed during the sack of Ilium by being hurled from a tower or city wall by Neoptolemus or Odysseus.
  • In The Iliad, Astyanax is Hector’s infant son, cherished and well provided for, whose mother fears his loss and future hardship after Hector’s death.
  • In The Iliad, Hector’s child is Hector and Andromache’s beloved infant son, named Scamandrius by Hector and Astyanax by the people, for whom Hector prays to become a renowned ruler.
  • In The Library, Astyanax is killed after Troy’s capture, thrown from the battlements or a high tower, possibly by Ulysses or Neoptolemus.
  • In The Fall of Troy, Astyanax is Hector’s infant son who is torn from his mother and thrown from a tower by the Danaans, who kill him out of hatred for Hector’s seed.

Astyanax in The Trojan Women of Euripides

Euripides · -0414

Astyanax is Hector’s son, a child and prince of Troy. Carried by Andromache, he is condemned by the Greek army because they fear he may restore Troy. A soldier takes him to Troy’s wall, where Greeks hurl him from Ilion’s tower. Dead before knowing life and love, he is mourned by Hecuba, wrapped in garments, and buried on Hector’s shield, with a grave.

Astyanax in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Astyanax is the child and son of Hector and Andromache, grandson of Hecuba, and the only one of Andromache’s loved ones remaining in Troy. After Troy is captured, the Greeks sentence him to death because he is Hector’s son, fearing he might grow up and rebuild Troy. Andromache gives him a final embrace and kiss before he is taken away. He is thrown from Troy’s towers or walls, killed, and his disfigured body is brought to Hecuba. He is buried in Hector’s shield, with a tomb prepared after his death.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHOR. Attendants of the aged Hecuba,

Astyanax, the son of Hector and Andromache, is the only one of Andromache’s loved ones remaining in Troy. The Greeks decree that he must be thrown from the towers because they will not allow the son of Hector to grow up. Andromache gives him a final embrace and kiss before he is taken away.

TALTHYBIUS, HECUBA, CHORUS.

Astyanax has been thrown from the walls of Ilion and killed by the Greeks because they feared that he might someday rebuild Troy. His disfigured body is brought to Hecuba for burial, and he is interred in Hector’s shield.

ANDROMACHE.

Astyanax was the son Andromache bore to Hector. After Troy was captured, the enemies threw him headlong from the city’s towers.
