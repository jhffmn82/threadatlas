Also called: Argus the All-seeing, Argus’ eyes, Earth-born, He, The keeper appointed by Juno, great and strong Argus, herdsman, herdsman of Io, myriad-eyed, one myriad-eyed, the All-seeing, the child of Earth, the son of Aristor, the starry Argus

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Argus watches over Io for Hera with four ever-wakeful eyes until Hermes slays him. In Four Plays of Aeschylus, Argus is Hera’s earth-born, many-eyed herdsman who watches Io until Hermes kills him. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Argus guards Io for Juno until Mercury lulls and kills him, after which Juno places his eyes in the peacock’s tail. In The Library, Argus the All-seeing guards Io, Hera’s white cow, after many heroic killings, until Hermes kills him. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Argus guards Io with his hundred eyes until Mercury lulls and kills him, and Juno places his eyes in the peacock’s tail.

Argus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Argus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Argus is a watchman, Io’s uncle, and son of Aristor or Arestor, appointed by Juno to guard Io. Described as highly vigilant and wise, he has a hundred eyes, traditionally representing his wisdom and penetration; while some sleep, others watch. He keeps Io under the greatest care from a lofty mountain, watching on all sides. He is also said to have been the fourth king of Argos after Inachus. Mercury approaches Argus with music and conversation and lulls all his eyes asleep. One account says Hierax wakes him before Mercury kills him with a stone; another says Mercury wounds, beheads, and casts his body from the rock. Argus dies, his eyes’ light is extinguished, and Juno sets the hundred eyes in a peacock’s tail.

Argus the All-seeing in The Library

Apollodorus · about AD 70 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Argus the All-seeing first appears

Abstract written by a model

Argus the All-seeing, descended from Ecbasus through Agenor and also described as Arestor’s son or earthborn, had eyes throughout his body. He killed threats including a bull ravaging Arcadia, a satyr, Tartarus, Echidna, and Apis’s killers, wearing the bull’s hide. Hera made him guardian of Io, a white cow; after tethering her near Mycenae, he was killed by Hermes.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK ITI

Argus the All-seeing was descended from Ecbasus through Agenor. He had eyes throughout his body and killed a bull ravaging Arcadia, a satyr harming the Arcadians, and Tartarus and Echidna while Echidna was asleep; he also avenged the killing of Apis by killing those responsible.

THE LIBRARY, II. 1. 1-3

Hera appointed Argus the All-seeing to guard Io after Zeus transformed her into a white cow. Argus tethered Io to an olive tree in the grove of the Mycenaeans, but Hermes killed him with a stone after Hierax revealed Hermes’s attempt to steal the cow; this is why Hermes was called Argiphontes.

Argus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Argus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Argus, guardian of Io with a hundred eyes, represents the starry sky and builds Argo. He constantly watches Io, but Mercury, disguised as a shepherd, lulls him asleep and kills him; Juno ornaments her peacock’s tail with his eyes.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter III

Argus has a hundred eyes and keeps constant watch over Io, sleeping with no more than two eyes at a time. Mercury disguises himself as a shepherd, lulls Argus to sleep with stories and music, and cuts through his neck. Juno places Argus's eyes as ornaments on her peacock's tail.
