  • In The Aeneid, Diomede is the Greek king who fights Troy, steals the Palladium with Ulysses, rules Argyripa, and refuses Venulus’s request for an alliance against the Trojans.
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Diomede is a Greek warrior and son of Tydeus who joins Ulysses in the Trojan camp, kills Thracian soldiers, and escapes after Rhesus is slain.
  • In The Rhesus of Euripides, Diomedes is a Greek chieftain who joins Odysseus in a night mission, kills Rhesus and sleeping Thracians, and escapes after Athena prevents him from killing Paris.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Diomedes brings Philoctetes from Lemnos after Helenus’s prophecy and helps Odysseus carry the Palladium from Troy.
  • In The Iliad, Diomed is an Achaean warrior, chief, and commander who fights fiercely at Troy, performs notable deeds under Minerva’s protection, leads the night reconnaissance with Ulysses, and supports the Achaeans in battle and contests.
  • In The Library, Diomedes is a Greek leader at Troy who wounds Aphrodite, exchanges arms with Glaucus, kills Dolon and Rhesus with Ulysses, helps bring the Palladium from Troy, and sails home with Nestor and Menelaus.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Diomedes is a Grecian prince and Ulysses’ companion who captures Helenus, kills Rhesus and Dolon, rescues Nestor, marries Daunus’s daughter, founds Argos Hippium, and refuses Turnus aid.
  • In The Fall of Troy, Diomedes is Tydeus’s Argive and Achaean warrior-son who urges the Argives to fight, competes in the funeral games, travels with Odysseus to summon Achilles’ son, and brings Philoctetes from Lemnos to the Achaean host.
  • In The Fall of Troy, Tydeides rebukes Menelaus’s proposal to flee, calls the Achaeans to prepare for battle, accompanies Odysseus to Scyros, and fights against Eurypylus and the Trojans.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Diomedes is a Greek warrior second only to Achilles who brings Philoctetes back for his arrows and enters Troy with Ulysses to steal the Palladium.

Diomed in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Diomed is an Achaean warrior, chief, and commander of eighty ships from Argos, Tiryns, Hermione, Asine, Troezene, Eionae, Epidaurus, Aegina, and Mases. Son of Tydeus, he is renowned for fierce fighting, though Agamemnon compares him unfavourably with his father; Diomed accepts the rebuke, restrains Sthenelus, and enters battle. Dear to Jove and Minerva according to Nestor, he fights with exceptional fury, panics the Trojans, and continues despite wounds. With Minerva’s protection he wounds Venus and Mars, while Apollo warns him away from Aeneas. He kills Axylus, Calesius, Agelaus, Dolon, Rhesus, twelve Thracian soldiers, Thymbraeus, and others in the Trojan fighting. His conduct also shows restraint and loyalty: when he learns that Glaucus is linked to his father’s house, he refuses to fight him and exchanges armor; when Nestor is endangered, he takes him into his chariot. During the night reconnaissance he volunteers, chooses Ulysses, takes sword, shield, and helmet, prays to Pallas Minerva, captures Dolon, and returns with Ulysses after Minerva’s warning. He attacks Hector repeatedly but retreats when Jove frightens his horses and later withdraws after Alexandrus wounds his foot. Even wounded, he counsels the Achaean leaders to return and encourage the fighters. After battle, he wins the chariot race with Trojan horses taken from Aeneas, supports Euryalus in boxing, and earns a sword and equipment after threatening Ajax in the armor contest.

Diomedes in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

Diomedes, Tydeus’ battle-eager, stalwart son, is an Argive and Achaean warrior and hero. After Achilles’ death, he urges the Argives to fight but waits for Thetis and the funeral games. He defeats Aias son of Telamon in their first wrestling fall, loses the second, and receives two of Thetis’ handmaids when Nestor ends the contest. He travels with Odysseus to Scyros to summon Achilles’ son, then fights with Odysseus and other defenders. Finally, guided by Calchas’ counsel, he and Odysseus bring Philoctetes from Lemnos back to the Achaean host.

Greek warrior second only to Achilles, Diomedes brings Philoctetes back because Troy needs Hercules's arrows, then enters Troy disguised with Ulysses and steals the Palladium.
