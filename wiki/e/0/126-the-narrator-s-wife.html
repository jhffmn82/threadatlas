Abstract written by a model

The narrator’s wife, Elizabeth Pepys, is Samuel Pepys’s spouse, active in household, family, and social life. She appears first dressed in a black silk gown laced with black gimp lace, looking very pretty as she walks with him to the Wardrobe and stays with my Lady. Her sore belly becomes serious enough for Dr. Williams to attend her. She travels with Pepys to Whitehall, helps with visits, reports the theft of their silver tankard, and gives him his linen for safekeeping. She visits and travels with relatives and acquaintances, including a Walthamstow visit, trips to Uncle Fenner’s and Aunt Wight’s, the Opera, and the Wardrobe; she also goes with Mr. Hater during his wife’s labour and is expected back from Brampton. Lady Batten and her daughter treat her coldly. Most urgently, she tearfully pleads for help for her brother Balty.

opening

Elizabeth Pepys was not well, so Pepys went with Sir W. Batten to dinner instead of remaining at home. Dr. Williams came to see her, as her sore belly had grown dangerous in her opinion.

Samuel Pepys’s wife accompanies him by water to Whitehall and assists with household and family visits. She tells him that their silver tankard has been stolen, later gives him his linen to keep in his own custody, and visits Sir William Pen’s daughter.

Pepys's wife goes with Mr. Hater when he comes to fetch her because his wife is falling into labour. She is also expected to return from Brampton in about three weeks, possibly traveling with Pepys's father.

opening

The narrator’s wife accompanies him to his uncle Fenner’s house and to his aunt Wight’s house, where they supped on Westphalia ham. She meets the narrator by appointment at W. Joyce’s, goes with him to the Opera to see "The Witts," and then visits his Lady at the Wardrobe.

opening

The diarist's wife is treated coldly by Lady Batten and her daughter because she does not seek their acquaintance. She is distressed about her brother Balty's condition and urges the diarist to help him, telling his story with tears.
