Ascra

place · 2 works · 6 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: wretched hamlet

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Ascra was a small Boeotian settlement near Thespiae and Helicon, described as a wretched hamlet and possibly the birthplace of Hesiod and Perses. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Ascra was a settlement at Helicon’s foot, of which only a tower remained in the author’s time.

Ascra in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · about 699 BC · the work’s page · read it where Ascra first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ascra was a small Boeotian settlement near Thespiae and Helicon, miserable in winter and sultry in summer. Hesiod’s father settled there after leaving Cyme; it may have been Hesiod and Perses’s birthplace, before Thespians destroyed it.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

INTRODUCTION

Ascra was the Boeotian settlement near Thespiae where Hesiod’s father settled after leaving Cyme, and it may have been the place where Hesiod and Perses were born. The tradition connecting Hesiod with Ascra was reportedly never disputed, despite the place’s insignificance and later destruction by the Thespians.

Ascra in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Ascra first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ascra was a settlement at Helicon’s foot, built by figures named in differing accounts; by the author’s time, only a tower remained.

CHAPTER XXIX.

Ascra was built at the foot of Helicon by Œoclus and the sons of Aloeus, according to Hegesinous. In the author's time, only a tower remained at Ascra.
