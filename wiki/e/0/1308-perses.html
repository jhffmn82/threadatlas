Perses in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

INTRODUCTION

Perses was Hesiod’s brother, and the two divided their father’s farm after his death. Perses is represented as an idler and spendthrift who secured the larger share by bribing corrupt lords, wasted his inheritance, and eventually came to want; the text argues that he and the quarrel with Hesiod were probably historical rather than fictional.

HESIOD / HESIOD’S WORKS AND DAYS

Perses is urged to listen to right, reject violence, work diligently, and settle his inheritance dispute through true judgement. He is warned against idleness, dishonest gain, and taking another person's property.

(ll. 383-404) When the Pleiades, daughters of Atlas, are rising 1310,

Perses is urged to work according to the seasons, pay his debts, and avoid hunger. He is told to prepare a house, a woman, an ox, ploughs, slaves, tools, and provisions, and to carry out ploughing, sowing, harvesting, and storage at the proper times.

(ll. 618-640) But if desire for uncomfortable sea-faring seize you;

Perses is told to remember that all works have their season, especially sailing. He is advised about safe sailing times, limited cargo, marriage, friendship, ritual purity, social conduct, and the proper days for work.
