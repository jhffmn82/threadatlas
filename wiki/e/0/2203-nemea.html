Nemea in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

Nemea is a sacred games venue and deep-lying plain, marked by a far-famous grove and the games it hosts. It inspires triumphal song, beginning with Chromios’s victorious deeds, and is repeatedly invoked as the place of athletic crowns: Timodemos’s first victory, Aristokleides’ renown, Timasarchos’s wrestling, and Pytheas’s pankration. It concludes with Alkimidas returning from a boys’ wrestling victory, Praxidamas crowned there three times, and Kreontidas marked by parsley associated with the Nemean lion.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

Nemean Ode I

Nemea and Chromios’s car stir the speaker to compose a triumphal song for Chromios’s victorious deeds.

Nemean Ode II

Nemea is described as the famous grove of Nemeaian Zeus, where Timodemos gained his first foundation of victory in the sacred games.

Nemean Ode III

Nemea is the deep-lying plain where Aristokleides won his victory. Its games, together with those of Epidauros and Megara, provide the occasion for his great renown.

Nemean Ode IV

Nemea is invoked together with Zeus and Timasarchos's wrestling. The ode was sung for a victory won there.

Nemean Ode V

Nemea is where Pytheas won the pankratiast's crown. It is described as favorable to Pytheas.

Nemean Ode VI

Nemea is the site from which Alkimidas returns after winning the boys' wrestling-match. Praxidamas was crowned there three times, and parsley associated with the Nemean lion marked another victory by Kreontidas.

Nemea in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Nemea, 15 stades from Cleonæ, contains Nemean Zeus’s temple with fallen roof and missing statue, Opheltes’s walled tomb with altars, and Adrastea; Argives sacrifice to Zeus and hold winter armored running contests.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XII.

Nemea lies about 15 stades from Cleonæ and contains a temple and grove of Nemean Zeus, the tomb and precinct of Opheltes, and a fountain called Adrastea. The Argives sacrifice to Zeus there and hold a winter contest in running for men in armor.
