GAR

method · 1 work · 61 quoted facts · 17 connections
Also called: GAR +DPR, Generation- Augmented Retrieval, Generation- Augmented Retrieval (GAR), Generation-Augmented Retrieval, generation-augmented queries, generation-augmented query
  • In Generation-Augmented Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering: GAR is a generation-augmented retrieval method and query-expansion approach for open-domain question answering. It uses a pre-trained language model and standard seq2seq learning to generate relevant query contexts from a question, including an answer, an answer-containing sentence, and the title of a passage containing the answer. These contexts are treated as retrieval aids rather than necessarily final answers: each is appended to the original question to form generation-augmented queries, retrieval is run separately for diverse contexts, and the results are fused. GAR commonly uses BM25 and sparse representations for Wikipedia passage retrieval, while it can also use or be fused with dense representations such as DPR. Its central value is extracting factual knowledge and relevant contexts stored in PLMs without external resources as supervision, thereby extending sparse retrieval beyond the information in the original question. Generated contexts need only be pertinent for retrieval, not perfect answers. GAR improves BM25 retrieval accuracy on NQ and Trivia, can achieve comparable or better retrieval than DPR at many cutoffs, and sparse GAR is complementary to DPR. With an extractive reader, it achieves state-of-the-art end-to-end OpenQA results, including 41.8 extractive EM on NQ, 62.7 on open-domain Trivia, and 74.8 on hidden Wikipedia Trivia. It also consistently outperforms retrieval methods when the same generative reader is used and is competitive under the generative setup. The method is presented as efficient: training one generation target requires 3–6 hours on one GPU, Wikipedia index construction takes around 30 minutes with 35 CPUs, and it retrieves 1,000 passages for the NQ test set. From generation through augmentation, separate retrieval, and fusion, GAR improves retrieval and QA and can be extended through improved generation, fusion, joint optimization, and other text-matching tasks.

GAR in Generation-Augmented Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering

Yuning Mao, Pengcheng He, Xiaodong Liu, Yelong Shen, Jianfeng Gao, Jiawei Han, Weizhu Chen · 2021

GAR is a generation-augmented retrieval method and query-expansion approach for open-domain question answering. It uses a pre-trained language model and standard seq2seq learning to generate relevant query contexts from a question, including an answer, an answer-containing sentence, and the title of a passage containing the answer. These contexts are treated as retrieval aids rather than necessarily final answers: each is appended to the original question to form generation-augmented queries, retrieval is run separately for diverse contexts, and the results are fused. GAR commonly uses BM25 and sparse representations for Wikipedia passage retrieval, while it can also use or be fused with dense representations such as DPR. Its central value is extracting factual knowledge and relevant contexts stored in PLMs without external resources as supervision, thereby extending sparse retrieval beyond the information in the original question. Generated contexts need only be pertinent for retrieval, not perfect answers. GAR improves BM25 retrieval accuracy on NQ and Trivia, can achieve comparable or better retrieval than DPR at many cutoffs, and sparse GAR is complementary to DPR. With an extractive reader, it achieves state-of-the-art end-to-end OpenQA results, including 41.8 extractive EM on NQ, 62.7 on open-domain Trivia, and 74.8 on hidden Wikipedia Trivia. It also consistently outperforms retrieval methods when the same generative reader is used and is competitive under the generative setup. The method is presented as efficient: training one generation target requires 3–6 hours on one GPU, Wikipedia index construction takes around 30 minutes with 35 CPUs, and it retrieves 1,000 passages for the NQ test set. From generation through augmentation, separate retrieval, and fusion, GAR improves retrieval and QA and can be extended through improved generation, fusion, joint optimization, and other text-matching tasks.

Abstract

Generation-Augmented Retrieval (GAR) augments queries by generating heuristically discovered relevant contexts without external resources as supervision. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on Natural Questions and TriviaQA with an extractive reader and consistently outperforms other retrieval methods when the same generative reader is used.

Introduction

Generation-Augmented Retrieval augments questions with relevant contexts generated by a pre-trained language model rather than rewriting the original query. It generates answers, answer-containing sentences, or passage titles, appends them to the question, and improves retrieval and question-answering performance; combining its results with DPR provides further gains.

Related Work

GAR expands queries with relevant contexts learned through text generation from knowledge stored in PLMs rather than from retrieved passages. It uses freely accessible in-domain contexts such as passage titles as generation targets and standard seq2seq learning, making it more efficient and effective for OpenQA than the methods discussed. GAR extends sparse retrieval beyond question information and can also be used with dense representations.

Generation-Augmented Retrieval / Task Formulation .. Generative Reader

GAR generates query contexts and uses them to augment questions before retrieval. It generates answers, sentences containing answers, and titles of relevant passages, retrieves separately for different contexts, and fuses the results.

Experiment Setup / Datasets .. Implementation Details

GAR is evaluated as a query-expansion approach designed for open-domain question answering. It generates query contexts and uses them as query augmentation for retrieval, while its extractive reader uses 100 retrieved passages during training and 500 during inference.

Experiment Results .. Efﬁciency of GAR

GAR generates answer, sentence, and title query contexts to augment questions before retrieval. It improves BM25 retrieval and end-to-end QA, achieves comparable or better retrieval than DPR at many cutoffs, and is more computationally efficient than DPR.

Conclusion .. Future Extensions

Generation-Augmented Retrieval uses generated relevant contexts to expand queries rather than reformulating them. It achieves strong retrieval and OpenQA results, can combine sparse and dense representations, and may be extended through improved generation, fusion, joint optimization, and application to other text-matching tasks.
