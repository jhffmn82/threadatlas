the estate of Ulysses in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Ulysses's estate is a wealthy property with cattle, sheep, pigs, goats, wine, and other provisions. While Ulysses and then Telemachus are away, Penelope's suitors consume its resources without payment, depleting what it cannot sustain. Their threat escalates from wasteful feasting to a plan to kill Telemachus, divide the property, and take Penelope.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK II

Telemachus's estate is being depleted by the suitors, who consume its oxen, sheep, goats, wine, and other provisions without payment. Telemachus warns that the estate cannot withstand their recklessness and that they will continue eating it unless stopped.

BOOK XIV

Ulysses’s estate is described as exceptionally wealthy, including herds of cattle, flocks of sheep, droves of pigs, and large flocks of goats. The suitors waste this wealth by taking its animals, wine, and other provisions without restraint.
