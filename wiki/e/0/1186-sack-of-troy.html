Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, The Sack of Ilium is a two-book epic poem by Arctinus of Miletus describing the wooden horse, Laocoon, Sinon, the Greeks’ return from Tenedos, the capture, sack, and burning of Ilium, the division of the spoils, and the Greeks’ departure. In The Odyssey, the sack of Troy occurs in the war’s tenth year after nine years of effort, caused by Achaean warfare and stratagems; after the city is sacked, the Achaeans divide into groups and sail home, dispersed by heaven, beginning difficult homeward voyages. In The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies, the sack of Troy is announced by the beacon signal and celebrated with sacrifices throughout Argos; the Herald says Troy has been taken and razed, and Agamemnon describes its destruction as the gods’ judgment. In The Trojan Women of Euripides, the sack of Troy includes Priam’s murder at the altar, the destruction of the citadel, the killing of men in the streets and houses, and the capture of the women; the chorus remembers the city changing from celebration to bloodshed and desolation. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the capture and sack of Troy involves the destruction of Ilion, the death of Priam, the enslavement of the Dardanian matrons, and the burning of the city’s roofs and temples; Astyanax is thrown from the towers, and Hecuba is carried away after mourning the dead.

The Ionic School

The Sack of Troy, also called the Sack of Ilium and attributed to Arctinus, described the wooden horse, Laocoon, Sinon, the Greek return from Tenedos, the sack and burning of Troy, and the division of the spoils. Its account was described as similar to Vergil’s version in Aeneid II.

THE SACK OF ILIUM

The Sack of Ilium is described as a two-book work by Arctinus of Miletus. It recounts the capture, sack, and burning of Ilium, the division of spoils, and the Greeks' departure.

THE RETURNS
