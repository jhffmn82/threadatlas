  • In The Amores, or Amours, Andromache places Hector’s helmet on his head before he goes to battle.
  • In The Aeneid, Andromache is Hector’s widow and a Trojan queen at Epirus who lives with Helenus, mourns Hector and Astyanax, recognizes Aeneas, asks after Ascanius and his mother, and gives Ascanius gifts.
  • In The Trojan Women of Euripides, Andromache is Hector’s widowed wife and Astyanax’s mother, enslaved after Troy’s fall and taken by Pyrrhus as his captive and intended concubine; she pleads for her son’s burial rites.
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Andromache is a captive former queen of Troy, Hector’s widow and Astyanax’s mother who is taken to Greece, bears Neoptolemus a son, survives Hermione’s attempt to kill her, and is directed to marry Helenus.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Andromache is Hector’s well-girded wife who is taken by Neoptolemus as a prize after the Greeks sack Ilium.
  • In The Iliad, Andromache is Hector’s wife and the mother of his child who begs Hector not to leave, then mourns his death and fears widowhood, captivity, and the future of their child.
  • In The Library, Andromache is Eetion’s daughter who marries Hector, is awarded to Neoptolemus after Troy’s sack, and bears him Molossus.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Andromache is depicted among the Trojan women with a baby boy at her breast, who Lescheos says was later hurled from a tower because of Neoptolemus’s private hatred.
  • In The Fall of Troy, Andromache is Hector’s faithful wife and Astyanax’s mother who mourns their deaths after Troy falls and is dragged into Danaan slavery.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Andromache is Hector’s widow who mourns him, fears captivity and her son’s future after Troy’s ruin, and becomes Helenus’s wife.

Andromache in The Trojan Women of Euripides

Euripides · -0414

Andromache is a Trojan noblewoman, Hector’s devoted and widowed wife and Astyanax’s mother, renowned as a virtuous wife. After Troy’s fall, the Greeks enslave her; Pyrrhus, Achilles’ son, takes her as captive, prize, and intended concubine, driving her toward Thessaly. Grieving Hector, Polyxena, Troy, her lost life, and Astyanax, she pleads for her son as soldiers take him away, then asks that he receive burial rites rather than be displayed on Hector’s shield.

Andromache in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Andromache is a woman from Thebes, formerly queen of Troy, wife and later widow of Hector, and mother of Astyanax. After Troy is destroyed, she sees Hector slain and Astyanax thrown from the city’s towers. Taken among the Trojan spoils, she is carried to Greece as a captive and slave, grieving the loss of Troy and unable to bury Astyanax herself before leaving with Neoptolemus, who chooses her and forces her into his household and marriage. In Phthia she bears Neoptolemus a son, Molossus, while remaining marked by captivity and the death of her first husband and child. When Hermione accuses her of using magic drugs and, aided by Menelaus, seeks to kill her and Molossus, Andromache takes sanctuary at Thetis’ temple. She denies the accusation, stresses that she was forced into her master’s bed, and submits to death to save Molossus. Torn from the altar and led toward execution with her son, she appeals to Peleus, who frees and protects them, though she fears renewed violence. At the end, Thetis directs her to marry Helenus and live in Molossia; through her, a surviving royal line connected with Aeacus will continue.

CHOR. Attendants of the aged Hecuba,

Andromache is taken away by the Greeks with Astyanax and the spoils of Hector. She tells Hecuba that Polyxena was sacrificed at the tomb of Achilles and that her own fate as a captive forced into Neoptolemus’ household is worse. She grieves over Astyanax after Talthybius announces that he will be thrown from Troy’s towers.

TALTHYBIUS, HECUBA, CHORUS.

Andromache leaves the land with Neoptolemus, lamenting her country and addressing her plaints to Hector’s tomb. She entrusts Astyanax’s body to Hecuba because Neoptolemus’s haste prevents her from burying him herself.

ANDROMACHE.

Andromache recounts that she was Hector’s wife, saw Hector slain, and saw Astyanax thrown from Troy’s towers after the city was sacked. She now lives as a captive in Phthia, has borne a son to Neoptolemus, and takes refuge in Thetis’ temple because Hermione and Menelaus seek her life. She denies using drugs against Hermione and argues that virtue, patience, and good conduct win a husband’s love.

MENELAUS, MOLOSSUS, ANDROMACHE, CHORUS.

Andromache argues that Menelaus is committing a great crime for a small wrong and that she was forcibly taken to her master’s bed. To save Molossus, she leaves Thetis’s altar and submits to death, while condemning Menelaus, Hermione, and Sparta for treachery.

PELEUS, MENELAUS, ANDROMACHE, MOLOSSUS, CHORUS.

Andromache is dragged toward death with Molossus after being torn from the shrine of Thetis. She begs Peleus to rescue her and her innocent son, warning that Menelaus's followers may seize them again. Peleus frees her, and she praises him while fearing further attack.

THE NURSE OF HERMIONE, CHORUS.

Andromache is the captive wife of Hector whom Achilles’ son loves. Hermione attempted to kill her and her son, but Peleus protected them.

PELEUS, CHORUS.

Thetis directs Andromache to marry Helenus and live in Molossia. Through her, the remaining royal line connected with Aeacus will continue.

Andromache in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Andromache, daughter of Eetion and born and raised in Thebes, is Hector’s wife and the mother of his child. At the Scaean gates she meets Hector with their child, recalls Achilles’ killing of her father and brothers, and begs Hector not to leave, fearing widowhood and captivity if Troy falls. After Hector’s death, she first prepares his bath, then sees his body dragged away, faints, and mourns for herself and their child. She leads Hector’s lamentation, grieving her husband and fearing the future.

Andromache, Hector's widow and daughter of Eetion, silently condemns Penthesileia's boast that she will defeat Achilles. Remembering Hector's greater strength and death, she mourns her husband and the bitterness Achilles has brought into her life.

Andromache sees her husband Hector and her child Astyanax dead and longs to die. Despite her pleas to be killed, the Argives drag her into slavery.
