Leda

person · 3 works · 4 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Thestius’ daughter, her mother, my mother, Λήδαν, Λήδας
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Leda is Helen’s mother, reportedly bearing Helen from an egg after Zeus approached her as a swan, then dying by hanging, possibly connected to Helen’s supposed guilt.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Leda is the mother of Castor and Polydeuces, bearing them beneath Taygetus after Zeus privately bent her to his will and after lying with Zeus.
  • In The Library, Leda is the daughter of Thestius and wife of Tyndareus, bearing Pollux and Helen to Zeus and Castor and Clytaemnestra to Tyndareus.

Leda in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Leda, Helen’s mother, reportedly bore Helen from an egg after Zeus approached her as a swan, then died by hanging, possibly connected to Helen’s supposed guilt.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

HELEN.

Leda is Helen’s mother, whom the story says bore Helen from an egg after Zeus approached her in the form of a swan. Teucer reports that Leda died by hanging, and Helen believes that her mother’s death was connected to Helen’s supposed guilt.

Leda in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Leda, neat-ankled and stately, is mother of Castor and Polydeuces, bearing them beneath Taygetus after the dark-clouded Son of Cronos privately bent her to his will and after lying with Zeus.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

XVII. TO THE DIOSCURI

Leda is described as stately and as having borne Castor and Polydeuces beneath the heights of Taygetus. The dark-clouded Son of Cronos had privately bent her to his will.

XXXIII. TO THE DIOSCURI

Leda is described as the mother of Castor and Polydeuces. She bore them beneath the peak of Taygetus after lying with Zeus.

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Leda was the daughter of Thestius and the wife of Tyndareus. Zeus approached her in the form of a swan, and Tyndareus also cohabited with her on the same night. She bore Pollux and Helen to Zeus, and Castor and Clytaemnestra to Tyndareus.
