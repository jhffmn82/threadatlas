Hippodamia

person · 3 works · 5 quoted facts · 8 connections
Also called: Hippodameia, Hippodamia the daughter of Œnomaus, his bride, the maiden, Ἱπποδάμεια, Ἱπποδάμειαν
  • In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Hippodameia was Oinomaos’s daughter whom Pelops sought to marry and won as his bride after overcoming Oinomaos in the contest.
  • In The Library, Hippodamia was Oenomaus’s daughter who fell in love with Pelops and persuaded Myrtilus to help him win the marriage contest.
  • In Description of Greece, Hippodamia was the woman whom Pelops married after winning a race against her suitors’ killer Oinomaus; she inaugurated Hera’s games and was honored by annual rites at the Hippodamium.

Hippodamia in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Hippodamia was a woman whose suitors Oinomaus killed before Pelops won a race against him and married her; Pelops later buried the suitors together partly from affection for her. In gratitude for the marriage, she selected sixteen matrons and inaugurated Hera’s games, and they maintained Hippodamia’s dance. After Pelops grew angry over Chrysippus’s death, she fled to Midea; her remains were brought to Olympia, where women performed annual rites at the Hippodamium.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XV.

Hippodamia was said to have selected sixteen matrons and inaugurated the games for Hera in gratitude for her marriage with Pelops. The sixteen matrons also maintain a dance called Hippodamia’s.

CHAPTER XVII.

Hippodamia was honored in the Hippodamium, where women performed annual rites for her. She was said to have fled to Midea after Pelops became angry with her over the death of Chrysippus, and her remains were later placed at Olympia; a statue of her also stood near one of the goals of the course.

CHAPTER XXI.

Hippodamia was the woman whose suitors were killed by Oinomaus before Pelops won the race against him. Pelops' burial of the suitors together was presented partly as an act of affection for Hippodamia.
