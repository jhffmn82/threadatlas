In The Return of Sherlock Holmes, Cambridge contains stationers’ shops and Trinity College, is home to the university rugby team, and is where Holmes and Watson investigate Godfrey Staunton’s disappearance.

Cambridge in The Diary of Samuel Pepys

Samuel Pepys · 1660 to 1669 · the work’s page · read it where Cambridge first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cambridge is a place containing Christ College, King's College chapel, Magdalen, and the University of Cambridge. It is repeatedly a narrator's destination: after preparing to ride there, he arrives, visits colleges, the Rose tavern and university friends, attends Assizes at Castle Hill, dines and drinks, notices university tensions, then rides onward.

opening

Pepys reaches Cambridge after riding with a letter-carrier and attends the Assizes at Castle Hill. He dines at Trinity Hall, drinks with Mr. Pechell and others at the Rose, and observes tensions between older university doctors and better scholars whom they had found there.

Cambridge in The Return of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1903-09-26 · the work’s page · read it where Cambridge first appears

Abstract written by a model

It contains four stationer's shops of consequence and Trinity College, where Jeremy Dixon is located. It is home to the university rugby team and a destination in Godfrey Staunton's disappearance: Holmes and Watson stay there, search nearby villages, and Holmes follows Armstrong's carriage to a lonely cottage.
