Oechalia

place · 4 works · 5 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Oechalia's highland, Oechaliam, land of plenteous woe, lofty-towered Oechalia, Οἰχαλίαν

Abstract written by a model

In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Oechalia is sacked by Heracles for the sake of Iole. In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Oechalia is ruled by Eurytus and attacked and destroyed by Heracles because of his desire for Iole, becoming a land of plenteous woe. In Heroides and Amores, Oechalia is conquered and falls to Hercules; Deianira fears Iole’s proud display may make it seem to stand again. In The Library, Oechalia is attacked and captured by Hercules after he gathers an army to punish Eurytus; its city is pillaged, and Iole is taken captive.
