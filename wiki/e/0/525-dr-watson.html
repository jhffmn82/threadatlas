Abstract written by a model

In A Study in Scarlet, Dr. Watson meets Sherlock Holmes, assists in investigating the Lauriston Gardens murder, helps examine evidence, and records the case. In The Sign of the Four, Dr. Watson investigates the Sholto case with Sherlock Holmes, provides medical assistance, pursues the fugitives, and declares his love for Mary Morstan. In The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes, Dr. Watson investigates cases with Sherlock Holmes, provides medical aid, carries a revolver, and chronicles their adventures. In The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes, Dr. Watson accompanies Sherlock Holmes, provides medical assistance, records his cases, and helps evade Professor Moriarty. In The Hound of the Baskervilles, Dr. Watson acts as Sherlock Holmes’s agent in Devonshire, investigates the case, protects Sir Henry, and helps defeat the hound. In The Return of Sherlock Holmes, Dr. Watson is Sherlock Holmes’s friend and chronicler and recounts the meeting at Baker Street and the murder of Eduardo Lucas. In The Return of Sherlock Holmes, Dr. Watson narrates, investigates, assists Sherlock Holmes, provides medical expertise, and records their cases. In His Last Bow, Dr. Watson records and assists Sherlock Holmes’s investigations, independently traces Lady Frances Carfax, treats Holmes, and helps restrain Von Bork.

Doctor Watson in A Study in Scarlet

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1886 · the work’s page · read it where Doctor Watson first appears

Abstract written by a model

Doctor Watson is a doctor of medicine and army doctor who served as an Assistant Surgeon with the Fifth Northumberland Fusiliers and with the Berkshires during the Afghan campaign. Wounded in the shoulder by a Jezail bullet at Maiwand, rescued from Ghazis by Murray, and later afflicted with enteric fever at Peshawar, he returns to England with his health irretrievably ruined, weak and convalescent. With only eleven shillings and sixpence a day, no relatives in England, and a desire to avoid rows because of shaken nerves, he seeks economical rooms while staying at a private hotel in the Strand. Through young Stamford, a former dresser under him at Barts, Watson meets Sherlock Holmes in a chemical laboratory and agrees to share rooms at 221B Baker Street. He moves in, keeps his old service revolver and a bull pup, and becomes Holmes’s companion and narrator. Initially curious about Holmes’s uneven knowledge and doubtful of his deductions, Watson witnesses their accuracy and records them. During the Lauriston Gardens and Brixton Road investigation, he accompanies Holmes, observes Enoch J. Drebber’s body, reads Gregson’s letter, questions John Rance with Holmes, follows newspaper reports and police accounts, and anxiously awaits Holmes’s return from pursuing Mrs. Sawyer. He assists with examining pills and testing them with a dying terrier, helps restrain Jefferson Hope, examines Hope’s chest and recognizes an aortic aneurism, and verifies Hope’s statement through Lestrade’s notebook. At the end, Watson records the case in his journal and intends to make Holmes’s role public.

9 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER I.

Dr. Watson returns to England after serving as an army surgeon in Afghanistan, wounded at Maiwand and weakened by enteric fever. In London, he seeks cheaper lodgings and meets Sherlock Holmes through young Stamford, agreeing to inspect rooms with him in Baker Street.

CHAPTER II.

Dr. John Watson moves his belongings from the hotel into 221B, Baker Street, and begins living with Sherlock Holmes. He becomes increasingly curious about Holmes, records Holmes’s areas of knowledge and ignorance, initially dismisses Holmes’s theories of deduction, and witnesses Holmes’s accurate deductions.

CHAPTER III.

The Doctor accompanies Sherlock Holmes to 3, Lauriston Gardens and observes the investigation and the body of Enoch J. Drebber. He reads Tobias Gregson’s letter aloud and records Holmes’s deductions about the crime.

CHAPTER IV.

Doctor Watson accompanies Sherlock Holmes from No. 3, Lauriston Gardens to question John Rance. He remains puzzled by several aspects of the case but recognizes that Rance’s description matches Holmes’s idea of the second suspect.

CHAPTER V.

Dr. Watson rests at 221B Baker Street after the Brixton Road murder but is unable to sleep because of the case. He speaks with Sherlock Holmes, prepares his service revolver, receives Mrs. Sawyer, and later waits anxiously for Holmes to return from following her.

CHAPTER VI.

Doctor Watson reads the newspaper reports with Sherlock Holmes and hears Tobias Gregson’s account of the investigation. He confirms details about the hat found beside Enoch J. Drebber’s body.

CHAPTER VII.

Dr. John Watson listens to Lestrade’s account, assists Holmes by examining the pills, and fetches the dying terrier for Holmes’s test. He helps restrain Jefferson Hope after Hope tries to escape through the window.

CHAPTER VI.

John Watson, M.D., accompanies Sherlock Holmes, Gregson, Lestrade, and Jefferson Hope to the police-station. He examines Hope's chest, recognizes an aortic aneurism, and later records that Hope's statement was accurate by consulting Lestrade's note-book.

CHAPTER VII.

Doctor Watson discusses Hope’s death with Sherlock Holmes and listens as Holmes reconstructs the investigation. He records the facts in his journal and intends to make the public aware of Holmes’s role.

Abstract written by a model

Dr. John Watson is a medical doctor, army surgeon, half-pay surgeon, and Sherlock Holmes’s fellow lodger and investigative companion in Baker Street. Before these events, he served in the Afghan campaign, was wounded by a Jezail bullet, and suffered damage to his tendo Achillis, leaving him with a weak leg and limited finances. He is the author of A Study in Scarlet and, in this account, objects to Holmes’s cocaine use while admiring Holmes’s ability to infer a watch’s history. The record follows Watson through the investigation involving Miss Mary Morstan, the Sholtos, Jonathan Small, and the missing treasure. At Mary’s request, he joins her and Holmes, becomes strongly attracted to her, and accompanies them through a threatening expedition. He uses his medical skills to examine Thaddeus Sholto and later Bartholomew Sholto’s body, concluding that a powerful vegetable alkaloid caused Bartholomew’s death. He comforts Mary, escorts her home, obtains Toby for Holmes, and assists in following the trail of the fugitives. Watson continues as Holmes’s practical partner: he observes evidence, pursues leads, reports progress to Mary, represents Holmes while he is absent, receives his telegram, and helps manage Holmes’s disguise. Carrying a stick and later his old service-revolver, Watson joins Holmes and Athelney Jones in the launch pursuit of the Aurora and fires at the Islander, only afterward recognizing how narrowly a dart missed them. After Jonathan Small is captured, Watson delivers the iron treasure box to Mary, opens it, and finds it empty. The central personal change is Watson’s relationship with Mary. Her possible fortune initially makes him confront the selfishness in his feelings, but once the treasure proves empty, he openly declares his love. Mary accepts him as a prospective husband, and Watson believes this may end his role in Holmes’s investigations, despite defending his choice against Holmes’s dissatisfaction.

Abstract written by a model

Dr. Watson is a married medical practitioner and physician in civil practice in London, living near Paddington Station after having shared rooms with Sherlock Holmes at Baker Street. A veteran of the Afghan campaign who carries an army revolver and bears a jezail bullet in a limb, he is Holmes’s longtime friend, trusted companion, colleague, medical adviser, and chronicler. His marriage separates him socially from Holmes, but he repeatedly visits or travels with him, records his cases, and narrates their investigations. Across the records, Watson begins by observing Holmes’s deductions and agreeing to assist in the operation at Briony Lodge, where he follows instructions, throws a smoke-rocket, and despite misgivings about Irene Adler remains loyal to Holmes. He then acts as listener, observer, and armed partner in cases including the Red-Headed League bank ambush, Mary Sutherland’s interview, the Boscombe Valley inquiry, the Openshaw investigation, Neville St. Clair’s disappearance, the blue carbuncle, and the danger at Stoke Moran. He often admits that he cannot see what Holmes sees, initially misses significant details, or waits for Holmes’s explanation; nevertheless, he carefully records testimony, evidence, newspaper reports, conversations, and outcomes. Watson’s medical role is also central. He searches for Isa Whitney, treats Victor Hatherley after the loss of his thumb, and later treats the injured Mr. Rucastle. He introduces Hatherley’s case to Holmes and is described as the hydraulic engineer’s patient’s companion. In dangerous investigations he carries his revolver, keeps watch, helps protect Helen Stoner, and ultimately shoots a dog to save Rucastle while assisting Holmes in freeing a prisoner at the Copper Beeches. From Baker Street consultations through journeys to Boscombe Valley, Fairbank, Stoke Moran, and Winchester, Watson remains Holmes’s dependable collaborator and the written recorder of their adventures.

Abstract written by a model

Dr. Watson is an English doctor, physician, former military doctor, and retired Army surgeon whose service in India left him with an old wound and whose health can be shaken by autumn weather. He is Sherlock Holmes’s longtime friend, fellow-lodger, companion, narrator, chronicler, and investigative associate. He shares chambers and rooms with Holmes at Baker Street, keeps notes on Holmes’s past cases, and writes Holmes’s memoirs, including A Study in Scarlet and The Sign of Four. Watson’s medical life runs alongside his partnership with Holmes. He has a medical practice, later takes over a practice in Paddington, makes long rounds by hansom, and after marriage lives at his London home with Mrs. Watson. He can also substitute for his doctor neighbour. His training repeatedly makes him useful: he identifies a cataract knife, examines injuries and deaths, aids Arthur Harry Pinner, treats Mr. Melas, and attends the apparent needs of others during investigations. Across the records, Watson travels with or accompanies Holmes through numerous cases, usually observing, questioning, proposing explanations, recording evidence, and witnessing revelations and arrests. He visits Devonshire for the Silver Blaze case; goes to Croydon, Birmingham, Reigate, Aldershot, Brook Street, the Diogenes Club, Woking, and other locations; and helps in moments of danger, including rescuing victims at The Myrtles and rushing to Holmes during the Cunningham investigation. He is also linked to Percy Phelps as a schoolfriend and to Colonel Hayter as an old friend. The account culminates when Holmes warns Watson about Professor Moriarty. Watson leaves his consulting-room, follows Holmes’s elaborate precautions, travels with him through Brussels and Switzerland, and helps evade pursuit. A forged call draws Watson back to Meiringen; when he returns to Reichenbach Falls, he finds evidence of Holmes’s struggle with Moriarty.

Dr. Watson in The Return of Sherlock Holmes

Abstract written by a model

Dr. John Watson is a doctor, medical practitioner, narrator, chronicler, and investigator closely bound to Sherlock Holmes as his friend, former companion, assistant, and professional partner. He keeps notes on unusual cases, writes investigations as stories rather than scientific exercises, and records events with admiration for Holmes while sometimes offering his own theories, doubts, or practical aid. He carries a revolver and check book, smokes tobacco, has irregular meals, and had helped wean Holmes from drug addiction. After selling his Kensington practice, Watson returns to share the Baker Street rooms with Holmes. Their renewed work begins after Watson investigates Ronald Adair’s murder, learns that Holmes is alive, and helps trap Colonel Sebastian Moran. Across subsequent cases, Watson accompanies Holmes to Norwood, Norfolk, Farnham, Mackleton, Sussex, Cambridge, Abbey Grange, Westminster, and elsewhere. He reads reports and messages, observes interviews and physical clues, assists surveillance and arrests, follows suspects, and records Holmes’s explanations. Watson also repeatedly contributes through his medical knowledge: reviving Thorneycroft Huxtable, examining injured people, reporting on Jack Woodley’s condition, and checking whether Violet Smith has recovered. His investigative efforts are not always praised, Holmes finds his position and methods in the cyclist inquiry inadequate, but Watson remains dependable in danger, helping stop a runaway dog-cart, restraining Patrick Cairns, holding Williamson at gunpoint, and assisting with the capture of Beppo. His loyalty sometimes leads him beyond ordinary legality. Though he disapproves of the planned burglary of Charles Augustus Milverton’s house, he prepares masks and rubber-soled shoes, stands guard, witnesses a woman’s revenge, helps burn documents, and escapes with Holmes. He safeguards the black pearl of the Borgias in a safe and is entrusted with confidential state information in the second-stain matter. At Abbey Grange, he serves as Holmes’s informal jury and finds Captain Crocker not guilty. By the end, he remains Holmes’s observant companion, sharing responsibility, secrecy, travel, and the continuing work of investigation.

Dr. Watson is a physician, medical doctor, and surgeon by training; a former military doctor who served in the Indian Army; and a medical man with a not inconsiderable practice. From beginning to end, Watson is portrayed as Holmes's trusted companion and witness: active in investigations, medically capable, occasionally mistaken or uncertain, but consistently loyal, observant, and essential to preserving the cases in narrative form.
