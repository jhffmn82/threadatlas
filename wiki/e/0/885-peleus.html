Abstract written by a model

In The Iliad, Peleus is an aged ruler of the Myrmidons, father and former adviser of Achilles, who gives Achilles horses and counsel and welcomes Phoenix as a son. In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Peleus is a mortal man honoured by Zeus who escapes Acastus's plot, marries Thetis, and saves Achilles from her cauldron. In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Peleus is a righteous mortal hero and king who conquers Iolkos, escapes Akastos' ambush, marries Thetis, and becomes a blissful son-in-law of the gods. In Hecuba and other plays, Peleus is an aged king who shelters Andromache and Molossus, mourns Achilles and Neoptolemus, and is transformed by Thetis into an immortal being. In The Argonautica, Peleus kills a murderous boar, urges the grieving heroes to continue their voyage, and recalls losing Thetis while fathering Achilles. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Peleus accidentally kills Phocus, is banished, is purified by Actor, succeeds him as king, and fathers Achilles by Thetis. In The Library, Peleus is a mortal hero who is exiled, purified, marries Thetis, fathers Achilles, aids others, and eventually dies and is buried in Icos. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Peleus kills Phocus, is exiled, seeks refuge and expiation, fights Centaurs, and marries Thetis, with whom he has Achilles.

and 2 more works

In The Fall of Troy, Peleus welcomes Phoenix, entrusts him with infant Achilles, and is expected to grieve overwhelmingly at Achilles' death. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Peleus, aided by Chiron, wins and marries Thetis and becomes the father of Achilles.

Peleus in The Iliad

Homer · 749 to 724 BC · the work’s page · read it where Peleus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Peleus is a mortal, aged ruler of the Myrmidons, their counsellor and orator, and father and former adviser of Achilles. Blessed with prosperity, immortal horses, and Thetis as his goddess wife, he gives the horses and counsel to Achilles and welcomes Phoenix as a son. In old age, his only royal child Achilles is doomed to die and cannot care for him.

Euripides · about 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Peleus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Peleus is an aged but capable king, descended from Aeacus, husband of Thetis, father of Achilles, and grandfather of Achilles’ son, Neoptolemus, as well as of Molossus through Achilles. A veteran ruler of Phthia who also rules Pharsalia, he bears a sceptre as authority. When Andromache and Molossus face death, he arrives, has their chains removed, condemns Menelaus, and shelters them; he had also protected them from Hermione’s attack. Though others question his age, he asserts that courage can surpass youth. Later he learns that Hermione has fled and that Achilles’ son was murdered at Delphi. Having already lost Achilles, he mourns his grandson and his childlessness. Thetis consoles him, directs burial of the body, and transforms him into an immortal, incorruptible being.

Peleus learns that Hermione has fled and that Achilles’ son has been killed at Delphi. He mourns the loss of his grandson and laments that he is left childless, but Thetis consoles him, commands him to bury the body, and promises to make him immortal.

Peleus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Peleus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Peleus, a son of Æacus, kills his brother Phocus and is exiled from Phthia. He seeks refuge with Ceyx in Trachyn, asks Psamathe to stop her wolf’s attack on his herds, then receives expiation from Acastus among the Magnetes. In fighting the Centaurs, he avenges Crantor with an ashen lance against Demoleon and strikes Dorylas. Following Proteus’s advice, he restrains and marries the goddess Thetis; they have Achilles.
