Telamon in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

Telamon is a son of Aiakos and a chief whose courage in battle never weakened. He fought beside Iolaos in sacking Laomedon’s city and against the Amazons, then accompanied Herakles in destroying Troy, the Meropes, and Alkyoneus. At a feast he challenged Herakles to make the opening libation and received a prayer for a brave son. He was Eriboia’s husband and Aias’s father; his descendants include Teukros and Aias.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Nemean Ode III

Telamon helped sack the city of Laomedon while fighting beside Iolaos. He also followed Iolaos against the Amazons, and the terror of battle never weakened his courage.

Nemean Ode IV

Telamon helped Herakles destroy Troy, the Meropes, and Alkyoneus. His descendants include Teukros, Aias, and the line of Achilles through his connection with the Aiakids.

Isthmian Ode V

Telamon accompanied Herakles to Troy and helped him kill the Meropes and Alkyoneus. During a feast, Telamon challenged Herakles to make the opening libation and received Herakles’ prayer for a brave son.

Telamon in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Telamon is a son, described as born either to Aeacus and Endeis or to Actaeus and Glauce. After killing Phocus with a quoit, he was exiled from Aegina. He succeeded Cychreus as king of Salamis, married Periboea and fathered Ajax, received Hesione from the expedition against Troy, and fathered Teucer with her.

Telamon was described either as a son of Aeacus and Endeis or, by Pherecydes, as a son of Actaeus and Glauce. He killed Phocus by throwing a quoit at his head, was driven from Aegina, received the kingship of Salamis from Cychreus, and later fathered Ajax with Periboea and Teucer with Hesione.
