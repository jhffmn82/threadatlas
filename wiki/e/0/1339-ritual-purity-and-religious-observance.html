ritual purity and religious observance in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

A topic of ritual purity and religious observance: washed hands and prayer are required before libations and river crossings, while pollution, mockery, and other impure acts are forbidden. It expands from water rites to cleanliness, burial, childbirth, exposure, urination, plant-cutting, and divine punishment.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

(ll. 618-640) But if desire for uncomfortable sea-faring seize you;

The speaker forbids making libations with unwashed hands, crossing rivers without washing, mocking sacrifices, polluting rivers and springs, and using uncharmed pots. He also gives rules about bodily cleanliness, burial, childbirth, exposure, urination, and cutting plants at a festival, warning that the gods punish ritual offenses.
