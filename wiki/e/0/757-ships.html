ships of the Achaeans in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

The ships of the Achaeans are a military fleet for the Trojan War, part of the Achaean camp and stationed in rows on the shore by the broad Hellespont, opposite the Trojan city near the Scaean gates. They transport and hold the Danaan contingents, whose ships are counted from two under Guneus to a hundred under Agamemnon; after nine years, their timbers and tackling are deteriorated. Ulysses’ ship stands centrally in the line, between the ships of Ajax and Achilles, while Achilles’ ships remain apart from the fighting. The fleet matters above all as the Achaeans’ refuge, defensive position, and threatened means of escape. When the army thinks of returning home, it rushes toward the ships, though Minerva and Ulysses stop it from launching them. As Hector and the Trojans advance, they seek to reach, capture, and burn the ships, while the Achaeans repeatedly retreat to, rally at, and defend them. Their threatened destruction prompts the embassy to Achilles; commanders inspect guards, conduct scouting, and debate whether to put the ships offshore, a plan Ulysses rejects as harmful to morale. The battle increasingly closes around the fleet. Trojans breach the wall and gates, fight beside and beneath the ships, and drive the Achaeans back from the first row. Defenders use pikes from the decks, fight among the ships and tents, and prevent fire from taking hold despite heavy losses. The ships also frame Achilles’ withdrawal and return to concern: he remains beside them while the army suffers, Patroclus moves among them, and later Patroclus’s body is contested and recovered near them. At the end, Achilles says Patroclus will receive funeral rites at the ships.

Achaean fleet in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

The Achaean fleet is a group of vessels beside the Hellespont, serving as the Argives’ camp, refuge, and route home. It is repeatedly threatened by Trojans with fire, defended behind a rampart, and reached by wounded or fleeing Danaans. The ships stand amid mourning for Achilles, shelter retreating fighters, and carry Odysseus, Diomede, and later Achilles’ son on a journey to and from Scyros. Fearing destruction, the Argives guard the fleet closely. At the end, they load it with treasure, armor, trophies, captives, and Trojan women, launch from the Hellespont, and sail toward Hellas.

The Argives load their ships with treasure, armor, trophies, captives, and Trojan women. They launch from the Hellespont, pass landmarks including Tenedos, Chrysa, Cilla, Lesbos, and Lecton's foreland, and sail toward Hellas.
