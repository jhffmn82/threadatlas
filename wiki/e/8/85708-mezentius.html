Mezentius in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Mezentius is a formidable, contemptuous king and warrior, father of Lausus, exiled from Italy and hated by Tuscan troops. He rallies allies and kills many foes, fights Aeneas, mourns Lausus, returns wounded, and dies after Rhoebus falls, asking burial beside his son.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Meantime, by Jove’s impulse, Mezentius arm’d,

Mezentius rallies his fainting friends, repels the attackers, and kills several warriors, including Hebrus, Latagus, Palmus, Mimas, Acron, and Orodes. He later fights Aeneas, grieves deeply when Lausus is killed, and returns to battle despite his wound. After Rhoebus is struck and falls, Mezentius asks that his body be buried beside Lausus and dies.

Mezentius in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Mezentius is a chief, warrior, and tyrant who rules Caere arbitrarily and cruelly, provoking revolt through brutal murders and punishments. Rejected by Etruria and driven from his palace, he flees with his defeated host to aid Turnus, appearing on the plain with scorn and defiance. As a danger to the Trojans, he inspires widespread Tuscan hatred, drawing five hundred warriors to their side. He becomes Aeneas’s dead foe and the war’s first major casualty; Aeneas strips his arms and displays them as a trophy.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

And now the mighty labour is begun

Mezentius appears first on the plain, marked by scorn and disdain and defying earth and heaven. After Etruria rejected him, he brings his defeated host to aid Turnus.

Now night had shed her silver dews around,

Mezentius is described as a cruel tyrant who ruled Caere and subjected people to brutal murders and punishments. Driven from his palace by revolt, he fled to Turnus’s court, while the Tuscans sought his death.

BOOK X

Mezentius is identified as a source of danger to the Trojans, and Aeneas tells Tarchon of the vengeance Mezentius has prepared. Five hundred warriors join the Tuscan forces because of their hatred of Mezentius.

BOOK XI

Mezentius lies dead as the first fruits of the war. Aeneas strips his arms and displays them on an oak as a trophy dedicated to the God of War.

Mezentius in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Mezentius is a brave, able, and exceptionally cruel soldier, tyrant, and former king. His cruelty, including fastening the dead to the living, causes his people to drive him out. He joins Turnus, fights his revolted subjects and Aeneas, is wounded, returns after Lausus dies, and is killed by Aeneas, asking burial with his son.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXVI

Mezentius is a brave and able soldier known for detestable cruelty, including fastening the dead to the living and leaving them to die together. His people drove him from his city, and he took refuge with Turnus. The Etruscans seek his surrender, while their priests declare that a foreign leader must guide them to victory against him.

NISUS AND EURYALUS

Mezentius fights fiercely against his revolted subjects and encounters Aeneas. Aeneas wounds him, and after learning that Lausus has died, Mezentius returns to the fight and is killed by Aeneas; he asks that his body be buried with his son.
