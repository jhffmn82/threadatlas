Pallantium in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Pallantium is an Arcadian town founded by Pallas, Orestheus, and Phigalus, associated with Evander and Arcadians who founded a settlement near the Tiber. Elder Antonine made it a town and granted tax exemption. Beyond the Mound on the left-hand road, it contains statues of Pallas, Evander, and nearby Polybius, a temple of Proserpine, and a hill that once served as its citadel, with remains of a temple of the gods called Pure.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XLIII.

Pallantium was an Arcadian town associated with Evander and the Arcadians who founded a settlement near the Tiber. The elder Antonine made it a town and granted it freedom and exemption from taxation.

CHAPTER XLIV.

Pallantium lies beyond the Mound on the left-hand road and has a temple, stone statues of Pallas and Evander, and a temple to Proserpine. Its hill once served as the citadel and still contains remains of a temple of the gods called Pure, and a statue of Polybius stands nearby.
