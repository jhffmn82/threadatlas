Æginetans

group · 2 works · 7 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Aeginetans, ^giniana, iEgineans, the Æginetans, £^oa

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Aeginetans rebel against the Athenians, fight them at sea, are defeated, and submit. In Description of Greece, the Æginetans are the inhabitants of Ægina who become a major naval power, provide the second-largest fleet in the Persian War, are expelled to Thyrea, and later recover Ægina.

Æginetans in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Æginetans first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Æginetans inhabit Ægina, speak Doric language and manners after Argive settlers mixed with earlier inhabitants, and become a naval power greater than the Athenians. They provide the second-largest fleet in the Persian War, are expelled by Athenians to Lacedæmonian-given Thyrea, then recover Ægina after Athenian triremes are captured.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIX.

The Æginetans inhabit Ægina and became a major power, exceeding the Athenians in naval strength and supplying the greatest number of ships after them in the Persian War. The Athenians expelled them to Thyrea, though they later recovered Ægina after Athenian triremes were captured at the Hellespont.
