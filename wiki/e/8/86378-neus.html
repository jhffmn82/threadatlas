Œneus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Œneus is king of Calydon, son of Parthaon, father of Meleager, Deïanira, and other sons. He rules Calydon and offers first fruits to many gods but omits Diana. A wild boar ravages his lands, especially a cherished vineyard; later he mourns Meleager’s death. He also promises Deïanira to the bravest suitor.

Œneus offered the first fruits to the gods but forgot Diana in his sacrifices. A wild boar then ravaged part of his dominions, especially a vineyard on which he had spent much effort.

Œneus, the son of Parthaon, is Deïanira's father. He agrees that Deïanira will marry whichever suitor proves to be the bravest.

Oeneus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Oeneus ruled Calydon, first received a vine-plant from Dionysus, and was married to Althaea and later Periboea, by whom or Gorge he had Tydeus. After failing to sacrifice to Artemis, he organized the Calydonian boar hunt. He was imprisoned and tormented by Agrius’s sons, restored by Diomedes, then killed by surviving sons of Agrius; Diomedes buried him at Argos.

Oeneus ruled Calydon and was the first to receive a vine-plant from Dionysus. He sacrificed to all the gods except Artemis, whose anger brought the destructive boar to his land, and he summoned the noblest men of Greece to hunt it. He was later deprived of his kingdom, imprisoned and tormented by the sons of Agrius, restored through Diomedes, and eventually killed by the surviving sons of Agrius; Diomedes buried him at Argos.

THE LIBRARY, II. vir. 6

Oeneus was feasting with Hercules when Eunomus, his kinsman, was accidentally killed.
