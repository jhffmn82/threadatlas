Lipara

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Lipara is described as the largest named city and island of the Aeolian group, founded by Liparus, whose inhabitants cultivated land, resisted Tyrrhenian piracy, and prospered through its natural resources and harbors. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Lipara is described as a Cnidian city-colony led by Pentathlus whose colonists later defeated Tyrrhenians at sea and erected statues.

Abstract written by a model

Lipara is the largest named city and island of the Aeolian group, said to have been founded by Liparus. Its inhabitants shared and cultivated land, resisted Tyrrhenian piracy, and became wealthy through harbors, medicinal baths, fish, fruit, and abundant alum.

Lipara in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Lipara first appears

Abstract written by a model

Lipara, a Cnidian city-colony led by Pentathlus, was occupied by colonists; its people later defeated Tyrrhenians at sea and erected statues.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XI.

The people of Lipara, a colony of Cnidians, erected statues after a naval victory over the Tyrrhenians. Lipara was one of the islands occupied by the colonists associated with the islands of Æolus.
