Tuscans in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

The Tuscans are an army allied with Aeneas. Rebelling against Mezentius because of his tyranny and forbidden by an augur to have a native ruler, they seek a foreign leader; their chief Tarchon awaits Aeneas. After accepting his treaty, they join him, sail toward the Trojan camp, land to fight the Latian forces, and are met by Camilla and the Volscians.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Now night had shed her silver dews around,

The Tuscans rebelled against Mezentius and now seek a foreign leader because an augur forbade a native ruler from commanding them. Their chief Tarchon offers Evander the crown, but the Tuscans ultimately await a leader such as Aeneas.

BOOK X

The Tuscan army joins Aeneas after Tarchon accepts his treaty and brings forces from several Tuscan cities and peoples. It sails with Aeneas toward the Trojan camp and lands to fight the Latian forces.

While they debate, nor these nor those will yield,

The Tyrrhene troops are the force that Camilla and the Volscians undertake to meet. Turnus also refers to them as the enemy against whom Camilla’s forces will fight.
