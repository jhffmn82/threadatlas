the divine command to leave Carthage

Abstract written by a model

Jupiter’s command, delivered through Mercury, orders Aeneas to leave Carthage, abandon pleasure and delay, and pursue the Latian lands, the promised city and martial race, and Ascanius’s future. Though it terrifies him and conflicts with his desire, Aeneas treats it as unavoidable and rejects Dido’s pleas.

BOOK IV

Jupiter's command requires Aeneas to leave Carthage and stop wasting his days in pleasure and delay. Through Mercury, Jupiter reminds him that he must pursue the city and martial race promised by fate, as well as Ascanius's future in Ausonia and Rome.

The pious prince was seiz’d with sudden fear;

The divine command comes from Jove through the herald of the gods and directs Aeneas toward the Latian lands. Aeneas presents it as an unavoidable order, and it causes him to reject Dido’s pleas for delay.
