Icarus

person · 5 works · 11 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Icarus his son, Icarus’s, his son, his unfortunate son, the boy, Ἴκαρος

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Icarus flees Crete with his father, Daedalus; in one account he falls into the sea while trying to land on an island, and in another he falls when the sun melts the wax holding his artificial wings together, after which the sea and island are called Icarian. In The Library, Icarus escapes the labyrinth with Daedalus using wings, flies too high despite the warning, and dies falling into the Icarian Sea. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Icarus helps make wings, ignores advice to fly between the Sun, abandons his guide, and rises toward the Sun. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Icarus overturns his boat and drowns; Hercules finds, recognizes, and buries him, after which an island and nearby sea receive his name. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Icarus escapes on waxed feather wings, flies too near the sun, falls into the sea and dies, and is buried by Daedalus, who names places in his memory.

FABLE III. [VIII.183-259]

Icarus assists Dædalus while the wings are being made, playfully handling the feathers and softening the wax. Although Dædalus warns him to keep to the middle course, Icarus becomes exhilarated by a bolder flight, abandons his guide, and rises toward the Sun.

CHAPTER XI.

Icarus, the son of Daedalus, steered his boat awkwardly, causing it to overturn, and was drowned. Hercules found and recognized his body and buried it by a promontory jutting into the Aegean Sea; the island and nearby sea were then named after Icarus.

Chapter XIII

Icarus escapes with Daedalus using wings made of feathers, thread, and wax. He flies too close to the sun, causing the wax to melt, falls into the sea, and gives his name to it; Daedalus buries him and names the land Icaria in his memory.
