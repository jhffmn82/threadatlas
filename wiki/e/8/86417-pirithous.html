Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Perithous marries Hippodamia, invites Theseus and the Centaurs to the wedding, joins the Lapiths against the drunken Centaurs, and later descends with Theseus to seek Proserpina, remaining in eternal punishment after Theseus is released. In The Library, Pirithous marries Hippodamia, fights drunken Centaurs with Theseus after they attack her at the wedding, helps abduct Helen, descends into Hades seeking Persephone, and remains bound there forever by serpents. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Pirithoüs, son of Ixion and Dia, marries Hippodame and hosts the wedding feast where a Centaur's attempt on his bride begins a battle in which he opposes the Centaurs and kills Petræus, Lycus, and Chromis. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Pirithous is praised for his friendship with Theseus, sits with him on an enchanted rock, and indignantly regards his useless sword during their venture. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Pirithous raids the king of Athens's herds, becomes Theseus's lifelong friend, helps carry off Helen, and remains trapped in the underworld after pursuing Pluto's wife.

Perithous in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Perithous first appears

Abstract written by a model

Perithous, son of Ixion and Dia, married Hippodamia, invited Theseus and the Centaurs to their wedding, and joined the Lapiths against the drunken Centaurs. After Hippodamia’s death, he went to Athens and joined Theseus in plans to obtain wives. Seeking Proserpina, they descended below and were bound; Theseus was released, but Perithous remained in eternal punishment.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

,CHAP. IV.

Perithous, the son of Ixion, went to Athens after the death of his wife and advised Theseus to abduct Helen. After Helen was assigned to Theseus by lot, Perithous and Theseus agreed that each would help the other obtain another wife. Perithous later persuaded Theseus to accompany him in an attempt to court Proserpina; both descended into the shades below, where they were bound, though Theseus was later released for the sake of Hercules. Perithous remained subject to eternal punishment according to the account.

Perithous was the son of Ixion and Dia. He married Hippodamia, invited Theseus and the Centaurs to the wedding, and joined the Lapiths against the drunken Centaurs.

Pirithoüs in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Pirithoüs first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pirithoüs, son of Ixion and Dia, marries Hippodame and hosts a wedding feast attended by Centaurs and Lapithæ. A Centaur’s attempt on his bride disrupts the wedding, beginning a battle over succession claims in which Pirithoüs opposes the Centaurs and kills Petræus, Lycus, and Chromis.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

FABLES III. AND IV. [XII.146-535]

Pirithoüs, the son of Ixion, marries Hippodame and hosts the wedding feast attended by the Centaurs and Lapithæ. During the ensuing battle, he kills Petræus and is also credited with killing Lycus and Chromis.

EXPLANATION.

Pirithoüs was the son of Ixion by Dia and became the opponent of the Centaurs when they claimed a right to share in the succession to Ixion’s dominions. His wedding to Hippodamia was disrupted by Eurytus, or Eurytion, whose attempt on the bride led to the battle with the Centaurs.
