Abstract written by a model

The hanging garden near the citadel, a 400-foot-square garden, was built by Cyrus rather than Semiramis for a Persian courtesan. It rises through masonry-supported arches and planted terraces, irrigated by Euphrates water drawn through conduits, conveyances, and engines.

The hanging garden near the citadel is said not to have been built by Semiramis but by a later prince called Cyrus for a Persian courtesan. It consists of ascending arches and planted terraces supported by strong masonry, with conduits and engines that draw water from the river to irrigate the garden.
