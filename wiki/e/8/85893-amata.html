Amata in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Amata is the queen of Latium, wife of the Latian king, and mother of Lavinia. She fiercely opposes Lavinia’s marriage to the Trojan guest and champions Turnus, Lavinia’s former betrothed, fearing both for him and for her throne under a Trojan son. Poisoned with rage by Alecto, she publicly protests the match, leads matrons in Bacchic rites, and demands war. Near the end, she begs Turnus to avoid single combat. When she sees the enemy enter the city and believes Turnus dead, grief and guilt drive her to hang herself.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Thus having said, she sinks beneath the ground,

Amata is enraged that Turnus has been displaced and that Lavinia is to marry the Trojan guest. Alecto poisons her with rage, after which she argues against the marriage, runs through the public places howling, and leads the women in Bacchic rites to delay the Trojan match and demand war.

BOOK XII

Amata fears for Turnus’s life and begs him to abandon the single combat and continue the war instead. She declares that her own life depends on his safety and that she cannot endure seeing her throne taken by strangers or a Trojan son.

When Turnus saw the Trojan quit the plain,

The queen sees the enemy entering the city and, finding no sign of Turnus, concludes that he has been killed. Overcome by grief and believing herself responsible for the disaster, she hangs herself.
