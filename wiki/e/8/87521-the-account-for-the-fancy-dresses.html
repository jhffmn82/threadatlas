Abstract written by a model

The account for the fancy dresses is a cash bill from Lewis Isaacs, Costumier to the Queen, for costumes hired for the masked ball at Durnstone. It totals forty pounds, nineteen shillings, including fifteen guineas for Salome’s gown, ten guineas for Sheba’s costume, trimmings, extras, and mislaid brown-paper patterns. Salome and Sheba owe the bill without Augustin’s knowledge and possess only fifteen shillings and threepence, so they seek Georgiana’s help to win money. The Dean, himself unable to settle his bills, later discovers the account and demands that the girls explain it; they promise to pay with their winnings.

DANDY DICK. / THE FIRST ACT.

The account for the fancy dresses totals forty pounds, nineteen shillings and demands cash payment. It includes fifteen guineas for Salome's gown, ten guineas for Sheba's costume, additional charges for trimmings and extras, and a note that the girls' brown paper patterns were mislaid.

_GEORGIANA TIDMAN enters.

Salome and Sheba disclose that they owe forty pounds, nineteen shillings without Augustin's knowledge. They say they have only fifteen shillings and threepence of their own and ask Georgiana to help them win enough money to pay what they owe.

GEORGIANA and SIR TRISTRAM enter by the window.

The Dean acknowledges that he has bills he intends to discharge when he can, but he lacks the money to settle them. Georgiana connects the bills with the need to raise funds for the threatened Spire.

_The second scene is the Morning Room at the Deanery again.

The £40 19s costume bill is a debt to Lewis Isaacs, Costumier to the Queen, for costumes used at the masked ball at Durnstone. The Dean discovers it and orders Salome and Sheba to account for it, but they say they can pay it with their winnings.
