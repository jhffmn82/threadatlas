Egyptian capital punishment

topic · 1 work · 8 quoted facts · 0 connections
Also called: executed, punishment, put to death, to die

Abstract written by a model

Egyptian capital punishment is a body of penalties for offenses including perjury, failing to rescue or report a threatened victim, false accusation, unjust livelihood, murder, and parricide. Penalties can mirror the threatened punishment or involve death, while parricide is punished by mangling, thorns, and burning. Execution of pregnant women is delayed until delivery. Some offenses receive nonfatal punishments: parents who kill children must embrace their bodies for three days and nights, and military secret revelation brings tongue-cutting or degradation.

Egyptian capital punishment is in one work on this site, The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Egyptian capital punishment is imposed for perjury, failure to rescue or report a threatened victim in certain circumstances, false accusation, false livelihood reports, murder, parricide, and other offenses. Pregnant women are not executed until after delivery, parents who kill their children are instead forced to embrace them for three days and nights, and some military offenders are degraded rather than killed.
