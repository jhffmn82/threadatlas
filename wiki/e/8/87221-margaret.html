Margaret in Gallegher and Other Stories

Richard Harding Davis · 1890

Margaret is a female baby left in a tenement room while her parents are imprisoned on Blackwell's Island. She lies in bedding, needs milk, and is weakened by heat, inadequate food, and lack of care; deep blue rings surround her closed eyes, and her breathing becomes dangerously irregular. At first she uncertainly recognizes Rags Raegen, then greets him with smiles, coos, affection, and trust. Raegen gives her water and softened bread, watches over her through the night, and carries her to the station-house when she becomes ill after two days without food. A char-woman gives her milk and arranges medical care. After the Society rejects her family as caretakers, Raegen cares for her. Later, she visits him in the Tombs, reaches for him, and kisses him through the bars.

" MY DISREPUTABLE FRIEND, MR.

Margaret is a baby who crawls toward Rags while he is hiding and greets him with smiles, coos, and affection. Her parents are said to be serving thirty days on Blackwell's Island, and she appears to be left alone in the tenement. Rags gives her water and softened bread, cares for her through the night, and holds her as she sleeps.

The sun rose hissing out of the East River, a broad, red disc of heat.

Margaret is a baby in Rags's care who becomes dangerously ill from the heat and lack of food. She breathes irregularly, has blue rings around her eyes, rejects Rags's first attempted food, and later sleeps so deeply that he can barely detect her breathing. Her presence gives Rags an unfamiliar sense of responsibility and self-respect and prevents him from immediately escaping.
