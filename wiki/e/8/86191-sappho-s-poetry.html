Sappho's poetry in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Sappho’s poetry is lyric verse, turning to elegy when grief demands tears rather than lyre music. Inspired by the daughters of Pegasus, it unfolds from mourning to claims of sweet song, worldwide fame, and comparison with Alcaeus.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

XV

Sappho says that elegy suits her grief because she must weep, while the lyre is not suited to her tears. She claims that the daughters of Pegasus inspire her sweetest songs and that her name is already sung throughout the world; she also compares her fame with that of Alcaeus.
