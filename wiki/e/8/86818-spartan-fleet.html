Spartan fleet in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Spartan fleet first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Spartan fleet was rebuilt after losses and Mindarus's death, funded by Cyrus and led by Lysander to victory at Notium. Under Callicratides, it grew to 150 ships, defeated Conon near Mitylene, then, with 140 ships, suffered major losses at Arginusae and fled.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. IX.

The Spartan fleet was rebuilt after losing much of its strength and its commander Mindarus. Lysander gathered ships from Rhodes, Ephesus, Miletus, and Chios, received funding from Cyrus, and used the fleet to defeat the Athenians at Notium.

CHAP. X.

The Lacedaemonian fleet delivered to Callicratides numbered 150 ships after additions from other places, while Conon's Athenian fleet numbered seventy ships. The fleets fought near Mitylene, and Callicratides ultimately captured thirty ships and secured the harbour.

CHAP. XIV.

The Spartan fleet, commanded by Callicratides, consisted of 140 ships. It suffered a major defeat at Arginusae, losing seventy-seven ships, after which its surviving forces fled.

Lacedaemonian fleet in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Lacedaemonian fleet first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Lacedaemonian fleet, an eighty-five-galley force under Pisander, operated from Rhodes and sought to besiege Caunus under Pharax; Conon gained Persian support against it, and near Cnidus it was defeated, losing ships, prisoners, and sovereignty of the sea.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. vm.

The Lacedaemonian fleet operated from Rhodes and attempted to besiege Caunus under Pharax. It was later targeted by Conon, who obtained Persian funding and support to ruin it.

CHAP. IX,

The Lacedaemonian fleet, commanded in the account by Periarchus and identified in the heading with Pisander, fought the Persian fleet near Cnidus. It was defeated, fifty ships were captured, five hundred men were taken prisoner, and Lacedaemon lost sovereignty of the sea.
