Hannibal

person · 1 work · 11 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Hannibal's, the Carthaginian general

Abstract written by a model

Hannibal was a Carthaginian general, chief magistrate of Carthage, and grandson of Amilcar. Appointed to lead an expedition to Sicily, he raised mercenaries in Spain, recruited citizens and Africans, equipped a fleet, and commanded an invasion army supplied by transport ships. He captured and destroyed Selinus, then besieged and razed Himera in revenge for his family’s earlier defeat, burning its temples and returning to Carthage with abundant booty. Later, as general with full authority over the war, he sailed with fifty galleys after the naval defeat at Eryx to aid the army’s transport. Commanding a siege with Amilcar, he ordered monuments and tombs destroyed for siege mounds, and died in a plague among the Carthaginian army.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. V.

Hannibal, chief magistrate of Carthage and grandson of Amilcar, was appointed general for the expedition to Sicily. He raised mercenaries in Spain, recruited citizens and Africans, and equipped a fleet for the following spring.

CHAP. VII.

Hannibal led the Carthaginian invasion of Sicily with a large army and fleet. He captured and destroyed Selinus, then besieged and destroyed Himera, ordered prisoners and temples treated according to his stated purposes, and finally returned to Carthage with abundant booty.

CHAP. XII.

Hannibal, who had previously razed Selinus and Himera, was appointed general of the Carthaginian forces with full authority over the war. After the naval defeat at Eryx, he sailed with fifty galleys to make the Syracusan victory fruitless and help secure the transport of the army.
