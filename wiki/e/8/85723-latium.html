Latium in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Latium is the promised future homeland and realm where Aeneas’s wandering and vows are to end: a quiet kingdom, royal marriage, restored Trojan line, and land destined for his martial descendants. Jupiter assigns it as the realm of Aeneas and his heirs, with Ascanius later moving the seat from Lavinium to Alba Longa. While Aeneas lingers in Libya, he is commanded to steer for Latium. On arrival, it is ruled by Latinus; its shores are foretold to receive a foreign prince, and the Trojans are permitted to settle. The promised sharing of the land becomes a war: Jupiter says peace had been commanded, while Juno calls the settlement an unlawful seizure. Latium’s towns are emptied, its land exhausted, and its people mourn. Peace ultimately preserves it as the land of the Latins: its native name and traditions remain, the natives command, and the foreign Trojans merge into a new people.

Narrative, section by section

9 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK I

Latium is the realm destined for Aeneas and his descendants. Jupiter promises that Aeneas will reach its promised throne and that Ascanius will later transfer the seat from Lavinium to Alba Longa.

“Then, not before, I felt my curdled blood

Latium is identified by Creusa’s ghost as the destination decreed for Aeneas after many years of wandering by land and sea. There he will find a quiet kingdom and a royal bride, and the Trojan line will be restored.

BOOK IV

Latium is the land where Aeneas is destined to rule a martial race descended from Teucer. Jupiter's message contrasts this promised Latian future with Aeneas's lingering in Libya.

The pious prince was seiz’d with sudden fear;

Latium is the promised place to which Aeneas is commanded to steer. He says that all his vows are terminated there and that he must pursue its promised kingdom.

BOOK VII

Latium is the land sought by the Trojans and ruled by Latinus. Its shores are foretold to receive a foreign prince, and Latinus grants the Trojans permission to settle there.

BOOK X

Jupiter says that he had commanded peace and assigned the Latian lands, but the war continues despite his command. Juno defends the Latians against what she calls the Trojans’ unlawful seizure of foreign realms.

BOOK XI

Aeneas says that fate sent him to share the Latian land. He maintains that he does not wage an unjust war because Latinus denied his friendship and promised bride.

Thus, full of anxious thought, he summons all

Latium is suffering from war, with its towns depopulated, its land exhausted, and its people mourning. Drances says that its peace and public safety depend on ending Turnus’s conflict with the Trojans.

Now Turnus rolls aloof o’er empty plains,

Latium is to remain the land of the Latins after the peace. Juno asks that its name and traditions be preserved, and Jove agrees that the natives will command while the foreign Trojans subside into the new people.
