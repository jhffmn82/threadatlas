Haliartus

place · 2 works · 8 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: city, district of Haliartus, town and district
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Haliartus was a city besieged by Lysander and the Phocians, aided by the Boeotians and Athenians, where a battle resulted in Lysander's death and the Lacedaemonians' defeat.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Haliartus is a city that supported the Greeks during the invasion of the Medes, was attacked by part of Xerxes' army, and contained the tomb of Lysander, a hero-chapel, temples, and the river Lophis in its district.

Haliartus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Haliartus is a city that supported the Greeks during the invasion of the Medes and was attacked by part of Xerxes' army. It contains the tomb of Lysander, who fell when forces from Thebes and Athens sallied out, as well as a hero-chapel, temples, and the river Lophis in its district.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXII.

Haliartus was attacked by part of Xerxes' army during the invasion of the Medes because its people supported the Greeks. It contains the tomb of Lysander, who fell when forces from Thebes and Athens inside the city sallied out against him.

CHAPTER XXXIII.

Haliartus contains Lysander’s tomb, a hero-chapel to Cecrops, the temple of the Praxidicæ, and roofless temples whose dedications the author could not ascertain. The river Lophis flows through its district.
