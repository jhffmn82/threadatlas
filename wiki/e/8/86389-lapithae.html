Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Lapithae take their name from Lapithus and later fight the Centaurs, forcing survivors to flee to Pholoe and Malea. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the Lapithae are a civilized race that drive the Centaurs into the mountains and fight them at Pirithous’s wedding and afterward.

Lapithae in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Lapithae first appears

Abstract written by a model

Lapithae are presented as a civilized race that found towns and drive their wild neighbors, the Centaurs, into the mountains. At Pirithous’s wedding, they oppose the Centaurs after Hippodame is seized; several Lapithae are attacked or killed. They continue fighting in the Lapithaean house until Centaurs are killed, flee, or disperse, then pursue them toward the Promontory of Malea.

The Lapiths fight the Centaurs in the Lapithæan house and continue using their weapons until some of the Centaurs are killed and the rest flee or are dispersed by night. Mopsus calls Cæneus the glory of the Lapithæan race.

EXPLANATION.

The Lapithæ are presented as a more civilized race that founded towns and gradually drove their wild neighbors, the Centaurs, into the mountains. They pursued the Centaurs after their battle and forced them toward the promontory of Malea.
