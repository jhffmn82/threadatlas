Thespians in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The Thespians are Bœotians who possessed Ceressus, colonized Sardinia in Iolaus’s expedition, and honored Eros above all gods, holding a festival at Helicon’s grove. Regretting ill-will toward the Thebans, they fled their city to Ceressus, where Epaminondas captured them. Their city had Eros images, Black Aphrodite’s temple, theatre, marketplace, and other monuments.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIV.

The Thespians, regretting their former ill-will toward the Thebans and fearing their current fortunes, abandoned their city and fled to Ceressus. After Epaminondas captured Ceressus, he took the Thespians who had taken refuge there captive.

CHAPTER XXVII.

The Thespians have always honored Eros above the other gods and possess an old rude stone statue of him. They also have a statue of Eros by Menodorus, a temple of Black Aphrodite, a theatre, a marketplace, a statue of Hesiod, a Victory, and a small temple of the Muses.
