medals and decorations in Gallegher and Other Stories

Richard Harding Davis · 1890

Medals and decorations are awarded for public service and high virtues such as bravery, statesmanship, and artistic genius. After recipients pawn or sell them, they enter Miss Catherwaight’s collection, including French crosses, English war medals, a Victoria Cross, and a congressional gold medal; she especially values discreditable histories.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE CYNICAL MISS CATHERWAIGHT Miss Catherwaight's collection of orders and decorations and medals was her chief offence in the eyes of those of her dear friends who thought her clever but cynical.

The medals and decorations in Miss Catherwaight's collection were originally rewards for bravery, statesmanship, artistic genius, or other high virtues. Many were later pawned or sold, including French crosses of the Legion of Honor, English war medals, a Victoria Cross, and a congressional gold medal. Miss Catherwaight values them especially when their histories are discreditable.
