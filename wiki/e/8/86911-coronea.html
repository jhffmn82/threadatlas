Coronea

place · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 4 connections
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Coronea is the site of a battle between Agesilaus and the Boeotians and their confederates, in which the Boeotians defeated the opposing Lacedaemonian wing while Agesilaus routed the other part.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Coronea is given to Thersander through Haliartus and Coronus, has marketplace altars of Hermes Epimelius and the Winds and a temple with an ancient Hera statue by Pythodorus, and lies near Mount Libethrium and Mount Laphystium.

Coronea in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Coronea, given to Thersander through Haliartus and Coronus, has marketplace altars of Hermes Epimelius and the Winds, and a temple with an ancient Hera statue by Pythodorus; Mount Libethrium and Mount Laphystium lie nearby.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXIV.

Coronea is described as having an altar of Hermes Epimelius and an altar of the Winds in its market-place. It also has a temple and ancient statue of Hera by Pythodorus the Theban, while Mount Libethrium lies about 40 stades away and Mount Laphystium about 20 stades away.
