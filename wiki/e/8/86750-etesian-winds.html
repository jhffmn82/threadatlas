Etesian winds in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Etesian winds include Aparctias, a north-east wind, and Argestes, a north-west wind. Thales says they flood the Nile by blocking its mouth, but this is disputed because the Nile rises before and falls after the winds.

Thales attributes the Nile’s flooding to the Etesian winds blocking its mouth and preventing it from flowing into the sea. Democritus also uses the winds in his explanation, but this is rejected because the Nile begins swelling before the winds occur and falls after they have ended.
