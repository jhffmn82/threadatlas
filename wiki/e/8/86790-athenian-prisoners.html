Athenian prisoners in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Athenian prisoners, surrendered captives and suppliants of the Syracusans, become the subject of a Syracuse assembly. Diocles urges severe punishment, while Hermocrates and Nicholaus urge mercy. They are depicted as either distressed suppliants or aggressors; most suffer harsh imprisonment, but some are released because of useful liberal education.

The prisoners are the subject of the Syracuse assembly's deliberation after surrendering with their arms. Diocles proposes severe punishments for them, while Hermocrates and Nicholaus argue that they should be treated mercifully.
