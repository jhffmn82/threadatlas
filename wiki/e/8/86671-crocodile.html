crocodile in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The crocodile is a sacred river creature of the Nile in Egypt. From a small egg, it grows to sixteen cubits, covered in extraordinarily hard scales and armed with powerful teeth and destructive claws. It devours people and animals near the river, though the ichneumon destroys its eggs. Some inhabitants worship it as a god; after rescuing Menas across the lake of Meris, it was commanded to be worshipped. It also protects Egypt by deterring raiders.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. III.

The crocodile is described as growing from a small egg to a creature up to sixteen cubits long, with a heavily scaled body, powerful teeth, and destructive claws. It devours people and animals near the river, but its eggs are broken by the ichneumon; some Egyptians also worship it as a god.

CHAP, VII.

The crocodile is worshipped in Egypt despite sometimes devouring people. One account says that a crocodile rescued the ancient king Menas from his pursuing dogs by carrying him across the lake of Meris, after which he built the city called Crocodile, dedicated the lake to crocodiles, and commanded that they be worshipped as gods.
