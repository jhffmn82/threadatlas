palaces of Babylon in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The palaces of Babylon are two palaces at opposite ends of the bridge, one on each side of the Euphrates, overlooking the city. Built by Semiramis, they were richly decorated with walls, creatures, hunting scenes, statues, and armies, and connected by a vault beneath the river. They later decayed or were greatly damaged.

Semiramis builds two palaces at the ends of the bridge, one on the east side of the Euphrates and one on the west, with views over the city and access to its convenient places. The western palace has several enclosing walls decorated with representations of living creatures and hunting scenes, while the other has statues, armies, and hunting scenes; the palaces later decay or are greatly damaged.
