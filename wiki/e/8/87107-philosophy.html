philosophy in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Philosophy is said to be improved by Theban rules and preserved through Chaldean hereditary learning. It unfolds from Theban claims of ancient priority and exact improvement to a Chaldean doctrine of an eternal, providentially ordered world, contrasted with changing Greek sects.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

The Thebans boast they were the most antient philosophers and

The Thebans claim to have been the first people to discover exact rules for improving philosophy.

CHAP. III.

The Chaldeans hold that the world is eternal, without a definite beginning or end, and that divine providence orders and sustains it. Their philosophical learning is transmitted from fathers to sons, while the text contrasts this continuity with the competing sects and changing opinions of the Greeks.
