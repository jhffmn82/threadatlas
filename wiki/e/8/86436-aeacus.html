Aeacus

person · 3 works · 9 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: AEacus, iGacus

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Aeacus was the son of Zeus and Aegina, later king of Aegina, and father of Peleus and Telamon. In The Library, Aeacus was the son of Zeus and Aegina, husband of Endeis, father of Peleus, Telamon, and Phocus, a pious man who prayed for Greece, and after death a guardian of Hades's keys. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Aeacus was Jupiter's son and king of Aegina, who received Cephalus, endured a plague that killed his people, and gained ant-numbered citizens through prayer.

Chapter VII

Aeacus, king of Aegina, receives Cephalus and promises him military assistance. After the plague destroys his people, he prays to Jupiter for citizens as numerous as the ants on a sacred oak, and Jupiter grants his request.
