five hundred defenders (at Thermopylae) in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The five hundred defenders at Thermopylae, unmatched in valour, attack an immense Persian multitude despite hopelessly unequal terms. Exhausted and surrounded by danger, they choose their country’s laws over life, remain unconquerable in mind, seek fame through extraordinary resistance, terrify the Persians, and become a lasting example contributing to Greece’s common liberty.

On the other hand, this terror and amazement of the Persians

The five hundred defenders attacked an immense multitude despite having no hope of overcoming it, choosing to defend their country's laws rather than preserve their lives. Although surrounded by dangers and exhausted in body, they remained unconquerable in mind and became a lasting example of valour.
