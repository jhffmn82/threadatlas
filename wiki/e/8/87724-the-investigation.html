Abstract written by a model

The investigation, conducted by the police with Holmes's collaboration, concerns the Oxshott murder. It begins when Garcia is found dead with Eccles's letter in his pocket, prompting inquiries into the note, Wisteria Lodge, the vanished servant and cook, and the time of death. Holmes and Inspector Baynes pursue separate lines: Baynes arrests a suspected cook, while Holmes doubts that solution and searches for the giant visitor. Though Holmes finds the main features simple, evidentiary gaps make arrest difficult, so he focuses on High Gable and plans a direct inquiry without a warrant.

the investigation is in one work on this site, His Last Bow. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

The Adventure of Wisteria Lodge / 1. The Singular Experience of Mr. John Scott Eccles

The investigation begins after the police find Garcia dead and discover Eccles's letter in his pocket. Sherlock Holmes agrees to collaborate with Inspector Baynes and seeks further facts about the note, the house, the disappearance, and the timing of Garcia's death.

2. The Tiger of San Pedro

The investigation involves the death of Mr. Garcia, the flight of his servant and cook, the unusual evidence in Wisteria Lodge, and the search for the giant visitor. Sherlock Holmes and Inspector Baynes work independently, with Baynes arresting a suspected cook while Holmes remains unconvinced by that theory.

“Just sit down in that chair, Watson,” said Sherlock Holmes when we had

Holmes says the case is simple in its leading features but has serious difficulties, especially gaps preventing an arrest. He narrows the investigation to High Gable and plans a direct investigation because there is not enough evidence to obtain a warrant.
