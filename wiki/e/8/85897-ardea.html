Ardea

place · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Ardea’s walls, Ardua, capital, city, his town, mighty
  • In The Aeneid, Ardea was a city founded by Danae and her Argive followers, later ruled by Turnus from his lofty palace, and eventually decayed with age.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Ardea was Turnus's capital, fell after his death, burned, and rose from its ashes as a bird retaining its name and traits.

Ardea in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Ardea is a city, formerly fair and founded by Danae and her Argive followers, later ruled by Turnus from his lofty palace, then decayed with age.

Thus having said, she sinks beneath the ground,

Ardea is the city where Turnus lies in his lofty palace. It was formerly called Ardua, was founded by Danae and her Argive followers, and is described as once fair but now consumed by age.

Ardea in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Ardea, Turnus's capital while he lives, falls after his death, burns, and from its ashes rises a bird retaining its name and traits.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLES VII. AND VIII. [XIV.441-526]

Ardea falls after Turnus falls and is consumed by ruthless flames. A bird rises from its ashes, retaining the voice, leanness, paleness, and name associated with the captured city.
