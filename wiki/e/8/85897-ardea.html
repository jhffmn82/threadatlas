Ardea

place · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Ardea’s walls, Ardua, capital, city, his town, mighty

Abstract written by a model

In The Aeneid, Ardea was a city founded by Danae and her Argive followers, later ruled by Turnus from his lofty palace, and eventually decayed with age. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Ardea was Turnus's capital, fell after his death, burned, and rose from its ashes as a bird retaining its name and traits.

Ardea falls after Turnus falls and is consumed by ruthless flames. A bird rises from its ashes, retaining the voice, leanness, paleness, and name associated with the captured city.
