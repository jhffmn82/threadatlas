Eriphyle in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Eriphyle, sister of Adrastus and wife of Amphiaraus, judged in favor of Adrastus and that Amphiaraus should join the war. Polynices gave her a golden chain and Thersandra later gave her a rich garment to influence the war's outcome, and Alcmaeon killed her at his father's command.

Eriphyle in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Eriphyle’s agreed decisions settle disputes between Amphiaraus and Adrastus. Bribed with Polynices’ necklace, she persuades Amphiaraus to march; later, after receiving a robe from Thersander, she persuades her sons to join the expedition. Alcmaeon learns she was bribed and, following Apollo’s instruction, kills her, alone or with Amphilochus.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

VI. ᾿Ἑτεοκλῆς δὲ καὶ ἸΠολυνείκης περὶ τῆς

Eriphyle is the person whose decision determines whether Amphiaraus will join the war. Although Amphiaraus forbids her to accept gifts from Polynices, she accepts his necklace and persuades Amphiaraus to march with Adrastus.

VII. Κρέων δὲ τὴν Θηβαίων βασιλείαν παραλα-

Eriphyle received the robe of Eriphyle from Thersander and persuaded her sons to join the expedition against Thebes. After Alcmaeon learned that she had been bribed, Apollo instructed him to kill her, and he did so, either alone or with Amphilochus.

Eriphyle in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Eriphyle is Alcmæon’s mother and possesses a famous gold necklace, received from her husband and concealed in her hand. She sold her husband for gold; Alcmæon killed her, fled Argos, and was pursued by her avenger under a prophecy.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIV.

Eriphyle was Alcmæon’s mother, whom he killed before fleeing Argos. The prophecy concerning Alcmæon stated that the Avenger of his mother Eriphyle would pursue him except on land newly formed by the sea.

CHAPTER XLI.

Eriphyle received the necklace associated with Harmonia as a gift from her husband. The text cites Homer as saying that she sold her husband for gold and says that her necklace was entirely gold.
