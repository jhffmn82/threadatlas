  • In Heroides and Amores, Omphale is a Lydian queen and Hercules's mistress who makes him submit, wear women's clothing, and perform wool-working, taking his glory.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Omphale receives Hercules as a slave, recognizes and frees him, marries him, and becomes mother of their son Lamon.
  • In The Library, Omphale is the daughter of Iardanes and queen of Lydia who buys Hercules from Hermes after an oracle orders his sale; he serves her as a slave for three years, performing deeds.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Omphale is the queen whom Hercules serves as a slave for three years after killing his friend Iphitus in a fit of madness; during his service, he lives effeminately while she wears his lion's skin.

Omphale in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Lydian queen and Hercules's mistress, she makes him submit, wear women's clothing, and perform wool-working, taking his glory.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

IX

Omphale is represented as Hercules's mistress and as having made him wear women's clothing, perform wool-working, and submit to her threats. Deianira says that Omphale has taken the glory belonging to Hercules.

Omphale in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Omphale was the queen whom Hercules served as a slave for three years after killing his friend Iphitus in a fit of madness. During that service Hercules lived effeminately, sometimes wearing women's clothing and spinning wool, while Omphale wore his lion's skin.

Narrative, section by section

Omphale was the queen whom Hercules served as a slave for three years after killing his friend Iphitus in a fit of madness. During that service Hercules lived effeminately, sometimes wearing women's clothing and spinning wool, while Omphale wore his lion's skin.
