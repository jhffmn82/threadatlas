Abstract written by a model

In The Royal Book of Oz, Sir Hokus is Dorothy’s loyal Knight Errant who joins the quest for the Scarecrow, aids the travelers through many dangers, and returns to Oz pledged to serve Dorothy, Ozma, and Oz. In Kabumpo in Oz, Sir Hokus of Pokes is the Giant Killer, a courageous knight who helps confront Ruggedo after being rescued from unconsciousness in the shaking palace. In The Cowardly Lion of Oz, Sir Hokus of Pokes is Dorothy’s warlike knight errant who falls beside Mustafa at Mudge’s lion enclosure. In The Lost King of Oz, Sir Hokus of Pokes investigates a mysterious feather with the Scarecrow, travels to Morrow, and follows its instructions while trapped against a rising castle’s flagpole. In The Gnome King of Oz, Sir Hokus is a brave Knight of Oz who offers to defend Dorothy, then helps decorate a banquet hall and compose Ozma’s welcome. In The Yellow Knight of Oz, Sir Hokus of Pokes is a brave elderly knight who seeks his lost history, becomes the young Yellow Knight and Prince of Corumbia, regains his memories, and marries Princess Marygolden. In The Yellow Knight of Oz, the Yellow Knight is the young, adventurous Prince of Corumbia who pursues the Comfortable Camel, escapes captivity, and seeks to rescue a maiden and continue his quest.

Sir Hokus in The Royal Book of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1921 · the work’s page · read it where Sir Hokus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sir Hokus is a gentle, melancholy, brave knight of Pokes, also called a Knight Errant of Oz. He has been imprisoned in Pokes for centuries under a mysterious knight’s curse, sleeping so long that he has forgotten much of his past. Wearing stiff, rusty armor and carrying a sword, he joins Dorothy and the Cowardly Lion on the Quest for the Scarecrow after Dorothy declares him her knight. During the escape from Pokes, he sings an old English ballad and helps Dorothy when she tires, but is dragged over cobbles and left with badly battered armor. Though he thinks he has failed, Dorothy encourages him. He remains loyal to her colors and follows her through danger, even when his sword breaks against Bangladore or when he feels an easy victory is not a proper fight. As the journey continues, Sir Hokus grows especially concerned for the Scarecrow, helps the travelers in Fix City, helps buy a road, and is rescued from drowning by the Cowardly Lion after falling into a stream in heavy armor. In gratitude he knights the Lion. He cheers the company with songs, serves as the Comfortable Camel’s Karwan Bashi, and is treated as family by both camels. He is briefly transformed into a raven, then resumes his knightly role. He offers his sword and lance to the Scarecrow, supports the Scarecrow’s wish to remain himself, helps the party through the beanstalk adventure, kills the rheumatic dragon, and assists in restoring order on the Silver Island. Returning toward Oz, he cuts through the earth above the beanstalk. After eating a red bean, a branch grows from his shoulders and lifts him into the air, but Dorothy and the Scarecrow bring him safely to Oz, where the branch breaks away. He ends welcomed as Dorothy’s Knight Errant, promising to fight for Dorothy, Ozma, and Oz.

CHAPTER 5

Sir Hokus is a gentle, melancholy knight who has slept in Pokes for centuries and has forgotten much of his past. He explains that a mysterious knight cursed him to live in Pokes, then joins Dorothy and the Cowardly Lion on the Quest for the Scarecrow as Dorothy's true knight.

CHAPTER 6

Sir Hokus sings an old English ballad while escaping from Pokes and pulls Dorothy along when she begins to tire. He is knocked down and dragged over the cobbles after the Cowardly Lion ties a rope around him, leaving his armor dented, scraped, and squashed in back. He believes he has failed, but Dorothy encourages him, and his old-fashioned speech begins returning.

CHAPTER 11

Sir Hokus expects a great adventure and attacks Bangladore with his sword, which breaks at the handle. He refuses to run because he is wearing Dorothy's colors, demands an apology for Bangladore's song, and later feels that defeating a candy giant was not a proper fight.

CHAPTER 12

Sir Hokus becomes increasingly fond of the Scarecrow and worries that he may be in danger. He helps Dorothy in Fix City, speaks with King Fix It, obtains dinner and sleeping arrangements for the travelers, and asks whether the city has seen the Scarecrow.

CHAPTER 13

Sir Hokus helps choose and purchase a five-mile road to take the travelers toward the Emerald City. He falls into the stream in his heavy armor and is rescued by the Cowardly Lion, whom he knights in gratitude.

CHAPTER 16

Sir Hokus travels with Dorothy and the Cowardly Lion and cheers them by singing during their difficult journey. He nearly steps onto the Deadly Desert, is saved by Dorothy's warning, helps unload food and cushions from the camels, and becomes the Comfortable Camel's Karwan Bashi.

CHAPTER 17

Sir Hokus is thrown from his seat by the Comfortable Camel's sobs and is transformed into a raven. Dorothy wishes him back, and he later suggests that a proper wish might help the group before Dorothy stops everyone from speaking.

CHAPTER 18

Sir Hokus of Pokes initially mistakes Tappy for an enemy and draws his sword, but learns that he is a friend. He pledges his sword and lance to the Scarecrow, supports the Scarecrow's choice to remain himself, and helps Dorothy begin climbing the bean pole.

CHAPTER 19

Sir Hokus follows the others down from the beanstalk and helps gather the group at its foot. He wakes the Doubtful Dromedary, brings it across the hall, calls the Comfortable Camel to the throne, and sits with Happy Toko near the throne. He proposes sticking together when the footsteps are heard.

CHAPTER 20

Sir Hokus reacts furiously when he sees the rheumatic dragon and kills it with his sword, an act that fills the hall with smoke and confusion. He remains brave despite the angry crowd and later claps his iron gauntlets to call for silence at the Scarecrow's request.

CHAPTER 21

Sir Hokus proposes Happy Toko as the new ruler of the Silver Island and offers to help him with a trusty sword if needed. He helps prepare the party for departure and is tied to the magic parasol.

CHAPTER 23

Sir Hokus cuts an opening through the earth above the beanstalk and helps the party return to Oz. He intends to remain in Oz with Dorothy, but after eating a red bean, a green branch grows from his shoulders and carries him high into the air.

CHAPTER 24

Sir Hokus grows upward after eating a red bean from the beanstalk, which becomes rooted in his back. Dorothy and the Scarecrow bring him to Oz, where the beanstalk snaps off and disappears, freeing him. He is welcomed as Dorothy's Knight Errant and promises to fight for Dorothy, Ozma, and Oz.

Yellow Knight in The Yellow Knight of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1930 · the work’s page · read it where Yellow Knight first appears

Abstract written by a model

Yellow Knight is a strange, young and hearty knight in gold armor, the King of Corumbia’s missing son and a central figure in the announced tale. He rides Stampedro, carries and swings a battle-axe, and continually seeks adventure. He promises to return to Ploppa and pursues the Comfortable Camel, the King of the Golden City, and that King’s subjects. While imprisoned, he escapes through a window onto Ploppa’s shell, resolved to save a maiden, serve a monarch, destroy a monster, and continue his quest. He later charges into the Sultan’s throne room, demands the Camel and information, knocks down two spearmen, and escapes. Others seek him and suspect magic dates caused his enchantment. Absent when requested by the King, he is to be restored by morning through the Queen’s final date enchantment; the tale also announces his return and a later matter involving a princess’s hand.
