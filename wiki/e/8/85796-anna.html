Anna in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Anna is Dido’s sister, confidante, and intermediary with Aeneas. She first urges Dido to accept love and an alliance with Aeneas, hoping it will strengthen Carthage. When Aeneas plans to leave, Anna carries Dido’s plea and adds her own tears, but fails to change him. Believing Dido’s rites will end love, she helps build the funeral pile, then mourns and tends Dido after her self-inflicted wound.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK IV

Anna listens to Dido's confession and urges her not to waste her life in grief or remain without a husband and children. She argues that an alliance with Aeneas would strengthen Carthage, and her counsel encourages Dido to pursue the relationship.

The pious prince was seiz’d with sudden fear;

Anna receives Dido’s request to approach Aeneas and ask him to delay his departure. She carries Dido’s mournful message and adds her own tears, but Aeneas refuses her appeals.

The wretched queen, pursued by cruel fate,

Anna believes Dido’s rites are intended to end her love rather than her life and helps construct the funeral pile. After Dido wounds herself, Anna rushes to her, embraces her, tries to staunch the blood, and laments that Dido has destroyed herself and her city.
