Egyptian religion

topic · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Egyptian religion and worship, Egyptian worship and mythology, adoration of the gods, divine worship, piety towards the gods, religion of the Egyptians, worship of the gods

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Egyptian religion and worship is described through offerings to the gods, temple building, reverence for sacred animals, sacrifices using agrostis, and the teachings of Menis. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Egyptian religion is described as a religion whose theology varied across periods and schools, centering on the worship of Ra and Osiris, divine triads, and gods in increasingly human forms.

Egyptian religion in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Egyptian religion first appears

Abstract written by a model

Egyptian religion is a religion whose theology varied across periods and schools. It was interpreted as polytheistic local groups, manifestations of one divine being, or pantheism. It centers on worship of Ra and Osiris, divine triads, and gods in increasingly human forms.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXVII

Egyptian religion is described as having been interpreted either as an extended polytheism of local groups or as manifestations of one divine being and a form of pantheism. Its theology changed across times and schools, while solar worship, Osiris worship, divine triads, and increasingly human forms of the gods remained important themes.
