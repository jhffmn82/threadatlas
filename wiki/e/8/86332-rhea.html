  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Cybele is the Mother of the Mountains, a Phrygian goddess who was nursed by wild beasts, invented musical instruments, treated diseases, bore Corybas, brought sacred mysteries to Asia, and was later worshiped by the Phrygians.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Rhea is the daughter of Coelus, sister and wife of Saturn, former wife of Ammon, mother and savior of Jupiter, and a beloved foster mother to Dionysius.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the great mother of the gods is the source of sacred mysteries taught by Electra to Jasion and brought into Asia by Dardanus, Cybele, and Corybas.
  • In The Argonautica, the Mother of the gods is the universal divine mother honored on Dindymum, and Jason and the heroes build her image and altar and sacrifice to end a storm.
  • In The Argonautica, Rhea is identified with the Mother of the gods and is invoked as the venerable mother of Dindymum; the heroes sacrifice to her, after which the winds cease and nature flourishes.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, the Mother of the Gods is celebrated as the mother of all gods and men, delighting in rattles, timbrels, flutes, wolves, lions, hills, and wooded valleys.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Rhea bears Cronos's children, saves Zeus from him with the aid of Earth and Heaven, conceals Zeus's birth in Crete, and serves as his messenger.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Cybele is the Phrygian holy Mother of the Gods who punishes Hippomenes and Atalanta by transforming them into lions and protects Aeneas's pine-built ships by turning them into sea Nymphs.
  • In The Library, Rhea, a Titanide and Cronus's sister-wife, bears their children, flees to Crete while pregnant, gives birth to Zeus in Dicte's cave, and deceives Cronus with a wrapped stone.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Rhea protects Zeus from Cronos, bears him on Mount Lycæeus, and deceives Cronos with a swaddled stone after substituting a foal for Poseidon.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the Mother of the Gods is represented by several statues and honored as the Dindymene Mother at Patrae in a stone temple.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Cybele, also called Rhea and Ops, is the wife of Cronos and mother of Zeus, attended by lions, and transforms Atalanta and Hippomenes into lions.

Cybele in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Cybele is a Phrygian goddess, daughter of Mæon according to Diodorus Siculus, and the holy Mother of the Gods. Associated with pine trees, Attis, and worship in Phrygia, she punishes Hippomenes and Atalanta for defiling her shrine by making them lions to draw her chariot. She later protects Aeneas’s pine-built ships from Turnus’s fire, transforming them into sea Nymphs.

BOOK THE TENTH.

Cybele is the Mother of the Gods and is connected with the pine tree. The explanation describes several traditions about her love for Attis and the development of her worship in Phrygia.

FABLE IX. [X.519-707]

Cybele has a temple and shrine that Hippomenes and Atalanta defile with their embraces. In response, she transforms them into lions, who thereafter draw her chariot.

FABLES VII. AND VIII. [XIV.441-526]

Cybele, called the holy Mother of the Gods, remembers that Aeneas’s ships were made from pines cut on Mount Ida. She prevents Turnus’s fire from destroying them and transforms the fleet into sea Nymphs.

While pregnant, she comes to Thaumasium, receives protection from Hoplodamus and other Giants, and bears Zeus on Mount Lycæeus. After Nymphs purify her, she deceives Cronos by presenting him a swaddled stone as the newborn, as she had substituted a foal for Poseidon.

CHAPTER XXXVI.

According to the people of Methydrium, Rhea came to Mount Thaumasium while pregnant with Zeus and received protection from Hoplodamus and the other Giants in case Cronos attacked her. They say she bore Zeus on part of Mount Lycæeus, but that the deception of Cronos and the offering of a stone instead of the child took place at Thaumasium. Rhea’s Cave is on the mountain, and only women sacred to her may enter it.

Cybele in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Cybele is a goddess, also called Rhea and Ops, wife of Cronos and mother of Zeus; attended and depicted with lions, she transforms Atalanta and Hippomenes into lioness and lion after they fail to honor Venus.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

MEDEA AND AESON

Cybele is offended when Atalanta and Hippomenes fail to honor Venus and transforms them into a lioness and a lion. She is identified with Rhea and Ops, is shown with lions, and is sometimes depicted in a chariot drawn by lions.
