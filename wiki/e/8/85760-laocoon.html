Laocoon

person · 4 works · 9 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Neptune’s priest, his blind eyes, old man, priest of Neptune, the presumptuous, the priest, the wretched father, their friend, Λαοκόωντος

Abstract written by a model

In The Aeneid, Laocoon, Neptune’s priest, warns the Trojans that the wooden horse threatens their city and is killed with his children by two serpents while sacrificing a steer, persuading the Trojans that Minerva punished him. In The Library, Laocoon is described as a seer who warns that the Wooden Horse contains an armed force; Apollo sends two serpents from nearby islands, and they devour Laocoon’s sons. In The Fall of Troy, Laocoon is a Trojan father and opponent of the Wooden Horse who recognizes it as deadly fraud, urges the Trojans to burn and inspect it, continues warning them after Athena blinds him, and mourns alone after serpents kill his sons. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Laocoon, a priest of Neptune, warns the Trojans against the Wooden Horse and strikes it; serpents strangle him and his sons, leading the Trojans to misread the attack as approval.

Laocoon in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · about AD 200 to 299 · the work’s page · read it where Laocoon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Laocoon is a Trojan father and opponent of the Wooden Horse. Recognizing it as deadly fraud, he urges the Trojans to burn and inspect it. Athena blinds him, yet he continues warning them. When serpents come, the fleeing Trojans leave him with his sons; unable to save them, he mourns their deaths alone.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XII

Laocoon recognizes the Wooden Horse as a deadly fraud and urges the Trojans to burn it and search its timbers. Athena strikes him blind after he opposes the Horse, but he continues warning the Trojans, who still refuse to listen.

But then a yet more hideous punishment

Laocoon is left alone with his sons when the Trojans flee from the serpents. He is unable to save them as they are killed, and he is described as blind while mourning over their empty tomb.
