Neleus

person · 4 works · 19 quoted facts · 10 connections
Also called: Νηλέα, Νηλέως, Νηλεὺς, Νηλεῖ
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Neleus was the son of Tyro and Neptune who entered Messenia, founded Pylos, married Chloris, and fathered twelve sons including Nestor.
  • In The Library, Neleus was the son of Poseidon and father of Pero who promised Pero to whoever recovered Phylacus’s kine; Melampus brought the kine to Pylus as her bridal gift.
  • In The Library, Neleus was Tyro’s son and Pelias’s twin; after being exposed, rescued, and raised by horse-keepers, he helped kill Sidero, quarreled with Pelias, was banished, founded and ruled Pylus in Messene, married Chloris, fathered children, and was killed by Hercules at Pylus.
  • In Description of Greece, Neleus was the son of Cretheus who fled Pelias at Iolcus, received Aphareus’ maritime Messenian lands including Pylos, established his government there, expelled Pylos with the Pelasgians of Iolcus, brought Pylos renown, and possessed oxen descended from Iphiclus’ herd.

Neleus, Tyro’s son and Pelias’s twin, was exposed, rescued, and raised by horse-keepers. After helping kill Sidero, he quarreled with Pelias and was banished, then lived in Messene, founded and ruled Pylus, married Chloris, fathered children, and was killed by Hercules at Pylus.

Neleus was exposed with Pelias, rescued by a horse-keeper, and raised with him. After recognizing Tyro, he helped kill their stepmother Sidero, but later quarreled with Pelias and was banished. He went to Messene, founded Pylus, married Chloris, and fathered Pero, Nestor, and other children.

Neleus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Neleus, son of Cretheus, fled Pelias at Iolcus, received Aphareus’ maritime Messenian lands including Pylos, and established his government there. With the Pelasgians of Iolcus he expelled Pylos, brought Pylos renown, and possessed oxen descended from Iphiclus’ herd.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK IV.--MESSENIA.

Neleus, the son of Cretheus, fled from Pelias at Iolcus and was received into Aphareus’ house. Aphareus gave him the maritime parts of Messenia, including Pylos, where Neleus established his seat of government.

CHAPTER XXXIV.

Neleus and the Pelasgians of Iolcus expelled Pylos, the son of Cleson, from Pylos. Neleus later advanced Pylos to such renown that Homer called it the city of Neleus. He was said to have possessed oxen descended from the herd of Iphiclus.
