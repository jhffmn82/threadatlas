the bean pole in The Royal Book of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1921

The bean pole is a magic beanstalk, also called the Scarecrow's family tree, growing from a crocus and reaching from the Silver Islands toward Oz. It is the Scarecrow's only claimed family tree and the support from which he first descended. When he digs beside it, he slides down its dark passage, clinging to it past the Middlings' kingdom until he lands in the silver palace. After he touches it on the other side of the world, Chang Wang Woe's spirit enters him and brings him to the Silver Islands. Bound to the tall pole there, the group considers its notches a climbable route home, though the Scarecrow judges descent too difficult and dangerous. It also helps guide Dorothy's parasol through the passage and gives her something to grasp during a fall. Finally, the party returns to Oz by climbing it, and its upper opening is covered.

Narrative, section by section

7 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 2

The bean pole is the Scarecrow's only claimed family tree and the support from which he was first descended. When the Scarecrow digs beside it, the ground gives way and he discovers that he is sliding down it.

CHAPTER 3

The bean pole carries the Scarecrow downward through a dark passageway and past the Middlings' kingdom. The Scarecrow clings to it throughout his fall and is still holding it when he lands in the silver palace.

CHAPTER 7

The magic beanstalk grew from the crocus planted by Tsing Tsing and extended from the palace into the clouds. The Scarecrow touched it on the other side of the world, causing the spirit of Chang Wang Woe to enter him and bringing him to the Silver Islands.

CHAPTER 18

The Scarecrow's family tree is the tall bean pole on Silver Island to which he had been bound. Because it reaches toward Oz and has handy notches, the group plans to climb it as a route home.

CHAPTER 19

The beanstalk is the route the group has used to reach the hall, but the Scarecrow decides that descending it would be too difficult and dangerous. He takes a parasol from the beanstalk and later chooses an escape plan that does not depend on climbing down it.

CHAPTER 22

The bean pole helps Dorothy keep the parasol centered in the dark passage. Dorothy also grabs it when the parasol crashes and the party begins to fall.

CHAPTER 23

The beanstalk serves as the route by which the party returns to Oz. Sir Hokus climbs ahead and cuts through the earth covering its upper opening, after which the opening is covered with boards, dirt, and cornstalks.
