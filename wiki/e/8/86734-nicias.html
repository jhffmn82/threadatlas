Nicias

person · 1 work · 13 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Nicias the general, Nicias the son of Niccratus, Nicies, Nieias, Nleias

Abstract written by a model

Nicias is an Athenian admiral and general whose record centers on military command and his conflicted role in the Sicilian expedition. He led naval expeditions against Melos, Boeotia, Corinth, Cythera, and the Peloponnesian coast; won engagements against Corinthians; captured Cremmyon, Cythera, and Thyrea; fortified Methone; and later helped take Menda before besieging Scione. Though he opposed the expedition to Sicily from the beginning, warning that Athens could not safely fight Sparta while seeking Sicily, he was appointed one of three generals and commanded the siege of Syracuse. After Alcibiades fled and Lamachus was killed, he remained in command, requested reinforcements, and initially resisted abandoning the siege. He accepted retreat only later, then, being naturally superstitious, delayed after a lunar eclipse on soothsayers' advice. He led one division in the retreat, was captured with Demosthenes after the army's defeat, and was executed with the other generals.

Nicias is in one work on this site, The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Nicias was sent with Nicostratus and fifty ships against Scione. He first helped take Menda and then besieged Scione by surrounding it with a wall and making continual assaults.

Nicias commanded Athenian operations that captured Cithera and Nicaea and stormed Melus. He opposed the expedition to Sicily, warning that Athens could not safely fight Sparta while attempting to conquer Sicily, but he was appointed one of its three generals.
