Garcia in His Last Bow

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1908-04-17

Garcia is a young, good-looking Spaniard of Spanish descent who lives at Wisteria Lodge and is understood by Scott Eccles to have some connection with the Spanish embassy. Quick-witted and apparently involved in a criminal enterprise, he suddenly cultivates Eccles's friendship and brings him to Wisteria Lodge so that Eccles's presence can provide an alibi for an action planned that night. Garcia is also the son of San Pedro’s former highest dignitary and an avenger in a revenge society seeking vengeance on Don Murillo. He waits near High Gable with two companions to attack Murillo, but Murillo's men discover the plan. Garcia is killed before it succeeds, his companions disappear, and his body is found on Oxshott Common, violently beaten about the head and apparently not robbed.

The Adventure of Wisteria Lodge / 1. The Singular Experience of Mr. John Scott Eccles

Aloysius Garcia is described by John Scott Eccles as a young, good-looking man of Spanish descent who was connected in some way with the embassy. He is found dead on Oxshott Common after being beaten to death, apparently without being robbed.

Garcia is identified as the young Spaniard who cultivated Scott Eccles's friendship and brought him to Wisteria Lodge. Holmes suspects Garcia planned an action before one o'clock and relied on Eccles's testimony to show that he had remained at home.

2. The Tiger of San Pedro

Mr. Garcia is identified as the deceased man connected with Wisteria Lodge. He was found dead on Oxshott Common, and the newspaper states that his body showed signs of extreme violence.

Garcia arranged for Scott Eccles to be present in order to establish an alibi, indicating that he had a criminal enterprise planned for the night of his death. Holmes believes Garcia received a note from a confederate at High Gable and was killed by the person against whom his enterprise was directed.
