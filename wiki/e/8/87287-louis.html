Louis in Gallegher and Other Stories

Richard Harding Davis · 1890

Louis is a handsome young Frenchman, husband, apprentice to old Carbut in baking, and owner of a third of Carbut's bakery store. Though not usually a gambler, he plays dominoes at cafes and, at Monte Carlo, wins money at roulette, including 20,000 francs. Excited by repeated bets on red, he risks his winnings hoping to buy another third of the store. Cecil stops his final all-in gamble; Louis leaves for Paris with his wife, grateful, humble, and resolved never to return to Monte Carlo.

Louis is the woman's husband, an apprentice to old Carbut the baker and a part-owner of the bakery store. Though he is not usually a gambler, he has become excited after winning money at the tables and continues to risk it, hoping to buy another third of the store.

The Frenchman repeatedly bets his winnings on red and becomes increasingly excited when he wins. After Cecil prevents him from risking everything in one final coup, he leaves for Paris with his wife, becomes grateful and humble, and promises never to return to Monte Carlo.
