Thermopylae in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Thermopylae is a strategic Greek passage of guarded straits and passes. Leonidas and Greek forces, including Lacedaemonians and Thespians, occupied it to hold Xerxes’ forces, repelling repeated attacks. After a local guide revealed a route around them, the defenders refused to abandon their post and were destroyed; the Persians gained the passes at great cost, discouraging the Grecians.

Thermopylæ in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Thermopylæ is a narrow pass defended by Leonidas and 300 Lacedæmonians against Xerxes’ army. Greek volunteers under Callippus tried to block the Galati, but they used Ephialtes’ route, routed the Phocians, entered Greece, and Athenians suffered near the Lamiac Gulf; Hydarnes likewise flanked Leonidas’ position.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Description of Greece / BOOK I.--ATTICA.

The Athenians and other Greek volunteers tried to block the Galati at Thermopylæ under Callippus. The Galati discovered the pass previously used by Ephialtes, routed the Phocians stationed there, and entered Greece, while Athenian forces suffered heavily in the muddy waters near the Lamiac Gulf.

BOOK III.--LACONIA.

Thermopylæ was the narrow pass where Leonidas and his 300 Lacedæmonians resisted Xerxes. Their position could have prevented the barbarians from entering Greece had a Trachinian not led Hydarnes’ army around the flank.

Thermopylæ in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Thermopylæ is a strategic Greek pass and entry route into Greece, defended by Phocians through the mountain. It hosted Greek forces during the Persian and Galatian invasions. Leonidas fought there without the Achæans in the Persian invasion; later, Greeks gathered there in greater numbers against Brennus and the Galatians. Its narrow, smooth, rocky ground and streams hindered cavalry and helped cause Galatian losses. When Brennus crossed Mount Œta to the Greek rear, Athenians evacuated troops by triremes and the contingents dispersed.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

The Achæans did not join Leonidas at the pass of Thermopylæ during the invasion of Greece by Xerxes and the Medes. The pass was later overlooked by the Peloponnesians and the Achæans when the Galati invaded, because they believed the Isthmus of Corinth was sufficiently fortified.

CHAPTER XX.

Thermopylæ is the gathering place of the Greek forces opposing the Galatians. The text compares the much smaller Greek force that fought there during the Persian invasion with the larger allied force assembled there in the present conflict.

CHAPTER XXI.

Thermopylæ was the site where a strong force from the Greek cities confronted Brennus and the Galati. Its narrow, smooth, rocky ground and mountain streams made cavalry ineffective and contributed to the Galati's defeat and losses during their retreat.

CHAPTER XXII.

Thermopylæ was the main Greek defensive position when the Galati expedition returned from Ætolia. Brennus's passage over Mount Œta brought his forces to the Greek rear, after which the Athenians took the Greek troops aboard triremes and the contingents dispersed.
