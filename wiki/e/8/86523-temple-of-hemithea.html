temple of Hemithea in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The temple of Hemithea at Castabus became famous, drawing inhabitants and strangers with sacrifices and gifts. Protected by religious devotion without guards or walls, it was said to escape Persians and thieves; its mysteries used water and honey offerings and excluded those touched by or eating swine.

The temple of Hemithea at Castabus became famous and attracted inhabitants and strangers with sacrifices and gifts. It was said to have been spared by the Persians when they destroyed other temples and by thieves, and its mysteries included offerings of water and honey while excluding anyone who had touched or eaten swine.
