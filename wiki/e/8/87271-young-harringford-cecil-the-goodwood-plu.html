Young Harringford (Cecil, the Goodwood Plunger) in Gallegher and Other Stories

Richard Harding Davis · 1890

Young Harringford, Cecil, is a young man known publicly as the Goodwood Plunger or The Plunger. Eldest son of the Harringford family, he is estranged from and effectively disinherited by his father after gambling losses, debts, and disgrace, though they still love one another. Loved by the girl from Surrey and attended by the longtime servant Walters, Cecil reaches Monte Carlo in worsening mental and physical distress. Having lost his fortune and unable to cover his debts, he seeks a final second chance through the Grand Prix and the English horse; if it fails, he plans suicide in Monte Carlo, away from home. There he is regarded as fortunate and rich by a distressed woman, who asks him to stop her husband Louis from gambling. Cecil learns Louis has won money but is risking it, intervenes, divides the winnings for separate bets, takes the money from the table, and places it in the woman's skirt. He sends the couple to Paris, abandons his suicidal course, and plans to return to England by the first available train with Walters.

Young Harringford, whose name is Cecil, comes to Monte Carlo alone and disinherited after losing his fortune through gambling. Once celebrated as the “Goodwood Plunger,” he now intends to kill himself and tears up his father’s letter instead of returning home.

The Goodwood Plunger is approached by a distressed French woman who asks him to persuade Louis to stop gambling. He is initially disoriented and misunderstands her, but eventually understands that Louis has won money and is risking it, and asks her to point Louis out.

Cecil Harringford stops the Frenchman from continuing to gamble and divides the winnings so that the Frenchman, his wife, and Cecil can place separate bets.
