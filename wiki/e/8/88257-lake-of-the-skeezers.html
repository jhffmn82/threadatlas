lake in Glinda of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1920

The lake is a body of water in the Skeezer country, of unknown size, surrounding and containing the sunken island with a glass side visible from the Dome. Coo-ee-oh casts three transformed Adepts into it as fishes; the Flatheads try to catch one, and Rora tries to poison them. Queen Coo-ee-oh falls beneath its surface, becomes a Diamond Swan, and swims beyond the Flatheads’ weapons. When the island rises, the lake’s level lowers and the bridge reaches its shore.

Chapter Nine

The lake is where Coo-ee-oh cast the three transformed fishes after changing them from the Adepts. The Flatheads came to it to catch one of the fishes, and Rora later tried to poison all the fishes in it.

Chapter Eleven

The lake receives Queen Coo-ee-oh when she falls beneath its surface. After becoming a Diamond Swan, she swims across it to a place beyond the reach of the Flatheads' arrows and spears.

Chapter Fourteen

The lake in the Skeezer country contains the sunken island. Its size is unknown, and Scraps jokingly suggests pumping its water out.

Chapter Twenty-Three

The lake surrounds the submerged island and has a glass side visible from the Dome. When the island rises, the level of the water is lowered, and the bridge reaches the shore of the lake.

Lake of the Skeezers in Glinda of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1920

The Lake of the Skeezers is a beautiful, blue, sparkling lake about a mile from shore to shore, believed by Ozma and Dorothy to lie beyond the palm trees near the flat mountain. Owned by the Skeezers, it contains their island, called Magic Isle, at its center; the lake surrounds and, when the island submerges, covers it and the Great Dome beneath it. Its clear waters hold seaweed, colorful water foliage, shellfish, and many fishes, which the Skeezers claim and protect. When the Flatheads seek to fish there, Ozma rules that both lake and fish belong to the Skeezers. The Flatheads later come to poison the fishes. The lake is also the home of three enchanted gold, silver, and bronze fishes: the transformed Adepts of Magic, changed by Queen Coo-ee-oh. It holds a drifting iron boat, supplies water for a copper kettle, and is crossed above and below by an enchanted submarine during the rescue. At the end, Glinda, the Wizard, and the restored Adepts chant an incantation that makes the water recede, exposing the Dome while leaving enough water for the fishes until the island can be raised.

Narrative, section by section

11 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Four

The Lake of the Skeezers is believed by Ozma and Dorothy to lie just beyond the line of palm trees near the flat mountain.

Chapter Six

The Lake of the Skeezers contains fish that the Skeezers claim belong to them and place under their protection. The Flatheads want to fish there, but Ozma rules that the lake and its fish belong to the Skeezers.

Chapter Seven

The Lake of the Skeezers is a beautiful body of water about a mile from shore to shore, with blue, sparkling waves. A lovely island lies at its center, but no boats or signs of life are visible on either shore.

Chapter Ten

The lake surrounds the island and rises over the Great Dome when the island submerges. Its clear water reveals fish, swaying seaweed, the dome, and the distant shore.

Chapter Twelve

The Lake of the Skeezers contains the submerged Skeezers' island and is filled with many kinds of fishes, shellfish, and colorful water foliage. Dorothy watches its clear waters in hopes of finding the gold, silver, and bronze fishes.

Chapter Thirteen

The lake surrounds and covers the submerged Magic Isle of the Skeezers. The Flatheads come to it to poison the fishes, and the transformed Queen Coo-ee-oh and the three fishes made from the Adepts of Magic are associated with it.

Chapter Sixteen

The lake holds the drifting iron boat and is where the three transformed Adepts have lived ever since Queen Coo-ee-oh changed them. Its water is used to clean and fill the copper kettle, and its shore is where the boat comes to rest.

Chapter Seventeen

The Lake of the Skeezers contains the submerged Magic Isle and the Great Dome. It also contains many fish, including three enchanted fishes who were formerly Adepts at Magic.

Chapter Twenty

The lake surrounds the sunken island and the Great Dome. The enchanted submarine crosses it on the surface, travels beneath it, and later circles the Great Dome at the level of its glass covering.

Chapter Twenty-One

The lake contains the fishes, including the three Adepts while they were transformed, and is the home of the fishes. The rescuers consider lowering its level rather than drying it up completely, so the Great Dome can emerge while water remains for the fishes.

Chapter Twenty-Two

The lake recedes from the shore when Glinda, the Wizard, and the three Adepts chant their incantation. Its falling water exposes the Dome, though enough water remains for the fish until the island can be raised.
