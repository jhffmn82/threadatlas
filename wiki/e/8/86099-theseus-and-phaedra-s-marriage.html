Theseus and Phaedra’s marriage in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Theseus and Phaedra’s marriage is a marital covenant threatened by Phaedra’s love. Though she says she would not break it or stain her reputation, she claims Theseus neglects it and urges a disguised union with Hippolytus.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

IV

Phaedra says that she would not break her marriage bond through wanton baseness and that her reputation has been free from reproach. She argues that Theseus neglects their marriage and urges Hippolytus to disregard the names of stepmother and stepson. She proposes that their union could remain openly disguised as a family relationship because they already share one household.
