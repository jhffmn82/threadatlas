Comfortable Camel in The Royal Book of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1921

The Comfortable Camel is a comfortable, happy camel of Oz and friend of the Doubtful Dromedary. Separated from its caravan and absent Karwan Bashi by a sandstorm, it arrives carrying meal supplies, cushions, a tent, and other goods, feeds the travelers, and accepts Sir Hokus as Karwan Bashi. When the Dromedary disappears, it cries, causing Sir Hokus to fall, then melts into air; the Cowardly Lion wishes it back, reuniting it with both companions. Returning to Oz, it is welcomed as family, grieves when the others vanish upward, and is blown toward the Emerald City with the Lion and Dromedary.

CHAPTER 16

The Comfortable Camel arrives with the Doubtful Dromedary after a sandstorm separates them from their caravan and Karwan Bashi. It carries food, cushions, a tent, and other goods, feeds the travelers, and allows Sir Hokus to become its Karwan Bashi.

CHAPTER 17

The Comfortable Camel cries when the Doubtful Dromedary disappears, causing Sir Hokus to fall from his seat. It then melts into air, but the Cowardly Lion wishes it back, and it is reunited with Sir Hokus and the Doubtful Dromedary.

CHAPTER 23

The Comfortable Camel returns to Oz with the group and is welcomed by Sir Hokus as part of the family. He becomes distressed when Sir Hokus and the others disappear upward, but is ultimately blown toward the Emerald City by a cyclone with the Cowardly Lion and Doubtful Dromedary.

Comfortable Camel in The Yellow Knight of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1930

The Comfortable Camel, also called Camy, is a camel and Sir Hokus's traveling mount and steed. After being found near the Deadly Desert, he lives in Emerald City, can run as fast as a horse in Oz, and remains friendly with Peter Pun despite Peter's troublesome behavior. He helps Sir Hokus investigate a deserted castle and bewitched forest, eating leaves, seeking breakfast, and warning that they should leave. He later warns Hokus about a magic date, then is lassoed by slaves from Tuzzle's boat, dragged into the river, and carried away on a royal barge. Said to be stolen by the fat owner of a yellow boat, he is pursued and sought from the Sultan. By the end, he is again with the companions, distressed by Hokus's transformation and carrying Speedy and Marygolden after the Yellow Knight.

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER TEN

The Comfortable Camel helps Sir Hokus investigate the deserted castle and forest, while hoping to find breakfast and eating leaves from a young tree. He sleeps beside the stream while Sir Hokus discovers the jester, then agrees that the forest is bewitched and urges them to leave before they are transformed.

CHAPTER ELEVEN

The Comfortable Camel warns Sir Hokus that the magic date might cause him to change and complains about Peter Pun's behavior. While traveling beside the river, he is suddenly lassoed around the neck by slaves from Tuzzle's boat and dragged into the water. He is carried away from Sir Hokus and Peter aboard the royal barge.

CHAPTER FIFTEEN

The Comfortable Camel was stolen by the fat owner of the yellow boat. Sir Hokus and Peter Pun are pursuing the thief, and Speedy concludes that the Camel's saddle sack contained the magic dates connected with the enchantments.

CHAPTER SIXTEEN

Sir Hokus demands that the Sultan restore the Comfortable Camel, whom he believes the Sultan has stolen. The Comfortable Camel is not recovered or otherwise accounted for in this unit.

CHAPTER EIGHTEEN

The Comfortable Camel is distressed when Sir Hokus becomes the Yellow Knight and forgets his companions. He carries Speedy and Marygolden in pursuit of the Knight and urges them to use the remaining dates to restore him and the enchanted people.

the Comfortable Camel in The Yellow Knight of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1930

The Comfortable Camel, also called Camy, is a valuable, beautiful camel and beast of burden from Samandra, formerly the Sultan’s favorite. Lost in a sand storm for ten years, he is believed to be the missing camel from the Sultan’s royal herd. Sir Hokus brings him to the Emerald City of Oz, where he is kept in Queen Ozma’s Royal Stable, though his stall later stands empty. Loyal to Sir Hokus and intending to join his quest, Camy secretly follows him from the capital with saddle sacks containing supplies, including a tent and a package, as well as shawls and other comforts. He finds Sir Hokus by hearing his battle song, is frightened when a field flies through the sky, and is thrown against a tree when it descends. Tuzzle captures Camy and returns him to Samandra. Unable to speak there, Camy is brought before the Sultan and tied to an iron ring in the Royal Camel Quarters. Remembering his difficult former life, he realizes the Sultan wants missing dates rather than him and hopes Sir Hokus and Peter will rescue him. Sir Hokus finds and frees him, and Camy carries him to the enchanted forest, joins the restoration celebration, and spends the night with Stampedro.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER FIVE

The camel was the Sultan's favorite and is described as an exceptionally valuable and beautiful beast. It was lost in a sand storm, and Chinda reports that it is now in the Royal Stable of Queen Ozma of Oz in the Emerald City.

CHAPTER SIX

The Comfortable Camel was brought to the Emerald City by Sir Hokus and is believed to be the camel lost from the Sultan of Samandra's royal herd. It is missing from the Royal Stables, and neither the stable staff nor the Doubtful Dromedary knows where it has gone.

CHAPTER NINE

Camy follows Sir Hokus from the capital, traveling around the swamp and carrying trappings, saddle sacks, a tent, shawls, and other comforts. He finds Sir Hokus after hearing his battle song and joins him on the journey. Camy is frightened when the field flies through the sky and is thrown against a tree when it returns toward the ground.

CHAPTER TWELVE

Camy, the Comfortable Camel, recognizes Tuzzle when he is captured and remembers his difficult former life in Samandra. He cannot speak in Samandra, is brought before the Sultan, and is tied in the Royal Camel Quarters, where he regains his good judgment and temper while eating peas and rice. Camy realizes that the Sultan wants the missing dates rather than him and hopes Sir Hokus and Peter will come to his aid.

CHAPTER SEVENTEEN

Camy is found and freed by Sir Hokus in the Sultan's Camel Quarters. He carries Sir Hokus to the enchanted forest, joins the restoration celebration, and spends the night in the courtyard with Stampedro.
