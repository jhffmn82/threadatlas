Cydippe and Acontius's marriage in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Cydippe and Acontius’s marriage is a promised but unfulfilled union. Acontius seeks marriage and pledged fidelity, claiming their oath binds Cydippe to him as her destined husband. As the marriage approaches, Cydippe’s illness repeatedly delays it; she questions whether her words were a deliberate promise that grants him marital rights.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

XX

Acontius asks Cydippe for marriage and the pledged marital fidelity, maintaining that he loves her as her destined husband. The marriage has not yet taken place, and its timing is repeatedly delayed by Cydippe's illness. Acontius treats the oath as a betrothal bond that should lead to their union.

XXI

Cydippe's marriage is approaching, but her illness threatens her at the very time appointed for it. She argues that if she truly promised marriage, Acontius may claim the rights of the promised marriage, but if she gave only words without deliberate intent, he holds those words in vain.
