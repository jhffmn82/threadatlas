Corinthian War

event · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Coriathiaa war, Corinthian tvar, Corinthian war, Corinthian war, as it is called, the Corinthian war
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Corinthian war is described as a conflict between Corinth and Corcyra arising over Epidamnus and as an eight-year struggle involving the Lacedaemonians, Boeotians, Athenians, Argives, and Corinthians.
  • In Description of Greece, the Corinthian War is described as beginning with the Lacedæmonian invasion of Bœotia, compelling Agesilaus to return from Asia Minor, and ending with his victory over the Thebans and allies at Coronea.

Corinthian war in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

A war between Corinth and Corcyra arising from conflict over Epidamnus; as both prepared larger fleets and Athens joined Corcyra, it contributed to Potidea's revolt. Another account places fighting around Corinth in an eight-year struggle involving Lacedaemonians, Boeotians, Athenians, Argives, and Corinthians.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. VI.

The Corinthian war arose from the conflict over Epidamnus between Corinth and Corcyra. Corinth and Corcyra later prepared larger fleets, while Athens joined Corcyra and the conflict contributed to the revolt of Potidea.

CHAP. IX,

The Corinthian War arose from the fighting around Corinth and lasted eight years. It involved the Lacedaemonians, their exiles and confederates, and opposing forces from the Boeotians, Athenians, Argives, and Corinthians.

Corinthian War in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The Corinthian War began with the Lacedæmonian invasion of Bœotia, compelled Agesilaus to return from Asia Minor, and ended with his victory over the Thebans and allies at Coronea.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

The Corinthian War began with the Lacedæmonian march into Bœotia and grew into a major conflict. It compelled Agesilaus to bring his army home from Asia Minor before he defeated the Thebans and their allies at Coronea.
