Also called: myrrh distilling from the bark

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, myrrh comes from myrrh tree roots when earth is dug; on Holy Island, rich-soil trees produce deep red spring myrrh and white later myrrh twice a year. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, myrrh is linked to the Arabic “Mor,” explaining Myrrha’s tree transformation through an equivocal name, and is noted as having flavored wine in ancient practice.

CHAP. III.

Myrrh grows on the Holy Island and flows forth when the earth around the roots of its tree is dug up. Trees in rich soil produce it twice a year, with the spring myrrh deep red and the later myrrh white.

Myrrh is associated with the Arabic word “Mor,” which is given as the meaning of Myrrha’s name. The explanation says that Myrrha’s transformation into a tree was invented because of this equivocal name. The text also states that the ancients flavored their wines with myrrh.
