Pallas in The Aeneid

Virgil · 28 to 18 BC · the work’s page · read it where Pallas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pallas is Evander’s son, an Arcadian prince and young, inexperienced warrior, partly Sabine through his mother. He first approaches Aeneas’s ships, questions the strangers, and welcomes Aeneas after hearing his peaceful purpose. Evander sends him with Aeneas as leader of two hundred horsemen, hoping he will learn courage and military conduct. Pallas travels beside Aeneas, curious about the voyage, then leads and rallies the Arcadians in battle. He kills several enemies, prays to the Tuscan Tiber, and challenges Turnus. Turnus kills him with a wound in the breast. Aeneas praises his courage, honors him with a solemn funeral procession, and sends his body to Evander, whose mourning calls for vengeance.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK VIII

Pallas approaches Aeneas’s ships, questions the strangers, and welcomes Aeneas after learning who he is. Evander sends him with Aeneas.

Now night had shed her silver dews around,

Pallas accompanies Evander when he meets Aeneas and is offered as a leader of troops under Aeneas’s command. He is to command two hundred horsemen, while Evander hopes he will learn courage and military conduct from Aeneas.

BOOK X

Pallas travels with Aeneas and remains beside him on the voyage, asking about the winds, stars, and course at sea.

But, in another part, th’ Arcadian horse

Pallas rallies the fleeing Arcadians with threats and prayers, urging them to fight through the enemy rather than retreat. He kills Lagus, Hisbon, Anchemolus, Laris, and Thymbrus, and, after praying to the Tuscan Tiber, kills Halesus. He then challenges Turnus, but Turnus pierces his breast and kills him.

BOOK XI

Pallas lies dead from a wound in his manly bosom, and Aeneas praises his courage and honorable death. His body is carried with costly funeral honors to Evander’s city, where his father mourns him and demands Turnus’s vengeance.

Pallas in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Pallas first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pallas, Evander's son and a youthful champion under Aeneas, first challenges approaching newcomers, then welcomes the Trojans and joins Aeneas to learn war. Though brave, he is unequally matched against Turnus and killed in battle; Turnus's taking his belt later prompts Aeneas to kill Turnus.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXVI

Pallas is Evander's son and initially approaches Aeneas's vessel with a weapon to discover who the newcomers are. After learning that they are Trojans seeking alliance, he welcomes Aeneas and later accompanies him to the Etruscan camp to learn the art of war.

NISUS AND EURYALUS

Pallas fights Turnus despite being unequally matched and is killed by him. Turnus takes Pallas's belt, which later causes Aeneas to kill Turnus.
