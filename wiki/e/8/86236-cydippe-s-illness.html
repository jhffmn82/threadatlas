Cydippe's illness in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Cydippe’s illness is a persistent, unexplained languor that resists physicians and becomes severe whenever her marriage rites are prepared. It leaves her fevered, weak, wasted, bloodless, and pale, recurring as she attempts to evade a pledge. Its cause is disputed: some cite chance, divine disapproval, or Acontius’s arts, while Acontius says Diana sends it because Cydippe has broken her oath. He maintains that rejecting the rival suitor and honoring the pledge will restore her lasting health.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

XX

Cydippe's illness repeatedly occurs when she is about to marry. Acontius says that Diana causes the illness whenever Cydippe attempts to break her oath, seeking to preserve her by preserving her faith. He is distressed both by her illness and by the possibility that another person may attend her while he is kept away.

nam quod habes et tu gemini verba altera pacti, 155

Cydippe’s illness repeatedly follows her attempts to evade the pledge and leaves her suspected by Diana. Acontius claims that rejecting the rival suitor and honoring the pledge will bring Cydippe lasting health.

XXI

Cydippe suffers from a persistent languor whose causes are not apparent, and physicians have not helped her. Her illness grows severe as her marriage approaches, and some attribute it to chance, divine disapproval, or Acontius's magical arts.

Cum tamen haec dixi, cum me tibi firma negavi,

The illness leaves the speaker fevered, weak, wasted, bloodless, and pale. It repeatedly appears when marriage rites are prepared, and the speaker seeks divine help to recover.
