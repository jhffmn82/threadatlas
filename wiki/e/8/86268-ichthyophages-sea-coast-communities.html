Ichthyophages' sea-coast communities

Abstract written by a model

Ichthyophages' sea-coast communities are groups living in shore caves, cliffs, and rocky valleys. Their customary subsistence centers on trapping fish with stone-blocked passages, gathering catches together, and processing fish by drying, boiling, or mixing it with Paliurus seed; fish bones serve as emergency food and other uses.

Now We shall treat of those nations that lie scattered along the

These communities live near caves, cliffs, and narrow valleys along the shore. They block passages with stones so that receding water leaves fish trapped in hollows, then gather the catch in groups and process it for food.
