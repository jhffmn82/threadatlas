Major Tarver in Dandy Dick

Arthur W. Pinero · 1893

Major Tarver is a middle-aged Army officer and gentleman in the Hussars, an invalid with an unstable liver whose excitement alternates with depression. At the Deanery, he helps arrange the evening and the girls’ costumes, plans to attend the ball as Charles the First, and loves Salome Jedd passionately. After dinner he suffers indigestion, loses the music for his intended song, and leaves when the Swan Inn fire is controlled. Later, shabby and miserable in his Cavalier costume, he carries a carriage umbrella, says the Military were summoned to St. Marvells, and begs Salome to smile on him again. Finally, weak and supported by Mr. Darbey, he claims rejection will ruin his health and is accepted by Salome.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

DANDY DICK. / THE FIRST ACT.

Gerald Tarver visits the Deanery with Nugent Darbey to arrange the evening's plans and has arranged the girls' costumes. He is described as a middle-aged officer with an unstable liver, alternating between excitement and depression, and intends to appear as Charles the First at the ball.

THE SECOND ACT.

Major Tarver suffers from indigestion after dinner and jokes about needing charcoal biscuits in battle. He intends to sing "Come into the garden, Maud," but discovers that his music is missing, then leaves with Mr. Darbey after the Swan Inn fire is brought under control.

The curtains at the window are pushed aside, and SALOME and SHEBA enter; both in

Gerald Tarver appears as a miserable, shabby Cavalier carrying a carriage umbrella. He explains that the Military were summoned to St. Marvells, asks Salome to smile on him again, and is helped to escape by Salome and Sheba.

_The second scene is the Morning Room at the Deanery again.

Major Tarver arrives at the Deanery in an extremely weak condition, supported by Mr. Darbey. He claims that rejection by Salome will destroy his health, produces a doctor's certificate, and is accepted by Salome.
