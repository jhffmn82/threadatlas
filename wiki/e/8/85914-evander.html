Evander in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Evander is the aged Arcadian king and founder of Pallanteum, ruler of the Arcadians and father of Pallas. He warmly receives Aeneas as a guest and ally, recalling friendship with Anchises, explaining local rites and history, and agreeing to support Aeneas against the Latins. Though his own forces are insufficient, he offers Tuscan aid and sends Pallas under Aeneas’s command. He declines rule of the Tuscans because age and declining strength make him unfit. Fearing for Pallas, he later receives his slain son, collapses in grief, blames his own long life, and resolves to live until Turnus is avenged.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK VIII

Evander receives Aeneas warmly, remembers his former friendship with Anchises, agrees to the requested alliance, and promises military supplies. He sends Pallas with Aeneas and explains the rites of Hercules and the early history of the settlement.

Now night had shed her silver dews around,

Evander tells Aeneas that his own forces are insufficient but offers the support of the Tuscans and entrusts Pallas to Aeneas’s command. He refuses the Tuscan crown because of age and declining strength, then grieves at Pallas’s departure and fears for his son’s life.

But, in another part, th’ Arcadian horse

Evander is identified as Pallas’s father and as the former host of Hercules. After Pallas’s death, Aeneas remembers Evander, Pallas, and the hospitality and friendship connecting them.

BOOK XI

Evander rushes from his city when he hears that Pallas has been slain and collapses upon his son’s corpse. He blames himself for having lived too long, refuses to reproach Aeneas, and says that he will continue living to see vengeance taken on Turnus.

Evander in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Evander, an Arcadian from Pallantium and son of Hermes by a Nymph, excels in counsel and war, then leads Arcadians to found Rome near the river Tiber.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XLIII.

Evander was said to be the best of the Arcadians in council and war, and the son of Hermes by a Nymph who was the daughter of Lado. He led Arcadians from Pallantium to establish a colony near the river Tiber.

Evander in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Evander is an aged Arcadian exile and chief ruling a small state near the future Rome. Long hostile to Turnus, he warmly allies with Aeneas against Turnus and the Rutulians, directs him to lead the Etruscans, and sends his only son Pallas with him.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXVI

Evander is an aged Arcadian chief who lives near the place where Rome will later rise and is hostile to Turnus and the Rutulians. Guided by Tiber, Aeneas visits him, and Evander receives him warmly and offers an alliance. Evander directs Aeneas to lead the Etruscans and sends his son Pallas with him.
