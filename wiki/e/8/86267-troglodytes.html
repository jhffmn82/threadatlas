Troglodytes in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Troglodytes are a pastoral people, including communities called Nomades, living in tribes under monarchs. They move between pastures, feed on milk, blood, and meat from old or sick cattle, wear beast skins around the loins, and share wives and children except for the monarch’s wife. They practice circumcision except among those called cripples, fight over grazing pastures, and bind and stone the dead in burial. They also kill the helpless or incurably ill, hunt wild beasts, prize the wild bull’s strength, and occupy a coast near Ethiopia beside the Arabian gulf as far as deserts.

The Troglodytes live as pastoral nomads divided into tribes under monarchs, with wives and children held in common except for the king's wife. They live on milk, blood, and the meat of old or sick cattle, migrate between pastures, fight over grazing land, and have customs involving circumcision, communal burial practices, and the killing of those who become helpless or incurably ill.

Sphinxes are bred near to the Troglodytes in Ethiopia, not nradi

The Troglodytes are said to live near Ethiopia and to occupy the coast on the right side of the Arabian gulf as far as the deserts. They hunt and capture wild beasts, regard the wild bull as the strongest of animals, and include Nomades among their communities.
