Egyptian chronology

topic · 1 work · 2 quoted facts · 0 connections
Also called: computation of the Egyptians, computation of time

Abstract written by a model

Egyptian chronology records priests’ vast reckonings from Sol or from Osiris and Isis to Alexander the Great’s passage into Asia. Its central issue is reconciling these extraordinary divine reign lengths: earlier Egyptians may have counted lunar years or shortened four-month years rather than solar years.

Egyptian chronology is in one work on this site, The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

From Osiris and Isis, to the reign of Alexander the Great, who

Egyptian priests reckon more than twenty-three thousand years from the reign of Sol to Alexander's passage into Asia, while other calculations give more than ten thousand years or nearly twenty-three thousand lunar years from Osiris and Isis to Alexander. The text explains the extraordinary lengths of divine reigns by suggesting that earlier Egyptians counted lunar or shortened years rather than solar years.
