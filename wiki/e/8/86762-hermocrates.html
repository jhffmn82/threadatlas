Hermocrates in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Hermocrates is a Syracusan general and assembly member, esteemed for service in the war against the Athenians. During the night assault on Epipolae, he led selected young men to aid Syracuse and helped drive out the Athenians; his later advice to deploy forces at night and block roads was rejected. In assembly he opposed harsh treatment of prisoners, urging moderation and humanity over victory by force. Though favored by Pharnabazus and commanding thirty-five ships for the Lacedaemonians, he was banished by a contrary Syracusan faction and forbidden to enter Syracuse. From Selinus, after support for recall and failed restoration, he built forces, raided Carthaginian territories, honored Syracusan dead from Himera, and sought return. He entered with three thousand armed men, but was killed at Syracuse with many accomplices.

Hermocrates the Syracusan was highly esteemed among the Syracusans for his service in the war against the Athenians. After being banished, he received money from Pharnabazus, built galleys at Messana, hired soldiers, and gathered a force of six thousand men at Selinus. He raided Motya, Panormus, and other territories subject to the Carthaginians, winning high esteem among the Sicilians and seeking to return to Syracuse.

CHAP. X.

Hermocrates marched from Selinus to Himera, gathered the bodies of the Syracusans killed there, placed them in richly adorned chariots, and sent them to Syracuse. He later attempted to return to Syracuse with three thousand armed men, but the Syracusans killed him and many of his accomplices.
