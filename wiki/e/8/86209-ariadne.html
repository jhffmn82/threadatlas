  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Ariadne, daughter of Minos, helps Theseus escape the Labyrinth with thread, is left on Naxos or Dia, and is embraced and aided by Bacchus.

Ariadne in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Ariadne is a woman who aids Theseus with a thread, then awakens abandoned on an island after his departure. Alone, threatened by wild beasts, injured and fearful of stranger-men, she searches the shore, calls to him, and signals his ship with her veil. She fears exile and an unburied death, begs to be remembered, and later is married to Bacchus.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

X

Ariadne awakens alone after Theseus has departed and searches the shore, calling his name and signaling to his ship. She remembers giving Theseus the thread that enabled his return from the Labyrinth, and she fears abandonment, exile, and death without burial. She begs Theseus to turn back or at least remember and recount her abandonment.

Ariadne, daughter of Minos, loves and helps Theseus kill the Minotaur and escape the labyrinth. Theseus secretly takes her from Crete but abandons her on Dia. Bacchus takes and marries her, leads her to Mount Arius, and she is not seen again; after her death, her crown becomes a constellation.

Ariadne, the daughter of Minos, fell in love with Theseus because of his gallant appearance and conduct. She advised and assisted him in killing the Minotaur and finding his way out of the labyrinth. Theseus secretly took her from Crete, but at Dia, later called Naxos, Bacchus took her from Theseus and married her. After her death, Bacchus made her crown a constellation called Ariadne’s crown.

Ariadne in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Ariadne, daughter of Minos, deeply loves Theseus and helps him defeat the Minotaur and escape the labyrinth with a sword and thread. Sailing with him toward Athens, she is abandoned asleep on Naxos. Bacchus finds, consoles, and marries her, giving her a crown; after her death, he places the golden crown in the sky.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter XIII

Ariadne, the daughter of Minos, falls in love with Theseus and gives him a sword and a clew of thread to help him defeat the Minotaur and escape the labyrinth. She sails toward Athens with Theseus, but he abandons her asleep on Naxos after Minerva warns him that Ariadne is destined to marry Bacchus.

Chapter XIV

Ariadne, the daughter of King Minos, helps Theseus escape the labyrinth but is abandoned by him asleep on Naxos. Venus promises her an immortal lover, and Bacchus finds, consoles, and marries her. After her death, Bacchus places her golden crown in the sky.
