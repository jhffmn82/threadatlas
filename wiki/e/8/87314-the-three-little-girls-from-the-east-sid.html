the three little girls from the East Side in Gallegher and Other Stories

Richard Harding Davis · 1890

The three little girls from the East Side live on Hester Street with their mother and carry a lunch basket on an outing. In the Park, they watch the swan-boats and imagine lake sights. Unable to afford a ride, they are taken by Van Bibber, enjoy it, receive week-long tickets, and return to their tenement sunburned and delighted.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

" VAN BIBBER AND THE SWAN-BOATS It was very hot in the Park, and young Van Bibber, who has a good heart and a great deal more money than good-hearted people generally get, was cross and somnolent.

The three little girls from the East Side watch the swan-boats and discuss the sights that might be visible from the lake, including fishes, ducks, snakes, bears, and other wild animals. They cannot afford a ride until Van Bibber offers to take them. They enjoy the trip greatly, receive tickets for a week of rides, and return to their tenement sunburned and delighted.
