Telephus

person · 4 works · 15 quoted facts · 8 connections
Also called: a son, Τήλεφον, Τήλεφος, Τηλέφου
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Telephus is hidden on Mount Parthenius, found being suckled by a hind, raised by Corythus, reunited with his mother through an oracle, and made Teuthras’s heir and successor.
  • In The Library, Telephus is king commanding the Mysians against the Greeks, wounds Achilles, is wounded, comes in rags to be healed, and shows the Greeks the course to Troy.
  • In The Library, Telephus is exposed as a babe on Mount Parthenius, suckled by a doe, raised by the herdsmen of Corythus, seeks his parents at Delphi, and becomes Teuthras’s adopted son, successor, and ruler of Mysia.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Telephus is born to Auge and Hercules, exposed on Mount Parthenium, and survives after being suckled and raised by a doe.

Telephus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Telephus was the son of Hercules and Auge. After Auge left him hidden on Mount Parthenius, shepherds found him being suckled by a hind and brought him to Corythus, who raised him. He later discovered his mother through an oracle, married Teuthras's daughter Agriopa, and became Teuthras's heir and successor.

Telephus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Telephus, son of Auge and Hercules, is exposed as a babe on Mount Parthenius, suckled by a doe, and raised by the herdsmen of Corythus. On reaching maturity he seeks his parents at Delphi. After receiving the god’s information, he travels to Mysia, becomes Teuthras’s adopted son, succeeds him on his death, and rules Mysia.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE LIBRARY, II. vr. 3-4

Telephus is born to Auge and Hercules and is exposed on Mount Parthenius. A doe provides milk for him, shepherds rescue him, and they name him Telephus.

IX. ᾿Αρκάδος δὲ καὶ Λεανείρας τῆς ᾿Αμύκλου

Telephus was Auge's exposed baby, who was suckled by a doe on Mount Parthenius and raised by the herdsmen of Corythus. He sought his parents, went to Delphi, and then traveled to Mysia, where he became Teuthras's adopted son and succeeded him as ruler.

Telephus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Telephus, son of Auge and Hercules, is born to Auge, exposed on Mount Parthenium as a boy, and survives by being suckled and brought up by a doe, according to tradition.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XLVIII.

Telephus is identified as the son born to Auge. In one tradition he was exposed on Mount Parthenium and suckled by a doe, a detail also recorded by the people of Tegea.
