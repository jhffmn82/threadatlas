golden apples in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

The golden apples are gifts associated with Hera’s marriage to Zeus, presented by Earth; another account says they came from Aphrodite. They are located at Atlas among the Hyperboreans, not in Libya, and guarded by the Hesperides and an immortal dragon. In one account Atlas brings three to Hercules, who gives them to Eurystheus; in another Hercules kills the guardian snake and plucks them. Later, Melanion uses them in his race against Atalanta, who stops to pick them up and loses.

The golden apples were said to be located by Atlas in Hyperborea, not in Libya as some claimed. Earth gave them to Zeus when he married Hera, and they were guarded by an immortal dragon and the Hesperides.

THE LIBRARY, 11. v. 11

The golden apples were presented by Earth to Zeus after his marriage with Hera and were guarded by the Hesperides and an immortal dragon. Atlas brings three of them to Hercules, who gives them to Eurystheus; another account says Hercules plucks them himself after killing the guardian snake.

IX. ᾿Αρκάδος δὲ καὶ Λεανείρας τῆς ᾿Αμύκλου

The golden apples came from Aphrodite. Melanion threw them down during the race, and Atalanta stopped to pick them up, allowing him to defeat her.
