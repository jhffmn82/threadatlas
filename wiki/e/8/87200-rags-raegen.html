Rags Raegen in Gallegher and Other Stories

Richard Harding Davis · 1890

Rags Raegen, also called Ragsey or Rags, is a young tough, ice-cart driver, and exceptional underwater swimmer tied to East River and Cherry Street thieves and bullies. Burdened by criminal bonds and a record dating to age ten, he is their hero but eventually shakes the gang's control. After a fight in which he shoots Pike McGonegal, he is pursued by police and accused of murder, though he claims self-defence. He escapes by seeming to drown in the East River and hides in Case's tenement, where he finds the sick, starving baby Margaret. Protecting, feeding, cooling, and comforting her changes him: despite considering flight after learning McGonegal has died, he cannot abandon her. He carries Margaret to the station-house for food and medical attention and voluntarily surrenders. Confined in the Tombs as the case awaits its outcome, he receives daily visits from the baby and later says he does not regret surrendering or leaving the gang, even when he expected to hang.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

" MY DISREPUTABLE FRIEND, MR.

Rags Raegen is an experienced swimmer who has previously escaped police by diving into the East River, but he is unfamiliar with running the roofs in McGonegal's neighborhood. After hiding from the police, he discovers Margaret and spends the night protecting, feeding, cooling, and comforting her. The baby's trust and tenderness leave him feeling guilty, changed, and unusually happy.

The sun rose hissing out of the East River, a broad, red disc of heat.

Ragsey Raegen, called Rags, cares for Margaret in an abandoned tenement room during extreme heat. He tries to cool and feed her, but after drinking whiskey he sleeps through much of the day. When he learns that Pike McGonegal has died and that he is wanted for murder, he considers fleeing but cannot bring himself to leave Margaret.

The surly old sergeant of the Twenty-first Precinct station-house had read the e

Raegen brings the sick, starving baby to the station-house and demands that she receive food and medical attention. When detectives recognize him as the man they have been pursuing, he says that nobody took him and that he gave himself up. Three months later, he says he has left the gang and does not regret what he did, even when he expected to hang.
