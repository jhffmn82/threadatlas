Laomedon

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Laomedon is king of Troy and father of Priam; he refuses promised horses, imprisons Iphiclus and Telamon, plots against the Argonauts, and is killed by Hercules when Troy is captured. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Laomedon is king of Troy and Phrygian and the father of Hesione; he builds Troy’s walls, refuses Apollo and Neptune’s reward, commits perjury, and later refuses Hercules the promised horses. In The Library, Laomedon is Troy’s ruler and a son of Hercules by Meline; he withholds divine wages, exposes Hesione, refuses Hercules’s payment, and is killed by Heracles when Troy falls.

Laomedon in The Library

Apollodorus · about AD 70 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Laomedon first appears

Abstract written by a model

Laomedon is presented as Troy’s ruler and as a son of Hercules by Meline. After Apollo and Poseidon fortify Pergamum, he withholds their wages; divine anger brings plague and a sea monster. He exposes his daughter Hesione, then refuses Hercules’s payment. He attacks the ships, kills Oicles, is repelled and besieged, and when the city is captured Heracles shoots him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

THE LIBRARY, II. v. 4-5

Laomedon is the ruler of Troy. He fails to pay Apollo and Poseidon after they build the city's walls, and the resulting divine anger brings a plague and a sea monster against Troy.

THE LIBRARY, II. v. 9

Laomedon hired Apollo and Poseidon to fortify Pergamum but refused to pay their wages. He exposed his daughter Hesione to the sea monster after oracles foretold that this would deliver the people from the pestilence and the monster, and he later refused to pay Hercules as well.

Μετὰ δὲ τὴν λατρείαν ἀπαλλαγεὶς τῆς νόσου

Laomedon came against the ships with the multitude and killed Oicles while fighting. After being driven back by Heracles's men, he was besieged; when the city was captured, Heracles shot him.
