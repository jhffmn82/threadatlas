Curetes

group · 2 works · 12 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: The Curetes, nine Curetes, the Curetes of Ætolia

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the nine Curetes manage flocks, develop food-gathering and warfare practices, organize communities, invent armed dancing, and use their noisy dance to conceal and raise Jupiter from Saturn. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the Curetes watch over Jupiter during his infancy, conceal Epaphus from Jupiter’s wrath, and are slain by Jupiter.

Curetes in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Curetes first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Curetes were goddesses associated with Crete and Sicily, honored by Cretans in Sicily and neighboring peoples. Nine mountain- and cave-dwelling Curetes managed flocks, developed food-gathering and warfare practices, organized communities, and invented armed dancing. Their noisy dance helped conceal and raise Jupiter, protecting him from Saturn.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. V.

The Curetes were goddesses said to have come from Crete to Sicily. They were believed to have secretly raised and concealed Jupiter from Saturn and were honored by the Cretans settled in Sicily and by neighboring peoples.

CHAP. IV.

Nine Curetes followed the Idæi Dactyli, dwelling in mountains, caves, and beneath trees. They were credited with managing flocks, taming cattle, gathering honey, hunting, making darts, swords, and helmets, organizing communities, and inventing armed dancing; their noise concealed Jupiter from Saturn while they raised him.

Curetes in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Curetes first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Curetes are described as ancient inhabitants of Crete, said to emerge from a plenteous shower of rain and to descend from Uranus and Tita. They watch over Jupiter during his infancy, later conceal Epaphus from Jupiter's wrath, and are slain by Jupiter for doing so.

notes .. notes

The Curetes were described as ancient inhabitants of Crete and were said to have sprung from the earth after rain. They may have been understood as descendants of Uranus and Tita, and they were said to have watched over Jupiter's infancy before Jupiter slew them for concealing Epaphus from his wrath.
