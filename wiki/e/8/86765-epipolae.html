Epipolae in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Epipolae is high land near Syracuse overlooking and capable of commanding the city. Its strategic value lay in controlling or pressuring Syracuse: Athenians seized it in night attacks, briefly held forts and built or used fortified positions, but Gylippus and the Syracusans defeated them and destroyed their works. Dionysius later chose it for major defensive fortification.

DIODORUS SICULUS. .. CHAP. I.

Epipolae was seized by the Athenians in a night attack and became part of their fortified position around Syracuse. Gylippus and the Syracusans later assaulted it, defeated the Athenians, and demolished its walls and fortifications.

Epipolae was the high position that Demosthenes believed the Athenians had to seize in order to surround and pressure Syracuse. The Athenians attacked it at night, briefly took some forts, entered the fortifications, and demolished part of the wall before being driven out.

CHAP. III.

Epipolae overlooked Syracuse and was considered capable of commanding the city. Dionysius therefore selected it as the site for a major defensive fortification.
