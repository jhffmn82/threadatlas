Xenophon

Also called: Xem^pbon, Xenophon (the son of Gryllus)

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Xenophon was chosen general by nearly five thousand soldiers who had served with Cyrus, led them against the Thracians of Salmydessus, burned many towns and villages, and then marched to join Thimbron after being promised pay. In Description of Greece, Xenophon, son of Gryllus, was exiled from Athens for joining Cyrus against the Persian king, received Scillus from the Lacedæmonians, built a temple and grove there to Ephesian Artemis, and was reportedly acquitted over accepting Scillus.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

CHAPTER V.

Xenophon, son of Gryllus, was exiled from Athens for joining Cyrus against the Persian king. The Lacedæmonians gave him Scillus, where he lived and built a temple and grove to Ephesian Artemis. The people of Elis said he was tried before the Olympian council for accepting Scillus but was acquitted, and they showed a tomb with an effigy said to represent him.
