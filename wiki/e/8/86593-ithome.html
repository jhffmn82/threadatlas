Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Ithome was besieged by the Lacedaemonians and their confederates during the war with the Helots and Messenians, which continued for ten years without resolution. In Description of Greece, Ithome was a mountain and stronghold encircling Messene, later razed by the Lacedæmonians and subsequently serving as a Messenian Helot stronghold and the site of Messene’s citadel.

Ithome in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Ithome first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ithome is a mountain encircling Messene, with a small town enlarged by Messenians who abandoned inland towns after losses, desertions, and pestilence. Its high, difficult access made it their stronghold and a defensive position behind their army. A sacred enclosure of Zeus stood on its summit; its neglected worship was restored by Glaucus. After a siege of about five months at the end of the twenty-year first Messenian War, the Messenians evacuated Ithome and the Lacedæmonians razed it. Later it was a stronghold of revolting Messenian Helots, held recovered Mysteries, and contained Messene's citadel, Clepsydra, and Zeus's annual feast.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK IV.--MESSENIA.

Ithome was associated with Messenia, and the later city of Messene was built near it. A sacred enclosure of Zeus stood on its summit, though it had been neglected by Polycaon and Messene until Glaucus restored its worship.

CHAPTER VI.

The Messenians abandon their inland towns and settle at Ithome after their losses, desertions, and pestilence. They enlarge its small existing town because the mountain is a strong and difficult-to-access defensive position.

CHAPTER XI.

Ithome served as the Messenian stronghold and was positioned behind the Messenian army during the battle in Chapter XI. After a siege lasting about five months at the end of the twenty-year first Messenian War, the Messenians evacuated it, and the Lacedæmonians subsequently razed it to the ground.

CHAPTER XXIV.

Ithome became the stronghold of the revolting Messenian Helots. It was also the place where the recovered Messenian Mysteries were found, and Zeus of Ithome was honored during the restoration of Messene.

CHAPTER XXIX.

Ithome was known to Demetrius, son of Philip, who used his knowledge of the road to enter Messene secretly. It was also the site of Messene's citadel, the spring Clepsydra, and the annual feast of Zeus of Ithome.
