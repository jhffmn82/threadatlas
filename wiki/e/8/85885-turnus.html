Turnus in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Turnus is a high-born Daunian, Rutulian, and Latian prince who rules Ardea, descends from Pilumnus, and is the son of Daunus and brother of Juturna. Formerly betrothed to Lavinia, he loves and seeks to marry her, claims both bride and throne, and becomes the chief rival and enemy of Aeneas. Favored by the Latian queen and supported, protected, and at times rescued by Juno, he leads the Rutulians, Latins, and an opposing coalition in war against the Trojans. He gathers allies, seeks Diomede’s aid, attacks and besieges the Trojan camp, and displays bold martial leadership in splendid arms. He kills Pallas and takes Pallas’s golden belt, but Juno draws him away from battle; ashamed, he longs for death before returning to his native shores. Accused by Drances and grieving Latins of causing the war for Lavinia and power, he rejects the charges, recounts victories, and repeatedly offers to meet Aeneas alone. He uses an ambush, commands defenses, and responds to Camilla’s death by leaving his mountain pass. At the end, despite warnings and pleas, he demands the duel for Lavinia. Juturna repeatedly prevents his encounter with Aeneas, but when Aeneas attacks the town, Turnus resolves to finish the conflict. His borrowed sword breaks; he is pursued, wounded, and killed by Aeneas while wearing Pallas’s golden belt.

Narrative, section by section

16 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK VII

Turnus is a neighboring prince of high descent who loves Lavinia and is favored by the Latian queen. He is presented as the principal rival to the foreign prince foretold by the prophecies.

Thus having said, she sinks beneath the ground,

Turnus is described as Lavinia's once-plighted lord and objects to the Latian king transferring the bride and scepter to a foreigner. Alecto's attack makes him awaken calling for arms, and he commissions an embassy, gathers the Rutuli, and prepares to fight the Trojan and Ausonian forces.

And now the mighty labour is begun

Turnus is the leader whom the Italian chiefs and their armies come to aid. He rides among the troops in splendid armor, wearing a triple-plumed crest topped by the blazing Chimaera, and carries a shield depicting Io, her keeper, and the wat’ry god.

BOOK VIII

Turnus assembles the Latian forces, plants his standard at Laurentum, and sends Venulus to Diomede to request aid against Aeneas.

Now night had shed her silver dews around,

Turnus is identified as the enemy whom Aeneas must confront. Aeneas predicts that Turnus will pay dearly and that the Tiber will carry the bodies, swords, and shields of the conflict.

BOOK IX

Iris tells Turnus that Aeneas has left the Trojan camp and urges him to attack. He leads the Rutulians against the camp, burns the Trojan ships, and encourages his troops to continue the assault after the ships are transformed.

Now rose the ruddy morn from Tithon’s bed,

Turnus rallies his troops at dawn and displays the heads of Nisus and Euryalus to terrify the Trojans. He throws a flaming brand at a Trojan tower, kills many defenders, enters the camp through the gate opened by Pandarus, and slaughters numerous Trojans before Mnestheus and the rallied defenders drive him back. Exhausted and overwhelmed, he reaches the river, where the yellow god carries him safely to the farther coast.

BOOK X

Turnus continues to besiege the Trojan camp and organizes the defense when Aeneas’s fleet approaches. He urges his troops to attack the arriving forces before they can establish themselves and leaves some men to keep the city enclosed.

But, in another part, th’ Arcadian horse

Turnus confronts Pallas in single combat and kills him with a spear. He tramples Pallas’s corpse and takes his golden belt, then is lured by Juno’s phantom of Aeneas onto a ship and carried away from the battle. Ashamed of his flight, he wishes for death or shipwreck, but the ship eventually returns him to his native shores and his father.

BOOK XI

Turnus is named by Aeneas as the opponent who must fight for Lavinia. Drances blames him for the war and urges him to meet Aeneas in single combat, but Turnus retains supporters in the Latian court and the queen’s protection.

Thus, full of anxious thought, he summons all

Turnus is accused by Drances of causing the war, abandoning the fight, and pursuing Lavinia for power. He angrily rejects these accusations, recounts his own victories over the Trojans and their allies, and offers to face Aeneas alone.

While they debate, nor these nor those will yield,

Turnus urges the Latins to prepare for the approaching enemy and assigns commanders to defend the town and its approaches. He establishes an ambush in a narrow, elevated valley, then agrees that Camilla and the Volscians will face the Tyrrhene troops while he blocks another route.

She said. The faithful nymph descends from high

Turnus is informed that the Volscians have been defeated and Camilla has been killed. He leaves the hilly pass where he had been concealed and goes down onto the plain.

BOOK XII

Turnus, angered by the Latins’ retreat and by doubts about his promised fight, demands single combat with Aeneas. He rejects Latinus’s warnings and Amata’s pleas, arms himself, and agrees that the victor shall receive Lavinia. During the treaty ceremony he appears fearful and despondent, while the Rutulians begin to doubt his chances.

When Turnus saw the Trojan quit the plain,

Turnus drives furiously across the battlefield, killing numerous Trojans and Latins, including Eumedes, Phegeus, and Menoetes. He avoids Aeneas because Juturna, disguised as his charioteer, continually carries him away from the encounter.

Now Turnus rolls aloof o’er empty plains,

Turnus hears that Aeneas is attacking the town and, despite his despair over the deaths of Murranus and Ufens and the queen's death, chooses to end the fight himself. He challenges Aeneas in single combat, but his borrowed sword breaks, he is pursued across the battlefield, and he is finally wounded and killed.

Turnus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Turnus is king and leader of the Rutulians, favored to marry Lavinia. Incited by Alecto over Aeneas’s claim to her, he leads forces against the Trojans, protects Mezentius, attacks their camp, and kills Pallas, taking his belt. In the final conflict Aeneas defeats and kills him after seeing the belt.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXVI

Turnus, king of the Rutulians, is favored by Lavinia's parents as her prospective husband. Alecto incites him by claiming that Aeneas is attempting to take his bride, and Turnus becomes the recognized leader of the forces opposing the Trojans. He protects the exiled Mezentius and is opposed by Evander and the Etruscans.

NISUS AND EURYALUS

Turnus gathers his forces and attacks the Trojan camp while Aeneas is away. He kills Pallas and takes his belt, but later loses the final combat with Aeneas and is killed after Aeneas sees the belt.
