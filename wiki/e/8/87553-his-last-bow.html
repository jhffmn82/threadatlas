His Last Bow in His Last Bow

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1908-04-17

This document is a collection of Sherlock Holmes cases introduced by Dr. John H. Watson’s preface, which says Holmes has retired to a farm near Eastbourne but returns to Government service as war approaches. Holmes and Watson investigate crimes ranging from murder, espionage, poisoning, abduction, and secret societies to international political revenge. In the Wisteria Lodge case, John Scott Eccles’s strange night at Garcia’s deserted house leads Holmes, Watson, and Inspector Baynes to uncover an attempt to kill Don Murillo, the former dictator of San Pedro. Miss Burnet escapes imprisonment and reveals Murillo’s identity; Murillo and Lopez flee England and are later reported murdered in Madrid. In the Bruce-Partington Plans case, Holmes investigates Arthur Cadogan West’s death and the theft of vital submarine plans, exposing Colonel Valentine Walter and Hugo Oberstein. In Cornwall, Holmes discovers that the devil’s-foot poison caused the terror, insanity, and deaths of the Tregennis family; Dr. Leon Sterndale admits killing Mortimer Tregennis in revenge, and Holmes lets him go. Other cases focus on hidden lives and rescue. Holmes traces signals and advertisements connected with the Red Circle, where Gennaro Lucca kills Giuseppe Gorgiano while protecting his wife Emilia. Watson’s search for Lady Frances Carfax leads Holmes to Henry and Mrs. Peters, and they stop a funeral in time to save Lady Frances from a coffin after she has been drugged. Holmes also pretends to be dying in order to trap Culverton Smith, who confesses that he murdered Victor Savage and tried to kill Holmes with a spring-loaded ivory box. The volume ends with Holmes’s war service in August 1914. Disguised as the agent Altamont, he deceives and captures the German spy Von Bork, seizes documents from his safe, and reveals that he has spent two years infiltrating German networks and feeding them unreliable information. With Watson and the undercover Martha assisting, Holmes takes Von Bork to London as war begins.

The preface to His Last Bow states that Sherlock Holmes is alive and well but occasionally suffers from rheumatism. He has retired to a small farm on the Downs near Eastbourne, where he divides his time between philosophy and agriculture. The approach of the German war leads him to offer his intellectual and practical abilities to the Government, producing historical results recounted in His Last Bow. John H. Watson explains that several earlier experiences from his portfolio have also been included in the volume.

Sherlock Holmes and Dr. John Watson hear John Scott Eccles describe his bizarre overnight stay at Wisteria Lodge, where Aloysius Garcia and his foreign household disappeared before morning. Inspectors Gregson and Baynes reveal that Garcia was murdered on Oxshott Common and that Eccles is connected to the case by a letter found in Garcia's pocket. Inspector Baynes produces a note addressed to Garcia containing coded instructions, while Holmes begins to examine the circumstances of the murder, the disappearance, and Eccles's claim that Garcia spoke to him during the night.

Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson analyze Scott Eccles's strange experience, the murder, the disappearance of Garcia's companions, and the mysterious message connected with Wisteria Lodge. Holmes hypothesizes that Garcia recruited Eccles as a respectable witness who could provide an alibi, while the message may have been an assignation at a large nearby house. A telegram supplies a list of possible houses and their occupants, narrowing the investigation to the area near Oxshott. Holmes, Watson, and Inspector Baynes travel to Esher and then set out for Wisteria Lodge.

Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson visit Wisteria Lodge with Inspector Baynes after Mr. Garcia is found dead and his servants flee. Constable Walters reports seeing a giant, terrifying face at the window, while the kitchen contains a strange mummified figure, a dismembered white cock, blood, and burned lamb or kid bones. Holmes and Baynes pursue separate theories, and Baynes later arrests a huge, savage man believed to be Garcia's cook. Holmes warns Baynes that he may be pursuing the wrong suspect, but Baynes insists on following his own methods.

Sherlock Holmes and Inspector Baynes secure Miss Burnet, who escapes from the High Gable household after being drugged and taken toward a train. The Adventure of the Bruce-Partington Plans

Sherlock Holmes is asked by his brother Mycroft to investigate the death of Arthur Cadogan West, a Woolwich Arsenal clerk whose body was found beside the Underground at Aldgate Station. Seven of ten stolen Bruce-Partington plans were found in West’s possession, while three essential plans were missing. Mycroft explains the national and international importance of the Bruce-Partington submarine and urges Holmes to determine why West took the plans, how he died, and where the missing plans went. Holmes, accompanied by Dr. Watson and Lestrade, begins the investigation at Aldgate Station.

Holmes and Watson visit Woolwich, where they learn of Sir James Walter's death, question Miss Violet Westbury and Mr. Sidney Johnson, and examine the government office from which the Bruce-Partington plans were stolen. The Adventure of the Devil’s Foot

In the spring of 1897, Sherlock Holmes and Dr. John Watson stay in a cottage near Poldhu Bay, Cornwall, while Holmes recovers from overwork. Mr. Roundhay and Mortimer Tregennis bring Holmes news of the Tredannick Wartha tragedy, in which Brenda Tregennis is found dead and Owen and George Tregennis are driven insane by apparent terror. Holmes visits Tredannick Wartha house, examines the sitting-room and surrounding garden, and finds no immediate explanation for the family’s mental affliction. The event becomes known as the Cornish Horror.

Sherlock Holmes confronts Dr. Leon Sterndale with evidence that he killed Mortimer Tregennis using Radix pedis diaboli, the devil's-foot poison. The Adventure of the Red Circle / PART I

Sherlock Holmes investigates Mrs. Warren’s mysterious male lodger, who pays generously, remains concealed, communicates through printed notes, and appears to have been replaced by a hidden woman. Holmes identifies newspaper advertisements and coded signals linking the lodgers, while an attack on Mr. Warren suggests that their enemies are searching for the man. The hidden female lodger is discovered briefly, and the male lodger sends her urgent Italian warnings by flashes of light before the signal suddenly stops.

PART II

Sherlock Holmes, Gregson, and Mr. Leverton investigate an interrupted signal from a London flat and discover that Giuseppe Gorgiano has been stabbed to death. Holmes summons Emilia Lucca, who identifies herself as Gennaro Lucca’s wife and explains that Gennaro killed Gorgiano to protect her and escape the Red Circle. Emilia recounts how Gorgiano’s society planned to bomb Tito Castalotte’s house after Castalotte resisted its blackmail. Gregson takes Emilia to the Yard, while Holmes concludes that Gennaro is unlikely to face punishment if her account is corroborated.

The Disappearance of Lady Frances Carfax

Sherlock Holmes sends Dr. Watson to investigate the disappearance of Lady Frances Carfax, who has stopped writing to her governess after leaving Lausanne. Watson traces Lady Frances through Lausanne, Baden, and Montpellier, learning that she was followed by a frightening bearded Englishman and later travelled with Dr. Shlessinger and his wife. The bearded man is identified as the Hon. Philip Green, who says he loves Lady Frances and has been trying to find her. Holmes identifies Dr. Shlessinger as the criminal Holy Peters and suspects that Lady Frances has been abducted, confined, or possibly killed in London.

Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson investigate the disappearance of Lady Frances Carfax after evidence links her jewellery to Henry Peters, formerly Dr. Shlessinger, and Mrs. Peters. Philip Green follows Mrs. Peters to No. 36, Poultney Square, where a coffin is delivered, leading Holmes to suspect that Lady Frances has been murdered and concealed for burial. The Adventure of the Dying Detective

Mrs. Hudson summons Dr. Watson to Sherlock Holmes's first-floor flat because Holmes appears to be dying from a supposedly contagious disease contracted during a case in Rotherhithe. Holmes refuses Watson's examination, claims that his illness is a deadly coolie disease from Sumatra, and eventually sends Watson to fetch Culverton Smith, an expert on the condition. Holmes gives Watson elaborate instructions involving his money, the gas, papers, and a small black-and-white ivory box before Watson leaves. Outside the flat, Inspector Morton asks about Holmes's condition and appears strangely pleased by the news.

Dr. John Watson visits Culverton Smith at Sherlock Holmes's request, claiming that Holmes is gravely ill from an Eastern disease. Holmes has actually feigned the illness and hides Watson so Smith will believe he is dying and confess to killing Victor Savage and attacking Holmes with an ivory box. His Last Bow: The War Service of Sherlock Holmes

Von Bork, a German secret-service agent posing as an English country squire, prepares to leave England after collecting extensive intelligence for Germany. Baron Von Herling, the German legation secretary, praises Von Bork’s work and discusses Germany’s plans regarding England, France, and Belgium as war approaches in August 1914. Altamont, an Irish-American agent, arrives with a copy of the British naval signals code in exchange for £500, but the parcel is actually a baited book and he is revealed to be Sherlock Holmes in disguise. Holmes chloroforms Von Bork and captures him as the First World War begins.
