Charon

person · 4 works · 9 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: a pilot, a sordid god, ferryman, old Charon, the boatman, the ferryman, Charon, the stern ferryman, the surly boatman

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Charon pilots and governs a funerary ship carrying the dead across a lake in a ceremony Orpheus saw. In The Aeneid, Charon rules the dreary coast, ferries ghosts in a leaky, canvas-sailed boat, initially refuses living Aeneas, and then carries Aeneas and the Sibyl across after being appeased by the golden bough. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Charon rows a boat on Acheron and unwillingly admits living Theseus and Pirithous. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Charon carries buried dead souls across Cocytus, challenges living, armed Aeneas, and admits him and the Sibyl after seeing the golden branch.

CHAP, VII.

Charon is the pilot who governs the ship prepared to carry the dead across the lake. The Egyptians call the pilot Charon, and the text says that Orpheus saw this ceremony in Egypt.

Chapter XXV

Charon is an old, squalid, but strong ferryman who carries dead souls across the river Cocytus. He initially challenges Aeneas because Aeneas is living and armed, but the Sibyl displays the golden branch and Charon allows them aboard.
