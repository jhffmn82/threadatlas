universe in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The universe, or world, begins as Chaos: sea, earth, and heaven mixed together. From separated pre-existent matter, its architect arranges the elements into an ordered creation of land, waters, air, heavens, climates, stars, and living creatures. It provides the narrative’s starting point. Because the impious human race commits grave crimes, Jupiter threatens the world with ruin and floods it, leaving only Deucalion and Pyrrha alive. After Jupiter disperses clouds and the waters recede, the world is restored, though it seems empty and silent to Deucalion.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

THE METAMORPHOSES. / BOOK THE FIRST.

The world provides the beginning point for the lengthened narrative.

opening

At first, the universe contains only the sea, the earth, and the heaven, all mixed together in Chaos. It is subsequently ordered as the separated elements take their proper places.

notes .. notes

The universe is described through ancient theories as having been formed by arranging pre-existent matter and its elements. Sanchoniatho is said to have transmitted ideas about its original construction through a priest of Jehovah.

opening

The universe is formed from a separated mass that is divided into distinct members and ordered into land, waters, air, heavens, climates, stars, and living creatures.

opening

The world is threatened with destruction because of the crimes of the impious human race. Jupiter says that he surveyed the earth and found that the reports of its iniquity were less serious than the reality.

opening

The world is overflowed by liquid waters, leaving only Deucalion and Pyrrha alive from the many thousands who had lived. After Jupiter disperses the clouds and the waters retreat, the world is restored, though it appears empty and silent to Deucalion.
