Phyllis in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Phyllis, a Thracian queen and Demophoon’s hostess, faithfully loves and aids him with refuge, gifts, and her virginity. Deceived by his promises and absence, she watches for his return, reproaches him, and despairingly contemplates suicide.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

II

Phyllis, who welcomed Demophoon to Rhodope, gave him refuge, gifts, aid, and her virginity, reproaches him for failing to return. She continues watching the sea for his ships, but in despair considers throwing herself into the waves, taking poison, using a sword, or hanging herself.

Phyllis in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Phyllis, daughter of the Bisaltians’ king, marries Demophon in love; when he fails to return on time, she curses him and kills herself.
