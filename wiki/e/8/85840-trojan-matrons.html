Trojan matrons in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Trojan women, exiled and distressed, lament continued wandering and long to settle in Italy. Persuaded by Iris disguised as Beroe, they burn the fleet, their means of passage; despite Pyrgo’s warning, they persist until Ascanius and Aeneas arrive, then flee ashamed.

1 section; each heading opens the text

Thus far the sacred sports they celebrate:

The Trojan matrons lament their continued wandering and are persuaded by Iris, disguised as Beroe, to burn the fleet. Pyrgo exposes the disguise, but the matrons still set the ships on fire until Ascanius and Aeneas arrive; afterward they flee in shame and recognize their error.
