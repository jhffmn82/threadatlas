Gylippus, a Lacedaemonian general sent to aid Syracuse, raised troops in Sicily, reinforced Syracuse, and helped lead resistance against the Athenians and recover Epipolae. He argued against sparing Athenian prisoners. Later, after taking three hundred talents, he was condemned to die for theft.

DIODORUS SICULUS. .. CHAP. I.

Gylippus was appointed general by the Lacedaemonians to aid Syracuse. He raised troops at Himera and elsewhere in Sicily, entered Syracuse with reinforcements, and led the Syracusans against the Athenians, helping them recover the fortifications at Epipolae.

Gylippus, identified as the Laconian speaker, argues that the Athenian prisoners should not be spared. He presents their invasion, intended enslavement of Sicilian peoples, and earlier cruelty toward other cities as reasons for their punishment.
