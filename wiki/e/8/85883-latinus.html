Abstract written by a model

In The Aeneid, Latinus is the aged, peaceful king of Latium and father of Lavinia who welcomes Aeneas, accepts their fated marriage, seeks to prevent war, and mourns its consequences. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Latinus is an elderly king and father of Lavinia who, guided by an oracle, first favors her marriage to Turnus, then allies with Aeneas as the foretold foreign son-in-law, though he ultimately yields to pressure for war.

Latinus in The Aeneid

Virgil · 28 to 18 BC · the work’s page · read it where Latinus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Latinus is the aged, mild, and peaceful king of Latium and ruler of the Latins. He is descended from Faunus and Marica, through Faunus from Picus and Saturn, and is Lavinia’s father; she is his only daughter and heir. At his palace he receives the Trojan embassy, offers the Trojans land, hospitality, and use of his kingdom, and accepts the marriage of Aeneas and Lavinia because he understands fate to require a foreign husband for her and to determine the Latian succession. He initially opposes war, refusing to open the gates despite pressure to honor an earlier promise to Turnus; when Juno forces them open, he rejects responsibility, warns Turnus of the cost, and withdraws. As war brings grief, division, and danger, Latinus convenes the council, recognizes too late that Heaven’s anger will remain until Aeneas marries Lavinia, and proposes peace: land and ships for the Trojans, gifts including gold, and envoys authorized to negotiate. He later judges his own easy temper culpable for complying with the broken treaty concerning the promised bride. In the final crisis he urges Turnus not to risk single combat, helps establish a truce and swears the union will not be broken, yet is swept between calls for peace and war. After the queen’s death, he mourns in dust and torn garments, blaming his weakness for abandoning the intended peace.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

7 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK VII

Latinus is the aged and peaceful king of Latium, descended through Faunus, Picus, and Saturn. He receives the Trojan embassy, grants the Trojans land and hospitality, and declares that his daughter must marry a foreign prince whom he believes to be the man required by fate.

Thus having said, she sinks beneath the ground,

The Latian king is urged to reject the Trojan marriage and honor his former promise to Turnus, but he remains unmoved and refuses to open the gates of war. When Juno forces the gates open, he disclaims responsibility, warns that Turnus will pay for the conflict, and withdraws into his walls.

BOOK XI

Latinus calls the Latian court together amid grief over the war and the refusal of Diomedes to help. He recognizes too late that fate has pointed out a foreign son and that Heaven’s anger remains until Aeneas marries Lavinia.

Thus, full of anxious thought, he summons all

The Latian king presides over the council and laments that the Latins face an unequal war, factions within, and an enemy outside the walls. He proposes giving the Trojans land, building ships for them, and sending senators with gifts and authority to negotiate peace.

While they debate, nor these nor those will yield,

Latinus dismisses the council after seeing the gathering danger. He recognizes that his easy temper led him to comply with breaking the treaty for the promised bride.

BOOK XII

Latinus warns Turnus that he already possesses wealth, cities, and power, and urges him not to risk his life in single combat. He nevertheless helps establish the truce, swears that the union will never be broken, and later flees the battle carrying away his gods.

When Turnus saw the Trojan quit the plain,

Latinus is caught amid the city’s conflicting calls for peace and war and is carried along by whichever side prevails. After the queen’s death and the city’s crisis, he tears his garments, covers himself with dust, and blames his weakness for abandoning the intended peace.

Abstract written by a model

Latinus is an elderly king descended from Saturn, ruling the country and father of Lavinia. Guided by an oracle, he first favors her marriage to Turnus, then allies with Aeneas as the foretold foreign son-in-law. Though resisting war against the Trojans, he yields to pressure and withdraws.

Chapter XXVI

Latinus, an elderly ruler descended from Saturn, governs the country and has no male descendant but has a daughter, Lavinia. After receiving Aeneas's messengers, he grants friendship and alliance, believing that Aeneas is the foreign son-in-law foretold by the oracle. He resists the demand to drive away the Trojans but finally yields to the pressure for war and withdraws.
