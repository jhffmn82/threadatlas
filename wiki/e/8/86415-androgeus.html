Androgeus

person · 3 works · 6 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: Androgens
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Androgeus attends the Panathenaean solemnities, wins all the sports and contests, becomes familiar with the sons of Pallas, and is murdered near Oenoe in Attica by country people acting on Aegeus’s order.
  • In The Library, Androgeus defeats all competitors at the Panathenian games and is killed by the bull of Marathon after Aegeus sends him against it; another account says jealous competitors ambush and kill him while he travels to Thebes for the games in honor of Laius.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Androgeus attends the Panathenæa as a combatant, wins all prizes, befriends the sons of Pallas, and is assassinated near Œnoë, leading Minos to prepare retaliatory war.

Androgeus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Androgeus, a son of Minos, went to Athens during the Panathenaean solemnities and won all the sports and contests. He became familiar with the sons of Pallas, which made Aegeus jealous. While traveling to Thebes to see a sacred procession, Androgeus was waylaid by country people acting by Aegeus’s order and was murdered near Oenoe in Attica.

Androgeus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Androgeus, son of Minos, attends the Panathenæa at Athens as a combatant and wins all prizes. His skill and polished manners gain the friendship of the sons of Pallas, but he is assassinated near Œnoë; his death leads Minos to prepare retaliatory war.
