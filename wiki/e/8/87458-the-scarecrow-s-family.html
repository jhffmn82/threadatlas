the Scarecrow's family in The Royal Book of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1921

The Scarecrow's family is a group he first believes does not exist, prompting him to seek traces of relatives at the Munchkin farm. It later arrives as three royal sons, the Princes, and fifteen grandsons. Their cold reception, apparent plotting, disbelief in Oz, reminders of his age, and the eldest son's binding him to a bean pole make the family a source of misery.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 2

The Scarecrow believes he has no family, ancestors, aunts, or past. He envies the A-B-Sea Serpent's many relatives and returns to the Munchkin farm to find traces of his own family.

CHAPTER 14

The Scarecrow's family arrives after the three Princes and their fifteen sons have been cruising on the royal pleasure barge. The family gives the Scarecrow a cold and formal reception, and the Princes later appear to plot against him.

CHAPTER 18

The Scarecrow's family includes royal sons and grandsons who remind him of his age and do not believe in Oz. His eldest son bound him to the bean pole, and the Scarecrow describes his sons as unnatural villains who make his life miserable.
