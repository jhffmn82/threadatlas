Latimer's past in Gallegher and Other Stories

Richard Harding Davis · 1890

Latimer's past is examined for hidden dangers to Ellen. Young Latimer says he lived as other young men did but avoided dissipation as vulgar, and denies wild oats, an ugly story, an unreturned consequence, or any woman who could expose him by letter or accusation.

The bishop asks whether Latimer's past contains an ugly story, an unreturned consequence, or another danger that could reach Ellen in the future. Latimer says that he has lived as other young men have lived but avoided dissipation because it seemed vulgar, and he denies having wild oats or a woman who could expose him through an anonymous letter or accusation.
