Hypermnestra

person · 3 works · 11 quoted facts · 10 connections
Also called: Belide, Hypermestra, Inachides, sororis, Ὑπερμνήστρα
  • In Heroides and Amores, Hypermestra is Danaus’s pious daughter who refuses to kill Lynceus, warns him to flee, and remains confined and unrepentant, valuing filial duty and hands free from bloodshed.
  • In The Library, Hypermnestra is Danaus’s eldest daughter, who spares Lynceus because he respected her virginity, is imprisoned by Danaus, and becomes mother of Abas.
  • In Description of Greece, Hypermnestra is Danaus’s only daughter to spare her husband Lynceus; she is tried by Danaus and acquitted by the Argives, then dedicates a statue of Victorious Aphrodite, with Artemis Persuasion later associated with her victory, and signals safety from Larissa.

Hypermestra in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Hypermestra, Danaus’s pious daughter, is confined after faithfully refusing to kill Lynceus. Fearful, she warned him to flee and remains unrepentant, valuing filial duty and hands free from bloodshed.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

XIV

Hypermestra writes to Lynceus from confinement, explaining that she was punished because she remained faithful and did not kill him. She describes her fear, her refusal to strike, her warning that he should flee, and her continued lack of regret for keeping her hands free of blood.

Hypermnestra in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Hypermnestra, Danaus's eldest daughter, was allotted and later united to Lynceus; she spared him because he respected her virginity, was imprisoned by Danaus, and became mother of Abas.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE LIBRARY, II. 1. 1-3

Hypermnestra was the eldest daughter of Danaus and was allotted to Lynceus. She spared Lynceus when the other daughters killed their bridegrooms because he had respected her virginity; Danaus imprisoned her but later united her with Lynceus.

Hypermnestra in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Hypermnestra, Danaus’s only daughter to spare her husband Lynceus, disobeys Danaus’s order to kill him. Danaus tries her, but the Argives acquit her. Afterward she dedicates a statue of Victorious Aphrodite; Artemis Persuasion is later associated with her victory in the trial. She signals safety from Larissa.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIX.

Hypermnestra was the only daughter of Danaus who disobeyed his order and did not kill Lynceus. Danaus brought her to trial, but the Argives acquitted her, after which she dedicated a statue of Victorious Aphrodite; a later temple of Artemis called Persuasion was also associated with her victory over Danaus in the trial.
