Laodamia in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Laodamia, Protesilaus’s wife from Phylace, writes from Thessaly after his departure, grieving anxiously, praying and dreaming of him, and urging his safe return.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

XIII

Laodamia writes to Protesilaus from Thessaly after his departure. She grieves, neglects her appearance, prays at the altars, dreams of him, and repeatedly urges him to avoid danger and return safely.

ES 28 | ᾿Αναχθέντες δὲ ἀπὸ τῆς Τενέδου 1 προσέπλεον

Laodamia loved Protesilaus after his death and made an image resembling him. When Hermes brought Protesilaus up from Hades, she rejoiced on seeing him, but after he was taken back to Hades she stabbed herself to death.
