Ethiopians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Ethiopians are an autochthonous people inhabiting Ethiopia, who claim to be the first people and to have originated Egyptians through a colony drawn by Osiris. They say they instituted religious worship and sacrifices, gave Egyptians customs and institutions, and remained free from foreign rule. Their peoples differ in laws, beliefs, work, dress, arms, food, and funerary practices; most eat flesh, milk, and cheese. They captured Lambulus and another stranger, then sent them away by sea to purify the land and secure six hundred years of peace and prosperity.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

And now we purpose to say something briefly of a certain island*

The Ethiopians captured Lambulus and another stranger and carried them to the maritime parts of Ethiopia. They believed that sending two strangers away by sea would purge their land and preserve peace and prosperity for six hundred years.

DIODORUS SICULUS- / BOOK III.

The Ethiopians claim to be autochthonous and the first people, arguing that the southern regions were the first places where living creatures arose. They say they founded religious worship and sacrifices, remained free from foreign domination, and gave Egyptians several customs and institutions. Different Ethiopian peoples are described as having varied laws, beliefs, occupations, clothing, weapons, diets, and funerary practices.
