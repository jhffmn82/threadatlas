Mr. Darbey in Dandy Dick

Arthur W. Pinero · 1893

Mr. Darbey, Nugent Darbey, is a young, pompous Hussar lieutenant, officer, gentleman, and violin player, though also classified as a civilian in the military-civilian distinction. He loves and is attracted to Sheba Jedd. He first visits the Deanery with Gerald Tarver, helps arrange the girls’ attendance at a ball, and plans to appear as Charles the Martyr. He patronizes and quarrels with the Dean, plays violin with Sheba accompanying, resents Major Tarver’s singing, and later returns for his violin. In shabby Cavalier dress, he says the ball was cancelled and guards the ruined Swan Inn with Tarver. Finally, he brings Major Tarver to Salome and Sheba, then proposes to Sheba; after reflection, she accepts.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

DANDY DICK. / THE FIRST ACT.

Nugent Darbey visits the Deanery with Gerald Tarver and helps arrange the girls' attendance at the ball. He is described as a young, pompous officer who intends to appear as Charles the Martyr.

THE SECOND ACT.

Mr. Darbey patronizes the Dean, argues with him about an army question, and plays his violin while Sheba accompanies him. He resents Major Tarver's singing ambitions, later invites the Dean to visit him, and returns to retrieve his violin after the Dean declares that he will not see him again.

The curtains at the window are pushed aside, and SALOME and SHEBA enter; both in

Nugent Darbey appears in shabby Cavalier dress with Gerald Tarver and apologizes for the cancelled ball. He says that officers and gentlemen were responding to the sacred call of duty and claims that he and Tarver are guarding the ruins of the Swan Inn.

_The second scene is the Morning Room at the Deanery again.

Mr. Darbey brings the weak Major Tarver to Salome and Sheba and claims that the Major must propose to recover his health. He proposes to Sheba, who accepts him after asking for a brief period to ponder the question.
