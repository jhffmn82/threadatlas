Abstract written by a model

Latimer is a young man, known well to the bishop and a frequent visitor to his study, who loves Ellen deeply and seeks to marry her. He presents himself as professionally established, self-respecting, and personally disinclined to dissipation, denying wild oats, anonymous letters, or any damaging past account. Yet he has loved another woman in a former forbidden relationship and remains separated from her. When the bishop raises a thirty-day hypothetical, Latimer refuses to answer, protests that its conditions are impossible, and says the other woman is neither dead nor available to a man like him, perhaps being married. He insists that he now prefers and loves Ellen above all else, believing God sent her as a reward. In pleading with Ellen, he says he overcame the earlier love by refusing to yield to it, and that the struggle made him honest, clean, and worthy of her. When Ellen tells him to go to the other woman, he leaves, turning away from the north where she is located toward rooms in the south.

Latimer is in one work on this site, Gallegher and Other Stories. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

" THE OTHER WOMAN Young Latimer stood on one of the lower steps of the hall stairs, leaning with one hand on the broad railing and smiling down at her.

Young Latimer tells the bishop that he loves Ellen and wants to marry her. He describes himself as unwilling to sacrifice his self-respect for dissipation and denies having wild oats, an anonymous letter, or a damaging story in his past. He refuses to answer the bishop's thirty-day hypothetical and angrily objects when the bishop says that he has answered it.

"Well, and suppose this other woman did exist, what then?

Latimer argues that the conditions described by the bishop are impossible. He says the other woman is beyond the love of any man such as himself, confirms that she is not dead, and suggests that she may be married.

The older man stepped quickly across the floor, but before he reached the middle

Latimer pleads with Ellen to believe that he loves only her and argues that the test based on hypothetical conditions is unjust and meaningless. He says that he overcame his love for another woman when it became impossible and that this struggle made him honest, clean, and worthy of Ellen. When Ellen tells him to go to the other woman, he leaves and turns resolutely away from the north, where she is located.
