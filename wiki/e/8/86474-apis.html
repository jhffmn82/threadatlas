Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Apis is a sacred bull or ox consecrated to Osiris and worshipped at Memphis, generally regarded by Egyptians as a god; after his death, priests found a resembling calf, ended mourning, led it through the Nile's city, barged it to Vulcan's grove, and installed it there. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Apis is the sacred bull of Memphis, worshipped as an incarnation of Osiris whose soul inhabited his body; he was reverently housed, fed, transported, sacrificed to, and celebrated, gave oracles by accepting or rejecting food, and could not live beyond twenty-five years, after which a new Apis was found.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

MYTH OF OSIRIS AND ISIS

Apis was the sacred bull of Memphis and was worshipped with great reverence. He was ceremonially housed, fed, transported to Memphis, honored with sacrifices and festivals, and consulted as an oracle according to whether he accepted food from an inquirer. He was not permitted to live beyond twenty-five years, and a new Apis was found as late as the reign of Hadrian.
