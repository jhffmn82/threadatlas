Osymandyas's sepulchre

place · 1 work · 4 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: monument of the sepulchre, sepulchre

Abstract written by a model

Osymandyas's sepulchre is a ten-furlong circuit containing monumental marble porticoes, a four-square gallery borne by stone figures, and Egypt's largest seated statue of Osymandyas. It unfolds from its entrance through sculptures, altar, judgment court, and the sacred library called “The cure of the Mind,” then rooms of food and sacred animals to a royal burial chamber. Its summit carries a gold border recording days and astronomical observations.

Osymandyas's sepulchre is in one work on this site, The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

DIODORUS SICULUS. / BOOK I.

Osymandyas's sepulchre was described as ten furlongs in circuit, with large marble porticoes, galleries supported by colossal stone figures, statues, sculptures, an altar, a court of judgment, a library called the cure of the Mind, rooms containing food and sacred animals, and a royal burial chamber. Its summit had a gold border measuring three hundred and sixty-five cubits, marked with the days of the year and astronomical observations.
