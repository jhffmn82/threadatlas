Egyptian kings in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Egyptian kings first appears

Abstract written by a model

Egyptian kings were mostly natives chosen for usefulness and service rather than inheritance. Beginning with Menis after the gods, later rulers were remembered for buildings, conquests, worship, monuments, and discoveries supporting human life. They had to follow Egyptian laws and customs in public and private conduct; at death, judgment of their rule and funeral honors encouraged justice and virtue.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

DIODORUS SICULUS. / BOOK I.

Egyptian kings were credited with discovering things necessary for human support, and kingship was said originally to have been given to those most useful and serviceable to the people rather than inherited. Menis was identified as the first king after the gods, while later kings were recorded for their buildings, conquests, worship, and monuments.

CHAP. VI.

Egyptian kings were expected to conform to the laws in their public administration, private conduct, meals, punishments, and rewards. They were praised or condemned at death according to how justly they had ruled, and the fear of losing funeral honors encouraged later kings to act virtuously.
