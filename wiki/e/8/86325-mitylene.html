Abstract written by a model

Mitylene is a city built around an old city on a small island and a later mainland addition. Though an exception among Lesbos’s cities in remaining loyal to Athens, its people revolted and the Athenians besieged it; famine forced surrender, the inhabitants were spared, and its walls demolished. Later, it held an Athenian garrison, endured another siege, and was relieved after Arginusae.

Mitylene is in one work on this site, The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Diotinus executing the place of chief magistrate at Athens, the

Mitylene was besieged by the Athenians after its people revolted from Athens. When famine and internal disorder forced its surrender, the Athenians debated severe punishment but ultimately spared the inhabitants, while demolishing the city's walls.

CHAP. X.

Mitylene contained an Athenian garrison and was reached by Conon after the naval engagement. The city received Athenians and Mityleneans from the countryside, prepared for defense, and was then surrounded by Callicratides.

CHAP. XIV.

Mitylene was under siege when the Athenians sailed to Arginusae to relieve it. After the battle and storm, the Athenians reached Mitylene, took Conon and forty ships, and then went to Samos.
