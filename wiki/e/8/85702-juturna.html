Juturna in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Juturna is an immortal water nymph and goddess, Turnus’s sister, devoted to protecting him from Aeneas. Commanded by Juno, she tries to save Turnus: disguised as Camertus, she incites the Rutulians and Latins against the treaty and encourages Tolumnius; later, as Metiscus, she drives Turnus’s chariot away whenever Aeneas approaches. She urges Turnus to ignore the town’s alarms, restores his Daunian sword after his borrowed weapon breaks, and prays to Faunus to stop Aeneas’s spear. After Jove sends a Fury, she abandons the field and plunges into her stream in despair, though immortal and unable to die.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XII

Juturna, Turnus’s sister, is urged by the Queen of Heaven to try to save him. She disguises herself as Camertus, inflames the Rutulians and Latins against the treaty, and encourages King Tolumnius to interpret an eagle attacking a swan as a favorable omen.

When Turnus saw the Trojan quit the plain,

Juturna sees Aeneas approaching with his troops and flees in fear. She takes the shape, armor, and appearance of Metiscus, Turnus’s charioteer, and repeatedly drives Turnus away whenever Aeneas nears him.

Now Turnus rolls aloof o’er empty plains,

Juturna drives Turnus's chariot and urges him to ignore the alarms in the town. She restores Turnus's Daunian sword after his borrowed weapon breaks, but Jove sends a Fury against her, causing her to abandon the field and plunge into her stream in despair.
