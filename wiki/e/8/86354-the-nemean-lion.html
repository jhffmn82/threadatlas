the Nemean lion

animal · 3 works · 5 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Nemean lion, lion, lion in the forest of Ne- niea, monster, the beast, the lion, this monster, τὸν λέοντα

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Nemean lion is a monstrous, invulnerable beast that Hercules strangles and whose skin he wears as protection. In The Library, the Nemean lion is an invulnerable beast that Hercules hunts, chokes, and carries to Mycenae after Eurystheus orders him to bring its hide. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Nemean lion terrorizes the valley of Nemea until Hercules strangles it and carries its body to Eurystheus.

The Nemean lion is an invulnerable beast begotten by Typhon. After Eurystheus orders Hercules to bring its hide, Hercules hunts it, drives it into its two-mouthed cave, blocks one entrance, chokes it through the other, and carries its body to Mycenae.
