Abstract written by a model

Air is in continual agitation during the formation of the world. Its fiery part rises upward by lightness, with stars and sun in the highest region. It is called Minerva by the Egyptians, regarded as naturally incorruptible, and contains vapours. In dry regions it thickens; it can condense, drift, consolidate, fall, adhere to living bodies, and produce movements and chilling effects attributed to apparitions.

DIODORUS SICULUS. / BOOK L

The air is described as being in continual agitation. Its fiery part rose upward because of its lightness, while the stars and the sun were contained in the highest region.

We shall first speak of the barbarians; not that we judge them

The air is associated with Minerva and is regarded as naturally incorruptible and situated in the highest part of the universe.

Having now given an account of these countries, it will well agree

The air in the dry region is said to become thick because few vapors arise to produce winds. According to the natural philosophers cited, it can condense into various shapes, drift and consolidate, fall to the ground, adhere to living bodies, and produce the movements and chilling effects attributed to the apparitions.
