overflowing of the Nile

event · 1 work · 4 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Nile inundation, increase of the Nile, inundation, inundation of the Nile, overflowing of the river, risings of this river

Abstract written by a model

The Nile inundation is Egypt’s annual overflowing. It begins around the summer solstice, rises through summer until the autumnal equinox, then gradually falls toward the winter solstice. It spreads water and new soil over tilled and untilled ground, fertilizing Egypt and enabling abundant crops with little labor. Records attribute it most nearly to summer rains in mountainous Ethiopia.

CHAP. III.

The Nile inundation begins at the summer solstice, rises until the autumn equinox, and gradually falls again toward the winter solstice. It spreads water and new soil over Egypt, enabling farmers to irrigate fields easily and producing abundant crops with little labor.

The Nile begins to swell around the summer solstice and falls again after the autumnal equinox. The unit reviews several explanations for this annual overflowing and concludes that the abundance of summer rains in the mountainous parts of Ethiopia comes nearest to explaining it.
