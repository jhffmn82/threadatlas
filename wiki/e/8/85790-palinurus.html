Palinurus in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Palinurus is the watchful master pilot and steersman of Aeneas’s Trojan fleet. He recognizes a storm, orders the crew to shorten sail and row, and advises Aeneas to seek Sicily rather than continue toward Italy. Though he resists Sleep and clings to the rudder, he is overcome, thrown into the sea, and dies after reaching land. As a newly dead, unburied ghost, he cannot cross the Styx until the Sibyl promises him a tomb on a coast named for him.

BOOK V

Palinurus, serving as pilot, recognizes the approaching storm and orders the crew to shorten sail and use their oars. He advises that the fleet cannot safely continue toward Italy and recommends steering to Sicily.

Thus far the sacred sports they celebrate:

Palinurus resists Sleep’s offer to steer and keeps hold of the rudder while fighting drowsiness. Sleep overcomes him and throws him into the sea, after which Aeneas takes the helm and mourns his death.

Ye realms, yet unreveal’d to human sight,

Palinurus tells Aeneas that he fell asleep while steering and was swept from the ship, later dying after reaching land. Because he remains unburied, he cannot cross the Styx; the Sibyl promises that the coast will receive a tomb named for him.

Palinurus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Palinurus is Aeneas’s faithful pilot and steersman of the Trojan fleet. He refuses to leave the helm, but Somnus makes him sleep and casts him overboard; Neptune keeps the ship on course as Aeneas mourns and takes control. Drowned and initially unburied, Palinurus is later promised burial and a promontory named Cape Palinurus.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXIV

Palinurus is Aeneas’s faithful pilot who refuses to abandon the helm despite the calm sea and favorable wind. Somnus puts him to sleep with Lethaean dew, pushes him overboard, and takes the helm from his grasp. Neptune keeps the ship on course, while Aeneas mourns Palinurus and assumes control of the ship.

Chapter XXV

Palinurus, Aeneas's pilot, was drowned after the ship's rudder broke and carried him away. Because he lacked burial rites, he could not cross the river immediately; the Sibyl consoles him by saying that the people who found his body would bury it and that the promontory would be called Cape Palinurus.
