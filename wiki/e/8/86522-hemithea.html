Hemithea in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Hemithea, a goddess worshipped at Castabus, was formerly Molpadia, daughter of Staphylus and Chrysothemides. After Apollo brought her safely there, Chersonesians honored her revelations; she appeared in dreams with remedies, healed the sick, and protected childbirth.

Hemithea was the name given to Molpadia, one of the daughters of Staphylus and Chrysothemides, after Apollo brought her safely to Castabus. She was honored by the Chersonesians for her revelations and was believed to heal the sick and safely deliver women in childbirth.
