Androgeus's death

event · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Androgeus to be murdered, Androgeus’s death, death of his son Androgeus, death of {his son} Androgeus, killed, son’s death, the murder committed, the murder of Androgeus

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the murder of Androgeus is described as an ambush arranged by Aegeus near Oenoe in Attica, leading Minos to seek justice and to conflict, drought, famine, war, and the Minotaur's tribute. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Androgeus's death is described as a murder or assassination at Athens near Œnoë; Ægeus or the Bull of Marathon may have caused it, prompting Minos to seek retribution and prepare war against Athens.

Abstract written by a model

The murder of Androgeus was arranged by Aegeus because he feared that Androgeus’s association with the sons of Pallas, aided by Minos, might deprive him of his kingdom. Androgeus was ambushed and killed near Oenoe in Attica. Minos sought justice for the murder, and the resulting conflict led to drought, famine, war, and the Minotaur’s tribute.

Androgeus's death in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Androgeus's death first appears

Abstract written by a model

Androgeus’s death was a murder or assassination at Athens, near Œnoë. Accounts differ: Ægeus caused or ordered it, while Apollodorus attributes it to the Bull of Marathon. Minos sought retribution, gathered auxiliary forces, and prepared war against Athens.

Androgeus’s death occurred near Œnoë, where Ægeus caused him to be assassinated. Apollodorus gives a different account, saying that the Bull of Marathon killed him, while Diodorus Siculus and Plutarch state that Ægeus ordered the murder.
