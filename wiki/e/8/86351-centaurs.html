Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Centaurs are hybrid beings, horse-like in body and human in understanding, with reported origins from Ixion and Nephele or a cloud shaped like Juno. They fight the Lapiths, attack women at Perithous and Hippodamia’s marriage feast, raid neighbors, then attack Hercules after Pholus opens wine; Hercules kills many and drives the rest away.

Centaurs in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Centaurs are described as cloud-born monsters with a twofold form, partly human and partly horse, though they are also explained as Thessalian horsemen, a rude northern race, or rapacious people. Said to descend from Centaurus and Ixion, with a cloud as mother, many live in Nephele and claim succession to Pirithoüs. Called wild beasts and associated with wild, horse-riding life, they include female Centaurs. At Pirithoüs’s wedding, Eurytus attacks Hippodame and the Centaurs fight the Lapiths with banquet objects and weapons. Many die, others flee or scatter; after defeat they retreat through Arcadia toward Malea, and in some accounts Italy.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

FABLES III. AND IV. [XII.146-535]

The Centaurs are guests at Pirithoüs’s wedding feast. After Eurytus attacks Hippodame, they join the fighting, using banquet vessels and other objects as weapons; many are killed or wounded.

The Centaurs attack the Lapiths and include Demoleon, Dorylas, Phæocomes, Latreus, Monychus, Cyllarus, and others named by Nestor. They are repeatedly killed in combat, and their weapons cannot wound Cæneus; after burying him beneath trees, the surviving Centaurs are eventually killed, driven into flight, or scattered by night.

EXPLANATION.

The Centaurs are variously explained as Thessalian horsemen, a rude northern race, or a rapacious people who ravaged neighboring country. They were said to descend from Centaurus, the son of Ixion, and were later portrayed as beings partly human and partly horse; after their defeat by the Lapithæ, they retreated through Arcadia toward Malea and, in some accounts, Italy.

Centaurs in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Centaurs are half-man, half-horse people, admitted to human companionship and regarded as nobler than Satyrs. Some fought the Lapithae violently at Pirithous’s marriage, while Chiron was renowned as wise and just.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter X

The Centaurs were beings who were men to the waist and horses below, and they were regarded as nobler than Satyrs. Some Centaurs caused a violent battle with the Lapithae at the marriage of Pirithous, while Chiron was renowned as wise and just.
