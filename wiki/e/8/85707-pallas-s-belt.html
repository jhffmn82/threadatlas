Pallas’s belt in The Aeneid

Virgil · 28 to 18 BC · the work’s page · read it where Pallas’s belt first appears

Abstract written by a model

Pallas’s belt is an ornate golden belt made by Eurytion. After killing Pallas, Turnus takes it as spoils and wears it as a trophy; its images show fifty brides killing their bridegrooms. Seeing the belt turns Aeneas’s compassion into anger, and he kills Turnus in Pallas’s name.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

But, in another part, th’ Arcadian horse

Turnus takes Pallas’s gold-inlaid belt after killing and trampling him. The belt depicts fifty brides who deprived their bridegrooms of life on one mournful night. The narrator says that Turnus will later wish he had never touched or worn these fatal spoils.

Now Turnus rolls aloof o’er empty plains,

Turnus wears the golden belt that he took from the dying Pallas as a trophy. When Aeneas sees it, his compassion for the wounded Turnus turns back into anger, and he kills Turnus in Pallas's name.
