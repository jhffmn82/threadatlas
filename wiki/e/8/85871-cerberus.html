Cerberus

Also called: Cerberus, with his foam, Echidnean dog, dog, the barking porter, the dog, the keeper, the three-headed dog Cerberus, the triple porter of the Stygian sound, three-headed dog, Κέρβερον, οὗτος, τοῦτον, τὸν Κέρβερον

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Cerberus is the dog of hell whom Hercules brings up from the infernal regions in his chain and presents to open view. In The Aeneid, Cerberus is the triple porter guarding the Underworld entrance, whom the Sibyl puts to sleep with a drugged sop so Aeneas can pass. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Cerberus is an Echidnean dog guarding the gates and portals of the Infernal Regions, whom Hercules drags from Tartarus in chains. In The Library, Cerberus is a three-headed dog and guardian of the underworld who is mastered by Hercules, brought to Eurystheus, and returned to Hades. In Description of Greece, Cerberus is described either as a deadly serpent at Tænarum or as a three-headed dog of Hades whom Hercules brings through Tænarum's cave. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Cerberus is a three-headed snake-necked monster of the lower world whom Hercules takes unarmed to Eurystheus and returns below.

Cerberus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Cerberus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cerberus, an Echidnean dog with three heads or three simultaneous barks, guards the gates and portals of the Infernal Regions. Homer calls it a dog, while Pausanias describes a serpent. Hercules drags it from Tartarus through Acherusia in chains; it barks furiously and scatters white foam. Its name means “one that devours flesh,” and its foam was believed to poison herbs and produce aconite.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Cerberus guards the gates of the Infernal Regions and has three heads. Hercules drags him away in chains; the dog barks furiously and scatters white foam over the fields.

notes .. notes

Cerberus was said to have been dragged from Tartarus by Hercules through Acherusia, a cavern regarded as an entrance to the Infernal Regions. The explanation says that the story may have been based on a serpent near Tænarus, though Homer described Cerberus as a dog; its name was said to mean “one that devours flesh.” Its foam was believed to have poisoned herbs and produced aconite, although this may reflect the abundance of poisonous plants in Thessaly.

Cerberus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Cerberus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Cerberus was described either as a deadly serpent reared at Tænarum or, by later writers, as a three-headed dog of Hades; tradition says Hercules brought the dog from the lower world through Tænarum's cave.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIII.

Cerberus was traditionally described as the dog that Hercules brought up from the lower world through the cave at Tænarum. Hecatæus instead said that Cerberus was a deadly serpent reared at Tænarum, while the text notes that Homer did not give the underworld dog this name or a complete description.
