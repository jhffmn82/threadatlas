Tyrians in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

The Tyrians are Dido’s people, building Carthage through organized labor: walls, a citadel, foundations, laws, and a theatre. They receive the shipwrecked Trojans, attend Dido’s feast, admire Trojan gifts and Cupid in disguise, praise Iopas, and celebrate proposed peace. At the end, Dido imagines them destroying the Trojans at her command and curses them to perpetual hatred and war against Aeneas and his people.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

To whom, with sorrow streaming from his eyes,

The Tyrians are building Dido’s new city by raising walls and a citadel, laying foundations, making laws, and preparing a theatre. They are compared to bees laboring together, and they pity and receive the shipwrecked Trojans.

Cupid meantime assum’d his form and face,

The Tyrian people are admitted to the feast and recline on the painted couches. They admire the Trojan gifts and Cupid in disguise, praise Iopas's song with loud shouts, and join in the celebration of the proposed peace.

The wretched queen, pursued by cruel fate,

Dido imagines that the Tyrians would have attacked and destroyed the Trojan band at their queen’s command. In her dying curse, she orders the Tyrians to maintain perpetual hatred and mortal wars against Aeneas, his people, and their name.
