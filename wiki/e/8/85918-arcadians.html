Abstract written by a model

In The Aeneid, the Arcadians are Evander's people who ally with Aeneas, provide troops against the Latins, and mourn Pallas after his death. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the Arcadians are a Peloponnesian people with ancient traditions, religious practices, shifting alliances, and a long history of warfare.

Arcadians in The Aeneid

Virgil · 28 to 18 BC · the work’s page · read it where Arcadians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Arcadians are Evander’s people, living at Pallanteum, where they perform rites to Hercules. Led by Evander and allied with Aeneas and Pallas against the Latins, they provide troops and march with the Trojans toward Tuscan land. Their horsemen first scatter when rough ground hinders them, but Pallas’s example and their shame, grief, and anger bring them back to the charge. Turnus kills Pallas and routs them; they carry him away and mourn him in a torchlit funeral procession.

Arcadians in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Arcadians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Arcadians are a people or nation whose inhabitants were called Pelasgi before Arcas’s reign, when they became Arcadians. Under Arcas they learned agriculture, breadmaking, weaving, and spinning; they were later ruled by Echemus, Agapenor, and other kings. They preserve a very old tradition of wolf transformation and maintain local sacred accounts: Mount Lycæus is Olympus or the Sacred Hill, and its Cretea, not the island of Crete, was where Zeus was reared. They associate Arne with a lamb, connect Mantinea’s river Ophis with Antinoe’s serpent guide, place the giants’ battle with the gods at Bathos, and sacrifice there to thunder, lightning, and storms. Their strongest religious distinction is worship of Despœna above all gods. They perform her Mystic rites, exclude pomegranate wood from her temple, and sacrifice animals by cutting off whichever limb a worshipper first touches. Their history is marked by shifting warfare and alliances. They fought at Ilium, supported Messenia and Aristodemus against the Lacedæmonians, and punished two men named Aristocrates by stoning: one for defiling Artemis Hymnia’s sanctuary and one for bribery and betrayal of the Messenians. They fought at Platæa, at times served the Lacedæmonians against the Athenians under compulsion, joined Agesilaus in Asia Minor, and fought at Leuctra. After the Lacedæmonian reverse there, they joined the Thebans, though they later stayed neutral against Philip and the Macedonians, Antipater, and the Galati. They became especially zealous members of the Achæan League. Most supported Antony at Actium, while Mantineans supported Augustus. They accompanied Evander from Pallantium in a colony near the Tiber. In the Peloponnese, they invited Epaminondas, evacuated insecure small towns, and inhabited the new Megalopolis. Finally, about a thousand picked men who had fought with Critolaus went to Elatea and were received through old kinship, but Romans met and cut them to pieces at Chæronea, presented as punishment for their earlier abandonment of Greeks fighting Philip and the Macedonians.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

10 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XV.

About 1,000 picked Arcadian men who had fought with Critolaus marched to Elatea and were received there through old kinship. They were later met by Metellus and the Romans at Chæronea and cut to pieces, which the text presents as punishment for their earlier abandonment of Greeks fighting Philip and the Macedonians.

CHAPTER IV.

During Arcas’s reign, the inhabitants were called Arcadians instead of Pelasgi. Arcas taught his people agriculture, breadmaking, weaving, and spinning.

CHAPTER V.

The Arcadians were ruled by Echemus, Agapenor, and a succession of later kings. They supported the Messenians against the Lacedæmonians, stoned Aristocrates to death for defiling the sanctuary of Artemis Hymnia, and later stoned another Aristocrates for accepting Lacedæmonian bribes and betraying the Messenians.

CHAPTER VI.

The Arcadians fought against Ilium and alongside the Messenians against the Lacedæmonians, and they took part in the battle of Platæa. They fought under the Lacedæmonians when compelled, but after the Lacedæmonians were defeated at Leuctra they joined the Thebans; they remained neutral against Philip, the Macedonians, and Antipater, and they were among the most zealous members of the Achæan League.

CHAPTER VIII.

The Arcadians said that Poseidon was raised among flocks and that Rhea deceived Cronos with a foal, and they associated the fountain Arne with the word for lamb. They also regarded Antinoe’s serpent guide as the reason the river beside Mantinea was called Ophis. Most Arcadians supported Antony at Actium, unlike the Mantineans, who supported Augustus.

CHAPTER XXXV.

The Arcadians claim that Belemina originally belonged to them and was taken by the Lacedæmonians. Their account is judged unlikely because the Thebans would presumably have restored the territory if the claim had been just.

CHAPTER XXXVII.

The Arcadians worship Despœna above all the gods and bring every kind of wood into her temple except pomegranate wood. In her Mystic rites they sacrifice whatever animal each person has, cutting off whichever limb of the victim he happens to touch rather than cutting its throat.

CHAPTER XXXVIII.

The Arcadians call Mount Lycæus Olympus or the Sacred Hill, and they say Zeus was reared there. They maintain that the mountain's Cretea, rather than the island of Crete, was the place where Zeus was reared, and they honor the Nymphs Thisoa, Neda, and Hagno in connection with this story.

CHAPTER XLIII.

Arcadians accompanied Evander from Pallantium and formed a colony near the river Tiber. They inhabited part of what later became Rome, and that area was called Pallantium in memory of their town in Arcadia.

CHAPTER XIV.

The Arcadians invited Epaminondas to cooperate in the Peloponnese. He persuaded them to evacuate their insecure small towns and established one large town for them, called Megalopolis.
