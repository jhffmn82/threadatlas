the plague in the Carthaginian army in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

A plague raged in Africa and the Carthaginian army after temples of Ceres and Proserpina were plundered. Heat, crowding, marshes, and unburied bodies intensified it, causing severe illnesses and rapid deaths, killing many soldiers and leaving Carthage unprepared for Dionysius's attack.

The plague in the Carthaginian army followed the plundering of the temples of Ceres and Proserpina. It was intensified by the hot season, crowded and marshy conditions, and unburied bodies, causing fevers, swellings, dysenteries, boils, madness, and rapid death.

plague among the Carthaginians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

A disease seized the Carthaginians after they began operations against Syracuse. As their fortunes reversed following earlier successes in Sicily and defeat by sea and land, the plague killed one hundred and fifty thousand men, leaving their bodies unburied on the ground.
