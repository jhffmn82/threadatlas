Dorians

group · 3 works · 9 quoted facts · 11 connections
Also called: DORlA, Dorian, DorianS, Dorienses, the Dorians, the Doriaoi

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Dorians settled Crete, enrolled in Himera, and lost and regained cities around Parnassus with Lacedaemonian aid. In Description of Greece, the Dorians were a people who entered and occupied the Peloponnese during the return of the Heraclidae, displacing and expelling earlier inhabitants and founding settlements. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the Dorians were an original participating Greek state at the general Council of the Greeks and later returned to the Peloponnese by ship.

Dorians in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Dorians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Dorians are a people who came from Mount Œta and, during the return of the Heraclidæ, sought entry into the Peloponnese under the sons of Aristomachus, with Oxylus as leader. They displaced Achæans, drove out descendants of Neleus, and occupied territory. They assigned Argos to Temenus; Cresphontes obtained Messenia, where older Messenians accepted him and land was divided among Dorians. They colonized Hermione and seized Sicyon under Phalces, after which Sicyonians became Dorians and joined Argolis. They entered Phlius amid divided response, destroyed Amyclæ, besieged Helus, made its inhabitants Helots, expelled Achæans from Geronthræ, and settled colonists there.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

The Dorians seized Sicyon with Phalces, the son of Temenus, who spared Lacestades because he too was a descendant of Hercules. The Sicyonians subsequently became Dorians and joined the political territory of Argolis.

CHAPTER XII.

Dorians entered Phlius under Rhegnidas, who demanded that they and he receive lands and that he become king. Some Phliasians accepted these terms, while others resisted.

CHAPTER XIX.

The Dorians destroyed Amyclæ and reduced Helus by siege. They gave the name Helots to the inhabitants of Helus and later used the term generally for their slaves, including Messenians. They also drove the Achæans from Geronthræ and settled colonists of their own there.

BOOK IV.--MESSENIA.

The Dorians assigned Argos to Temenus and allowed Cresphontes to obtain Messenia by lot. The older Messenians remained in the country and agreed to Cresphontes as king and to a division of the land among the Dorians.

BOOK V.--ELIS.

The Dorians came from Mount Œta and displaced the Achæans from their original land. Under the sons of Aristomachus, they sought to return to the Peloponnese and accepted Oxylus as their leader.

Dorians in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Dorians first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Dorians were an original participating Greek state at the general Council of the Greeks. They possessed Lacedæmon when Preugenes took the statue, built a fleet at Naupactus, and, after an unsuccessful return under Hyllus, drove the Achæans from Argos and Lacedæmon. Three generations later they returned by ship near Rhium.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Description of Greece, Volume II / BOOK VII.--ACHAIA. / CHAPTER I.

The Dorians drove the Achæans and their king Tisamenus out of Argos and Lacedæmon. Their presence led the displaced Achæans to seek settlement among the Ionians.

CHAPTER V.

The Dorians, led by Hyllus, attempted to return to the Peloponnese but were defeated by the Achæans near the Isthmus of Corinth, and Echemus killed Hyllus in single combat. Three generations later they returned by ship and landed near the Promontory of Rhium.
