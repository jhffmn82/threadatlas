Lesbian women in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Lesbian women, including unmarried and married proles, are sea-daughters named by the Aeolian lyre who come to hear Sappho; loved by her despite reproach, they are asked to stop until Phaon returns.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Cur tamen Actiacas miseram me mittis ad oras, 185

The Lesbian women are described as daughters of the sea who come to Sappho's citharas. Sappho asks them to stop coming until Phaon returns, because his absence has removed what they formerly admired in her.
