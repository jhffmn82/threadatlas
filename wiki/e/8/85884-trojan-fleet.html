Trojan fleet in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

The Trojan fleet is Aeneas’s fleet of Trojan ships, carrying him and his allies and later allied with the Tuscan army for Troy’s relief. It begins prepared for departure from Sicily, but Aeolus’s winds scatter it: one ship sinks, others strike rocks or sand, and only seven ships remain to reach a secure Libyan harbor. Dido offers timber, repairs, and convoy ships; Aeneas repairs and secretly readies the galleys, then sails away before dawn, provoking Dido’s fury. Driven from the Punic shore, the fleet shelters again in Sicily, resumes its voyage after rites and games, survives an attack by the matrons when Jove’s storm saves most ships, and is repaired before sailing toward Italy under Neptune’s protection. Beneath the Trojan camp walls, Turnus burns it, but divine transformation rescues the ships. Finally, thirty ships carry Aeneas and chosen allies toward the camp; the transformed galleys warn him of Ascanius’s danger, and, despite Tarchon’s wreck during landing, the other vessels reach shore.

Narrative, section by section

8 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK I

The Trojan fleet leaves the Sicilian shores but is attacked by winds raised by Aeolus. One ship is overwhelmed, several are driven onto rocks or sand, and seven ships eventually reach a secure harbor on the Libyan coast.

To whom, with sorrow streaming from his eyes,

The Trojan fleet is reduced from twice ten ships to seven after storms. Venus predicts that the scattered ships and their crews have reached the harbor or are approaching, and Dido offers timber, repairs, and ships of convoy.

The pious prince was seiz’d with sudden fear;

Aeneas orders the fleet repaired and secretly prepares it for departure. The fleet is soon afloat, with its galleys ready in the harbor, and the Trojans continue loading it.

The wretched queen, pursued by cruel fate,

The Trojan fleet is prepared for departure during the night and sails away before dawn. When Dido sees the ships at sea, she is overcome with fury and despair and wishes to pursue, burn, and destroy them.

BOOK V

The Trojan fleet is driven from the Punic shore by a storm and takes refuge in Sicily. After the funeral rites and games, it is prepared to resume the voyage toward Italy.

Thus far the sacred sports they celebrate:

The fleet lies securely in port when Iris incites the matrons to attack it. Jove’s storm saves most of it, and the surviving ships are repaired before the fleet sails toward Italy under Neptune’s protection.

BOOK IX

The Trojan fleet lies close beneath the camp walls, where Turnus finds it vulnerable to attack. The fleet is set on fire, but the ships are rescued by divine transformation.

BOOK X

The fleet carries Aeneas and his allies toward the Trojan camp, with thirty ships transporting the chosen forces. The former Trojan galleys appear as Nereids and warn Aeneas that Ascanius is under attack; during the landing, Tarchon’s ship is wrecked while the other vessels reach shore.

the ships in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

The ships, built at Mount Ida from sacred-grove timber, carry Aeneas’s fleet toward Latium. Battered by storms, they suffer torn sails, broken cables, wrecks, and strandings; seven survivors shelter in harbor and Neptune frees them. Repaired, fitted out, garlanded, and readied with their crews, they sail from Thrace through Delos, Crete, and later coasts, eventually reaching Cajeta’s bay.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK I

The ships are battered by waves, torn sails, broken cables, and rushing seawater. Several are wrecked or stranded, while seven surviving ships enter the sheltered harbor and are later freed from the shoals by Neptune.

BOOK III

Aeneas’s fleet is built from timber near Antandros and Ida. It sails from Thrace to Delos and Crete, is scattered by a storm after leaving Crete, and later reaches the Strophades, Leucate, Epirus, and Chaonia.

The pious prince was seiz’d with sudden fear;

The ships are repaired, fitted out, and prepared for sailing. Their crews gather aboard, bind them with garlands, invoke the sea gods, and prepare to use the wind.
