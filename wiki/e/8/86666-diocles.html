Diocles

Abstract written by a model

Diocles is a Syracusan political leader, lawgiver, and chief among lawmakers, with great authority among the people. He advises revenge and punishment; after the Syracusans change their minds, his advice leads to executions and the imprisonment of Athenian citizens. He prescribes the Dioclean laws, opposes dishonesty, and finally kills himself to enforce his own law against entering court armed.

Diocles is in one work on this site, The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Diocles's advice is confirmed after the Syracusans change their minds. The decision following his advice results in the execution of the generals and their confederates and the imprisonment of the Athenian citizens in the quarries.

CHAP. 111.

Diocles prescribed laws for the Syracusans and became the chief of the lawmakers who created the Dioclean laws. He strictly opposed dishonesty and once killed himself after entering court armed, thereby enforcing the law he had made against doing so.
