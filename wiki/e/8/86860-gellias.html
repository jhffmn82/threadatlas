Gellias, a wealthy Agrigentine known for integrity and ingenuity, generously housed, fed, and clothed strangers and horsemen despite a mean appearance; he later died in Minerva after setting fire to the temple.

CHAP. XII.

Gellias was described as the richest of the Agrigentines at that time. He built rooms for public entertainment, invited passing strangers into his house, once clothed and entertained five hundred Gelonian horsemen, and possessed an enormous wine cellar and cistern; despite his wealth, he had a mean appearance and was known for ingenuity.
