Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Semiramis is an Assyrian queen who rules as queen regent, directs Babylon’s construction and other works across Asia, invades India, and eventually yields the crown and disappears. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Semiramis is associated with Babylon as the builder of its brick walls and is said to have consecrated a temple near Hieropolis to her mother, Dercetis.

Semiramis in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Semiramis first appears

Abstract written by a model

Semiramis is a queen of Assyria, portrayed as exceptionally beautiful, intelligent, courageous, able, and ambitious. Raised by Simma and named from pigeons, she first marries Menon and has Hypates and Hydaspeas. She helps capture Bactria, becomes Ninus’s wife, bears Ninyas, buries Ninus, and rules as queen regent. Seeking to surpass earlier rulers, she governs Asia except India for forty-two of her sixty-two years and directs Babylon’s construction and many works across Asia. Her Indian invasion ends in defeat and wounding; after her son’s assault, she yields the crown and disappears.

Semiramis, ambitious to surpass her predecessors, organizes the building of Babylon and gathers two million men from throughout her empire for the work. She oversees the city wall, bridge, embankments, palaces, vaulted passage, temple, and other works, and establishes additional cities and marts along the Euphrates and Tigris.

Semiramis built or commissioned remarkable works in Babylon, Media, and throughout Asia, including gardens, roads, palaces, canals, and monuments.
