Agis in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Agis succeeds Archidamus as Lacedaemonian king and commands the Lacedemonians. Restrained by Pharax at Mantinea, he offers battle against Argos but is stopped by a truce; spared after near execution, he gains ten advisers. He later attacks Athens, loses cavalry, abandons the siege, and returns to Peloponnesus.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAR VIIL

Agis commanded the Lacedaemonians against Argos and offered battle near the city, but a four-month truce prevented the engagement. Afterward, the Lacedaemonians nearly put him to death, spared him after his promise of future success, and assigned ten advisers to supervise his decisions.

CHAP. IX.

Agis marched against Athens with a large force and initially seized two parts of the walls. The Athenians defeated his cavalry and forced him to abandon the attempt to besiege the city before he returned to Peloponnesus.

Agis in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Agis, son of Archidamus and Spartan king, succeeded Archidamus, led the Lacedæmonians against Elis despite an earthquake withdrawal, ravaged Elis, attacked Attica, and fortified Decelea. Before dying fighting Macedonians, he affirmed Leotychides as his son, but Agesilaus excluded him from kingship.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

Agis succeeded Archidamus and led the Lacedæmonians against Elis, although an earthquake initially forced his army to withdraw. He later ravaged Elis, attacked Attica, and fortified Decelea; before his death he declared that he believed Leotychides was his son, though Agesilaus used his earlier doubts to exclude Leotychides from the kingship.
