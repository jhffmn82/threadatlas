Brasidas

person · 1 work · 8 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: a Spartan

Abstract written by a model

Brasidas was a young, strong, courageous Spartan soldier, commander, admiral, and general. He won honor in Sparta by entering threatened Methone through Athenian forces and helping preserve it. At Pylos he forced his ship ashore and fought the Athenians despite severe wounds and losing his shield. He relieved Megara, led an expedition through Thessaly to Macedonia, gained Thracian communities for the Lacedaemonians, captured Amphipolis, Torone, and nearby places, and fortified Scione and Menda. He was killed fighting Cleon at Amphipolis among heaps of enemies.

Brasidas is in one work on this site, The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Brasidas, a young Spartan of great strength and courage, entered Methone through the Athenian forces when the city was threatened. He helped preserve the city and gained high honor among the Spartans for his defense.

Stratocies being archon of Athens, the consular dignity was con-

Brasidas distinguished himself during the fighting at Pylos by forcing his ship ashore and bravely engaging the Athenians, though he was severely wounded and lost his shield. He later relieved Megara, brought several Thracian communities to the Lacedaemonian side, and captured Amphipolis, Torone, and other neighboring places.

In Asia, Xerxes the Persian king died, after lie had reigned one

Brasidas was the Lacedaemonian general in Thrace who protected Scione and Menda and placed strong garrisons in them. He later fought Cleon near Amphipolis, where he was killed in the battle.
