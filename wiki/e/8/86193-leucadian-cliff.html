Leucadian cliff in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

The Leucadian cliff is a high cliff in Ambracia, watched by Phoebus over the Aetian and Leucadian sea. Its deadly waves and rocks permit leaps that, under local mythic law, free sufferers from love: Deucalion did so. A Naiad directs Sappho there, though she prefers Phaon’s aid to its wave.

Narrative, section by section

XV

The Leucadian cliff is in Ambracia, where Phoebus looks over the sea called Aetium and Leucadian. A Naiad tells Sappho that people who leap from it can be freed from love, citing Deucalion's leap as an example.

Cur tamen Actiacas miseram me mittis ad oras, 185

Leucadia is associated with waters that Sappho might seek, but Sappho says that Phaon could be more beneficial to her than the Leucadian wave.
