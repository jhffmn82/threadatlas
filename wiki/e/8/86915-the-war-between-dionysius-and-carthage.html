the war between Dionysius and Carthage in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The war was planned by Dionysius after securing Sicily and making peace, secretly preparing by fortifying Epipolae. It unfolded through invasion of Carthaginian territories, the siege of Egesta, capture of Motya, destruction of Messana, and naval battle of Catana. Carthaginian victories brought fighting to Syracuse, where Dionysius defended with fortified positions, recruits, and foreign assistance.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. III.

After making peace with the Carthaginians, Dionysius decided to make war against them. He kept the plan secret while preparing, and strengthened Syracuse first by fortifying Epipolae.

The war involved invasions, shifting alliances, the capture and destruction of cities, and major land and naval engagements. Carthaginian victories at Messana and Catana brought the conflict to Syracuse, where Dionysius continued the defense with fortified positions, newly recruited troops, and foreign assistance.
