mercenary soldiers in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Mercenary soldiers serve and guard Dionysius, supporting him against opposition. Dionysius sends a thousand with horsemen against the Carthaginian camp, then abandons them to be killed. Distrusting the survivors under Aristotle, he seizes their general, pays about ten thousand with Leontium, and recruits more mercenaries.

The mercenary soldiers supported Dionysius when Pharacides opposed Theodorus's proposal. Dionysius later sent horsemen and a thousand mercenaries against the Carthaginian camp and ordered the horsemen to abandon them; the mercenaries were all killed.

The Carthaginians being thus routed both by sea and knd, seat

Dionysius distrusted his mercenaries because he believed they might depose him. He seized Aristotle, their general, and pacified about ten thousand soldiers by giving them Leontium as their pay; he then recruited additional mercenaries to protect his government.
