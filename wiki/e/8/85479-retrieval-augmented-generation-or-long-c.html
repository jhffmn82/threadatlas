RAG retrieves query-relevant external information and provides it to an LLM, making it comparatively easy to set up, potentially host on the customer side, and use at low cost, though retrieval itself can add cost. LC models such as Gemini-1.5, GPT-4, and Claude-3 can handle much larger contexts, Gemini 1.5 is cited at up to 1 million tokens, but long-context prompting is expensive because transformer computation grows quadratically with input length. LongBench contexts average 7k words and ∞Bench contexts average 100k tokens; tasks are scored with F1, accuracy, or ROUGE. In contrast with earlier findings that LC models lagged behind RAG, the reported results show that sufficiently resourced LC models generally achieve higher average performance and better long-context understanding than RAG. Analyses address retrieval failures, retriever choice, synthetic-data artifacts, and attempts to exclude models’ internal knowledge. Dragon-retriever and non-commonsense-subset results broadly support the main trend, while internal knowledge differs by model: on MuSiQue, GPT-3.5-Turbo scores 14.53 and Gemini-1.5-Pro 23.58 using only internal knowledge. To combine LC quality with RAG efficiency, the authors propose SELF-ROUTE. Through model self-reflection, it sends a query to RAG when retrieved context appears sufficient and otherwise uses LC processing. SELF-ROUTE approaches LC performance while using fewer tokens and lowering cost, reported as a 65% reduction for Gemini-1.5-Pro and 39% for GPT-4O. The document concludes that RAG remains valuable for lower-cost use and inputs beyond a model’s context window, while dynamic routing offers a practical route to LC-comparable performance at substantially reduced computation cost. Abstract

The unit compares Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) with long-context (LC) Large Language Models (LLMs) for processing lengthy contexts. Across public datasets and three latest LLMs, LC consistently achieves higher average performance when sufficiently resourced, while RAG has substantially lower cost. The authors propose SELF-ROUTE, which uses model self-reflection to route queries between RAG and LC. SELF-ROUTE reduces computation cost while maintaining performance comparable to LC.

Introduction

The introduction concerns retrieval augmented generation (RAG) as an effective and efficient way for large language models to use external knowledge. RAG retrieves information relevant to a query and supplies it to an LLM as context for response generation, expanding access to information at minimal cost. It also concerns Gemini and GPT-4, whose ability to understand long contexts motivates a systematic comparison between long-context LLMs. Gemini 1.5 is cited as being able to process up to 1 million tokens.

opening

The unit compares long-context LLMs and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) on performance and computational cost across public datasets. It reports that long-context LLMs generally outperform RAG in long-context understanding, while RAG is substantially more cost-efficient. The authors propose SELF-ROUTE, which uses model self-reflection to route queries to RAG or long-context processing, achieving performance comparable to long-context LLMs at lower cost. SELF-ROUTE reduces cost by 65% for Gemini-1.5-Pro and 39% for GPT-4O.

Related Work

The unit reviews efforts to enable long-context LLMs, including work by Guo et al. (2022), Beltagy et al. (2020), and Chen et al. (2023b). It notes that Gemini-1.5, GPT-4, and Claude-3 have larger context windows, but long-context prompting remains expensive because transformers have quadratic computation costs with respect to input token numbers. It describes prompt compression, model distillation, and LLM cascading as approaches for reducing this cost. It also introduces retrieval-augmented generation, which complements LLMs with externally retrieved information and has been applied to language modeling and question answering.

This unit describes the benchmarking of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) against long-context (LC) prompting on nine datasets from LongBench and ∞Bench. The study evaluates Gemini-1.5-Pro, GPT-4O, and GPT-3.5-Turbo with Contriever and Dragon retrievers, using F1, accuracy, or ROUGE depending on the task. LongBench provides shorter contexts averaging 7k words, while ∞Bench provides contexts averaging 100k tokens. The authors note that prior work found long-context models lagging behind RAG, whereas their study investigates this comparison with stronger LLMs and longer contexts.

opening .. Exclusion of LLM’s internal knowledge

The unit compares RAG, LC, and SELF-ROUTE across Gemini-1.5-Pro, GPT-4O, and GPT-3.5-Turbo on LongBench datasets, finding that LC generally outperforms RAG while SELF-ROUTE approaches LC performance with fewer tokens. SELF-ROUTE routes queries to RAG when the retrieved context appears sufficient and sends other queries to LC, reducing token usage while preserving accuracy. The analysis examines retrieval failures, retriever variation, synthetic-data artifacts, and methods for limiting models’ use of internal knowledge. Results using the Dragon retriever and non-commonsense subsets broadly support the reported trends.

conclusion

The paper compares RAG and LC in terms of performance and computational cost. LC performs better in long-context understanding, while RAG remains useful because it costs less and handles inputs that exceed the model’s context window. The proposed dynamic routing method uses model self-reflection to select between RAG and LC, achieving performance comparable to LC at significantly lower cost. The findings inform practical applications of long-context LLMs and future optimization of RAG techniques.

notes

Different models may learn different internal knowledge, which can lead to different numbers of non-commonsense questions. GPT-3.5-Turbo achieves a performance of 14.53 on MuSiQue using only internal knowledge. Gemini-1.5-Pro achieves a performance of 23.58 on MuSiQue under the same condition.
