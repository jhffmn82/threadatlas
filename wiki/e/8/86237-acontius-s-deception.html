Abstract written by a model

Acontius conceals a marriage oath on an apple and arranges for Cydippe to read it in Diana's temple, thereby ensnaring her without openly proposing. Cydippe condemns the attempted compulsion; Acontius admits deception, invokes love and union, calls his letter another stratagem, and threatens more.

XX

The deception consists in Acontius's use of the inscribed apple to make Cydippe pronounce a marriage oath without openly asking her. Acontius admits that she may call herself deceived, but argues that love was the motive and that the deception sought only to unite them. He calls his letter another deception and threatens that many further stratagems remain.

XXI

Acontius concealed his purpose by placing a marriage oath on an apple and arranging for Cydippe to read it in Diana's temple. Cydippe reproaches him for trying to compel her instead of persuading her through an honest proposal.
