Abstract written by a model

In “A Survey of Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Customized Large Language Models,” Graphs as Knowledge Carrier is described as a primary paradigm for structured knowledge organization that extracts summarized knowledge into a unified knowledge base for retrieval, reasoning, and resolving linguistic variation. In “A Survey of Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Customized Large Language Models,” graph-structured knowledge representation is described as explicitly capturing entity relationships and domain hierarchies. In “A Survey of Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Customized Large Language Models,” graph-structured input is described as difficult for large language models to process natively and as supporting node classification, link prediction, and graph reasoning when accurately modeled. In “GRAG: Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation,” graph-structured data is described as containing complex structural information absent from ordinary text, with retrieval methods incorporating textual and topological information to capture structural relationships. In “HopRAG: Multi-Hop Reasoning for Logic-Aware Retrieval-Augmented Generation,” a graph is described as an effective structure for modeling logical relations and as organizing knowledge across documents for graph-based reasoning, despite being costly and returning triplets instead of plain text. In “LightRAG: Simple and Fast Retrieval-Augmented Generation,” graph-based knowledge structures are described as representing knowledge, synthesizing information from multiple sources, improving contextual coherence, and helping large language models interpret complex relationships and graph-structured data.

Graph-structured input is not natively processed by large language models and is commonly converted into natural-language descriptions, which may not convey complex geometric properties. Accurate modeling supports node classification, link prediction, and increasingly complex graph reasoning.

C. Discussion on GraphRAG

Graph-structured input is difficult for large language models because they commonly convert graph data into natural-language descriptions, which do not fully convey complex geometric properties. Accurate modeling of graph-structured input is important for tasks such as node classification, link prediction, and increasingly complex graph reasoning.
