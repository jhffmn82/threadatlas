Tanagra

place · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Taoagra, district of Tanagra, town

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Tanagra in Boeotia is the place toward which the Lacedaemonians turned while returning from the Phocian war, where the Athenians followed them and the opposing armies fought. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Tanagra is a place with temples to Dionysus, Themis, Aphrodite, Apollo, Artemis, Leto, and Hermes, whose people separate houses from temples, keep temples pure, greatly honor the gods, and preserve traditions about Hermes, Corinna, and two kinds of cocks.

CHAPTER XXII.

Tanagra has temples to Dionysus, Themis, Aphrodite, Apollo, Artemis, Leto, and Hermes. Its people keep their houses and temples apart, place the temples in pure locations, honor their gods greatly, and preserve traditions about Hermes, Corinna, and two kinds of cocks.
