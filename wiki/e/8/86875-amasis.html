Amasis in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Amasis was an Egyptian law-maker and later king of Egypt. He made laws for the Nomarchi, brought the provinces into order, and was regarded as wise, just, and good. Though not of royal blood, he was elevated to the throne, advised the Eleans, warned Polycrates, and moved Psammetichus’s mercenary soldiers to Memphis.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. V.

Amasis is mentioned as a king who reigned many years after Psammetichus and transplanted Psammetichus's mercenary soldiers from their settlement near Pelusium to Memphis.

• And now it is necessary for us to speak of the legislators of Egypt^

Amasis made laws to direct the Nomarchi in their governments and brought the provinces of Egypt into order. He was regarded as wise, just, and good, and was elevated to the throne despite not being of royal blood; he also advised the Eleans and warned Polycrates against tyrannical rule.
