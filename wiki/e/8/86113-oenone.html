Oenone in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Oenone is a fountain nymph, celebrated in the Phrygian forests and daughter of a great river. Paris’s formerly loved, faithful wife, she possesses healing herbs and is loved by Faunus. Abandoned when Paris returns with Helen, she writes grieving, condemning his new marriage, and begging him to return.

Oenone Paridi 2

Oenone identifies herself as a fountain nymph famous in the Phrygian forests and writes to Paris as his abandoned wife. She recalls their life together, mourns his departure and return with Helen, condemns his new marriage, and asks him to return to her.

Oenone in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Oenone, daughter of Cebren, learns prophecy from Rhea and can heal wounded people with drugs. She warns Alexander not to sail for Helen and says only she can heal him. When he returns wounded, resentment makes her refuse treatment; she later brings medicines, finds him dead, and hangs herself.

Oenone in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

Oenone, Paris’s former wife and a virtuous Nymph, loves him more than life and alone can heal his venom. Angry that he abandoned her for Helen, she refuses his plea. After Paris dies, she genuinely mourns, regrets her refusal, runs to his pyre, and dies by fire clasping him.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK X

Oenone refuses Paris’s plea for healing, bitterly recalling that he abandoned her for Helen. After Paris dies, she regrets her refusal, mourns him in solitude, and declares that she would dare to die with him.

Then, while her father and her maidens slept,

Oenone runs through the night over rocks, mountain slopes, and torrent-beds to reach Paris's pyre. She leaps onto the pyre with her face muffled in her mantle and burns there clasping Paris.
