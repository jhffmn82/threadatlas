Abstract written by a model

In Heroides and Amores, Leander is a young man from Abydos who repeatedly swims across the sea to reach Hero, relying on love and boldness despite the dangers of storms and rough waters. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Leander is a youth of Abydos who loves Hero and swims nightly to visit her until a tempest overwhelms and drowns him.

Leander in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · about 24 to 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where Leander first appears

Abstract written by a model

Leander is a young man from Abydos, an Abydenus and swimmer who repeatedly crosses the sea to reach his beloved, Hero, guided by a light. Separated from her by the waters, he is defined by their love and by his boldness in overcoming the sea, even when boats face peril. In his letter from Abydos, a storm prevents him from crossing; he recalls earlier swims, takes strength from love, and intends to try again despite the danger. Others urge him to return to Hero but warn him not to trust his arms in a rough sea, suggesting he meet her halfway if necessary.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

XVIII..

Leander writes to Hero from Abydos, explaining that the storm prevents him from crossing the sea. He recalls swimming to Hero, draws strength from his love for her, and plans to attempt the crossing again despite the danger.

XIX

Leander is urged to cross the sea and return to Hero. He is described as a bold swimmer who has previously faced a sea as fierce as the present one, though Hero now wonders why he has become more fearful. His body and Hero's hopes depend on the same wave.

165 Perhaps you fear the time may fail you for

Leander is urged to meet the girl halfway across the sea if he cannot endure the full journey. He is warned that his arms are not stronger than oars and is asked not to trust them except in a tranquil sea.
