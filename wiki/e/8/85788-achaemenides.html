Achaemenides

person · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Achæmenides, Achæmenides, once deserted in the midst of the crags of Ætna, A Greek

Abstract written by a model

In The Aeneid, Achaemenides is a Greek from Ithaca abandoned in the Cyclops’ cave who survives three moons on wild food before being rescued by Aeneas and taken aboard Trojan ships. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Achæmenides is an Ithacan Greek deserted near Ætna who survives on wild food, is rescued by a Trojan ship, and recounts Polyphemus’ terrifying cannibalistic rage.

Abstract written by a model

Achæmenides, an Ithacan Greek, is deserted near Ætna after Polyphemus, survives hidden on wild food, then is rescued by a Trojan ship; he praises Æneas and recounts Polyphemus’ terrifying cannibalistic rage.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE IV. [XIV.154-247]

Achæmenides, an Ithacan, was left behind among the crags of Ætna when Ulysses and his companions fled from Polyphemus. He hid for many days, surviving on acorns, grass, and leaves, until a Trojan ship found and rescued him. He praises Æneas for preserving his life and relates the terror of witnessing Polyphemus’ rage and cannibalism.
