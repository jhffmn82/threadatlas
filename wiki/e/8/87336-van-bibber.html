Van Bibber in Gallegher and Other Stories

Richard Harding Davis · 1890

Van Bibber is a twenty-five-year-old New Yorker: a wealthy, popular yet unspoiled young club man, respectable in society, fond of adventure, and possessed of a good heart. Though experienced as a best man or usher, he is not practised at lying. His emotional story begins when he breaks off his engagement and tells himself he should remain a bachelor. Remembering the three months with his fiancée, he realizes she was the source of his happiness, returns with the ring to their secluded place in Central Park, and rushes to her when he finds her there. On a hot day in the Park, initially cross and sleepy, he pays for three East Side girls to ride the swan-boats, accompanies them, shows them the sights, and buys them more tickets. After confronting and disarming a burglar, he chooses not to surrender him; instead he sends him west to Helena with a ticket and money, later learning the man was the escaped Abe Hubbard, alias Jimmie the Gent. Finally, as Cortlandt Van Bibber, he protects young Standish's elopement with Miss Cambridge through a false claim, organizes their formal wedding, and serves as best man, though he regrets where he sent Standish's brother.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

" A WALK UP THE AVENUE He came down the steps slowly, and pulling mechanically at his gloves.

Young Van Bibber walks north after breaking off his engagement, initially claiming that he has acted rightly and that he is meant to remain a bachelor. As he remembers his life with his fiancée, he recognizes that he has lost the source of his happiness, goes to their secluded place in Central Park with the engagement ring, and rushes to her when he finds her there.

" VAN BIBBER AND THE SWAN-BOATS It was very hot in the Park, and young Van Bibber, who has a good heart and a great deal more money than good-hearted people generally get, was cross and somnolent.

Van Bibber is hot, cross, and waiting for his groom when he notices three little girls admiring the swan-boats. He offers to pay for their ride, joins them despite his embarrassment, and points out the sights around the lake. After meeting the Girl He Knew and her brother, he buys the girls long strings of tickets for additional rides.

VAN BIBBER'S BURGLAR There had been a dance up town, but as Van Bibber could not find Her there, he accepted young Travers's suggestion to go over to Jersey City and see a "go" between "Dutchy" Mack and a <DW52> person professionally known as the Black Diamond.

Van Bibber encounters a burglar at dawn after seeing a man's head emerge from a door in the wall of a great house. He tackles the man, takes his revolver, and escorts him to Grand Central station instead of surrendering him. Van Bibber buys him a ticket to Helena, Montana, gives him money, and later reads that he has helped the escaped Abe Hubbard, alias Jimmie the Gent, leave the city.

" VAN BIBBER AS BEST MAN Young Van Bibber came up to town in June from Newport to see his lawyer about the preparation of some papers that needed his signature.

Cortlandt Van Bibber recognizes young Standish and learns that he and Miss Cambridge have eloped without a clergyman or witnesses. He sends young Standish's older brother away by falsely saying that the couple has gone to Chicago, then arranges their wedding at the Little Church Around the Corner with invited guests, flowers, music, and other formalities. He acts as best man and afterward regrets sending the brother to Chicago rather than Jersey City.
