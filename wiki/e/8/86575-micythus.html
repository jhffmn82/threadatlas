Micythus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Micythus, faithful trustee and tutor for Anaxilaus’s sons, governed Rhegium, built Teuxuntum, restored rule when the sons came of age, honestly accounted for his administration, refused to resume governing, and settled at Tegea.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAP. XIII.

Micythus gave a full and honest account of his administration to the sons of Anaxilaus. Although they asked him to resume governing, he refused, left Rhegium, and settled at Tegea in Arcadia.

Micythus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Micythus was treasurer of Anaxilas and, when Anaxilas was king at Rhegium, his slave. The inscriptions name him son of Chœrus and native of Rhegium, alternatively Messene near the Strait. After Anaxilas’s death he lived at Tegea. At Olympia he placed many votive offerings, fulfilling a vow for his son’s recovery from a wasting consumption: Proserpine, Aphrodite, Ganymede, Artemis, Homer, Hesiod, Æsculapius, Hygiea, and Agon with dumb bells, including works by Argive Glaucus and Dionysius. Nero later removed some offerings.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXVI.

He placed many votive offerings at Olympia, including works by Argive Glaucus and Dionysius. The inscriptions identify him as the son of Chœrus and a native of Rhegium, and state that his offerings fulfilled a vow for the recovery of his son from a wasting consumption. Herodotus is said to have reported that Micythus was the slave and treasurer of Anaxilas, later went to Tegea, and that Nero removed some of his offerings.
