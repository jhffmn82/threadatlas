Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Erechtheus brought corn from Egypt to Athens during a widespread drought, then became king and instituted at Eleusis the festivals, Egyptian rites, and mysteries of Ceres. In The Library, Erechtheus became king after inheriting the kingdom, married Praxithea, sacrificed his youngest daughter to secure victory over the Eleusinians, killed Eumolpus in battle, and was then killed by Poseidon.

Erechtheus in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Erechtheus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Erechtheus, twin son of Pandion, became king after receiving the kingdom as his inheritance. He married Praxithea and had three sons and four daughters. In war with the Eleusinians, an oracle required the slaughter of one daughter for victory; he sacrificed his youngest daughter, after which the others killed themselves. He killed Eumolpus in battle, then Poseidon killed him and destroyed his household.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

XV. Πανδίονος δὲ ἀποθανόντος οἱ παῖδες τὰ

Erechtheus received the kingdom after Pandion’s death. He married Praxithea and had sons named Cecrops, Pandorus, and Metion and daughters named Procris, Creusa, Chthonia, and Orithyia. During the war with the Eleusinians, he sacrificed his youngest daughter as required by an oracle, killed Eumolpus in the ensuing battle, and was then killed by Poseidon, who also destroyed his household.

Erechtheus was told by the oracle that the Athenians would win their war if he slaughtered one of his daughters. After he slaughtered his youngest daughter, the others also killed themselves, and he killed Eumolpus in the ensuing battle. Poseidon destroyed Erechtheus and his house.
