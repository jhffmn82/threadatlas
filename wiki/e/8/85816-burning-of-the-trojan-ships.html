burning of the Trojan ships in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

The burning of the Trojan ships is caused by Juno through Iris, who persuades Trojan women to ignite the fleet. Flames spread through timber, seams, keels, sails, and oars, destroying four ships despite human efforts. As the Trojans depart Africa, Jupiter ends the blaze with a violent storm and rain.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK V

The Trojans see a mounting blaze on the Punic shore as they depart from Africa. The argument states that Trojan women, persuaded by Iris sent by Juno, set fire to the ships, burning four before Jupiter extinguishes the flames with rain.

Thus far the sacred sports they celebrate:

The fire spreads through the ships’ seams and timber, consuming the tow, keels, sails, banks, and oars. Human efforts cannot stop it until Jove sends a violent storm and rain.
