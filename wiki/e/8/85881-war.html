war in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

War is the fated armed conflict between the Trojans and the Latin, Rutulian, Ausonian, and other Italian peoples, centered on the city and destined to end there. It is foretold as arising from Lavinia’s fate and is described as bringing change, ruin, waste, grief, and heavy losses. Anchises also presents future wars against Carthage, Gaul, Greece, other foreign powers, and among descendants of the Trojan line, warning Aeneas to pursue his fortune or endure their hardships. The immediate war begins when Juno intends the marriage to cause bloodshed, and Alecto inflames Amata and Turnus. A dispute over a household stag becomes violence between countrymen and Trojans; Juno opens the gates of war, and Ausonian cities arm. Turnus gathers varied Italian forces, weapons, armor, leaders, and allies, while Aeneas seeks Evander’s support, joins the Tuscans, and receives Vulcan’s divinely forged armor. Turnus also seeks Diomede’s aid. Fighting first presses the defended Trojan camp while Aeneas is absent. Assaults strike its trenches, walls, towers, and gates, causing many deaths before the Trojans rally and force Turnus to withdraw. Aeneas’s return with Tuscan allies creates a new clash. Pallas rallies Arcadians, Lausus leads the opposing force, and Pallas’s death prompts Aeneas’s broad attack; the war grants renown but also sends warriors to die far from home. Losses bring mourning, faction, and debate among the Latins over negotiation, renewed battle, alliance, or single combat, especially after Diomede refuses aid. Yet neither side yields. Aeneas advances toward the city, Turnus plans an ambush, and Camilla confronts Tyrrhene troops. In the final fighting, a truce is broken after Tolumnius throws a spear, sacrificial equipment becomes weaponry, and open battle resumes. Aeneas tries to halt the violence, then declares that the conflict began in the city and must end there, attacking the Latian towers.

Where long extended plains of pleasure lay:

The future wars include conflicts against Carthage, Gaul, Greece, and other foreign powers, as well as civil strife between descendants of the Trojan line. Anchises warns Aeneas about these wars and prepares him to pursue his fortune or endure their hardships.

BOOK VII

The portents foretell that Lavinia’s fate will bring war and change to the country. Juno plans to make the marriage a cause of bloodshed and to set the son-in-law and father against one another, threatening both peoples with ruin and waste.

Thus having said, she sinks beneath the ground,

War begins when Alecto inflames Amata and Turnus, the dispute over the household stag turns violent, and the countrymen and Trojans fight. Juno then forces open the gates of war, causing the Ausonian cities to arm themselves and prepare for battle.

And now the mighty labour is begun

The passage presents the forces gathering for war against the Trojans under Turnus. It emphasizes the varied weapons, armor, origins, leaders, and battle habits of the assembled Italian peoples.

BOOK VIII

The war begins as Turnus gathers allies and prepares the Latian forces, while Aeneas seeks assistance from Evander. Turnus requests help from Diomede, and Evander agrees to support Aeneas.

Now night had shed her silver dews around,

The war against Turnus is presented as imminent, with Aeneas gathering Trojan and Arcadian forces and joining the Tuscans. Divine assistance, including Vulcan’s armor, is given to support Aeneas in the conflict.

BOOK IX

The war continues as Turnus attacks the defended Trojan camp while Aeneas is away. The opposing forces prepare for further fighting, and Nisus and Euryalus undertake a dangerous mission to obtain Aeneas’s aid.

Now rose the ruddy morn from Tithon’s bed,

The war intensifies into a large assault on the Trojan camp, with attacks on the trenches, walls, towers, and gates. Both sides suffer many deaths, and the fighting continues until the Trojans rally against Turnus and force him to withdraw.

BOOK X

The war for Latium continues despite Jupiter’s order that the gods remain neutral. Aeneas’s return with Tuscan allies produces a new clash between the landing forces and Turnus’s defenders.

But, in another part, th’ Arcadian horse

The fighting shifts as Pallas rallies the Arcadians, Lausus leads the opposing force, and Aeneas launches a broad attack after Pallas’s death. The unit presents war as producing both glory and grief, with warriors winning renown while dying far from home.

BOOK XI

The war produces heavy losses and causes universal mourning among the Latins, Trojans, and Arcadians. Its immediate continuation depends on the contest between Aeneas and Turnus, while the refusal of Diomedes leaves the Latins seeking either another alliance or peace on hard conditions.

Thus, full of anxious thought, he summons all

The war has brought heavy losses, mourning, internal faction, and danger to the Latins. The speakers debate whether to end it through negotiation or continue it through renewed fighting and a possible single combat.

While they debate, nor these nor those will yield,

The war approaches as Aeneas leads the Trojans toward the city. The Latins mobilize, Turnus prepares an ambush, and Camilla is sent to confront the Tyrrhene troops.

BOOK XII

The battle begins when King Tolumnius throws a spear after accepting Juturna’s omen. The armies overturn the altars, use sacrificial equipment as weapons, and exchange heavy attacks, with Aulestes, Ebusus, and Alsus among those killed before Aeneas attempts to halt the violence.

When Turnus saw the Trojan quit the plain,

The war intensifies after the truce is broken and both sides pursue victory through open battle. Aeneas declares that the conflict began in the city and should end there, leading him to attack the Latian towers.
