Athenian expedition to Sicily in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Athenian expedition to Sicily was a military expedition, preceded by Athenian fleets sent to support Leontium and pursue wider designs; those forces raided the Lipari islands, fought near the Myleans, captured a Locrian castle, and returned after peace between Leontium and Syracuse. After appeals from the Leontines and Egestaeans, Athens approved a larger expedition against the Syracusans under Alcibiades, Nicias, and Lamachus. Accounts describe a large fleet and army, including 130 ships and 5,000 armed men, or more than 7,000 armed men. It travelled through Corcyra and Italy, captured Hyccara, occupied Catana, and began operations against Syracuse. Its intended policy was to enslave Selinus and Syracuse and impose tribute on other cities. Condemned as unjust, it ended in complete destruction: neither ship nor man returned, and its army and generals were left at Syracusan mercy.

CHAPTER XV.

The Athenians were preparing for the expedition to Sicily when, according to Clitodemus, an immense number of crows came to Delphi and damaged the statue of Athene and its accompanying objects. Clitodemus related this and other prodigies as warnings against the expedition.
