The pious prince was seiz’d with sudden fear;

Dido says that she and Aeneas have exchanged vows, shared new nuptial pleasures, and that she took him to her throne and bed. Their relationship is broken by Aeneas’s decision to leave, which Dido experiences as betrayal while he treats it as obedience to fate and the gods.

The wretched queen, pursued by cruel fate,

Dido remembers her love for Aeneas as the source of her ruin and despair. She says that if the Trojan had never reached her shore, she might have remained free and avoided this suffering.
