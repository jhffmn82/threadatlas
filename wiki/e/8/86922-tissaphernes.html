Tissaphernes in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Tissaphernes is a noble Persian, Artaxerxes’s faithful friend and relative by marriage, who becomes governor and lieutenant of the sea-coast provinces formerly under Cyrus. When Artaxerxes is wounded in battle, he takes command, rallies the soldiers, fights bravely, and kills many of Cyrus’s men. Rewarded as the bravest man, he receives authority to pursue the Greeks, seizes Clearchus and other commanders by treachery, and attacks the retreating Greeks. He then threatens Cyrus’s supporters, attacks the Cumeans, and joins Pharnabazus in raising forces against the Lacedaemonians. Commanding a vast army against Agesilaus, he is defeated near Sipylus, flees to Sardis, and is seized and executed by Artaxerxes as the alleged cause of the war.

CHAP. V-

Tissaphernes, a noble Persian, took Artaxerxes's place when the king was carried from the battle as if dead. He encouraged the soldiers, fought valiantly, and killed many of Cyrus's men while attempting to avenge the king.

Artaxerxes rewarded Tissaphernes for being judged the bravest man, gave him a marriage alliance, and appointed him governor and lieutenant of the provinces formerly commanded by Cyrus on the sea-coast. Tissaphernes persuaded Artaxerxes to let him pursue the Greeks, then treacherously summoned and seized Clearchus and the other commanders and attacked the retreating Greeks without facing their whole army.

CHAP. VI.

Tissaphernes was appointed by Artaxerxes to govern the sea-coast territories and threatened cities that had supported Cyrus. He attacked the Cumeans, ravaged their countryside, took prisoners, and later raised a large force with Pharnabazus against the Lacedaemonians.

CHAP. vm.

Tissaphernes commanded ten thousand horse and fifty thousand foot against Agesilaus. His forces were defeated near Sipylus, he fled to Sardis, and Artaxerxes later had him seized and executed because he was considered the occasion of the war.
