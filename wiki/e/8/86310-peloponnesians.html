Peloponnesians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Peloponnesians are a people inhabiting the Peloponnese, hosted by Debse because of Hercules's ancient familiarity with the nation. They sought security by favoring defense near the isthmus and helping build a wall from Lechaeum to Cenchreae. In later fighting, they served in the Lacedaemonian fleet and suffered defeats near Cyzicum and Claros, but also won actions near Mitylene. Under Lysander, they defeated the Athenian fleet, captured Sestos, and helped blockade Athens until it surrendered.

Having now said enough of these gallant men, we shall return

The Peloponnesians favored fighting near the isthmus so that they could withdraw into Peloponnesus if necessary. They helped enclose the isthmus with a wall extending from Lechaeum to Cenchreae.

CHAP. 111.

The Peloponnesians formed part of the Lacedaemonian fleet under Mindarus in the battle between Sestos and Abydos. They initially exceeded the Athenians in ship numbers but were defeated after the arrival of Athenian reinforcements.

CHAP. V.

The Peloponnesians fought the Athenians at Dardanium and later at Cyzicum and Claros. Their forces were defeated at Cyzicum and Claros, and the survivors withdrew to Pharnabazus's camp.

CHAP. X.

The Peloponnesians pursued Conon's fleet and fought the Athenians near Mitylene. They forced many Athenians ashore, later overcame the Athenian position in the harbour, and helped Callicratides besiege the city.

CHAP. XV.

The Peloponnesians refused to engage the Athenian fleet at first, allowing the Athenians to grow short of provisions. Under Lysander, they defeated the Athenian fleet, captured Sestos, and helped blockade Athens until its surrender.

Peloponnesians in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The Peloponnesians, including the Achæans, came to Athens under Codrus but mostly withdrew from Attica after his death. They used the title Alea for Athene, later failed to guard Thermopylæ against the Galati, and, with other Greek allies, supplied war money for the Athenian portico.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

The Peloponnesians, including the Achæans, overlooked the pass at Thermopylæ when the Galati advanced because they believed the barbarians could not threaten them by sea and that the Isthmus of Corinth was strongly fortified.

CHAPTER XXV.

The Peloponnesians came to Athens during the reign of Codrus son of Melanthus. Most of their army withdrew from Attica after hearing of Codrus’s death and the circumstances surrounding it.

CHAPTER XI.

The Peloponnesians and other Greek allies were among those from whom the Athenians obtained war money used to build the Athenian portico.
