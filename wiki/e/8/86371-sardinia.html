Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Sardinia is described as a fertile island colonized by Hercules' followers, later conquered by the Carthaginians, and resisted by its inhabitants. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Sardinia is described as a large, fertile island also called Ichnusa, with various settlers, cities, geographical features, and distinctive wildlife.

Abstract written by a model

Sardinia is an island as large as Sicily, fertile and cultivated. Hercules, following an oracle, established a colony there governed by the sons of the Thespiadae under Iolaus. Later the Carthaginians conquered it, but its lolaeian inhabitants withdrew to mountains and resisted both Carthaginian and Roman subjugation.

Sardinia in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Sardinia first appears

Abstract written by a model

Sardinia is a large, fertile island, also called Ichnusa by Greeks engaged in commerce and named after Sardus. It is 1,120 stades long and 470 broad. It received settlers and colonists including Libyans, Greeks, Iberes, Thespians, Athenians, and Trojans, and contains Nora, Olbia, Ogryle, Caralis, and Sulci. Its northern range toward Italy lacks harbours and sends violent winds over the sea; central air is pestilential. It lacks serpents and wolves, but has swift rams and deadly grass near springs.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XVII.

Sardinia is described as a large and prosperous island, called Ichnusa by the Greeks because of its footprint-like shape. It received successive settlers and colonists, including Libyans, Greeks, Iberes, Thespians, Athenians, and Trojans, and later contained settlements such as Nora, Olbia, Ogryle, Caralis, and Sulci. The island has mountainous regions, few harbours in the north, turbulent winds, pestilential air in some central areas, no serpents or wolves, unusually swift rams, and a deadly grass associated with Sardonic laughter.
