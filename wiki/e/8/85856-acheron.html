Acheron

place · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: the Acheron

Abstract written by a model

In The Aeneid, Acheron is an innavigable, troubled river surrounding the underworld’s dark abode, whose coast Charon rules and where Aeneas seeks passage to see Anchises. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Acheron is represented as a river in the painting, with reeds, indistinct fish, and Charon’s boat.

Acheron in The Aeneid

Virgil · 28 to 18 BC · the work’s page · read it where Acheron first appears

Abstract written by a model

Acheron is an innavigable, troubled river surrounding the underworld’s dark abode. Its oozy, clay-thick eddies flow into Cocytus; Charon rules its dreary coast and ferries permitted ghosts, while Aeneas seeks passage to see Anchises.
