household gods in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Household gods, including ancient Vesta and relics of immortal fire, are rescued from Troy as its temples are defiled. They accompany Aeneas in exile, appear in dreams, redirect him from Crete, and identify Hesperia, or Italy, as the destined future settlement.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

“’Twas in the dead of night, when sleep repairs

Hector brings the venerable statues of the Trojan gods, ancient Vesta, the wreaths, and relics of the immortal fire to Aeneas. Their temples are defiled and their abodes abandoned during the Greek assault.

BOOK III

The household gods travel with Aeneas from the burning Troy and appear to him in a dream. They explain that Crete is not his destined home and instruct him to search for Hesperia, or Italy.
