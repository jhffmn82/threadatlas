Meroe in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Meroe is a large, shield-shaped island in the Nile near Ethiopia, inhabited by Ethiopians and containing many cities, foremost Meroe city. Three thousand furlongs long and a thousand wide, it has mines of gold, silver, iron, and brass, ebony trees, and precious stones. Its priests possess greatest authority and formerly commanded kings to die.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. III.

Meroe island is a large island formed by the Nile near Ethiopia, with a city of the same name. It is described as being shield-shaped, containing many cities, and possessing mines of gold, silver, iron, and brass, as well as ebony trees and precious stones.

DIODORUS SICULUS- / BOOK III.

Meroe is identified as the capital city or central place of the Ethiopians described near Egypt. The priests serving the gods at Meroe are said to possess the greatest authority and formerly commanded kings to die.
