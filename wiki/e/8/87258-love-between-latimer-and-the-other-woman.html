love between Latimer and the other woman in Gallegher and Other Stories

Richard Harding Davis · 1890

This is Latimer’s past love for another woman, suppressed and sacrificed by both. Though he says overcoming it made him honest and worthy and insists he loves Ellen certainly above all else, the bishop raises that changed circumstances could revive the love after Latimer marries Ellen. Latimer calls the woman beyond the love of a man like himself, making it a forbidden or impossible temptation.

"Well, and suppose this other woman did exist, what then?

The bishop suggests that Latimer could love the other woman again, even after choosing to marry Ellen, if the circumstances separating them changed. Latimer says that the woman is beyond the love of a man such as himself.

The older man stepped quickly across the floor, but before he reached the middle

Latimer says that he loves Ellen more than all the world and that his love for her is certain without needing words to prove it. He also describes a former love that he and the other woman suppressed and sacrificed, claiming that overcoming it helped make him honest and worthy.
