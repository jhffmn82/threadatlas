Abstract written by a model

Ellen’s father is a bishop and priest, young for his high office, and Ellen’s sole surviving parent, guardian, and caregiver. Concerned about her future after she leaves his household, he knows Latimer’s family and likes young Latimer. When Latimer proposes marriage, he listens closely but tests whether Latimer’s past, especially another woman, could endanger Ellen’s happiness; Latimer’s refusal to answer a hypothetical convinces him that it reveals a true feeling. Later, after hearing Latimer explain his past and feelings, he pities Ellen, judges Latimer honorable, says he would have been proud to hear such words from a son, urges Ellen to understand him, and watches Latimer depart with sympathy and concern.

Ellen's father is in one work on this site, Gallegher and Other Stories. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

" THE OTHER WOMAN Young Latimer stood on one of the lower steps of the hall stairs, leaning with one hand on the broad railing and smiling down at her.

The bishop receives young Latimer's marriage proposal and listens carefully to his account of his love for Ellen. He says that he trusts Latimer's future treatment of Ellen but wants to know whether anything in Latimer's past could later threaten her. He asks the thirty-day hypothetical and concludes that Latimer's refusal to answer conceals his true feeling.

"Well, and suppose this other woman did exist, what then?

The bishop warns Latimer that whatever separated him from the other woman might end after Latimer married Ellen, leaving Ellen to bear the resulting trouble. He asks whether the other woman is dead and then whether she is married.

Ellen's father receives her with pity and concern and listens as Latimer explains his past and his feelings. He becomes convinced that Latimer has spoken honorably and says he would have been proud if a son had spoken as Latimer did. He urges Ellen to understand Latimer and watches him leave with sympathy and concern.
