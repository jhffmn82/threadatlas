Styx in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Styx is the Greek name for water dropping from a very high cliff near the ruins of Nonacris, also named as water near Pheneus. In different accounts it is Oceanus’s daughter, wife of Pallas or Piras, and mother of Echidna. Homer describes it as a divine oath witness, a river of Hades, and water flowing into the Titaresius. At Nonacris it drips from the cliff, falls on and oozes through a rock, then flows into the Crathis. Its water is harmful and deadly to people, beasts, and goats, damages glass, crystal, porcelain, stone, and pottery, and melts horn, bone, metals, amber, and gold; a horse’s hoof is said to resist it.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XVII.

The Styx is the water that drops from a very high cliff near the ruins of Nonacris. The Greeks call this falling water the Styx.

CHAPTER XVIII.

Styx is described in different traditions as the daughter of Oceanus and either the wife of Pallas or of Piras, to whom she bore Echidna. Homer presents Styx as a divine oath witness, as water flowing into the Titaresius, and as a river of Hades. Near Nonacris, its water drips from a cliff, flows into the Crathis, is deadly to humans and animals, and damages or melts many materials; a horse’s hoof is said to resist it.

CHAPTER XIX.

Styx is the name of the water near Pheneus. Unlike the water at Cynætha, it is said to be harmful to people.
