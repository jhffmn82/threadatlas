Deucetius

person · 1 work · 16 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Dcu- cetius, Deucctius, Deucetius's, Deucetius^ king of Sicily, Driicettus

Abstract written by a model

Deucetius is a prince and general of the Siculi whose chief aim was sovereignty and unity among them. He built Menenum, allotted neighboring land, and fought Catana over Sicilian land. As prince, he united nearly all Siculi cities except Hybla, moved Neas to a plain, founded Palicon near the temple of the Palici, and distributed its surrounding country. He later took Aetna, led his army into Agrigentine territory, captured Motya, and then suffered defeat by the Syracusans, desertion by soldiers, and loss of treasure near Noma. He fled to Syracuse and submitted as a suppliant; the Syracusans preserved him, sent him to Corinth, and ordered him to live privately there. He later returned to Sicily with colonists, planted and settled colonies in the country of the Callatines, joined by some Siculi.

Deucetius is in one work on this site, The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. XIX.

Deucetius, general and later king of the Sicilians, built Menenum and divided its neighboring lands by lot. He also made war against Catana over land claimed by the Sicilians.

CHAP. XXIV.

Deucetius, prince of the Siculi, united nearly all the Siculi cities into one community. He moved Neas to a plain, founded Palicon near the temple of the Palici, and distributed the surrounding country among the inhabitants.

CHAP. XXVIII.

Deucetius, the general of the Siculi, took Aetna and invaded the country of the Agrigentines, capturing Motya after defeating an Agrigentine relief force. He was later defeated by the Syracusans, abandoned by many of his soldiers, and deprived of his treasure. He fled to Syracuse, submitted as a suppliant, and was sent to Corinth with a promise of support.

Deucetius was the prince of the Siculi whose power the Syracusans broke. After submitting to the people, he was pardoned and commanded to live privately at Corinth, but he later broke his agreements, returned to Sicily, and brought many men to establish a colony; some Siculi, including Archonides, prince of Erbita, joined him.
