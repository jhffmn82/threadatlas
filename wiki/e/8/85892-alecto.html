Alecto in The Aeneid

Virgil · 28 to 18 BC · the work’s page · read it where Alecto first appears

Abstract written by a model

Alecto is a Fury, daughter of eternal Night and sister of the feared Furies, dwelling in the Stygian flood with snakes and an infernal torch. Delighting in war and human suffering, she infects Amata, provokes Turnus into calling for arms, inflames conflict over a household stag, signals battle, and returns to the underworld through Amsanctus.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Thus having said, she sinks beneath the ground,

Alecto, a Fury who delights in wars and human woes, infects Amata with rage and then appears to Turnus in the shape of an old woman to incite him against the Trojans. After Turnus resists her disguised appeal, she reveals her infernal form, drives a fiery torch into his breast, and causes him to call for arms. She later excites the conflict over the household stag, sounds the signal for battle, and returns to the underworld through Amsanctus.

Alecto in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855 · the work’s page · read it where Alecto first appears

Abstract written by a model

Alecto, a Fury summoned from Erebus by Juno, stirs discord against the Trojans by possessing Amata, inciting Turnus, and provoking conflict over Silvia’s stag.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXVI

Alecto is summoned from Erebus by Juno to stir up discord against the Trojans. She possesses Queen Amata, incites Turnus against Aeneas, and provokes conflict between Iulus's hunting party and the herdsmen by leading them to Silvia's tame stag.
