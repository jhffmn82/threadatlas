Soroosh Tayebi Arasteh · 2024-07-22 · the work’s page · read it where RadioRAG first appears

Abstract written by a model

RadioRAG is a 2025 end-to-end, radiology-focused online retrieval-augmented generation framework for diagnostic question answering, published in Radiology: Artificial Intelligence by Soroosh Tayebi Arasteh and colleagues. It uses large language models as reasoning engines but first extracts search keywords from a user question, queries Radiopaedia in real time, chunks and embeds retrieved text with text-embedding-ada-002, stores vectors in Chroma, retrieves the three most similar passages by cosine similarity through LangChain, and supplies them as context to a final model. In this study, Radiopaedia was its sole information source. Its central contribution is grounding answers in current, domain-specific retrieved material rather than relying on static databases or unaided model knowledge. Across evaluations of GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4, Mixtral-8x7B, Llama3-8B, Llama3-70B, and Mistral-7B, it generally improved diagnostic performance and factuality, with relative accuracy gains up to 54%. It was particularly effective in breast imaging and emergency radiology, made open-weights models competitive with GPT-4, and matched or exceeded human radiologist performance in question answering across radiologic subspecialties. It improved most tested models, though effectiveness varied: Mistral-7B-instruct-v0.2 had unchanged accuracy on RSNA-RadioQA, while irrelevant retrievals could lead to incorrect answers. The work unfolds by introducing RadioRAG as a novel framework for typical diagnostic-radiology inquiries, detailing its real-time retrieval pipeline and evaluation against conventional question answering, then reporting its generally improved results and reduced hallucinations. It concludes that the system can improve radiology question-answering accuracy without additional model training, while noting limitations: added response time from on-the-fly database generation, continual load on Radiopaedia, dependence on that source, and limited external validation. Source code is available on GitHub and the system uses Python 3.11.

RadioRAG: Online Retrieval-augmented Generation for Radiology Question Answering

RadioRAG is the subject of a work on online retrieval-augmented generation for radiology question answering.

Abstract

RadioRAG is an end-to-end framework that retrieves context-specific information from www.radiopaedia.org in real time for radiology question answering. It improved diagnostic accuracy across most evaluated models, with relative gains of up to 54%, but showed variable effectiveness across models.

1. Introduction

Radiology RAG (RadioRAG) is introduced as a framework tailored to typical inquiries in diagnostic radiology. It uses large language models to process questions, determine which external sources to query, collect relevant data, and compile answers. Unlike systems using static literature databases, RadioRAG retrieves current information from Radiopaedia.

2. Materials and Methods / 2.1. RSNA Cases .. 2.6. Code and Data Availability

RadioRAG is an end-to-end radiology retrieval-augmented generation system that extracts search key phrases, retrieves relevant Radiopaedia articles, chunks and embeds their text, and supplies the three most similar text vectors as context to a final language model. It was evaluated across six language models and compared with conventional question answering.

3. Results / 3.1. Dataset Characteristics .. 3.5. Comparison to Human Performance

RadioRAG generally improved diagnostic performance across the evaluated LLMs and produced particularly strong gains for open-weights models on ExtendedQA. It also reduced hallucinations by grounding answers in retrieved source content, although irrelevant retrievals sometimes caused incorrect answers.

4. Discussion

Radiology RAG, also called RadioRAG, retrieves contextually relevant information from an established radiological source and provides it to large language models during inference. It generally improved diagnostic question-answering accuracy without additional model training, but occasionally produced incorrect answers when irrelevant source material was retrieved. Its limitations include retrieval time, dependence on Radiopaedia, potential website overload, and limited external validation.
