abduction of Proserpine in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The abduction of Proserpine is an event in which Pluto forcibly carries Proserpina away while she gathers violets and white lilies near Henna in Sicily. She cries for Ceres and her companions as he drives through lakes, sulfurous pools, and a passage toward Tartarus. Ceres learns of it from Proserpine’s girdle and Arethusa, calls it an outrage, and asks Jupiter for her daughter’s return.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Pluto abducts Proserpina while she is gathering violets and white lilies near Henna. She cries for Ceres and her companions as Pluto drives her away through lakes, sulfurous pools, and a passage opened toward Tartarus.

notes .. notes

The abduction of Proserpine is traditionally placed near Henna, where Pluto was said to emerge from a deep cavern in his chariot. Other accounts place it in Attica, Asia, or Spain, while Le Clerc’s historical explanation says that pirates carried her to the seaside and embarked for Epirus.

opening

Proserpine’s abduction is revealed to Ceres through the girdle found at Cyane and Arethusa’s account of seeing Proserpine in the world of darkness. Ceres calls the act an outrage and asks Jupiter to have her daughter restored.

rape of Proserpine in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The rape of Proserpine is a story associated by many authors with Henna and involving Pluto. It may preserve natural philosophy, ancient history, or both: Pluto's seizure of Proserpine is variously explained through pirates acting for Aidoneus, mining regions ruled by Pluto, buried grain, or Erectheus's intended sacrifice of his daughter.

notes .. notes

The rape of Proserpine is treated as a story that may preserve natural philosophy, ancient history, or both. Proposed explanations connect it with the seizure of Proserpine by pirates acting for Aidoneus, the mining regions ruled by Pluto, or the burial of grain beneath the earth.

notes .. notes

The story of the rape of Proserpine by Pluto may have been partly inspired by the tradition that Erectheus was told to sacrifice his daughter Proserpine.

rape of Proserpine in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The rape of Proserpine involves Demeter’s daughter being carried away by Pluto. It is associated with Demeter’s disguised arrival at the Well of Flowers and with Pluto’s descent to his underground realms at Erineus, marked by stones near the Chimarrus.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXVIII.

The rape of Proserpine was associated with Demeter's arrival at the Well of Flowers, where she sat disguised as an old woman. It was also said that Pluto descended at Erineus after carrying Proserpine away.

CHAPTER XXXV.

The Rape of Proserpine is connected with the tradition that Pluto descended to his underground realms after taking Proserpine, and a circle of stones near the river Chimarrus marks the supposed place of his descent.

the abduction of Proserpine in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The abduction of Proserpine begins when Pluto, struck by Cupid’s arrow, seizes her gathering flowers at Enna and takes her to Tartarus, prompting Ceres’s barren-world search and ending in Jupiter’s compromise enabling Proserpine’s seasonal return.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

PROSERPINE

Pluto abducts Proserpine while she gathers flowers in the vale of Enna. She cries for help and drops the flowers from her apron, while Pluto drives his chariot to the River Cyane and opens a passage into Tartarus with his trident. Her abduction causes Ceres to search the world and withhold fertility from the earth.
