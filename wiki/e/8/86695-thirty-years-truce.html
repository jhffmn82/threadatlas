Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, it was the truce and peace agreement concluded by the Athenians and Lacedemonians after ten years of war. In Description of Greece, it was the 30-year peace agreement between the Lacedæmonians and Athenians, made after Athens’ second reduction of Eubœa, excluding Argives but permitting private friendship, and recorded on a brazen pillar near Zeus’ statue.

30-year peace between Sparta and Athens in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where 30-year peace between Sparta and Athens first appears

Abstract written by a model

The 30-year peace was an agreement between Lacedæmonians and Athenians, made after Athens’ second reduction of Eubœa, excluding Argives but permitting private friendship, and recorded on a brazen pillar near Zeus’ statue.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXII.

The 30-year peace between the Lacedæmonians and Athenians was recorded on a brazen pillar near the statue of Zeus dedicated by the Greeks who fought at Platæa. It was made by the Athenians after their second reduction of Eubœa and excluded the Argives from sharing in the peace, while allowing private friendship between Athenians and Argives.
