the prophecies and portents in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Prophecies and portents foretell the Trojan arrival and war in Latium. Bees, Lavinia’s fire, Faunus’s oracle, and the eaten food cakes reveal a foreign prince and son-in-law, Lavinia’s honor, a powerful race, and the end of Trojan wandering.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK VII

A swarm of bees settling on the laurel foretells the arrival of a foreign prince who will conquer the town. Fire surrounding Lavinia foretells her honor and crown as well as war, and Faunus’s oracle declares that a foreign son-in-law will bring a powerful race to Latium; the Trojan consumption of their food cakes then signals that their wandering has ended.
