frankincense

object · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 3 connections

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, frankincense comes from a small tree on the Holy Island whose bark yields drops like tears when cut, and it is used for the worship of the gods throughout the world. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, frankincense is a gum burned in sacrifice to the gods, whose sweet smoke was thought to reach the heavens and which is especially fertile in Persia and Arabia.

Frankincense is described as a gum whose smoke and sweet odors were thought to reach the heavens when burned in sacrifice to the gods. Persia and Arabia are identified as especially fertile in frankincense and other aromatic plants.
