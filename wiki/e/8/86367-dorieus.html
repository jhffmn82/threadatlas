Dorieus

person · 2 works · 6 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Dorieus the Rhodian

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Dorieus was a Peloponnesian admiral of Italian galleys who left Rhodes for the Hellespont and defended ships at Dardanium after an Athenian surprise until Mindarus arrived. In Description of Greece, Dorieus was a Rhodian pancratiast who won three successive Olympic victories and other games, migrated to Thurii and later returned to Rhodes, fought for Spartan interests against Athens, was captured and released by Athenians, and was condemned and executed by Spartans.

Dorieus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Dorieus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Dorieus, youngest son of Diagoras and a Rhodian pancratiast, won three successive Olympic victories and other games. After migrating to Thurii and later returning to Rhodes, he fought for Spartan interests against Athens, was captured and released by Athenians, then condemned and executed by Spartans.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

Dorieus, the youngest son of Diagoras, won three successive Olympic pancratium victories, as well as victories at the Isthmian, Nemean, and Pythian games. He was entered as a Thurian for some contests after migrating from Rhodes to Thurii, later returned to Rhodes, fought for Spartan interests against Athens, was captured and released by the Athenians, and was eventually condemned and executed by the Spartans according to Androtion.
