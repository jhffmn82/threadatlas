Athenian fleet

group · 1 work · 8 quoted facts · 8 connections

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Athenian fleet, numbering seventy-four ships at Dardanium, defeated an opposing fleet after Alcibiades arrived, assembled at Sestos and Cardia, captured the Peloponnesian fleet after victories at Cyzicum, and later operated under Theramenes during the siege of Chalcedon and around several cities. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Athenian fleet returned to Athens with about two hundred captured ships, prisoners, and rich spoils, later suffered defeat at Notium after Antiochus engaged Lysander without waiting for Alcibiades, and ultimately had its command surrendered to Conon by Alcibiades. In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the Athenian fleet began with 150 ships, won at Arginusae while losing twenty-five ships and most aboard them, later numbered 173 ships at Samos, left twenty there, assembled 180 ships at the Hellespont, and was captured at Aegos Potamos.

Athenian fleet in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Athenian fleet first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Athenian fleet, seventy-four ships at Dardanium, defeated an opposing fleet after Alcibiades arrived. It assembled at Sestos and Cardia, sailed to Cyzicum under Alcibiades, and captured the Peloponnesian fleet after victories at sea and land. Later, seventy sail under Theramenes served during Chalcedon’s siege and operations around several cities.

Athenian fleet in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Athenian fleet first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Athenian fleet began with 150 ships from Athenians, other islands, and Samians. It won at Arginusae but lost twenty-five ships and most aboard them. Later it had 173 ships at Samos, left twenty there, sailed toward the Hellespont, assembled 180 ships, and was captured at Aegos Potamos.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. XIV.

The Athenian fleet consisted of 150 ships assembled from sixty ships raised by the Athenians, eighty brought from other islands, and ten obtained from the Samians. It won at Arginusae but lost twenty-five ships and most of the men aboard them.

CHAP. XV.

The Athenian fleet numbered 173 ships at Samos, with twenty left there, and later assembled 180 ships for the Hellespont. Lysander surprised and captured it at Aegos Potamos, where only about ten of its commanders and officers escaped.
