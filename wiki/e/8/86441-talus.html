Talus is Daedalus's sister's son in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, where he invents useful tools before Daedalus murders him from jealousy. Perdix, also called Talos, is Daedalus's eager-to-learn nephew in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, where he invents the saw and compasses before Daedalus throws him from Minerva's citadel and Minerva transforms him into a partridge.

Abstract written by a model

Talus, Daedalus's sister's son, trained by Daedalus, surpassed him by inventing useful tools, including potter's wheel and saw, before Daedalus murdered him from jealousy.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAP. V.

Talus, Daedalus's sister's son, was trained by Daedalus and surpassed him in ingenuity. He invented the potter's wheel, the saw, the turning lathe, and other architectural tools, but Daedalus secretly murdered him out of jealousy.

Perdix (Talos) in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Perdix (Talos) first appears

Abstract written by a model

Perdix, also called Talos, is Dædalus's eager-to-learn nephew. He invents the saw from a fish backbone and compasses from joined iron arms; Dædalus, jealous, throws him from Minerva's citadel, and Minerva transforms him into a partridge.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE III. [VIII.183-259]

Perdix, also called Talos in the explanation, is Dædalus's nephew, a boy eager to learn. He invents the saw by imitating the backbone of a fish and makes a compass from two arms of iron, but Dædalus, jealous of his talent, throws him from the sacred citadel of Minerva.
