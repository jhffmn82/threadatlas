Abstract written by a model

Paris and Helen's love is a fierce, enduring, consuming passion, described as a deep wound from a heavenly arrow and as a newly begun feeling between them. Paris's desire begins through Helen's fame and Venus's promise, intensifies when he sees her, and drives him to travel, toil, declarations, and visible suffering over her closeness with Menelaus; he says only his funeral pyre will end it. Helen doubts the faith of his words but recognizes his signs, writing, affection, and hardships. She admits they are mutually drawn through beauty, nights, conversation, and shared dwelling, yet urges resistance before their fresh flame grows beyond control or fades as quickly as it began.

Paris and Helen's love is in one work on this site, Heroides and Amores. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Paris and Helen's love in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · about 24 to 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where Paris and Helen's love first appears

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

XVI

Paris says that love for Helen began through fame and through Venus's promise before he had seen her. After seeing Helen, his passion intensifies, and he declares that only the flames of his funeral pyre will end it.

nun ego coniugium generosae degener opto,

Paris declares that he is Helen's true lover and repeatedly reveals his concealed passion. He suffers when he sees Menelaus embrace or kiss her, tries unsuccessfully to hide his feelings, and says that his wound reaches to the bones.

XVII

Helen questions whether Paris's professed love is genuine, since men's words are said to lack faith. She nevertheless recognizes his sighs, looks, signals, and written declaration of love, and admits that his affection and the hardships he has undertaken for her would move her if she were willing to sin.

Prima mea est igitur Veneri plaeuisse voluptas ;

Helen acknowledges that she and Paris are already drawn to each other through beauty, long nights, conversation, and living under one roof. She argues that their new love should be resisted while it is still fresh, since a small amount of water can extinguish a new flame and love between strangers may disappear as quickly as it began.
