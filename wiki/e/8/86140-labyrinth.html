Abstract written by a model

In Heroides and Amores, the Labyrinth is a winding structure of stone halls and uncertain paths from which Theseus escapes with Ariadne’s guiding thread. In The Library, the Labyrinth is a prison-like place built by Daedalus to confine the Minotaur, where Theseus kills it and escapes with Ariadne’s thread. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the Labyrinth is a dwelling of many divisions and confusing passages built by Dædalus to contain the Minotaur, from which Theseus escapes with Ariadne’s help. In The Library, the Labyrinth is a place of tangled passages constructed by Daedalus to contain the Minotaur and confuse the way out. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the labyrinth is a winding prison built by Daedalus for Minos to contain the Minotaur, from which Theseus escapes using Ariadne’s thread.

Labyrinth in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Labyrinth first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Labyrinth is a prison-like place of many winding turns, constructed by Daedalus to confine the Minotaur and prevent entrants from finding the way out. Each year, Athenians send seven youths and seven maidens there as food for the Minotaur. Daedalus reveals its exit route to Ariadne; Theseus enters with thread, kills the Minotaur, and follows the thread out. Later, Minos imprisons Daedalus and Icarus in the Labyrinth before pursuing Daedalus to Sicily.
