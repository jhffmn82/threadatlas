Gorgias of Leontini

person · 2 works · 6 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Coronas, Gorgias, Gorgias the orator

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Gorgias was an eloquent Leontine orator and ambassador who taught rhetoric’s rules and persuaded Athens to ally with the Leontines. In Description of Greece, Gorgias of Leontini was a rhetorician and ambassador to the Athenians, famed for his eloquence at Olympia and reportedly living 105 years.

Gorgias of Leontini in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Gorgias of Leontini first appears

Abstract written by a model

Gorgias of Leontini, son of Carmantides, was a rhetorician said to be the first practitioner of rhetoric. Famous for eloquence at Olympia, he went with Tisias on an embassy to the Athenians, had a statue there placed by Eumolpus, and reportedly lived 105 years.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XVII.

Gorgias of Leontini had a statue at Olympia placed there by Eumolpus, his relative through marriage. He was the son of Carmantides and was said to have been the first to practise rhetoric, to have been famous for eloquence at the festival at Olympia, to have gone with Tisias on an embassy to the Athenians, and to have lived 105 years.
