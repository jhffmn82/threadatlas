Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, it is a contest in which Marsyas and Apollo compete to determine the greater artist; Apollo wins after performing with his harp and voice, and Marsyas is flayed alive. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, it is a challenge and trial of musical proficiency in which Marsyas challenges Apollo, Apollo defeats him, and Marsyas receives the described punishment. In The Library, it is a musical contest in which Apollo and Marsyas agree that the victor may do whatever he wishes to the defeated contestant; Apollo wins by turning his lyre upside down, and kills Marsyas.
