  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, it is a contest in which Marsyas and Apollo compete to determine the greater artist; Apollo wins after performing with his harp and voice, and Marsyas is flayed alive.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, it is a challenge and trial of musical proficiency in which Marsyas challenges Apollo, Apollo defeats him, and Marsyas receives the described punishment.
  • In The Library, it is a musical contest in which Apollo and Marsyas agree that the victor may do whatever he wishes to the defeated contestant; Apollo wins by turning his lyre upside down, and kills Marsyas.

contest between Marsyas and Apollo in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Marsyas and Apollo competed to determine who was the greater artist, with the Nysians serving as judges. Apollo ultimately won after performing with his harp and voice, and Marsyas was subsequently flayed alive.
