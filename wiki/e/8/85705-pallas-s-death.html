Pallas’s death in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Pallas’s death occurs when Turnus kills him in single combat with a spear through the heart, tramples his corpse, and takes his belt. Arcadians return Pallas’s body to Evander; grief strikes Aeneas, Evander, and the Arcadians, prompting truce and vengeance against Turnus.

But, in another part, th’ Arcadian horse

Pallas dies when Turnus’s spear pierces his heart. Turnus tramples his corpse and takes the belt from it, while the Arcadians carry his body back to Evander. Aeneas responds by attacking the enemy and killing many warriors in vengeance.

BOOK XI

Pallas’s death brings grief to Aeneas, Evander, and the Arcadians and prompts a solemn truce. Evander treats Turnus as responsible for the vengeance still owed to the living and the dead.
