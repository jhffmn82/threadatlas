Ovid's Paelignian homeland

Also called: Paeligni ruris, Paelignian acres, Paelignian country, Paelignians, Paelignos

Abstract written by a model

The Paelignian country is Ovid’s small, healthy, water-rich homeland, fertile in corn, grass, grain, grapes, olives, and the berry-bearing tree of Pallas. It begins as abundant, refreshed by streams and breezes; without the beloved it feels no longer healthy. Ovid then claims its people, their honorable arms, and his future glory.

Ovid's Paelignian homeland is in one work on this site, Heroides and Amores. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

XV

The Paelignian country is described as small, healthy, fertile, and rich in flowing water. Its fields produce grass, grain, grapes, and the berry-bearing tree of Pallas, while streams and cool breezes refresh the land. Without the beloved, however, the speaker does not experience it as his healthy native country.

XII

Ovid identifies himself as a child of the Paelignian countryside and predicts that he will be called the glory of the Paelignian people. He links that people with the honorable arms they took up when Rome feared its allied forces.
