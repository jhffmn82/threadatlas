Selinuntines in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Selinuntines are a group defined by territorial conflict and shifting alliances. They crossed their boundary with the Egestaeans, seized land, defeated them, and later fought Egesta again over disputed borders, rejecting Syracusan arbitration before suffering defeat by Egestaeans and allies. Allied with Carthaginians and joining Dionysius against Motya, they finally resisted Hannibal but were overwhelmed, with heavy deaths, captivity, and escapees to Agrigentum.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAR VIIL

The Selinuntines crossed the river marking their boundary with the Egestaeans and seized neighboring lands. They defeated the Egestaeans in battle, prompting the latter to seek outside assistance.

CHAP. V.

The Selinuntines made war on Egesta over disputed boundaries and rejected arbitration by the Syracusans. After dispersing across Egesta's countryside, they were surprised and defeated by the Egesteans and their allies.

CHAP. VII.

The Selinuntines resisted Hannibal's army with all their available forces, aided by women and children who carried food and weapons and defended the streets with stones and tiles. After repeated assaults and the loss of their walls, they were overwhelmed; sixteen thousand were killed, more than five thousand were carried away captive, and about twenty-five hundred escaped to Agrigentum.
