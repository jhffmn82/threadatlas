Introduction

The unit concerns biomedical research and the development of automated lay summaries that make complex studies understandable to non-specialists. It presents RAG-RLRC-LaySum, a framework that retrieves trustworthy external explanations, including from Wikipedia, using Retrieval-Augmented Generation and a neural re-ranker. The framework also uses a reward-based approach with readability metrics to improve clarity beyond what traditional fine-tuning and ROUGE scores provide. During training, it uses the Longformer Encoder-Decoder, and the method ranked 11th in the BioLaySumm challenge.

Methodology .. 2.3

The methodology describes a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) framework for processing entire scientific papers and generating lay summaries with relevant background information. RAG uses an initial lay summary as a retrieval query, retrieves Wikipedia descriptions with Pyserini, and applies ColBERT or BGE-v2 neural rerankers. Reinforcement Learning for Readability Control (RLRC) uses PPO and a Gaussian-based reward related to the Flesch Reading Ease score to adjust summary readability. Large Language Models, implemented with Gemini-1.0-pro, paraphrase initial generations and directly produce layman summaries while aiming to include input keywords.

This unit reviews related work on automatic summarization in the biomedical domain, focusing on simplifying scientific content for laypersons while preserving factuality. It describes approaches that use external knowledge, Wikipedia, and trained re-rankers to improve summary accuracy and relevance. The RAG-RLRC-LaySum framework is reported to improve readability and factual accuracy through knowledge retrieval and readability optimization.
