peace treaty in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

The peace treaty is a proposed settlement to end the Latin-Trojan conflict, urged by Diomede, the Latian king, and Drances to save the Latins. Its terms include land, ships, gifts, Lavinia’s possible marriage to Aeneas, and single combat between Turnus and Aeneas. Aeneas and Latinus solemnize it with sacrifices, oaths, and divine witnesses, but fighting immediately violates it. Aeneas later declares it profaned twice and invokes its rites in attacking Turnus.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Thus, full of anxious thought, he summons all

Peace is urged by Diomede, the Latian king, and Drances as a way to save the Latins and stop the bloodshed. The proposed terms involve land, ships, gifts, and possibly Lavinia’s marriage to Aeneas.

BOOK XII

Aeneas and Latinus establish a solemn treaty through sacrifices, oaths, and divine witnesses. Aeneas promises that the Trojans will not rule over the Italians, while Latinus swears that no force or fortune will ever break the union; the treaty is immediately violated when fighting erupts.

When Turnus saw the Trojan quit the plain,

The peace is described as having been violated, and Aeneas invokes the gods and the rites of peace against the Ausonian prince. He claims that the peace has been profaned twice and uses this as justification for his attack.
