Assyrian empire in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Assyrian empire was a polity ruling Asia, introduced through the conquests of Ninus, its first recorded king, and expanding across many Asian countries. It continued through thirty generations, with accounts giving its duration as more than thirteen hundred and sixty or fourteen hundred years, while Herodotus gives five hundred years of Asian empire. Weakened under Sardanapalus, it was overthrown by the Medes with allied revolters, who received its rule.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

DIODORUS SICULUS. / BOOK IL

The Assyrian empire is introduced through the conquests of Ninus, its first king recorded in history. It expanded across many countries of Asia under his rule.

CHAP. IL

The Assyrian empire continued through thirty generations from Ninus and, according to the accounts cited in the unit, for more than thirteen hundred and sixty or fourteen hundred years. It was weakened by Sardanapalus's rule and was ultimately overthrown by the Medes.

CHAP. III.

The Assyrian empire is said to have been overthrown and its rule transferred to the Medes. Herodotus is reported to have said that the Assyrians had held the empire of Asia for 500 years before their defeat by the Medes.
