Paris's letter to Helen

object · 1 work · 2 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: epistula, epistula dives, letter, littera nostra, the letter

Abstract written by a model

Paris’s letter to Helen declares his love, offering promised gifts and urging her to accept his proposed abduction. It opens with a greeting and confession, explains his journey, divine promise, and first sight of Helen, then asks her not to disappoint his hopes. Helen finds it dishonoring yet replies, noting its lavish gifts and appeal to abandon honor.

Paris's letter to Helen is in one work on this site, Heroides and Amores. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

XVI

Paris sends Helen a greeting and asks her to read his confession with a gracious expression. He uses the letter to explain his journey, his divine promise, his first sight of Helen, and his request that she not disappoint his hopes.

XVII

Paris's letter profanes Helen's eyes but prompts her to reply because she considers answering less burdensome than remaining silent. It promises gifts great enough, Helen says, to move even the goddesses, and reveals Paris's hope that she will cross the limits of honor for him.
