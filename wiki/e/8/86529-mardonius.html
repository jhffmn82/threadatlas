Mardonius in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Mardonius is an esteemed Persian commander and relative of Xerxes, noted for valour and prudence. Seeking command, he urged the conquest and destruction of the Greeks, led Persian forces in Greece and Attica, tried to win Peloponnesian cities, destroyed Athens after its rejection of terms, and died valiantly at Plataea in a Greek victory.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

LAST TEN BOOKS / BOOK XI.

Mardonius, a relative of Xerxes and a highly esteemed Persian commander, urged Xerxes to undertake the conquest and destruction of the Greeks. He desired command of the expedition.

CHAP. HI.

Mardonius attempted to win over the cities of the Peloponnesus and then invaded Attica with a large army after the Athenians rejected Persian terms. He destroyed Athens and its remaining temples, fought at Plataea, and was killed while leading the Persian forces.

Mardonius in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Mardonius, son of Gobryas and a general of Xerxes, fought with Persian cavalry against a Greek army, fouling its fountain. After a battle his body was missing; traditions disputed his burial, later honored by his son Artontes.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

Mardonius is associated with a tomb on the road near the ruins of Hysiæ and Erythræ. His body was missing after the battle, and different traditions identify who buried him; his son Artontes rewarded Dionysophanes and several Ionians for not neglecting his burial.

CHAPTER IV.

Mardonius and the Persian cavalry fouled the fountain Gargaphia because the Greek army opposed to them drank from it.
