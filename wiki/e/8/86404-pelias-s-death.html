Pelias's death

Also called: Pelias's murder, a fatal blow, another instance, where she made them the instruments of revenge, avenges the injuries, boil their father, cudgelled him to death, death of Pelias, immediately killed him, mangled, pretended friendship, revenge against Pelias, severs his throat, stab him to death, their vengeance

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Pelias's death is caused by his daughters, except Alcestis, who cudgel him to death after Medea deceives them into believing that boiling him will restore his youth. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, revenge against Pelias is carried out by Medea through deception of his daughters, leading them to stab him while she completes his destruction. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the death of Pelias is presented as a possible consequence of Medea's revenge: his daughters stab him as he sleeps, and Medea severs his throat and places his body in a boiling cauldron. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Pelias's murder is Medea's revenge against him: she deceives his daughters, bewitches him and his guards, incites the attack, kills him, and escapes.
