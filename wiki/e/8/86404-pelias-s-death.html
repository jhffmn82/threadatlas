Pelias's death

  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Pelias's death is caused by his daughters, except Alcestis, who cudgel him to death after Medea deceives them into believing that boiling him will restore his youth.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the death of Pelias is presented as a possible consequence of Medea's revenge: his daughters stab him as he sleeps, and Medea severs his throat and places his body in a boiling cauldron.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, revenge against Pelias is carried out by Medea through deception of his daughters, leading them to stab him while she completes his destruction.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Pelias's murder is Medea's revenge against him: she deceives his daughters, bewitches him and his guards, incites the attack, kills him, and escapes.

Pelias's murder in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Pelias's murder is Medea's revenge against Pelias: she deceives his daughters, bewitches him and his guards, incites them to attack, kills him, and escapes.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

MEDEA AND AESON

For Pelias's murder, Medea prepares a caldron containing only water and simple herbs. She casts a spell over Pelias and his guards, urges his daughters to strike him, delivers the fatal blow herself, and escapes before the daughters discover the treachery.
