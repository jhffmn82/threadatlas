Auge in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Auge, daughter of Aleus and priestess of Athena, is seduced by Heracles and secretly bears Telephus, hiding him in Athena’s precinct. When the hidden birth is revealed amid barrenness in the land, Aleus gives her to Nauplius to be killed or sold abroad. Nauplius instead brings her to Teuthras in Mysia, who receives and marries her.

THE LIBRARY, II. vr. 3-4

Auge, daughter of Aleus, is unknowingly violated by Hercules and secretly gives birth to a child. Aleus discovers her pregnancy, and Auge is given to Nauplius, who takes her to Teuthras in Mysia; Teuthras makes her his wife.

IX. ᾿Αρκάδος δὲ καὶ Λεανείρας τῆς ᾿Αμύκλου

Auge was Aleus's daughter and held the priesthood of Athena. After being seduced by Heracles, she hid her baby in Athena's precinct; when the land became barren and the impiety in the precinct was revealed, her father handed her over to Nauplius for execution. Teuthras received and married her.

Auge in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Auge, daughter of Aleus and from Arcadia, is linked to Hercules and Teuthras. At a spring north of Athene’s temple at Tegea, local tradition says Hercules violated her, while Hecatæus says she had a child by him. She bore Telephus, described as the son most like Hercules. Accounts differ: she secretly gave birth from her father, or, when Aleus sent her with Nauplius to be drowned, she fell on her knees and gave birth. She later went to Teuthras in Mysia and married him. She is represented in temples of Athene and Ilithyia, including kneeling.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XLVII.

Auge is represented by an effigy in the temple of Athene at Tegea in imitation of a painting. A spring north of the temple is said to be the place where Hercules violated her, though the Tegean legend differs from Hecatæus’s account.

CHAPTER XLVIII.

Auge is represented on her knees in the temple of Ilithyia. One tradition says that Aleus ordered Nauplius to drown her in the sea, but that she fell on her knees and gave birth to a son while being led there; another says that she gave birth to Telephus secretly from her father.

CHAPTER XXVIII.

Auge appears in the painting near Eurynomus. She came to Teuthras in Mysia and bore a son by Hercules who was said to resemble him more than the sons born to Hercules by any other woman.
