Althæa

person · 4 works · 16 quoted facts · 6 connections

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Althea is Meleager’s mother and the sister of the men he killed; she curses him, regrets his death, and hangs herself. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Althæa is Meleager’s mother and the sister of Plexippus and Toxeus; she burns the fatal billet, causing Meleager’s death, and then takes her own life. In The Library, Althaea is the daughter of Thestius, wife of Oeneus, and mother of Meleager and Deianira; she burns the brand on which Meleager’s life depends, causing his death. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Althea is the queen of Calydon and Meleager’s mother; she burns his life-controlling brand to avenge her brothers and then takes her own life.

Althæa in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Althæa first appears

Abstract written by a model

Althæa is the mother of Meleager and sister of Plexippus and Toxeus. She first rejoices in Meleager’s victory, but grief and vengeance follow when he kills her brothers. Torn between being mother and sister, she devotes Meleager to the Furies and burns the fatal billet preserved from his birth, killing him. Afterward, she takes her own life with a sword.
