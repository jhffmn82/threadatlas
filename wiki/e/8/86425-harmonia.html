Harmonia

person · 5 works · 15 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Hermione, his exiled wife, his wife, most unhappy one, wife, ‘Appovias, “Appovias
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Harmonia, a child of Jupiter and Electra, marries Cadmus in a marriage celebrated in the presence of the gods and is the sister of Jasion.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Harmonia is described as the daughter of Mars and Venus and wife of Cadmus; she accompanies Cadmus into exile and retirement in Illyria, becomes a serpent as he does, and asks the celestial Gods to transform her too.
  • In The Library, Harmonia, daughter of Aphrodite and Ares, is given by Zeus in marriage to Cadmus, receives a robe and necklace made by Hephaestus, leaves Thebes with Cadmus for the Encheleans, leads them against the Illyrians, becomes a serpent, and is sent to the Elysian Fields.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Harmonia is said to have made three ancient wooden statues of Aphrodite as votive offerings, using wood from the gunwales of Cadmus' ships, and associated their titles with pure love, common sensual love, and the turning of men's hearts toward lawless passion and unholy deeds.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Harmonia, Venus's daughter, marries Cadmus and receives Vulcan's brilliant necklace; when he becomes a serpent, she asks to share his fate and transforms with him.

Hermione in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Hermione, also called Harmonia, is described as the daughter of Mars and Venus and wife of Cadmus. She accompanies Cadmus into exile and retirement in Illyria. As he changes into a serpent, she laments, asks the celestial Gods to transform her too, and becomes a serpent; other accounts call her a singing girl or link her name to Mount Hermon.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Hermione accompanies Cadmus into exile and wandering and laments as he changes into a serpent. She asks the celestial Gods to transform her likewise and becomes a serpent.

notes

Hermione was Cadmus's wife and retired with him into Illyria. The story later claimed that she and Cadmus became serpents.

Harmonia in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Harmonia, daughter of Aphrodite and Ares, is given by Zeus in marriage to Cadmus and receives a robe and necklace made by Hephaestus. She leaves Thebes with Cadmus for the Encheleans, leads them against the Illyrians, then becomes a serpent and is sent to the Elysian Fields.

Harmonia was the daughter of Aphrodite and Ares. Zeus gave her in marriage to Cadmus, and Cadmus gave her a robe and a necklace made by Hephaestus, according to one account.

Harmonia leaves Thebes with Cadmus and goes to the Encheleans. She leads the Encheleans with Cadmus against the Illyrians, and later she is transformed into a serpent with Cadmus and sent to the Elysian Fields by Zeus.
