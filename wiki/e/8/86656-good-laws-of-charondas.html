good laws of Charondas in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The good laws of Charondas are a civic legal code emphasizing public education, military duty, household and office standards, guardianship, and strict obedience to written law. It punishes desertion, cowardice, false accusation, and association with wicked men, while regulating public office and orphans. Its culminating safeguard requires amendment proposers to wear a rope until a public vote, risking hanging if rejected, though some later changes passed.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

agreed on upon certain conditions. Whilst things were thus acting

Charondas’s laws excluded from public office men who brought a second wife and stepmother into their households, punished false accusers by parading them through the city crowned with tamarisk, and fined those who kept company with wicked men. They also regulated orphan guardianship, punished cowardice with public humiliation rather than death, and required strict obedience to the wording of the law. One law required anyone proposing a legal alteration to wear a rope around his neck until the people voted, with rejection resulting in hanging; several later proposals nevertheless succeeded in amending particular laws.
