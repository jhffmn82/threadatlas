Cydippe's oath to Acontius

Abstract written by a model

Cydippe’s oath to Acontius is a disputed pledge of marriage made when Cydippe read aloud words on an apple Acontius threw to her in Diana’s temple. Acontius says Diana witnessed the binding oath and demands that Cydippe honor it rather than commit perjury. Cydippe argues that she did not swear freely: only deliberate intention makes words binding.

Cydippe's oath to Acontius is in one work on this site, Heroides and Amores. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Cydippe's oath to Acontius in Heroides and Amores

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

XX

Cydippe's oath was made when she read aloud the words written on the apple that Acontius threw to her in Diana's temple. The oath promised that she would marry Acontius, and Acontius claims that Diana witnessed and remembered it. He argues that Cydippe cannot deny the oath and should honor it even if she will not do so for his sake.

nam quod habes et tu gemini verba altera pacti, 155

The pledge was made by Cydippe to Acontius before Diana, while the rival suitor has only his father’s promise of Cydippe. Acontius insists that Cydippe must honor the pledge and avoid perjury.

XXI

Cydippe maintains that the mind is what swears, because only deliberate judgment can give words their binding force. She says that she herself did not swear freely but merely read words that appeared to swear.
