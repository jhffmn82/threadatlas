Clearchus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Clearchus is a Laconian harmost and tyrant of Byzantium who commands large mercenary and Greek forces. Sent to govern Byzantium, he gathers mercenaries, kills magistrates and leading citizens, confiscates goods, and banishes others. Defeated by the Lacedaemonians at Porus, he escapes to Selymbria and then Ionia, where Cyrus makes him a general. He commands Peloponnesians, Lacedaemonians, and mercenaries in battle, orders a close attack, restrains pursuit, defeats the king's forces, and refuses to surrender Greek arms. Going to Tissaphernes with other commanders, he is seized and killed.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. III.

Clearchus was sent to govern Byzantium but gathered mercenaries and acted as a tyrant, killing magistrates and leading citizens, confiscating their goods, and banishing others. Defeated by the Lacedaemonians at Porus, he escaped to Selymbria and later to Ionia, where Cyrus made him a general.

CHAP. V-

Clearchus commanded the Peloponnesians except the Achaians and led the Lacedaemonians and some mercenaries in the right wing. He ordered the Grecians to run in upon the enemy at close range, and later stopped his men from pursuing too far because he feared the barbarians would cut them off.

Clearchus, after he had made a great slaughter among them, (it

After making a great slaughter among the king's forces, Clearchus erected a trophy and returned to camp. He refused Artaxerxes's demand that the Greeks surrender their arms, led the army toward the sea, and later went with the other commanders to Tissaphernes, who seized him and had him killed.
