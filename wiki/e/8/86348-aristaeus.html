Aristaeus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Aristaeus, son of Apollo and Cyrene, was raised by nymphs, who named him Agreus and taught him cheese-making, beekeeping, and olive cultivation. Prudent, honest, and learned, he tutored Bacchus, first sacrificed to him as a god, married Autonoe, and fathered Actaeon. He aided Coos, Sardinia, and Sicily, learned Bacchus’s rites in Thrace, disappeared near Mount Hiemus, and was worshipped as a god for useful arts.

The poet in his hymns confirms what we have before declared^

Aristaeus was one of Nysa’s sisters’ associates and was appointed to tutor the child Dionysius. He is described as prudent, honest, and learned, and he was said to have first offered sacrifices to Dionysius as a god.

CHAP. V.

Aristaeus was the son of Apollo and Cyrene and was raised by nymphs, who taught him to curdle milk, make beehives, and plant olive-yards. He married Autonoe and was the father of Actaeon, later helped end a plague on Coos, civilized inhabitants in Sardinia, taught useful practices in Sicily, and learned the rites of the orgia from Bacchus in Thrace. He eventually disappeared near Mount Hiemus and was honored as a god.

Aristaeus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Aristaeus, son of Cyrene, is a shepherd and first teacher of bee management. His advances cause Eurydice to flee into a snake’s path. After losing his bees, he captures Proteus, appeases the nymphs, honors Orpheus and Eurydice, and restores a swarm through sacrifice.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XVII

Aristaeus was a shepherd who made advances toward Eurydice, causing her to flee into the path of a snake. He was the son of Cyrene and was said to have first taught the management of bees. After his bees perished, he followed Cyrene's instructions, appeased the nymphs, and recovered a swarm from the carcass of a sacrificed bull.
