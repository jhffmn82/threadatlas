Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Tiber is crossed by the Roman army and by Romans fleeing the defeat at the Allia, and Pontius Camillus crosses it by night to reach the Capitol. In The Aeneid, Tiber is a sacred river of yellow waters that guides Aeneas and the Trojans, marks territorial borders, and bears signs and omens of settlement and war. In The Aeneid, Tiber is a river god who appears to Aeneas in a dream, foretells his success and the future city, and promises safe passage to Rome. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Tiber surrounds the island that becomes Æsculapius’s Roman dwelling, its waters dividing around the island.

Tiber in The Aeneid

Virgil · 28 to 18 BC · the work’s page · read it where Tiber first appears

Abstract written by a model

Tiber is a sacred stream and river of yellow waters flowing through wooded Latium, past meadows, folds, the Trojan camp, and into the sea. It marks borders between Arcadians, Evander’s kingdom, and the Latian state. It guides Aeneas and the Trojans inland toward Apollo’s destined place, reverses and calms its current for their ships, and contains signs of settlement and the white sow. By war’s end, it is foretold to bear bodies and weapons.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK VII

The Tiber carries its yellow waters through a wooded region and into the sea. Aeneas and the Trojans follow it inland, and its presence confirms the place toward which Apollo has sent them.

BOOK VIII

The Tiber River reverses and smooths its current so that Aeneas’s ships can pass easily. Its banks contain Evander’s settlement and the site where the white sow and her thirty young are found.

The Tiber bounds Evander’s narrow kingdom on one side and the Latian state on the other. Aeneas predicts that the river will be choked with the bodies, swords, and shields of the war against Turnus.
