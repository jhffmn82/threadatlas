Celtiberians in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Celtiberians were a people formed through the union of Celts and Iberians. They inhabited rich, fertile Iberia and became notable for strong cavalry, hardy infantry, varied shields and armor, and well-tempered swords and daggers. Their customs combined cruelty toward enemies and criminals with hospitality to strangers, and included drinking honey-based beverages and purchased wine.

The Celtiberians arose from the union of Celts and Iberians and became famous because they occupied a rich and fertile country. They fielded strong cavalry and hardy infantry, used several kinds of shields and armor, and carried well-tempered two-edged swords and short daggers. Their customs included washing and cleaning their teeth with urine, showing cruelty to enemies and criminals but hospitality toward strangers, and drinking honey-based beverages as well as purchased wine.
