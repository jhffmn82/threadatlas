Lavinia in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Lavinia is Latinus’s only daughter, heir to his crown and state, a royal unmarried noblewoman promised to Turnus yet fated to marry the foreigner Aeneas. A sacrificial fire foretells her future honor and the war her fate will bring to Latium. Proposed as Aeneas’s bride to secure peace, she instead remains central to the conflict through Turnus’s desire. As Amata pleads with Turnus, Lavinia blushes and weeps, deepening his jealous resolve to fight. She ultimately survives as Aeneas’s wife and Silvius’s mother.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK VII

Lavinia is Latinus’s only daughter and heir to the royal state. During a sacrifice, fire catches her hair and clothing, and the seer interprets the portent as meaning that she will be honored and crowned but that war will devastate the country because of her fate.

Thus, full of anxious thought, he summons all

Lavinia is proposed by Drances as a bride for Aeneas so that the Latins and Trojans can establish a lasting peace. Turnus’s desire for her is presented as a cause of the continuing conflict.

BOOK XII

Lavinia blushes and sheds tears when Amata pleads with Turnus to avoid the combat. Her reaction intensifies Turnus’s desire, jealousy, and resolve to fight.

Lavinia in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Lavinia, Latinus’s charming daughter, is sought by neighboring chiefs and favored for Turnus by her parents, but an oracle foretells a foreign husband; after Aeneas triumphs over his foes, she becomes his bride.

Chapter XXVI

Lavinia is the charming daughter of Latinus and is sought in marriage by neighboring chiefs. Her parents favor Turnus, but the oracle declares that her destined husband will come from a foreign land.
