Antiope in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Antiope, Nycteus’s daughter, becomes pregnant by Zeus, flees and marries Epopeus, is punished and abused by Lycus and Dirce, then is recognized and rescued by her sons Zethus and Amphion.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Ὁ δὲ Κάδμος pera ‘Appovias Θήβας ἐκλιπὼν

Antiope is the daughter of Nycteus, and Zeus has intercourse with her. When she becomes pregnant, she flees to Epopeus at Sicyon and marries him; Nycteus later commands Lycus to punish her. She gives birth to Zethus and Amphion, is imprisoned and mistreated by Lycus and Dirce, and is later recognized and rescued by her sons.

Antiope in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Antiope, driven mad after Dirce’s cruel death attributed to Dionysus’s anger, wanders Greece, is cured and married by Phocus, and is buried with him.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XVII.

Antiope was driven mad and wandered through Greece after Dirce’s cruel death, which was attributed to Dionysus’s anger toward her. Phocus cured her and married her, and she was buried with him; her tomb was also the intended destination for earth taken from the sepulchre of Zethus and Amphion.
