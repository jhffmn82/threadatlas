Coroebus

person · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Corœbus, our leader, young Coroebus

Abstract written by a model

In The Aeneid, Coroebus leads Trojan troops defending Troy beside Aeneas, driven by love for Cassandra; after proposing captured Greek arms, he dies at Pallas’ altar trying to rescue her. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Coroebus, Mygdon’s son, comes intending to marry Cassandra but is killed, with accounts differing between Neoptolemus and Diomede.
