Coroebus in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Coroebus leads Trojan troops defending Troy beside Aeneas, driven by love for Cassandra; after proposing captured Greek arms, he dies at Pallas’ altar trying to rescue her.

Coroebus joins Aeneas and the other Trojans in the defense of Troy, motivated by his love for Cassandra. He proposes that the Trojans wear captured Greek arms, but he is later killed by Peneleus at Pallas’ altar while trying to rescue Cassandra.

CHAPTER XXVII.

Coroebus, the son of Mygdon, came to wed Cassandra and was killed by Neoptolemus according to the prevalent tradition, but by Diomede according to Lescheos. He is depicted among the dead above Eioneus and Admetus.
