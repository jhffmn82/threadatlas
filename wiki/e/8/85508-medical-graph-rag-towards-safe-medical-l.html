This document presents MedGraphRAG, a graph-based retrieval-augmented generation framework for safer, more reliable medical large language model responses. Authored by Junde Wu, Jiayuan Zhu, Yunli Qi, Jingkun Chen, Min Xu, Filippo Menolascina, and Vicente Grau, it addresses medical LLM limitations including restricted context, specialized terminology, insufficiently verifiable evidence, and the need to handle private medical data safely. MedGraphRAG builds medical knowledge graphs from documents through semantic chunking, entity extraction, and relationship linking. Its Triple Graph Construction connects user documents to credible RepoGraph medical sources and controlled-vocabulary definitions, producing Meta-MedGraphs. These graphs are summarized with hierarchical medical tags. Its U-Retrieval process then uses top-down precise retrieval, entity-neighbor retrieval, and bottom-up response refinement by a response LLM, aiming to provide traceable, evidence-based answers with source documentation and definitions. The work evaluates the system against standard RAG, GraphRAG, and no-retrieval baselines using Llama2, Llama3, Gemini-pro, and GPT-4. It uses MIMIC-IV as RAG data, MedC-K and evidence publications as upper repository data, and the UMLS graph as bottom repository data. Across nine medical Q&A benchmarks, two health fact-checking benchmarks, and a long-form generation dataset, MedGraphRAG improves accuracy, particularly for smaller models, and reaches new state-of-the-art results with larger models. Human assessments find its long-form answers more pertinent, correct, citation-supported, and understandable. Ablations show gradual gains from Med-MetaGraph, Triple Graph Construction, and U-Retrieval, with especially large improvements from Triple Graph Construction. Comparisons adding Med-Paper and Med-Dictionary data indicate that both retrieval method and data matter, with reported gains exceeding 6% and 8%. The document situates the framework among medical LLMs, RAG, citation systems, and GraphRAG, arguing that existing approaches insufficiently support attributed medical responses and term explanations. It concludes that MedGraphRAG is effective, while future work should support real-time updates and validation on real-world clinical data.

Abstract

MedGraphRAG is a graph-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework designed for the medical domain to help Large Language Models generate evidence-based medical responses and handle private medical data more safely and reliably. It introduces Triple Graph Construction, which links user documents with credible medical sources and controlled vocabularies, and U-Retrieval, which combines Top-down Precise Retrieval with Bottom-up Response Refinement. The framework was evaluated on 9 medical Q&A benchmarks, 2 health fact-checking benchmarks, and a collected long-form generation dataset. MedGraphRAG consistently outperformed state-of-the-art models and produced responses with credible source documentation and definitions.

Introduction

The unit examines challenges faced by large language models in medicine, including limited context windows, specialized terminology, and the need for verifiable evidence. It introduces Medical GraphRAG, a medical-domain graph-based retrieval-augmented generation method that uses Triple Graph Construction and U-Retrieval to improve evidence-based responses. MedGraphRAG links user data to medical papers and dictionaries, supports traceability, and retrieves information through hierarchical medical tags. Evaluations on medical Q&A and health fact-checking benchmarks, along with clinician assessments, report improvements over classic RAG, GraphRAG, and many specifically trained medical LLMs.
