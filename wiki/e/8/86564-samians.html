Samians

group · 1 work · 7 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Samian, SamianS, Sann'ans, Simians, Sninians, the Samians

Abstract written by a model

The Samians, Asian people, first joined the Greeks at Mycale, where their arrival encouraged the forces and helped defeat the Persians. They then fought Milesians over Priene and revolted from Athens. Though aided by Persians and briefly successful against part of Pericles' fleet, they were defeated, lost their fleet, gave hostages, paid fines and war costs, and had democracy restored.

Samians is in one work on this site, The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. IV.

The Samians joined the Greek forces at Mycale and marched swiftly toward the enemy. Their arrival revived the courage of the Greeks and contributed to the Persian defeat.

CHAP. V.

The Samians fought the Milesians over Priene and revolted from Athens. After Pericles initially subdued them, factions in Samos restored their independence with Persian aid; they defeated part of Pericles' fleet, but were eventually defeated, fined, disarmed, and subjected to a restored democracy.
