Inspector Baynes

Abstract written by a model

Inspector Baynes is a provincial police inspector of the Surrey Constabulary and the inspector in charge of Garcia's murder investigation. Methodical and independent, he traces John Scott Eccles from a telegram, carefully examines Wisteria Lodge, finds a partly burned note to Aloysius Garcia, and concludes Garcia died before the rain. He shows Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson the lodge's unusual evidence but resists Holmes's advice. Baynes appears to pursue Garcia's cook, arranging an ambush and arresting a huge, savage suspect. He later reveals this was a deliberate false line: his real investigation targeted Henderson, whom he identifies as Don Murillo, while a plain-clothes officer watched High Gable. Expecting Miss Burnet's evidence, Baynes plans to prosecute the household and ultimately uses the mulatto cook's return to set a trap and capture him.

The Adventure of Wisteria Lodge / 1. The Singular Experience of Mr. John Scott Eccles

Inspector Baynes traces John Scott Eccles from the telegram and examines Wisteria Lodge. He finds the partly burned note addressed to Aloysius Garcia, reports that Garcia had been dead before the rain, and invites Sherlock Holmes to inspect other remarkable findings at the house.

We sat for some time in silence after our visitors had left. Holmes

Inspector Baynes is expected to return with further information and is later identified as Holmes and Watson's companion. He travels with them to Esher and joins their visit to Wisteria Lodge.

2. The Tiger of San Pedro

Inspector Baynes shows Holmes and Dr. Watson around Wisteria Lodge and points out the strange kitchen evidence. He insists on pursuing the case independently and later arranges an ambush that results in the arrest of a huge, savage man believed to be Garcia's cook. Baynes rejects Holmes's warning that he may be following the wrong line.

Inspector Baynes reveals that he had been investigating Henderson and deliberately arrested the wrong man to make Henderson believe that the police were no longer watching him. He confirms Henderson's identity as Don Murillo, explains that a plain-clothes officer had been tracking the High Gable household, and intends to prosecute its occupants when Miss Burnet gives evidence. He later recognizes the importance of the mulatto cook's return and leaves a trap that leads to the cook's capture.
