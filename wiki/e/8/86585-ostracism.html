Abstract written by a model

Ostracism at Athens required citizens to write on shells the name of a person they feared might overturn the popular government. The person named most often was banished for five years, ostensibly to humble powerful and aspiring men. Ostracism at Athens is described as the model for the Syracusan practice of Petalism. It involved voting to banish a citizen considered powerful enough to seek sovereignty, rather than punishing a specific crime.
