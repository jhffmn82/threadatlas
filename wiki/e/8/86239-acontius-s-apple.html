Acontius's apple

object · 1 work · 2 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: apple, fetus, fruit, malum, pomi, pomum, that apple of yours, the apple

Abstract written by a model

Acontius’s apple is a rolling apple carrying a written oath and verse declaring his marriage claim. It comes or is thrown before Cydippe at Diana’s temple, where she reads its deceptive-looking but binding words in Diana’s presence and is caught into accepting the oath, nearly swearing again. Its bloodless color is compared with her pallor, and Acontius proposes a golden image at Delos as testimony that the inscription was fulfilled.

Acontius's apple is in one work on this site, Heroides and Amores. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

nam quod habes et tu gemini verba altera pacti, 155

The apple came to Cydippe bearing Acontius’s deceptive-looking but binding written words, which she read in Diana’s presence. Acontius proposes that a golden image of the apple be dedicated at Delos as a testimony that its inscription was fulfilled.

XXI

An apple bearing Acontius's verse was thrown before Cydippe's feet at Diana's temple. When Cydippe read it, she nearly swore to Acontius again, and she compares her being caught by the apple with the way an apple caught Schoeneus's daughter.

Cum tamen haec dixi, cum me tibi firma negavi,

Acontius’s apple carried the writing through which the speaker was deceived into accepting the oath. The speaker compares its bloodless color with the pallor of her own sick body.
