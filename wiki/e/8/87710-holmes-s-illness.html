Holmes's illness

event · 1 work · 5 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: desperately ill, disease, his illness, his trouble, how is he, pathological possibilities, problems of disease, the disease, the sad condition, this illness, tropical disease, your end

Abstract written by a model

In His Last Bow, Holmes's illness was a tropical, supposedly infallibly deadly disease from Sumatra that appeared after a Rotherhithe case and caused three days without food or drink, fever, wasting, spasms, breathing difficulty, and contagiousness by touch. In His Last Bow, the tropical disease was described as a deadly, poorly understood condition from Sumatra, with related conditions including Tapanuli fever and black Formosa corruption; Culverton Smith had unusual knowledge of such diseases. In His Last Bow, Holmes's illness was a three-day simulated severe Eastern disease, presented through delirium, cramp, weakness, and breathing difficulty before being revealed as a performance.

Holmes's illness is a tropical, supposedly infallibly deadly disease from Sumatra. After work on a Rotherhithe case, it appears severe and fatal, causing three days without food or drink, fever, wasting, spasms, breathing difficulty, and contagiousness by touch.

The Adventure of the Dying Detective

Holmes's illness is presented as a severe and apparently fatal condition that began after he worked on a case in an alley near the river at Rotherhithe. He claims that it has left him unable to eat or drink for three days, causes fever, wasting, spasms, and difficulty breathing, and is contagious by touch.
