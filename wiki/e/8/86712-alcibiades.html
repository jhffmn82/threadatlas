Alcibiades in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Alcibiades is an Athenian, Pericles's underage nephew and ward, portrayed as noble, wealthy, eloquent, and skilled in martial affairs. Early on, he advised Pericles to evade an inquiry into entrusted money by pursuing a great war. He supported Argos, helped restore its democracy by expelling Lacedaemonian supporters, and became one of three generals sent against Syracuse, as well as the principal promoter of the Sicilian expedition. Accused at Athens over the mutilation of Minerva's statues and condemned to death in absentia with fellow fugitives, he escaped from Sicily and fled to Sparta. There he urged the Lacedaemonians to invade Athens and assist Syracuse, while avoiding revenge from both Syracusans and Athenians. While banished, he initially aided the Lacedaemonians, then prevented Pharnabazus from sending three hundred ships to them. Recalled by Athens, he became general and helped restore its fortunes through victories and support for troops. He commanded forces at Dardanium and Cyzicum, where deception helped defeat and pursue Mindarus; Athens sent thirty additional ships afterward. He gathered money and Thracian aid, took Selymbria, captured Byzantium with Theramenes, and returned to Athens greatly admired. There he defended himself, recovered confiscated property, was absolved of condemnation and alleged profanation, and received full authority by sea and land. After defeating the Andrians, his position declined following the defeat at Notium, attributed to Antiochus's disobedience. He devastated Cuma, faced accusations of attacking a confederate city, yielded the fleet to Conon, and withdrew into self-imposed exile in Thrace, where he had friends Medocus and Seuthes. Seeking access to the Persian king after learning of Cyrus's plans, he was trapped in Phrygia, his lodging burned, and he was killed by fire and missiles.

Alcibiades, who was under age and in the care of Pericles, advised him to consider how he might avoid giving an account of the money entrusted to him. His advice led Pericles to seek a great war as a way to escape the financial inquiry.

CHAR VIIL

Alcibiades supported the Argives and joined the Athenian force sent to them, although he held no command at that time. He later restored democracy at Argos by banishing those who favored the Lacedaemonians and was appointed one of the three generals of the expedition to Sicily.

DIODORUS SICULUS. .. CHAP. I.

Alcibiades was appointed one of three Athenian generals for the expedition against Syracuse. After being accused at Athens in connection with the mutilation of the statues of Minerva, he escaped from Sicily to Sparta, where he persuaded the Lacedaemonians to invade the Athenians and aid Syracuse.

Alcibiades is described as the principal promoter of the war against Sicily. He avoided revenge from the Syracusans and Athenians by flight.

CHAP. 111.

Alcibiades, while banished from Athens, aided the Lacedaemonians but later persuaded Pharnabazus not to send three hundred ships to their assistance. He was recalled, made an Athenian general, helped restore Athens's fortunes, and won further support by sharing plunder with the troops.

CHAP. V.

Alcibiades unexpectedly joined the battle at Dardanium with twenty ships and signaled the Athenians with a purple flag, causing the Lacedaemonians to flee. At Cyzicum he used a feigned flight to lure Mindarus away from the city, then attacked and pursued him to Claros.

Alcibiades led Athenian operations in the region of Pharnabazus, gathered money and Thracian support, and took Selymbria by surrender. He then joined Theramenes in attacking Byzantium and captured it after promising that none of the Byzantines would be harmed.

CHAP. IX.

Alcibiades returned to Athens amid great admiration, defended himself before the assembly, recovered his confiscated property, and was absolved from his condemnation and alleged profanation of the mysteries. He was appointed commander with full authority by sea and land, defeated the Andrians, and later suffered defeat at Notium because Antiochus disobeyed his orders. He devastated Cuma, was accused of attacking a confederate city, surrendered the fleet to Conon, and withdrew to Thrace rather than return to Athens.

CHAP. III.

Alcibiades learned that Cyrus was secretly preparing war against Artaxerxes and sought help from Pharnabazus to reach the Persian king. When Pharnabazus refused, Alcibiades traveled toward Paphlagonia, but enemies surrounded his lodging in Phrygia, burned it, and killed him with fire and missiles.
