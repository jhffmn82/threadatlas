agriculture in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Agriculture is advanced worldwide through teaching tillage, husbandry, and management of earth’s fruits. Osiris teaches it in Ethiopia and Attica as part of civilizing humanity, while Isis introduces wheat and barley for bread. Its origins and instruction are also linked to Ceres’s seed-corn, sowing, storage, and baking, amid rival claims of Egyptians, Athenians, and Sicilians.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

We shall first speak of the barbarians; not that we judge them

Osiris taught the methods of tillage and the proper management of the earth's fruits, while Isis introduced the use of wheat and barley for bread. Agriculture formed part of Osiris's larger effort to civilize humanity.

CHAP. 11.

Osiris teaches husbandry and tillage in Ethiopia and assigns Triptolemus the cultivation of land in Attica. He is credited with spreading useful food and drink, including a barley drink for places where vines do not grow.

CHAP. IV.

Agriculture is chiefly represented through Ceres's discovery and teaching of seed-corn, sowing, housing, and baking. The account also records competing claims by Egyptians, Athenians, and Sicilians concerning where corn was first discovered or introduced.
