Holmes's investigation in His Last Bow

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1908-04-17

Sherlock Holmes independently investigates two tragedies marked by combustion, stuffiness, terror, deaths, and madness. He examines the vicarage’s windows and lamp, collects ashes and powder, tests the substance’s effects, and traces Dr. Leon Sterndale through gravel, shoes, lamp evidence, and movements. Holmes reconstructs the events, verifies Sterndale’s account, and declines further action.

Holmes's investigation focuses on the shared presence of combustion, stuffiness, and terrifying effects in the two tragedies. He examines the vicarage windows and lamp, collects ashes and powder, verifies Sterndale’s account, and performs an experiment to test whether the substance caused the deaths and madness.

Fortunately, there is one man in England who knows all about it, and I

The investigation traced Dr. Leon Sterndale's actions through the reddish gravel at the window, his tennis shoes and movements, the lamp shining in daylight, and powder remains on the lamp shield. Holmes independently reconstructs the events but chooses not to take further action.
