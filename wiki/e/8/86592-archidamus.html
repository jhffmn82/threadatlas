Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Archidamus is a Spartan king and general who saves citizens during an earthquake, organizes Sparta’s defense, and commands invasions of Attica and Boeotia, the siege of Platea, and an unsuccessful attempt to provoke its revolt. In Description of Greece, Archidamus is a Spartan king variously described as the grandson of Leotychides or the son of Agesilaus, associated with invasions of Attica, the capture of Platæa, aid to the Phocians, and death fighting barbarians near Tarentum.

Archidamus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where Archidamus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Archidamus is a king of Sparta and the Lacedaemonians, made their general. During an earthquake he saves citizens by leading them into open ground and organizes Sparta’s defense. He then commands invasions of Attica and Boeotia, devastating territory, besieging castles and Platea, and unsuccessfully seeking Platea’s revolt.

CHAP. XIII.

Archidamus, king of Sparta, preserved many citizens by leading them into the open field during the earthquake. He then organized them for the defense of Sparta and its territory.

Archidamus was made general by the Lacedaemonians and led an invasion of Attica, where his army besieged castles and devastated the country. He later invaded Boeotia, sought to make Platea revolt, and besieged the city without taking it.

Archidamus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Archidamus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Archidamus is a Spartan king described in conflicting accounts as either grandson of Leotychides or son of Agesilaus. One reign features invasions of Attica, the capture of Platæa, and succession by Agis; another centers on aiding the Phocians after their seizure of Delphi’s temple while preventing Delphi’s destruction. He dies fighting barbarians near Tarentum.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

Archidamus, grandson of Leotychides, succeeded to the Spartan kingship after Leotychides went into exile. He repeatedly invaded and ravaged Attica and captured Platæa after a siege; his death was followed by the accession of Agis rather than Agesilaus.

CHAPTER X.

Archidamus, the son of Agesilaus, reigned after him. During his reign the Phocians seized the temple of Apollo at Delphi, and Archidamus assisted them despite their sacrilegious attack. He successfully prevented the Phocians from killing all the young men at Delphi, enslaving the women and children, and razing the city.
