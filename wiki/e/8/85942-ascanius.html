Ascanius in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Ascanius is a young Trojan, royal prince, and repeatedly identified son and heir of Aeneas, whose future carries the Ausonian scepter, a state, Rome after thirty years, and the beginning of the Julian line. He begins amid Troy’s fall: Aeneas protects and leads him by the hand as he escapes with Aeneas and Creusa. A flame around his head and a heavenly sign mark him with omens. He later receives Andromache’s gifts because he resembles Astyanax in appearance and bearing. His youth is intertwined with the Trojans’ journey and future. Aeneas sends for him with gifts for Dido, though Venus puts him to sleep and substitutes Cupid in his form. He recognizes the cakes used as trenchers as an omen, leads Trojan youths in hunting, and rides Dido’s Tyrian horse in the riding game that later tradition says he teaches to the Latins. When the fleet is attacked by fire, he rides to the shore, rebukes the matrons, and helps end the attack. His hunting shot, guided by Alecto, wounds a household stag and ignites conflict between countrymen and Trojans. During the war, he sits in council, supports Nisus and Euryalus with promises of rewards and care for Euryalus’s mother, and defends the camp in Aeneas’s absence. In his first martial combat he kills Numanus after invoking Jupiter, but Apollo orders him to stop because he is too young. Venus then asks Jupiter to preserve him from danger.

Narrative, section by section

10 sections; each heading opens the text

To whom, with sorrow streaming from his eyes,

Ascanius is the boy whom Aeneas sends for from the ships with precious gifts for Dido. Venus places him in a pleasing sleep, carries him away to her blissful groves, and substitutes Cupid in his form.

“Then, not before, I felt my curdled blood

Ascanius escapes Troy with Aeneas and Creusa. A lambent flame appears around his head, and a subsequent thunderous celestial sign confirms the omen. Aeneas leads him by the hand during the flight.

“The prophet first with sacrifice adores

Ascanius receives gifts from Andromache, including a robe and a Phrygian vest, because his features and form remind her of Astyanax.

BOOK IV

Ascanius leads the Trojan youths during the hunt and eagerly rides through the valley in pursuit of game. Jupiter's command reminds Aeneas that Ascanius is destined to receive the Ausonian scepter and state.

The race thus ended, and rewards bestow’d,

Ascanius leads the riding game on a fiery Tyrian steed given by Queen Dido. He teaches the military game to the Latins, who pass it to succeeding generations.

Thus far the sacred sports they celebrate:

Ascanius sees the fleet burning while leading young warriors and rides quickly to the shore. He rebukes the matrons, reveals himself, and helps cause them to abandon their attack.

Thus having said, she sinks beneath the ground,

Ascanius hunts with his youthful companions and shoots at the household stag. Guided by Alecto, his arrow pierces the stag's bowels and sets off the violent conflict between the countrymen and the Trojans.

BOOK IX

Ascanius admits Nisus and Euryalus to the council and approves their plan to reach Aeneas. He promises generous rewards and vows that Euryalus’s mother will receive the benefits intended for Euryalus if he dies.

Now rose the ruddy morn from Tithon’s bed,

Ascanius kills Numanus with his first shot in martial combat after Numanus insults the Trojans. He invokes Jupiter before shooting and promises an annual offering, but Apollo later orders him to cease fighting because he is too young.

BOOK X

Ascanius is defending the Trojan camp while Aeneas is away and is hard pressed by the insulting enemy forces. Venus asks Jupiter to free him from danger and let him live without a crown, while the arriving Arcadian and Etrurian forces advance to support him.

Ascanius in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Ascanius, also called Iulus, son of Æneas, escapes Troy with Æneas and Anchises, travels to Delos, receives parting gifts from Anius, and rules Alba and the Latin state.
