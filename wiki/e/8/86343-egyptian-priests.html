Egyptian priests

group · 1 work · 10 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: high priest, order of the priests, orders of the priests, priests, priests of Egypt, sacred scribes, the chief priest, the priests, those priests that have the charge of the business

Abstract written by a model

Egyptian priests are a privileged religious body in Egypt, entrusted with sacred animals, rites, records, learning, and counsel to the reigning king. They keep the manner of Osiris's death secret, receive responsibility for his burial and worship, preserve his rites, and mourn him through consecrated animals, including sacred bulls. They claim a long religious chronology from Osiris and Isis, or from the earliest divine reigns and the reign of Sol, to Alexander the Great, while rejecting the claim that Osiris was born at Thebes in Boeotia. Supported by a third of the country and later by revenues, they are exempt from public taxes and maintain families, servants, sacrifices, and services. They act as royal advisers, interpret sacrifices and astrology, preserve secret and sacred histories, teach youth arithmetic, geometry, sacred and ordinary learning, and observe the stars. They marry one wife. Their animal duties include seeking a new Apis after a funeral, leading him through the city of Nile to Memphis, and inheriting service to the goat god from fathers.

CHAP. 11.

The Egyptian priests initially keep the manner of Osiris's death secret in their registers. Isis assigns them the burial and worship of Osiris, gives them a third of the country for their support, and they preserve his rites and mourn him through the sacred animals.

From Osiris and Isis, to the reign of Alexander the Great, who

Egyptian priests reckon more than ten thousand years, or nearly twenty-three thousand lunar years, from Osiris and Isis or from the earliest divine reigns to Alexander's time. They reject the Greek account that Osiris was born at Thebes in Boeotia and attribute Greek gods, heroes, rites, and colonies to Egypt. They also preserve accounts of divine reigns, Egyptian chronology, and the origins of Egyptian religious customs.

CHAP. VI.

Egyptian priests were highly honored, exempt from public taxes, and supported by revenues that maintained their families, servants, sacrifices, and religious services. They advised the king, interpreted sacrifices and astrology, preserved sacred records, and educated their descendants in priestly duties.

And since now we are come to mention the laws^ we conceive it

Egyptian priests marry only one wife, unlike other Egyptians, who may have as many wives as they please. They teach the youth sacred and ordinary learning, including arithmetic and geometry, and observe the movements of the stars.

Tlie adoration and worshipping of beasts among the Egyptians,

Egyptian priests maintain secret explanations concerning the worship of the gods and oversee the sacred animals. The priests responsible for Apis find his successor, lead the young ox through the city of Nile, and transport him to Memphis; priests who serve the goat god inherit their office from their fathers and are initiated into its service.
