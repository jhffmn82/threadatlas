Dr. Jedd in Dandy Dick

Arthur W. Pinero · 1893

Dr. Jedd, also called Augustin and the Dean of St. Marvells, is a widowed father of Salome and Sheba, brother of Georgiana Tidman, and resident master of the Deanery. He is institutionally aligned with the Bishop of St. Marvells and concerned with the Minister Spire’s restoration. Though he has unpaid bills and no spare cash for his daughters’ dresses, he conditionally pledges one thousand pounds to the Restoration Fund if seven others match it. When seven wealthy donors do so, his obligation becomes pressing. Georgiana comes to live at the Deanery, embarrassing the dignified Dean through her racing language, manners, and association with Dandy Dick. He fears both her influence and the approaching races. After the Swan Inn burns, Georgiana seeks to house Dandy Dick at the Deanery; the Dean objects but cannot prevent Dandy Dick and Sir Tristram from staying. Financial pressure over the Spire leads him to let Georgiana place a fifty-pound bet on Dandy Dick through his long-serving butler, Edward Blore. Believing he is humanely treating a suffering horse’s chills, Dr. Jedd prepares and administers a veterinary bolus to Dandy Dick. It is taken as an attempt to give the horse an unfair advantage, and Blore identifies him as the supposed villain. Arrested in the Deanery stables for trespassing, handcuffed, and taken to the lock-up, he is also associated with accusations over Dandy Dick’s treatment and the Swan Inn fire. His disappearance alarms others; his family influence seems lost and his cherished dignity crushed. In the cell, Hannah recognizes and briefly frees him, but he refuses escape from conscience and concern for dignity. Noah recaptures him. Once identified as the Dean rather than an unknown intruder, he mildly accepts Noah’s error, withdraws any charge against the person found on his premises, and invites Noah and Hannah to dinner.

DANDY DICK. / THE FIRST ACT.

Dr. Jedd, the Dean of St. Marvells, learns that Salome and Sheba want money for their dresses and tells them that he has no spare cash. He explains that he has subscribed £1,000 to the restoration of the Minister Spire, conditional on seven other donors contributing the same amount, and announces that Georgiana Tidman will live with the family.

_GEORGIANA TIDMAN enters.

Augustin is shocked and embarrassed by Georgiana's manners, racing language, and public connection with horse racing. He hopes that the serene atmosphere of the Deanery will change her, but becomes increasingly disturbed when Sir Tristram and Georgiana discuss racing and Dandy Dick. He also worries about the effect of Georgiana's arrival before the races at St. Marvells.

THE SECOND ACT.

The Dean is distressed by Mr. Darbey and anxious about the newspaper's report on his thousand-pound offer to the Restoration Fund. He learns that seven wealthy people have each offered a thousand pounds, then becomes alarmed when the Swan Inn burns and Georgiana proposes housing Dandy Dick at the Deanery. He nearly behaves rudely toward Mr. Darbey and later chases after him to offer an explanation.

GEORGIANA and SIR TRISTRAM enter by the window.

The Dean objects to Dandy Dick and Sir Tristram remaining at the Deanery but is unable to prevent it. Facing bills connected with the threatened Spire, he is tempted by Georgiana's proposal to bet fifty pounds on Dandy Dick and gives Blore the money to place the wager. He then prepares a veterinary bolus for Dandy Dick, while believing he is acting humanely and not seeking to give the horse an unfair advantage.

Augustin Jedd in Dandy Dick

Arthur W. Pinero · 1893

Augustin Jedd is the Dean and Georgiana’s brother. He secretly directs Blore to back Dandy Dick with fifty pounds from the Restoration Fund, then deceives Sir Tristram and his daughters about his absence and the race wager. Detained by police for playing the three-card trick, he is rescued by Georgiana and Sir Tristram after being forcibly taken home from the Hill. He is horrified to discover that a bolus contained strychnine. Noah Topping also alleges that he is Hannah’s unknown lover and holds him in lawful custody before he is rescued, though Hatcham disputes that the man in the stable was the Dean.
