Mr. Latimer in Gallegher and Other Stories

Richard Harding Davis · 1890

Mr. Latimer, a young man, is Lewis L. Lockwood’s junior law-office partner, adopted charge, and ward, as well as a dancing acquaintance of Miss Catherwaight. He tells her that Lockwood’s stolen silver medal marked Lockwood’s boyhood friendship with Henry Burgoyne, recounting the friends’ history and later quarrel. He accompanies Lockwood to visit Miss Catherwaight, waits for Lockwood’s return, notes Lockwood’s unused hunting preparations, and agrees to manage the office while Lockwood and Burgoyne travel.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE CYNICAL MISS CATHERWAIGHT Miss Catherwaight's collection of orders and decorations and medals was her chief offence in the eyes of those of her dear friends who thought her clever but cynical.

Mr. Latimer is the junior partner in Lewis Lockwood's law office and a dancing acquaintance of Miss Catherwaight. He tells her that the silver medal belonged to Lockwood, had been stolen in a burglary, and commemorated Lockwood's boyhood friendship with Henry Burgoyne. He also recounts the friends' shared history and their later quarrel.

"} They had been sitting there some time, he with his hands folded on the evenin

Mr. Latimer accompanies Lockwood when Lockwood visits Miss Catherwaight and later waits at home for Lockwood's return. He explains that Lockwood has repeatedly prepared his gun for hunting but never gone, and he agrees to look after matters at the office while Lockwood and Burgoyne travel.
