REANO: Optimising Retrieval-Augmented Reader Models through Knowledge Graph Generation

document · 1 work · 0 quoted facts · 0 connections

Abstract written by a model

This document presents REANO, a retrieval-augmented generative reader for open-domain question answering (ODQA), where questions are answered using an external corpus such as Wikipedia. In a retriever-reader pipeline, a retriever such as DPR first obtains relevant passages, then a reader generates the answer. The work focuses on improving the reader, especially the Fusion-in-Decoder (FiD) approach, which separately encodes question-passage pairs and can overlook semantic relationships across passages. REANO generates a latent knowledge graph from retrieved passages and answers questions using the question, passages, and that graph. Its KG Generator recognizes entities and extracts knowledge triples. Intra-context relations are extracted with DocuNet, trained on REBEL, while inter-context relations among entities are retrieved through the Wikidata API. This passage-derived graph is intended to address missing information in existing knowledge graphs and support multi-hop questions. REANO's Answer Predictor uses a graph neural network to score and select the most relevant intra-context and inter-context triples. The top-K triples are concatenated as an additional passage for a T5-based answer generator derived from FiD. Training is decoupled: the KG generator uses distant supervision, while the answer predictor uses answer-entity alignment alongside answer generation and entity-relevance training. Experiments compare REANO with DPR, FiD, FiDO, RAG-Seq., and knowledge-graph-enhanced readers on Natural Questions, TriviaQA, EntityQuestions, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and MuSiQue. REANO achieves the best reported Exact Match scores on all five: 50.4, 69.1, 71.0, 77.1, and 31.8, respectively. Its gains over second-best models range from 0.8 to 2.7 percentage points, with the largest gain on EntityQuestions and an average improvement of 1.8%. Ablations find that intra- and inter-context triples, REM, and the GNN contribute to results, while selected triples can help preserve performance when fewer passages are used. A case study illustrates useful generated triples, including that Muzaffargarh Railway Station and Raisan Railway Station are located in Pakistan. Limitations include evaluation only with T5, not BART or decoder-only models, and use of a frozen retriever without joint optimization.

Abstract

Open domain question answering (ODQA) uses knowledge from an external corpus to answer questions. The Fusion-in-Decoder (FiD) retrieval-augmented reader model independently encodes passages, overlooking semantic relationships between them. REANO enhances this model with a knowledge graph generator that generates knowledge graphs from passages and an answer predictor that generates answers from the passages and generated knowledge graphs. Across five ODQA datasets, REANO improves exact match scores by up to 2.7% on the EntityQuestion dataset, with an average improvement of 1.8%.

The unit concerns retrieval-augmented models for open-domain question answering (ODQA), focusing on improving the reader model in a retriever-reader pipeline. Each question is paired with an answer and a set of passages obtained by a retriever such as DPR. It describes Fusion-in-Decoder (FiD), a generative reader based on an encoder-decoder T5 model. FiD encodes each question-passage pair separately, concatenates the resulting passage representations, and provides them to the decoder to generate the answer.

This unit introduces REANO, a framework that generates answers from a question, relevant passages, and a latent knowledge graph. REANO models answer generation with a KG Generator, which produces knowledge triples from passages, and an Answer Predictor, which uses the question, passages, and generated knowledge graph. The KG Generator performs entity recognition and relation extraction, with relation extraction divided into intra-context and inter-context components. Intra-context relation extraction uses DocuNet, while inter-context relation extraction uses the Wikidata API to retrieve relations among entities.

This unit describes REANO's answer predictor, which uses passages and a knowledge graph to generate an answer. A graph neural network identifies relevant triples from intra-context and inter-context relation triples, and the top-K relevant triples are concatenated into an additional passage for T5 answer generation. The unit also explains REANO's decoupled training strategies, including distant supervision for the KG generator and answer-entity alignment for training the answer predictor. DocuNet is trained on REBEL for intra-context relation extraction, while the T5 model and graph neural network are trained for answer generation and entity relevance prediction.

This unit describes experiments evaluating REANO for open-domain question answering on Natural Questions, TriviaQA, EntityQuestions, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and MuSiQue. The experiments compare REANO with DPR, FiD, FiDO, RAG-Seq., and several knowledge-graph-enhanced readers using Exact Match. REANO achieves the best performance on all five datasets, with scores of 50.4 on Natural Questions, 69.1 on TriviaQA, 71.0 on EntityQuestions, 77.1 on 2WikiMultiHopQA, and 31.8 on MuSiQue. The reported gains over the second-best models are 0.8, 1.7, 2.7, 2.5, and 1.4 percentage points, respectively.

opening

The unit evaluates REANO through ablation studies, passage-reduction experiments, hyperparameter tests, and a case study on knowledge-triple generation. REANO performs better when it uses both inter-context and intra-context triples, as well as its REM and GNN modules, on EQ, 2WQA, and MuSiQue. On NQ and TQA, triples selected from all 50 passages help maintain performance when fewer passages are provided. The case study shows that REANO generates and selects useful triples for answering questions in 2WQA.

The unit presents a case study showing that REANO generates meaningful knowledge triples from passages and uses its GNN module to identify triples useful for answering questions. It gives the example of identifying that Muzaffargarh Railway Station and Raisan Railway Station are both located in Pakistan. The related work section reviews retrieval-augmented models for ODQA, including efforts to improve retrievers, readers, and joint optimization. It also contrasts KG-Enhanced Retrieval-Augmented Models for ODQA that use existing knowledge triples with REANO, which generates triples from passages and selects relevant ones with a GNN.

6

The paper proposes REANO for the ODQA task. REANO generates knowledge graphs from passages and uses an answer predictor based on FiD to generate answers from both passages and the generated knowledge graphs. A GNN model helps identify and select knowledge triples relevant to questions. Experiments on five ODQA datasets show that REANO improves EM score by up to 2.7% compared with state-of-the-art baselines.

Limitations

This unit concerns REANO, a knowledge graph generation module intended to improve retrieval-augmented readers. Its effectiveness was verified using an encoder-decoder T5 model, while its performance with BART and decoder-only generative models remains unexplored. The experiments used a frozen retriever, so the effect of retriever effectiveness and joint optimization of the retriever with REANO were not investigated and are deferred to future work.
