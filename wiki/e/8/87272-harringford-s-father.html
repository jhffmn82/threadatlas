Harringford's father

Abstract written by a model

Harringford's father rejects Young Harringford after gambling disgraces the family name, but later recognizes his error. Through Walters, he invites Cecil home, offering reconciliation and declaring him dearer than his other sons. He depends on Cecil to continue the family line and hopes his return will make him happy.

"THERE WERE NINETY AND NINE" Young Harringford, or the "Goodwood Plunger," as he was perhaps better known at that time, had come to Monte Carlo in a very different spirit and in a very different state of mind from any in which he had ever visited the place before.

Harringford’s father had rejected him for bringing the family name into disgrace through gambling, but later recognized his own error and invited Cecil to return home. He writes that Cecil is dearer to him than his other sons and that he cannot be at rest unless Cecil comes back.
