Aeneas’s voyage in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Aeneas’s voyage is the seven-year journey of Trojan exiles after their expulsion from Troy, intended for Italy. It is defined by repeated sea dangers, storms, and lost or scattered ships: Juno drives the surviving fleet to the Libyan coast; from Helenus’s court it passes Sicily, Charybdis, Mount Etna, and the Cyclopes, while Anchises dies. After repairs, favorable winds and Neptune’s calm, Sleep casts Palinurus overboard, and Aeneas steers past the Sirens’ cliffs.

BOOK I

The Trojan voyage has lasted seven years after the departure from Troy and has carried the exiles through repeated dangers. The fleet is now sailing toward Italy when Juno’s storm forces the surviving ships to the Libyan coast.

Aeneas’s voyage begins after the Trojans are expelled from Troy and is marked by varied seas, storms, and the loss or scattering of ships. Its intended destination is Italy, but the surviving fleet is driven to the Libyan coast.

The Trojan voyage proceeds from Helenus’s court through the waters around Sicily and toward Italy. The fleet faces storms, Charybdis, Mount Etna, the Cyclopes, and the loss of Anchises before Aeneas completes this part of his account.

Thus far the sacred sports they celebrate:

Aeneas’s voyage resumes after the fleet is repaired and favorable winds arise. Neptune calms the sea, but Sleep causes Palinurus to fall overboard; Aeneas then steers the fleet past the dangerous Sirens’ cliffs.
