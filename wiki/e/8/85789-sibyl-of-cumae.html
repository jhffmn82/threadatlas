Sibyl of Cumae in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

The Sibyl of Cumae, also named Deiphobe, is a mad, prophetic priestess of Apollo who lives in a cave near the temple. Possessed by Apollo, she writes prophecies and fates on leaves and foretells Italy’s wars and hardships. She instructs Aeneas to obtain the golden bough and bury Misenus, then leads him into the Underworld. Carrying the bough and a sop for Cerberus, she explains its regions, laws, inhabitants, and punishments, accompanies Aeneas through Elysian fields, and seeks Anchises with Musaeus.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK VI

The Sibyl of Cumae lives in a cave near the temple and becomes possessed by Apollo while delivering prophecy. She foretells wars and hardships in Italy, explains that Aeneas must obtain the golden bough and bury Misenus, and then leads Aeneas toward the underworld.

Ye realms, yet unreveal’d to human sight,

The Sibyl guides Aeneas through the Underworld and explains its regions, inhabitants, laws, and punishments. She carries the golden bough, prepares the sop that subdues Cerberus, and fixes the bough before Pluto’s palace.

Where long extended plains of pleasure lay:

The Sibyl accompanies Aeneas through the Elysian fields and asks the happy souls where Anchises can be found. She is led with Aeneas by Musaeus and Anchises through the meadows and the gathering shades.

Sibyl in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Sibyl is a prophetess of extraordinary longevity who, after rejecting Apollo’s offers, remains unmarried. She guides Aeneas through Avernus to visit Anchises and explains that Apollo granted long life without lasting youth; as vigor fades, she endures crazy old age, retaining only her prophetic voice.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE III. [XIV.101-153]

The Cumæan Sibyl guides Aeneas through Avernus so that he can visit Anchises. She reveals that Apollo granted her an extremely long life, but because she failed to request lasting youth, she must endure prolonged old age.

the Sibyl in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The Sibyl is a mortal prophetic guide who guards a cave linked to a temple and grove sacred to Apollo and Diana. Apollo granted her a thousand-year lifespan without youth; after seven hundred years, she leads Aeneas through the dead, gains Charon’s passage, and writes fates on leaves that wind can scatter beyond recovery.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XXV

The Sibyl guides Aeneas through the infernal regions, restrains him from attacking apparitions, explains the laws governing the dead, and uses a golden branch to gain Charon's passage. She later says that she is mortal, has lived seven hundred years, and obtained prolonged life from Apollo without preserving her youth. She writes prophecies on leaves, but their disorder by the wind makes them irretrievably lost.

is_a the Sibyl [the most celebrated Sibyl] The Cumaean Sibyl is identified as the most celebrated of the various Sibyls described by Ovid and Virgil.
