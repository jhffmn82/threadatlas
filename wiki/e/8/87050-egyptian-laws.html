Abstract written by a model

Egyptian laws are remarkable ancient institutions, admired by Grecians especially by learned men, and said to have preserved Egyptian prosperity and order. They regulate kings, royal schedules, diet, sacrifices, occupations, taxation, courts, crimes, military conduct, commerce, theft, marriage, education, medicine, lending, and repayment. They require livelihood reports, protect debtors from imprisonment, and use rewards and punishments to promote temperance, virtue, honesty, and obedience. They also govern religious observance, burial, and the dead. The account ends by attributing written laws to Menes, who claimed to receive them from Mercury.

CHAP. VI.

Egyptian laws and customs regulated the conduct of kings, royal schedules, diet, sacrifices, social occupations, taxation, and judicial proceedings. They were regarded by the Egyptians as ancient institutions that had helped preserve prosperity and order.

And since now we are come to mention the laws^ we conceive it

Egyptian laws concern capital crimes, military conduct, commerce, theft, marriage, education, and medicine. They require written reports of livelihood, regulate lending and repayment, protect debtors from imprisonment, and prescribe punishments intended to deter wrongdoing.

CHAP, VII.

Egyptian laws are presented as remarkable because they regulate religious observance, diet, burial, and the treatment of the dead. Their purpose is said to include teaching temperance, rewarding virtue, punishing wrongdoing, and encouraging honest dealings.

• And now it is necessary for us to speak of the legislators of Egypt^

Written laws were first instituted in Egypt, according to the account, by Menes, who pretended to have received them from Mercury. The device of attributing laws to a god or divine being is said to have encouraged people to obey them.
