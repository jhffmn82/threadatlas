Leotychides the Lacedaemonian in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Leotychides, king of Sparta for twenty-two years, co-commanded the Greek fleet to Mycale, encouraged revolt, defeated and pursued Persians, and gained Ionian and Eolian confederates.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAP. IV.

Leotychides the Lacedaemonian commanded the Greek fleet with Xantippus the Athenian. He led the fleet to Mycale, used a herald to encourage revolt among the Greeks serving the Persians, directed the Greek army in battle, pursued the defeated Persians, and later received the Ionians and Eolians as confederates.

Leotychides in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Leotychides became king after Demaratus, fought at Mycale and in Thessaly, accepted payment from the Aleuadæ, was impeached, exiled himself to Tegea, and was succeeded by Archidamus.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

Leotychides became king in place of Demaratus and fought with the Athenians at Mycale and against the Aleuadæ in Thessaly. He allowed the Aleuadæ to buy him off, was impeached at Sparta, and went voluntarily into exile at Tegea; his grandson Archidamus succeeded him.
