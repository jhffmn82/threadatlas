Melampus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Melampus is a soothsayer and son of Amythaon, brother of Bias. Given prophetic powers by snakes, he learns animals’ language. He accepts a year’s imprisonment to win Phylacus’s kine for Bias, cures Iphiclus through prophecy and birds, brings the kine for Pero, later heals Argive women and receives part of Argos.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

IV.—MELAMPUS AND THE KINE ΟΕ PHYLACUS

He is named in the title “Melampus and the kine of Phylacus.”

IV.—MELAMPUS AND THE KINE OF PHYLACUS

Melampus undertook to obtain the kine of Phylacus for his brother Bias, although he knew that he would be kept prisoner for a year. After being captured, he used his prophetic knowledge and the birds' testimony to cure Iphiclus, received the kine, and brought them to Pylus as Pero's bridal gift.

Melampus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Melampus is an excellent soothsayer and seer, son of Amythaon and Idmene, who gains understanding of birds and divination through Apollo, birds, and snakes that purge his ears. Imprisoned after stealing Phylacus’s kine, he learns from a vulture how Iphiclus may have children and secures the kine for Bias’s marriage to Pero. He devises cures with drugs and purifications, heals Proetus’s daughters and the mad women of Argos, gains part of the sovereignty, and settles there with Bias.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Now Tyro, daughter of Salmoneus and Alcidice,

Melampus learned to understand the voices of birds after raising young snakes whose tongues purged his ears while he slept, and he became an excellent soothsayer. He obtained the kine of Phylacus for his brother Bias after being detected stealing them and kept in bondage for a year. He later healed the women of Argos whom Dionysus had driven mad and settled there with Bias.

THE LIBRARY, 11. 1. 5—u. 1

Melampus, son of Amythaon and Idmene, was a seer who was the first to devise a cure by drugs and purifications. He agreed to cure Proetus's daughters in exchange for a third of the sovereignty and later pursued the women to Sicyon, where the surviving daughters were purified and recovered their wits.
