Theramenes

Abstract written by a model

Theramenes was an Athenian commander and political figure described as sober, prudent, influential, just, and moderate. He served as a common soldier before commanding fleets and part of the right wing at Arginusae. He tried unsuccessfully to prevent the Boeotians from blocking the Euripus, restored democracy at Paros, fought with Chares and Thrasybulus at Claros, besieged Chalcedon, arranged its tribute, and joined Alcibiades against Byzantium. In Athens he helped abolish the four hundred, supported democracy, and advised recalling Alcibiades. After Arginusae he defended himself over the unburied dead. Though selected among the Thirty, he opposed both their establishment and their abuses, and was condemned and executed.

CHAP. 111.

Theramenes was described as sober, prudent, and influential in Athens. He helped abolish the authority of the four hundred, supported the restoration of democracy, and advised the recall of Alcibiades.

CHAP. V.

Theramenes attempted to stop the Boeotians from blocking the Euripus but withdrew when overpowered. He later helped restore democracy at Paros and, at Claros, joined Chares's infantry to reinforce Thrasybulus and defeat the enemy on land.

Theramenes was left with fifty ships to besiege Chalcedon and later agreed with the Chalcedonians that they would pay their former tribute. He then moved against Byzantium and joined Alcibiades in attacking the city.

CHAP. XIV.

Theramenes commanded part of the Athenian right wing, having previously been a common soldier. He and Thrasybulus came to Athens before the other officers and sent letters blaming themselves for the failure to bury the dead; after he cleared himself, the people turned their anger against the other commanders.

DIODORUS SICULUS. / BOOK XIV.

Theramenes opposed the establishment of the Thirty and argued that it violated the peace terms requiring Athens to be governed by its own laws. Although he was chosen as one of the Thirty because of his reputation for justice and moderation, he later resisted their abuses and was condemned and executed.
