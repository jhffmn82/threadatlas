lions

animal · 3 works · 5 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: These lions, lion, the tawny lions
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, lions left deserts for watered champaign country and the Rizophages’ land, where they sought shelter, pasture, and prey and tore apart unarmed Rizophages until swarms of gnats drove them away.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, lions were fierce wild beasts avoided by Venus, warned against by Venus to Adonis, and became the creatures drawing Cybele’s chariot after Hippomenes and Atalanta were transformed.
  • In Description of Greece, lions lived in mountainous Thrace inside the river Nestus, prowled near Mount Olympus, attacked Xerxes’ army, killed grain-carrying camels, and were later hunted by Polydamas.

lions in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Lions lived in mountainous Thrace inside the river Nestus, sometimes prowled near Mount Olympus, attacked Xerxes’ army and killed grain-carrying camels; later Polydamas slew a large lion.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER V.

Lions lived in the mountainous part of Thrace inside the river Nestus and sometimes prowled near Mount Olympus. They once attacked the army of Xerxes and killed many of the camels carrying grain, and Polydamas later slew a large lion on Mount Olympus.
