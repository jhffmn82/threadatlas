Gelon

person · 1 work · 12 quoted facts · 7 connections
Also called: GeloD, Geloa, his

Abstract written by a model

Gelon, ruler of Sicily and ruler of Syracuse, led fifty thousand foot and five thousand horse to relieve Himera. Through raids, disguised horsemen, and battle, he killed Amilcar, burned the Carthaginian fleet, and defeated the Carthaginians. Praised across Sicily, he made peace with Carthage, built two temples from the spoils, and was called benefactor, king, and deliverer of his country. He governed with justice, equity, moderation, and kindness, having enfranchised more than ten thousand mercenaries. After six years, Hiero, his eldest brother and heir, succeeded him; his family governed for three descents.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. U.

Gelon marched from Syracuse to relieve Himera with fifty thousand foot soldiers and five thousand horse. He used cavalry raids and a stratagem involving disguised horsemen to kill Amilcar and burn the Carthaginian fleet, then defeated the Carthaginian army. He was praised throughout Sicily, made peace with Carthage, built temples from the spoils, and was proclaimed benefactor, king, and deliverer of his country by the Syracusans.

CHAP. XIII.

Gelon was remembered as a brave and disciplined ruler who defeated the Carthaginians and governed the Sicilians with moderation and kindness. His reputation restrained revolts during the harsher rule of his brother Hiero.

CHAP. XIX.

Gelon had enfranchised more than ten thousand mercenaries, over seven thousand of whom remained when the new government excluded them from political honors.
