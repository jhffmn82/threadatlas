Naupactus

place · 4 works · 6 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Naapaetus, Naupactiis, it

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Naupactus was a city inhabited by Messenians and became their colony and defensive stronghold. In The Library, Naupactus was the place in Locris where Temenus built ships for the expedition and where the army suffered disaster. In Description of Greece, Naupactus was given by the Athenians to the Messenians who surrendered at Ithome, serving as their base against the Peloponnese until its loss scattered them. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Naupactus was a town near Œanthea, traditionally named for the fleet built there by the Dorians and sons of Aristomachus, and later held by the Locrians, Athenians, Messenians, and Locrians again.

Naupactus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · about AD 155 to 175 · the work’s page · read it where Naupactus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Naupactus was a town near Œanthea, named in tradition for the fleet built there by the Dorians and sons of Aristomachus to cross to the Peloponnese. It passed from the Locrians to the Athenians, then the Messenians, and back to the Locrians after the Lacedæmonians expelled the Messenians. It had sanctuaries of Poseidon, Artemis, Aphrodite, and ruined Æsculapius, and Phalysius was cured there of blindness.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXVIII.

Naupactus lay near Œanthea and was said to have been named because the Dorians and the sons of Aristomachus built a fleet there to cross to the Peloponnese. The town passed from the Locrians to the Athenians, then to the Messenians, and finally back to the Locrians after the Lacedæmonians expelled the Messenians. It contained temples and statues of Poseidon, Artemis, and Aphrodite, ruins of a temple of Æsculapius, and was the place where Phalysius was cured of blindness after receiving a sealed letter from the god of Epidaurus through Anyte.
