Abstract written by a model

A cheap, tarnished silver heart medal, engraved with clasped hands and inscribed “From Henry Burgoyne to his beloved friend Lewis L. Lockwood” and “Through prosperity and adversity,” belongs to Lewis L. Lockwood as a keepsake. Stolen from his safe during a burglary, it is pawned and appears in the Bowery, where Miss Catherwaight buys or finds it in an old curiosity shop. After Mr. Latimer tells her its painful history, she refuses to keep or exhibit it and returns it to Lockwood. Though he first sees it as a reminder of the past, he keeps it at her urging.

The silver heart medal is a cheap, tarnished object engraved with clasped hands and the words "From Henry Burgoyne to his beloved friend Lewis L. Lockwood" and "Through prosperity and adversity." Miss Catherwaight buys it from a Bowery pawnbroker, then learns from Mr. Latimer that it had been stolen from Lockwood's safe during a burglary. She decides she cannot keep or exhibit it after learning the painful story attached to it.
