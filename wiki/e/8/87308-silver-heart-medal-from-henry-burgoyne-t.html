THE CYNICAL MISS CATHERWAIGHT Miss Catherwaight's collection of orders and decorations and medals was her chief offence in the eyes of those of her dear friends who thought her clever but cynical.

The silver heart medal is a cheap, tarnished object engraved with clasped hands and the words "From Henry Burgoyne to his beloved friend Lewis L. Lockwood" and "Through prosperity and adversity." Miss Catherwaight buys it from a Bowery pawnbroker, then learns from Mr. Latimer that it had been stolen from Lockwood's safe during a burglary. She decides she cannot keep or exhibit it after learning the painful story attached to it.

The medal belonged to Lockwood and had been stolen and pawned before Miss Catherwaight found it in an old curiosity shop and returned it. Lockwood initially considers it a reminder of the past, but keeps it after Miss Catherwaight urges him to do so.
