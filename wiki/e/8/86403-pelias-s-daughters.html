Pelias's daughters

group · 4 works · 6 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Pelias*s daughters, The people of Mantinea say they dwelt here, damsels, daughters, daughters of Pelias, his daughters, the daughters of Pelias, the sisters, the young ladies

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Pelias's daughters are deceived by Medea, kill Pelias while seeking to restore his youth, and lament when Jason and the Argonauts enter and are spared. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the daughters of Pelias are deceived by Medea's false promises of restoring his youth; after witnessing a ram's transformation, they stab their sleeping father and cause his death. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the daughters of Pelias are named Asteropea and Antinoe in Mico's inscriptions, are deceived by Medea into helping cut up and boil their father in hope of making him young again, and later live and die in Arcadia, where they receive tombs. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the daughters of Pelias, loving their father, are deceived by Medea's attempted rejuvenation and, after helping attack him, Medea kills him.

Pelias having thus extinguished Jason's whole family, in a short

Pelias’s daughters were persuaded by Medea to assist in restoring their father’s youth. They helped prepare the supposed treatment, killed Pelias at Medea’s direction, and later lamented when Jason and the Argonauts entered the palace; Jason spared and honorably treated them.

Abstract written by a model

The daughters of Pelias, affectionate toward their father, are deceived by Medea’s false promises of restoring his youth; after witnessing a ram’s transformation, they stab their sleeping father and cause his death.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

opening

The daughters of Pelias believe Medea’s claim that old age can be removed after seeing her transform an aged ram into a lamb. At Medea’s urging, they stab their sleeping father, turning away their eyes because they cannot bear to see their own blows.

MEDEA AND AESON

The daughters of Pelias love their father and ask Medea to perform the same rejuvenation for him. After seeing an old sheep become a lamb in the caldron, they help attack Pelias, but Medea has deceived them and kills him.
