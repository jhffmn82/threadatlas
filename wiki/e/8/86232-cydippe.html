Cydippe in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Cydippe is a maiden and virgin of marriageable age whose future is governed by an oath she unknowingly makes to Acontius. While traveling to Delos, she reads aloud the inscription on his apple without understanding its trap, thereby swearing before Diana to marry him. Although she is also intended for another husband in a marriage desired by her parents, she becomes bound to Acontius and fears Diana’s judgment if she breaks the pledge. Whenever marriage preparations arise, she suffers recurring illness—languor, wasting, fever, and weakness—and Hymenaeus flees her wedding rites. At first she protests that Acontius deceived an inexperienced girl and gained no glory by doing so; she writes to him pale and barely able to finish her letter. Yet she blushes at the oath, confesses that she wants to join him, rejects the intended husband, and tells her mother the circumstances. Her nurse protects her by concealing correspondence and signaling visitors. Her course moves from unwitting entrapment and illness toward acknowledging the pledge and accepting Acontius as husband.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

XX

Cydippe is addressed as the maiden who read Acontius's inscription aloud and thereby pledged herself to him. She blushes when reminded of the oath, repeatedly falls ill around the time of marriage, and is urged to keep faith with Acontius. Acontius praises her beauty and imagines her both punishing him in anger and eventually accepting his devoted service.

nam quod habes et tu gemini verba altera pacti, 155

Cydippe is ill and under suspicion from Diana because she has not fulfilled the oath written on the apple. Acontius urges her to tell her mother the circumstances of their meeting and to accept him as her husband.

XXI

Cydippe writes to Acontius while weak, pale, and scarcely able to raise herself or complete the letter. She recalls being deceived by his written oath and protests that he gained no true glory by tricking an inexperienced girl.

Cum tamen haec dixi, cum me tibi firma negavi,

The speaker has become ill whenever preparations for marriage are made and has seen Hymenaeus flee from her wedding ceremonies three times. She rejects her intended husband, confesses her feelings to Acontius, admits that she wants to join him, and tells her mother about the oath.
