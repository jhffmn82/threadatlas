Ephorus

person · 1 work · 4 quoted facts · 0 connections
Also called: Ephortis^, Ephorus the Cumaean, Ephorus the Cumiean

Abstract written by a model

Ephorus, a historian and scholar of Isocrates, begins history with the return of the Heraclides, passing over earlier ages. He also explains Egypt’s flooding by subterranean water in loose soil, though this Nile theory is rejected as inadequate.

Ephorus is in one work on this site, The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Ephorus says that Egypt is raised from the river and contains loose, spongy soil with cavities holding water, which issues out in summer and raises the Nile. His explanation is rejected because the Nile is already high before reaching Egypt and because such cavities could not contain enough water to cause the inundation.
