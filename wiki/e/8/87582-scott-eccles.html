Scott Eccles in His Last Bow

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1908-04-17

Scott Eccles is a sociable, conventionally respectable English bachelor living at Popham House, Lee. Aloysius Garcia befriends him and invites him for an overnight stay at Wisteria Lodge. Eccles sends Sherlock Holmes a telegram seeking advice, then reports that he awoke to find Garcia and the entire household gone. He denies knowledge of Garcia’s fate, agrees to make a formal police statement, and is viewed by Holmes as the impressive witness and possible alibi Garcia intended him to be.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

The Adventure of Wisteria Lodge / 1. The Singular Experience of Mr. John Scott Eccles

John Scott Eccles tells Sherlock Holmes and the inspectors that he stayed overnight at Wisteria Lodge with Aloysius Garcia, but awoke to find Garcia and the entire household gone. He denies knowing anything about Garcia's fate and asks Holmes to investigate, while agreeing to give the police a formal statement.

We sat for some time in silence after our visitors had left. Holmes

Scott Eccles is described as a conventionally respectable Englishman whom Garcia quickly befriended and brought to Wisteria Lodge. Holmes believes Garcia intended Eccles to serve as an impressive witness and possible alibi.
