Abstract written by a model

Acontius’s letter is a second stratagem and evidence of his continued pursuit of Cydippe. It wounds the speaker from afar and contributes to her illness. Asking to be read so her illness may depart, it defends deception, invokes an oath, threatens further action, pleads for acceptance, and is answered by her confession that she wants union with him.

Acontius's letter in Heroides and Amores

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

XX

Acontius's letter is presented as a second stratagem intended to persuade Cydippe and as evidence of his continued pursuit. He asks her to read it through so that her illness may depart and uses it to defend his deception, invoke her oath, threaten further action, and plead for her acceptance. He also imagines the letter as a written crime, though he argues that it is less violent than the force used by others to seize women.

Cum tamen haec dixi, cum me tibi firma negavi,

Acontius’s letter wounds the speaker from afar like a dart and contributes to her illness. She nevertheless uses her own letter to confess her feelings and communicate that she wants to be united with him.
