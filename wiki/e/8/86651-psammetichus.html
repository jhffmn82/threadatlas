Abstract written by a model

Psammetichus is an Egyptian king. Beginning as one of twelve rulers of coastal Egypt, he grows wealthy through Phoenician and Greek trade, defeats the other kings with mercenaries from Arabia, Caria, and Ionia, rules all Egypt, promotes foreign ties, allies with Athens and other Greeks, and teaches his son Greek learning. Later, despite benefits from Tamos, he murders Tamos, his children, and attendants from covetousness.

Psammetichus is in one work on this site, The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

CHAP. V.

Psammetichus, one of the twelve kings and ruler of a coastal province, grew wealthy through trade with Phoenicians and Greeks. He defeated the other kings near Moniemphis with hired soldiers from Arabia, Caria, and Ionia, gained all Egypt, encouraged foreign trade, allied with Athens and other Greeks, and had his son taught Greek learning.

CHAP. VI.

Psammetichus, king of Egypt and a descendant of the ancient Psammetichus, had previously received benefits from Tamos. He nevertheless murdered Tamos, his children, and his attendants out of covetousness.
