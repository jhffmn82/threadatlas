Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Asopus dwells at Phlius, marries Medon, and fathers two sons and twelve daughters, including Aegina, Salamis, and Sinope. In The Library, Asopus is a river god whose parentage is variously given; he marries Metope, fathers several children including Aegina, and pursues Zeus after Zeus carries Aegina away. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the Asopus is the place from which participating communities drive their wagons to the top of Mount Cithæron.

Asopus in The Library

Apollodorus · AD 1 to 200 · the work’s page · read it where Asopus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Asopus is a river god whose parentage is given variously as Ocean and Tethys, Poseidon and Pero, or Zeus and Eurynome. He marries Metope and fathers Ismenus, Pelagon or Pelasgus, and twenty daughters, including Aegina. After Zeus carries Aegina away, Asopus pursues him to Corinth, but Zeus drives him back to his own streams with thunderbolts.
