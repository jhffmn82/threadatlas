Italy in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Italy is the promised, fruitful land and future homeland toward which Aeneas, the Trojans, and their voyage are directed. Foreordained as their settlement, it is destined to be ruled by Aeneas’s race, whose reign and fame will extend from it. The journey toward it is prolonged: the Trojans search for seven years, first glimpse its hills and plains, land only briefly, continue along its coast, and are delayed by storm and diversion to Sicily. Prophecy makes clear that arrival will not mean peace: Aeneas is to wage war, impose laws, build cities, and establish the Trojan succession, while hostile forces, bloodshed, and a new Achilles await. Once Aeneas arrives with his banished gods and forces, Italy becomes the contested setting of conflict between Trojan newcomers and Latian peoples. Juno seeks to expel the Trojans from it; its peace fails as cities arm and alliances form. Though its lawful possession is disputed, fate decrees that Aeneas’s race will reign there.

Italy in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Italy is a region containing Lucania and the country of the Samnites. Its record begins with movements from it by Pythagoras and Clinlias, and with a mixed multitude gathering in Lucania, raiding before gaining discipline and conquering cities. It then follows Roman dealings in Italy: consuls entered with an army during conflict with the Samnites, and affairs included Romans, Etruscans, Samnites, Tarentines, Lucanians, Cleonymus, and the works of Appius Claudius. Hannibal brought its greatest recorded devastation: after a long stay in Campania, his army weakened through luxury, while Italy was wasted and harassed for seventeen years, cities were burned, and the country was almost depopulated. Later it suffered wandering, poverty, civil war, slaughter, and destruction after conflicts involving Marius and Sylla. Finally, Italians divided Italy into two regions for consular authority, organized government on an ancient Roman model, and called their new city Italy.

Italy in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Italy is a region and land traversed by Hercules after Gaul and the Alps, including Liguria, Tuscany, Rome’s region, and maritime coasts; it contains the Tiber, where Hercules camped, and lies above the Tyrrhenian Sea. It is presented as a source of migration to Sicily and Liparus’s flight, and as the home of the Tyrrhenians, whose institutions and architecture influenced the Romans. Its fertile Campania flourished, while Tarentines founded Heraclea after expelling Siris’s inhabitants. Later, Dionysius sought power in Italy through wars involving Lucanians, Thurians, and Greek cities.

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Italy is the western land called Hesperia, sought by the Trojans as their future home and said to be Dardanus’s original homeland. After wandering, they reach its shores, where Aeneas consults the Sibyl, then follow its coast toward the Tiber. Further wars divide its peoples over Trojan settlement before Aeneas and his followers become established there.

Chapter XXIV

Italy is the western land called Hesperia that the Trojans are instructed to seek as their future home. It is said to be the land from which Dardanus, the founder of the Trojan race, originally migrated. The Trojans eventually reach its shores, where Aeneas consults the Sibyl.

Chapter XXV

Italy is the land in which Aeneas and his followers are to become established after further wars and battles. Anchises tells Aeneas about the events still required before that complete establishment.

Chapter XXVI

Italy is the land whose shores Aeneas follows after leaving the Sibyl and whose Tiber coast becomes his destined home. Its peoples are divided when opposition to the Trojan settlement leads to war.
