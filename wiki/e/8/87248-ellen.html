Ellen in Gallegher and Other Stories

Richard Harding Davis · 1890

Ellen is the bishop’s only daughter, a woman loved by young Latimer, whom she loves and intends to marry if her father agrees. Her father sees her future happiness as his responsibility and fears she would suffer if Latimer loved another woman again. Ellen initially teases Latimer for seeming afraid before he speaks to the bishop. After secretly testing him as a joke meant to frighten him, she enters distressed and exhausted, asks her father to send Latimer away, and tells Latimer to go to the other woman.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

" THE OTHER WOMAN Young Latimer stood on one of the lower steps of the hall stairs, leaning with one hand on the broad railing and smiling down at her.

Ellen loves young Latimer and has agreed to marry him if the bishop does not oppose them. Before Latimer speaks to her father, she teases him for seeming afraid and urges him not to be long. She is the bishop's only child and is described as the person whose future happiness he feels responsible for protecting.

"Well, and suppose this other woman did exist, what then?

The bishop says that Ellen would bear the trouble if Latimer came to love the other woman again after marrying her.

The older man stepped quickly across the floor, but before he reached the middle

Ellen enters with her head lowered after listening to the discussion. She says she conducted the test as a joke because she wanted to frighten Latimer and laugh at his response, but she becomes exhausted and distressed. She draws close to her father, asks him to make Latimer leave, and later tells him to go to the other woman.
