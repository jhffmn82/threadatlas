Abstract written by a model

Artaxerxes is repeatedly presented as king of Persia and Asia, ruling the Persians with justice and equity for forty years. His rise began in violence: persuaded by Artabanus, he killed his brother Darius and took the throne; when Artabanus later attacked him, he defended himself, killed Artabanus, and secured his kingship. After the death of Xerxes, he punished those involved in his father's murder, reorganized government, stored provisions, and prepared forces. He faced revolt in Egypt, raising a fleet and sending armies under Archemenes, then Artabazus and Megabyzus. Warned of his brother Cyrus's conspiracy, he assembled a vast army, marched against him, was wounded, but recovered as his forces defeated Cyrus and his army. He punished captured Greek commanders, rewarded loyal soldiers, and entrusted coastal governments and later commands to Tissaphernes, Tithraustes, and Struthius. After Persian setbacks in Cyprus and conflict with the Greeks, he accepted peace: Greek cities in Asia were to return to obedience, while other Greeks governed by their own laws. Free from that war, he prepared for war against Evagoras in Cyprus, ordering ships and aid for opponents of Evagoras.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

10 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. XIII.

Artaxerxes was persuaded by Artabanus to kill his brother Darius and take the Persian throne. When Artabanus later attacked him, Artaxerxes defended himself, killed Artabanus, and became king of Persia.

CHAP. XIX.

Artaxerxes became king of Persia after the death of Xerxes, punished those involved in his father's murder, reorganized the government, stored provisions, and prepared his forces. He sent armies under Archemenes and later under Artabazus and Megabyzus against Egypt.

DIODORUS SICULUS. / BOOK XIL

Artaxerxes, king of Persia, convened a council after hearing that Persian armies had been defeated in Cyprus. The council decided that Persia should make peace with the Greeks, and Artaxerxes’ officers were ordered to negotiate on any terms.

CHAP. III.

Artaxerxes was the Persian king and the brother whom Cyrus intended to attack. Alcibiades planned to inform him of Cyrus's conspiracy, but Pharnabazus disclosed it first.

CHAP. V-

Artaxerxes learned of Cyrus's preparations and assembled forces from throughout his territories. He marched against Cyrus with an army that Ephorus estimated at no fewer than four hundred thousand horse and foot. Cyrus wounded him in battle, but he was carried from the fight as if dead; his troops later recovered and defeated Cyrus's army.

Clearchus, after he had made a great slaughter among them, (it

Artaxerxes defeated Cyrus's forces, demanded that the Greeks lay down their arms, and later granted them a three-day truce to pass through his country with guides and provisions. He returned to Babylon, rewarded Tissaphernes and other courageous soldiers, and had the captured Greek commanders put to death except Menon.

CHAP. VI.

Artaxerxes, king of Asia, defeated Cyrus and sent Tissaphernes to take charge of the governments on the sea-coasts. His decision whether there would be peace or war was later awaited during the truce between Dercyllidas and Pharnabazus.

CHAP. vm.

Artaxerxes, king of Asia, became afraid and angry after hearing that his army had been routed. He appointed Tithraustes to seize Tissaphernes and later received Conon at Babylon, rewarding him and supplying him with money for operations against the Lacedaemonian fleet.

CHAP. XL

Artaxerxes responded to the appeals of the Amathusians, Solians, and Cittans by preparing aid against Evagoras. He ordered sea-port towns to build ships and commanded Hecatomnus to make war on Evagoras.

CHAP. XIII.

Artaxerxes agreed to peace with the Grecians on condition that the Greek cities in Asia return to obedience and that the other Grecians govern their cities according to their own laws. After the peace, he prepared an army for the Cyprian War against Euagoras.
