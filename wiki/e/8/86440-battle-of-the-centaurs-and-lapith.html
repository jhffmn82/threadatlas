war of the Lapiths and Centaurs in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The war began after the Centaurs, invited to the marriage of Perithous and Hippodamia, became drunk and attempted to ravish the women. Theseus and the Lapiths killed many Centaurs and drove the rest away, after which the Centaurs made war on the Lapiths, killing most of them and forcing the survivors into exile.

Battle of the Centaurs and Lapithæ in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

The Battle of the Centaurs and Lapithæ is a battle arising at Pirithoüs’s wedding feast. It begins when Eurytus and other Centaurs attack Hippodame and other women. Centaurs and Lapiths fight with banquet objects, weapons, trees, and stones; many are killed, and Cæneus is buried beneath trees. By night the surviving Centaurs flee or disperse, retreating to Arcadia as pursuing Lapithæ drive them toward Malea.

FABLES III. AND IV. [XII.146-535]

The battle begins when Eurytus and the other Centaurs attack Hippodame and other women. Banquet objects, altars, torches, tree branches, and weapons are used in the fighting, and many Centaurs and Lapithæ are killed.

“Demoleon could not any longer endure Theseus enjoying this success in

The battle involves close combat, javelins, swords, pikes, trees, stones, and other improvised weapons. Peleus, Nestor, Cæneus, and other Lapith fighters kill numerous Centaurs, while the Centaurs attack Cæneus collectively and bury him beneath a huge heap of trees. The fighting ends for the time being when some Centaurs are dead and the rest flee or are dispersed by night.

EXPLANATION.

The battle followed Eurytus’s or Eurytion’s attempt on Hippodamia. Afterward, the Centaurs retreated to the mountains of Arcadia, and the pursuing Lapithæ drove them to the promontory of Malea in Laconia.
