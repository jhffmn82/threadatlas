Tempe in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Tempe was selected as a passage to be defended against the Persians. The Greek forces abandoned it after learning that most Thessalians and the inhabitants of the nearby straits had submitted to the king.

LAST TEN BOOKS / BOOK XI.

Tempe was selected as a passage to be defended against the Persians. The Greek forces abandoned it after learning that most Thessalians and the inhabitants of the nearby straits had submitted to the king.

Tempe in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Tempe is a Hæmonian grove and Thessalian valley, enclosed by wood amid craggy rocks; Peneus flows through it, where the river and water Nymphs retreat, and swan-formed Cycneus frequents it.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

opening

Tempe is a grove of Hæmonia enclosed by a wood on every side and situated among craggy rocks. The river Peneus flows through it, and it is the home and retreat of that river and of the Nymphs inhabiting its waters.
