Tempe

Also called: Cycneian Tempe, Thessalian Tempe

Abstract written by a model

In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Tempe was selected as a passage to defend against the Persians, but the Greek forces abandoned it. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Tempe is a Hæmonian grove and Thessalian valley where Peneus flows and water Nymphs retreat.

Tempe in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Tempe first appears

Abstract written by a model

Tempe is a Hæmonian grove and Thessalian valley, enclosed by wood amid craggy rocks; Peneus flows through it, where the river and water Nymphs retreat, and swan-formed Cycneus frequents it.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

opening

Tempe is a grove of Hæmonia enclosed by a wood on every side and situated among craggy rocks. The river Peneus flows through it, and it is the home and retreat of that river and of the Nymphs inhabiting its waters.
