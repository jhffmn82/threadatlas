Percy Phelps in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1892~/1893~

Percy Phelps is a young diplomatist and Foreign Office official, formerly a brilliant Cambridge scholar, Lord Holdhurst’s nephew, and the fiancé of Annie Harrison. Nervous and sensitive, he lives in Woking and is recovering from a long brain-fever after the original Naval Treaty vanishes while he is copying it at the Foreign Office. The loss ruins his career and puts his honour and position at stake. Unable to find any route by which the thief entered, he pursues Mrs. Tangey without recovering the papers, then falls ill for nine weeks. As Sherlock Holmes’s client, Phelps recounts the theft at Briarbrae, where an armed man later attempts to enter his bedroom, making him fear for his life as well as his reputation. Holmes sends the still-weak Phelps to London with Dr. John Watson while the inquiry continues. Phelps despairs that the effort has failed, but is overwhelmed with joy when Holmes returns the papers, which had remained in Phelps’s room at Briarbrae throughout his ten weeks of suffering, restoring his honour.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

XI. The Naval Treaty

Percy Phelps is a former schoolmate of Dr. John Watson and a successful Foreign Office official whose career is ruined by the theft of the original Naval Treaty. After suffering nine weeks of brain-fever, he asks Watson to bring Holmes to Briarbrae, where he recounts how the treaty disappeared while he was copying it.

“We examined the room also. There is no possibility of a secret

Percy Phelps tells Holmes and Watson that the treaty was stolen from the Foreign Office after he found no possible secret door, trap-door, fireplace access, or other route into the room. He pursued Mrs. Tangey without recovering the papers, suffered a prolonged illness from the shock and disgrace, and now asks Holmes to restore his honour and position.

“Where are we going to now?” I asked, as we left the office.

Percy Phelps remains weak but is recovering under care at Woking. He reports that an armed man tried to enter his bedroom during the night and begins to suspect a conspiracy against his life as well as his honour. Holmes sends him to London with Dr. John Watson so that he will be away while the investigation continues.

It was seven o’clock when I awoke, and I set off at once for

Percy Phelps is initially depressed and fears that Holmes has failed, but he reacts with overwhelming joy when Holmes places the recovered papers before him. He thanks Holmes for saving his honour and learns that the papers had remained in his room at Briarbrae throughout his ten weeks of agony.
