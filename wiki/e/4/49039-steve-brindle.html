Steve Brindle in Pen Pictures of Eventful Scenes and Struggles of Life

B. F. Craig · 1880

Steve Brindle, also called Steve Sharp, describes himself as a come-by-chance, the son of Brindle Bill and Nancy Wade, whose parents were unmarried; his mother was a sister of Sundown Hill. A private in General Pearce’s Arkansas army and a federal soldier, he recognizes Cole Conway as Cæsar Simon and becomes his old comrade. After sharing money and family history, Steve saves Cæsar’s life at Wilson’s Creek with a Cherokee Indian’s help. Cæsar later denies him amid wealth and power; Steve is reported wounded after Elkhorn, with a card alleging Cæsar deserted.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

SCENE SIXTH.

Steve Brindle is the name used by Steve Sharp, whose father was Brindle Bill and whose mother was a sister of Sundown Hill. He tells Cole Conway that he is a child born outside marriage and recognizes Conway as Cæsar Simon, heir to S. S. Simon's estate in Arkansas.

SCENE SEVENTH--WAR BETWEEN THE STATES.

Steve Brindle serves as a private in General Pearce’s army and saves Cousin Cæsar’s life at Wilson’s Creek with the help of a Cherokee Indian. He has previously shared his money and family history with Cæsar, but Cæsar later refuses to acknowledge him when surrounded by wealth and power. After the battle at Elkhorn, Steve is reported to have been wounded, and a card stating that Cæsar Simon had deserted is associated with him.
