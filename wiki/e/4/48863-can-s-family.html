Can's family

group · 1 work · 3 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: CAN, CAN'S family, Can and the members of his family, Cans

Abstract written by a model

Can's family, named CAN, is described as seven members, Can, Coz, Cay, Aak, Chaacmol, Moo, and Nicte, who founded the principal cities of Mayab, including Uxmal and Chichen-Itza. Their name appears on monuments there. Associated with mounds, temples, priesthood, rulership, and posthumous worship, they revered Deity through a mastodon's head and also worshipped the sun and fire.

Can's family is in one work on this site, Vestiges of the Mayas, or, Facts tending to prove that Communications and Intimate Relations must have existed, in very remote times, between the inhabitants of Mayab and those of Asia and Africa. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

The Cans, or family of CAN, are said to have founded Uxmal and Chichen-Itza, with their name appearing on monuments in both cities. They worshipped Deity under the symbol of the mastodon's head and also worshipped the sun and fire.

Circumcision was a rite in usage among the Egyptians since very remote times.

Can's family is described as consisting of seven members who founded the principal cities of Mayab. The named members are Can, Coz, Cay, Aak, Chaacmol, Moo, and Nicte, and the text connects them with mounds, turrets, temples, priesthood, rulership, and worship after death.
