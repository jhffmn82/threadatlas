Allied nations

Abstract written by a model

The Allied nations are a coalition expected to aid Serbia during war and to receive medical assistance from the Scottish Women’s Hospitals, whose units served their armies and wounded. They matter chiefly because their failure to prevent Bulgarian mobilisation, stop the attack on Serbia from north and east, prevent the German breakthrough toward the Turks, or provide timely relief leaves Serbia exposed. Despite this, the Scottish unit hopes Allied action, including British troops at Salonika, may eventually help.

The Allied nations were to receive medical assistance from the Scottish Women’s Hospitals. Elsie Maud Inglis’s units were organised to serve the armies and wounded of the Allied nations.

opening

The Allies were expected by the Serbs to come to their aid, but they did not prevent the Bulgarian mobilisation or provide help during the invasion. Their failure to intervene left Serbia exposed to the attack from the north and east.

’ As the fever died out, a worse enemy came in.

The Allies are presented as having failed to prevent the German breakthrough toward the Turks or to provide timely relief for Serbia. The Scottish unit nevertheless hoped that Allied action, including British troops at Salonika, might eventually help.
