purity and innocence in Hippolytus; The Bacchae

Euripides · -0427

Purity is a virtue associated with freedom from human corruption. Hippolytus treats it as abstention from ordinary desire, offering Artemis a garland gathered by pure hands and claiming innocence; Phaedra doubts inner cleanliness, while Theseus rejects Hippolytus’s claim as proof.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

HIPPOLYTUS

Hippolytus associates purity with a life untouched by ordinary human desire and offers Artemis a garland gathered only by pure hands. Phaedra calls herself clean in hand but questions whether her heart is clean.

[_The Voice of the_ NURSE _is heard from within, crying,

Hippolytus presents himself as pure and innocent, saying that he has never touched a woman's flesh and that no one on earth is more free of sin than he is. Theseus mocks his claims of purity and refuses to accept them as evidence.
