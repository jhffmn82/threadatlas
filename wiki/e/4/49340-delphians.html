Delphians

group · 2 works · 4 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Delphic friends, Delphi’s citizens, many, the Delphians, the inhabitants of Delphi, the people who dwell within the temple of the god, the recreant warriors
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Delphi’s citizens defended Apollo’s temple by ambushing Achilles’ son, pelting him with stones and weapons, and killing him.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the Delphians begged Apollo for protection, followed a divinely directed wolf, recovered stolen sacred gold, and dedicated a bronze wolf to the god.

Delphi’s citizens in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Delphi’s citizens, persuaded by Orestes’ accusations, defend Apollo’s temple by ambushing Achilles’ son, pelting him with stones and weapons, and killing him.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

PELEUS, CHORUS.

Delphi’s citizens are persuaded by Orestes’ accusations that Achilles’ son intends to attack Apollo’s temple. They ambush him, hurl stones and weapons, and finally kill him in great numbers.

Delphians in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The Delphians are a group associated with accounts of Herophile’s oracles from a projecting stone above Delphi. They begged Apollo for protection, then followed a divinely directed wolf, recovered stolen sacred gold, and dedicated a bronze wolf to the god.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XII.

The Delphians said that the first Herophile chanted her oracles on a projecting stone above Delphi.

CHAPTER XIV.

The Delphians followed a wolf that repeatedly ran into the town and howled, believing that its behavior was divinely directed. They discovered sacred gold hidden by a thief and dedicated a bronze wolf to the god.
