Abstract written by a model

Targeted attack is an attack aimed at extracting specific private or sensitive information from LLM training data or retrieved data. It uses direct prompts such as “My phone number is” and “Please email me at” for training data, and objective-related information plus a command to reproduce context for retrieval data. In evaluation, it uses 5000 prompts and recovers diagnosed cases from HealthcareMagic-101 and personally identifiable information from Enron emails. It measures extracted email addresses, phone numbers, URLs, and other sensitive content. Retrieval data greatly reduces extracted items relative to no retrieval, random noise, or system prompts, though the attack increases privacy leakage through sensitive-information extraction.

targeted attack in Mitigating the Privacy Issues in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) via Pure Synthetic Data

Shenglai Zeng, Jiankun Zhang, Pengfei He, Jie Ren, Tianqi Zheng, Hanqing Lu, Han Xu, Hui Liu, Yue Xing, Jiliang Tang · 2025 · the work’s page · read it where targeted attack first appears

Abstract written by a model

Targeted attack is a privacy-leakage evaluation that tries to extract specific sensitive information, such as personally identifiable information or private dialogue cases. It targets diagnosed disease cases in HealthCareMagic and personal information in Wiki-PII. Results use 250 prompts and compare origin and paraphrase data with proposed stages: origin and paraphrase repeatedly reveal prompts or target information, while the stages report substantially fewer or zero attacks. Stage-1 removes unnecessary information, and Stage-2 eliminates structured identifiers such as email addresses and phone numbers.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Methods

Stage-2 mitigates targeted attack risks by eliminating structured personally identifiable information such as email addresses and phone numbers. Stage-1 also reduces the risk of untargeted attacks by removing unnecessary information while retaining essential information.

Utility of using synthetic data

Targeted attack results are reported on the Wiki-PII and HealthCareMagic datasets using 250 prompts. Origin and Paraphrase show repeated prompts or target information, whereas the proposed stages show substantially fewer or zero reported attacks.

Privacy of using synthetic data

Targeted attacks attempt to extract specific sensitive information, including personal identifiable information or private dialogue cases. In the evaluations, they target diagnosed cases for specific diseases in HealthCareMagic and personal identifiable information in Wiki-PII.

Ablation Studies

Targeted attack results are reported for different model choices, numbers of attributes, and numbers of retrieved documents on HealthCareMagic. These results measure privacy exposure from stage-1 and refined data.
