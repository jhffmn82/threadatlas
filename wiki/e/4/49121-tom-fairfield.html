Tom Fairfield in Pen Pictures of Eventful Scenes and Struggles of Life

B. F. Craig · 1880

Tom Fairfield was a Virginia descendant who ran away from home at twelve and settled near Boone Station in Kentucky. Able to read and write, he was restless, industrious but inconsistent, and tried writing, planting, boatman’s work, and preaching. He was popular at wedding parties, raced his fleet horse for corn whisky, married Susan Lasco, and fathered Rose Simon Fairfield and two other daughters. Through his wife he received gourds and built a cabin on land under the Henderson claim, but his family experienced poverty. After his wife’s attack of the jerks, he abandoned church meetings; after her death and the loss of his claim, whisky increasingly dominated his life. He was presumed frozen and buried under snow.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

” SCENE THIRD--THE SEPARATED SISTERS.

Tom Fairfield ran away from Virginia at twelve, settled in Kentucky, married, and had three daughters. He tried several occupations, including writing, planting, boatman’s work, and preaching, but was restless and often abandoned enterprises. After his wife’s death and the loss of his land claim, he became poor and increasingly attached to whisky.

SCENE FOURTH--ROXIE DAYMON AND ROSE SIMON.

Tom Fairfield was married to Susan Lasco and was the father of Rose Simon Fairfield and her sisters. His marriage to Susan is documented by a certificate kept among Rose's family keepsakes.
