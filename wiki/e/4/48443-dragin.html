Abstract written by a model

In “DRAGIN: Dynamic Retrieval Augmented Generation based on the Information Needs of Large Language Models,” DRAGIN is presented as a dynamic retrieval-augmented generation framework for making retrieval decisions from an LLM’s real-time information needs. In “DioR: Adaptive Cognitive Detection and Contextual Retrieval Optimization for Dynamic Retrieval-Augmented Generation,” Dragin serves as a baseline dynamic retrieval-augmented generation method and scores below DioR across the reported datasets and metrics. In “SEAKR: Self-aware Knowledge Retrieval for Adaptive Retrieval Augmented Generation,” DRAGIN serves as a strong baseline that retrieves on low-probability tokens and reformulates queries using attention weights, while remaining below SEAKR.

DRAGIN in DRAGIN: Dynamic Retrieval Augmented Generation based on the Information Needs of Large Language Models

Weihang Su, Yichen Tang, Qingyao Ai, Zhijing Wu, Yiqun Liu · 2024 · the work’s page · read it where DRAGIN first appears

Abstract written by a model

DRAGIN is a dynamic retrieval-augmented generation framework for large language models that makes retrieval decisions from their real-time information needs during text generation. Its central purpose is to address both when retrieval should occur and what should be retrieved, optimizing retrieval timing and query formulation without further training, fine-tuning, or prompt engineering for Transformer-based LLMs. During continual generation, Real-time Information Needs Detection (RIND) monitors generated-token importance and uncertainty to trigger timely retrieval. Query Formulation based on Self-attention (QFS) then uses the LLM’s attention over the entire context to formulate a precise query. DRAGIN retrieves external knowledge and continues generation using that knowledge. It is evaluated with three LLMs on four knowledge-intensive datasets covering multihop reasoning, reading comprehension, and commonsense reasoning. It achieves superior or strong results on all evaluated datasets, with especially substantial gains on 2WikiMultihopQA and HotpotQA; it outperforms FLARE, FL-RAG, and FS-RAG in its timing and query-formulation methods, while making fewer retrieval calls than FS-RAG and FL-RAG. Its threshold sensitivity is low across RIND settings, and its code, data, and models are open-sourced in the GitHub repository for DRAGIN.
