Horners in The Patchwork Girl of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1913-07-01

The Horners are a separate, more important nation living beyond a mountain, in the Horner city. They are little brown, round-bodied people with short limbs, long pointed ears, two legs, a central forehead horn, and red, yellow, and green hair; they wear snow-white robes. Their city is larger but plainer than the Hoppers’ city. They mine radium beneath the mountain and use it to decorate their homes. They mock the Hoppers’ one-legged bodies and understanding, while raising girls under strict rules of politeness and behavior. After a proposed war is avoided, they welcome the strangers and laugh at Diksey’s jokes.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Twenty-One

The Horners are a separate nation living on the other side of the mountain. They have two legs, and one of them insulted the Hoppers by saying that having only one leg showed a lack of understanding.

Chapter Twenty-Two

The Horners are small, round-bodied people with short limbs, long pointed ears, one forehead horn, brown skin, white robes, and hair in red, yellow, and green sections. They live in a larger but plainer city than the Hoppers and laugh heartily at jokes about the Hoppers’ understanding.

Chapter Twenty-Three

The Horners spend their time digging radium and use it to decorate the interiors of their homes. They raise girls according to strict rules of politeness and proper behavior. They welcome the strangers after the proposed war is avoided and laugh at Diksey's jokes.
