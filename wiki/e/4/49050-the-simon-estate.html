the Simon estate in Pen Pictures of Eventful Scenes and Struggles of Life

B. F. Craig · 1880

The Simon estate, inherited by Young Simon from S. S. Simon, includes four thousand-acre plantations and more than six hundred slaves and is described as one of Arkansas’s largest. Valued at more than a million dollars, or more than a million and a quarter, it passes after Young Simon’s death to Roxie Daymon. Cousin Cæsar contests the inheritance with others involved, and the plantations and residence are later devastated by fire.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

SCENE SIXTH.

Young Simon's estate includes four plantations of one thousand acres each and more than six hundred slaves inherited from S. S. Simon. Steve Brindle later describes S. S. Simon's estate in Arkansas as one of the largest.

Roxie Daymon, whose death has been recorded, left an only daughter, now grown to

Young Simon’s estate is reported to be worth more than a million dollars. After his death, the entire estate is left to Roxie Daymon, prompting cousin Cæsar to seek legal action against the inheritance.

SCENE SEVENTH--WAR BETWEEN THE STATES.

The Simon estate is the subject of a legal contest involving Cousin Cæsar, Cliff Carlo, Roxie Daymon, and Governor Morock. Governor Morock tells Cliff Carlo that it is worth more than a million and a quarter, while the Simon plantations and residence are later devastated by fire.
