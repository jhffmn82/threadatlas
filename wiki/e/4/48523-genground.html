  • In Generate-then-Ground in Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Multi-hop Question Answering: GenGround is a proposed generate-then-ground framework and method for multi-hop question answering. It combines the parametric knowledge of large language models with external documents, aiming to use each more effectively and address possible conflict between generated and retrieved knowledge. Beginning with a complex question, it formulates a simpler single-hop sub-question and generates an immediate answer through answer deduction. It then enters instructional knowledge grounding, retrieving documents, using Wikipedia as an external corpus, and citing relevant content to ground the question-answer pair. Grounding revises or amends wrong predictions; the revised answer and sub-question are incorporated into later context, and the process alternates over several iterations until the final answer is derived. GenGround uses parametric knowledge to supplement retrieved knowledge and can use BM25 or Google Search with ChatGPT as backbone. Experiments on four datasets report that it outperforms retrieve-then-read, ReAct, DSPy, SearChain, and other retrieval and generation baselines; it achieves 52.26 F1, 47.27 Accuracy, and 55.73 semantic accuracy on HotpotQA. It remains strongest with either retriever, has stable outputs across repeated runs, and uses fewer tokens than compared baselines. Its ablations indicate that deduction, grounding, and batch processing matter. Its effectiveness may be limited when the initial answer is not meaningful, decomposition is challenging, or sources are incomplete or misleading.

GenGround in Generate-then-Ground in Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Multi-hop Question Answering

Zhengliang Shi, Shuo Zhang, Weiwei Sun, Shen Gao, Pengjie Ren, Zhumin Chen, Zhaochun Ren · 2024

GenGround is a proposed generate-then-ground framework and method for multi-hop question answering. It combines the parametric knowledge of large language models with external documents, aiming to use each more effectively and address possible conflict between generated and retrieved knowledge. Beginning with a complex question, it formulates a simpler single-hop sub-question and generates an immediate answer through answer deduction. It then enters instructional knowledge grounding, retrieving documents, using Wikipedia as an external corpus, and citing relevant content to ground the question-answer pair. Grounding revises or amends wrong predictions; the revised answer and sub-question are incorporated into later context, and the process alternates over several iterations until the final answer is derived. GenGround uses parametric knowledge to supplement retrieved knowledge and can use BM25 or Google Search with ChatGPT as backbone. Experiments on four datasets report that it outperforms retrieve-then-read, ReAct, DSPy, SearChain, and other retrieval and generation baselines; it achieves 52.26 F1, 47.27 Accuracy, and 55.73 semantic accuracy on HotpotQA. It remains strongest with either retriever, has stable outputs across repeated runs, and uses fewer tokens than compared baselines. Its ablations indicate that deduction, grounding, and batch processing matter. Its effectiveness may be limited when the initial answer is not meaningful, decomposition is challenging, or sources are incomplete or misleading.

Generate-then-Ground in Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Multi-hop Question Answering

It appears in the title as the subject of the work.

Abstract

GenGround is a generate-then-ground framework that combines the parametric knowledge of large language models with external documents. It alternates between formulating a simpler single-hop question and directly generating an answer, then grounding the pair in retrieved documents to correct wrong predictions.

Introduction

Generate-then-ground is the proposed method for answering multi-hop questions. It first allows a large language model to generate an immediate answer, then uses retrieved evidence to ground and revise the answer; the revised answer informs the next sub-question until the final hop.

2 / 2.1 .. 2.2

The generate-then-ground framework is proposed to incorporate the parametric knowledge of large language models and external documents more effectively. It addresses the potential knowledge conflict that can arise when retrieved and generated documents are used together.

3 .. 3.3

GenGround enables LLMs to alternate between answer deduction and instructional knowledge grounding until a final answer is derived. It integrates revised answers and sub-questions into the context used in later iterations.

5 / 5.1 .. 5.4

GenGround is the authors' method and achieves the highest reported main-table results across the listed HotpotQA, MuSiQue, and 2Wikimultihopqa F1, Accuracy, and semantic accuracy scores, as well as StrategyQA Accuracy. Its ablations show lower performance without deduction, grounding, or batch processing.

6 / 6.1 .. 6.3

GenGround achieves the best reported performance across the evaluated datasets and metrics, outperforming retrieval and generation baselines. It remains strongest when using either BM25 or Google Search and benefits from answer deduction, instructional knowledge grounding, and batch grounding.

7 / 7.1 .. 7.3

The authors' framework shows stable performance across two repeated experiments, with no significant difference detected by a two-tailed paired t-test. Its answer deduction and instruction knowledge grounding trajectories have high lexical similarity, and it achieves better performance while using fewer tokens than the compared baselines.

8

GenGround is a generate-then-ground framework for multi-hop question answering. It alternates between generating answers to simpler questions and grounding question-answer pairs in retrieved documents, amending incorrect predictions.

Limitations

The framework generates an initial answer, breaks complex questions into simpler ones, and uses external documents to correct initially non-factual statements. Its applicability and effectiveness may be limited by task difficulty, challenging decomposition, and incomplete or misleading sources.

Ethics Statement

The generate-then-ground framework enables Language Learning Models to alternately deduce answers and use established evidence to revise those answers across several iterations.
