Uxmal inscriptions in Vestiges of the Mayas, or, Facts tending to prove that Communications and Intimate Relations must have existed, in very remote times, between the inhabitants of Mayab and those of Asia and Africa

Augustus Le Plongeon · 1881

Uxmal inscriptions are Maya-language inscriptions arranged in squares or parallelograms, combining phonetic, symbolic, and ideographic signs. They include characters resembling Chaldee, Egyptian, and Etruscan signs and were used to identify Uxmal’s founders.

1 section; each heading opens the text

Circumcision was a rite in usage among the Egyptians since very remote times.

The Uxmal inscriptions are described as containing phonetic, symbolic, and ideographic signs arranged in squares or parallelograms. The author claims that they include characters resembling ancient Chaldee, Egyptian, and Etruscan signs and that they helped identify the names of Uxmal's founders.
