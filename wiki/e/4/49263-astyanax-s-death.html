Abstract written by a model

In Hecuba and other plays, Astyanax’s execution is the Greek-ordered throwing of Hector's son from Troy's towers after Troy's fall. In The Trojan Women of Euripides, Astyanax's death is the Greek-ordered killing of Hector's son by being dashed from Troy's wall. In The Library, the death of Astyanax is described as his fall from the battlements, with differing accounts of the agent and timing.

Astyanax's death in The Trojan Women of Euripides

Euripides · 414 BC · the work’s page · read it where Astyanax's death first appears

Abstract written by a model

Astyanax's death is a Greek-ordered child killing. On Odysseus's advice, the Greek army fears Hector's son may restore his fallen land and become a threat. Soldiers take him from Andromache, who cannot save him, to dash him from Troy's wall. Hecuba condemns the murder as shameful and mourns his lost youth and greatness.

Astyanax's death is presented as a murder committed by the Greeks out of fear that the child might restore his fallen land. Hecuba calls it a strange and shameful death for a supposedly brave people and mourns that he died before reaching youth or greatness.
