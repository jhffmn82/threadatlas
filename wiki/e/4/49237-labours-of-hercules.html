In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, the twelve labours of Hercules are tasks imposed by Eurystheus for Hercules to perform for immortality. In Heroides and Amores, Hercules' labours are a long series of victories over monsters and enemies imposed by Juno. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Hercules's labours are a series of heroic trials culminating in the destruction of monsters and conquest over death. In Description of Greece, the Labours of Hercules are exploits represented in relief at Olympia. In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, the 12 Labours of Hercules are represented mainly as carvings by Praxiteles on the gables of the temple of Hercules called Champion.

the Labours of Hercules in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · about 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where the Labours of Hercules first appears

Abstract written by a model

The Labours of Hercules are deeds performed for Eurystheus in exchange for restoring Hercules’ residence in Argos, and they bring fame lasting after death. They unfold through battles against the Nemean lion, Hydra, monsters, centaurs, giants, Amazons, Geryon, and the dragon, as well as captures and the relief of Atlas, purging sea and land of dangers. Though Lycus minimizes them, Amphitryon and the chorus praise Hercules’ courage; Hercules ultimately says his present disaster and his children’s deaths are more painful.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

HERCULES DISTRACTED.

The Labours of Hercules include defeating the Hydra, killing the Nemean lion, overcoming other monsters, and purging the sea and land of dangers. Lycus minimizes these deeds, while Amphitryon presents them as evidence of Hercules’ greatness and courage.

CHORUS.

Hercules’ labours include killing the Nemean lion, wounding the Centaurs, capturing the golden-horned hind and Diomedes’ horses, defeating Cycnus, killing the dragon of the Hesperian gardens, relieving Atlas, defeating the Amazons, subduing the Hydra, and killing Geryon.

HERCULES, MEGARA, AMPHITRYON, CHORUS.

Hercules recalls fighting the Hydra and the lion and carrying out Eurystheus’s commands. He declares that such labours would not make him victorious if he failed to prevent the deaths of his own children.

THESEUS, AMPHITRYON, HERCULES, CHORUS.

Hercules recalls his many labours, including killing lions, Typhons, giants, centaurs, and the hydra, and bringing the three-headed dog from the realm of the dead. He says that these labours were less painful than the present disaster.

twelve labours of Hercules in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · 59 to 29 BC · the work’s page · read it where twelve labours of Hercules first appears

Abstract written by a model

The twelve labours of Hercules are tasks imposed by Eurystheus after an oracle at Delphi says Hercules must perform them for immortality. They are extraordinarily numerous and difficult, including killing the Nemean lion and Hydra of Lerna; capturing the Erymanthian boar and golden-horned hind; driving away the Stymphalian birds; cleansing Augeas's stable; bringing the Cretan bull; obtaining Diomedes's mares and Hippolyta's belt; and bringing Cerberus out of hell. After his tenth labour, Eurystheus imposes another: the last is fetching the golden apples of the Hesperides. On completion, Hercules expects immortality.

Hercules's labours in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Hercules's labours first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hercules's labours are a series of heroic trials culminating, in the old mythus, in destruction of monsters and conquest over death. They begin with serpents, the Nemean lion, Stymphalian pests, and the Hydra; continue through capturing the Erymanthian boar and Ceryneian hind and cleansing Augeas's stables; and extend to the bull, Amazons' belt, Geryon, Antæus, Atlas, Cerberus, and other dangers. Hercules says Juno's commands did not exhaust him.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

FABLE II. [IX.101-272]

Hercules recalls subduing Busiris and Antæus, Geryon and Cerberus, the bull, the Nemean lion, the Hydra, the Thracian horses, the Amazons' belt, the apples guarded by the dragon, the Centaurs, and other dangers. He also recalls supporting the heavens and says that Juno's commands had not exhausted him.

EXPLANATION.

Hercules's labours are portrayed as including the killing of serpents, the Nemean lion, the Stymphalian pests, and the Hydra; the capture of the Erymanthian boar and Ceryneian hind; and the cleansing of the stables of Augeas. Other labours and adventures included encounters with the Amazons, Diomedes, Geryon, Antæus, Cacus, Atlas, Cerberus, and the monster at Troy, though the unit suggests that some distant exploits may belong to other heroes.

Abstract written by a model

The Twelve Labours are Hercules’s twelve principal heroic exploits, symbolizing Zodiac signs: they range from destroying or capturing dangers through cleaning Augeas’s stables and recovering valuable objects to journeys to distant lands.

EXPLANATION.

The Twelve Labours were the traditional name for Hercules’s principal exploits, although the unit states that his recorded adventures were considerably more numerous. They included the destruction or capture of dangerous animals and monsters, the cleansing of the stables of Augeas, the recovery of valuable objects, and journeys to distant lands.

Abstract written by a model

The labours of Hercules are tasks initially numbered ten, imposed by Eurystheus and to be performed at Tiryns; their completion is said to bring immortality. The first five are the Nemean lion, Lernaean hydra, Cerynitian hind, Erymanthian boar, and Augean cattle. Eurystheus excludes the hydra because Iolaus helped.
