Paul Kratides in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1892~/1893~

Paul Kratides is a Greek gentleman from Athens and Sophy Kratides’s brother. He is abducted and held prisoner in England by conspirators, restrained with plaster over his face and mouth, weakened by starvation and cruelty, and coercively questioned. They seek his signature in a dispute over his and Sophy’s property, but he refuses unless Sophy is married before him by a Greek priest. After three weeks of captivity, he is found bound at The Myrtles and dies from charcoal poisoning in a room filled with fumes.

X. The Greek Interpreter

Kratides is a Greek-speaking man who is emaciated, weak, and restrained with sticking-plaster over his face and mouth. He refuses to sign papers unless he sees Sophy married before a Greek priest, and he reveals through Mr. Melas’s covert questions that he is from Athens and has been held for three weeks.

Paul Kratides, a Greek gentleman from Athens who cannot speak English, is held prisoner by Harold Latimer and Wilson Kemp. They use cruelty, starvation, and threats to try to make him sign away his own and his sister's property, but he refuses. He is found bound and dead in a room filled with charcoal fumes.
