The Trick River in The Patchwork Girl of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1913-07-01

The Trick River is a broad, fast-moving river with high banks, a strong current, and no bridge or visible boat. It alternates direction between the Country of the Winkies and mountains where dangerous people dwell, trapping travelers on a raft until they resist it with a fish, tree, pole, and rock. After a wall of water, it flows steadily into the Winkie Country.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Twenty-Five

The river is broad, fast-moving, and bordered by high banks, with no bridge or visible boat for crossing. It flows toward the Country of the Winkies and eventually runs between two mountains where dangerous people dwell.

Chapter Twenty-Six

The Trick River changes its direction repeatedly, carrying the raft first toward the Winkie Country and then back toward the mountains. Its strong current prevents the travelers from reaching shore until they use a fish, a tree, a pole, and a rock to resist it. After the raft passes a wall of water, the river continues steadily forward and carries them into the Winkie Country.
