Hercules’ madness in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Hercules’ madness, caused by Juno through the Fiend and linked to hydra venom, drives Hercules into delusions of fighting and traveling to Mycenae. In the frenzy he kills his children and Megara; awakening without memory, he later understands and mourns the killings.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

IRIS, A FIEND, CHORUS.

Hercules’ madness is caused by the Fiend under the direction of Iris and Juno. It makes him mistake his father for Eurystheus’ father, imagine himself fighting and traveling to Mycenae, and murder his children and Megara before awakening without memory of the acts.

THESEUS, AMPHITRYON, HERCULES, CHORUS.

Hercules’ madness seizes him after he is sprinkled with the venom associated with the hundred-headed hydra. In that frenzy he kills his own children and wife, then recovers sufficiently to understand and mourn what he has done.
