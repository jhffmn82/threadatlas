Hercules’ madness

event · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: This frenzy seized him, accursed repose, distraction, driven mad, frantic, frenzy, madness, perturbation of the soul, the jealousy of Hera, the madness of Hercules, trance, wild confusion

Abstract written by a model

In Hecuba and other plays, Hercules’ madness drives Hercules to delusions of fighting and traveling to Mycenae, during which he kills his children and Megara before awakening without memory and mourning the killings. In The Library, the madness of Hercules, attributed to Hera’s jealousy after the battle with the Minyans, leads Hercules to throw his children by Megara and two children of Iphicles into a fire.

Hercules’ madness in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · about 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Hercules’ madness first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hercules’ madness, caused by Juno through the Fiend and linked to hydra venom, drives Hercules into delusions of fighting and traveling to Mycenae. In the frenzy he kills his children and Megara; awakening without memory, he later understands and mourns the killings.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

IRIS, A FIEND, CHORUS.

Hercules’ madness is caused by the Fiend under the direction of Iris and Juno. It makes him mistake his father for Eurystheus’ father, imagine himself fighting and traveling to Mycenae, and murder his children and Megara before awakening without memory of the acts.

THESEUS, AMPHITRYON, HERCULES, CHORUS.

Hercules’ madness seizes him after he is sprinkled with the venom associated with the hundred-headed hydra. In that frenzy he kills his own children and wife, then recovers sufficiently to understand and mourn what he has done.
