Theoclymenus in Hecuba and other plays

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

HELEN.

Theoclymenus is the son of Proteus and the ruler of the Egyptian palace where Helen resides. He seeks to marry Helen after Proteus’ death and is said to kill captured Greek strangers. At the time of Teucer’s arrival, he is away hunting.

CHOR. My sentiments on prophets well accord

Theonoe’s brother is the monarch and royal Proteus’ son who has ordered her to report Menelaus’s arrival. He seeks to marry Helen and is expected to kill Menelaus if he discovers him.

CHORUS.

Theoclymenus believes that Menelaus has died and agrees to help Helen perform Greek funeral customs. He orders a Tyrian bark with fifty oars and sailors, offers funeral gifts, and continues planning his marriage to Helen without learning the truth.

Swift bark of Sidon, by whose dashing oars

Theoclymenus learns that Helen has escaped with Menelaus and blames his sister for betraying him. He intends to kill her, but the Chorus restrains him until Castor and Pollux explain the divine will; he then abandons the attack and allows Helen to return home.
