Chorus of Theban old men

Abstract written by a model

The Chorus is composed of aged Theban men, belonging to Thebes. Though too weak to fight, it loyally supports Amphitryon, Megara, and the children, opposes and condemns Lycus, and defends Hercules’ honor. It later laments Hercules’ frenzy and the deaths of Megara and the children, helps guard him, and condemns Jove and the gods.

HERCULES DISTRACTED.

The Chorus consists of aged Theban men who come to support Amphitryon, Megara, and the children. Though too weak to fight, they condemn Lycus as a foreign tyrant, defend Hercules’ honor, and declare that they will not willingly allow the children to be killed.

IRIS, A FIEND, CHORUS.

The Chorus laments that Hercules, Greece’s benefactor and son of Jove, is about to be overwhelmed by an infernal frenzy. It mourns the deaths of his children and Megara, helps Amphitryon guard Hercules after the murders, and condemns Jove and the gods for allowing his suffering.
