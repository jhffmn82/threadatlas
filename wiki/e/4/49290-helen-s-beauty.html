Abstract written by a model

In Hecuba and other plays, Helen's beauty causes the Trojan War through the Judgment of Paris and becomes a source of ruin and false reputation. In The Trojan Women of Euripides, Helen's beauty is a birthright regarded by Helen as both potential glory and stigma, and by Hecuba as a dangerous appeal and source of pride. In Heroides and Amores, Helen's beauty is exceptionally powerful, surpassing Venus's promise and provoking Paris's desire and jealousy.

Helen's beauty in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · about 24 to 15 BC · the work’s page · read it where Helen's beauty first appears

Abstract written by a model

Helen's beauty is exceptionally powerful, whiter than snow and milk in Paris's view, and difficult to reconcile with chastity. In Paris's judgment of the goddesses, it surpasses Venus's promise and fame's reports, merits limitless adornment, and provokes his desire and jealousy.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

XVI

Paris says that his own beauty and strength revealed his hidden nobility. He considers Venus, Pallas, and Juno all worthy of victory, but says that Helen's beauty surpasses both Venus's promise and the reports spread by fame.
