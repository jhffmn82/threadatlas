Helen's beauty in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Helen's beauty is exceptionally powerful, whiter than snow and milk in Paris's view, and difficult to reconcile with chastity. In Paris's judgment of the goddesses, it surpasses Venus's promise and fame's reports, merits limitless adornment, and provokes his desire and jealousy.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

XVI

Paris says that his own beauty and strength revealed his hidden nobility. He considers Venus, Pallas, and Juno all worthy of victory, but says that Helen's beauty surpasses both Venus's promise and the reports spread by fame.

nun ego coniugium generosae degener opto,

Helen's beauty is described as exceptionally powerful and difficult to reconcile with chastity. Paris says that her appearance deserves limitless adornment and that it has caused him intense desire and jealousy.
