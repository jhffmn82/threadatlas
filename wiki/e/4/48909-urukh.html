Augustus Le Plongeon · 1881

Urukh is the first king of the Chaldees and a great architect. Credited with the most archaic monuments of the plains of Lower Mesopotamia, he conceived plans for the Babylonian Temple. His name is derived from HUK and LUK, interpreted as “to make everything” and “mud.”

Urukh is identified as the first king of the Chaldeans and a great architect credited with the most archaic monuments of Lower Mesopotamia and with conceiving the plans of the Babylonian Temple. His name is interpreted through the Maya words HUK, “to make everything,” and LUK, “mud.”
