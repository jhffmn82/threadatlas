Silenus

person · 3 works · 13 quoted facts · 4 connections
Also called: SIL, SIL., SILENUS, The old man, aged father, foster-father or tutor, his tutor, old school master and foster father, our aged father, this associate and companion

Abstract written by a model

In Hecuba and other plays, Silenus is a satyr, devoted follower of Bacchus and father of the Chorus of Satyrs, who becomes Polypheme’s enslaved shepherd, servant, and cupbearer and helps Ulysses escape. In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Silenus is an immortal aged Satyr, foster-father and tutor of Bacchus, who is found by Phrygian peasants and returned to Bacchus after being entertained by Midas. In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Silenus is Bacchus’s old school master and foster father, who wanders away drunkenly, is entertained by Midas, and is returned safely to Bacchus.

Silenus in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · about 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Silenus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Silenus is a satyr, devoted follower of Bacchus and father of the Chorus of Satyrs. Captured with his sons while seeking Bacchus, he becomes Polypheme’s enslaved shepherd, servant, and cupbearer. He trades wine to Ulysses for food, aids the Greeks, deceives Polypheme, serves him wine while drinking himself, claims to be Ganymede, and flees before the attack.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE CYCLOPS.

Silenus recounts that he and his sons were captured by a Cyclops while seeking Bacchus and are now enslaved by Polypheme. He trades Bacchus’s wine to Ulysses for food, helps the Greeks obtain lambs and cheeses, and lies to Polypheme about the transaction before Polypheme takes the strangers into the cave.

ULYSSES, CHORUS.

Silenus helps Ulysses serve wine to Polyphemus and repeatedly tastes and delays the drink for himself. He identifies himself as Ganymede to the intoxicated Polyphemus and then leaves, fearing the effects of the coming attack.

Silenus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · about AD 8 · the work’s page · read it where Silenus first appears

Abstract written by a model

Silenus is an immortal aged Satyr, bald, flat-nosed, red-faced, drunken, and foster-father and tutor of Bacchus. Separated from companions while Bacchus is away, he is found by Phrygian peasants, brought to Midas, entertained ten days and nights, then returned to Bacchus.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE II. [XI.85-145]

Silenus, the intoxicated foster-father and tutor of Bacchus, becomes separated from his companions while Bacchus is away. Phrygian peasants find him staggering with age and wine, bind him with garlands, and bring him to Midas, who entertains him for ten days and nights before returning him to Bacchus.
