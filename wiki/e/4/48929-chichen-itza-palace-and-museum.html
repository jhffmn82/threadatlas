Augustus Le Plongeon · 1881

Chichen-Itza is a site included among the monuments of Yucatan and Mayab, noted for its funeral chamber, gymnasium, ruined monumental buildings, and sculptures. Along with stone buildings, bas-reliefs, statues, mural paintings, inscriptions, tombs, architectural forms, and decorative symbols, it is presented as enduring evidence for reconstructing Maya civilization. Its remains are used to examine ancient customs, religious practices, history, relationships among peoples, and possible movements, while distinguishing earlier Maya worship from later Nahualt influences. The records stress that such monuments require systematic, patient archaeological study and further interpretation.
