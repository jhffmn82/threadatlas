Cæsar Simon in Pen Pictures of Eventful Scenes and Struggles of Life

B. F. Craig · 1880

Cæsar Simon, son of S. S. Simon’s brother and raised by two penniless sisters, grows up imaginative, mischievous, and unwilling to work. He leaves Tennessee, becomes a gambler, and learns he is heir to S. S. Simon’s estate. Knowing family circumstances involving Roxie Daymon and Simon, he helps Simon pursue the inheritance dispute, hires Gov. Morock to challenge the will after Simon’s death, and goes to Kentucky for Cliff Carlo’s help. He then leaves for Missouri, joins Price’s army as private number 77, and proves brave and penetrating-minded despite a selfish disposition. Severely wounded at Wilson’s Creek, he is saved by Steve Brindle and a Cherokee Indian. His recurring fear of 77 and his failure to acknowledge Steve in prosperity trouble him. At Helena he recognizes Steve among federal soldiers, falls in battle during the retreat, and becomes the last male link of the Simon family.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

SCENE SIXTH.

Cæsar Simon is the son of S. S. Simon's brother and is raised by his impoverished sisters, "big-sis" and "little-sis." Imaginative, mischievous, and averse to work, he later leaves Tennessee, becomes a gambler, and learns that he is heir to S. S. Simon's estate.

Roxie Daymon, whose death has been recorded, left an only daughter, now grown to

Cousin Cæsar knows much of the circumstances surrounding Roxie and Simon but initially keeps them to himself. He tells Simon the story, accompanies him, hires Gov. Morock to break the will after Simon’s death, pays one thousand dollars in advance, and then travels to Kentucky to seek help from Cliff Carlo.

SCENE SEVENTH--WAR BETWEEN THE STATES.

Cousin Cæsar Simon leaves Kentucky for Missouri to consult members of the Carlo family and joins General Sterling Price’s army. He fights at Wilson’s Creek, is severely wounded in the leg, and is rescued by Steve Brindle and a Cherokee Indian. He later worries over the recurring number 77, neglects to acknowledge Steve when he is prosperous, and considers whether Steve’s reported desertion is a message intended for him.

The federals had fortified Helena, a point three hundred miles above Vicks burg

Cæsar Simon observed the battle while crouched beneath an old stump and recognized Steve Brindle among the federal soldiers. He fell at Helena, and his death ended the Simon family in the male line.
