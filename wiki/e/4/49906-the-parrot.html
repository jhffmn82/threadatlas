the parrot in Sky Island

L. Frank Baum · 1912

The parrot is a talking, jolly bird and pet that barks like a dog, recites rhymes, sings, and imitates sounds. It travels through the Fog Bank with Trot, Cap'n Bill, and Button-Bright, riding on Button-Bright's shoulder and later perching on Trot. In the Fog Bank it complains about being wet, warns the group about a giant frog, and emerges into sunshine with them. In the Pink Country it dries its feathers, alerts the travelers to the Pinkies, and frightens the Pinkies by speaking. After the Pinkies and Blueskins meet peacefully, it celebrates with verses and barking, then joins Trot at the feast, where she feeds it.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

THE TRIBULATION OF TROT

The blue parrot barks like a dog and recites rhymes while asking for food. Trot feeds it seeds and strokes its feathers, and it follows her, pecks Princess Indigo's ear, and declares that the pets like a fight.

THROUGH THE FOG BANK

The parrot complains and sings about the wetness of the Fog Bank, including its soaking paths and the difficulty of following a frog in the fog. It warns the group about the giant frog and makes remarks to the lizard and Cancer. It rides with the others and emerges with them into sunshine.

THE PINK COUNTRY

The parrot dries and prunes its feathers after emerging from the Fog Bank. It alerts the travelers to the approaching Pinkies, mocks their suspicions, and frightens them by speaking, causing them to regard it as evidence of magic.

TROT CELEBRATES THE VICTORY

The parrot celebrates the peaceful meeting of the Pinkies and Blueskins by shouting verses, barking like a dog, and later flying to Trot's shoulder. It sits on the back of Trot's chair during the feast, where she feeds it.
