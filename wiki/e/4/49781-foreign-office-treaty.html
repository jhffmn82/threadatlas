Foreign Office treaty in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1892~/1893~

The Foreign Office treaty, also called the Naval Treaty, is a secret French-language State document between England and Italy, consisting of twenty-six articles and signed by high dignitaries. It defines Great Britain’s position toward the Triple Alliance and its policy should the French fleet gain naval superiority over Italy in the Mediterranean. Lord Holdhurst assigns Percy Phelps to copy it, but it disappears from Phelps’s room, threatening his career and honour and raising fears of grave consequences if disclosed. For nearly ten weeks, there is no evidence that it has reached the French or Russian Foreign Office, though Joseph Harrison apparently plans to sell it to the French embassy. Harrison hides it beneath a removable floorboard over a gas-pipe joint in Phelps’s bedroom at Briarbrae. Holmes lets Harrison retrieve it, takes it from him, and returns it to Phelps before its secrecy ends in a few months.

The Naval Treaty is a secret agreement between England and Italy concerning Britain's position toward the Triple Alliance and its policy if the French fleet gains naval superiority over Italy in the Mediterranean. It is written in French, contains twenty-six articles, and bears the signatures of high dignitaries.
