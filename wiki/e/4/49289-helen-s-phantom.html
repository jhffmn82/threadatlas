Abstract written by a model

Helen’s phantom is a living image or cloud resembling Helen, made from ether and secretly formed in her likeness. It was created by divine power, attributed to Hera, Juno, and the son of Maia, to keep Paris from obtaining or marrying the real Helen, who remained in Egypt. Given to Paris and taken to Troy in Helen’s place, it was believed to be Helen and became the object connected with the Trojan War. Menelaus later took it from Troy, believing it his wife; it was associated with his ships and guarded in a cave by his surviving companions. It then declared that it had been substituted for the real Helen, vanished, and returned to the ether or sky. The Dioscuri also say it was secretly conveyed to an island near the Athenian realm.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

HELEN.

Helen’s phantom was a living image formed by Hera and given to Paris in Helen’s likeness. Paris believed that he possessed the real Helen, and the phantom was later taken from Troy by Menelaus.

MENELAUS.

Helen’s phantom was created from ether by divine power and was taken to Troy in Helen’s place. Menelaus brought it from Troy, believing it to be his wife, but it later vanished into the heavens after declaring that it had been substituted for the real Helen.

CHOR. My sentiments on prophets well accord

Helen’s image is said to have been taken from Menelaus along with his ships. Theonoe’s prophecy distinguishes the living Helen in Egypt from the image connected with the events at Troy.

CHORUS.

Helen’s phantom is described as a cloud resembling Helen that went to Troy and later mounted to the sky. The Chorus and Theoclymenus connect this phantom with the Greeks’ disastrous war against Troy.
