Scraps in The Patchwork Girl of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1913-07-01 · the work’s page · read it where Scraps first appears

Abstract written by a model

Scraps is a living Patchwork Girl: a girl-shaped artificial creature made by Dame Margolotte from her grandmother's many-colored patchwork quilt, stuffed with cotton-wadding, and intended to serve in Dr. Pipt's household. Her patched body, skirt, and apron are vividly colored; she has red pointed shoes, gold fingernails and ears, brown yarn braids, silver suspender-button eyes, pearl teeth, and no pink face because Margolotte lacked pink cloth. Brain powders, including extra qualities Ojo secretly added, are sewn into her head. The Crooked Magician's Powder of Life awakens her after an accident, and she chooses the name Scraps. Though made to be a servant, Scraps delights in freedom, rejects dignity, and ultimately becomes her own master. She has no heart, cannot eat, and is regarded by some as crazy, but she is clever, good tempered, bold, and habitually speaks in nonsense poetry. Ojo is her first friend, and loyalty to him becomes central: she promises to help find ingredients needed after the accident, defends him when he is arrested and tried, hides the six-leaved clover in Dorothy's room, and insists on continuing his journey. Her travels take her through forests, houses, the Woozy's country, the Emerald City, and many other places. She first sees stars and the moon, confronts dangers with the travelers, is seized by a monstrous plant, shields Ojo from quills, has her patch holes repaired, is tossed and thrown without being torn apart, and refuses to swim lest water ruin her patches. She admires the Emerald City and wants to live there rather than return to the Crooked Magician. Along the way she makes friends, argues against unfair rules and war, and is admired by the Scarecrow, Dorothy, and Ozma. In the end Ozma recognizes her as a remarkable, clever, good-tempered curiosity of Oz and allows her to live in the palace or wherever she pleases, free from service to anyone.

Chapter Two

The Patchwork Girl is a girl-shaped figure that Dame Margolotte made from her grandmother's many-colored patchwork quilt and stuffed with cotton-wadding. Margolotte intends for Dr. Pipt to bring her to life as a servant.

Chapter Three

The Patchwork Girl is made from a patchwork quilt, stuffed with cotton, and dressed in a patchwork skirt and apron. She has red leather shoes, gold fingernails, yarn hair, silver button eyes, gold ears, pearl teeth, and a multicolored patched face. Margolotte places a mixture of brain powders inside her head, but she is not brought to life during this unit.

Chapter Four

The Patchwork Girl lies limp and lifeless on a bench, made from a bundle of differently colored scraps. Margolotte intends her to be a servant, while the Glass Cat criticizes her appearance; Ojo has added extra qualities to the brains inside her head without telling Margolotte.

Chapter Five

The Patchwork Girl is frightened by the accident but quickly recovers and takes delight in her unusual appearance. She suggests using the Powder of Life to restore the petrified pair, chooses the name Scraps, and joins Ojo's search for the needed ingredients.

Chapter Six

Scraps enjoys the forest and is pleased by her freedom, bright colors, and many brains. She cannot eat because she has no opening beyond her mouth, and she teases the Glass Cat while leaping over a brook. At the roadside house, she refuses to keep quiet and is seized by an unseen hand, thrown outside, and locked out.

Chapter Seven

Patchwork Girl spends the night outside watching the stars, the moon, and a gray wolf. She welcomes the phonograph, calls it Vic, asks it to play music, and then helps drive it away after finding both of its records unpleasant.

Chapter Eight

Scraps suggests entering the house of the Foolish Owl and Wise Donkey to get advice. She compares the Foolish Owl's poem with her own poetry and later recognizes the owl's final poem as a hint to continue traveling.

Chapter Nine

Scraps climbs over the fence into the Woozy's forest and encourages Ojo to face the Woozy. She helps Ojo try to pull out one of the Woozy's tail hairs and later realizes that the Woozy can escape by using its eye fire to burn the fence. She joins Ojo and the Glass Cat in shouting "Krizzle-Kroo!" and continues traveling with the Woozy.

Chapter Ten

Scraps worries that the colors of her patches might fade. A monstrous plant captures her, but the Shaggy Man cuts down the leaf and releases her; she immediately likes him after he laughs and calls her a wonder.

Chapter Eleven

Scraps jokes with the Shaggy Man, rejects dignity, and urges the group to reach the Emerald City quickly. She is entertained by the Shaggy Man's song and later carefully considers his advice to make friends with Eureka.

Chapter Twelve

Scraps travels with Ojo and the others and follows the Shaggy Man's instructions to cross the sliding road. She shields Ojo during Chiss's quill attack, becoming covered with quills, then helps gather them; the quills leave holes in her patches that the flesh-wound charm cannot repair.

Chapter Thirteen

Scraps helps the travelers pass through the wall by following Ojo with her hands on his shoulders, although she cannot close her button eyes. A farmhouse woman repairs the holes in her body, and Scraps refuses an offer to stay for a new hat. She meets the Scarecrow of Oz, exchanges compliments with him, and attracts his enthusiastic admiration.

Chapter Fourteen

Scraps admires the Emerald City and says she wants to live there rather than return to the Crooked Magician. She argues that Ojo has broken no Law when the Soldier with the Green Whiskers arrests him.

Chapter Fifteen

Scraps receives Ojo's basket for safekeeping and is told to give it to the Crooked Magician if Ojo never leaves prison. She questions whether Ozma is making too much of Ojo's offense and later becomes distracted by the wonders of the Emerald City.

Chapter Sixteen

Scraps is a living Patchwork Girl made from an old bed-quilt by Margolotte and brought to life with a Powder of Life made by the Crooked Magician. She says she has no heart and does not want to help Margolotte and Unc Nunkie, but she considers Ojo her first friend and decides to help him. She secretly removes the six-leaved clover from Ojo's basket and hides it in a golden vase in Dorothy's room.

Chapter Seventeen

The Patchwork Girl is staying in Dorothy's room, and Dorothy has become fond of her unusual character. Ozma considers her bewildering but says her brightly colored patchwork is gorgeous, while the Scarecrow considers her beautiful.

Chapter Eighteen

Scraps boldly defends Ojo during his trial, claiming that the clover cannot be proven to have been picked. The Wizard reveals that she hid it in the golden vase, and she later insists on joining Ojo, Dorothy, and the Scarecrow because she promised to help him.

Chapter Nineteen

The Patchwork Girl admires Jack Pumpkinhead but says he is not as beautiful as the Scarecrow. The Tottenhots toss her through the air, loosening her cotton stuffing, and she later sleeps in their circular house with the other travelers.

Chapter Twenty

Scraps recites a playful rhyme about Yoop and joins the attempt to run past the cave. Mister Yoop grabs and throws her, but her strong stitches keep her from being ripped apart.

Chapter Twenty-Two

Scraps proposes being thrown up to rescue the Scarecrow, but the Champion throws her over the fence and into Horner Country instead. She talks with the Chief of the Horners and argues that the Horners should explain their joke to the Hoppers to avoid war.

Chapter Twenty-Three

Scraps accompanies Chief Horner to his home and meets his nineteen daughters. She criticizes the strict rules that prevent the young Horner girls from playing, laughing, or making jokes, and later helps receive the Scarecrow after he is rescued.

Chapter Twenty-Five

Scraps recites a poem about being afraid of getting wet and refuses to swim in the river. She minds the Quadling children while the raft is built and becomes friendly with them.

Chapter Twenty-Six

Scraps says she cannot risk swimming because it would ruin her patches. She is drenched when the raft crosses a high bank of water, but the Scarecrow dries her patches and they do not fade or run together. She admires the lilies and is amazed by the Tin Woodman's castle.

Chapter Twenty-Seven

Scraps says that she would kill butterflies to help Ojo rescue Unc Nunkie because she has no heart. She suggests taking a live yellow butterfly to the Crooked Magician so that he can remove its left wing, but the Tin Woodman rejects the idea. She later says that thirteen is her lucky number because she has thirteen patches on her head.

Chapter Twenty-Eight

The Patchwork Girl is praised as remarkable in appearance, clever, and good tempered. Ozma will preserve her as one of the curiosities of Oz, and she may live in the palace or wherever she pleases without being anyone's servant.

Scraps in The Lost Princess of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1917 · the work’s page · read it where Scraps first appears

Abstract written by a model

Scraps is a magically alive Patchwork Girl: a soft-bodied, cotton-stuffed person made from a quilt cut and stuffed into shape, with a round quilt head, brown-yarn hair, and two black button eyes. Daffy, dancing, clever, and a favorite of Oz’s subjects, she has much brains despite her stuffing. She never sleeps at night, sees well in darkness, cannot get lost, and is proud to be Oz’s unique Original and Only Cotton-Stuffed person. She is friends with the Woozy, rides it during the search for Ozma, and spends a sleepless night talking with it and the Sawhorse. From the start she suspects Ozma was stolen and presses the group to confront wickedness. She repeatedly leads through danger: devising a harness-strap crossing of a gulf, surviving the whirling mountains, recognizing and dancing through the Wall of Illusion, questioning the High Coco-Lorum, finding fruit and the lost Button-Bright, and bringing the Frogman to the others. Immune to Ugu’s actions, she mocks fear and helps expose another illusion by dancing through apparent soldiers. At Ugu’s, she urges action; when the room is restored, she falls from a chandelier and is rolled and patted back into shape.
