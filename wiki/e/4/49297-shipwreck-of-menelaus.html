Abstract written by a model

Menelaus’s ship is shattered on rocks, leaving only its keel; he and Helen’s phantom survive, while he reaches Egypt with few companions, tattered and without food, clothing, or royal possessions. Reported as drowned in a Libyan wreck, Menelaus uses the false account, supported by his appearance, for Helen’s funeral deception and escape plan.

MENELAUS.

Menelaus’s ship is shattered against the rocks, leaving only the keel intact. He and Helen’s phantom survive on it, while Menelaus reaches Egypt with few friends and without food, clothing, or his former royal possessions.

CHOR. My sentiments on prophets well accord

Menelaus’s shipwreck leaves him in tattered clothing, separated from his ship, and apparently the only survivor able to report the disaster. Helen turns this misfortune into evidence for the false funeral and the escape plot.

CHORUS.

The false account says that Menelaus was engulfed by the waves and wrecked on the rocks of Libya, which offered no harbor. In truth, Menelaus survives and reaches Egypt, where he uses the report to deceive Theoclymenus.
