Abstract written by a model

In The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes, Professor Moriarty is a brilliant mathematician turned criminal mastermind who leads a powerful organization, threatens Sherlock Holmes, and confronts him at Reichenbach Falls, where both are believed to fall while fighting. In The Return of Sherlock Holmes, Professor James Moriarty is the deceased leader of the Moriarty gang who pursues Sherlock Holmes to Switzerland, confronts him at Reichenbach Fall, and falls to his death. In The Valley of Fear, Professor Moriarty is a wealthy, publicly unsuspected professor who secretly directs a London criminal organization and intervenes in the murder of John Douglas.

Professor Moriarty in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · about 1892 to 1893 · the work’s page · read it where Professor Moriarty first appears

Abstract written by a model

Professor Moriarty is a phenomenally able mathematician, author of a Binomial Theorem treatise at twenty-one, and a former university mathematics professor who later became a mathematical coach. He is also the hidden central power and terrible chief of a criminal organization, employing numerous agents to plan and execute serious crimes; he is described as Europe’s most dangerous and capable criminal and as Holmes’s intellectual equal. As Holmes investigates him and planned arrests threaten his gang, Moriarty demands that Holmes stop, threatens destruction, and uses agents to attack while avoiding direct exposure and declaring he will never stand trial. After the police secure the gang, Moriarty escapes London, pursues Holmes and Watson through Victoria and beyond, and follows Holmes to Switzerland. At Reichenbach Falls, he confronts Holmes; experts conclude that the two fell together while locked in combat.

Professor Moriarty in The Valley of Fear

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1914-09 to 1915-05 · the work’s page · read it where Professor Moriarty first appears

Abstract written by a model

Professor Moriarty is a learned, talented, unmarried professor and the concealed criminal mastermind directing a large London organization. Though publicly unsuspected and earning seven hundred a year, he is very wealthy, owns a Greuze painting, uses accounts at six banks, and may keep wealth abroad. Famous among criminals, he rules with absolute severity, employs and pays Colonel Sebastian Moran six thousand a year, and is feared by members such as Fred Porlock. Holmes describes him as the controlling brain of the underworld. In the death of John Douglas, Moriarty's organization first located Douglas, then advised on the crime; after an agent failed, Moriarty personally intervened.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

3 sections; each heading opens the text

PART I / Chapter I

Professor Moriarty is described by Sherlock Holmes as the controlling criminal brain behind an extensive underworld organization. Fred Porlock fears that Moriarty suspects him of betraying the organization.

Chapter II

Professor Moriarty is presented as a highly learned and talented man whose unexplained wealth includes ownership of a valuable Greuze painting. Sherlock Holmes believes Moriarty is the hidden force behind London criminals, pays Colonel Sebastian Moran six thousand pounds a year, maintains numerous bank accounts, and rules his organization with a death penalty for disobedience.

Epilogue

Professor Moriarty is identified by Holmes as the criminal mastermind behind John Douglas's death. Holmes says that Moriarty first used his machinery to locate Douglas, then advised on the crime, and finally intervened personally after an agent failed.
