Holmes’s methods, close observation, reconstruction of motives, and attention to overlooked clues, drive every case, while Watson accompanies him, records events, and sometimes supplies the personal connection that brings a client. The collection opens with the disappearance of the racehorse Silver Blaze and John Straker’s death: Holmes clears Fitzroy Simpson, finds the horse concealed by Silas Brown, and proves Straker drugged Ned Hunter and tried to lame the horse before Silver Blaze killed him in self-defence. Other cases reveal Jim Browner’s jealous murder of his wife and Alec Fairbairn; Effie Munro’s concealed daughter Lucy and Grant Munro’s acceptance of them; and a fraud in which the Pinner brothers use Hall Pycroft’s handwriting to infiltrate Mawson & Williams. Holmes then recounts formative earlier cases. In the Gloria Scott matter, Victor Trevor’s father is exposed as James Armitage, a transported convict whose past aboard the mutinied ship returns through Hudson’s blackmail. In the Musgrave Ritual, Holmes follows an ancestral catechism to a hidden chamber, Brunton’s body, and a crown concealed for Charles II. Later investigations identify the Cunninghams as burglars and William Kirwan’s murderers; explain Colonel Barclay’s death through Henry Wood’s history of betrayal; and uncover Blessington as Sutton, an informer killed by former bank-robbery accomplices. The remaining cases broaden Holmes’s circle and stakes. Mycroft Holmes introduces the kidnapped Greek interpreter Melas, leading to the death of Paul Kratides and the escape of Sophy with her captors. Holmes restores Percy Phelps’s stolen naval treaty by catching Joseph Harrison retrieving it from beneath a floorboard. Finally, Holmes identifies Moriarty as the force behind extensive crime in London. Pursued across England and Europe, Holmes and Watson reach Reichenbach Falls, where Moriarty lures Watson away. Watson returns to find signs that Holmes and Moriarty struggled and fell into the chasm; neither body is recovered, but Holmes’s evidence exposes Moriarty’s gang.

I. Silver Blaze

Sherlock Holmes travels with John Watson from London to Dartmoor to investigate the disappearance of Silver Blaze, the favourite for the Wessex Cup, and the murder of trainer John Straker at King’s Pyland. Holmes reviews the known facts: a stranger drugged stable-boy Ned Hunter, Silver Blaze vanished, and Straker was found dead with a head wound and a cut thigh. Fitzroy Simpson, a bookmaker who had bet heavily against Silver Blaze, has been arrested because he visited the stables and possessed a weighted stick, but important details weaken the case against him. Holmes intends to test the police theory at the scene.

Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson travel with Colonel Ross to Winchester for the Wessex Cup, where Silver Blaze unexpectedly runs and wins the Wessex Plate disguised as a bay horse. Holmes reveals that the horse's markings were concealed by a faker and that Silver Blaze killed John Straker in self-defence. Holmes then directs Watson’s attention to a newspaper paragraph about a packet sent to Miss Cushing of Cross Street, Croydon. Arthur sends Pycroft to Birmingham with £100 and a written agreement, where Harry Pinner supposedly confirms the appointment and assigns him work compiling lists from a Paris directory. Pycroft realizes that Arthur and Harry are the same man and asks Holmes to explain why he was sent from London to Birmingham.

Hudson gained power over Mr. Trevor, and a strange coded message sent from Hampshire caused Mr. Trevor to suffer a fatal stroke. Brunton had been caught studying the family’s ritual document, while Rachel’s footprints led to the mere, where a linen bag containing rusted metal and dull pebbles was recovered. Sherlock Holmes and Dr. John Watson travel through Switzerland while Professor Moriarty, having escaped the London police, pursues Holmes.
