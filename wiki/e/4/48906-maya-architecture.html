Augustus Le Plongeon · 1881

Maya architecture uses stone in Yucatan to form mounds, cones, or pyramids of superposed platforms, often oriented toward cardinal points. Generally seven platforms rise by exterior staircases to the summit. Temples have thick walls, flat roofs, triangular-arched long narrow chambers, squared recesses, and an inner Sanctum Sanctorum.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

I will make a brief parallel between the architectural monuments of the primitiv

Maya architecture is described as using mounds of superposed square or oblong platforms, often oriented toward the cardinal points. Its temples generally contained two chambers, with the smaller inner chamber serving as the Sanctum Sanctorum.
