Book of Daniel in Vestiges of the Mayas, or, Facts tending to prove that Communications and Intimate Relations must have existed, in very remote times, between the inhabitants of Mayab and those of Asia and Africa

Augustus Le Plongeon · 1881

A book recounting King Belshazzar’s banquet, where “Mene, mene, Tekel, upharsin” appeared on a wall and eluded wise men until Daniel interpreted them as “numbered, numbered, weighed, dividing”; it also claims corresponding Maya meanings.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

If from the names of god and goddesses, we pass to that of places, we will find

The Book of Daniel recounts that the words “Mene, mene, Tekel, upharsin” appeared on the wall during King Belshazzar’s banquet and could not be read by the summoned wise men. Daniel interpreted them as “numbered, numbered, weighed, dividing,” and the text claims these meanings correspond to Maya words.
