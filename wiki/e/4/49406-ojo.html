  • In The Patchwork Girl of Oz: Ojo is a young Munchkin boy, Unc Nunkie’s nephew, who lives with him in a lonely forest house and is known by reputation, and sometimes by himself, as Ojo the Unlucky. Curious about the world despite limited knowledge of the Land of Oz, he leaves home with Unc Nunkie wanting to travel and see people. At Dr. Pipt’s home, Ojo secretly adds every bottle of qualities from the Brain Furniture to the Patchwork Girl’s brains. Soon Unc Nunkie and Margolotte are turned to marble, and Ojo, devastated and fearing his part in the accident, commits himself to restoring them. He undertakes a long quest for the Crooked Magician’s five required ingredients, traveling with Scraps, the Glass Cat, Woozy, and others. He seeks Woozy’s tail hairs, a six-leaved clover, water from a dark well, a drop of oil from a live man’s body, and the left wing of a yellow butterfly. His affection for Unc Nunkie drives him to persist through dangers, including a monstrous plant, Yoop, mines, and the Trick River. Although unable to swim, he helps his companions build and guide a raft. He finds the Woozy hairs, clover, dark-well water, and oil. Ojo knowingly picks the forbidden six-leaved clover despite warning that it violates Ozma’s law. He is arrested at the Emerald City gate, imprisoned, tried, and admits his guilt because he acted to save Unc Nunkie. After reflecting on his wrongdoing, he is forgiven and freed by Ozma. Near the end, he becomes utterly discouraged when the yellow butterfly wing seems unattainable and believes his quest has failed. Yet Unc Nunkie is restored after all. Ojo rejoices, receives with Unc Nunkie a nice house outside the Emerald City, and is protected by Ozma; the Tin Woodman considers him Ojo the Lucky.

Ojo in The Patchwork Girl of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1913-07-01

Ojo is a young Munchkin boy, Unc Nunkie’s nephew, who lives with him in a lonely forest house and is known by reputation, and sometimes by himself, as Ojo the Unlucky. Curious about the world despite limited knowledge of the Land of Oz, he leaves home with Unc Nunkie wanting to travel and see people. At Dr. Pipt’s home, Ojo secretly adds every bottle of qualities from the Brain Furniture to the Patchwork Girl’s brains. Soon Unc Nunkie and Margolotte are turned to marble, and Ojo, devastated and fearing his part in the accident, commits himself to restoring them. He undertakes a long quest for the Crooked Magician’s five required ingredients, traveling with Scraps, the Glass Cat, Woozy, and others. He seeks Woozy’s tail hairs, a six-leaved clover, water from a dark well, a drop of oil from a live man’s body, and the left wing of a yellow butterfly. His affection for Unc Nunkie drives him to persist through dangers, including a monstrous plant, Yoop, mines, and the Trick River. Although unable to swim, he helps his companions build and guide a raft. He finds the Woozy hairs, clover, dark-well water, and oil. Ojo knowingly picks the forbidden six-leaved clover despite warning that it violates Ozma’s law. He is arrested at the Emerald City gate, imprisoned, tried, and admits his guilt because he acted to save Unc Nunkie. After reflecting on his wrongdoing, he is forgiven and freed by Ozma. Near the end, he becomes utterly discouraged when the yellow butterfly wing seems unattainable and believes his quest has failed. Yet Unc Nunkie is restored after all. Ojo rejoices, receives with Unc Nunkie a nice house outside the Emerald City, and is protected by Ozma; the Tin Woodman considers him Ojo the Lucky.

Narrative, section by section

26 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter One

Ojo is a Munchkin boy who lives alone with Unc Nunkie. He notices that their food is nearly gone, asks why they are poor, and urges Unc Nunkie to travel to the Munchkin Country to find food.

Chapter Two

Ojo leaves his lonely forest home with Unc Nunkie because he wants to travel and see people. He learns that he is known as Ojo the Unlucky, meets Dr. Pipt and Dame Margolotte, and hears about the Powder of Life, the Glass Cat, Jack Pumpkinhead, and the Patchwork Girl.

Chapter Three

Ojo examines the Patchwork Girl and praises her mouth. He secretly adds powder from every bottle on the Brain Furniture shelf to the dish intended for her brains, then comforts himself with the thought that one cannot have too much cleverness.

Chapter Four

Ojo is identified as a boy who is small because he is young and is expected to grow larger with age. He is fascinated by the Glass Cat and eager to see the Patchwork Girl brought to life, but he becomes worried because he secretly added extra qualities to the brains sewn into her head.

Chapter Five

Ojo is devastated when Unc Nunkie is turned to marble and initially believes the accident may be his fault. He agrees to find the ingredients for the restoring compound and leaves at once with the Patchwork Girl and Bungle.

Chapter Six

Ojo travels with Scraps and the Glass Cat toward the Country of the Winkies to obtain the left wing of a yellow butterfly. He discovers that the Magician has given him enchanted bread and cheese that remain the same size when eaten, and he guides the group through the dark by tying a string to the Glass Cat. He enters the roadside house and falls asleep after Scraps is thrown outside.

Chapter Seven

Ojo wakes in a Munchkin house, washes, eats the breakfast left for him, and leaves with the Glass Cat. He objects to the phonograph joining the party, threatens to smash its record when it will not stop playing, and decides to continue straight ahead toward the Emerald City.

Chapter Eight

Ojo visits the Wise Donkey and Foolish Owl's house with the other travelers. He asks how to reach the Emerald City and learns that he should follow the path until it reaches the yellow brick road.

Chapter Nine

Ojo travels with Scraps and the Glass Cat while seeking the road of yellow bricks and items needed to save Unc Nunkie and Margolotte. He enters the Woozy's forest, feeds the Woozy bread and cheese, and asks for its three tail hairs, but cannot pull them out. He then agrees to take the Woozy to the Crooked Magician so the hairs can be removed there.

Chapter Ten

Ojo travels with Scraps, the Glass Cat, the Woozy, and Bungle toward the Emerald City. A monstrous plant captures him and holds him inside a folded leaf until the Shaggy Man cuts him free. Ojo then asks the Shaggy Man to rescue his companions.

Chapter Eleven

Ojo tells the Shaggy Man about the Crooked Magician's requirements and his attempt to save Unc Nunkie and Margolotte. He continues traveling toward the Emerald City, shares bread and cheese with the Woozy, and asks the Shaggy Man for a story.

Chapter Twelve

Ojo travels with the Shaggy Man, Scraps, the Woozy, and the Glass Cat toward the Emerald City while seeking what is needed to restore Unc Nunkie. He notices the road's backward-sliding trick, helps collect Chiss's quills, and uses a flesh-wound charm to heal the Shaggy Man's injured leg.

Chapter Thirteen

Ojo travels with the others through the optical-illusion barrier toward the Emerald City. He searches for a six-leaved clover despite the Shaggy Man's warning that picking one violates Ozma's law, but eventually returns to the road. He tells the Scarecrow of Oz about Unc Nunkie's transformation and insists that he will restore his uncle with or without Ozma's permission.

Chapter Fourteen

Ojo travels with the Shaggy Man, Scraps, the Woozy, and the Glass Cat toward the Emerald City. He secretly plucks a six-leaved clover for his efforts to restore Unc Nunkie to life, then is arrested at the city gate for allegedly breaking a Law of Oz.

Chapter Fifteen

Ojo is arrested for breaking a Law of Oz and is taken to prison in a white prisoner's robe and handcuffs. He initially feels ashamed and angry about his treatment, but he refuses to escape from Tollydiggle's house and begins considering his wrongdoing.

Chapter Sixteen

Ojo sets out to find the things needed for a magic charm that will restore Unc Nunkie and Margolotte to life. He finds the Woozy and takes him along, then gives Scraps his basket, apparently to keep the six-leaved clover from being found in his possession. He is arrested after being accused of wilfully breaking a Law of Oz, and Scraps insists that no one can prove he broke it.

Chapter Eighteen

Ojo is tried for picking a six-leaved clover after being warned not to do so. He admits his guilt, explains that he acted to save Unc Nunkie, is forgiven by Ozma, and begins preparing to search for water from a dark well.

Chapter Nineteen

Ojo travels with Dorothy's group and carries a basket containing supplies, including Square Meal Tablets and fruit. He is held down by Tottenhots during their attack, then lowers himself into a circular house and finds it has soft cushions and is not very deep.

Chapter Twenty

Ojo points out the warning sign about Yoop and advises returning when they see the giant’s cave. He escapes past Yoop but is knocked down when the giant throws the Scarecrow.

Chapter Twenty-One

Ojo offers to climb the mountain alone because the search for the dark well is on his account, but Dorothy insists that everyone go together. He helps enter the tunnel and asks about the Horner Country and the way to reach it.

Chapter Twenty-Three

Ojo climbs the ladder and pulls the Scarecrow away from the picket. He asks whether the Horners have a dark well and eagerly investigates when Diksey says there is one in the radium mine.

Chapter Twenty-Four

Ojo follows Diksey into the mine and slides with the others to the bottom of the tunnel. He finds water in the dark well, fills the gold flask, and carries the precious water in his pocket.

Chapter Twenty-Five

Ojo needs to find a yellow butterfly, so he travels with Dorothy and the others toward the Country of the Winkies. He suggests making a raft and helps strengthen it by nailing strips of wood across its logs.

Chapter Twenty-Six

Ojo is unable to swim and is discouraged by the Trick River's changing current. He catches a great fish with a gold nail used as a hook, causing the fish to pull the raft forward. He later helps raise the Scarecrow to see across the country and helps guide the raft toward shore.

Chapter Twenty-Seven

Ojo catches a drop of oil from the Tin Woodman's knee joint and completes four of the five tasks required for the magic charm. He becomes discouraged when the Tin Woodman refuses to let him obtain a yellow butterfly's left wing, believing that he cannot save Unc Nunkie. The Tin Woodman argues that Ojo's Friday birth, birth on the thirteenth day, left-handedness, and wart are not signs of bad luck.

Chapter Twenty-Eight

Ojo enters the room, kisses the statue of Unc Nunkie, and says that he did his best but failed to restore him. He is overjoyed when Unc Nunkie is restored, and Ozma gives him a house outside the Emerald City to share with his uncle.
