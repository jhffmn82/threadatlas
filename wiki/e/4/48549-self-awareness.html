Abstract written by a model

In RetrievalQA: Assessing Adaptive Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Short-form Open-Domain Question Answering, self-awareness ability concerns deciding when to retrieve external resources, and the RetrievalQA dataset evaluates it in LLMs. In Unified Active Retrieval for Retrieval Augmented Generation, the self-aware criterion uses TriviaQA to build model-specific IDK datasets by labeling questions according to sampled correctness, and self-awareness has the lowest single-classifier accuracy. In Unified Active Retrieval for Retrieval Augmented Generation, Self-awareness of LLMs means that large language models can be aware of what they know and do not know; corresponding datasets and alignment methods can enhance their uncertainty expression, refusal of questions beyond their knowledge scope, and recognition of unanswerable questions. In SEAKR: Self-aware Knowledge Retrieval for Adaptive Retrieval Augmented Generation, self-aware knowledge retrieval is the strategy evaluated for deciding when and how to use external knowledge, improving complex question-answering performance and generalizing across QA tasks without fine-tuning. In SEAKR: Self-aware Knowledge Retrieval for Adaptive Retrieval Augmented Generation, self-awareness is examined through the internal states of an LLM to support adaptive retrieval, with self-aware uncertainty extraction, re-ranking, and reasoning.
