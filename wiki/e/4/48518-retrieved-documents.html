Abstract written by a model

In Generate-then-Ground in Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Multi-hop Question Answering, retrieved documents supply evidence for grounding question-answer pairs and revising incorrect predictions, but their length, noise, and plausible irrelevant statements can mislead large language models. In R2AG: Incorporating Retrieval Information into Retrieval Augmented Generation, retrieved documents are passed to large language models in RAG and may contain noise or low-quality material; R2AG adds retrieval-information embeddings to help models use them during generation. In CODERAG-BENCH: Can Retrieval Augment Code Generation?, external programming sources, including tutorials, benefit code generation when they are retrieved. In MAIN-RAG: Multi-Agent Filtering Retrieval-Augmented Generation, retrieved documents are judged for relevance and collaboratively filtered and ranked by multiple LLM agents to improve relevant-information recall and reduce irrelevant content. In MAIN-RAG: Multi-Agent Filtering Retrieval-Augmented Generation, retrieved documents are evaluated through Document-Query-Answer triplets, scored, ranked, reordered, and filtered before the retained documents support the final prediction. In Question Decomposition for Retrieval-Augmented Generation, documents are retrieved separately for multi-hop questions and reranked because some may be overly specific, partly relevant, irrelevant, or misleading to large language models. In TurboRAG: Accelerating Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Precomputed KV Caches for Chunked Text, the document consists of external documents that are enclosed by markers, encoded for vector similarity search, and prefetched so their KV caches can later be retrieved and combined for a query.

retrieved documents in R2AG: Incorporating Retrieval Information into Retrieval Augmented Generation

Fuda Ye, Shuangyin Li, Yongqi Zhang, Lei Chen · 2024 · the work’s page · read it where retrieved documents first appears

Abstract written by a model

Retrieved documents are passed unidirectionally to large language models in RAG, usually in sequential or retriever preference order, but can include noise and low-quality material; long collections can obscure important information. Enhanced RAG methods use truncation, selection, evaluation, re-ranking, or compression. R2AG encodes each document and adds relevance, precedent-similarity, and neighbor-similarity retrieval-information embeddings to token embeddings. It is reported to help LLMs separate irrelevant documents, handle multiple documents, and use retrieval information during generation.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Introduction

Retrieved documents are passed to LLMs in RAG while primarily preserving sequential relationships. Low-quality documents can appear among them, and overly long document collections can make it difficult for LLMs to identify and use the most important information.

Related Works / Retrieval Augmented Generation .. Enhanced RAG

Retrieval results can contain noise and strongly affect the performance of Retrieval Augmented Generation. Enhanced methods attempt to improve them through truncation, selection, evaluation, re-ranking, and compression before they are provided to large language models.

R2AG / Problem Formulation and Overview .. Training Strategy

The documents are returned by the retriever in preference order and are encoded to obtain representations. Each document receives relevance, precedent similarity, and neighbor similarity features, and its corresponding retrieval-information embedding is prepended to its token embeddings.

Experiments / Datasets and Metrics .. Discussions

Retrieved documents are provided to LLMs in standard RAG and are processed using retrieval information in R2AG. R2AG is reported to help LLMs disentangle irrelevant documents and handle multiple documents.

Conclusion and Future Work

R2AG captures a comprehensive understanding of retrieved documents by incorporating retrieval information into the LLMs’ generation process.

Each document is encoded into an embedding vector and stored in a vector index for similarity search. An LLM also performs offline prefill on each document so that its KV cache can later be retrieved and combined for a query.
