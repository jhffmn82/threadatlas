Ra

deity · 2 works · 6 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: RA

Abstract written by a model

In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Ra is described as a hawk-headed sun deity who was the divine disposer and organizer of the world and was adored throughout Egypt. In Vestiges of the Mayas, or, Facts tending to prove that Communications and Intimate Relations must have existed, in very remote times, between the inhabitants of Mayab and those of Asia and Africa, Ra is described as a supreme sun god at the head of the Egyptian and Chaldean pantheons.

Augustus Le Plongeon · 1881 · the work’s page · read it where Ra first appears

Abstract written by a model

Ra is a god at the head of the Pantheon, described as a supreme sun god worshiped throughout Egypt. Its emblem is a disk or circle, sometimes topped by the serpent Uraeus. Ra is also placed at the head of the Chaldean pantheon and said to mean God in the highest sense, while related Maya terms mean that which exists, eternal truth, or truth.
