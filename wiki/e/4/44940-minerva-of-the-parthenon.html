Minerva of the Parthenon in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The Minerva of the Parthenon was a lost forty-foot statue made by Phidias for the temple of Minerva at Athens. Composed of ivory and gold in chryselephantine form, it portrayed Athene standing with a spear and a statue of Victory. It represented Athene as guardian of Athens and preserver of Hellas, and was designed as an outward symbol of her protecting divinity. Counted among Phidias’s greatest works, it and the Olympian Zeus inspired wonder and admiration throughout the Greek world.

Chapter XXVIII

The Minerva of the Parthenon was made by Phidias and stood in the temple of Minerva at Athens. It showed the goddess standing with a spear and a statue of Victory, and like the Olympian Jupiter it was made of ivory and gold; it is now lost.

SKETCH OF THE HISTORY OF GREEK SCULPTURE

The Athene of the Parthenon is identified as one of the splendid examples of ancient Greek plastic art. Pheidias portrayed Athene as the guardian deity of Athens and the preserver of Hellas.

The Athene Parthenos was one of Pheidias’s most wonderful statues and, with the Olympian Zeus, inspired the wonder and admiration of the Greek world. It was designed as an outward symbol of the divinity whose protecting power had enabled Athens to conquer and grow strong, and it was made of chryselephantine materials with nearly perfect form and intellectual beauty.
