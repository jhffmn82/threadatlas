Abstract written by a model

Maya language is described as an ancient language and the vernacular of the aborigines of Yucatan and other parts of Central America. It is said to have endured wars, invasions, political and religious upheavals, and Spanish attempts to suppress ancient customs, remaining in use and, in some localities, surviving in pristine purity. The records present individual Maya words, including Thallac, Axul, Mene, Tekel, Upharsin, MIZ, and a phrase translated as describing darkness covering a sinking speaker's face. They also state that it was known in Chaldea in remote ages and understood by some learned men in Judea at the beginning of the Christian era. Across the text, Maya is chiefly used as a source of etymologies: the author identifies inscriptions as Maya and employs its words to interpret names, religious terms, place-names, peoples, deities, and ancient expressions in Yucatan, Afghanistan, Babylon, Greece, Chaldea, Assyria, Phoenicia, and Egypt. The account further says the language changed through altered customs, invasions, and contact with other peoples, while claiming that the name Maya and names of tribes and cities in Afghanistan belong to it.

Maya language is in one work on this site, Vestiges of the Mayas, or, Facts tending to prove that Communications and Intimate Relations must have existed, in very remote times, between the inhabitants of Mayab and those of Asia and Africa. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.
