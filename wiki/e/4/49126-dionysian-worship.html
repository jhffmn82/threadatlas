the Dionysian revelations in The Bacchae of Euripides

Euripides · -0406/-0405

Dionysian revelations, given by Dionysus to the initiated, center on holy, fearful worship with ivy, fawn-skins, thyrsi, music, dancing, prayer, wine, mystic songs, blood, and ecstatic mountain rites. They bring joy, release from grief, and honor, while Pentheus spies on and opposes them before being destroyed.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

The Bacchae

Dionysian worship involves ivy crowns, fawn-skins, thyrsi, music, dancing, prayer, wine, and ecstatic mountain rites. Cadmus and Teiresias maintain that it can bring joy, release from grief, and honor to both gods and mortals.

CHORUS.

The Dionysian rites are portrayed as holy and fearful practices of the Maenads, involving dancing, mystic songs, wands, and blood. Pentheus attempts to spy on and oppose them, and is destroyed during their observance.

the worship of Dionysus in The Bacchae of Euripides

Euripides · -0406/-0405

The worship of Dionysus is a night-centered worship, sometimes practiced by day, performed by the Bacchae on Kithaeron. Women worship through dancing, songs, timbrels, mysteries, and cries to Dionysus; water, wine, milk, and honey accompany it. Dionysus calls its benefits beyond price, while its mysteries reject those who love sin, and even the mountain seems to join.

Dionysian worship in Hippolytus; The Bacchae

Euripides · -0427

Dionysian worship is a sacred divine service taught by Dionysus throughout the world, centered on his dances, rites, and mysteries. It is practiced by women in ecstatic mountain rites, including the Bacchic procession, dances, songs, music, prayer, sacrifice, wine, ivy crowns, fawn-skins, thyrsoi, timbrels, pipes, and sacred regalia. Its mysteries are restricted to chosen followers and reject sinners, while their emblems and benefits remain hidden from the uninitiated. Teiresias and Cadmus defend it as ancient and legitimate. Dionysus seeks Thebes’s recognition and participation in his divine rites.

Dionysus says he has taught his dances and rites throughout the world and has bound the women to the harness of his rites. He intends to make Thebes recognize and participate in them.
