the Cunninghams in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1892~/1893~

The Cunninghams are a father-and-son household employing coachman William Kirwan. They jointly write a letter, alternating words, then lie about Kirwan's killing by inventing a burglar, a forced door, a struggle, and an escape route. After allowing Holmes to inspect their house, they attack him to stop his exposure. Holmes identifies their shared note and staging; their guilt is apparent, and they are arrested.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

We passed the pretty cottage where the murdered man had lived,

The Cunninghams claim that a burglar forced the house door but did not enter, and they allow Holmes to inspect the house. They attack Holmes after he searches the rooms, and Holmes accuses them of murdering William Kirwan. Their expressions appear to the Inspector and Dr. Watson to show guilt, and they are arrested.

Sherlock Holmes was as good as his word, for about one o’clock he

The Cunninghams are shown to have written the note together, with the older man and younger man alternating words. They lied about the shooting and escape, staged the appearance of an unknown burglar, and attacked Holmes to prevent him from exposing them.
