Abstract written by a model

In The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes, Dartmoor is the wilderness surrounding Tavistock and the setting for the search for Silver Blaze and investigation of John Straker’s murder. In The Hound of the Baskervilles, Dartmoor surrounds Baskerville Hall and provides the dangerous, desolate setting for the legend, investigation, concealment, pursuit, and hound attack.

Dartmoor in The Hound of the Baskervilles

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1901 to 1902 · the work’s page · read it where Dartmoor first appears

Abstract written by a model

Dartmoor is a sparsely inhabited, desolate moor and plain around Baskerville Hall: a vast, bleak, granite-flecked landscape rolling to the horizon, marked by tors, cairns, boulders, cold wind, prehistoric huts, graves, monoliths, scattered farms and dwellings, and a nearby prison. It contains Grimpen Mire, dangerous bogland; many abandoned stone huts; sheep; Cleft Tor; Stapleton's abode; Selden in hiding; and the recurring strange cry. Its barrenness, broken ground, fog, rain, cliffs, slopes, gorse, and morass make travel, searching, and pursuit difficult and dangerous. From the beginning, it surrounds the Baskerville estate and frames the legend's pursuit and Hugo Baskerville's death. Sir Charles stopped at its gate before his death, where Dr. James Mortimer found footprints of Barrymore and an alleged gigantic hound. It becomes the source of the warning that Sir Henry should keep away, especially after dark, and Dr. Mortimer considers it unsafe for the Baskerville family. As Sir Henry approaches Baskerville Hall, the moor's grey, melancholy, jagged, isolated character intensifies the family's feared danger. It then becomes the setting for exploration, concealment, and surveillance. Stapleton claims extensive knowledge of it; Beryl warns Watson away; Barrymore watches it at night; and the Barrymores secretly supply Selden there. Watson and Sir Henry cross it, hear its frightening cry, and see a tall man on a tor. As weather worsens, it conceals Selden and another unidentified man. Watson searches its lonely huts and discovers that Holmes has used one as a secret retreat. Later, screams are heard, Selden's body is found, and finally the moor provides the fog-bound ground for the ambush, the hound's attack, and the pursuit.

Chapter 2.

The moor surrounds the Baskerville estate and is the setting of the legend's pursuit and Hugo Baskerville's death. Sir Charles Baskerville stopped at a gate leading onto the moor before his death, and Dr. James Mortimer found no other footprints on the ground near the route except those of Barrymore and the alleged gigantic hound.

Chapter 3.

Dartmoor is presented as a desolate, lifeless moor containing Baskerville Hall, Grimpen, scattered farms, and the prison at Princetown. Holmes studies a large-scale map of it while assessing where the tragedy occurred and whether danger remains there.

Chapter 4.

The moor is the place from which the warning message tells Sir Henry to keep away. Holmes and Dr. Mortimer consider the warning evidence that someone knows more than they do about what happens there.

Chapter 5.

Dartmoor is the region where Baskerville Hall is located and where Sir Henry Baskerville is expected to go. Holmes says he cannot be absent from London for an indefinite time to travel there.

Chapter 6.

The moor first appears to the travelers as a grey, melancholy hill with a jagged summit. As they approach Baskerville Hall, it becomes a vast, bleak, desolate landscape of tors, cairns, boulders, and cold wind. Sherlock Holmes warns Sir Henry to avoid it during the hours of darkness.

Chapter 7.

The moor is portrayed as vast, barren, mysterious, and filled with unusual natural and prehistoric features. Jack Stapleton says he has explored it extensively, while Beryl warns Dr. Watson to leave it.

The moor is described as bleak, granite-flecked, and stretching unbroken to the horizon. Watson associates it with melancholy, vague fears, and the danger believed to threaten the Baskerville family.

Chapter 8.

The moor is portrayed as vast, grim, isolated, and filled with prehistoric huts, graves, and monoliths. It provides hiding places for the escaped convict and forms the dark landscape watched by Barrymore during his nighttime vigil.

Chapter 9.

The moor can be viewed most clearly from the western window of Baskerville Hall, and Barrymore appears to watch it at night. Sir Henry and Miss Stapleton meet there, where she warns that it is a place of danger.

The moor is where Selden hides while the Barrymores secretly provide him with food. Watson and Sir Henry cross it during their pursuit, hear a frightening cry there, and observe a mysterious tall man on a tor.

Chapter 10.

The moor is described as cold, rain-soaked, foggy, and increasingly dangerous, with the firm uplands becoming a morass. It conceals Selden and another unidentified man, and its noises and apparent watchers continue to trouble Watson and the household.

Chapter 11.

The moor contains the clue to the identity or whereabouts of the lurking man. After the inconclusive interview with Mrs. Laura Lyons, the narrator decides to return to that line of investigation.

And that was a most vague direction. I realised it as I drove

The moor contains many abandoned huts and is described as lonely, barren, and difficult to search. Watson crosses it to reach the hill and the hut where the stranger lives.

Chapter 12.

The moor is where Sherlock Holmes has maintained a secret retreat in a hut while investigating the case. It is also the setting in which Dr. John Watson searches for information and later meets Holmes at dusk.

A terrible scream—a prolonged yell of horror and anguish—burst

The moor is the dark, desolate setting where the screams are heard and Selden’s body is found. Its broken ground, rocks, cliffs, slopes, and gorse make the search difficult and contribute to the danger of the pursuit.

Chapter 14.

The moor is the dark setting for the ambush, the hound’s attack, and the pursuit. The fog gradually covers much of it, making movement and visibility increasingly dangerous.

Chapter 15.

Stapleton returned to Dartmoor after realizing that Holmes had taken over the London investigation. The Stapletons later went down to Devonshire, where Sir Henry and Watson followed them.
