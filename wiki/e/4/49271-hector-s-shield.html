Hector’s shield

object · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: Dear Hector's shield, Hector's shield, Mother-Shield, O brazen orb, dear shield of Hector, great bronze-fronted shield, guardian true of Hector's fair Left arm, shield, that orbed shield, this shield, war-shield

Abstract written by a model

In Hecuba and other plays, Hector’s shield guards Hector’s arm in battle, bears his shape, and is given to Hecuba, who wreathes it for Astyanax’s burial. In The Trojan Women of Euripides, Hector’s shield is a bronze-fronted, battle-worn war shield that protects Astyanax as a burial covering and carries him to the grave.

Abstract written by a model

Hector’s brazen, refulgent shield guarded his arm in battle, bears his shape, and is given to Hecuba, who wreathes it for Astyanax’s burial.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

1 section; each heading opens the text

TALTHYBIUS, HECUBA, CHORUS.

Hector’s shield, once used to guard Hector’s arm in battle, is given to Hecuba for Astyanax’s burial. Hecuba adorns it with wreaths and treats it as more honorable than the arms of Ulysses.

Hector's shield in The Trojan Women of Euripides

Abstract written by a model

Hector's bronze-fronted, battle-worn war shield, once feared in battle, bears his hand and sweat, protects Astyanax as a burial covering, and carries the child to the grave.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

[TALTHYBIUS, _followed by one or two Soldiers and bearing the child_

Hector's bronze-fronted shield, once feared in battle, is given to Astyanax as his final house. It carries the marks of Hector's hand and sweat and bears the child's body to the grave.
