Russian Revolution in Dr. Elsie Inglis

Lady Frances Balfour · 1918

The Russian Revolution is a political upheaval in Russia associated with changes reported from Petrograd and a major reverberation that altered the tone of Russian soldiers. Russian officers and patients supported the change, though revolutionary conditions fostered suspicion during a spy incident. The upheaval imperilled the Serbian Army on the Rumanian front, cut communications with Dr. Inglis’s unit, and led Inglis to send members home with verbal messages while the London Committee awaited news and urged her return.

The Russian Revolution is associated with the changes reported from Petrograd. Elsie observes that Russian officers and patients support the change and that revolutionary conditions contribute to suspicion during the spy incident.

‘_On Active Service.

The unit refers to the great reverberation that has shaken Russia and to a new tone among Russian soldiers. It does not name the Russian Revolution directly, but connects the soldiers’ changed attitude with the upheaval in Russia.
