Abstract written by a model

Bacchus’s wine is Maron’s unmingled gift, carried by Ulysses and associated with Bacchus. Silenus trades cheese and lambs for it and drinks eagerly. Ulysses repeatedly gives it to Polyphemus, who becomes drunk, staggers, sees visions, and sleeps, enabling his blinding.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE CYCLOPS.

Bacchus’s wine is carried by Ulysses and identified as a gift from Maron, whom Silenus once held as a child. Silenus eagerly drinks it and exchanges it for cheese and lambs, despite Polypheme’s authority.

ULYSSES, CHORUS.

Ulysses gives Polyphemus unmixed wine from Maron’s supply and uses repeated cups to make him drunk. The wine causes Polyphemus to stagger, see the gods and Ganymede, and fall into sleep, enabling the blinding.
