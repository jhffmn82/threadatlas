William Kirwan in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1892~/1893~

William Kirwan is the Cunninghams’ longtime coachman, described as a faithful servant, who becomes their blackmailer after secretly following them during their raid on Mr. Acton’s house. Alec Cunningham decoys and shoots him when he threatens exposure. Kirwan is found shot through the heart after apparently confronting a burglar, dies without speaking, and leaves a fragment of a note; Holmes concludes that the Cunninghams murdered him.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

VII. The Reigate Squires

William Kirwan, the Cunninghams' coachman, was shot through the heart while confronting a burglar at the house. He died without speaking, and a fragment of a note was found between his finger and thumb; he had received a letter by the afternoon post on the day of his death.

We passed the pretty cottage where the murdered man had lived,

William Kirwan is identified as the Cunninghams’ coachman and the murdered man. He was found wounded after apparently tackling a burglar, and Holmes concludes that the Cunninghams murdered him.

Sherlock Holmes was as good as his word, for about one o’clock he

William Kirwan secretly followed the Cunninghams during their raid on Mr. Acton's house and threatened to expose them. He used this knowledge to levy blackmail, so Alec decoyed him and shot him.
