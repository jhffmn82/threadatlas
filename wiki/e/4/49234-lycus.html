Lycus in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Lycus is a tyrant who becomes ruler of Thebes by invading amid civil tumult, murdering Creon, and seizing the throne. He seeks to prevent vengeance by ordering the execution of Megara, Amphitryon, and the children of Hercules, disparaging Hercules and planning to burn the family. Though he allows Megara to prepare the children, he enters the palace to bring them out and is killed there by Hercules in an ambush of swords.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

HERCULES DISTRACTED.

Lycus has killed Creon, seized the throne of Thebes, and threatens to kill Megara, Amphitryon, and the children of Hercules. He disparages Hercules’ achievements and calls the bow a weapon of a coward. He orders wood to be gathered so the family can be burned, but permits Megara to enter Hercules’ house and dress the children before their deaths.

HERCULES, MEGARA, AMPHITRYON, CHORUS.

Lycus rules Thebes after seizing the sceptre through revolt and orders Megara, Amphitryon, and the children to appear for execution. He enters the palace to bring them out but is killed there by Hercules.
