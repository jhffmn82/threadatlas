Glass Cat in The Patchwork Girl of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1913-07-01

Glass Cat is a living, transparent glass cat and magical creature made by Dr. Pipt with his first batch of Powder of Life. Created partly so ordinary cats would not drink too much milk, she has lived ever since at the Magician's house, without growing. Her clear glass body contains pink brains, emerald eyes, a hard blood-red ruby heart, and a beautiful spun-glass tail. She can see in darkness, cannot eat, and her body cannot be scratched by quills. Proud, intelligent, saucy, sarcastic, and permanently inconsiderate, Glass Cat admires herself and believes her visible brains, heart, and transparent body make her superior. Her ruby heart matches her hard-hearted disposition. She finds Dr. Pipt and Margolotte boring, disdains the Patchwork Girl as a bundle of scraps, refuses to catch mice because it is undignified, and dislikes both classical music and rag-time. After previously remaining near the Magician's house, Glass Cat joins Ojo, Scraps, and later Patchwork Girl on the Journey. She leads them to a roadside house but is frightened by an unseen voice. She sleeps in a Munchkin house, enters the Woozy's forest through the fence bars, warns about the Woozy's hairs, and joins in shouting "Krizzle-Kroo!" Later she tells Dorothy of her creation and years with Dr. Pipt, and Dorothy has her taken to another room for the night.

Glass Cat in The Patchwork Girl of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1913-07-01

The Glass Cat is a living, transparent cat made entirely of brittle glass, a curious creation of Dr. Pipt. Her pink brains are visible through her body, along with her finely polished ruby heart and glass paws; she sees poorly by day and fears hard knocks. Made alive by an early batch of the Powder of Life, she enters Dr. Pipt's home, hides during an accident, mocks others, and joins Ojo and the Patchwork Girl. She is rescued after a monstrous plant traps her in a leaf, then boasts of her visible brains and beauty, comparing herself favorably to Scraps. She follows the Woozy through an optical-illusion barrier and refuses to remain at a farmhouse as children's plaything, preferring Ojo's adventures because children might smash her. At the end, her pink brains are replaced by transparent brains, changing her from conceited and disagreeable to modest and well behaved. Ozma keeps her as a palace pet.

the Glass Cat in The Magic of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1919

The Glass Cat is a magical, transparent crystal tabby made entirely of glass by Dr. Pipt before Ozma forbade magic; it is also described as made by a careless magician. Its body includes delicate pink jewel-like brains, a cold, hard ruby heart, large emerald eyes, a beautiful spun-glass tail, sharp glass claws and teeth, and glass feet. When clean, its moving brains and heart can be seen through its crystal body. It has no stomach or machinery for eating, so it neither eats mice nor feels hunger or thirst, despite having been made to catch mice. Tireless, free to move on the Magic Isle, and able to walk under a river, it is vain about its appearance and readily persuaded by praise. It knows Oz, including every path in the Great Forest of Gugu, hidden places, and the remote Munchkin Country location of the Magic Flower. It has visited the Isle of the Magic Flower, seen the flower bloom, and identifies its gold flower-pot. It tells Dorothy, Trot, and Cap'n Bill where the flower is hidden and guides Trot and Cap'n Bill to the island. After roots trap them there, Trot's promise to praise it as a hero persuades the Glass Cat to walk along the river bottom and seek the Wizard of Oz. Though it does not want Trot and Cap'n Bill to know it is sorry, it is troubled by having led them to the island. It travels to the Great Forest of Gugu, learns that the Wizard and Dorothy have been transformed, and helps search for the black bag and the Goose. It climbs an avocado tree, finds the black bag among leaves within five minutes, hides it, and leads the Wizard to it, demonstrating that its pink brains are effective hunters. It then guides travelers toward the river and reaches Trot and Cap'n Bill under the water, helping bring the Wizard to save them. Later, it teases monkeys by pulling their tails. They cover it in dark blue mud, making it dull blue and unable to see or hear until Dorothy cleans its eyes and ears. It washes in a nearby lake and is later found at the Royal Palace pretending not to notice the travelers. It is friends with Trot and Cap'n Bill despite its disagreeable ways and rivals the Pink Kitten over flesh and glass.

Narrative, section by section

8 sections; each heading opens the text

5. A Happy Corner of Oz

The Glass Cat is a transparent creature made by Dr. Pipt, with pink balls for brains, a ruby heart, emerald eyes, and a spun-glass tail. It tells Dorothy, Trot, and Cap'n Bill where the Magic Flower is hidden and agrees to guide them there.

9. The Isle of the Magic Flower

The Glass Cat guides Trot and Cap'n Bill to the river beside the Isle of the Magic Flower. It explains that it does not need food or water, describes its visible pink brains, recounts how Kalidahs once failed to hurt it, and identifies the gold flower-pot on the island.

10. Stuck Fast

The Glass Cat crosses the river on the raft and remains able to walk freely on the island. It observes that roots have grown from Trot's and Cap'n Bill's feet into the ground. After being persuaded by Trot's promise that it will be praised as a hero, it leaves by walking along the river bottom to tell the Wizard of Oz what happened.

13. The Loss of the Black Bag

The Glass Cat feels troubled that its actions led Trot and Cap'n Bill to the Isle of the Magic Flower, though it does not want them to know it is sorry. It travels quickly from the Emerald City to the Great Forest of Gugu, learns that the Wizard and Dorothy have been transformed, and agrees to help search for the bag and the Goose.

16. The Glass Cat Finds the Black Bag

The Glass Cat climbs an avocado tree to observe the giant soldiers and discovers the Wizard's black bag hanging from a limb. She hides it under leaves, then leads the Wizard to it and proves that her pink brains are effective hunters. She also tells the Wizard that Trot and Cap'n Bill are in trouble.

17. A Remarkable Journey

The Glass Cat guides the travelers across the mountains and through the forest toward the river, relying on its knowledge of the Land of Oz. When tangled growth blocks the way, it admits that it has not traveled that exact route before; later, it walks under the river to tell Trot and Cap'n Bill to be patient.

19. Dorothy and the Bumble Bees

The Glass Cat is credited with discovering the Magic Flower, leading Trot and Cap'n Bill to it, and bringing the Wizard to save them. Its glass feet are noted as unaffected by the Magic Isle's enchantment.

20. The Monkeys Have Trouble

The Glass Cat repeatedly pulls the monkeys' tails for amusement, causing them to retaliate by covering her in dark blue mud. The mud makes her dull blue and prevents her from seeing or hearing until Dorothy cleans her eyes and ears. the Glass Cat in Glinda of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1920

The Glass Cat is a transparent glass cat made and brought to life by Dr. Pipt, with a visible ruby heart, pink whirling brains, emerald eyes, and a spun-glass fluffy tail. Intelligent but extremely lazy, unpleasant at times, and devoted to admiration, she joins a rescue expedition, survives a leopard attack, obeys Glinda, finds Button Bright, and complains of his impoliteness.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter Fifteen

The Glass Cat joins the rescue expedition and is attacked by an enormous leopard in the Great Gillikin Forest, but the leopard breaks several teeth and drops her unharmed. Glinda orders her to find Button Bright, and because she fears Glinda, she obeys and returns with him. The Glass Cat complains that Button Bright was impolite to her.
