Hermione’s marriage in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · about 429 BC · the work’s page · read it where Hermione’s marriage first appears

Abstract written by a model

Hermione’s marriage is a bridal union whose rights Menelaus defends during her husband's absence. Her father first betrothed her to Orestes, then promised her to Achilles’ son, who married her after Troy but loves captive rival Andromache; Andromache predicts Hermione's rejection.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

MENELAUS, MOLOSSUS, ANDROMACHE, CHORUS.

Menelaus says that Hermione’s bridal rights must be defended while her long-absent husband is away. Andromache predicts that Hermione will be rejected by the prince of Phthia and asks who would marry her afterward.

THE NURSE OF HERMIONE, CHORUS.

Orestes says that Hermione’s father first betrothed her to Orestes and later promised her to Achilles’ son if Troy were conquered. Achilles’ son then married Hermione after returning from Troy, while also loving Andromache, whom Hermione calls a captive rival.
