Carians in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The Carians were a group settled near Miletus before the Cretans, sharing the country with them and holding much of the land around Claros when the first Greeks arrived. They mixed with Greeks at Teos and Erythræ, but were driven from several cities and from Colophon’s territory.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II.

The Carians had settled near Miletus before the arrival of Miletus and the Cretans and shared the country with them. They were later driven from Miletus, Myus, and Priene by the Ionians or their associated settlers.

CHAPTER III.

The Carians possessed most of the country around Claros when the first Greeks arrived. They were also mixed with the Greeks at Teos and Erythræ, and Mopsus later drove them altogether from the territory associated with Colophon.

Carians in Vestiges of the Mayas, or, Facts tending to prove that Communications and Intimate Relations must have existed, in very remote times, between the inhabitants of Mayab and those of Asia and Africa

Augustus Le Plongeon · 1881

Carians are described as an ancient, roving, piratical people of Asia Minor with unknown origin. They were reportedly mocked for their Greek speech; the author claims they may have spoken Maya or a Maya dialect and compares their customs with those of Yucatan.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

When, on January 28, 1873, I had the honor of reading a paper before the New Yor

The Carians are described as an ancient roving and piratical people of Asia Minor whose origin was considered unknown. The text claims that they may have spoken Maya or a Maya dialect, compares their customs with those of Yucatan, and says that Herodotus described them as being mocked for the way they spoke Greek.
