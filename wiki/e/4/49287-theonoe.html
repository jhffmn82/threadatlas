Theonoe in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Theonoe is a prophetic woman, daughter of Proteus and of Nereus’s line, whose divine knowledge reveals past, present, and future. She knows Menelaus has survived, reached Egypt, and is destined, with Helen, for a safe return to Sparta despite Venus’s opposition. When Helen and Menelaus seek her counsel, she places justice, the gods, and her father’s honor above loyalty to her brother Theoclymenus. She conceals Menelaus’s arrival, withholds the truth from Theoclymenus, protects Helen and Menelaus, and urges their escape. Theoclymenus later accuses her of silence, but the Dioscuri affirm that her piety and obedience to the gods made her choice wise.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

HELEN.

Theonoe is the daughter of Proteus and a woman who knows the gods’ counsels concerning the present and the future. Helen and the Chorus plan to question her about whether Menelaus is alive.

MENELAUS.

Theonoe, described as prophetic, tells Helen that Menelaus is alive and has reached the Egyptian coast after many sufferings. Helen regrets not asking her whether Menelaus would arrive safely.

CHOR. My sentiments on prophets well accord

Theonoe prophesies that Menelaus has arrived in Egypt and that Juno wishes to return him and Helen safely to Sparta, while Venus wants to prevent his return. She chooses justice and loyalty to her father’s honor, promises not to reveal Menelaus’s presence, and urges the pair to escape.

Swift bark of Sidon, by whose dashing oars

Theonoe is accused by Theoclymenus of failing to tell him that Menelaus was alive. The Dioscuri defend her as pious and obedient to the gods and say that she acted wisely.
