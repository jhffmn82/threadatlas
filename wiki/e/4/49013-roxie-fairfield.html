Roxie Fairfield in Pen Pictures of Eventful Scenes and Struggles of Life

B. F. Craig · 1880

Roxie Fairfield, also known as Roxie Daymon, is Tom Fairfield’s eldest daughter, a young, intelligent woman of unstained character. Educated briefly each year at a country school, she becomes the heroine of a snowstorm when she keeps her sisters warm and leaves their cabin to find help; Aunt Katy’s party rescues them, and Aunt Fillis Foster later takes her in. Roxie runs away with her lover Daymon, marries him by common law, and helps build a wealthy life in Chicago after leaving Louisville. She has one daughter and dies of cholera, unaware of Susan Lasco’s fate. After her disappearance, she is believed drowned, but appears alive in court after Daymon’s conviction.

Roxie Daymon in Pen Pictures of Eventful Scenes and Struggles of Life

B. F. Craig · 1880

Roxie Daymon, also called Roxie Fairfield, is a near relative of the Simon family and part of its hidden history. During the first year of the war, she lives in Chicago, is reputed rich despite receiving no Simon-estate dividends, and remains loyal to the federal government. She consults Governor Morock about contesting the Simon will and seeking plantation cotton. Later, married to a young mechanic, she lives happily in a cottage and reads The Workman's Saturday Night to forget the past; her possible survival or descendants torment S. S. Simon.

SCENE SIXTH.

Roxie Daymon, also called Roxie Fairfield, is connected to the hidden history of S. S. Simon's family. S. S. Simon believes that she or her descendants may still survive, and her name torments him during his final illness.

SCENE SEVENTH--WAR BETWEEN THE STATES.

Roxie Daymon spends the summer and fall in Chicago with a reputation for wealth, although she receives no dividends from the Simon estate. She consults Governor Morock about challenging the Simon will and authorizes him to try to obtain cotton from the Simon plantations.
