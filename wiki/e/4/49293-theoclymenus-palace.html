Theoclymenus’ palace in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Theoclymenus’ palace is a splendid, wealthy royal residence in Pharos, belonging to Proteus and later ruled by his son. Proteus’ tomb stands at its entrance. Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

HELEN.

Theoclymenus’ palace is a splendid royal residence at the entrance to which Proteus’ tomb stands. Helen lives there under the protection formerly provided by Proteus, while Theoclymenus seeks to marry her.

MENELAUS.

Proteus’s palace is a wealthy and imposing residence with gilded pinnacles and grand gates. Menelaus approaches it seeking aid, but the female servant warns him that Greeks are forbidden to enter and that the palace is dangerous.

CHOR. My sentiments on prophets well accord

The barbarian palace belongs to the royal Proteus’ son and contains Theonoe, who can reveal Menelaus’s presence to her brother. Helen has lived there among the king’s servants and plans to use that familiarity to obtain help for the escape.
