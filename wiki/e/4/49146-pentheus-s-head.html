Pentheus's head

object · 2 works · 1 quoted fact · 1 connection
Also called: a lion's, his head, lion's head, the head, the prize, the trophy, this thing

Abstract written by a model

In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, Pentheus's head is carried from Kithaeron by Agave, who mistakes it for a lion's head and intends to display it on the palace, until Cadmus makes her recognize it as the head of her son. In The Bacchae of Euripides, Pentheus's head is a trophy from the Bacchic hunt, carried by Agave on a wand; she mistakes it for a lion's head until Cadmus makes her recognize it as her son's severed head.

Agave carries Pentheus's head on a wand as if it were the head of a lion. She brings it toward the walls of Thebes while calling upon Bromios and celebrating the deed.

CHORUS.

Agave carries Pentheus's severed head from Kithaeron, mistaking it for the head of a lion and intending to display it above the palace entrance. Cadmus makes her look at it until she recognizes that it is the head of her son.
