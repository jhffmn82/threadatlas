Pentheus's head in The Bacchae of Euripides

Euripides · -0406/-0405

Pentheus's head is a trophy from the Bacchic hunt, carried by Agave pierced on a wand. Mistaking it for a lion's head, she brings it from Kithaeron toward Thebes to display it; Cadmus makes her recognize it as her son's severed head.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS.

Agave carries Pentheus's head on a wand as if it were the head of a lion. She brings it toward the walls of Thebes while calling upon Bromios and celebrating the deed.

CHORUS.

Agave carries Pentheus's severed head from Kithaeron, mistaking it for the head of a lion and intending to display it above the palace entrance. Cadmus makes her look at it until she recognizes that it is the head of her son.
