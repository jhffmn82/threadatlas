Uxmal

Also called: Uxmal and Chichen, Uxmal contained, ruined city of Uxmal

Abstract written by a model

Uxmal is a ruined Maya city near a low range of hills with possible marble quarries and red iron oxide, and with aguadas about a mile away. It is called U LUUMIL KIN, “the land of the sun,” a designation also identified by its sanctuary’s coat-of-arms and escutcheon. The city is chiefly defined by its monuments: temples, palaces, mounds, inscriptions, sculptures, bas-reliefs, mural paintings, and buildings including the Governor’s house, the palace of the priests, and the monument called the Dwarf’s House. Its monuments include sculptured marble ornaments, mastodon-head ornaments, red imprints of open upraised hands, a temple of death, a sacrificial altar, cynocephali, and figures including Aac, whose statue is described as surrounded by the Sun. Water was managed through artificial reservoirs on monument terraces and courts. Buildings are described as unhewn-stone filling enclosed by thick hewn-stone walls, with successively diminishing platforms upward. Uxmal is said to have been founded by Cans together with Chichen, while another account says Nahualts established their capital there and set religious symbols in palace courts and temple platforms. Cay, a high priest, a serpent-linked effigy, Can’s family, seven mounds, and the number seven are associated with it. Surveying and photographs of its ruins, along with discoveries at the Dwarf’s House, are presented as evidence for Maya religious symbolism, writing, architecture, initiation mysteries, and phallic worship. Its monuments and sculptures are also compared with those of Asia.

Uxmal is in one work on this site, Vestiges of the Mayas, or, Facts tending to prove that Communications and Intimate Relations must have existed, in very remote times, between the inhabitants of Mayab and those of Asia and Africa. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.
