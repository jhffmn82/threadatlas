Abstract written by a model

Senotes are immense circular holes, 50 to 500 feet across and 50 to 150 feet deep, usually containing subterranean water. They may have currents, be dry, or hold sulphurous thermal mineral water; local stories attribute unusual properties to their waters.

Vestiges of the Mayas

Senotes are immense circular holes, usually containing water, with diameters of about 50 to 500 feet and perpendicular walls 50 to 150 feet deep. Some have currents, some are dry, and some contain thermal mineral water with sulphurous odor and vapor; local stories attribute unusual properties to their waters.
