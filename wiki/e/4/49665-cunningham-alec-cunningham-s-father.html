Cunningham (Alec Cunningham's father) in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1892~/1893~

Cunningham, Alec Cunningham’s father, is an elderly Justice of the Peace who owns and lives in the Cunningham house. During the Cunningham burglary, he claims to hear the alarm, see a fleeing burglar, and guide Holmes and Inspector Forrester through the grounds and house. He corrects the reported time while signing a reward notice, drawing suspicion. Holmes identifies him as the older writer of a note and Alec’s accomplice in William Kirwan’s murder. After accusation, restraint, and arrest, Cunningham confesses that he and Alec committed the burglary, lured and shot Kirwan, and tried to hide their roles.

VII. The Reigate Squires

Mr. Cunningham is a Justice of the Peace whose house was entered during the Cunningham burglary. He heard the alarm, saw the fleeing burglar from his bedroom window, and later showed Holmes and Inspector Forrester where the burglar crossed the garden hedge.

We passed the pretty cottage where the murdered man had lived,

Mr. Cunningham, the older Cunningham and a J.P., explains where he was during the incident and shows Holmes through his house. He signs a reward notice after correcting the time of the attempted burglary from a quarter to one to a quarter to twelve. After Holmes accuses him and Alec of murdering William Kirwan, he is restrained and arrested.
