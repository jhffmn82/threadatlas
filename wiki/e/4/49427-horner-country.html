Horner Country in The Patchwork Girl of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1913-07-01

Horner Country lies beyond the mountain from Hopper Country, called by Hip Hopper a black spot. A path from rocky lowlands may lead to a dark well at its entrance. It occupies half the great cave, with dull gray rock, square houses, crowded streets of Horners, and a city larger than Hopper village.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Twenty-One

The Horner Country is on the other side of the mountain and is described by Hip Hopper as a black spot on the face of the earth. A dark well may be there, and another path from the rocky lowlands leads directly to its entrance.

Chapter Twenty-Two

Horner Country occupies the other half of the great cave and has dull gray rock walls, a gray rock roof, and plain square houses. Its streets are crowded with Horners, including the people who gather around Scraps when she lands there.
