Abstract written by a model

The Rough Diamond is a collection of frontier observations and connected tales of American character, centered on families, inheritance, violence, migration, and war from the early nineteenth-century western and southern frontier through the War Between the States. It opens with the narrator’s travels through Bush Bayou, across the Mississippi, and to Helena for the race between Silver Heels and the Spotted Buck, alongside accounts of Alonzo Phelps’s failed escape from execution and Daymon’s trial, overturned when his supposedly drowned wife appears alive. At Shirt-Tail Bend, Don and Dan Carlo’s wood-yard partnership collapses because Dan wastes money. Brindle Bill, S. S. Simon, and Sundown Hill try to swindle him, but Don fights for the money; Dan later dies, while Don is presumed dead or dying after the Red Stone explodes and is remembered as the Hero of Shirt-Tail Bend. The story then follows Tom Fairfield’s three daughters, Roxie, Rose, and Suza, separated after their impoverished father disappears in a snowstorm. Roxie becomes Roxie Daymon and dies of cholera; Rose is used by Brindle Bill and S. S. Simon in their effort to secure the Lasco inheritance, then dies childless of yellow fever. Suza escapes captivity, inherits Aunt Katy Demitt’s property, and later becomes engaged to Don Carlo, who recognizes her as his rescuer from the Red Stone wreck. A second generation centers on the Simon estate. Young Simon, a wealthy but dying planter, becomes engaged to Roxie Daymon’s grown daughter and wills her his estate, worth more than a million dollars. His cousin Cæsar Simon, also called Cole Conway, is identified as an heir by Steve Brindle and hires Gov. Morock to contest Simon’s will. Against a background of expanding cotton, slavery, railroads, and telegraphs, Cæsar serves in General Sterling Price’s army, is wounded at Wilson’s Creek, and is haunted by the number 77 and his treatment of Steve. The war destroys Missouri, Arkansas, and the Simon plantations. At Helena on July 4, 1863, General Holmes’s attack fails; Cæsar falls, ending the Simon male line. After the Southern surrender, Simon land is sold to satisfy judgments, and the remaining figures enter separate postwar lives.

Craig Kansas City, Missouri 1880 [Ill cover] [Ill 0020] SCENE FIRST--INTRODUCTION.

The narrator introduces the Rough Diamond as a collection of true-to-life observations about American character and recounts experiences from the early nineteenth-century western and southern frontier. He describes traveling by canoe through Bush Bayou, crossing the Mississippi River, and reaching Helena, Arkansas, in time to see the race between Silver Heels and the Spotted Buck. He also recalls Alonzo Phelps, who escaped before his execution in Vicksburg but was shot and killed, and whose confession described a murder in Europe. Finally, he recounts Daymon's murder trial in Louisville, Kentucky, based on circumstantial evidence that his wife had drowned, until Daymon's wife unexpectedly entered the courtroom alive.

SCENE SECOND.

Don and Dan Carlo operate a wood yard at Shirt-Tail Bend, but Dan spends his share of the proceeds while Don pays the expenses. Brindle Bill, S. S. Simon, and Sundown Hill plot to swindle Dan at headquarters, where Don interrupts them and takes the money from the table. Don fights Brindle Bill until Simon's wife sets fire to the cabin, ending the contest and helping Don keep the money. Dan later dies in a saloon, while Don is identified among the dead or dying after the steamboat Red Stone explodes; he is remembered as the Hero of Shirt-Tail Bend.

” SCENE THIRD--THE SEPARATED SISTERS.

Tom Fairfield, a restless Virginian settler in Kentucky, moves through several occupations, becomes increasingly fond of whisky, and struggles with poverty after his wife dies. He and his wife have three daughters, Roxie, Rose, and Suza, but after Tom disappears during a severe snowstorm, the girls are left alone in their cabin. Roxie reaches Aunt Katy, who sends Joe and Dick to rescue the sisters. A settlers’ council then separates the children among Aunt Katy, Aunt Fillis Foster, and Mrs. Evaline Estep.

SCENE FOURTH--ROXIE DAYMON AND ROSE SIMON.

Roxie Fairfield Daymon and her husband Daymon leave Louisville for Chicago, where Daymon becomes wealthy through land dealings, but Roxie later dies of cholera without learning Susan Lasco's fate. After the burning of the Brandywine on the Mississippi River, John A. Lasco dies leaving a bequest to Susan Lasco and her descendants. Brindle Bill and S. S. Simon use evidence linking Rose Simon Fairfield to Susan Lasco and Tom Fairfield to obtain the Lasco inheritance, while concealing Rose's sisters. Rose later dies of yellow fever in Louisiana, intestate and childless.

SCENE FIFTH.

Suza Fairfield is captured by Tom Ditamus at the request of the Demitt relations but escapes during the night and returns to Aunt Katy Demitt in Port William. Aunt Katy reveals her savings and a will leaving all her possessions to Suza. More than a decade later, Don Carlo attends a camp meeting, learns that Suza is the young woman who rescued him from the Red Stone wreck, and begins courting her. The neighborhood soon says that Suza and Don Carlo are engaged.

SCENE SIXTH.

The Second Generation introduces Young Simon, heir to S. S. Simon's plantations and slaves, and follows his father's death, his own short life, and the early life of his cousin Cæsar Simon. Cæsar, later calling himself Cole Conway, becomes a gambler and meets Steve Brindle, who reveals that he is Steve Sharp's partner and the son of Brindle Bill. Cæsar tells Brindle about his family, and Brindle identifies him as the heir to S. S. Simon's large estate in Arkansas. The unit also describes the expansion of cotton cultivation and slavery in the Western States, the arrival of the iron horse and magnetic telegraph, and Cæsar's dream of a steam carriage.

Roxie Daymon, the penniless daughter of Roxie Fairfield, joins a pleasure party traveling from Chicago to New Orleans and meets young Simon, a wealthy but terminally ill cotton planter. After learning that Roxie may be connected to heirs of a twenty-thousand-dollar claim against Simon’s estate, Simon becomes convinced of a mystery involving her family and becomes engaged to her. When Simon returns to Chicago in April 1861, his health fails, and he dictates a will leaving his entire estate, worth more than a million dollars, to Roxie. After Simon’s death, cousin Cæsar hires Gov. Morock to challenge the will and pays him one thousand dollars in advance.

SCENE SEVENTH--WAR BETWEEN THE STATES.

The unit follows Cousin Cæsar Simon through the early years of the War Between the States, especially his service in General Sterling Price’s army in Missouri and Arkansas. Cæsar is wounded at the battle of Wilson’s Creek, rescued by Steve Brindle and a Cherokee Indian, and later becomes troubled by the recurring number 77 and by his neglect of Steve. The unit also describes Roxie Daymon and Governor Morock’s efforts to profit from the Simon estate and challenge the Simon will. Cliff Carlo joins the Southern armies, while the war devastates Missouri, Arkansas, and the Simon plantations.

The federals had fortified Helena, a point three hundred miles above Vicks burg

The federals fortified Helena with three forts and a gunboat and were attacked by General Holmes's approximately 4,900 men on July 4, 1863. General Price carried the center position, but Marmaduke and Fagan failed to carry the other forts, and Price's men later retreated with heavy losses. Cæsar Simon fell at Helena, ending the Simon family in the male line. Nearly two years later the Southern soldiers surrendered, the war ended, the Simon land was sold to pay judgments, and the surviving named figures went on to different postwar lives.
