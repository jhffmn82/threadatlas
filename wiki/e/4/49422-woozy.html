Woozy in The Patchwork Girl of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1913-07-01

Woozy is the only one of its kind ever known: a remarkable, dark blue, square-bodied animal or creature. It has a square head, a block-shaped body twice as long as it is wide and high, four four-sided legs, a straight square tail, and thick smooth skin. Except for exactly three stiff hairs at the tip of its tail, it has no hair. Its skin is so thick and tough that it cannot be pierced or harmed by attacks such as Chiss's quills or the Sawhorse's kicks. It runs swiftly, jumps high, overtakes honey-bees in flight, and knows the yellow brick road, but cannot climb, dig, or gnaw. It is shy by nature but afraid of nothing, likes food it can chew and taste, especially bread and cheese, and prefers honey-bees. The three tail hairs matter because they are needed for the restoring compound intended to bring Unc Nunkie and Margolotte to life. Woozy has been confined by Munchkin farmers in a forest for many years. After Ojo feeds it bread and cheese, it agrees to give the hairs, but they cannot be pulled out. It escapes through an opening it burns in the fence with fire flashed from its eyes and travels with Ojo, Scraps, and the Glass Cat, promising not to eat honey-bees. It guides the group west, survives capture by a monstrous plant after the Shaggy Man frees it, and remains unharmed in later dangers. Though it boasts of a terrible growl, its attempt to frighten Chiss produces only a small squeak, and it admits it may have exaggerated. When angered by “Krizzle-Kroo,” it can flash real fire from its eyes. It helps Ojo because Ojo helped free it. In the end, it is recognized as good, honest, faithful, and unique, and is sent to the Royal Menagerie for lifelong care and food.

Narrative, section by section

9 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Five

Three hairs from the tip of a Woozy's tail are required for the restoring compound. Dr. Pipt has never seen a Woozy and cannot describe what kind of animal it is.

Chapter Nine

The Woozy is a dark blue, square-shaped creature with no hair except three stiff hairs at the extreme end of its tail. It has a thick skin that cannot be pierced, can run swiftly and jump high, but cannot climb, dig, or gnaw. After eating Ojo's bread and cheese, it agrees to give Ojo its three tail hairs, but the hairs will not come out, so it escapes from its forest with Ojo, Scraps, and the Glass Cat.

Chapter Ten

The Woozy directs the travelers west on the yellow brick road and warns them to run when the monstrous plants attack. A plant captures him, but the Shaggy Man cuts down the leaf and the Woozy escapes.

Chapter Eleven

The Woozy allows the Shaggy Man to try to pull hairs from its tail, but the hairs cannot be removed. It dislikes the Shaggy Man's condensed meal because it wants food it can chew and taste, so Ojo gives it bread and cheese.

Chapter Twelve

The Woozy claims that its growl is tremendously frightening and tries to scare Chiss, but it produces only a small squeak. It is not injured by Chiss's quill attack because its skin is thick and tough, and it admits that it may have been mistaken about the power of its growl.

Chapter Thirteen

The Woozy travels in the line behind Scraps and is kicked twice by the Sawhorse after sniffing at it, without being harmed. He boasts that his eyes can flash fire and that he sometimes has a terrible growl. Ojo explains that the Woozy's three tail hairs are needed for the charm intended to restore Unc Nunkie.

Chapter Sixteen

The Woozy agrees to help Ojo and Unc Nunkie because Ojo once helped him when he was shut up in a forest. He can make his eyes flash real fire when angered by the words "Krizzle-Kroo," but Dorothy does not think this will help Ojo's uncle. Dorothy has him taken to a pleasant room and given suitable food.

Chapter Seventeen

The Woozy is discussed at the banquet as one of the most remarkable animals the friends have seen. Ozma did not know that such a creature existed in the Land of Oz because there is only one Woozy and it had been confined in its forest for many years.

Chapter Twenty-Eight

The Woozy is recognized as good and faithful. He is sent to the Royal Menagerie, where he will receive good care and plenty to eat.

Woozy in The Lost Princess of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1917

Woozy is a peculiar, square-bodied animal and thick-skinned creature with tough, leather-like skin. Though its movements seem clumsy, it travels with remarkable swiftness, eats fruit readily, and can stay awake for a month; when asleep, it snores loudly. A great friend of Scraps, it joins the search party, carries Scraps through the Emerald City and into the Winkie Country, then follows her across the gulf to protect her despite first suggesting they turn back. Bounced across mountains, it reaches safety unharmed. It fearlessly crosses thistles, carries companions and Hank, and later discusses beauty, insisting loveliness includes inward as well as outward qualities.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER 6

The Woozy asks to join the search party and carries Scraps through the Emerald City and into the Winkie Country. He is a square-bodied, tough-skinned, swift-moving creature who can remain awake for a month and stays awake talking with Scraps and the Sawhorse.

CHAPTER 7

The Woozy initially suggests turning back, but he follows Scraps across the gulf to protect her. He is bounced from mountain to mountain and reaches the far side unhurt.

CHAPTER 8

The Woozy carries Trot and the Patchwork Girl and walks fearlessly through the thistles because its skin is thick. It carries the Cowardly Lion to the city, returns for the others, and later transports the travelers and helps bring Hank across.

CHAPTER 10

The Woozy says that he does not care for Toto's growl and admits that he snores, although he cannot judge whether the sound is terrible or delightful while asleep. He debates beauty with Hank the Mule and argues that true loveliness includes both outward and inward qualities.
