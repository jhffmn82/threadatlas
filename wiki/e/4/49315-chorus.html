Chorus of Phthian women in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

The Chorus of Phthian women, a group native to Phthia, shares Andromache’s troubles while fearing Hermione and the rulers. Remaining at home, it guards absent friends’ fortunes, counsels Andromache to act mildly, links present grief to Troy’s destruction, reports Hermione’s flight and Orestes’ threat, mourns the dead youth and Peleus, and ends by seeing divine guidance in human affairs.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ANDROMACHE.

The Chorus of Phthian women sympathizes with Andromache but fears Hermione and the ruling authorities. It urges Andromache to consider her foreign and helpless position and later advises her to address Hermione mildly. It also sings that Paris, the goddesses’ contest, and the destruction of Troy caused grief throughout Greece and Troy.

PELEUS, CHORUS.

The Chorus tells Peleus that Hermione has fled because of Menelaus’ anger and that Orestes intends to kill Achilles’ son. It mourns the dead youth and Peleus’s suffering, then recognizes the arrival of Thetis and concludes that the gods guide human affairs in unexpected ways.
