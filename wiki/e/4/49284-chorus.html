Chorus of Grecian dames in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

The Chorus of Grecian dames, Helen’s Grecian attendants and companions, mourns her sufferings and Troy’s destruction, counsels her to seek Theonoe’s truth, and accompanies her palaceward. Astonished by Menelaus’s deception, it restrains Theoclymenus, defends pious Theonoe, and accepts the divine explanation.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

HELEN.

The Chorus of Grecian dames mourns Helen’s sufferings and the destruction of Troy. It urges Helen not to believe immediately that Menelaus is dead and advises her to ask Theonoe for the truth. The Chorus accompanies Helen toward the palace.

Swift bark of Sidon, by whose dashing oars

The Chorus reacts with astonishment to Menelaus’s deception and criticizes Theoclymenus for trying to kill Theonoe. It physically restrains him and defends Theonoe’s piety before accepting the divine explanation of events.
