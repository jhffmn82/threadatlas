Proteus in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Proteus was a virtuous king who ruled Egypt and lived on Pharos. Hermes placed Helen in his house to keep her undefiled. After he died, he was buried in a tomb before the palace gate; his son Theoclymenus and daughter Theonoe remained. Theoclymenus addresses his tomb and seeks to marry Helen, while Proteus’ hostility toward Greeks persists after their arrival.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

HELEN.

Proteus ruled Egypt and lived on Pharos. He was regarded as the most virtuous of men, and Hermes placed Helen in his house so that she could remain undefiled. After Proteus’ death, Theoclymenus seeks to marry Helen.

MENELAUS.

Proteus is the former king of Egypt, and his tomb is near the place where Helen resides. He is described as hostile to the Greeks, although he is not present during this unit.

CHORUS.

Theoclymenus addresses the tomb of his father, Proteus, which stands before the palace. Proteus is said to be dead and buried there, and Theoclymenus frequently speaks to him when entering or leaving.
