Ohio River in Pen Pictures of Eventful Scenes and Struggles of Life

B. F. Craig · 1880

Ohio River is a principal western waterway near a round-log cabin and passing Port William. Its steam navigation helped create new industries, and supporters of free navigation prized its bank for boatmen’s songs and steam engines. A Kentucky-shore explosion sends victims into its waters; later it frames Suza Fairfield’s homecoming.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

SCENE SECOND.

The Ohio River is described as a principal western waterway whose navigation by steam helped create new industries. Early supporters of free navigation wished to be buried on its bank so they could hear boatmen's songs and the sound of steam engines. The Red Stone explodes on the Kentucky shore of the Ohio, and some victims are consigned to its waters.

SCENE FIFTH.

The Ohio River is visible from the broad bottom land near Port William. Suza Fairfield recognizes the river landscape and the chimneys of Port William as she completes her return home.
