Abstract written by a model

Proteus’ tomb is a burial place at the entrance of Theoclymenus’ palace in Pharos, with a surrounding trench and burning altars. Helen kneels there to seek protection for her marriage, mourn Menelaus, and find refuge. After learning Menelaus lives, she meets him there; the tomb shelters him from the king’s force, hosts Helen’s supplication to Theonoe, and becomes the starting place of their deception and threatened joint death.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

4 sections; each heading opens the text

HELEN.

Helen kneels at Proteus’ tomb and asks him to protect her marriage and keep her from real dishonor. The tomb stands before Theoclymenus’ palace and is the place where Helen mourns Menelaus and seeks refuge.

MENELAUS.

Helen takes refuge at Proteus’s tomb, which is near the palace and has a surrounding trench and burning altars. She returns there after hearing that Menelaus is alive and meets him at the tomb.

CHOR. My sentiments on prophets well accord

Proteus’ tomb provides Menelaus with refuge from the king’s force and serves as the place where Helen supplicates Theonoe. Menelaus threatens that he and Helen will die beside the tomb if they are prevented from escaping.

CHORUS.

The tomb of Proteus is placed before Theoclymenus’s palace. Helen and Menelaus use it as the place where their deception begins, and Menelaus initially sits there disguised as a grieving stranger.
