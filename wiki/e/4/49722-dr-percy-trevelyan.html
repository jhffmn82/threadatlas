Dr. Percy Trevelyan

Abstract written by a model

Dr. Percy Trevelyan is a Brook Street physician, general practitioner, and nervous-disease specialist. An authority on catalepsy, he wins the Bruce Pinkerton prize and medal for related research and writes on catalepsy and nervous lesions. He operates a medical-practice partnership and treats Blessington as a resident patient, supported by household servants. Seeking Holmes's advice, he recounts visits from a supposed Russian nobleman and his son, Blessington's disappearance during a consultation, and footprints in Blessington's room. He later reports Blessington's apparent suicide, learns Blessington concealed the name Sutton, and is shaken by the events.

Dr. Percy Trevelyan is in one work on this site, The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes. This is the abstract a model wrote of it from that work.

IX. The Resident Patient

Dr. Trevelyan, a physician who specializes in nervous disease and has written on catalepsy and nervous lesions, seeks Holmes's advice about events at his house. He describes the visits by a supposed Russian nobleman and his son, the patient's disappearance during consultation, and the footprints found in Blessington's room.

Dr. Trevelyan reports Blessington's apparent suicide and admits that the event has shaken him. He explains that Blessington kept a coil of rope beside his bed because he feared fire, and later learns that his patient had concealed his identity as Sutton.
