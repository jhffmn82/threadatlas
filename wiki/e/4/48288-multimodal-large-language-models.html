Multimodal Large Language Models in Wiki-LLaVA: Hierarchical Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Multimodal LLMs

Davide Caffagni · 2024

Multimodal Large Language Models are a vision-and-language model family and the natural evolution of LLMs beyond pure text. They combine visual-input understanding with natural-language generation, integrating visual features into LLM backbones through vision-to-language adapters. Using an image, textual prompt, visual context, and knowledge stored in parameters, they model a probability distribution and generate textual output autoregressively. They generalize well to elaborate visual descriptions, but may struggle with highly specific queries, compositional reasoning, and long-tail information. Methods developed different image-feature and language-model integrations; Wiki-LLaVA adds retrieved external knowledge to help answer questions requiring it.

VLMs in VISA: Retrieval Augmented Generation with Visual Source Attribution

Xueguang Ma, Shengyao Zhuang, Bevan Koopman, Guido Zuccon, Wenhu Chen, Jimmy Lin · 2025

VLMs are vision-language models used in VISA, an end-to-end RAG framework, to process textual questions and one or more retrieved document screenshots. They combine query tokens from a language model with document representations from an image encoder, then generate answers and bounding boxes identifying supporting regions; in a multi-candidate setup, they can also generate the relevant document identifier. They can generate questions and short answers from highlighted content and are used because VISA requires multimodal processing. Their central challenge is visual source attribution: existing models can produce reasonable answers and image-to-text bounding-box coordinates, but struggle to pinpoint evidence in original documents with suitable location and granularity, especially under zero-shot prompting. VISA trains models to generate answers and corresponding boxes, initializing answer generation with Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct or Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct; fine-tuning on VISA datasets improves performance. VLMs may also enhance optical character recognition, with added latency and complexity.
