Abstract written by a model

In “LongRAG: A Dual-Perspective Retrieval-Augmented Generation Paradigm for Long-Context Question Answering,” Long-context LLMs are defined as models that can ingest entire relevant documents and answer directly, with limitations from context-window and training-sequence length. In “Retrieval Augmented Generation or Long-Context LLMs?”, long-context LLMs are discussed for their practical application and as motivation for optimizing RAG techniques. In “Retrieval Augmented Generation or Long-Context LLMs?”, LC is presented as a long-context LLM approach that processes longer inputs directly, is compared with RAG, and supplies fallback predictions in SELF-ROUTE. In “CLERC: A Dataset for U. S. Legal Case Retrieval and Retrieval-Augmented Analysis Generation,” long-context LLMs are reported to support context lengths of up to 1 million tokens in recent proprietary systems and to have contributed to interest in long-context retrieval models. In “CLERC: A Dataset for U. S. Legal Case Retrieval and Retrieval-Augmented Analysis Generation,” long-context retrieval models are described as an increasingly interesting model type whose performance on well-defined legal retrieval tasks is not shown by existing benchmarks. In “CLERC: A Dataset for U. S. Legal Case Retrieval and Retrieval-Augmented Analysis Generation,” long-context models are discussed as a category whose legal-retrieval performance remains insufficiently assessed and contrasted with BERT-based models. In “VisDoM: Multi-Document QA with Visually Rich Elements Using Multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation,” long-context language models are compared with VisDoMRAG and shown to perform less effectively when localized evidence must be retrieved from large collections.

Long-context LLMs in LongRAG: A Dual-Perspective Retrieval-Augmented Generation Paradigm for Long-Context Question Answering

Qingfei Zhao, Ruobing Wang, Yukuo Cen, Daren Zha, Shicheng Tan, Yuxiao Dong, Jie Tang · 2024 · the work’s page · read it where Long-context LLMs first appears

Abstract written by a model

Long-context LLMs are large language models that can ingest entire relevant documents and answer directly. Their central limitation is context-window and training-sequence length: overly long inputs degrade performance and can cause “lost in the middle.” Training or fine-tuning, restricted attention, and compression can extend context, with fine-tuning better but costlier.

Introduction

Long-context LLMs, including Gemini and GPT-4-128k, can ingest entire relevant documents and generate answers directly. They frequently encounter the “lost in the middle” issue and performed worse than LongRAG in the reported comparisons.

2 / 2.1 .. 2.3

Long-Context LLMs are limited by their training sequence length, and inputs exceeding the context window can cause substantial performance degradation. Their context length can be scaled through long-context training or fine-tuning methods, or through non-fine-tuned approaches such as restricted attention and context compression; fine-tuned methods generally perform better but require greater training and data costs.

LC in Retrieval Augmented Generation or Long-Context LLMs?

Zhuowan Li · 2024-11-12 to 2024-11-16 · the work’s page · read it where LC first appears

Abstract written by a model

LC is a long-context LLM approach that enables models to process longer inputs directly. It is examined through a systematic comparison with Retrieval Augmented Generation, using public datasets including LongBench and ∞Bench and considering degradation across context lengths, the lost-in-the-middle phenomenon, and exclusion of internal knowledge. Recent models such as Gemini-1.5, GPT-4, and Claude-3 are noted for larger context windows. Across the evaluated models, LC consistently outperforms RAG on average, especially for Gemini-1.5-Pro and GPT-4O, when sufficiently resourced, and supplies fallback predictions in SELF-ROUTE when RAG cannot answer. Its central tradeoff is higher token use and computational cost from longer inputs. The work concludes that dynamic routing can achieve performance comparable to LC at significantly reduced cost.

Introduction

Long-context language models are the subject of a systematic comparison prompted by the long-context understanding capabilities of recent LLMs.

opening

Long-context LLMs consistently outperform RAG in almost all evaluated settings when sufficiently resourced. They provide stronger long-context understanding but incur higher computational costs because they process longer inputs.

Related Work

Long-context LLMs have been studied extensively to enable models to handle longer inputs. Gemini-1.5, GPT-4, and Claude-3 are described as achieving significantly larger context window sizes.

opening .. Benchmarking results

Long-context models are evaluated on datasets from LongBench and ∞Bench, with research examining performance degradation across context lengths, the lost-in-the-middle phenomenon, and possible solutions. Xu et al. (2023) reported that long-context models still lagged behind RAG, while this study investigates the comparison with stronger LLMs and longer contexts.

opening .. Exclusion of LLM’s internal knowledge

LC consistently outperforms RAG across the evaluated models on average, with especially large advantages for Gemini-1.5-Pro and GPT-4O. It provides the fallback prediction in SELF-ROUTE for queries that RAG cannot answer and generally achieves higher performance at greater token cost. Its performance is also used to assess the effect of prompting models to rely only on the provided passage.

conclusion

LC demonstrates superior performance in long-context understanding compared with RAG. The proposed dynamic routing method achieves performance comparable to LC at significantly reduced cost.
