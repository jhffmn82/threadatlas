Molossus in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Molossus is the infant son of Andromache, linked to Neoptolemus and a Trojan lineage. Secretly brought from Thetis’s altar, he is threatened and condemned to die with Andromache for her alleged offense, though he pleads for mercy. Peleus declares him faultless, frees him from chains, and promises to educate him in Phthia.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

MENELAUS, MOLOSSUS, ANDROMACHE, CHORUS.

Molossus is brought secretly from Thetis’s altar and is threatened with death as the penalty for Andromache’s alleged offense. He pleads with Menelaus to spare his life and is condemned to die with his mother.

PELEUS, MENELAUS, ANDROMACHE, MOLOSSUS, CHORUS.

Molossus is taken with Andromache to be killed even though Peleus presents him as having committed no fault. Peleus frees him from the chains and promises to educate him in Phthia.
