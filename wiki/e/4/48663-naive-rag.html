Abstract written by a model

In "Crafting Personalized Agents through Retrieval-Augmented Generation on Editable Memory Graphs," the Naive RAG setup is the comparison point for training efficiency, which is not higher in the described setup. In "M-RAG: Reinforcing Large Language Model Performance through Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Multiple Partitions," Naive RAG is the standard index-retrieve-generate process that improves tasks over no RAG but has low precision, hallucination, redundancy, and weaker results than Selfmem, Self-RAG, and M-RAG. In "DioR: Adaptive Cognitive Detection and Contextual Retrieval Optimization for Dynamic Retrieval-Augmented Generation," conventional RAG is a single-turn retrieval process that works well for simpler tasks but struggles with complex tasks and extensive text generation. In "GRAG: Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation," Naive Retrieval-Augmented Generation retrieves individual documents with retrievers such as BM25, MiniLM-L12-v2, and G-Retriever, improving over an LLM-only baseline but lacking topological information and underperforming graph-aware approaches. In "LightRAG: Simple and Fast Retrieval-Augmented Generation," NaiveRAG is a baseline and ablation-study reference that records lower win rates than LightRAG across the reported datasets and evaluation dimensions. In "Retrieval augmented generation for large language models in healthcare: A systematic review," Naive RAG is a basic healthcare RAG technique involving indexing, retrieval, and generation, but its benefits are limited by low retrieval quality, redundancy, irrelevant context, and generation errors. In "TableRAG: A Retrieval Augmented Generation Framework for Heterogeneous Document Reasoning," NaiveRAG linearizes tabular data as Markdown and applies a standard RAG pipeline, achieving WikiTQ scores of 82.60 and 75.40 but performing below TableRAG. In "TurboRAG: Accelerating Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Precomputed KV Caches for Chunked Text," Naïve RAG is the conventional RAG baseline used to compare TurboRAG, with higher TTFT and computational load despite comparable accuracy.

Naïve RAG in TurboRAG: Accelerating Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Precomputed KV Caches for Chunked Text

Songshuo Lu · 2025 · the work’s page · read it where Naïve RAG first appears

Abstract written by a model

Naïve RAG is a conventional retrieval-augmented generation system and the baseline used throughout experiments for comparison with TurboRAG. In Table 10, it has 743 context tokens, 87 ms TTFT, and 2.42x speedup. It matches or slightly exceeds TurboRAG on some accuracy measures, but has substantially higher TTFT and computational load, particularly with long contexts and larger batches. Its RGB averages are 97.1 in Chinese and 98.4 in English; its RGB Information Integration Task averages are 51.7 in Chinese and 62.3 in English. Across reported noise ratios, RGB performance declines as noise increases, with Chinese reaching 91.7 at 0.8 noise and English 96.7 at 0.8.
