  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, the Euphrates was on Darius's left and the intended endpoint of Nearchus's sea-coast exploration, after which Alexander ordered his fleet to sail to it.

Euphrates in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Euphrates is one of Asia's noblest rivers, flowing through Mesopotamia, near Nineveh and through Babylon, which it divides into eastern and western sides before emptying into the Red Sea after Babylon. Semiramis bridged it and temporarily diverted it to build a passage between palaces. After continual rains, it overflowed during Nineveh's siege, breached the wall, and became the city's enemy. Later Cyrus crossed it, while Artaxerxes encamped and fought near it.

The Euphrates runs through the middle of Babylon, dividing the city into eastern and western sides. Semiramis builds a bridge over it and temporarily diverts its course while constructing a vaulted passage between the palaces; the river is then returned to its ancient channel.

CHAP. IL

During the third year of the siege of Nineveh, the Euphrates overflowed after continual rains and tore down twenty furlongs of the city's wall. Its destruction of the wall fulfilled the prophecy that the river would become Nineveh's enemy.

CHAP. V-

Cyrus crossed the Euphrates with his army after gaining the soldiers' agreement to continue the expedition. Artaxerxes encamped by the river, left his heavy baggage behind a trench, and fought Cyrus's forces near its borders.
