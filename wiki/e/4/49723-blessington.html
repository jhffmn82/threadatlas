Blessington in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1892~/1893~

Blessington is Dr. Trevelyan’s resident patient, investor, and proprietor of the premises, living under constant supervision for a weak heart. Though his life is irregular, he rigidly collects three quarters of Trevelyan’s earnings each evening and guards his money in a black strong-box, carrying a pistol and fearing fire and burglary. Unknown visitors intrude into his room, leaving him terrified and unwilling or unable to explain them. He is later revealed as Sutton, a former member and informer against the Worthingdon bank gang, whose evidence led to Cartwright’s execution and others’ imprisonment. Targeted by three assailants seeking revenge, he is attacked and found hanging in his bedroom, the victim of murder.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

IX. The Resident Patient

Blessington is Trevelyan's resident patient and financial partner, and he has become intensely fearful of unknown people entering his rooms. He keeps his money in a large black box, confronts Holmes and Watson with a pistol, and refuses or is unable to explain who the two intruders are. Holmes advises him to speak the truth.

A minute later we were in the street and walking for home. We had

Blessington is found hanging in his bedroom after being attacked by three men. Holmes establishes that he was actually Sutton, the informer whose evidence helped secure Cartwright's execution and the imprisonment of Biddle, Hayward, and Moffat.
