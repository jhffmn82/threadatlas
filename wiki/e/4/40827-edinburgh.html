Edinburgh in Dr. Elsie Inglis

Lady Frances Balfour · 1918

Edinburgh is the city that became the Inglis family’s home after Tasmania and remained the central setting of Elsie Inglis’s life. There she and Eva attended the Charlotte Square Institution, and Elsie formed enduring friendships and intellectual associations, including the Six Sincere Students Society. It was also the setting of her early medical study, the barriers faced by women students, and competing attempts to create medical education for women. Unable then to take surgery there, she trained clinically in Glasgow, but planned to return to begin a practice, perhaps with Miss MacGregor, and hoped to establish a hospital of her own. She advocated a mixed medical school rather than a separate women’s school. As Dr. Inglis’s career developed, Edinburgh became the centre of her medical, political, educational, and philanthropic work. She practised there, founded the Hospice in the High Street, supported women students and local organisations, and cared for families between Morrison Street and the High Street. Her home and family life were also rooted there: her household was at 8 Walker Street, her sisters lived nearby, and St. Giles’ was part of her regular religious and professional surroundings. When war began, she was living in Edinburgh; her 6th Edinburgh V.A.D. mobilised there, and the Edinburgh suffrage offices became headquarters of the Scottish Women’s Hospitals. After her death, the city received her body, held her funeral at St. Giles’, and saw large crowds follow the procession across the city to her grave. Edinburgh also houses the Scottish Women’s Hospitals at the former Suffrage offices and contains St. Giles’, where she lay in state.
