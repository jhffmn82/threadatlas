General Sterling Price in Pen Pictures of Eventful Scenes and Struggles of Life

B. F. Craig · 1880

General Sterling Price is a military commander in the war. In Missouri, he leads an improvised Southern army that marches, fights Federals, captures supplies and weapons, advances to Lexington, and retreats south. At Helena, he commands infantry, captures the center fort, shelters men from gunboat shelling, and orders their retreat.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

SCENE SEVENTH--WAR BETWEEN THE STATES.

General Sterling Price commands an improvised Southern army in Missouri during the first year of the war. His army marches extensively, fights the Federals wherever it finds them, captures supplies and weapons, and later moves north to Lexington before retreating south.

The federals had fortified Helena, a point three hundred miles above Vicks burg

General Price was assigned to attack the center fort and carried his position. He sheltered his men from the gunboat's shelling and later ordered the retreat of Price's men.
