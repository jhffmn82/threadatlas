brain-fever in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1892~/1893~

Brain-fever is a serious illness lasting more than nine weeks. It follows Percy Phelps’s failed recovery of the treaty and the misfortune that destroys his career. Overwhelmed by shame and distress, he suffers a fit, returns home unconscious and raving, then gradually regains memory and reason but remains extremely weak.

XI. The Naval Treaty

Percy Phelps's brain-fever lasts nine weeks and follows the misfortune that destroys his career. He has only recently recovered when he writes to Dr. John Watson and remains extremely weak at Briarbrae.

“We examined the room also. There is no possibility of a secret

After failing to recover the treaty, Percy Phelps became overwhelmed by shame and distress, suffered a fit at the station, and returned home practically a raving maniac. He remained unconscious and raving with brain-fever for more than nine weeks before his memory and reason gradually returned.
