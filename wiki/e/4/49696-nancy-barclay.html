Nancy Barclay in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1892~/1893~

Nancy Barclay, née Nancy Devoy, is the beautiful, still striking wife of Colonel James Barclay. Daughter of a former Royal Mallows colour-sergeant, she married Barclay when he was a sergeant. Devoted and faithful though less demonstrative than him, she remains popular with the regiment’s ladies in a marriage considered happy.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

VIII. The Crooked Man

Nancy Barclay, whose maiden name was Nancy Devoy, was the daughter of a former colour-sergeant of the Royal Mallows and married Colonel Barclay when he was still a sergeant. She was a beautiful and striking woman who remained popular with the regiment's ladies, and her marriage to Barclay was considered happy despite her being less demonstratively affectionate than he was.

Nancy Devoy Barclay in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1892~/1893~

Nancy Devoy Barclay, a Roman Catholic interested in the Guild of St. George, is Colonel James Barclay’s wife and Miss Morrison’s friend. Before her marriage, she and Henry Wood were old friends and he loved her, but her father wanted her to marry James Barclay. After meeting Wood again in Hudson Street following a Guild meeting, she returns home distressed, quarrels angrily with Barclay, and is found insensible. Suspected of attacking and accused in connection with Barclay’s death, she cannot explain events during brain-fever and temporary insanity. The inquest ultimately finds that Barclay died of apoplexy, not murder.

Mrs. Barclay, a Roman Catholic interested in the Guild of St. George, attends a meeting with Miss Morrison and returns to Lachine shortly after nine. She encounters Henry Wood in Hudson Street, becomes deeply distressed, and later angrily quarrels with her husband before being found insensible in the morning-room. She is suspected of attacking Colonel Barclay and is temporarily unable to explain events because of brain-fever.

Nancy Devoy loved Henry Wood, although her father wanted her to marry James Barclay, and she eventually became Barclay's wife. After recognizing Wood years later, she argued with Barclay and fainted when Wood appeared; she was initially at risk of being tried for Barclay's murder, but the inquest found that his death was due to apoplexy.
