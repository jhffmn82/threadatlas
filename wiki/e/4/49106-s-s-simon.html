S. S. Simon in Pen Pictures of Eventful Scenes and Struggles of Life

B. F. Craig · 1880

S. S. Simon is a wood-yard proprietor, neighborhood planter, husband of Rose Simon Fairfield, and father of Young Simon. He helps Brindle Bill establish Rose’s Lasco inheritance claim through false testimony, takes control of the money, and uses Rose’s share to become a keen financier, acquire four thousand acres and more than six hundred slaves, and build a costly residence. A hard master who works relentlessly for wealth, he dies of swamp fever, deliriously calling for Roxie Daymon.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

SCENE FOURTH--ROXIE DAYMON AND ROSE SIMON.

S. S. Simon helps Brindle Bill establish Rose's claim to the Lasco inheritance and persuades Rose to let him manage the matter. He participates in the false testimony, receives the money, and uses Rose's share to advance himself as a planter and build a new house.

SCENE SIXTH.

S. S. Simon owns four large plantations and more than six hundred slaves and works relentlessly to die a rich man. He knows the history of Roxie Daymon or Roxie Fairfield and dies from swamp fever after deliriously crying, "Take Roxie Daymon away."
