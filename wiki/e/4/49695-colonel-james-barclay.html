In The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes, Colonel James Barclay is a veteran Royal Mallows officer whose betrayal of Henry Wood during the Mutiny haunts him until he dies after Wood confronts him. In The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes, Colonel James Barclay is the affectionate veteran soldier who is found murdered after being overheard arguing with Mrs. Barclay.

Colonel James Barclay in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · about 1892 to 1893 · the work’s page · read it where Colonel James Barclay first appears

Abstract written by a model

Colonel James Barclay was a gallant veteran of the Royal Mallows who rose from private and sergeant to commissioned colonel after bravery during the Mutiny. He married Nancy Devoy and was generally seen as devoted to her, but could be violent, vindictive, superstitious, depressed, and afraid of being alone after dark. During the Mutiny, he betrayed Henry Wood by sending him to the rebels, a deed that troubled his conscience for thirty years. After Wood confronted him during an argument with Nancy, Barclay died.

Colonel James Barclay commanded the Royal Mallows until the Monday night before Holmes's visit. His character included occasional violence and vindictiveness, periods of deep depression, superstition, and a dislike of being left alone, especially after dark.

Arthur Conan Doyle · about 1892 to 1893 · the work’s page · read it where Colonel James Barclay first appears

Abstract written by a model

Colonel James Barclay, a veteran soldier with weapons collected from countries where he fought, is known to be affectionate toward Mrs. Barclay. After joining her following her return, he is overheard arguing, amid mention of David, and is found murdered with a fearful expression and head wound.

“Mrs. Barclay was, it appears, a member of the Roman Catholic

Colonel James Barclay joins his wife in the morning-room after her return and is heard arguing with her. He is found dead from a jagged cut at the back of his head, with his face contorted in fear and horror. His name is significant because Mrs. Barclay is heard referring to someone named David during the quarrel.
