Fitzroy Simpson

Abstract written by a model

Fitzroy Simpson is a well-born bookmaker, living by sporting-club business after losing his fortune, who wagered five thousand pounds against Silver Blaze. He visited King’s Pyland seeking information about the horses and became the natural suspect in the disappearance of Silver Blaze and the murder of John Straker. Suspicion rested on his betting interest, presence in the storm, alleged poisoning of the stable-boy, heavy stick, and cravat, which he owned and which was found in Straker’s hand after Simpson dropped it while fleeing. Yet the case had weaknesses: Simpson had no wound despite Straker’s bloodied knife, and there was no clear means for him to hide the horse. The curried mutton and powdered opium show he could not have arranged the poisoning, so Holmes eliminates him as the murderer.

I. Silver Blaze

Fitzroy Simpson is a well-born bookmaker who has lost his fortune and placed five thousand pounds in bets against Silver Blaze. He admits visiting the stables to obtain information, but denies sinister intentions; his cravat is found in John Straker’s hand, his weighted stick could have caused the fatal injuries, and he has no wound despite Straker’s bloodied knife.

Fitzroy Simpson is suspected because of his interest in Silver Blaze’s disappearance, the suspected poisoning of a stable-boy, his presence in the storm, his heavy stick, and his cravat being found in Straker’s hand. Holmes points out that the case against him has significant weaknesses, including the lack of a wound, duplicate key, or clear means of hiding the horse.
