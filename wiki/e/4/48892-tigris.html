Tigris in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Tigris is a river rising in the Uxian mountains and flowing from mountains; records place its outlet in the Persian sea and describe a seven-hundred-furlong course into the Red Sea. It is three or four furlongs wide in places, with a middle depth equal to elephants' height, and contains sea fish and whales. During marches and wars, it served as a barrier: Darius kept it on his right, Alexander found a ford, Eumenes crossed after flooding was drained, and Antigonus crossed by boats.

CHAP. n.

The Tigris was described as three or four furlongs wide in different places, with a central depth equal to the height of elephants. It flowed from the mountains for seven hundred furlongs into the Red Sea and was said to contain sea fish and whales. Eumenes used it as a defensive barrier, while Antigonus crossed it by a bridge of boats.
