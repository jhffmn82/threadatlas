Pentheus's opposition to Dionysus

topic · 2 works · 1 quoted fact · 4 connections
Also called: Bromios scorn, Pentheus's denial of Dionysus, Pentheus's war against Dionysus, Ye mocked me, being God, Ye wronged Him! Ye denied his deity!, a war with God, battle, battle to drag down, mocked my birth, persecutor, scorned me, thrusteth me away, to mock the God, war with God

Abstract written by a model

In Hippolytus and The Bacchae, Pentheus's war against Dionysus consists of rejecting Dionysus's person and refusing him drink-offerings and prayers; Dionysus responds by declaring that Pentheus and Thebes will face his power. (Hippolytus; The Bacchae) In The Bacchae of Euripides, Pentheus's opposition to Dionysus is his denial of the god: he rejects offerings and prayers, calls his birth a lie, suppresses his followers, and seeks Dionysus's death; his refusal leads him to Kithaeron and brings punishment on him and Cadmus's family.

The Bacchae

Pentheus opposes Dionysus by refusing him offerings and prayers, calling his birth a lie, imprisoning his followers, and ordering the destruction of Dionysian worship. He commands that Dionysus be captured, judged, stoned, and killed.

CHORUS.

Pentheus's refusal to honor Dionysus leads him to Kithaeron, where he goes to mock the god and Agave's ecstasy. Dionysus identifies this denial as the cause of the punishment inflicted on Pentheus and Cadmus's family.
