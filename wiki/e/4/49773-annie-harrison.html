Abstract written by a model

In The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes, Annie Harrison is Joseph Harrison’s sister and Percy Phelps’s devoted nurse, caring for him during his illness and agreeing to guard his room for Holmes. In The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes, Miss Harrison is Percy Phelps’s fiancée, nurses him through his illness, and helps Holmes’s recovery plan by occupying and securing Phelps’s bedroom.

Annie Harrison in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · about 1892 to 1893 · the work’s page · read it where Annie Harrison first appears

Abstract written by a model

Annie Harrison, Joseph Harrison’s sister and Percy Phelps’s fiancée, is Phelps’s devoted nurse during his illness at Briarbrae. She nurses him for two months, encourages his courage and patience, stays beside him during Holmes and Watson’s visit, and agrees to Holmes’s instructions to guard his room.

Narrative, section by section written by a model

2 sections; each heading opens the text

XI. The Naval Treaty

Annie Harrison is Joseph Harrison's sister and Percy Phelps's fiancée. She has nursed Phelps at Briarbrae for two months and remains beside him when Sherlock Holmes and Dr. John Watson arrive.

“Where are we going to now?” I asked, as we left the office.

Annie Harrison is present at Briarbrae and encourages Percy Phelps to keep his courage and patience. Sherlock Holmes insists that she remain in Phelps's room throughout the day and lock the room from the outside at night, and she agrees despite her surprise.

Miss Harrison in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · about 1892 to 1893 · the work’s page · read it where Miss Harrison first appears

Abstract written by a model

Miss Harrison, Percy Phelps’s fiancée from Northumberland, nurses him through his illness and stays at his home. Holmes judges her a strong-charactered, apparently good woman. She cooperates in his recovery plan, occupying Phelps’s bedroom by day and, on Holmes’s instructions, locking it from outside at night so Joseph believes entry is possible only through the window.

2 sections; each heading opens the text

“We examined the room also. There is no possibility of a secret

Miss Harrison has nursed Percy Phelps during his illness and remains with him at his home. Holmes learns that she is engaged to Phelps and considers her a strong-charactered and apparently good woman.

Miss Harrison follows Holmes’s instructions by locking Percy Phelps’s bedroom door from the outside and taking the key when she retires. Her cooperation helps Holmes make Joseph believe that the room can be entered only by the window.
