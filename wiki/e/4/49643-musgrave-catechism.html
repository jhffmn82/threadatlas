Musgrave catechism in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1892~/1893~

The Musgrave catechism is a family catechism required at manhood. It begins by asking whose possession something was and who will receive it, naming he who is gone and he who will come. It then asks where sun and shadow were, naming oak and elm; gives directional steps; and ends with all that is ours, for the sake of the trust.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

“He handed me the very paper which I have here, Watson, and this

The catechism asks whose possession something belonged to, who will receive it, where the sun and shadow were, how it was stepped, what should be given for it, and why it should be given. Its answers refer to the person who is gone, the person who will come, the oak, the elm, a sequence of directional steps, all that is owned, and the sake of the trust.
