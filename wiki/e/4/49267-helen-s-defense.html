Helen’s defense

topic · 2 works · 0 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Helen's guilt and innocence, all that thou canst urge, all the wrong she wrought, her defence, her sin, wronged and innocent
  • In The Trojan Women of Euripides, Helen presents herself as wronged and innocent, attributing her departure to divine power and claiming she tried to escape Troy, while Hecuba and Menelaus reject her defense and hold her responsible for the war and its dead.
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Helen’s defense attributes her departure from Sparta and life in Troy to divine influence, Paris, Priam, and force rather than her own will; she also claims she tried to escape after Paris’s death but was prevented and later forced to marry Deiphobus.
