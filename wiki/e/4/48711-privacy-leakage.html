privacy leakage in The Good and The Bad: Exploring Privacy Issues in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

Shenglai Zeng, Jiankun Zhang, Pengfei He, Yue Xing, Yiding Liu, Han Xu, Jie Ren, Shuaiqiang Wang, Dawei Yin, Yi Chang, Jiliang Tang · 2024

Privacy leakage is a risk in retrieval-augmented generation, arising from both retrieval datasets and LLM pre-training or fine-tuning data. The work first identifies extraction of sensitive retrieval records and memorized training-data leakage as concerns. It evaluates retrieval-data leakage by prompting systems for targeted or broad information, comparing reproduced outputs with returned documents. The evaluation finds substantial leakage, including exact or similar text and targeted sensitive information; command construction, summarization, and retrieval-distance thresholds affect it. Finally, reported experiments show retrieval data reduces training-data leakage, while underscoring the need to prevent leakage from retrieval data itself.

privacy leakage in Mitigating the Privacy Issues in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) via Pure Synthetic Data

Shenglai Zeng, Jiankun Zhang, Pengfei He, Jie Ren, Tianqi Zheng, Hanqing Lu, Han Xu, Hui Liu, Yue Xing, Jiliang Tang · 2025

Privacy leakage is a severe risk in Retrieval-Augmented Generation systems using private data, especially in safety-critical domains, because it can expose sensitive information. It may increase as more documents are retrieved when only stage-1 data is used. Synthetic data and agent-based refinement are investigated to reduce leakage while maintaining comparable performance; refinement keeps it minimal, and leakage varies with data quality and model choice.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

Abstract

Privacy risks in RAG systems can potentially lead to the leakage of sensitive information. The proposed approach substantially reduces these risks while maintaining comparable performance.

Ablation Studies

Privacy leakage is absent when Llama3-Chat-8b generates poor-quality data, and it is lower with GPT-4 than with GPT-3.5. Leakage can increase with more retrieved documents for stage-1 data but remains minimal after agent-based refinement.

Conclusions

Privacy concerns motivate the investigation of synthetic data for use in RAG systems. The proposed approach is intended to mitigate these concerns.

Ethic Statement

Privacy risks are the focus of using synthetic data in Retrieval-Augmented Generation, particularly in safety-critical domains.
