Colonel Ross in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1892~/1893~

Colonel Ross is a well-known sportsman, horse owner, and owner of the racehorse Silver Blaze and the King’s Pyland training-stable. When Silver Blaze disappears and John Straker is killed, he telegraphs Sherlock Holmes for help, seeking both the horse’s recovery and Straker’s murderer. He worries about Silver Blaze’s Cup entry, grows disappointed and doubtful when Holmes offers reassurance without producing the horse, and is distressed when the horse appears to run under his colours. Holmes reveals Silver Blaze’s identity and solves the murder, leading Ross to express gratitude and recognize Holmes’s achievement.

I. Silver Blaze

Colonel Ross owns Silver Blaze and operates the training-stable at King’s Pyland. He telegraphs Sherlock Holmes to request assistance after Silver Blaze disappears and John Straker is killed.

It was evening before we reached the little town of Tavistock,

Colonel Ross asks Sherlock Holmes to help avenge John Straker and recover Silver Blaze. He considers whether Silver Blaze’s name should be removed from the Cup entries, but Holmes advises him to leave it standing.

“My friend and I return to town by the night-express,” said

Colonel Ross is disappointed with Sherlock Holmes and doubts that Holmes has advanced the investigation. He says that Holmes’s assurance about the horse is less satisfactory than having the horse itself.

Colonel Ross is initially distressed because Silver Blaze is missing and believes the horse running under his colours is not his own. After Holmes reveals the horse's identity and solves Straker's murder, Ross expresses gratitude and acknowledges Holmes's achievement.
