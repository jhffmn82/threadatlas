  • In "RetrievalQA: Assessing Adaptive Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Short-form Open-Domain Question Answering," the creators stated that they adhered to OpenAI's terms of use and policies.
  • In "Optimizing LLM Based Retrieval Augmented Generation Pipelines in the Financial Domain," OpenAI was identified as a vendor of LLMs that showed a substantial difference from smaller-scale open-source alternatives in the experiments.
  • In "BERGEN: A Benchmarking Library for Retrieval-Augmented Generation," OpenAI was associated with the GPT-4 technical report and with earlier work on language models and instruction following.
  • In "RadioRAG: Online Retrieval-augmented Generation for Radiology Question Answering," OpenAI provided an API, including GPT-4, GPT-3.5-turbo, and text-embedding-ada-002, used in the study through version 1.12.
  • In "CODERAG-BENCH: Can Retrieval Augment Code Generation?," OpenAI was associated with a retrieval method and with OpenAI embeddings in the experiments, which found higher-quality programming solutions than GIST-large.
  • In "RAG LLMs are Not Safer: A Safety Analysis of Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Large Language Models," OpenAI provided the policy basis for the 16-category risk taxonomy used to label harmful questions.
  • In "Retrieval augmented generation for large language models in healthcare: A systematic review," OpenAI supplied proprietary GPT-3.5/4 models used by many healthcare RAG studies and the text-embedding-ada-002 model used by most reviewed studies for generating embeddings.
