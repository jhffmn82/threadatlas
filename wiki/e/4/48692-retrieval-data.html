retrieval data in The Good and The Bad: Exploring Privacy Issues in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

Shenglai Zeng, Jiankun Zhang, Pengfei He, Yue Xing, Yiding Liu, Han Xu, Jie Ren, Shuaiqiang Wang, Dawei Yin, Yi Chang, Jiliang Tang · 2024

Retrieval data is an external knowledge source and retrieval dataset used by RAG systems and integrated into large language models. It may contain sensitive, valuable domain-specific information, including patient prescription records, creating a new privacy risk: attackers can use structured prompting to extract verbatim or highly similar records from the dataset selected by a retriever for user queries. Evaluations examine the Enron Email and HealthcareMagic-101 datasets as retrieval data. At the same time, retrieval data can substantially reduce reproduction of memorized training information in targeted and prefix attacks. Disparate retrieval data is associated with less extraction of personal identifying information, while W3C-Email retrieval data leads the model to output more retrieval data rather than training data. Thus, retrieval data can both leak private database contents and help mitigate training-data leakage. Protecting it, including through differential privacy, remains future work.

Introduction

Retrieval data is incorporated into the input given to the large language model. The unit proposes replacing original retrieval data with synthetic retrieval data that preserves key contextual information while reducing privacy risks.

Utility of using synthetic data

Retrieval data is used to support answer generation for medical dialog and ODQA. The unit compares original and synthetic retrieval data and finds that synthetic data can preserve or improve answer-generation utility.

Privacy of using synthetic data

The retrieval dataset is the source from which attackers attempt to extract specific or broad information. The study compares leakage from original, synthetic, and refined data used in this role.
