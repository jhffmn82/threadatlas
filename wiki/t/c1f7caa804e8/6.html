This section, in brief. This unit reviews medical applications of large language models (LLMs), including BioGPT, PMC-LLaMA, BioMedLM, and Med-PaLM 2. It notes that prompt engineering offers a cost-efficient, non-fine-tuned approach, while retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is less explored in medicine and lacks support for evidence-based responses and term explanations needed in clinical settings. The unit describes RAG as improving accuracy and reducing hallucinations by allowing models to use specific datasets without additional training. It also discusses citation-generating systems and GraphRAG, which enhances complex reasoning through knowledge graphs but lacks features for attributed responses and has limited medical application.

Related Work 4.1 LLM for Medicine Large language models (LLMs) built on Trans- former architectures have advanced rapidly, lead- ing to specialized medical LLMs such as BioGPT (Luo et al., 2022), PMC-LLaMA (Wu et al., 2023), BioMedLM (Bolton et al., 2022), and Med-PaLM 2 (Singhal et al., 2023). While many are fine-tuned by large organizations, recent research has focused on cost-efficient, non-fine-tuned approaches, pri- marily using prompt engineering (Saab et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2023; Savage et al., 2024). RAG, as another non-finetuning approach, is rarely explored for medical applications (Miao et al., 2024; Xiong et al., 2024; Long et al., 2024) and lacks support for evidence-based responses and term explanations required in clinical settings. 4.2 Retrieval-augmented generation RAG (Lewis et al., 2021) enables models to use specific datasets without additional training, im- proving response accuracy and reducing hallucina- tions (Guu et al., 2020). RAG has shown strong results across various tasks, including generating responses with citations (Gao et al., 2023b; Slobod- kin et al., 2024; Qi et al., 2024; Nakano et al., 2021; Bohnet et al., 2022; Gao et al., 2023a,c; Schiman- ski et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024). GraphRAG (Hu et al., 2024) further enhances complex reason- ing by constructing knowledge graphs, but lacks specific design features for generating attributed responses, and its application in medical special- ization remains limited. 5
