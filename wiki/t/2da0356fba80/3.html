This section, in brief. The unit reviews safety research on LLMs, including fine-tuning, system-level guardrails, red-teaming, controllable generation, interpretability, unlearning, and benchmark evaluation. It notes that most safety studies focus on non-RAG settings, while safety research on RAG LLMs emphasizes corpus poisoning and also examines corpus leakage, retriever robustness, and document perturbations. The unit focuses on RAG scenarios with a carefully controlled corpus and relates them to the safety of LLM agents using tools. It also surveys red-teaming and states that no red-teaming methods have been designed specifically for RAG LLMs.

Related Work Safety of LLMs. Several stages in LLM develop- ment focus on aligning models with human values. Fine-tuning techniques, such as supervised learning (Köpf et al., 2024) and RLHF (Ouyang et al., 2022; Bai et al., 2022), are employed using safety-specific examples, like BeaverTails (Ji et al., 2024). System- level guardrails, such as Llama Guard (Inan et al., 2023) and ShieldGemma (Zeng et al., 2024b), help filter harmful outputs. Red-teaming (Lin et al., 2024) identifies vulnerabilities. Other work covers controllable safe generation (Xu et al., 2024), align- ment through interpretability (Zhou et al., 2024b; Sheshadri et al., 2024), unlearning unsafe behav- iors (Zhao et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2024b), and benchmark safety evaluation (Zhang et al., 2024a; Vidgen et al., 2024; Tedeschi et al., 2024; Longpre et al., 2024; Chao et al., 2024), etc. However, most studies focus on safety in non-RAG settings. Safety of RAG LLMs. Recent work on RAG LLM safety (Zhou et al., 2024a) mainly focuses on the threat of corpus poisoning, where injecting harmful documents leads to unsafe outputs (Gre- shake et al., 2023; Long et al., 2024; Xue et al., 2024; Zhong et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2024; Xi- ang et al., 2024a; Shafran et al., 2024; Deng et al., 2024a). Other concerns include: corpus leakage (Zeng et al., 2024a; Anderson et al., 2024), re- triever robustness to noise (Cho et al., 2024), and LLMs’ resilience to document perturbations (Yu et al., 2024). Our focus is a common scenario where the corpus is carefully controlled. This topic is also relevant to the safety of LLM agents using tools (Cai et al., 2023; Qin et al., 2024; Kapoor et al., 2024; Deng et al., 2024b; He et al., 2024; Xiang et al., 2024b; Chen et al., 2024b; Yuan et al., 2024; Tian et al., 2023; Hua et al., 2024), with a retriever being a special tool. Red-teaming LLMs. Red-teaming commonly in- volves jailbreaking LLMs (Schwinn et al., 2023; Jin et al., 2024; Chowdhury et al., 2024) to expose vulnerabilities using human-designed tests (Shen et al., 2023) or LLM-generated prompts (Ganguli et al., 2022; Perez et al., 2022; Hong et al., 2024; Ge et al., 2024). Black-box methods only require models’ discrete outputs (Liu et al., 2023; Lapid et al., 2023; Chao et al., 2023; Zeng et al., 2024c; Andriushchenko et al., 2024; Casper et al., 2023), while white-box techniques exploit internal param- eters for prompt optimization which are more effec- tive (Zou et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2023; Guo et al., 2024; Paulus et al., 2024). However, no methods have been designed specifically for RAG LLMs. 3
