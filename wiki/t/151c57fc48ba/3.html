This section, in brief written by a model
This unit discusses knowledge-intensive generation, where models must access external knowledge and produce natural-language responses, and the hallucination problem affecting pretrained language models. It examines retrieval marginalization and the false-negative passage problem, especially in multi-document question tasks. It reviews retrieval augmented generation methods including RAG, FiD, KG-FiD, KGI, RE2G, and Hindsight, emphasizing their effects on retrieval quality and passage selection. It also describes retrieval augmented generation with LLMs, which uses retrieved passages or knowledge graph triples to improve factuality and highlights the importance of powerful, robust retrievers.

Related Works Knowledge-intensive Generation Knowledge- intensive generation tasks are a series of Natural Language Generation (NLG) tasks that require ac- cess to large, external knowledge sources. Un- like other knowledge-intensive NLP tasks such as fact checking (Thorne et al., 2018) and slot filling (Levy et al., 2017; Elsahar et al., 2018), knowledge- intensive generation tasks further require models to generate natural language response, which are more challenging. Shuster et al. (2021) has shown that in knowledge-intensive generation tasks, pre- trained language models like BART (Lewis et al., 2020a), T5 (Raffel et al., 2020) and GPT series (Brown et al., 2020; OpenAI, 2023) significantly suffer from the hallucination problem (Roller et al., 2021), where they generate plausible looking state- ments that are factually incorrect. Research has been made to mitigate this problem by marginalizing the retrieval process and directly training the retriever with the final goal, e.g., answer label in multi-document QA (Ni et al., 2021). However, in knowledge-intensive gener- ation tasks, the marginalization methods do not work so well. Lewis et al. (2020b) reported that such marginalization methods get very limited im- provement in benefiting the retrieval quality on the generative QA task. Retrieval Augmented Generation To overcome the hallucination problem, people start to introduce retrieve-generate architectures for building retrieval augmented generation models. RAG (Lewis et al., 2020b) outperforms DPR (Karpukhin et al., 2020) by marginalizing the retrieval step to train the gen- erator and retriever jointly with the supervision of the label answer. FiD (Izacard and Grave, 2021) encodes the concatenation of the passages retrieved by pre-trained DPR and the original question sep- arately, and then fuses them with concatenation to the decoder. KG-FiD (Yu et al., 2022) utilize knowledge graphs to further enhance the retrieval quality by establishing the structural relationship among the retrieved passages. This illustrates the concept of integrating knowledge graphs with re- trieval augmented generation, but indirectly (i.e., the generator does not benefit from knowledge graphs). KGI (Chowdhury et al., 2022) provides a robust implementation of RAG, where the retriever is trained jointly with both the RAG setting and the vanilla DPR setting. RE2G (Glass et al., 2022) further extends RAG by adding a reranker in the retrieval step to integrate statistical retriever (e.g., TF-IDF and BM25) with DPR trained by RAG to improve the retrieval quality. Hindsight (Paranjape et al., 2022) trains another hindsight retriever that takes inputs of both queries and labels to retrieve label-relevant passages. The original retriever is trained by Evidence Lower Bound (ELBo) which includes both the marginalized loss as in RAG and the KL divergence for fitting the hindsight retriever. The state-of-the-art methods tend to retrieve label- relevant passages, whereas our approach seeks to consider a more diverse set of passages as suitable (knowledge-wise). Retrieval Augmented Generation with LLMs The utilization of retrieval augmented generation can be further expanded within the context of Large Language Models (LLMs) (Pan et al., 2023a), to address a critical concern associated with LLMs: the hallucination problem. He et al. (2023) use re- trieved passages to help LLMs "rethink" the ques- tion to remove factual errors in the decomposed reasoning steps obtained from the chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting (Wei et al., 2022). Zhao et al. 1015 (2023) utilize retrieved passages to post-edit the decomposed reasoning steps which contain factual errors obtained from the CoT prompting, to in- crease the prediction factuality. Besides retrieving documents (passages), Baek et al. (2023) shows that an off-the-shelf document retriever can also be used to retrieve knowledge graph triples for sup- porting LLMs. Most of these works utilize off-the- shelf retrievers, which emphasize the importance of powerful and robust retrievers. Their remark- able achievements underscore the central theme of our work, which focuses on training powerful and robust retrievers. 3
