The experiments evaluate the proposed methods on generative question answering and informative conversations using the MSMARCO QA dataset and KILT-WoW. They assess retrieval, end-to-end generation, and combined performance with metrics including recall, ROUGE-L, F1, BLEU, METEOR, R-Precision, and KILT scores. The knowledge sources contain 8.8M MSMARCO passages and approximately 24.5M passages from the 2019/08/01 Wikipedia dump. On the MSMARCO development set, the multi-task method performs best overall, while on KILT-WoW, RAC achieves the highest retrieval and end-to-end ROUGE-L results and DKS achieves the highest combined scores.

Experimental Setup We evaluate the proposed methods on two KIG tasks: generative question answering and informa- tive conversations. The designed experiments aim to answer the following research questions: • RQ1 Effectiveness: Can the proposed meth- ods improve the retrieval and end-to-end gen- eration quality? • RQ2 Robustness: Do the proposed methods facilitate better generalization at retrieving rel- evant passages? 5.1 Datasets For generative question answering, we use the NL- Gen split of the MSMARCO QA dataset (Nguyen et al., 2016). The answers of the NLGen split are rewritten by crowdworkers into well-formed answers for reducing overlaps between answers and gold passages. For the informative conversa- tion task, we use the Wizard of Wikipedia (WoW) dataset (Dinan et al., 2019) organized by KILT (Petroni et al., 2021). Since the test split of KILT-WoW is not pub- lished, we only report our best result on the test split as shown on the KILT leaderboard. All other experiments are done on the public development set. For MSMARCO dataset, since the challenge is retired, and we do not have access to the full test split, we further split the original train split to be the train set and validation set in our experiment, and report results on the original development split. All the evaluation metrics reported in this paper are obtained using the official scripts provided by MSMARCO4 and KILT.5 5.2 Metrics For the MSMARCO dataset, we report ROUGE-L and BLEU-1/4 scores for evaluating the end-to- end generation quality. We also report the recall rate of retrieved passages to evaluate the retrieval quality. For KILT-WoW dataset, we follow the evaluation setup in Petroni et al. (2021) and report: 1) ROUGE-L score and F1 score for the end-to- end evaluation; 2) R-Precision and Recall@5 for the retrieval evaluation; 3) KILT-RL and KILT-F1 for the combined evaluation. R-Precision metric is identical to Precision@1 in KILT-WoW dataset since the gold passage for every conversation is a single Wikipedia page. KILT-RL and KILT-F1 are two special metrics motivated by FEVER-score (Thorne et al., 2018), which calculate ROUGE-L score and F1 score as 0 when the retriever does not perfectly select the gold passage. Furthermore, for both datasets, we use METEOR (Banerjee and Lavie, 2005), which is a metric with a high corre- lation with human judgment, to mimic the human preference for the generations. 5.3 Knowledge Sources For both datasets, we retrieve passages from the knowledge sources which the gold passages are selected from. For MSMARCO, the knowledge source contains 8.8M passages extracted from 3.6M web documents retrieved by Bing, while for KILT-WoW, the knowledge source is extracted from the 2019/08/01 Wikipedia dump, which after our preprocessing contains approximately 24.5M passages. 5.4 Model Configuration Following Lewis et al. For the MSMARCO dataset, we find that using the same initialization strategy does not work well for baseline and our proposed meth- ods. We hypothesize that this is due to the questions of MSMARCO having a very different structure compared to the Natural Question dataset (where the initialized model is trained). Thus we first pre- train the DPR retriever on the MSMARCO passage ranking task, then use the pre-trained model as the initialization point for all MSMARCO exper- iments. For the training of sentence bottleneck auto-encoder, we use RoBERTaBASE provided on HuggingFace7 as the sentence encoder. At infer- ence time, we use top-5 retrieved passages to sup- port generation in both datasets. For fair com- parisons, we train our own RAG model with the same configuration as our proposed methods. Addi- tionally, to evaluate how baseline RAG and our pro- posed model benefit the retriever training, we also compare with the non-finetuned DPR (Karpukhin et al., 2020) model on the retrieval evaluation. We choose not to compare our model with other state-of-the-art works on the KILT leaderboard since the retriever settings are very different (both the retriever architecture and size) and the com- parison is helpless for showing the benefits of our work. 6
