This section, in brief. After the Trojan War, the Greeks were scattered to Libya, Italy, Sicily, Iberian islands, the Sangarius River, Cyprus, and other places. Antiphus son of Thessalus settled among the Pelasgians and named the country Thessaly, while Philoctetes settled in Crimissa in Italy and Demophon eventually settled in Cyprus after the death of Phyllis. Podalirius settled in the Carian Chersonese, and Amphilochus son of Alcmaeon and Mopsus killed each other in a contest for kingship. After a plague struck Locris, the Locrians sent two maidens to serve Athena at Ilium for a thousand years.

The dispute between Amphilochus and Mopsus is related more fully both by Tzetzes and Strabo (dl.cc.). According to them, Amphilochus wished to go for a time to Argos (probably Amphilochian Argos; see above, ili. 7. 7). So he departed after entrusting the kingdom or priesthood to Mopsus in his absence. Dissatisfied with the state of affairs at Argos, he returned in a year and reclaimed the kingdom or priesthood from Mopsus. But, acting on the principle Beati possidentes, the viceroy refused to cede the crown or the mitre to its proper owner; accordingly they had recourse to the ordeal of battle, in which both com- batants perished. Their bodies were buried in graves which could not be seen from each other; for the people built a tower between them, in order that the rivals, who had fought each other in life, might not scow] at each other in death.
