This section, in brief written by a model
Telegonus learned from Circe that he was the son of Odysseus and sailed to Ithaca in search of him. After Telegonus drove away cattle, Odysseus confronted him, and Telegonus wounded Odysseus with a spear fitted with a sting-ray spine, causing his death. Telegonus recognized Odysseus, lamented, and took his body and Penelope to Circe, where he married Penelope. The text also records alternative accounts of Penelope’s fate and of Odysseus’s later exile and death.

According to Aelian, the wound inflicted by the sting-ray is incurable. Hercules is said to have lost one of his fingers by the bite of a sting-ray (Ptolemy Hephaest., Nov.
