This section, in brief. The Russian story tells of a smith who seeks evil, meets Likho in the form of a one-eyed woman, and escapes after tricking and injuring her, though he loses a hand to a golden-handled hatchet. The Esthonian story similarly has a Barn-carl blind the devil with molten lead after calling himself “Myself,” a trick compared with Ulysses’ use of “Nobody” against Polyphemus. In a Lapp tale, four Lapps encounter a giant in his cave, blind him with molten lead, and escape in goat skins while carrying away treasure. A related Lapp variant describes Slyboots blinding the Stalo with molten lead and escaping from his sheepfold in a ram’s skin.

Many hundred years ago, we are told, when there were still giants and trolls among the mountains and hills, a man might easily stumble on a troll against his will when he passed the boundary of his home-land. Well, it chanced once on a time that four Lapps, who had gone out to seek their reindeer, lost their way on the mountains. Three whole days and as many nights did they wander about without coming to a human habitation, and they were near dead with hunger and weariness when at last they spied a light that seemed to shine at the foot of a mountain, whose top reached the clouds. As the Lapps were very sharp set, they gathered round the kettle and began to eat the beef. When they had satisfied their hunger, they put out the fire by pouring the hot water from the kettle on it, and having done so they filled the kettle with cold water. What was left of the beef in the kettle they hid. Then, poking about in the cave, they discovered great store of gold and silver and other precious things, but they did not dare to lay hands on them as not knowing to whom all these riches might belong.
