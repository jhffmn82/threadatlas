This section, in brief. The notes concern the Pleiades and ancient accounts of their names, ancestry, and identity. Ancient sources generally agreed on the names of the seven Pleiades, with Aratus giving the same names and order as Apollodorus. A Scholiast on Theocritus offered a different list except for Maia and reported, citing Callimachus, that the Pleiades were daughters of the queen of the Amazons. Their father was commonly identified as Atlas, leading to the alternative name Atlantides, while the origin of the name Pleiades was disputed.

There was a general agreement among the ancients as to the names of the seven Pleiades. Aratus, for example, gives the same names as Apollodorus and in the same order. However, with the exception of Maia, a different list of names is given by the Scholiast on Theocritus (xiii. 25), who tells us further, on the authority of Callimachus, that they were the daughters of the queen of the Amazons. As their father was commonly said to be Atlas, they were sometimes called Atlantides (Apollodorus, below ; Diodorus Siculus, iii. 60. 4; compare Hesiod, Works and Days, 382).
