This section, in brief. The unit compares the Homeric story of Ulysses and Polyphemus with oral and written variants from Sicily, Greece, Albania, Hungary, Syria, and The Arabian Nights. These stories commonly feature travellers trapped by a giant or Cyclops, the blinding of the captor, and escape among sheep or beneath a sheep’s body. The Sicilian, Greek, Albanian, Hungarian, and Arabian versions vary in the number of captives, the giant’s eyes, and the means of escape. Several versions omit either the blinding or the characteristic escape by sheep or sheepskin, while the Syrian tale reverses the usual pattern by restoring the giant’s sight.
