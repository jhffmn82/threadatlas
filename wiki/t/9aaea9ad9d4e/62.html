This section, in brief. This unit examines the widespread Ulysses and Polyphemus story and compares the Homeric account with later oral and literary versions. It focuses especially on John of Haute-Seille's Dolopathos, which preserves a version in which a famous robber encounters a giant, blinds him with boiling oil, escapes disguised among sheep, and loses a finger because of a magic ring. The Dolopathos version differs from the Odyssey by making the giant blear-eyed rather than one-eyed, having the blinding occur through a trick with the giant's consent, and adding the magic-ring episode. These differences are presented as evidence that John drew on oral tradition rather than directly on the Odyssey.

Arrived there, we had the pleasure of finding him not at home, so we carried off all the gold and silver on which we could lay hands.
