The unit compares two accounts of Idomeneus's exile from Crete after the siege of Troy: Apollodorus attributes it to Leucus's seizure of the kingdom, while Servius says that Idomeneus vowed to Neptune to sacrifice the first person he met and was banished after attempting to sacrifice his child during a pestilence. It compares this story with Maeander's vow to sacrifice the first person who congratulated him and with Jephthah's vow and sacrifice of his daughter. It then relates several European folktales in which a parent promises a supernatural being the first person encountered at home, usually the beloved youngest daughter, who is carried away but often discovers that the apparent monster is an enchanted prince.
