This section, in brief. Odysseus arranges for a forged letter from Priam to Palamedes and gold to be found, leading Agamemnon to have Palamedes stoned as a traitor. Menelaus and Odysseus go to Cinyras in Cyprus, who promises fifty ships but sends only one and fashions the rest from earth. The Greek army assembles at Aulis, where a serpent omen leads Calchas to declare that Troy will be captured after ten years. The Greek expedition first reaches Mysia, where Telephus wounds Achilles, but Telephus is later healed by the rust of the Pelian spear and shows the Greeks the course to Troy.

Aristophanes ridiculed the rags and tatters in which Telephus appeared on the stage in Euripides’s play (Acharn. 430 sqq.). Apollo- dorus may have had the passage of Euripides or the parody of Aristophanes in mind when he describes Telephus as clad in rags. The cure of a wound by an application to it of rust from the weapon which inflicted the hurt is not to be explained, as Pliny supposed, by any medicinal property inherent in rust as such, else the rust from any weapon would serve the purpose. It is clearly a folk-lore remedy based on the principle of sympathetic magic. Similarly Iphiclus was cured of impotence by the rust of the same knife which had caused the infirmity.
