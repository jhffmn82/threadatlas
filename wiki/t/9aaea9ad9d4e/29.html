This section, in brief. The unit recounts how Thyestes’s son Aegisthus killed Atreus and restored the kingdom to Thyestes, while the nurse saved Agamemnon and Menelaus by taking them to Sicyon. Polyphides and Oeneus sheltered them before Tyndareus brought them back, after which Agamemnon married Clytaemnestra and Menelaus married Helen. Alexander judged Aphrodite the fairest, abducted Helen from Sparta, and brought her toward Troy, prompting Menelaus and Agamemnon to invoke the suitors’ oaths and raise a Greek expedition. Odysseus feigned madness to avoid the expedition, but Palamedes exposed the deception by threatening Telemachus.

The usual story seems to have been that to support his pretence of in- sanity Ulysses yoked an ox and a horse or an ass to the plough and sowed salt. While he was busy fertilizing the fields in this fashion, the Greek envoys arrived, and Palamedes, seeing through the deception, laid the infant son of Ulysses in front of the plough, whereupon the father at once checked the plough and betrayed his sanity. However, Lucian agrees with Apollodorus in saying that Palamedes threatened the child with his sword, though at the same time, by mentioning the unlike animals yoked together, he shows that he had the scene of the ploughing in his mind. His description purports to be based on a picture, probably a famous picture of the scene which was still exhibited at Ephesus in the time of Pliny (Nat. Hist. xxxv. 129).
