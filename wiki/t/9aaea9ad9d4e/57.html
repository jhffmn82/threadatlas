This section, in brief. The unit compares beliefs about twins and their alleged power to multiply animals, fish, plants, and crops. It begins with Apollo, who was said to make the cattle of Admetus bear twins, and relates this to Jacob and other traditions about twins as sources of fertility. Among the Tsimshian, Nootkas, Kwakiutl, Baganda, Bateso, and Basoga, twins or their parents attract or increase salmon, livestock, children, plantains, crops, or seed. It also records opposing beliefs in which the birth of twins brings drought, famine, crop failure, disease, cattle deaths, or other misfortune, sometimes leading to the exposure or killing of twins.
