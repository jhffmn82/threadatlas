This section, in brief written by a model
Castor and Pollux, the Dioscuri, were renowned for war and boxing and married the daughters of Leucippus after carrying them off from Messene. With Idas and Lynceus, the sons of Aphareus, they obtained cattle booty from Arcadia, but Idas seized the entire spoil after rapidly consuming his own and his brother’s shares. The Dioscuri attacked Messene to recover the cattle and other booty, leading to the deaths of Castor, Lynceus, and Idas and to Pollux’s ascent to heaven. Because Pollux refused immortality while Castor was dead, Zeus granted them alternating existence among the gods and mortals.

And having driven booty of cattle from Arcadia, in company with Idas and Lynceus, sons of Aphareus, they allowed Idas to divide the spoil. He cut a cow in four and said that one half of the booty should be his who ate his share first, and that the rest should be his who ate his share second. And before they knew where they were, Idas had swallowed his own share first and likewise his brother’s, and with him had driven oft the captured cattle to Messene. But the Dioscuri marched against Messene, and drove away that cattle and much else besides. And they lay in wait for Idas and Lynceus. But Lynceus spied Castor and discovered him to Idas, who killed him. Pollux chased them and slew Lynceus by throwing his spear, but in pursuing Lynceus he was wounded in the head with a stone thrown by him, and fell down in a swoon.
