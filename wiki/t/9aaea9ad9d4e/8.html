This section, in brief. The unit recounts the origin and fate of the Palladium, an image made by Athena for Pallas and later thrown by Zeus into the land of Ilium, where Ilus built it a temple. It then traces the lineage of Laomedon, including Podarces, who became Priam after Ilium was captured by Heracles. Priam’s son Aesacus interpreted Hecuba’s dream of a firebrand as a prophecy that her unborn child would destroy the city and advised exposing the infant. The exposed child, Paris, was nursed by a bear, raised by Agelaus, and later called Alexander for defending flocks and repelling robbers.
