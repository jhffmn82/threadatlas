This section, in brief written by a model
This unit examines whether retrieval data affects the memorization and reproduction of training-set information by large language models in retrieval-augmented generation. Using GPT-Neo-1.3B and Enron_Mail examples, the study compares targeted and prefix attacks with no retrieval, noise or prompts, and several retrieval datasets. Retrieval data substantially reduces extraction of personal identifying information and reconstruction of memorized text, although W3C-Email retrieval data leads the model to output more retrieval data instead of training data. The authors suggest that retrieval shifts generation toward the retrieved context and may reduce privacy leakage from training data while creating risks for information extraction from retrieval data itself.

If the similarity score, measured by the ROUGE-L metric, exceeded 0.5, we considered a successful extraction. Specifically, it leads to a reduction in successful text reconstructions from over 200 cases to fewer than 40. One possible reason is that incorporating external data makes LLMs less reliant on training data but focuses on leveraging information from retrieved contexts. As evidenced by the Bayes Theorem in (Xie et al., 2021), when leveraging external diverse datasets during inference, the model generates new tokens based on the conditional distribution given the re- trieved data R(q, D) and q. Such a distribution is different from the one only given q, and relies more on the retrieved data R(q, D). We include more de- tailed theoretical discussion in Appendix A.1Such hypothesis is empirically supported by our results in Table 3. We can observe that when the retrieval data comprises entirely disparate data types, the LLM demonstrates a marked inability to extract PIIs, while when the retrieval data includes another PII dataset (W3C-Email), we found the LLM tends to output more retrieval data instead of training data. These findings have significant implications. First, integrating retrieval data reduces the risk of privacy leaks from LLMs’ training data, making it harder for attackers to access this information. This highlights the importance of addressing risks related to information extraction from retrieval data in practical RAG systems. Second, RAG can effec- tively protect private information in LLMs’ training data.
