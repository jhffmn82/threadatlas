This section, in brief. The unit concerns limitations in the study of retrieval augmentation, which focused primarily on inference-stage applications rather than pre-training or fine-tuning. It identifies privacy risks in commonly employed retrieval-augmented generation systems and notes that other retrieval-based language models have different components and architectures requiring further investigation. It also presents differential privacy as a potential strategy for protecting retrieval data and identifies the use of retrieval-augmented generation systems to safeguard training data as an open research question.

7 Limitations In our research, we concentrated primarily on the application of retrieval augmentation during the in- ference stage, without delving into its integration during pre-training or fine-tuning phases. Future work will aim to explore these compelling areas. Moreover, while our study has highlighted the pri- vacy risks associated with commonly employed retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems, other retrieval-based language models (LMs) fea- ture distinct components and architectures (Huang et al., 2023; Borgeaud et al., 2022) that warrant fur- ther investigation. In addition, developing effective strategies like differential privacy((Feyisetan et al., 2020; Utpala et al., 2023; Carvalho et al., 2023)) to protect retrieval data and leveraging RAG systems for the safeguarding of training data represent open research questions that we intend to pursue.
