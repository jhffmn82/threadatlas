Appendix A.1 Theoretical Understanding We consider the following theoretical setup. Denote Dtr = {(xi, yi)}N as the pre-training dataset with N pairs of prefix xi and suffix yi. Denote p(y|x) as the LLM’s output probability of y given input x, and further define L as the loss function. The pre-training loss then can be written as E(x,y)∈DtrL(p(y|x)), and the pre-training finds the optimal probability function p, denoted as p∗, to minimize the loss. To argue why the leakage of pre-training data is mitigated in RAG, we need to explain (1) why pre- training memorization happens using LLM without RAG, and (2) why pre-training memorization is reduced after introducing RAG. For (1), in literature of neural tangent kernel (e.g., Ba et al. 2019), double-descent phenomenon (e.g., Hastie et al. 2022), it is widely observed that when the model size is sufficiently large, the model smoothly interpolates the training data. That is, ∀x ∈{x1, . . . , xN}, arg max y p∗(y|xi) = yi, i.e., given a prefix in the pre-training dataset, the LLM can output the exact suffix yi. In addition to having a prefix x which exactly appears in Dtr, we can further relax the condition on the prefix x. Denote d(·, ·) as some distance metric between two sequences of tokens, e.g., cosine similarity, ROUGE, or BLEU. Also define xNN as the nearest neighbor of x in {xi}i=1,...,N based on the distance metric d(·, ·). Again, since the model smoothly interpolates the training data, we also have |p∗(·|x) −p∗(·|xNN)| = O(d(x, xNN)) Because of the smoothness, which means that as long as x is similar to xNN and leads to a small enough d(x, xNN), there is no great difference between p∗(·|x) and p∗(·|xNN), and the LLM tends to output the suffix corresponding to xNN. Such a result implies that the leakage of pre-training data in LLM can be a prevalent issue. On the other hand, to explain (2), when introducing RAG into the system, the retrieval dataset does not appear in the pre-training data, so it is not sufficiently close to the pre-training dataset. Consider a prefix x from Dtr, a standard RAG system will first retrieve the nearest contents from the retrieval dataset based on d(·, ·), denoted as xRAG. Then d(x, xRAG) can be quite large because xRAG /∈Dtr. Then the LLM will generate output based on xRAG, i.e. the output distribution is p∗(·|xRAG). According to Eq.(1), there exists a substantial shift between the output distribution with RAG, p∗(·|xRAG), and the output distribution without RAG, p∗(·|x). Therefore, with the involvement of retrieved contents, the pre-training data leakage is mitigated. We provide some intuition and theoretical analysis here. A more detailed quantification of the difference between the two distributions requires further investigation, and we leave it for future exploration.
