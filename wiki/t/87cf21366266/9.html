The limitations concern the dataset, retrieval process, task scope, and prompting methods used in the work. The dataset is mainly collected from existing sources, and GPT-4 is used to filter questions, though some filtering errors may remain despite preliminary human checking. The study focuses on short-form QA and does not assess long-form generation tasks, while acknowledging that retrieved documents may be noisy or insufficient. The authors also do not perform prompt tuning, although better prompts may further improve performance.

Limitations We identify the limitations of our work as follows: • We mainly collect data from existing data sources and use GPT-4 for filtering out answer- able questions. While we have done prelimi- nary human checking in Appendix A.5, it is possible that some questions in the dataset do not require additional information for LLMs to answer. Future work could develop ad- vanced algorithms to do more efficient and rigour filtering. • We primarily focus on short-form QA in this paper and do not assess long-form generation tasks. It should be noted that methods, includ- ing Jiang et al. (2023); Asai et al. (2023b), are capable of long-form generation tasks. Self-RAG can also perform sophisticated self- reflection, which goes beyond adaptive re- trieval. • We acknowledge that some of the retrieved documents may not contain the answers or the information needed to answer the questions. While improving retrieval relevance and ac- curacy is out of the scope of this paper, noisy context may interfere with LLMs and hurt the QA performance. • While we find our prompt templates work well, we do not perform prompt tuning in this paper. We acknowledge that prompt tem- plates can be sensitive to LLMs, and there are methods to find optimal prompts (Shin et al., 2020; Deng et al., 2022). We believe optimal prompts can be found and further improve performance. We leave this as a future work.
