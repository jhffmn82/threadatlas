This section, in brief. The unit describes how large language models (LLMs) have advanced natural language processing and enabled many applications. It explains that reliance on pre-trained knowledge limits LLMs’ ability to integrate and reason over dynamically updated external information, especially in academic research. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is presented as a framework that combines retrieval mechanisms with generative capabilities to improve contextual understanding and response quality. Recent research is described as focusing on two primary directions for improving RAG, although the unit provides only the beginning of the first direction.
