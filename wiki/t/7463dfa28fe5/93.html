This section, in brief written by a model
At an assembly in Syracuse, Diocles proposes executing the Athenian generals, sending the other prisoners to the quarries, selling those who aided the Athenians, and restricting prisoners' food. Hermocrates argues that victory should be used with moderation and humanity, while Nicholaus, despite losing two sons in the war, delivers an extended speech urging mercy toward the defeated Athenians. Nicholaus maintains that clemency would bring Syracuse honour, encourage peace, and protect the city if fortunes later changed. He appeals to Greek customs and examples in which surrendered prisoners were spared or ransomed.
