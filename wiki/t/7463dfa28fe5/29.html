This section, in brief written by a model
The unit describes gold mines in the confines of Egypt and the neighbouring countries of Arabia and Ethiopia, together with the methods used to extract and refine gold. Kings of Egypt send criminals, captives, falsely accused persons, and sometimes their relatives to the mines, where they labor in fetters under severe guard and punishment. Labourers break and grind gold-bearing rock, after which other workers wash, purify, weigh, and furnace-refine the gold. The account concludes that gold is obtained and preserved with great difficulty, although its use brings both pleasure and sorrow.

In the confines of Egypt, and the neighbouring countries of Ara- bia and Ethiopia, there is a place full of rich gold mines, out of which, with much cost and pains of many labourers, gold is dug. The soil here naturally is black, but in the body of the earth run many white veins, shining with white marble, and glistering with all sorts of other bright metals; out of which laborious mines those ap- pointed overseers cause the gold to be dug up by the labour of a vast multitude of people. For the kings of Egypt condemn to these mines notorious criminals, captives taken in war, persons sometimes falsely accused, or such against whom the king is incensed; and that not only they themselves, but sometimes all their kindred and relations together with them, are sent to work here, both to punbh them, and by their labour to advance the profit and gain of the king:.
