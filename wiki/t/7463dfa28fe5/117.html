This section, in brief. The Carthaginians, defeated by Dionysius on land and sea at Syracuse, offered him three hundred talents in exchange for permission to evacuate their army, but only Carthaginian citizens were allowed to depart. Himilco escaped with citizens aboard forty galleys, while Dionysius captured or dispersed the remaining army and incorporated the Spaniards into his mercenaries. After the defeat, Carthage faced a revolt in Africa, but the rebels dispersed because of poor supplies, quarrels, treachery, and Carthaginian access to provisions by sea. Dionysius then secured his rule by settling his mercenaries at Leontium, recruiting new troops, and extending his power in Sicily.
