This section, in brief. Diodorus Siculus’s Book V begins a description of the islands, starting with Sicily and its ancient peoples, geography, traditions, and religious practices. It recounts traditions linking Sicily with Ceres, Proserpine, Pluto, the first growth of wheat, and the rape of Proserpine at Enna and Syracuse. It describes the Sicanians, Sicilians, Greek colonization of Sicily, the Aeolian Islands and Lipara, and the reputed history of Aeolus. It ends by explaining how the island of Osteodes received its name after Carthaginian mercenaries were abandoned there and died.
