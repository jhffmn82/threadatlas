This section, in brief. Cyrus, supported by the Lacedaemonians and Greek mercenaries, raised an army and marched from Asia through Cilicia and Syria toward Babylon to challenge his brother, Artaxerxes, for the Persian kingdom. Artaxerxes assembled a much larger army and met Cyrus near the Euphrates, where the Grecians routed the forces opposing them. Cyrus wounded Artaxerxes with a javelin but was then mortally wounded and killed by a Persian soldier, causing his army to lose the battle. The Greek forces nevertheless defeated the barbarians who attacked them after Cyrus's death.
