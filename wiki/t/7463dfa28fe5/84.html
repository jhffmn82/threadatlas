This section, in brief written by a model
The Corinthian war began when Epidamnus, a colony associated with Corcyra and Corinth, was troubled by civil strife and appealed for aid, leading Corinth and Corcyra into conflict. Corinth and Corcyra prepared fleets, fought at sea, and drew Athens into an alliance with Corcyra, whose intervention helped secure victory over Corinth. Corinth and Macedonia then encouraged revolts at Potidea and among the cities of Chalcidica, prompting Athens to besiege Potidea and defeat a relieving force near Pallene. During the same period, the Romans fought the Volsci, colonies were established in Italy, and Thucydides began his history of the war between Athens and the Lacedaemonians.
