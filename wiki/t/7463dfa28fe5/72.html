This section, in brief. After the victory at Plataea, the Athenians rebuilt the walls of Athens, despite opposition from the Lacedaemonians, and Themistocles secured their completion through diplomacy and deception. Themistocles later promoted the enlargement of the harbor at Piraeus, the expansion of the Athenian fleet, and policies to attract foreign residents and artisans. Pausanias, the Spartan commander, was exposed for conspiring with the Persians, while Aristides gained the allegiance of the Greek cities through his justice and moderation. Aristides also established a common war fund at Delos and administered contributions totaling 560 talents.
