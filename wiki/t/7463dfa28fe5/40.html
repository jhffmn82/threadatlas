This section, in brief. Diodorus Siculus explains that he will treat the antiquities of the Grecians, including their gods, demi-gods, famous men, and beneficial inventions. He begins with Dionysius, also called Bacchus, whom the Greeks say discovered wine and wine-making, civilized many nations, and led an expedition to India. The account also describes Cadmus, Semele, Jupiter, Priapus, Hermaphroditus, and the Muses before turning to Hercules and the difficulty of relating his extraordinary labours. Diodorus argues that ancient traditions should not be rejected merely because their marvels seem incredible.
