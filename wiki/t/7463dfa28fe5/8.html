This section, in brief. Diodorus Siculus describes Egyptian accounts of their earliest food, dwellings, religion, rulers, and royal monuments. The Egyptians are said to have progressed from roots, herbs, fish, and cattle to cultivated fruits and bread, while their kings were credited with discovering necessities of life. The account names Menis as the first human king, describes the building and splendor of Thebes under Busiris's successor, and gives an extensive description of Osymandyas's sepulchre. It also records the destruction and removal of Egyptian treasures by Cambyses and the Persians.
