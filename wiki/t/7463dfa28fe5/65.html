This section, in brief. Diodorus Siculus begins Book XI with Xerxes's expedition into Greece, which was planned with Mardonius's encouragement and coordinated with a Carthaginian invasion of Greece. Xerxes assembled a vast army and navy, crossed the Hellespont, and advanced through Thrace while the Grecian confederacy prepared defenses at Tempe, Thermopylae, and Artemisium. Leonidas commanded the Greek forces at Thermopylae, where they repeatedly defeated Persian attacks until a Trachinian revealed a mountain route that allowed the Persians to threaten their rear. The unit ends with the Greeks holding a midnight council to decide whether to abandon the pass.
