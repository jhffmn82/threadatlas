This section, in brief. The unit describes Malta, Gaulus, Cercina, Cyrnus or Corsica, Sardinia, Pityusa, and the Gymnesian islands, including their locations, resources, inhabitants, customs, and political histories. Malta is presented as a wealthy Phoenician settlement, while Corsica and Sardinia are described through their cities, peoples, resources, and resistance to foreign powers. The Gymnesian islands, also called Baleares, are known for their skilled slingers and distinctive customs. The account then turns to Madeira, a fertile island in the ocean whose discovery is attributed to Phoenician merchants driven there by a storm.
