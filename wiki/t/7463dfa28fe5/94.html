This section, in brief. The unit recounts a debate at Syracuse over the fate of the defeated Athenians and their allies after the Sicilian expedition. Arguments for mercy emphasize Athens's importance to learning, the coercion of its allies, and Nicias's earlier opposition to the expedition, while Gylippus argues that the Athenians deserve punishment for invading Sicily and planning to enslave its peoples. Gylippus cites Athenian cruelty toward Mitylene, Melos, and Scione, and warns that releasing the prisoners would endanger Syracuse and offend the Lacedaemonians. The Syracusans follow Diocles's advice: the generals and their confederates are executed, the Athenian citizens are sent to the quarries, and some well-educated prisoners are released by young men.
