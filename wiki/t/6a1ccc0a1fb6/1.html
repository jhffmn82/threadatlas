Abstract Naive Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) focuses on individual documents during re- trieval and, as a result, falls short in han- dling networked documents which are very popular in many applications such as cita- tion graphs, social media, and knowledge graphs. To overcome this limitation, we in- troduce Graph Retrieval-Augmented Genera- tion (GRAG), which tackles the fundamental challenges in retrieving textual subgraphs and integrating the joint textual and topological in- formation into Large Language Models (LLMs) to enhance its generation. To enable efficient textual subgraph retrieval, we propose a novel divide-and-conquer strategy that retrieves the optimal subgraph structure in linear time. To achieve graph context-aware generation, incor- porate textual graphs into LLMs through two complementary views—the text view and the graph view—enabling LLMs to more effec- tively comprehend and utilize the graph con- text. Extensive experiments on graph reasoning benchmarks demonstrate that in scenarios re- quiring multi-hop reasoning on textual graphs, our GRAG approach significantly outperforms current state-of-the-art RAG methods. Our datasets as well as codes of GRAG are available at https://github.com/HuieL/GRAG. 1
