This section, in brief. After Hercules had completed his labours in eight years and a month, Eurystheus assigned him an eleventh labour: fetching golden apples from the Hesperides. The apples were said to be located by Atlas in Hyperborea rather than in Libya, and to have been given by Earth to Zeus when he married Hera. An immortal, hundred-headed dragon born of Typhon and Echidna guarded the apples, together with the Hesperides. On his journey, Hercules reached the river Echedorus, where Cycnus challenged him to single combat, but a thunderbolt separated them.

Ancient mathemat- icians defined a ‘‘ great year” as the period at the end of which the sun, moon, and planets again occupy the same positions relatively to each other which they occupied at the beginning ; but on the length of the period opinions were much divided.
