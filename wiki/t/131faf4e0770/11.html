This section, in brief. The ExtendedQA dataset consists of 24 expert-curated radiology questions with corresponding reference standard answers and subspecialty labels. The questions cover diagnostic findings and differentials in chest, cardiac, gastrointestinal, genitourinary, musculoskeletal, vascular, emergency, pediatric, and interventional radiology. Computed tomography, magnetic resonance imaging, and X-ray findings are used to identify conditions including malignancy, vascular disease, organ lesions, bone disorders, and imaging artifacts.

Appendix S2 The complete ExtendedQA dataset, consisting of 24 expert-curated questions, with all the questions and their corresponding answers is as follows: Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q1 Question: A 74 year-old patient presents with progressive shortness of breath over the last years. He is a former mining worker. CT shows calcified pleural plaques. What is the most likely diagnosis? Reference Standard Answer: Asbestosis. Supspecialties: Chest, Computed Tomography. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q2 Question: What is considered a malignant course of the coronary arteries? Reference Standard Answer: Interarterial course. Supspecialties: Cardiac. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q3 Question: What are the differential diagnoses of a solid mass in the anterosuperior mediastinum? Reference Standard Answer: Thymoma, thyroid (mass), lymphoma, teratoma (and germ cell tumors), thoracic aorta (e.g., aneurysm). Supspecialties: Chest. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q4 Question: What are differential diagnoses of pulmonary cavities? Reference Standard Answer: Cancer, autoimmune diseases (granulomatosis with polyangiitis, rheumatoid arthritis), vascular (bland/septic emboli), infection (e.g., tuberculosis), trauma (pneumatoceles), youth (CPAM, sequestration, bronchogenic cyst); Cavity mnemonic. Supspecialties: Chest. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q5 Question: Which MRI contrast agent can be used to detect an insufficiency of a biliary anastomosis? Reference Standard Answer: Eovist / Gadoxetic acid or disodium. Supspecialties: Gastrointestinal, Magnetic Resonance Imaging. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q6 Question: What are possible distributions of pumonary nodules? Name one differential diagnosis for each distribution. Reference Standard Answer: Perilymphatic (e.g., sarcoidosis), centrilobular (special form: tree-in-bud; e.g., tuberculosis), diffuse (e.g., disseminated pulmonary metastasis). Supspecialties: Chest. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q7 Question: I am looking at a prostate MRI and see a lesion in the right posterolateral peripheral zone. The lesion is hypointense in T2, hyperintense in DWI, hypointense in ADC and has a strong and early signal enhancement after contrast administration. What kind of lesion could this be? Reference Standard Answer: Prostate cancer. Supspecialties: Genitourinary, Magnetic Resonance Imaging. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q8 Question: I am looking at an X-ray of the lumbar spine on a sagittal view. There are prominent endplate densities at multiple contiguous vertebral levels, leading to a sclerotic-lucent-sclerotic appearance. The Patient has a history of renal insufficiency. What is this appearance of the spine called and what is the most likely diagnosis? Reference Standard Answer: Rugger jersey spine; secondary hyperparathyroidism. Supspecialties: Musculoskeletal. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q9 Question: I see a prominent pulmonary embolus in the right pulmonaly artery on a CT pulmonary angiography. What are signs of right ventricular dysfunction? Reference Standard Answer: Abnormal position of the interventricular septum, contrast reflux into inferior vena cava/liver veins, increased diameter of the right ventricle. Supspecialties: Cardiac, Chest, Computed Tomography, Emergency Radiology. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q10 Question: A 24 year-old patient presents with right upper quadrant pain, fever and jaundice. MRI of the liver shows T1-hypointense, T2-hyperintense, segmental dilatation of the intrahepatic bile ducts, leading to a multicystic appearance. There are multiple concrements in the intrahepatic bile ducts. The patient has no history of bowel disease. What is the most likely diagnosis? 37 Reference Standard Answer: Caroli-disease. Supspecialties: Emergency Radiology, Musculoskeletal. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q11 Question: I am looking at an X-ray of the knee on a anteroposterior view of a patient after trauma. I see an elliptic bone fragment projected parallel to the lateral aspect of the tibial plateau. What is the most likely diagnosis? Reference Standard Answer: Segond fracture, indicative of a rupture of the anterior cruciate ligament. Supspecialties: Gastrointestinal, Magnetic Resonance Imaging. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q12 Question: I see a right-sided enlarged lymph node posterolateral to the anterior belly of the digastric muscles on a CT of the neck. What is the corresponding neck lymph node level? Reference Standard Answer: Level Ib. Supspecialties: Computed Tomography, Head and Neck. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q13 Question: I am looking at a CT angiography of the abdomen at the arterial phase. I see a complete defect of contrast in the infrarenal aorta extending into both common iliac arteries. What is this condition called? Reference Standard Answer: Leriche syndrome. Supspecialties: Computed Tomography, Emergency Radiology, Vascular Imaging. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q14 Question: I see a lesion in an MRI of the liver that is hyperintense on T2 relative to the surrounding liver parenchyma, but less than the intensity of water. Upon contrast administration, the lesion shows early peripheral nodular enhancement that is shifting centripetally on delayed images. What is the most likely diagnosis? Reference Standard Answer: Hepatic hemangioma. Supspecialties: Gastrointestinal, Magnetic Resonance Imaging. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q15 Question: What is the most commonly used chemotherapy agent in transcatheter arterial chemoembolisation of hepatocellular carcinomas? Reference Standard Answer: Doxorubicin. Supspecialties: Interventional Radiology. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q16 Question: I see an adrenal lesion in an MRI of the epigastric region. The lesion demonstrates a strong signal dropout on opposed-phase images. What is the most likely diagnosis? Reference Standard Answer: Adrenal adenoma. Supspecialties: Gastrointestinal, Magnetic Resonance Imaging. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q17 Question: I am looking at a contrast-enhanced CT of the abdomen on the arterial phase in a patient with an infrarenal abdominal aortic aneurysm that has been treated by EVAR. I see contrast agent in the aneurysmal sac, originating from a left-sided lumbar artery. What is the most likely diagnosis? Reference Standard Answer: Endoleak type IIa. Supspecialties: Computed Tomography, Vascular Imaging. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q18 Question: Strongly hyperdense lesions are noted in the segmental pulmonary arteries of the right lower lobe on a non- contrast CT of the thorax. The patient has a history of vertebroplasty. What kind of lesions could that be? Reference Standard Answer: Pulmonary cement embolism. Supspecialties: Chest, Computed Tomography, Musculoskeletal. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q19 Question: I see a lesion in an MRI of the liver that is slightly hyperintense on T2 and hypointense on T1 relative to the surrounding liver parenchyma. The lesion shows heterogenous arterial enhancement and a washout in the venous phase. The lesion shows a central scar on T2w imaging. What is the most likely diagnosis? Reference Standard Answer: Fibrolamellar hepatocellular carcinoma. Supspecialties: Gastrointestinal, Magnetic Resonance Imaging. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q20 Question: I see prominent endplate densities at multiple contiguous vertebral levels on a sagittal X-ray of the spine and a well-defined lesion of the sixth rib on a chest X-ray. What is the most likely underlying condition? Reference Standard Answer: Hyperparathyroidism (Rugger-Jersey-Spine and 'brown tumor'). Supspecialties: Chest, Musculoskeletal. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q21 Question: After application of contrast agent into a percutaneous transhepatic biliary drainage, portal vein branches distal to the biliary entry of the drainage show contrast enhancement. What is the most likely diagnosis? Reference Standard Answer: Portobiliary fistula. Supspecialties: Gastrointestinal, Magnetic Resonance Imaging. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q22 38 Question: I see densily sclerotic endplates in a sagittal X-ray of the spine of a 14 year old patient. What is the most likely diagnosis? Reference Standard Answer: Osteopetrosis. Supspecialties: Musculoskeletal, Pediatric. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q23 Question: I see mixed opacified and unopacified blood in the IVC at and cranial of the renal veins after on a abdominal CT scan at an early contrast phase after i.v. contrast application. The infrarenal vena cava appears mostly unopacified. What is the most likely diagnosis? Reference Standard Answer: Contrast flow phenomenon in inferior vena cava (artifact). Supspecialties: Computed Tomography, Vascular Imaging. Question Identifier: ExtendedQA-Q24 Question: I see a synovial mass on a knee MRI. The mass is T1 and T2 hyperintense. There is a concomitant joint effusion. What is the most likely diagnosis? Reference Standard Answer: Lipoma arborescens. Supspecialties: Magnetic Resonance Imaging, Musculoskeletal.
