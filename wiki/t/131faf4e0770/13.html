Appendix S4 Time Comparison Between Conventional QA and RadioRAG-powered QA The time required for question answering (QA) varies depending on the model used and the retrieval approach. Below, we provide an estimate of the time differences between conventional QA and RadioRAG-powered QA, as observed in our experiments using the RSNA-RadioQA dataset. Conventional QA: For conventional QA, the time required is primarily dependent on the LLM’s response time. For instance, when using GPT-4 through the OpenAI API, it took approximately 30 seconds per question to generate a response. RadioRAG-powered QA: RadioRAG introduces additional steps for retrieving and processing relevant context before querying the LLM, which increases the total time. This process consists of the following steps: • Key-phrase extraction: Approximately 10 seconds, dependent on internet speed. • Searching Radiopaedia using key-phrases: Approximately 30 seconds, dependent on both internet speed and the response time of the Radiopaedia website. • Database creation and embedding: With the collection of around 25 articles, this step took approximately 40 seconds, influenced by the hardware resources available. • LLM response generation: This step took approximately 35 seconds, similar to conven- tional QA. In total, RadioRAG-powered QA took approximately 115 seconds per question using GPT-4, about four times longer than conventional QA (30 seconds).
