This section, in brief written by a model
The unit presents system prompts for short answers in English or a specified target language, including named-entity transliteration requirements. It illustrates a character 3-gram recall metric that gives partial credit for transliteration differences, with the example receiving 9/13 recall. The discussion of multilingual generation examines whether English-centric and multilingual instruction-tuned models support multiple languages. The experiments include LLaMA-2-7B-chat, SOLAR-10.7B, Mixtral-8x7B, and Command-R-35B.

Most of current state- of-the-art LLMs are either English-centric or sup- port a limited set of languages, possibly due to under-investigated effects of the "curse of multilin- guality" for large models (Conneau et al., 2020), i.e. it is yet unclear how many languages LLMs can fit without hurting performance, or due to limited availability of multilingual instruction tun- ing and alignment datasets. At the same time, it was shown that even English-centric LLMs, which were pretrained and finetuned mostly on English data, may exhibit good multilingual capabilities due to the occasional presence of multilingual data in pretraining (Ye et al., 2023; Chirkova and Nikoulina, 2024). As such, we experiment with both strong English-centric and recent multilingual models. Among English-centric models we pick commonly-used LLaMA-2-7B-chat (Touvron et al., 2023) and state-of-the-art SOLAR-10.7B (Kim et al., 2023), and among multilingual models we pick Mixtral-8x7B (Jiang et al., 2024) and Command-R-35B5.
