This section, in brief written by a model
The ablation studies investigate how model choice, the number of attributes, and the number of retrieved documents per query affect synthetic data quality and privacy. On the HealthCareMagic dataset, GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 perform well for the information extractor and data generator, while Llama3-Chat-8b produces poor utility and can perform worse than zero-shot prediction. Increasing the number of attributes generally improves performance until gains stop, while smaller attribute sets reduce privacy exposure. Increasing retrieved documents can amplify privacy leakage for stage-1 data, but agent-based refinement maintains minimal privacy leakage as retrieval increases.

Ablation Studies To investigate the factors that affect the quality of synthetic data, we conduct ablation studies regard- ing the impact of model choice, the number of attributes, and retrieved documents per query. Impact of model choice. To investigate the in- fluence of model choice on stage-1 generation, we change the models used for the information ex- tractor and data generator components in stage 1. Specifically, we experiment with different models, including GPT-4, GPT-3.5, and Llama3-Chat-8b, for these two components. For the experiments on the information extractor, we fix the data gen- erator as GPT-3.5 and vary the model used for the information extractor. Similarly, for the ex- periments on the data generator, we fix the model of information extractor as GPT-3.5 and vary the model of data generator. We conduct the utility experiments on the HealthCareMagic dataset and use BLEU-1 and ROUGE-L scores compared with groundtruth as performance indicators. The impact on performance is shown in Figure 3a and Figure 3b. We can clearly observe that if weak models like Llama-8b-chat are used as the data generator or the information extractor, the overall performance is poor, even worse than zero-shot prediction. This indicates that the generated data is of poor quality. Impact of the number of attributes. In this part, we investigate the influence of the number of at- tributes m. We change the number of attributes m and observe its impact on performance and privacy on the HealthCareMagic dataset. The performance results are shown in Figure 4a. From the figure, we can observe that when the number of attributes is very small (e.g., when the number of attributes is 2), the performance is likely to be poor.
