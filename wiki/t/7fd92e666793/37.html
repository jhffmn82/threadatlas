This section, in brief. These notes explain passages and translation issues in Euripides’ Alcestis, especially Alcestis’ approaching death, funeral preparations, and return from Death. They discuss Death as the masculine figure corresponding to Θάνατος, who cuts Alcestis’ hair and consecrates her to the dead. They also describe Greek customs concerning corpses, mourning, burial, purification, tombs, and funeral rites. Several notes compare the play with other Greek and Latin authors, Shakespeare, and ancient inscriptions.
