The unit consists of notes on the Heraclidae, especially their exile, military arrangements, religious observances, and related tragic passages. It discusses Macaria's voluntary sacrifice after an oracle required that a descendant of Hercules be devoted. The notes also compare passages from Aeschylus, Sophocles, Euripides, and other authors, while addressing disputed readings, emendations, and grammatical constructions.

Th. 40 sq., also Soph. Œd. T. 6 sqq. [13] i.e. μαντεις κατ' αστυ θυηφολουσι. ELMSLEY. [14] Pausanias, i. 32, states that the oracle expressly required that one of the descendants of Hercules should be devoted, and that upon this Macaria, his daughter by Deianira, voluntarily offered herself. Her name was afterward given to a fountain. Enripides probably omitted this fact, in order to place the noble-mindedness of Macaria in a stronger light. The curious reader may compare the similar sacrifices of Codrus, (Pausan. vii. 25. Vell. Patere. i. 4,) Menœceus, (Eur. Phœn. 1009, Statius Theb. x. 751 sqq.,) Chaon (Serv. on Virg. Æn. iii. 335). See also Lomeier de Lustrationibus, § xxii., where the whole subject is learnedly treated. [15] Cf. Æsch. Ag. 206 sqq. [16] I prefer understanding ‛ενεκα εξοδων εμων with Elmsley, to Matthiæ's forced interpretation. Compare Med. 214 sqq. [17] The cognate accusative to δρασειεν must be supplied from the context. [18] There is some awkwardness in the construction. Perhaps if we read σπερμα, της θειας φρενος! πεφ. the sense will be improved.
