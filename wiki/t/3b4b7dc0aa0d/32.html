This section, in brief. After Philip, the son of Amyntas, died, Alexander secured his accession by punishing his father's murderers, winning support in Thessaly and Greece, and compelling resistance by force. Athens sought pardon, Alexander was recognized as general of Greece, and the Greeks authorized aid against Persia. Attalus conspired against Alexander and was killed by Hecateus on the king's orders, while Bagoas murdered Persian kings before Darius Codomannus forced him to drink poison. Darius prepared Persia for war, while Memnon of Rhodes and other commanders conducted operations in Asia against Macedonian forces.
