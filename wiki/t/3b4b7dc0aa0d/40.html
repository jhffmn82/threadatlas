This section, in brief. After the battle of Issus, Alexander marched toward Egypt and besieged Tyre, which refused him entry and resisted for seven months. The Macedonians built a mole to Tyre and eventually captured the city after overcoming extensive Tyrian defenses and naval resistance. The Tyrians suffered heavy losses, enslavement, and the execution of surviving young men, while Ballominus, a poor man of royal lineage, was made king of Tyre. Amyntas attempted to raise opposition to Alexander in Egypt but was defeated and killed, and the Greeks later sent ambassadors to congratulate Alexander on his victories, including the capture of Gaza.
