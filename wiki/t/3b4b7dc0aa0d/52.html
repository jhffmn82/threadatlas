This section, in brief. Alexander reorganizes his forces by appointing Persians as body squires and incorporating Persian darters and slingers among his soldiers. He marches from Susa through Media, visits Bagistame, reaches Ecbatana, and mourns the death of Hephaestion. While Alexander is in Asia, unrest in Greece produces the Lamian War, as discharged mercenary soldiers and Persian commanders gather at Tenarus under Leosthenes. Alexander then conquers the Cussaei, marches toward Babylon, temporarily avoids the city because of a warning from the Chaldeans, and finally enters it after philosophers persuade him to disregard their astrology.
