This section, in brief. Agathocles moved his army to Tunis and razed Tunis and another city rather than leave them as places of refuge. The Carthaginians, alarmed by his arrival in Africa, appointed Hanno and Bomilcar as generals and brought an army of forty thousand foot, one thousand horse, and two thousand chariots against him. Agathocles defeated the Carthaginian army after Hanno was killed and Bomilcar ordered a retreat, then pursued it briefly and took the spoils. He subsequently blockaded Carthage, while the Carthaginians attributed their misfortune to divine anger and offered wealth and human sacrifices to their gods.
