This section, in brief. Philip captured several cities in the Hellespont, including Olynthus, after bribing Olynthus's chief magistrates, and sold its citizens into slavery. The Athenians, urged chiefly by Demosthenes, opposed Philip and encouraged Greek cities to defend their liberties, but Philip claimed that bribery expanded his power more effectively than warfare. The Phocians continued the Sacred War while their leaders and soldiers used the sacred treasures of Delphi, provoking accusations of sacrilege and eventual divine punishment. The war ended when Phalaecus and his mercenaries withdrew and the Phocians surrendered to Philip, after which the Amphictyonic Council imposed severe penalties on Phocis and admitted Philip and his posterity to the council with two votes.
