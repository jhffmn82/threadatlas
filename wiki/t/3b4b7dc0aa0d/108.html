This section, in brief. The unit recounts examples of virtue, impiety, justice, and good or bad government among figures including Lycurgus, Solon, Cyrus, and others. Lycurgus was highly esteemed, and while the Lacedaemonians observed his laws they became powerful and held sovereignty over Greece for more than four hundred years, but they lost that authority after neglecting the laws and embracing wealth and luxury. Solon was celebrated for wisdom, legislation, and virtue, and he helped turn the Athenians from luxury toward honorable conduct. Cyrus was distinguished for courage, prudence, and gentleness toward his subjects, while several other rulers and individuals suffered or were condemned for cruelty, treachery, or contempt of the gods.
