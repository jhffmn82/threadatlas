This section, in brief. Agathocles invades Africa from Syracuse, burns his ships, defeats Carthaginian forces, and conducts further wars in Africa and Sicily, marked by repeated cruelty and eventual mutiny. Cassander and Polyperchon maneuver over the succession after Alexander, while Ptolemy, Antigonus, Demetrius, Lysimachus, and Seleucus engage in wars and shifting alliances across Egypt, Cyprus, Greece, and Asia. Demetrius besieges Rhodes, but peace is later made with the Rhodians. Affairs also continue in Italy, the Cimmerian Bosporus, and other regions, while Agathocles and his forces suffer reverses before making peace with the Carthaginians.
