This section, in brief. The account turns to events in Italy, where the Etruscans attacked Sutrium and the Romans divided their forces against the Etruscans and Samnites. Fabius invaded higher Etruria, defeated the Etruscans near Perusia, made peace with several communities, and forced the lifting of the siege of Sutrium, while Marcius took Alifa and relieved the Iapyges. At Rome, the censors Appius Claudius and Caius Plautius undertook major public works and altered the composition of the senate and the organization of citizens, provoking opposition from the nobility. Ptolemy took Andros, Sicyon, and Corinth before returning to Egypt, while Cleopatra was stopped and secretly murdered on Antigonus's orders.
