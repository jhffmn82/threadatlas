This section, in brief. The unit describes Roman diplomatic and military dealings with the Albani, Grecians, Antiochus, the Aetolians, Perseus, and various rulers and cities of Asia. Rome negotiated peace with Antiochus on severe terms, including territorial concessions, tribute, surrender of elephants and ships, and the delivery of Hannibal and others. After victories over Antiochus, Roman commissioners redistributed territories in Asia and continued directing the affairs of Greece and neighboring kingdoms. The Roman Senate later declared war against Perseus, received embassies from many states, investigated Eumenes, and sharply rebuked the submissive Prusias.
