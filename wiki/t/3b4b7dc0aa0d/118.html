This section, in brief written by a model
Lucius iEmilius, who conquered Perseus and was consul and censor, died and received an exceptionally well-attended funeral at Rome. Although he had controlled immense wealth and authority, he left so little private property that his adopted sons had to sell land to pay his wife's dowry. His natural son Publius Scipio, adopted into the family of Scipio Africanus, studied Greek learning and philosophy and became renowned for sobriety, virtue, generosity, chastity, and courage. Publius Scipio distributed inheritances and money generously among his mother, sisters, and brother, and later surpassed the Macedonian kings in hunting.
