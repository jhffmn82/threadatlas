This section, in brief. Antigonus marched into Egypt with a large land army and fleet, but storms, Egyptian defenses, desert terrain, and shortages forced him to return to Syria. Ptolemy defended the Nile and Egypt, attracting some of Antigonus’s men with promises of payment. In Sicily, Agathocles offered to surrender his sovereignty and restore Syracuse to its citizens, but Dinocrates rejected the proposal; Agathocles then made peace with the Carthaginians. Antigonus’s conflict with the Rhodians escalated when Demetrius besieged Rhodes with a vast fleet, army, and siege equipment.
