This section, in brief. Alexander arrived at Nysa, whose inhabitants claimed that Bacchus had founded the city after conquering India. Acuphis, Nysa's chief man, asked Alexander to leave the city free in honor of Bacchus and described its foundation, laws, and surrounding landmarks. Alexander granted Nysa self-government, required three hundred horsemen, and accepted Acuphis's son and nephew instead of taking the city's leading men. He later visited Nysa and Mount Meros, sacrificed to Bacchus, and feasted with his friends.
