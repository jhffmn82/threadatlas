This section, in brief. Demetrius continued the siege of Rhodes, constructing the immense Helepolis and numerous supporting engines to attack the city by land and sea. The Rhodians strengthened their defenses, conducted naval raids, resisted mining and assaults, and received provisions and troops from Ptolemy, Cassander, and Lysimachus. After fierce fighting, including a failed night attack by Demetrius’s troops, negotiations led to peace between Demetrius and the Rhodians. Rhodes remained under its own laws without a garrison, retained its estates and revenues, agreed to join Antigonus except in wars against Ptolemy, and delivered one hundred hostages.
