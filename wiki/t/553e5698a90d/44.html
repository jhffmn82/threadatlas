This section, in brief. The speaker complains that a faithless girl has broken her oath without losing her beauty, while the gods punish men but seem to tolerate beautiful women who swear falsely. He argues that guarding a wife cannot make her faithful, since desire is strengthened by prohibition, and advises the hard husband to indulge his wife instead. He then recounts a dream in which a white heifer is disturbed by a crow, leaves her bull, and joins other bulls, and the nocturnal dream augur interprets this as a sign of adultery. The unit presents beauty, female chastity, divine punishment, and forbidden desire as forces that shape human and divine behavior.
