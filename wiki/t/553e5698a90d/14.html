This section, in brief. The speaker pleads with her husband to restore the marriage bed and reminds him that their two children resemble him and may suffer under a cruel stepmother. She appeals to the gods, her services to him, and their children, saying that she once helped him and became the mother of his children with him. She claims that the golden ram, the Grecian youth, and her husband's survival and prosperity constitute her dowry. Her anger is preparing great threats, though she does not yet state what punishment she will inflict.
