This section, in brief. The unit first mourns the death of Corinna’s parrot, praising its Indian origin, mimicry, loyalty to a turtle-dove, and final cry of “Corinna, vale!” It then presents the speaker’s defenses against Corinna’s jealousy, especially her accusation that he has slept with Cypassis. The speaker later admits that he did have an affair with Cypassis and threatens to reveal its details if she refuses him. The final poems portray Cupid and love as forces that repeatedly restore the speaker’s desire, including his simultaneous love for two women.
