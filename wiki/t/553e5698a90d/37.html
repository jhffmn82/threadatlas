This section, in brief written by a model
Ovid presents himself as a poet of love whose elegies are composed at Amor’s command and intended for lovers. He recalls abandoning grand epic subjects when his beloved mistress closed her door, after which song softened the barrier and brought her to him. He urges the mistress’s guardian to permit a discreet affair, arguing that secrecy will benefit the guardian and that jealous surveillance harms everyone. He also confesses that he is attracted to women of every kind and cannot control his continual inclination to love.
