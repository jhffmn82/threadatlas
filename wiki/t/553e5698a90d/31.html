This section, in brief. P. Ovidius Naso presents the Amores as a reduced collection of three books and begins by describing how Cupid redirected his intended epic poetry into elegy. The speaker acknowledges that Cupid has wounded him and submits to Love, while asking a beloved woman to accept his devotion and inspire his poetry. At a banquet, the speaker anxiously instructs the beloved woman how to communicate secretly and avoid intimacy with her husband. Later, Corinna visits the speaker, removes her clothing after a feigned resistance, and they have sex.
