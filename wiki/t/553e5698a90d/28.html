This section, in brief. Acontius argues that Cydippe’s oath to him, sworn before Diana, binds her more closely than her father’s promise to another suitor. He warns that the rival suitor’s pursuit has caused Cydippe’s illness and placed her under Diana’s suspicion, while keeping the oath would restore both their safety. Acontius urges Cydippe to tell her mother how they met, how the apple conveyed his written oath, and why she should marry him. He promises that, if she does so, a golden image of the apple will be dedicated at Delos.
