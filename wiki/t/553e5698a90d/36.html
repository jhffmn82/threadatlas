This section, in brief written by a model
The speaker laments tablets that return from the beloved woman bearing a refusal, then curses the tablets and the tree from which they were made. He reproaches Aurora for ending his night with the beloved, while praising the pleasures of sleep and intimacy. He mourns the beloved woman’s damaged hair, attributes its loss to her own treatment, and consoles her that it can be replaced. Finally, he defends poetry against Envy, claiming that poetic fame outlasts rulers, monuments, and death.
