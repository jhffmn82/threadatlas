This section, in brief. Ovid says that his books of verse have made Corinna known to many and have turned her into a woman shared by others. He asks Corinna to conceal her infidelity and deny what he sees, because discovering or hearing her confess her affairs causes him intense distress. The poems also describe Falerii’s annual rites to Juno and trace them to Halaesus, while Ovid celebrates Sulmo and his Paelignian origin. In another episode, Ovid suffers sexual impotence with a willing girl, despite having recently performed successfully with Chlide, Pitho, Libas, and Corinna.
