This section, in brief. Dipsas, an old bawd skilled in magic, counsels a young woman on how to attract and exploit wealthy lovers. She tells the young woman to use feigned modesty, rivalry, tears, deception, and repeated demands for gifts and payment. The narrator and poet-lover overhears the speech while hidden behind doors and nearly attacks Dipsas in anger. Dipsas presents the wealthy young lover as a desirable source of money, while dismissing the narrator's poetry as inadequate compensation.
