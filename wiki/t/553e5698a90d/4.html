This section, in brief. Phaedra writes to Hippolytus, confessing that Love has overcome her modesty and caused her to desire him. She presents her late and intense passion as a fate inherited from her family, citing Europa, Pasiphae, and Ariadne. Phaedra praises Hippolytus's appearance and hunting skills, urges him to abandon his exclusive devotion to Diana, and asks him to return her love. She also attacks Theseus for neglecting their marriage and wronging both her and Hippolytus, then begs Hippolytus to accept her despite their relationship as stepmother and stepson.
