This section, in brief written by a model
The speaker longs to embrace the addressee and questions whether the sea must govern their meetings. He promises to cross the swollen sea even in stormy weather, trusting either to survive through daring or to end his anxious love in death. He hopes to be cast onto the addressee’s shores, where the addressee will mourn him and acknowledge responsibility for his death. He asks for a brief calm during the crossing, but wishes winter to continue once he reaches the addressee’s shore, while his letter remains with the addressee until he arrives.

183 Am I, then, never to embrace you except when the wave so wills, and shall no tempest see me happy ? and, though nothing is less certain than the wind and wave, must winds and water ever be my hope ? And yet it still is summer. What when the seas have been assailed by the Pleiad, and the guardian of the Bear, and the Goat of Olenos ? Either I know not how rash I am, or even then a love not cautious will send me forth on the deep. And, lest you deem I promise this because the time is not yet come, I will give you no tardy pledge of what I promise. Let the sea be swollen still for these few nights, and I shall essay to cross despite the waves ; either happy daring shall leave me safe, or death shall be the end of my anxious love ! Yet I shall pray to be cast up on yonder shores, and that my shipwrecked limbs may come into your haven : for you will weep over me, and not disdain to touch my body, and you will say : " Of the death he met, I was the cause !
