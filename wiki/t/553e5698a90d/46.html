This section, in brief. The unit mourns Tibullus, whose death and funeral provoke grief from Elegy, Venus, Delia, Nemesis, his mother, and his sister, while poetry is presented as surviving the body and death. It argues that death can overcome the good and profane sacred things, though Tibullus may live on in poetic fame and in Elysium with other poets. The poem then describes Ceres’s festival and the goddess’s past love for Iasius, which once caused agriculture to fail everywhere except in Crete. Finally, the speaker declares his intention to escape a painful affair, but admits that love still conflicts with his hatred of his beloved woman and may continue to prevail.
