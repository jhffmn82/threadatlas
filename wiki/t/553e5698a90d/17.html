This section, in brief. Sappho writes to Phaon about his departure and her continuing love for him. She recalls their intimacy, praises his beauty, and says that neither the women of Lesbos nor her poetry now gives her pleasure. A Naiad tells Sappho to seek the Leucadian cliff, where Deucalion was freed from love by leaping into the sea. Sappho resolves to make the leap and dedicate her lyre to Phoebus if it cures her passion.
