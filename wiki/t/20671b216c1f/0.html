Chen, Zhang, Zheng, Yu, and Wang proposed a framework for enhancing treatment decision-making for chronic low back pain (CLBP) by integrating large language models with retrieval-augmented generation technology and least-to-most prompting. They generated a CLBP-related dataset to evaluate CLBP-GPT against GPT-4.0, ERNIE Bot, and DeepSeek on accuracy, relevance, clarity, benefit, and completeness. CLBP-GPT achieved the highest scores across all five dimensions, with a total score of 4.40. The authors state that the framework could provide technical support for personalized and efficient clinical treatment strategies for CLBP.

Enhancing treatment decision-making for low back pain: a novel framework integrating large language models with retrieval-augmented generation technology Rong Chen†, Siyun Zhang†, Yiyi Zheng, Qiuhua Yu* and Chuhuai Wang* Department of Rehabilitation Medicine, The First Afﬁliated Hospital, Sun Yat-sen University, Guangzhou, China Introduction: Chronic low back pain (CLBP) is a global health problem that seriously affects the quality of life among patients. The etiology of CLBP is complex, with non-speciﬁc symptoms and considerable heterogeneity, which poses a great challenge for diagnosis. In addition, the uncertain treatment responses as well as the potential inﬂuence of psychological and social factors further increase the difﬁculty of personalized decision-making in clinical practice. Methods: This study proposed an innovative support framework on clinical decision, which combined large language models (LLMs) with retrieval- augmented generation (RAG) technology. Moreover, the least-to-most (LtM) prompting technology was introduced, aiming to simulate the decision-making process of senior experts thereby improving personalized treatment for CLBP. Additionally, a special CLBP-related dataset was generated to verify effectiveness of the framework, which compared the proposed model CLBP-GPT with GPT-4.0, ERNIE Bot, and DeepSeek in terms of ﬁve key indicators: accuracy, relevance, clarity, beneﬁt, and completeness. Results: The results showed that the CLBP-GPT model proposed in this study scored signiﬁcantly better than other comparison models in all ﬁve evaluation dimensions. Speciﬁcally, the total score of CLBP-GPT was 4.40 (SD = 0.20), substantially higher than GPT-4.0 (4.03, SD = 0.48), ERNIE Bot (3.54, SD = 0.53), and DeepSeek (3.81, SD = 0.47). In terms of accuracy, the average score of CLBP-GPT was 4.38 (SD = 0.19), while the scores of other models were all below 4, indicating that CLBP-GPT could provide more accurate clinical decision- making recommendations. In addition, CLBP-GPT scored as high as 4.42 (SD = 0.19) in the completeness dimension, further demonstrating that the decision content output by the model was more comprehensive and covered more key information related to CLBP. Discussion: This study not only provides new technical support for clinical decision-making in CLBP, but also introduces a powerful tool for doctors to Frontiers in Medicine 01 frontiersin.org Chen et al. 10.3389/fmed.2025.1599241 formulate personalized and efﬁcient treatment strategies. It is expected to improve the diagnosis and treatment of CLBP in the future. KEYWORDS chronic low back pain, clinical decision-making, GPT-4.0, large language models, treatment
