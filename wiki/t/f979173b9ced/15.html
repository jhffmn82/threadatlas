This section, in brief written by a model
The unit concerns simulators for annotation developed to evaluate decisions in games and on DQA. Applying every decision to real games is described as impossible because games involve randomness and are not open-sourced. Each simulator takes D with a decision as input, calculates the decision's effects according to business rules, and returns an updated D. By simulating all decision candidates, the system annotates the best decision; in the example, Doab produces a profit increment of 0.23 and Ganges 0.03, making Doab the best decision.

Simulators for annotation Although applying every decision to real games and comparing the results is the most credible approach to annotate the best decision, it is impossible due to the following characteristics of games: (1) ran- domness and (2) not being open-sourced. Thus, we 6549 develop simulators for each scenario on DQA to calculate decision results deterministically. Our simulator takes D with decision as input, calculate effects of decision, and returns updated D by simulating based on business rules. To an- notate answers for a question, we simulates all decision candidates. For example, in Figure 1, we locates a merchant on Doab and Ganges, our sim- ulator calculates profit increments, 0.23 and 0.03 for each decisions, respectively. From these calcu- lated results, we can annotate that Doab is the best decision for the question. Detailed algorithms are in Appendix A.4 A.4
