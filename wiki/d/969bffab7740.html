The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04 · 28 sections

Abstract written by a model

This document is The Wonderful Wizard of Oz, introduced as L. Frank Baum’s 1900 wonder tale for children, designed to preserve fairy-tale joy while avoiding frightening or painful elements. Dorothy, a Kansas girl living with Aunt Em, Uncle Henry, and Toto, is carried by a cyclone to the Land of Oz. Her house kills the Wicked Witch of the East, freeing the Munchkins, and the Witch of the North gives Dorothy the dead Witch’s Silver Shoes and directs her toward the Emerald City, where Oz may help her return home. On the yellow brick road, Dorothy gathers companions with wishes of their own: the Scarecrow seeks brains, the Tin Woodman a heart, and the Cowardly Lion courage. They survive forests, chasms, a river, deadly poppies, and other dangers before reaching the Emerald City. Oz, appearing differently to each of them, promises to grant their desires only if they kill the Wicked Witch of the West. Traveling to the Winkies’ country, they withstand the Witch’s attacks, though the Winged Monkeys capture Dorothy and her friends. When the Witch steals one Silver Shoe, Dorothy throws water on her and melts her. Dorothy and the Lion free the Winkies, restore the damaged Scarecrow and Tin Woodman, and return to Oz with help from the Winged Monkeys, summoned through the Golden Cap. Back in the Emerald City, Oz is revealed as a frightened old man using tricks to seem powerful. He gives the Scarecrow, Tin Woodman, and Lion symbolic versions of what they seek, but fails to take Dorothy home when his balloon departs without her. The Scarecrow becomes ruler of the Emerald City. Dorothy and her companions then journey south toward Glinda, crossing the Dainty China Country, aiding a forest threatened by a giant spider, and passing the Hammer-Heads with the Winged Monkeys’ final help. Glinda tells Dorothy that the Silver Shoes could always have carried her home. In exchange for the Golden Cap, she sends Dorothy’s companions to their chosen homes: the Scarecrow to the Emerald City, the Tin Woodman to the Winkies, and the Lion to rule the forest animals. Dorothy uses the shoes to return to Kansas with Toto, losing the shoes in the desert, and is joyfully reunited with Aunt Em.

Introduction body

The introduction discusses the enduring appeal of folklore, legends, myths, and fairy tales to children. It says that old fairy tales may now be considered historical because newer wonder tales are replacing stereotyped supernatural figures and frightening incidents. L. Frank Baum wrote The Wonderful Wizard of Oz to retain wonder and joy while omitting heartaches and nightmares. The book was written in Chicago in April 1900.

Chapter I body

Dorothy lives with Uncle Henry and Aunt Em in a small, gray house on the Kansas prairies, where her black dog Toto keeps her cheerful. When a cyclone approaches, Uncle Henry goes to protect the animals, while Aunt Em orders Dorothy to take shelter in the cyclone cellar. Dorothy remains inside the house with Toto when the house is lifted into the air and carried away by the storm. After several frightening moments, Dorothy calms down and falls asleep as the house continues to sway.

Chapter II body

Dorothy awakens after the cyclone sets her house down in the Land of Oz and meets the Witch of the North and three Munchkins. They explain that the house killed the Wicked Witch of the East, freeing the Munchkins from bondage, and Dorothy receives the witch’s silver shoes. Unable to return directly to Kansas because deserts surround the Land of Oz, Dorothy is directed to walk to the City of Emeralds and ask Oz for help. The Witch of the North gives Dorothy a protective kiss and disappears.

Chapter III body

Dorothy prepares for her journey to the Emerald City, puts on the silver shoes, and follows the yellow brick road after spending a night with the Munchkins. Along the way, she meets the Scarecrow, removes him from his pole, and learns that he wants brains because he believes he has none. Dorothy invites the Scarecrow to travel with her and ask the Great Oz for help. Toto accompanies them but is initially suspicious of the Scarecrow, who reveals that he fears a lighted match.

Chapter IV body

Dorothy and the Scarecrow travel along the increasingly rough yellow brick road toward the Emerald City. During a rest, Dorothy tells the Scarecrow about Kansas and the cyclone that brought her to the Land of Oz, while he explains how he was made and why he wants brains from the Great Oz. They enter a dark forest and find a log-and-branch cottage where Dorothy sleeps while the Scarecrow keeps watch.

Chapter V body

Dorothy and the Scarecrow find the Tin Woodman rusted beside a partly chopped tree in the forest and free him by oiling his joints. The Tin Woodman joins them on their journey to the Emerald City, hoping the Great Oz will give him a heart, while the Scarecrow seeks brains and Dorothy wants to return to Kansas. He tells them how the Wicked Witch of the East caused his body parts to be replaced with tin, leaving him without a heart. The companions continue along the road of yellow brick, but Dorothy worries that their bread is nearly gone.

Chapter VI body

Dorothy, Toto, the Scarecrow, and the Tin Woodman encounter a Lion in the forest, and Dorothy calls him a coward after he attacks her companions and threatens Toto. The Cowardly Lion admits that he lacks courage and joins the group so Oz can give him courage. During the journey, the Tin Woodman accidentally kills a beetle, cries, and rusts his jaws until the Scarecrow oils them. The companions continue toward the Emerald City, where they hope Oz will grant their wishes.

Chapter VII body

Dorothy, the Tin Woodman, the Cowardly Lion, and the Scarecrow continue their journey to the Emerald City through the forest. They cross the first ditch by having the Lion carry them, then use a fallen tree as a bridge over the second gulf and defeat two attacking Kalidahs by chopping the bridge away. After leaving the forest, they reach a broad river and begin building a raft to cross it, but the raft is unfinished when night falls.

Chapter VIII body

Dorothy, the Scarecrow, the Tin Woodman, the Cowardly Lion, and Toto cross the river on a raft, but the current carries them away from the road of yellow brick, leaving the Scarecrow behind on a pole. A Stork rescues the Scarecrow, and the travelers continue along the riverbank until they enter a meadow of scarlet poppies. The deadly odor puts Dorothy, Toto, and the Cowardly Lion to sleep, while the Scarecrow and the Tin Woodman carry Dorothy and Toto out of the poppy field. They place Dorothy safely on grass, but the Lion remains asleep among the poppies because he is too heavy to carry.

Chapter IX body

The Tin Woodman kills a Wildcat that is chasing the Queen of all the Field Mice and saves her life. In gratitude, the Queen orders thousands of field mice to help rescue the Cowardly Lion from the poppy bed. Using strings and a truck made by the Tin Woodman, the mice pull the sleeping Lion to safety. Dorothy, the Scarecrow, and the Tin Woodman wait beside the rescued Lion while he sleeps.

Chapter X body

Dorothy, the Cowardly Lion, the Scarecrow, the Tin Woodman, and Toto resume their journey along the road of yellow brick toward the Emerald City. They spend a night with a family in the Land of Oz and learn that the Great Oz is difficult to see and may take many forms. At the Emerald City gate, the Guardian of the Gates warns them about Oz and fits each traveler with locked green spectacles before leading them into the city toward the Palace.

Chapter XI body

Dorothy and her companions enter the green City of Oz and are lodged in the Palace of Oz while waiting to see the Great Wizard. Oz appears to each visitor in a different form and promises to grant Dorothy's request to return home, the Scarecrow's desire for brains, the Tin Woodman's desire for a heart, and the Lion's desire for courage only after the Wicked Witch of the West is killed. The companions agree to travel to the land of the Winkies and destroy the Witch, then prepare to leave the next morning.

Chapter XII body

Dorothy and her companions leave the Emerald City and travel west into the country of the Winkies to find the Wicked Witch of the West. The Witch sends wolves, crows, bees, Winkies, and finally the Winged Monkeys against them, but the Scarecrow, Tin Woodman, Cowardly Lion, and the Good Witch’s protection help them survive. The Winged Monkeys capture the Tin Woodman, Scarecrow, and Lion and carry Dorothy to the Witch’s castle, where Dorothy is forced to work and secretly feeds the Lion. After the Witch steals one of Dorothy’s Silver Shoes, Dorothy throws water on her, causing the Wicked Witch of the West to melt away.

Chapter XIII body

Dorothy and the Cowardly Lion free the Winkies after the Wicked Witch is melted, then lead a rescue of the Tin Woodman and the Scarecrow. The Winkies repair the Tin Woodman and restore the Scarecrow with clean straw. After several happy days at the Yellow Castle, Dorothy and her friends decide to return to Oz to claim his promise. The Winkies give the travelers gifts, and the group begins the journey to the Emerald City.

Chapter XIV body

Dorothy, the Scarecrow, the Tin Woodman, the Cowardly Lion, and Toto lose their way while traveling through the great fields toward the Emerald City. The Queen of the Mice tells Dorothy to use the Golden Cap to summon the Winged Monkeys, who carry the travelers to the city. The Monkey King explains that the Winged Monkeys are bound to obey the owner of the Golden Cap three times because of a punishment imposed by Gayelette. The Winged Monkeys leave the travelers safely at the Emerald City.

Chapter XV body

Dorothy, the Scarecrow, the Tin Woodman, and the Cowardly Lion return to the Emerald City after Dorothy melts the Wicked Witch of the West and demand that Oz fulfill his promises. They discover that Oz is a frightened old man who has used mechanical tricks, disguises, fire, and ventriloquism to appear as different magical beings. Oz admits he is a humbug but agrees to provide the Scarecrow with brains, the Tin Woodman with a heart, and the Lion with courage, while promising to seek a way to send Dorothy back to Kansas. The travelers agree to keep Oz's secret and remain his guests.

Chapter XVI body

The Scarecrow, Tin Woodman, and Cowardly Lion visit Oz to receive the brains, heart, and courage they believe they need. Oz gives the Scarecrow a mixture of bran, pins, needles, and straw, places a silk-and-sawdust heart in the Tin Woodman, and makes the Lion drink a liquid he calls courage. Each is pleased and believes the desired quality has been supplied. Alone, Oz admits that he is a humbug and worries that he cannot send Dorothy back to Kansas.

Chapter XVII body

Dorothy, wanting to return to Kansas, helps Oz build a balloon to cross the desert through the air. Oz fills the silk balloon with hot air and attaches a clothes basket, then announces that the Scarecrow will rule the Emerald City while he visits a brother Wizard in the clouds. Dorothy misses the launch while searching for Toto, and Oz rises into the sky without her. The people of the Emerald City mourn Oz but accept the Scarecrow as their ruler.

Chapter XVIII body

Dorothy and her companions mourn Oz's departure, while the Scarecrow becomes ruler of the Emerald City. Dorothy still wants to return to Kansas, but the Winged Monkeys cannot carry her across the desert. The soldier with the green whiskers advises her to seek Glinda, the Witch of the South, whose castle stands near the desert. Dorothy, the Scarecrow, the Tin Woodman, and the Lion decide to travel to the Land of the South and begin preparing for the journey.

Chapter XIX body

Dorothy, the Scarecrow, the Tin Woodman, and the Lion leave the Emerald City and continue their journey toward the Land of the South so Dorothy can return to Kansas. They pass through green fields and a thick forest whose first-row trees attack them, but the Tin Woodman cuts branches so the group can proceed. At the far edge of the forest, they encounter a high, smooth wall made of white china, and the Tin Woodman decides to make a ladder to climb over it.

Chapter XX body

Dorothy, the Tin Woodman, the Scarecrow, the Lion, and Toto enter the Dainty China Country after climbing a wall. They find a tiny country of china houses, animals, and people, and must cross it carefully because its inhabitants can break easily. Dorothy meets a china Princess, who explains that china people become stiff when taken away from their country. The travelers reach the far wall, but the Lion accidentally smashes a china church while jumping onto it.

Chapter XXI body

The travelers cross a disagreeable country and enter a vast, ancient forest. There they meet hundreds of forest animals threatened by a giant spider that has killed the other lions. The Lion defeats the spider by striking off its head while it sleeps. The animals make him their King, and he promises to rule them after Dorothy is safely on her way to Kansas.

Chapter XXII body

The four travelers reach a steep hill guarded by hundreds of armless Hammer-Heads, whose shooting heads knock down the Scarecrow and the Lion. Dorothy summons the Winged Monkeys, who carry the travelers and Toto over the hill for the last time. In the country of the Quadlings, the travelers receive food and directions to the Castle of Glinda, where they are admitted to see Glinda.

Chapter XXIII body

Dorothy and her companions visit Glinda the Good Witch, who agrees to help Dorothy return to Kansas in exchange for the Golden Cap. Glinda arranges for the Winged Monkeys to carry the Scarecrow, Tin Woodman, and Lion to the places where they wish to live. She explains that Dorothy’s Silver Shoes can carry her anywhere in three steps. Dorothy uses them to return to Kansas, but the shoes fall off and are lost in the desert.

Chapter XXIV body

Aunt Em comes out of the house to water the cabbages and sees Dorothy running toward her. Aunt Em embraces Dorothy and kisses her, asking where she came from. Dorothy says she has returned from the Land of Oz with Toto and is glad to be home again.
