SDG target detection in environmental reports using Retrieval-augmented Generation with LLMs

Darío Garigliotti · 2024-08-16 · 10 sections
"SDG target detection in environmental reports using Retrieval-augmented Generation with LLMs" by Darío Garigliotti (2024), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.climatenlp-1.19

This paper studies how Large Language Models (LLMs) and Retrieval-augmented Generation (RAG) can support climate-aware NLP for environmental assessment reports by linking report content to Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) targets. The central need is to help environmental assessment practitioners report how activities, impacts, and mitigation measures address SDGs, a task that is time-consuming and knowledge-intensive. It defines two dual tasks: SDG Target Evidence Identification, which starts with an SDG target and seeks passages in environmental impact assessment reports that provide relevant evidence, and SDG Target Detection, which starts with a report passage and seeks the corresponding SDG-target identifiers. In the RAG approach, retrieval selects relevant material, prompts add that material as context, and an LLM generates the requested passage or target identifiers. The authors build manually annotated test collections from public reports supplied by the Ministry of Climate of the Republic of Estonia. They create 16,474 passages and pair them with 30 selected SDG targets. Experiments compare lexical and learned dense retrieval; SDG-explicit and SDG-implicit prompts; context order; few-shot example counts; and several open and closed LLMs. Precision and recall measure performance. Results show no single configuration dominates every setting. GPT-3.5 generally performs best for evidence identification and for precision-focused target detection, while ClimateGPT-13b often achieves the strongest recall for target detection. Lexical retrieval is generally preferred for evidence identification and gives a recall advantage in target detection; dense retrieval gives a precision advantage for target detection. Passage order and the number of examples also affect results differently depending on the task, model, and prompt. The paper concludes that RAG-based LLM methods can help automate SDG-related environmental analysis, but notes limits from the selected targets, regionally focused reports, annotation uncertainty, and imperfect task performance. Future work includes studying interactions between the tasks, claim detectors, larger LLM-labeled collections, fine-tuning, and automatic correctness assessment. The work is part of NRF project 329745 Machine Teaching For XAI.

Abstract body

The paper examines how Large Language Models (LLMs) can support climate-aware NLP and environmental analysis. It focuses on identifying evidence of, and detecting, the Sustainable Developmental Goal (SDG) target addressed in passages from environmental assessment reports. The authors develop test collections and evaluate methods within a Retrieval-augmented Generation (RAG) framework. The study assesses current LLM capabilities for SDG target evidence identification and SDG target detection.

1 body

Experts in the United Nations established the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) as a framework for guiding human activities toward the common good. Environmental assessment practitioners increasingly need to report how activities, impacts, and mitigation measures in environmental assessment documents address SDGs, particularly SDG targets. The unit presents SDG Evidence Identification and SDG Target Detection as dual tasks for finding correspondence between SDG targets and evidence in environmental impact assessment reports. It reports the development of test collections and the evaluation of Retrieval-augmented Generation configurations and Large Language Models for these tasks.

2 body

Climate-aware natural language processing has emerged to improve information access and process climate-related data, including climate change phenomena. Related work includes methods for extracting climate-centric information, climate-focused language models such as ClimateBert and ClimateGPT, and systems such as ChatClimate. Language models and LLMs support tasks including text classification, textual entailment, and question answering, while Retrieval-augmented Generation provides additional explicit knowledge to improve domain-specific responses. The addressed tasks use an SDG target or textual excerpt as a question and require an LLM to generate answers containing the correct report-passage or SDG-target identifiers.

This unit defines two related tasks concerning SDG targets and passages from environmental impact assessment (EIA) reports: SDG Target Evidence Identification (EI) and SDG Target Detection (TD). It describes a Retrieval-augmented Generation (RAG) approach in which retrieval, prompt augmentation, and LLM-based generation are combined to identify relevant passages or targets. The unit also introduces experiments over test collections that examine retrieval, augmentation strategies, and the selected LLM. These experiments address three research questions about how each component affects RAG performance.

4 / 4.1 .. 4.3 body

The Experimental Setup describes datasets, retrieval and prompting parameters, language models, and evaluation metrics for evidence identification and target detection in environmental impact assessment. The Ministry of Climate of the Republic of Estonia provided public environmental reports, from which 16,474 passages were created and paired with 30 selected SDG targets. The experiments compare lexical matching and learned dense retrieval, SDG-explicit and SDG-implicit prompts, different context orders and example counts, and several open and closed LLMs. Performance is evaluated using precision and recall over manually annotated test collections.

5 .. 5.4 body

This unit reports experimental results for the SDG Evidence Identification and SDG Target Detection tasks across retrieval methods, prompt configurations, passage orders, numbers of examples, and language models. GPT-3.5 generally performs best for SDG Evidence Identification and precision-oriented SDG Target Detection, while ClimateGPT-13b often provides the strongest recall for SDG Target Detection. Lexical retrieval is usually favored for evidence identification, whereas target detection shows a precision advantage for dense retrieval and a recall advantage for lexical retrieval. The results also show that passage order and the number of few-shot examples have effects that vary by task, model, and prompt version.

6 body

This work studies two dual problems in environmental analysis to support the automation of time-consuming, knowledge-intensive tasks in assessing environmental impact in reports and relating it to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). It proposes and assesses several retrieval-augmented generation methods powered by large language models for evidence identification and target detection. The work is limited by its selected SDG targets, regionally focused report collection, annotation uncertainties, and imperfect capabilities for identifying evidence and detecting targets. Future work will explore interactions between the two tasks, claim detectors, larger LLM-labeled test sets, model fine-tuning, and automatic assessment of prediction correctness.
