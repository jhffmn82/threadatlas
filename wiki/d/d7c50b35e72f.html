From Sand Hill to Pine

Bret Harte · 1894-04-24 · 25 sections

This document is a collection of frontier and mountain-set narratives centered on travel, crime, romance, mining, social scandal, and sudden reversals. It opens with Yuba Bill’s stagecoach near the home of Snapshot Harry’s relatives, then recounts a double robbery: while masked men seize the express box, the greenbacks have already been stolen by someone connected to the passengers. Expressman Ned Brice seeks Henry Dimwood, called Snapshot Harry, learns that members of Harry’s gang committed the earlier theft, and, guided by Harry’s niece Flora, finds the dead thief and recovers the money. Brice is promoted, Flora is sent to convent school, and the two begin courting. In “A Treasure of the Redwoods,” prospector Jack Fleming meets Katinka Jallinger after borrowing her bread pan. Her playful salting of the pan brings him back, but their investigation uncovers real gold-bearing quartz; the Jallinger strike and their engagement follow. “A Belle of Canada City” follows Cissy Trixit after her father Montagu Trixit’s bank fails and he is accused of absconding with money. Abandoned by the town and aided by Ah Fe, Cissy disguises herself and seeks her father. Engineer Dick Masterton, sent to recover securities held by Trixit, is rescued by Cissy after falling into deep snow. Trixit offers to return the securities under conditions, and Cissy and Masterton travel toward Sacramento. “What Happened at the Fonda” begins as an apparent series of assaults involving Colonel Starbottle and Richards. Editor Mr. Grey investigates Ramierez’s fonda and encounters Cota Ramierez and her pinto mustang. The supposed strangler proves to be the horse, which Richards kills; Cota leaves, and Grey declines to turn the affair into newspaper sensation. “A Jack and Jill of the Sierras” joins miner Ned Bray and Eugenia Neworth after she falls onto him from the stage road. Their rescue becomes an affectionate shared memory. Gold is found near the spring, Eugenia’s note points Bray toward the discovery, and after misunderstandings are resolved in San Francisco, they become partners in both mining and affection. The final story, “Mr. Bilson’s Housekeeper,” concerns stern, capable Euphemia Trotter at the Summit House. Chris Calton initially appears engaged to chambermaid Frida Jansen, but Frida marries Joshua Bilson instead. Chris later tells Trotter he never loved Frida and loves her; Trotter leaves, later learning that Frida has become Mrs. Chris Calton, while her own fate with Chris remains unresolved.

Flora reunites with Brice, explains that she buried the actual thief and burned the empty belt, and they begin courting. Tarbox reveals that the newspaper paragraph helped him become a land and timber agent and that Snapshot Harry may eventually sell his valley and water-power rights.

Jack Fleming, a youthful prospector, becomes lost in the redwood forest while returning to camp and discovers a spring and quartz outcrop. He borrows a battered bread pan from Katinka Jallinger at her isolated cabin, leaving his gold specimen ring as a deposit. Fleming washes soil from the outcrop but finds only a few tiny flakes of gold, then returns the pan and retrieves his ring. He learns that Katinka's father, Henry Boone Jallinger, dislikes miners and lives by hunting, while Katinka is a seventeen- or eighteen-year-old young woman.

PART II Mr. body

Mr. Jack Fleming receives a letter and gold flakes from Katinka Jallinger, who claims that gold escaped from his broken prospecting pan and appeared in biscuits made at the Jallinger cabin. Fleming returns to the spring and outcrop, finds no significant gold, and meets Katinka, who confesses that she salted the pan to make him come back. They investigate the Run and discover gold-bearing quartz, leading to Fleming and Katinka's engagement. Their discovery is associated with the Jallinger gold strike, while Fleming's partners remain skeptical of the story.

The failure of Montagu Trixit's bank caused widespread excitement, distrust, and suspicion in Canada City, though the reported defalcations were exaggerated. Montagu Trixit was linked to the suspended Excelsior Bank and had fled eastward across the Sierras, but was believed to be hiding near the Summit. The directors of the New Mill sent Dick Masterton to locate Trixit and, if possible, obtain an interview with him. They hoped to recover securities that Trixit held privately for money advanced to the New Mill, because he might declare them as assets of his bank.

Mr. Masterton travels by mountain wagon toward the next station while pursuing a mission connected with Montagu Trixit, whose financial scandal has prompted a staged search. During a severe snowstorm, the coach becomes stuck, and Masterton learns that Trixit may be hiding nearby and that Jake Poole, a deputy sheriff, is also involved. Suspecting that Chinese travelers are acting as spies through a signaling system, Masterton takes a saddled mustang and follows one of them toward a prospector's ruined hut. His horse breaks through the snow near the summit, throwing him into a suffocating depth and rendering him unconscious.

Montagu tells Masterton that the proceedings against him have stopped and offers to return the securities if the directors send renewed notes to Sacramento within three days. Masterton and Cissy travel toward the station, and Masterton agrees to follow her to Sacramento.

The editor of the Mountain Clarion, Mr. Grey, discusses Colonel Starbottle's reported horse accident with his foreman, Richards, who suspects the colonel was attacked. Colonel Starbottle insists that he was thrown by the mustang he rode after leaving the Ramierez fonda, but his uneasy response to questions about choking leaves Mr. Grey curious. Richards investigates the fonda and is himself nearly strangled while watching the road, apparently by an unseen assailant. In a playful contest, Grey mounts the mustang and uses his spurs to stop its attempts to throw him, though the animal is injured. Mr. Grey learns from Enriquez Saltillo about a Spanish-California legend involving Friar Pedro, a lasso, and a spirit horse that strangles people. After Joshua Bilson’s wife dies, he hires Miss Euphemia Trotter as housekeeper at the Summit House in Buckeye Hill, where her competence and severity make her respected and feared. Trotter, a poor and highly experienced working woman, nurses Chris Calton after he fractures his arm and collar-bone, while Calton becomes interested in the gentler chambermaid Frida Pauline Jansen. Frida’s attention helps Calton’s spirits during his recovery, and he returns to Roanoke Ledge after improving. He sends Trotter a valuable watch and chain in gratitude, while the nature of his gifts to Frida remains undisclosed.
