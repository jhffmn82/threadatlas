Alcestis

Euripides · 437 BC · 8 sections

Abstract written by a model

The document presents Euripides’ Alcestis as a pro-satyric, pathetic comedy: a refinement of a broad comic Satyr-play tradition in which grief, blame, hospitality, and heroic rescue lead to rejoicing rather than conventional tragic finality. Apollo explains that Admetus escaped death because his wife Alcestis agreed to die for him, though Thanatos arrives to claim her. Alcestis, devoted but practical, bids farewell to Admetus and their children, secures his promise not to replace her with another wife or stepmother, and dies. The Chorus mourns the Daughter of Pelias as a model wife who died in another’s stead, while condemning Admetus’s aged parents for refusing to do so. Admetus, generous and affectionate yet selfish and self-deceiving, conceals Alcestis’s death when Heracles arrives, preserving hospitality even as the funeral proceeds. He later bitterly reproaches his father Pheres. Heracles, initially a feasting heroic reveller, learns the truth from a Servant, sobers, and resolves to recover Alcestis from Death. After Admetus returns home declaring life without her worse than death, Heracles brings a veiled woman and compels him to receive her. He reveals that he has wrested Alcestis from Death. Silent for the moment, she enters the restored household, while Heracles leaves for further labors and Admetus celebrates renewed happiness.

INTRODUCTION body

The unit interprets Euripides’ Alcestis as a pro-satyric drama that adapts a traditional story of Alcestis’s sacrifice and Heracles’s rescue of her from Death. It argues that the play refines a formerly broad and comic Satyr-play tradition into a pathetic comedy with delicate psychological treatment. Admetus is presented as generous and affectionate but selfish and self-deceiving, while Alcestis is devoted, practical, and chiefly concerned with her husband and children. Heracles retains the character of a heroic reveller as he feasts, sobers, and wrestles with Death, and the play ends in rejoicing rather than in conventional tragedy.

ALCESTIS body

Apollo explains that Admetus was spared from death because Alcestis agreed to die in his place, but Thanatos comes to claim her. Alcestis prepares for death, says farewell to Admetus and their two children, and dies after asking Admetus not to take another wife or bring a stepmother into the house. Admetus promises to honor her, mourn her for the rest of his life, and arrange a great funeral. Apollo foretells that Heracles will come and wrest Alcestis from Thanatos, but this has not yet happened in the unit.

CHORUS. body

The chorus bids farewell to the Daughter of Pelias, who has died and entered the Sunless Houses. It praises her as a queen among women because she died in another’s stead and became a model of devotion to her lord. The elders condemn his aged parents for refusing to die for him and express admiration for the bride’s sacrifice. They also hope that no later bride will replace her.

[_As the song ceases there enters a stranger, walking strongly, but body

Heracles arrives at Admetus's house while Alcestis is being taken to her burial, but Admetus conceals the identity of the dead woman and welcomes him as a guest. After Alcestis's funeral procession leaves, Admetus quarrels bitterly with his father Pheres over Pheres's refusal to die in his son's place. Heracles feasts without knowing the truth until the Servant reveals that Alcestis is dead, whereupon Heracles resolves to retrieve her from Death and return her to Admetus. The unit emphasizes Admetus's hospitality, the pain and blame surrounding Alcestis's death, and the uncertainty and inevitability of mortal life.

[_The stage is empty; then_ ADMETUS _and the_ CHORUS body

Admetus returns to his house grieving Alcestis and declares that life without her is worse than death. The Chorus urges him to endure Fate, while Heracles returns with a veiled woman whom he asks Admetus to shelter. Heracles forces Admetus to take the woman’s hand and reveal her as Alcestis, whom he won from Death after a struggle. Alcestis cannot yet speak and is led into the house, while Heracles departs to continue his labors and Admetus proclaims the household’s renewed happiness.
