Part of The Sherlock Holmes Canon

A Study in Scarlet

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1886 · 17 sections

The document follows Dr. John Watson, an army surgeon returned from Afghanistan with damaged health, as he meets Sherlock Holmes in London and becomes his roommate at 221B Baker Street. Holmes, an eccentric chemist and consulting detective, impresses Watson with his powers of observation and deduction, including his inference that Watson has been in Afghanistan. Their first major case begins when Enoch J. Drebber is found dead in an empty house at Lauriston Gardens. Holmes studies blood, footprints, a cab track, a ring, cigar ash, and the blood-written word “RACHE,” concluding that Drebber was poisoned by a tall man who had arrived with him in a cab. While police inspectors Tobias Gregson and Lestrade pursue incorrect or incomplete theories, Gregson arrests Arthur Charpentier, Holmes follows clues through constable John Rance, a failed advertisement for the ring, and the murder of Drebber’s companion, Joseph Stangerson. Stangerson’s room contains the same word, “RACHE,” and two pills, one poisonous and one harmless. Holmes proves the poison and captures the killer, cabman Jefferson Hope, at Baker Street. Hope’s confession supplies the earlier history. John Ferrier and his adopted daughter Lucy, dying on the Great Alkali Plain, were rescued by a Mormon caravan and taken to Utah. Ferrier became prosperous, and Lucy fell in love with Hope. After Hope left to seek silver, Brigham Young ordered Lucy to marry either Stangerson’s or Drebber’s son. Ferrier resisted, and he, Lucy, and Hope fled, but Ferrier was killed and Lucy was captured. Forced to marry Drebber, Lucy died within a month. Hope devoted himself to pursuing Drebber and Stangerson across the United States and Europe to London. He made Drebber choose between two pills, killed Stangerson after Stangerson attacked him, and used Lucy’s ring and “RACHE” to mark his revenge. Hope dies in custody when his aneurism bursts, refusing to name an accomplice. Holmes later explains that he identified Hope through the physical evidence and his Street Arab detective corps, though public credit goes to Gregson and Lestrade.

Sections

front matter .. A STUDY IN SCARLET. / PART I. front matter

CHAPTER I. body

Dr. Watson, a recently returned army surgeon whose health was ruined in Afghanistan, meets young Stamford in London while seeking inexpensive lodgings. Stamford introduces him to Sherlock Holmes, a chemically skilled and eccentric researcher working in a hospital laboratory. Holmes demonstrates a new test that detects hæmoglobin and identifies blood stains, then agrees to share a suite of rooms with Watson in Baker Street. Holmes also infers that Watson has been in Afghanistan, though he does not explain how.

CHAPTER II. body

Dr. John Watson moves into 221B, Baker Street, with Sherlock Holmes, and observes Holmes’s unusual habits, appearance, knowledge, and abilities. Watson learns that Holmes works as a consulting detective, applying observation, deduction, chemistry, anatomy, knowledge of crime, and other specialized skills to cases brought by official and private investigators. Holmes explains how he deduced that Watson had come from Afghanistan and correctly identifies a visitor as a retired sergeant of Marines. The chapter establishes Holmes’s confidence in the Science of Deduction and Analysis and his disdain for fictional detectives such as Dupin and Lecoq.

CHAPTER III. body

Sherlock Holmes and the Doctor travel to 3, Lauriston Gardens after Tobias Gregson’s letter reports the body of Enoch J. Drebber in an empty house. Holmes examines the scene, the body, the blood, the footprints, and the objects found in Drebber’s pockets, concluding that Drebber was murdered by poison. Lestrade discovers the blood-red inscription “RACHE,” while Holmes argues that it means “revenge” in German rather than referring to a woman named Rachel. Holmes deduces that the murderer was a tall man who arrived with Drebber in a four-wheeled cab and smoked a Trichinopoly cigar, then leaves with the Doctor to question John Rance.

CHAPTER IV. body

Sherlock Holmes and Doctor Watson leave No. 3, Lauriston Gardens and visit constable John Rance to obtain firsthand evidence about the murder. Holmes explains his deductions about the two suspects, identifying Patent-leathers as shorter and Square-toes as a tall, agitated man who likely returned for the ring. Rance reports finding a tall, red-faced, drunken man in a brown overcoat near the house, whom Holmes believes was the second suspect. Holmes concludes that the writing “RACHE” was a deliberate false clue and continues pursuing the mystery.

CHAPTER V. body

Dr. Watson and Sherlock Holmes use an advertisement about a gold wedding ring found near Brixton Road to lure the suspected murderer in the brown coat to 221B Baker Street. Instead, Mrs. Sawyer arrives claiming the ring belongs to her daughter Sally, but Holmes suspects that the old woman is a disguised accomplice. Holmes follows her to Duncan Street, where she escapes from the cab and leaves no trace. Holmes concludes that the visitor was actually a young, agile man and that the murderer has allies willing to help him.

CHAPTER VI. body

Newspapers report on the Brixton Mystery, offering speculative political explanations and praising the police investigation. Tobias Gregson traces Enoch J. Drebber through his hat to Madame Charpentier’s boarding house, questions the family, and arrests Arthur Charpentier for Drebber’s murder. Gregson believes Arthur followed Drebber to Brixton Road and killed him with a heavy stick, while Sherlock Holmes listens skeptically. G. Lestrade then arrives and reveals that Joseph Stangerson has been murdered at Halliday’s Private Hotel.

CHAPTER VII. body

Inspector Lestrade reports that Joseph Stangerson has been murdered in his hotel room, with the word “RACHE” written in blood above him. Sherlock Holmes connects Stangerson’s death to the murder of Enoch Drebber through the discovery of two pills, one harmless and one poisonous, and confirms the poison by testing them on a dying terrier. Holmes identifies the murderer as Jefferson Hope and captures him after luring him into the room disguised as a cabman. Holmes, Dr. John Watson, Lestrade, and Gregson then secure Hope for transport to Scotland Yard.

PART II. / CHAPTER I. body

John Ferrier and the five-year-old Lucy Ferrier are stranded on the Great Alkali Plain after their twenty-one-person immigrant party loses its way and suffers fatal hunger and thirst. A large Mormon caravan discovers and rescues them, taking them toward Zion under the leadership of the Mormons. Ferrier agrees to join the Mormons on the condition that he and Lucy accept their creed. The caravan continues westward after providing them with food, drink, and shelter.

CHAPTER II. body

The Mormons establish a prosperous settlement in Utah, with Brigham Young organizing the city, farms, trades, and construction of a great temple. John Ferrier becomes a wealthy and respected farmer, while his adopted daughter Lucy grows into a skilled and admired young woman. Lucy meets Jefferson Hope when he rescues her from a frightened horse trapped among cattle, and they quickly fall in love. Hope leaves to develop silver lodes in the Nevada Mountains after securing Lucy’s promise to marry him when he returns.

CHAPTER III. body

Three weeks after Jefferson Hope left Salt Lake City, Brigham Young visits John Ferrier and warns him that Lucy must marry a Mormon, preferably Stangerson's or Drebber's son. Young gives Lucy one month to choose and threatens Ferrier and his daughter if they resist the orders of the Sacred Council of Four. Ferrier and Lucy fear the consequences, but Ferrier plans to send word to Jefferson and escape Utah before the month ends. That night, Ferrier secures the house and loads his shotgun despite reassuring Lucy that they are not in danger.

CHAPTER IV. body

John Ferrier refuses to let either Joseph Stangerson or young Drebber marry his daughter Lucy, defying the Mormon authorities and provoking threats against them. Young’s promised punishment begins with mysterious warnings and a daily countdown, while the watched roads and hidden agents leave Ferrier and Lucy nearly trapped. Jefferson Hope returns from the Nevada Mountains, helps them escape the farm at night, and leads them through guarded country toward Carson City. They evade the sentinels and pass into the mountain route with horses and supplies.

CHAPTER V. body

Jefferson Hope flees through the mountains with Lucy Ferrier and her father, John Ferrier, but returns from hunting to find John dead and Lucy captured by their pursuers. Lucy is forced to marry Drebber and dies within a month, after which Hope devotes his life to revenge against Drebber and Stangerson. He follows them from Salt Lake City through the United States and Europe, eventually tracking them to London.

CHAPTER VI. body

Jefferson Hope explains to John Watson, M.D., Sherlock Holmes, and the police that he pursued Enoch Drebber and Joseph Stangerson because they were responsible for the deaths of John Ferrier and Lucy Ferrier. He describes finding the men in London, killing Drebber with one of two poison pills, and later killing Stangerson. Hope says that he used Lucy Ferrier's marriage ring and the word "RACHE" to connect Drebber's death with his vengeance, and he confesses that his own aortic aneurism may soon kill him.

“That was how Enoch Drebber came to his end. All I had to do then was body

Jefferson Hope recounts how he killed Stangerson after giving him the same choice of poisoned pills he had offered Enoch Drebber. He says Stangerson attacked him, so he stabbed him in self-defence, and then continued working as a cabman while planning to return to America. Hope is arrested after being summoned to 221B, Baker Street, and he claims that he acted as an officer of justice. Sherlock Holmes asks about Hope’s accomplice, but Hope refuses to identify him.

CHAPTER VII. body

Jefferson Hope dies in custody after his aneurism bursts, preventing the magistrates from hearing testimony about the murders of Enoch Drebber and Joseph Stangerson. Sherlock Holmes explains to Doctor Watson how he reasoned backward from physical evidence, including footprints, a cab track, poison, a ring, and information from Cleveland, to identify Hope as the murderer and cabdriver. Holmes’s Street Arab detective corps located Hope among London’s cab proprietors, leading to his capture. The Echo wrongly credits Inspectors Gregson and Lestrade with the arrest and describes the crimes as the result of an old romantic feud involving love and Mormonism.
