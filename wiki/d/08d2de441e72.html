Improving Assessment of Tutoring Practices using Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Zifei (FeiFei) Han · 2024 · 11 sections
"Improving Assessment of Tutoring Practices using Retrieval-Augmented Generation" by Zifei, Jionghao Lin, Ashish Gurung, Danielle R. Thomas, Eason Chen, Conrad Borchers, Shivang Gupta, Kenneth R. Koedinger (2024), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2402.14594

This preliminary study, "Improving Assessment of Tutoring Practices using Retrieval-Augmented Generation," tests whether Generative Pre-trained Transformers, GPT-3.5 and GPT-4, can automatically assess human tutors’ social-emotional tutoring practices and support personalized tutor training. It analyzes five transcripts from real-world United States Zoom math tutoring sessions for Grades 6–8, led by novice tutors who circulated among students in breakout rooms. The evaluations use a rubric built around five social-emotional learning and relationship-building principles, including process-focused praise, growth-mindset encouragement, helping students correct errors, responding to negative self-talk, building self-efficacy, and motivational strategies. The researchers compare four prompting approaches: two Basic Zero-shot prompt types, Tree of Thoughts, and a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) prompt. These approaches generate tutoring-practice scores and evidence-based interpretations, using either binary or 0–5 scoring criteria. In the RAG approach, tutoring transcripts and social-emotional learning principles are converted to word embeddings, selectively retrieved, and used to generate scores and evidence. A human coder judges generated assessments for correctness and hallucination, while the study tracks GPT-3.5 Turbo and GPT-4 Turbo token costs. Results show GPT-3.5 generally performs poorly, whereas RAG produces correct, non-hallucinated evaluations; GPT-4 is more accurate overall, with RAG and Zero-shot P1 performing best. RAG consistently delivers the desired output across both models, is the most cost-effective strategy, and provides accurate, contextually relevant assessments. GPT-4 Turbo costs about ten times as much as GPT-3.5 Turbo. The study plans to expand to more competencies and transcripts and develop a training-lesson recommender system.

Abstract .. 1. Introduction body

This preliminary study examines whether Generative Pre-trained Transformers, including GPT-3.5 and GPT-4, can automatically assess human tutors’ use of social-emotional tutoring strategies. It compares two Zero-shot prompts, a Tree of Thought prompt, and a Retrieval-Augmented Generator (RAG)-based prompt. The RAG prompt produced more accurate assessment texts, with fewer hallucinations and greater correctness, while also having lower financial costs than the other strategies. The study investigates both model performance and cost to inform personalized tutor training.

2. Method / 2.1. Data .. 2.3. Large Language Model Generated Evaluation and Feedback body

The dataset consists of five tutoring dialogue transcripts from real-world United States middle-school math tutoring sessions conducted on Zoom by novice human tutors. The sessions involved Grade 6–8 math, individual students in breakout rooms, and tutors who circulated among three to five rooms. The transcripts were evaluated using a rubric based on five research-based social-emotional learning and relationship-building principles. Four prompt strategies were designed to have GPT models generate tutoring-practice scores and evidence-based interpretations, including Basic Zero-shot Prompt Types I and II, Tree of Thoughts, and Retrieval-Augmented Generation.

opening .. 2.4. Evaluation Metrics body

The study developed a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)-based prompt to help a GPT model evaluate tutoring transcripts using social-emotional learning principles. Tutoring transcripts and social-emotional learning principles were converted into word embeddings, which the RAG evaluation engine selectively retrieves to generate scores and supporting evidence. A single human coder evaluated GPT-generated assessments using correctness and hallucination metrics. The study also measured financial cost by recording input and output tokens for GPT-3.5 Turbo and GPT-4 Turbo.

3. Results body

The results for RQ1 compare GPT models’ ability to evaluate tutor competency in social-emotional learning from a tutoring transcript under different prompting strategies. Figure 3 shows that GPT-3.5 generally performed poorly, while the RAG strategy produced no hallucination and correct evaluation. GPT-4 produced more accurate evaluations overall, with RAG and Zero-shot (P1) performing better than other prompting strategies. The analysis concludes that RAG-based prompting consistently produced the desired output across GPT-3.5 and GPT-4.

notes notes

opening .. 4. Conclusion body

The study evaluates Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and other prompting strategies for assessing tutoring practices based on social-emotional learning competencies. It finds that GPT-4 Turbo costs approximately 10 times more than GPT-3.5 Turbo, while RAG is the most cost-effective strategy for both models. RAG also provides accurate, contextually relevant evaluations without hallucinations in tutoring transcripts. The researchers plan to extend the approach to additional competencies, broader real-world transcripts, and a training lesson recommender system.

Appendix A. Prompting Strategies appendix

The unit, titled "Improving Assessment of Tutoring Practices using Retrieval-Augmented Generation," describes prompting strategies for evaluating a tutor in a dialogue of a tutoring session. It presents Basic Zero-shot Prompt Types I and II, a Tree of Thought Prompt, and a Retrieval-Augmented Generation Based Prompt. These prompts use principles, criteria, rubrics, evidence, and scores to assess tutoring practices and Social Emotional Learning competencies. The strategies assign binary scores or scores from 0 to 5 based on whether criteria are met.

Appendix B. Social-Emotional Learning Principles appendix

Appendix B presents Social-Emotional Learning Principles in Table 6, titled Social Emotional Learning Principles. The principles concern how to support students through praise, growth mindset encouragement, responses to errors, responses to negative self-talk, and motivational strategies. They emphasize process-focused praise, guiding students to correct errors themselves, building self-efficacy, and using intrinsic and extrinsic motivation.
