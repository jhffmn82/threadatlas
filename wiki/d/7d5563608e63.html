DioR: Adaptive Cognitive Detection and Contextual Retrieval Optimization for Dynamic Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Hanghui Guo, Jia Zhu, Shimin Di, Weijie Shi, Zhangze Chen, Jiajie Xu · 2025 · 13 sections
"DioR: Adaptive Cognitive Detection and Contextual Retrieval Optimization for Dynamic Retrieval-Augmented Generation" by Hanghui Guo, Jia Xiong Zhu, Shimin Di, Weijie Shi, Zhangze Chen, Jiajie Xu (2025), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.148

This paper presents DioR, a Dynamic Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) method designed to reduce hallucinations in large language models during complex generation. Conventional single-round RAG retrieves information once, while Dynamic RAG retrieves during generation; however, existing dynamic methods are limited by poorly controlled retrieval triggers and inadequate evaluation of retrieved content. DioR addresses both questions: when an LLM needs retrieval and what information is useful. DioR has two main parts. Adaptive Cognitive Detection uses an RNN classifier to make early, confidence-based decisions about whether retrieval is needed, and an MLP classifier to detect hallucinations in real time after retrieval. Contextual Retrieval Optimization improves retrieval content through keyword prioritization before retrieval, then stepwise document selection and semantic segmentation afterward. The method uses Wikipedia data, LLaMA2-7B outputs, IG Attribution Entropy, BM25 retrieval, and a knowledge base. Experiments compare DioR with Dragin and other retrieval-augmented methods on 2WikiMultihopQA, HotpotQA, IIRC, and StrategyQA, using LLaMA2-7B-CHAT for comparisons. DioR consistently improves on the Base method and obtains the best or tied-best results across the four datasets. Ablation results show that early detection, real-time detection, pre-retrieval optimization, and post-retrieval optimization all contribute, since removing any component lowers performance. The paper concludes that DioR more effectively controls retrieval and selects useful context, outperforming popular dynamic RAG methods. Its limitations include the inference cost of long retrieved documents and difficulty with complex problems, including mathematical ones, when using a single direct reasoning process. Future work proposes document summarization and step-by-step reasoning that divides complex problems into smaller sub-problems. The work was supported by the National Key R&D Program of China, the Zhejiang Provincial Natural Science Foundation of China, and the National Natural Science Foundation of China.

Abstract body

The abstract concerns Dynamic Retrieval-augmented Generation (RAG) for mitigating hallucinations in large language models (LLMs) during generation. It identifies two limitations of existing dynamic RAG methods: ineffective control of retrieval triggers and insufficient scrutiny of retrieval content. It introduces DioR (Adaptive Cognitive Detection and Contextual Retrieval Optimization), which determines when retrieval is needed and what retrieval is useful for LLMs. Experimental results indicate that DioR achieves superior performance on all tasks.

Introduction body

The unit concerns large language models (LLMs), Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), and the proposed DioR method for reducing hallucinations in complex generation tasks. It explains that conventional RAG uses single-turn retrieval, while Dynamic RAG performs multi-turn retrieval by selecting retrieval timing and constructing queries during generation. Existing Dynamic RAG methods are described as having weak retrieval-trigger controls and inadequate scrutiny of retrieved content. DioR addresses these limitations through Adaptive Cognitive Detection, including early and real-time hallucination classifiers, and Contextual Retrieval Optimization, which iteratively refines query keywords and uses sentence-level document chunks.

Related Work body

Large language models perform well on downstream tasks but cannot integrate new information, making them prone to hallucinations. Retrieval-Augmented Generation improves their access to non-parametric knowledge through external data retrieval, while Single-round RAG is limited on complex tasks such as multi-step reasoning and long-form question answering. Multi-turn retrieval-augmented strategies, also called Dynamic RAG, retrieve information during generation, but existing methods may use ineffective trigger timing and lack document-quality optimization. DioR addresses these issues through adaptive cognitive detection and contextual retrieval optimization.

DioR is a dynamic Retrieval-Augmented Generation method composed of adaptive cognitive detection and contextual retrieval optimization. Adaptive cognitive detection uses an RNN classifier for early confidence-based retrieval decisions and an MLP classifier for real-time hallucination detection. Contextual retrieval optimization includes pre-retrieval keyword prioritization and post-retrieval stepwise document selection and semantic segmentation. The method uses Wikipedia data, LLaMA2-7B outputs, IG Attribution Entropy, BM25 retrieval, and a knowledge base to support more accurate generation.

Experiment / Experimental Setups .. Ablation Experiment body

This unit reports experiments evaluating DioR against the Base method (Dragin) and other retrieval-augmented generation methods on 2WikiMultihopQA, HotpotQA, IIRC, and StrategyQA. DioR consistently improves over the Base method across the reported retrieval strategies and metrics, and it achieves the best or tied-best results among the compared methods in the four datasets. The experiments use LLaMA2-7B-CHAT for comparisons with other RAG methods. An ablation experiment examines early detection, real-time detection, pre-retrieval, and post-retrieval, finding that removing any component reduces performance.

Conclusion and Future work body

The paper investigates how Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) techniques can mitigate hallucinations in large language models (LLMs). It identifies two limitations of existing dynamic RAG methods: ineffective control of retrieval triggers and insufficient scrutiny of retrieved content. The proposed method, DioR (Adaptive Cognitive Detection and Contextual Retrieval Optimization), determines when retrieval is needed and what should be retrieved. DioR outperforms popular dynamic RAG methods across four knowledge-intensive generation datasets, and future work will improve its step-by-step reasoning for complex problems.

Limitations body

The paper acknowledges limitations involving the performance impact of retrieving long documents for model inference. Chunking shortens individual knowledge pieces and improves understanding, but does not reduce the total length of the input knowledge. The paper also identifies challenges in solving complex problems, including mathematical problems, through a single direct reasoning process. It proposes document summarization and a step-by-step reasoning approach that divides problems into smaller sub-problems to improve efficiency, accuracy, and reliability.
