RAGBench: Explainable Benchmark for Retrieval-Augmented Generation Systems

Robert Friel · 2025-01-16 · 19 sections
"RAGBench: Explainable Benchmark for Retrieval-Augmented Generation Systems" by Robert Friel, Masha Belyi, Atindriyo Sanyal (2024), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2407.11005

RAGBench: Explainable Benchmark for Retrieval-Augmented Generation Systems, by Robert Friel, Masha Belyi, and Atindriyo Sanyal of Galileo Technologies Inc., presents a benchmark and evaluation framework for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). RAG combines document retrieval with a Large Language Model, but generated answers can still hallucinate or fail to use relevant retrieved evidence. The paper argues that automated, granular evaluation is needed for both retrievers and response generators. It introduces RAGBench, a roughly 100k-example collection standardized from 12 real-world academic and industry datasets. Its five broad domains are biomedical research, general knowledge, legal contracts, customer support, and finance; tasks include question answering, numerical reasoning over tables and text, and multi-document retrieval and reasoning. Components have non-overlapping train, validation, and test splits and vary in contexts, lengths, generators, and retrieval setups. The paper then defines TRACe, which evaluates Context Relevance, Context Utilization, Adherence, and Completeness. Relevance asks how much retrieved context pertains to the query; utilization asks how much is used in the answer; completeness measures whether relevant information is incorporated; and adherence tests whether the answer is grounded in context rather than hallucinated. GPT-4 produces relevance, utilization, and adherence labels, with completeness derived from span-level annotations. Post-processing uses explanations, redundancy, consistency checks, and re-annotation. Validation against DelucionQA human judgments reports high agreement, while hallucination rates differ substantially across datasets. A case study evaluates 32 simulated RAG systems using RAGTruth queries, different retrievers, document counts, models, and prompts. GPT-4o and chain-of-thought prompting generally reduce hallucinations and improve utilization and completeness. FAISS tends to yield higher relevance than TF-IDF, and retrieving two documents generally performs better than four. Estimating relevance remains especially difficult because semantically related material may lack the precise information needed. Finally, the authors compare GPT-3.5-judge, RAGAS, TruLens, and a fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3-Large evaluator. A 400M-parameter fine-tuned DeBERTa model outperforms few-shot LLM judges in many settings and competes with commercial systems, though a substantial gap from ground truth remains. The paper releases RAGBench data on Hugging Face and inference and evaluation code on GitHub, framing the benchmark and TRACe as resources for stronger, more explainable RAG evaluation.

RAGBench: Explainable Benchmark for Retrieval-Augmented Generation Systems front matter

Abstract body

The unit concerns Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems, which combine a document retriever with a Large Language Model to generate responses using domain-specific context. It introduces RAGBench, a 100k-example benchmark dataset covering five industry-specific domains and multiple RAG task types. It also formalizes TRACe, an evaluation framework with explainable and actionable metrics for RAG systems. Benchmarking finds that LLM-based RAG evaluation methods struggle to compete with a finetuned RoBERTa model, motivating the use of RAGBench and TRACe to improve RAG evaluation.

Introduction body

The unit concerns Large Language Models, Retriever-Augmented Generation systems, and the need for automated RAG evaluation. It explains that RAG systems combine retrievers and generator models to improve performance on knowledge-intensive tasks but can still hallucinate when generators fail to use retrieved context effectively. It introduces RAGBench, a multi-domain dataset with metrics for context relevance, answer faithfulness, context utilization, and answer completeness. The authors report that a fine-tuned 400M-parameter DeBERTa-large model outperforms few-shot LLM judges across numerous domains and task types.

Related Work .. Finetuned RAG evaluation models body

The unit introduces RAGBench as a benchmark for developing more mature RAG evaluation systems. It distinguishes RAGBench from ground-truth RAG datasets and hallucination-focused datasets by emphasizing granular evaluation of retriever relevance, response adherence, and response completeness. It reviews automated RAG evaluation approaches including RAGAS, ARES, and heuristic systems, noting that the lack of established benchmarks makes comparison difficult. It also describes fine-tuned NLI models as an approach to RAG evaluation and states that the model trained with RAGBench outperforms LLM judges in hallucination and attribution detection while performing well on the proposed metrics.

RAGBench Construction / Component Datasets .. Data Splits body

RAGBench is a collection of 12 real-world datasets covering biomedical research, general knowledge, legal contracts, customer support, and finance. The datasets are standardized into a common retrieval-augmented generation format with annotations, while varying source domain, number of context documents, context length, and response-generation model. RAGBench includes diverse tasks such as customer-support question answering, numerical reasoning over tables and text, and retrieval and reasoning across multiple documents. Each component is split into non-overlapping train, validation, and test sets, producing approximately 100k samples in total.

TRACe Evaluation Framework .. Adherence body

The TRACe Evaluation Framework evaluates the retriever and response generator components of RAG using Utilization, Relevance, Adherence, and Completeness. It defines Context Relevance as the proportion of retrieved context that is relevant to the user query, and Context Utilization as the proportion used to produce the response. Completeness measures how much relevant context is incorporated into the response, while Adherence measures whether the response is grounded in the context without hallucinations. The framework defines document-level and example-level calculations using relevant and utilized tokens or spans from context documents.

RAGBench Statistics .. Alignment with Human Judgements body

RAGBench contains component datasets with hallucination rates ranging from 1% to 20%, with ExpertQA, CovidQA, and MS Marco having the highest rates and Cuad, FinQA, and TAT-QA the lowest. GPT-4 is used to generate Adherence, Relevance, and Utilization labels, while Completeness is derived from span-level Relevance and Utilization annotations. On the human-annotated DelucionQA benchmark, the GPT-4 annotator achieves 93% example-level and 95% span-level agreement with human judgements, with additional relevance and utilization validation on DelucionQA(40). The unit also reports how retriever, LLM, and prompt choices affect RAG system metrics.

RAG Case Study .. Estimating Context Relevance is Difficult body

The RAG Case Study evaluates the TRACe framework using 100 RAGTruth queries, 2,512 context document chunks, and 32 simulated RAG systems varying by retriever, retrieved-document count, generation model, and prompt template. The study finds that GPT-4o with chain-of-thought prompting produces fewer hallucinations than GPT-3.5 and less detailed prompts, while chain-of-thought prompting generally improves context utilization and response completeness. The experiments compare GPT-3.5-judge, RAGAS, TruLens, and a fine-tuned DeBERTa-v3-Large judge on RAGBench datasets. Context Relevance is generally harder to predict than Utilization because relevance requires determining whether semantically related context contains the specific information needed to answer a question.

Conclusion body

The paper introduces RAGBench, a large-scale dataset of real-world retrieval-augmented generation examples for training and benchmarking RAG evaluation models. It also proposes TRACe, a framework measuring utilization, relevance, adherence, and completeness, and uses an automated LLM-based method to generate labels for RAGBench that correlates highly with human judgments. A 400M-parameter DeBERTa model fine-tuned on RAGBench performs competitively with billion-parameter LLM judges and commercial RAG evaluation systems. However, a substantial gap remains between the best-performing evaluator and ground truth, motivating further work on more powerful evaluation models.

Appendix / RAGBench Code and Data appendix

RAGBench data is released on Hugging Face at the RAGBench dataset page. The model card and documentation there provide further information. RAGBench inference and evaluation code is published on GitHub in the RAGBench repository.

RAGBench Dataset Details .. ExpertQA [25] appendix

RAGBench is a benchmark assembled from publicly released academic and industry datasets, generally reframing existing question-answering resources as retrieval-augmented generation tasks. It uses retrieved or provided context documents and usually generates new responses with a large language model, while HAGRID and ExpertQA reuse their existing LLM-generated responses. The included datasets cover biomedical research, COVID-19 literature, Wikipedia, search queries, legal contracts, product manuals, technical support, finance, multilingual retrieval, and expert-curated questions in science, arts, and law.

Response Generation Prompt appendix

GPT Labeling Prompt appendix

The GPT Labeling Prompt describes a template for using GPT-4 to assess whether each sentence in an answer is supported by one or more documents. It requires identifying relevant document sentences, utilized document sentences, and support for each answer sentence. The output must be a valid JSON object containing explanations, boolean support judgments, and sentence-key lists. The prompt also specifies rules for handling partial support, general statements, well-known facts, numerical reasoning, and unsupported sentences.

Annotation Post-Processing Steps appendix

The Annotation Post-Processing Steps describe how GPT-4-turbo annotations are improved through detailed explanations, chain-of-thought, redundancy, and consistency checks. For Adherence, response-level and sentence-level annotations are compared, and conflicting examples are re-annotated up to three times. Partially supported sentences are treated as hallucinations under the annotation schema, using the sentence-level "fully_supported" boolean to identify them. The process also removes or corrects off-schema keys that GPT-4-turbo sometimes adds to the output JSON.

Annotation Alignment with Human Judgements / Adherence Alignment with DelucionQA .. Rank-based Alignment for Adherence and Relevance appendix

This unit evaluates annotation alignment with human judgements using DelucionQA, a benchmark of Jeep Gladiator queries, context documents, and LLM responses. Human annotators label response sentences as Supported, Conflicted, or Neither, while the authors compare their adherence, relevance, and utilization annotations with these judgements. Examples containing "Neither" annotations are removed because such statements often represent grounded filler or claims of missing information rather than hallucinations. The unit also evaluates rankings of simulated RAG systems using Kendall’s tau, finding high agreement between GPT-4-turbo rankings and ground truth.

RAG Case Study appendix

The RAG Case Study uses four prompt templates for answering questions: NO CONTEXT, SHORT, LONG, and LONG + CoT. The templates vary in whether they provide documents, require explanations, and instruct the chatbot to acknowledge missing information rather than invent answers. Table 6 reports comprehensive results from the RAG Case Study.

DeBERTa model training appendix

The unit describes DeBERTa model training on a Google Cloud Platform A-100 GPU instance for 3 epochs, using separate initial learning rates for the base model layers and heads, with warmup and linear decay. It also presents a RAG case study comparing TF-IDF and FAISS retrievers with k values of 2 and 4, several prompt versions, and GPT-3.5-turbo-0125 or GPT-4o as generation models. The table reports average relevance, utilization, completeness, and the percentage of hallucinated responses. FAISS generally produces higher relevance than TF-IDF, while k=2 generally performs better than k=4.
