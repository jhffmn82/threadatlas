Gallegher and Other Stories

Richard Harding Davis · 1890 · 22 sections

Abstract written by a model

This document is a collection of short stories centered on New York, Philadelphia, and Monte Carlo characters whose crises are resolved through courage, compassion, reconciliation, or impulsive generosity. It opens with office-boy Gallegher identifying fugitive Stephen S. Hade at Torresdale. He helps Michael E. Dwyer and Detective Hefflefinger seize Hade during an illegal prizefight, then risks arrest and pursuit to bring Dwyer’s report to the Philadelphia Press before deadline; the managing editor restores Gallegher’s position. Young Van Bibber next breaks his engagement, realizes his love for his fiancée, finds her in Central Park, and is understood to become engaged again. Rags Raegen, hiding after striking Pike McGonegal, finds abandoned baby Margaret, struggles to keep her alive, and finally surrenders at the Twenty-first Precinct so she can receive care; months later, he values her affection despite awaiting trial. In “The Other Woman,” the bishop tests Young Latimer’s suitability for Ellen by probing an earlier love. Ellen’s joking intervention turns painful, and Latimer leaves when she rejects his explanation that his past suffering made him worthy. Snipes, a homeless trailer for Alf Wolfe’s gang, helps bring an old farmer and Abraham together, but declines an offer of farm life to remain in New York. The ruined Goodwood Plunger, Cecil Harringford, arrives at Monte Carlo intending suicide, but helping a French couple preserve gambling winnings for a bakery changes him; he decides to return to England. Miss Catherwaight’s investigation of a pawned medal reunites Lewis Lockwood and Judge Burgoyne, former friends divided by a misunderstanding involving her mother. Several Van Bibber stories close the collection: he pays for East Side girls to ride swan-boats, sends an escaped burglar toward his family despite later learning his identity, and ingeniously arranges the eloping Standish and Miss Cambridge’s proper public wedding while misleading Standish’s older brother.

Gallegher, an office-boy at the Philadelphia Press, identifies a gloved man traveling to Torresdale as Stephen S. Hade, the fugitive suspected of murdering Richard F. Burrbank and stealing $200,000. He learns that the man, calling himself Mr. Carleton, is staying at the Eagle Inn and may attend the big fight there that night. Gallegher telegraphs Detective Hefflefinger and reports the discovery to Michael E. Dwyer, who plans to have Hade arrested quietly and transported to New York. The Press's managing editor had discharged Gallegher for playing truant, but Dwyer proposes that the paper reinstate him if the arrest succeeds.

Gallegher and the sporting editor travel by cab to Torresdale to arrange Hade’s secret arrest during an illegal prizefight. At Keppler’s barn, Gallegher and Hefflefinger enter through a window and watch the fighters from a haymow while Dwyer identifies Hade in the crowd. When the police raid the barn, Hefflefinger seizes Hade and presents him as the burglar “Arlie” Lane, alias Carleton, so he can remove him secretly. Hade offers money for his release, but Hefflefinger refuses and takes him toward the train station.

After the raid, Captain Scott orders the newspaper correspondents arrested, but Mr. Dwyer secretly gives Gallegher his notebook and tells him to get it to the Press office before the forms are locked at twenty minutes to three. Gallegher escapes the officers by pretending that Keppler is his father, steals away in McGovern's cab, and drives toward Philadelphia while pursued by a patrol-wagon. He evades two city policemen by claiming he is taking the cab to a stable, but later the cabmen who know McGovern stop him only seven blocks from the Press office. Gallegher begs them to let him deliver the report, but they pull him from the cab and prevent him from continuing.

" The managing editor of the _Press_ took up the india-rubber speaking-tube at h body

The managing editor of the Press decides not to delay the paper any longer because no news has arrived about the fight or Hade's arrest. Just as the composing room begins preparing the paper, Gallegher arrives with Mr. Dwyer's copy, reporting that Mr. Dwyer was arrested and that they captured Hade. Gallegher is exhausted and soaked, but the managing editor cares for him and tells him he will not be dismissed this time. Gallegher proudly declares that he beat the town.

" A WALK UP THE AVENUE He came down the steps slowly, and pulling mechanically at his gloves. body

Young Van Bibber leaves his fiancée after their engagement ends and walks north up the Avenue, trying to convince himself that the breakup was right. As he recalls their shared life and realizes how much he depended on her, he becomes increasingly regretful and decides to bury the returned engagement ring in their secluded place in Central Park. He discovers his fiancée there, tearfully breaking off a sprig of box, and rushes to her; they are later seen driving home together in her brougham and are understood to be engaged again.

Rags Raegen, pursued by police after striking McGonegal, flees through Case's tenement and onto its rooftops but finds the area unfamiliar and hostile. He hides beneath bedding in a nearly empty room while the police search the building and rooftops. Rags discovers Margaret, a baby whose parents are serving time on Blackwell's Island, and becomes deeply concerned for her. He feeds and comforts her as best he can, then falls asleep holding her in the deserted tenement.

Ragsey Raegen, called Rags, struggles to keep the sick baby Margaret alive during a sweltering day while hiding from ward detectives after striking Pike McGonegal. He tries to cool and feed Margaret, but lacks milk and fears that she is near death. When a newsboy announces that McGonegal has died and calls the incident murder, Rags considers fleeing across the river. Margaret's helplessness makes him hesitate, and he remains trapped between escaping and returning to her.

Raegen enters the Twenty-first Precinct station-house carrying a sick, starving baby and demands medical help. The char-woman and station personnel care for the baby, while detectives recognize Raegen as the man pursued by the police for two days. Raegen says he gave himself up rather than being captured. Three months later, he says he does not regret surrendering because the baby is better off with him and her affection sustained him while he awaited trial in the Tombs.

" THE OTHER WOMAN Young Latimer stood on one of the lower steps of the hall stairs, leaning with one hand on the broad railing and smiling down at her. body

Young Latimer tells the bishop that he loves Ellen and asks permission to marry her. The bishop expresses concern about losing his only child and questions whether Latimer has any past involving women or other dangers that could later affect Ellen. He presents a hypothetical in which Latimer has thirty days to live and asks whether Latimer would spend them with Ellen or another woman. Latimer refuses to answer the hypothetical, but the bishop interprets that refusal as revealing his true feelings.

"Well, and suppose this other woman did exist, what then? body

Latimer and the bishop discuss the possibility that another woman exists whom Latimer once loved. The bishop warns that circumstances could change after Latimer married Ellen, causing trouble if Latimer came to love the other woman again. Latimer insists the situation is impossible and says the woman is beyond the love of a man such as himself. He confirms that she is not dead and suggests that she may be married, just as someone unlocks the door and interrupts their conversation.

Ellen returns while her father and Latimer are discussing a test involving Latimer's past love. She admits that she arranged the test as a joke, but recoils from Latimer and asks her father to make him leave. Latimer passionately defends his character, explaining that overcoming his love for the other woman made him honest and worthy of Ellen. Ellen still tells him to go to the other woman, so Latimer leaves and resolves to continue without returning to her.

THE TRAILER FOR ROOM NO. body

Snipes, a homeless boy who works as a trailer for Alf Wolfe's green-goods gang, encounters an old farmer searching for his son, Abraham. Believing Abraham has gone to buy counterfeit money, Snipes tries to protect him, but the farmer rushes into room No. 8 and confronts Wolfe before discovering that Abraham is actually in New York to buy a ring. The farmer and Abraham reunite at Grand Central Depot, and the farmer invites Snipes to live on the family farm, but Snipes chooses to remain in New York and return to watching for Wolfe's signal.

"THERE WERE NINETY AND NINE" Young Harringford, or the "Goodwood Plunger," as he was perhaps better known at that time, had come to Monte Carlo in a very different spirit and in a very different state of mind from any in which he had ever visited the place before. body

Young Harringford, also known as Cecil and the “Goodwood Plunger,” comes to Monte Carlo intending to kill himself after losing his fortune, estranging his family, and suffering severe mental and physical distress. He recalls how his gambling began with his successful backing of Siren in the Goodwood Cup, followed by greater winnings, mounting debts, and disgrace. His final gamble on the Grand Prix fails when Frou Frou defeats the English horse, leaving him convinced that suicide is his only escape. At the Casino, he destroys his father’s conciliatory letter and waits for the lights to go out so he can carry out his plan.

The Goodwood Plunger is approached at Monte Carlo by a short, plainly dressed French woman who asks him to speak with her husband, Louis. Louis has won a large amount gambling but refuses to stop, risking the money needed to buy the rest of Carbut's bakery store. The woman believes Louis will listen to the Goodwood Plunger, whom she considers fortunate, rich, and experienced. The Goodwood Plunger initially misunderstands her request but finally agrees to find Louis and speak with him.

Cecil Harringford intervenes when a Frenchman continues gambling at Monte Carlo despite his wife's concern, and he helps them protect their winnings. After the Frenchman wins more than 20,000 francs at roulette, Cecil puts the money in the woman's care and sends the couple by train to Paris. At the station, the couple thank Cecil for making them happy, which reminds him of his father's letter and strengthens his desire to return home. Cecil tells Walters that they are going home to England, and he falls asleep peacefully, speaking of happiness for the rest of their lives.

THE CYNICAL MISS CATHERWAIGHT Miss Catherwaight's collection of orders and decorations and medals was her chief offence in the eyes of those of her dear friends who thought her clever but cynical. body

Miss Catherwaight, known for her cynical wit, collects medals and decorations that their recipients have pawned or sold, calling them dishonored honors. She finds a tarnished silver heart medal inscribed as a gift from Henry Burgoyne to Lewis L. Lockwood and goes to Lockwood's law office to learn its history. Mr. Latimer explains that Henry Burgoyne, now Judge Burgoyne, and Lewis Lockwood were devoted boyhood friends who later quarrelled over a woman and have remained estranged. Miss Catherwaight learns that the medal was stolen from Lockwood during a burglary and decides she cannot keep or exhibit it.

Miss Catherwaight learns that Lewis L. Lockwood and Henry Burgoyne were once close friends whose friendship was damaged by a misunderstanding involving her mother. After Lockwood visits to thank her for returning his medal, she urges him to reconcile with Burgoyne and let him keep the medal. Lockwood visits Burgoyne that night, and he and Burgoyne decide to take a shooting trip to Maryland. Mr. Latimer reports Lockwood's renewed happiness and agrees to manage matters at the office while they are away.

" VAN BIBBER AND THE SWAN-BOATS It was very hot in the Park, and young Van Bibber, who has a good heart and a great deal more money than good-hearted people generally get, was cross and somnolent. body

Van Bibber, waiting in the Park for his groom, hears three little girls from the East Side wishing they could ride the swan-boats. He pays for them to take a ride and enjoys showing them the fishes, birds, and supposedly wild parts of the lake. Afterward, the Girl He Knew and her brother tease him about the outing, but the Girl He Knew responds kindly. Van Bibber buys the girls enough tickets for a week of rides, and they return home delighted.

Van Bibber attends a prizefight in Jersey City with young Travers and two reporters, then walks through New York at dawn and encounters a burglar escaping from a great house. He overpowers the burglar, who says he has just escaped prison and wants money to join his wife and daughter in the West. Instead of surrendering him, Van Bibber buys him a ticket to Helena, Montana, escorts him to the train, and gives him money. Later, Van Bibber reads that the burglar is Abe Hubbard, alias Jimmie the Gent, who has broken jail, and concludes that he has helped him escape the country.

" VAN BIBBER AS BEST MAN Young Van Bibber came up to town in June from Newport to see his lawyer about the preparation of some papers that needed his signature. body

Cortlandt Van Bibber discovers that young Standish and Miss Cambridge have eloped and are searching for a clergyman and witnesses to marry them. He deceives young Standish's older brother by claiming that the couple has gone to Chicago, then arranges a public wedding with suitable guests, flowers, music, and a church ceremony. The couple is married at the Little Church Around the Corner and leaves for the Fall River boat. Van Bibber later worries that his lie has sent the older brother racing around Chicago in extreme heat.
