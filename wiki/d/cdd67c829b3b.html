How to Build an Adaptive AI Tutor for Any Course Using Knowledge Graph-Enhanced Retrieval-Augmented Generation (KG-RAG)

Chenxi Dong · 2023-11-29 · 9 sections
"How to Build an Adaptive AI Tutor for Any Course Using Knowledge Graph-Enhanced Retrieval-Augmented Generation (KG-RAG)" by Chenxi Dong, Yuan, Yimin, Chen, Kan, Cheng, Shupei, Wen, Chujie (2023), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2311.17696

The document presents Knowledge Graph-enhanced Retrieval-Augmented Generation (KG-RAG), an Intelligent Tutoring System framework designed to make Large Language Model tutoring more accurate, coherent, and personalized. It addresses hallucination and the weakness of conventional Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), whose semantic-similarity retrieval can return isolated snippets rather than connected explanations. Using a finance-course example involving the subprime crisis, Mortgage-Backed Securities, and Collateralized Debt Obligations, it contrasts standard RAG with KG-RAG’s retrieval of concept relationships. KG-RAG combines DeepSeek-V3, RAG, and knowledge graphs. DeepSeek-V3 is the reasoning and response-generation engine; RAG grounds answers in source material; and knowledge-guided retrieval converts student queries into semantic vectors and traverses a graph of course entities and relationships. The graph is constructed by DeepSeek-V3 from course materials and validated by a finance lecturer. Final answers synthesize the user query with both similarity-based and graph-derived context. Evaluation compares RAG and KG-RAG in response quality, learner feedback, and assessment outcomes. KG-RAG produced more complete, precise, relationship-preserving responses. Feedback from 76 university participants was strongly positive on relevance, accessibility, understanding, and comparison with human tutoring, although perceived detail was not statistically significant. In a controlled study of 76 first-year university students, the KG-RAG group scored higher than the RAG group on a 10-point multiple-choice assessment, with a reported 35% increase in assessment scores and p<0.001. The document then considers affordability, accessibility, privacy, and limitations. It presents DeepSeek-V3 as a lower-cost alternative to GPT-o1, recommends local or private-cloud deployment for sensitive data, and proposes reusing chat history to reduce costs. It acknowledges the limited participant group and calls for broader evaluations, additional academic domains and learner populations, objective quality metrics, automated graph construction, and stronger adaptive scaffolding.

Abstract—Integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) in body

The abstract concerns integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) into Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITS) for personalized education. It identifies factual accuracy and coherent, context-aware instruction as major challenges, noting that Retrieval-Augmented Generation only partially addresses them because it relies on semantic similarity. The paper introduces Knowledge Graph-enhanced Retrieval-Augmented Generation (KG-RAG), which combines structured knowledge representation with context-aware retrieval. Controlled experiments with 76 participants reported a 35% increase in assessment scores (p<0.001), supporting KG-RAG as a solution for adaptable AI tutoring systems.

I. INTRODUCTION body

The unit introduces KG-RAG, a Knowledge Graph-enhanced Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework for Intelligent Tutoring Systems. It addresses information hallucination from Large Language Models and weak conceptual coherence in responses by integrating structured knowledge graphs with retrieval. Using a finance-course example about the subprime crisis, the text contrasts standard RAG's isolated snippets with KG-RAG's connected retrieval of concepts such as Mortgage-Backed Securities and Collateralized Debt Obligations. The paper reports controlled experiments and user studies with 76 participants and discusses deployment, cost, accessibility, and privacy.

II. FOUNDATIONS OF KG-RAG body

The KG-RAG framework combines Large Language Models, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, and Knowledge Graphs to support an AI Tutor. DeepSeek-V3 serves as the framework’s primary reasoning and generation engine because of its reported accessibility, reasoning ability, and cost efficiency. Retrieval-Augmented Generation grounds responses in retrieved source materials, while Knowledge-Guided Retrieval expands context by traversing a knowledge graph. The final KG-RAG response integrates similarity-based context, knowledge-guided context, and the user query.

III. AI TUTOR DESIGN: THE KG-RAG SOLUTION body

The AI Tutor integrates DeepSeek-V3, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and a knowledge graph into the KG-RAG framework to provide precise, context-aware educational responses. Knowledge graph construction uses DeepSeek-V3 to extract entities and relationships from course materials, followed by validation by a finance lecturer. Knowledge-guided retrieval converts student queries into semantic vectors and traverses the knowledge graph to retrieve interconnected content rather than isolated text snippets. Response synthesis uses DeepSeek-V3 and retrieved course material to generate relevant and complete answers to student questions.

IV. AI TUTOR EVALUATION body

The AI Tutor Evaluation examines response quality, student feedback, and learning outcomes when comparing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) with Knowledge-Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation (KG-RAG). Comparative examples show that KG-RAG improves completeness, precision, and preservation of relationships among concepts. Feedback from 76 university participants was strongly positive for relevance, accessibility, understanding, and comparison with human tutoring, while perceived detail was not statistically significant. In a controlled experiment with 76 first-year university students, the KG-RAG group scored higher than the RAG group on a 10-point multiple-choice assessment, with a large effect size.

V. DISCUSSIONS body

This unit discusses practical deployment considerations for AI tutoring systems, focusing on economic feasibility, global accessibility, data privacy, and limitations. It identifies DeepSeek-V3 as a lower-cost alternative to GPT-o1 and describes KG-RAG as a potentially affordable tutoring framework for resource-constrained educational institutions. It recommends local or private-cloud deployment to protect sensitive educational data and proposes a chat history re-usage mechanism to reduce processing costs. The unit also notes that the user study involved 76 university students and that broader, more quantitative evaluations are needed.

VI. CONCLUSION body

The paper presents KG-RAG as a framework for addressing fundamental challenges in AI tutoring through structured knowledge representations and advanced language models. KG-RAG substantially outperforms traditional RAG-based approaches in quantitative and qualitative evaluations of learning outcomes. Its implementation with DeepSeek-V3, a globally accessible and cost-effective LLM from Alibaba Cloud, aims to reduce over-reliance on US-based models and promote a more inclusive landscape for AI in education. Future research will examine diverse academic domains and learner populations, objective text quality metrics, automated knowledge graph construction, and improved adaptive scaffolding.
