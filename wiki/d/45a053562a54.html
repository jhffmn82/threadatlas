The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies

Aeschylus · -0457 · 16 sections

The document recounts the fall of Troy and the bloody inheritance of the house of Atreus, moving from private revenge toward public justice. In Agamemnon, a Watchman sees the beacon announcing Troy’s fall, while the Chorus recalls the expedition and Agamemnon’s sacrifice of Iphigenia. A Herald confirms victory but reports losses at sea and Menelaus missing. Agamemnon returns with Cassandra, and Clytemnestra persuades him to enter the palace over purple robes. Cassandra foresees both their deaths and the house’s continuing curse. Clytemnestra kills Agamemnon and Cassandra, claiming vengeance for Iphigenia and Agamemnon’s betrayal; Aegisthus claims revenge for Atreus’s crime against Thyestes and joins her in ruling Argos. In The Libation-Bearers, Orestes secretly returns with Pylades to Agamemnon’s tomb. Electra, sent with libations by Clytemnestra, recognizes signs of Orestes’ return, and the siblings pray for vengeance. Orestes says Apollo has commanded him to avenge Agamemnon. Disguised as travellers, he falsely reports his own death, gains entry to the palace, kills Aegisthus, and then kills Clytemnestra despite her pleas. Immediately, the Furies appear to him, and he flees toward Apollo’s shrine. In The Furies, Apollo protects Orestes at Delphi and sends him to Athena for judgment, while Clytemnestra’s ghost drives the Furies to pursue him. At Athena’s temple, Orestes claims Apollo’s command and purification; the Furies insist that his mother’s murder demands vengeance. Athena creates a sworn Athenian court to decide the case. Orestes admits the killing, Apollo argues for the greater claim of Agamemnon’s death, and the votes divide equally. Athena’s deciding vote frees Orestes. She then persuades the Furies to become honored protectors of Athens rather than agents of wrath. Their final covenant rejects civic bloodshed and promises justice, prosperity, fertility, marriage, and victory.

INTRODUCTORY NOTE front matter

AGAMEMNON body

A Watchman on the Palace of Atreus sees the beacon-fire announcing that Troy has fallen and expects Clytemnestra to celebrate Agamemnon's return. The Chorus of old men of Mycenae recalls the Greek expedition, the omens at Aulis, and Agamemnon's sacrifice of his daughter to obtain favorable winds. Clytemnestra explains the chain of beacon fires from Troy to Argos and warns that the victorious Greek army must respect the gods and avoid forbidden plunder. The Chorus praises Zeus and reflects on the suffering, deaths, and losses caused by the Trojan War.

And others, far beneath the Ilian wall, body

The speaker recalls the Trojan chiefs who sleep beneath the Ilian wall in graves within the land of the foe. The city is burdened by suppressed anger and awaits tidings of its coming fate, trusting that divine retribution will eventually punish unjust prosperity. Beacon-fires have spread joyful rumours of good news, but the speaker questions whether they are truthful. A herald from the shore approaches, bearing signs of a long journey and expected to confirm whether all is well.

_Enter_ HERALD body

The Herald reports that the Greeks have conquered Troy and that the Argive host is returning after ten years, though many ships and men were lost in a storm. Clytemnestra welcomes the news and presents herself as a faithful wife awaiting Agamemnon, while the Herald says that Menelaus was separated from the fleet and may still be alive. The Chorus reflects on Helen, Paris, inherited curses, divine jealousy, and the vengeance that brought ruin upon Troy. Agamemnon arrives in triumph, gives thanks to Argos and the gods, praises Odysseus, and announces that he will return to his palace and consult about the kingdom's future.

[_Enter Clytemnestra, followed by maidens bearing purple robes._ body

Clytemnestra welcomes Agamemnon home after his long siege of Ilion and describes the grief and fear she endured during his absence. She explains that Orestes has been kept in Phocis under the protection of Strophius. Clytemnestra persuades Agamemnon to walk over purple robes into the palace, despite his fear of pride and divine jealousy. Agamemnon orders that the stranger maid be led inside gently and enters his home in triumph.

[_Exeunt all but Cassandra and the Chorus._ body

Cassandra, captive prophetess of Troy, foresees the murder of Agamemnon and her own death within the palace of Atreus. She describes the house’s inherited crimes, the Furies dwelling within it, and the vengeance that will follow Agamemnon’s death. Clytemnestra orders Cassandra into the palace, and Cassandra eventually enters after renouncing Apollo’s prophetic gift. Agamemnon’s cries then announce that he has been struck, while the Chorus debates whether to intervene.

[_The scene opens, disclosing Clytemnestra, who comes forward. The body body

Clytemnestra openly declares that she trapped and killed Agamemnon, striking him three times after he returned from Troy with Cassandra, whom she also calls his captive paramour. She justifies the murder by Agamemnon’s sacrifice of their daughter Iphigenia and by his alleged infidelity, while the Chorus condemns her and foresees further vengeance. Aegisthus enters, claims to have planned the murder in revenge for Atreus’s crime against Thyestes, and announces his intention to rule Argos with Clytemnestra. The Chorus calls for resistance and invokes Orestes, but Clytemnestra stops the threatened fight, declaring that enough blood has been shed.

THE LIBATION-BEARERS body

Orestes returns secretly to the Tomb of Agamemnon with Pylades, dedicates locks of his hair, and prays to Hermes and Zeus for help in avenging his father’s murder. Electra arrives with the Chorus of Captive Women to pour libations sent by Clytemnestra, but she struggles to find words that do not honor the woman who helped kill Agamemnon. At the tomb, Electra and the women pray for Orestes’ return and for the slayers to be slain. Electra discovers a lock of hair and footprints that seem to belong to Orestes, though she is not yet certain he has returned.

ORESTES (_approaching suddenly_) body

Orestes reveals his identity to Electra and explains that Apollo’s oracle has commanded him to avenge Agamemnon’s murder. At Agamemnon’s tomb, Orestes, Electra, and the Chorus pray for aid, while Orestes interprets Clytemnestra’s dream of a serpent as a prophecy of her death. Orestes and Pylades plan to enter the palace in disguise and murder Aegisthus and Clytemnestra. The Chorus invokes the inherited curse of the house of Atreus and declares that Justice will repay bloodshed with bloodshed.

[_Enter Orestes and Pylades, in guise of travellers_. body

Orestes and Pylades, disguised as travellers, enter the palace and falsely announce that Orestes has died. Clytemnestra receives the news, while the Chorus recognizes the deception and urges Kilissa to summon Aegisthus without his guards. Orestes kills Aegisthus and, despite Clytemnestra’s appeals, kills her as well, fulfilling Apollo’s command and avenging Agamemnon. Orestes then sees the Furies, declares that madness is overtaking him, and flees toward Apollo’s shrine at Delphi.

THE FURIES body

The Pythian Priestess describes Delphi’s prophetic succession and discovers Orestes clinging to Apollo’s altar while the sleeping Furies surround him. Apollo defends Orestes, who killed his mother at Apollo’s command, and sends him with Hermes toward Athena’s city to seek judgment. The Ghost of Clytemnestra awakens the Furies and urges them to pursue Orestes for her murder. Apollo and the Furies dispute whether the killing of a mother or the killing of a husband deserves greater vengeance, leaving the case to be guarded and judged by Athena.

_The scene changes to Athens. In the foreground, the Temple of Athena body

Orestes takes refuge at the Temple of Athena, clinging to Athena’s statue while the Furies pursue him for killing his mother. He declares that Apollo commanded the deed and that purification has cleansed him of its blood guilt. Athena arrives, hears the claims of Orestes and the Furies, and decides that the matter must be settled by a sworn court in Athens. The Furies fear that Orestes’ acquittal would overturn the ancient laws of justice.

[_Re-enter Athena, with twelve Athenian citizens_. body

Athena convenes a trial at a newly established council-court to judge Orestes for killing his mother. Orestes admits the killing, while Apollo argues that he acted under Zeus's command and that a father's death is more significant than a mother's. Athena casts the deciding vote for Orestes when the judges' votes are equal, freeing him from blood-guilt. Orestes vows that Argive chieftains will never again bring war against Athens, and Athena establishes the court and its principles of lawful government.

CHORUS body

Athena persuades the Furies to abandon their wrath after the trial of Orestes and to remain in Athens as honored protectors. She offers them a sanctuary, worship, and power over the prosperity of the land, while they promise blessings for Athens and its people. Athena and the Furies establish a covenant that rejects civic bloodshed and promotes justice, fertility, marriage, and victory. Athena then leads the Furies in procession to their shrine beneath the Areopagus.
