Abstract written by a model

This is a long paper in the Proceedings of the 2025 Conference of the Nations of the Americas Chapter of the Association for Computational Linguistics: Human Language Technologies, titled “Rationale-Guided Retrieval Augmented Generation for Medical Question Answering.” Jiwoong Sohn, Yein Park, Chanwoong Yoon, Sihyeon Park, Hyeon Hwang, Mujeen Sung, Hyunjae Kim, and Jaewoo Kang, affiliated with Korea University, Kyung Hee University, and AIGEN Sciences, present RAG2, a framework intended to make large language models more reliable for biomedical question answering. The paper begins from the problems of hallucinations and outdated medical knowledge. RAG2 refines retrieval-augmented generation through three main components: it uses model-generated rationales rather than the original question to formulate retrieval queries; it retrieves an equal number of documents from PubMed, PMC, textbooks, and clinical guidelines, then reranks them with MedCPT; and it filters snippets according to their usefulness to the base model. The filtering approach uses model outputs and perplexity differences, while training only a small filtering model and using single-step retrieval, in contrast with methods requiring more computation, fine-tuning, API costs, or iterative retrieval. The study evaluates Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Meerkat-7B, and GPT-4o on the four-option multiple-choice benchmarks MedQA, MedMCQA, and MMLU-Med. RAG2 outperforms several RAG baselines, including MedRAG, improving average accuracy over the underlying models by 6.1% for Llama-3-8B-Instruct, 3.8% for Meerkat-7B, and 0.9% for GPT-4o; it improves state-of-the-art LLMs by up to 6.1% and the previous best medical RAG model by up to 5.6%. Analyses find that advanced retrieval and filtering matter, context length affects the best number of snippets, and GPT-4o produces the strongest rationale queries. A COPD exacerbation example shows filtering out an irrelevant clinical snippet enables selection of BiPAP rather than needle decompression. The paper concludes that RAG2 is a step toward reliable AI-assisted medical decision-making, while noting limits: testing is confined to biomedicine, one Flan-T5 filtering-model size was used, incorrect rationales can retrieve distractors, and the filter handles one snippet at a time. The research received support from several Korean funding programs.

Abstract body

The unit concerns large language models and their use in biomedicine, where hallucinations and outdated knowledge remain challenges. It introduces RAG2 (RAtionale-Guided RAG), a framework that improves retrieval-augmented generation by filtering document snippets, using LLM-generated rationales as queries, and retrieving evenly from four biomedical corpora. RAG2 improves state-of-the-art LLMs by up to 6.1% and outperforms the previous best medical RAG model by up to 5.6% across three medical question-answering benchmarks.

Introduction body

The unit concerns RAG2, a retrieval-augmented generation framework designed to improve the reliability of large language models in biomedical question answering. RAG2 uses model-generated rationales for query formulation, retrieves evidence equally from PubMed, PMC, textbooks, and clinical guidelines, and filters snippets according to their usefulness to the base model. The framework is evaluated on MedQA, MedMCQA, and MMLU-Med, where it improves the performance of models including Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Meerkat-7B, and GPT-4o. RAG2 outperforms four recent RAG baselines, including MedRAG, and improves baseline performance by up to 6.1% across the three datasets.

2 / 2.1 .. 2.2 body

This unit reviews Retrieval-Augmented Generation methods, including basic, adaptive, rationale-based, and uncertainty-based approaches. It explains that many existing methods require substantial computation, API costs, fine-tuning, or iterative retrieval, whereas the described method trains only a small filtering model and uses uncertainty during training. It also surveys Medical RAG systems such as MedCPT, MedRAG, Self-BioRAG, Bailicai, and i-MedRAG. The unit emphasizes improved training efficiency and runtime performance through single-step retrieval.

3 / 3.1 .. 3.4 body

This unit explains the training objective and retrieval pipeline of RAG models. It describes rationale-guided filtering, which evaluates retrieved documents using model outputs and perplexity differences before passing them to an LLM. It presents rationale-based query formulation, where model-generated rationales are used instead of the initial query to search for document snippets. It also introduces balanced retrieval, which selects equal numbers of documents from each corpus and reranks them with MedCPT.

4 / 4.1 .. 4.3 body

This unit evaluates medical question answering using MedQA, MedMCQA, and MMLU-Med, which contain four-option multiple-choice questions. It compares Llama-3-8B-Instruct, Meerkat-7B, and GPT-4o with several retrieval-augmented generation methods, including RAG2, MedCPT, and MedRAG. RAG2 improves average accuracy over the underlying models by 6.1% for Llama-3-8B-Instruct, 3.8% for Meerkat-7B, and 0.9% for GPT-4o. The experiments also find that RAG methods without advanced retrieval and filtering can produce inconsistent results, while the filtering model transfers effectively to MMLU-Med.

5 / 5.1 .. 5.3 body

This unit evaluates RAG methods, filtering methods, balanced retrieval, and rationale query quality using MedQA, MedMCQA, and MMLU-Med. RAG2 and rationale-guided filtering generally outperform MedRAG, while model context length affects the optimal number of retrieved snippets. GPT-4o produces the strongest rationale queries, followed by Meerkat-7B and Llama-3-8B-Instruct. A COPD exacerbation case study shows that filtering an irrelevant clinical document snippet helps the model choose BiPAP instead of needle decompression.

6 body

The study presents RAG2, a framework for improving the reliability and performance of LLMs in biomedical question answering. RAG2 integrates rationale queries, balanced retrieval, and rationale-guided filtering to refine the RAG pipeline. Experimental results show that it improved the accuracy of various state-of-the-art LLMs across multiple medical question answering benchmarks. The work is described as an important step toward more reliable AI-assisted medical decision-making.

Limitation body

The RAG2 framework has been tested only in the biomedical domain, so its applicability to general domains remains unexamined. The study used one size of the Flan-T5 model as a filtering model, while different model sizes or architectures may improve results. Incorrect rationales may cause the retriever to gather distracting evidence, although the risk is limited because errors are usually a small part of the rationale and the filter can discard distractors. Perplexity-based labels were created by evaluating snippets individually, and the Flan-T5 model can filter only one snippet at a time because of its limited context length.
