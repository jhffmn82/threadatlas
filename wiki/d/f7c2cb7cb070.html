M-RAG: Reinforcing Large Language Model Performance through Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Multiple Partitions

Zheng Wang, Shu Xian Teo, Jieer Ouyang, Yongjun Xu, Wei Shi · 2024 · 9 sections
"M-RAG: Reinforcing Large Language Model Performance through Retrieval-Augmented Generation with Multiple Partitions" by Zheng Wang, Shu Mei Teo, Jieer Ouyang, Yongjun Xu, Wei Shi (2024), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.acl-long.108

The paper presents M-RAG, a Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework that treats database partitions as the basic units of retrieval and generation. It argues that selecting among multiple partitions can reduce retrieval noise and better emphasize crucial memories than searching an entire database. M-RAG combines a frozen large language model with multi-agent reinforcement learning: Agent-S selects a database partition, while Agent-R iteratively refines retrieved memories. Both use Deep Q-Network policies trained by evaluating generated hypotheses against references; at inference, the selected partition and refined database support generation, with complexity comparable to naive RAG aside from training-time refinement. The paper situates M-RAG alongside Naive, Advanced, and Modular RAG, reinforcement-learning work including WebGPT, InstructGPT, R3, and MMQS, and distinguishes it from Multi-source Knowledge-grounded Dialogue System by its datasets, solutions, and tasks. Experiments compare M-RAG with Naive RAG, Self-RAG, and Selfmem across summarization, machine translation, and dialogue generation, including XSum, BigPatent, JRC-Acquis, and DailyDialog. Across seven datasets and three language-model architectures, M-RAG generally produces the strongest results, reporting improvements of 11% in summarization, 8% in translation, and 12% in dialogue generation. Ablations find both agents useful; four partitions for Agent-S and three candidates for Agent-R are effective, whereas too many partitions or whole-database retrieval can hurt performance. Top-1 retrieval can match Top-3 results more quickly. The paper notes quantized models and inefficient training caused by repeated LLM queries as limitations.

Methodology .. 3.4 / The M-RAG Framework body

M-RAG is a multi-partition retrieval-augmented generation framework for language generation tasks such as summarization, translation, and dialogue generation. It uses Agent-S to select a database partition and Agent-R to iteratively refine retrieved memories, with both agents trained through multi-agent reinforcement learning. Training evaluates generated hypotheses against references, while inference uses the refined database and the selected partition to support a frozen large language model. The framework uses Deep Q-Networks to learn policies for both agents and has inference complexity comparable to naive retrieval-augmented generation, apart from training-time memory refinement.

Experiments / Experimental Setup .. Experimental Results body

The unit evaluates M-RAG against Naive RAG, Self-RAG, and Selfmem across summarization, machine translation, and dialogue generation using XSum, BigPatent, JRC-Acquis, and DailyDialog. M-RAG generally achieves the strongest results, including improvements over Selfmem on Mixtral 8×7B and consistent gains across several language models and tasks. Ablation studies show that both Agent-S and Agent-R contribute to performance, while parameter studies identify four partitions for Agent-S and three candidates for Agent-R as effective settings. The experiments also find that excessive database partitioning or retrieving from the entire database can reduce performance, while Top-1 retrieval can offer faster runtime with comparable results to Top-3 retrieval.

Conclusion and Limitations body

The paper proposes a multiple partition paradigm for RAG to refine retrieval and emphasize pivotal memories. It introduces M-RAG, a multi-agent reinforcement learning framework designed to address challenges in applying RAG across multiple partitions. Experiments on seven datasets covering three language generation tasks validate M-RAG's effectiveness. The study uses quantized language models because of computational constraints and notes limitations in training efficiency because the LLMs must be queried during training.
