RAG-HAT: A Hallucination-Aware Tuning Pipeline for LLM in Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Juntong Song, Xingguang Wang, Juno Zhu, Yuanhao Wu, Xuxin Cheng, Randy Zhong, and Cheng Niu · 2024-11 · 11 sections
"RAG-HAT: A Hallucination-Aware Tuning Pipeline for LLM in Retrieval-Augmented Generation" by Juntong Song, Xingguang Wang, Juno Zhu, Yuanhao Wu, Xuxin Cheng, Randy Zhong, Cheng Niu (2024), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-industry.113

The document presents RAG-HAT, a Hallucination Aware Fine-Tuning pipeline for retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems using large language models (LLMs). Although RAG brings current retrieved information into LLM responses, models can still distort, misinterpret, or invent content. RAG-HAT addresses this through hallucination detection, correction, and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), without additional human annotations. It situates the work among detection methods based on token probabilities, entropy, consistency checks, and unified QA-trained detectors, as well as mitigation through contrastive decoding and factuality-based preference data. Its focus is specifically RAG scenarios. The work uses RAGTruth for training and evaluation, Marco and XSum to expand hallucination-suppression training data, and WebGLM for evaluation. Marco includes user queries and Bing web documents, WebGLM supports web-enhanced question answering, and XSum contains BBC News summaries. The pipeline first trains a hallucination detection model to classify hallucinations and generate explanations and defensive advice. GPT-4 Turbo expands RAGTruth with descriptions, and selective sampling regenerates examples with incorrect binary labels. Training occurs in two stages, using LoRA for interpretations and a cascaded inference setup. GPT-4 Turbo then rewrites hallucinated RAG responses; these revised outputs and original responses create 19,721 chosen/reject preference pairs for DPO. The mitigation model is trained from Qwen/Qwen1.5-4B-Chat, while Llama-3-8B Instruct serves as the detection model. On RAGTruth and a 1,000-example WebGLM sample, RAG-HAT reduces hallucination rates by about 26.9% and 26.8%, respectively, based on averaged detector, GPT-4 Turbo, and human metrics. GPT-4 Turbo comparisons also find improved answer quality. Analysis examines defensive advice, overly cautious penalization, and WebGLM augmentation: the full dataset reaches a 51.5% win rate, while removing OCP raises it by 2.6%. Direct label prediction outperforms jointly generated labels and descriptions on Llama-3-8B. NewsBreak is experimenting with RAG-HAT for local-information RAG systems. Limitations include no tests on larger models such as Llama3-70B and no evaluation in finance or medicine because expert annotators were unavailable.

Abstract body

The unit concerns retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), large language models (LLMs), and Hallucination Aware Tuning (HAT). RAG integrates up-to-date information into LLMs, but LLMs may still distort or misinterpret source content and produce hallucinations. HAT trains hallucination detection models to identify and describe hallucinations, after which GPT-4 Turbo corrects them. The original and corrected outputs form a preference dataset for Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), which reduces hallucinations and improves answer quality.

Introduction body

The introduction concerns transformer-based Large Language Models, Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG), and the persistent problem of hallucination. It presents RAG-HAT, a Hallucination Aware Fine-Tuning pipeline that combines hallucination detection with mitigation. RAG-HAT uses a detection model to identify and describe hallucinations, GPT-4 Turbo to revise RAG outputs, and Direct Preference Optimization to train the LLM used in the RAG setup. The method is reported to reduce hallucination rates and improve response quality without requiring additional human annotations.

Related Work / Hallucination Detection .. Hallucination Mitigation body

The unit reviews methods for hallucination detection and mitigation in large language models (LLMs). Manakul et al. (2023) use token probabilities and entropy, while Dhuliawala et al. (2023) and SelfCheckGPT analyze consistency across decomposed questions or sampled responses. Ravi et al. (2024) trained a unified hallucination detection model on QA datasets, but it does not cover summarization or structured-data answer generation. For mitigation, Shi et al. (2023) use contrastive decoding, while Tian et al. (2023) construct a pairwise dataset for Direct Preference Optimization using factuality signals, with the stated research focusing specifically on Retrieval-Augmented Generation scenarios.

Dataset body

The unit describes four datasets used for hallucination-related model training and evaluation. RAGTruth is a word-level hallucination evaluation resource designed for retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and it is used for both training and system evaluation. Marco and XSum are used to expand the hallucination suppression training set, while WebGLM is used for system evaluation. Marco contains users’ queries and Bing web documents, WebGLM is a web-enhanced question-answering dataset, and XSum consists of BBC News summaries.

Methodology / Hallucination Detection Model Training / 4.1.1 .. Data Source Extension body

The methodology describes training a hallucination detection model to classify hallucinations and generate explanations and defensive advice. GPT-4 Turbo expands the RAGTruth dataset with descriptions, while selective sampling regenerates examples when the binary label is incorrect. The detection model is trained in two stages, with LoRA training used for interpretations, and a cascaded setup used during inference. For DPO training, GPT-4 Turbo rewrites hallucinated responses, additional datasets extend the preference data, and 19,721 chosen/reject pairs are generated.

Experiments / Implementation Details .. Answer Quality body

The experiments evaluate RAG-HAT for hallucination suppression and answer quality using the RAGTruth test set and a 1,000-example WebGLM sample. Llama-3-8B Instruct is used for the hallucination detection model, while Qwen/Qwen1.5-4B-Chat is used for hallucination mitigation training with DPO. RAG-HAT reduces hallucination rates by about 26.9% on RAGTruth and 26.8% on WebGLM, according to averaged detector, GPT-4 Turbo, and human-based metrics. GPT-4 Turbo comparisons also indicate that RAG-HAT improves answer quality over the original model.

Analysis / Cautious Penalization (OCP) .. Industry Application body

The analysis evaluates defensive advice, overly cautious penalization (OCP), and data augmentation in WebGLM, finding that the full dataset achieved a 51.5% win rate and that removing OCP increased the win rate by 2.6%. It also compares two-stage detection-model training with approaches that jointly generate hallucination descriptions and prediction labels, finding that direct label prediction performs better on Llama-3-8B. NewsBreak is experimenting with RAG-HAT in its Retrieval-Augmented Generation systems to improve the accuracy and relevance of local information. The conclusion presents RAG-HAT as a hallucination-aware tuning pipeline comprising detection, rewriting, and mitigation, which reduces hallucinations while improving answer quality.

Limitions body

The unit concerns the limitations of the authors' evaluation of their method and model. Due to limited computational resources, they did not test their method on larger LLMs such as Llama3-70B. They also did not evaluate their model in domains requiring expert knowledge, including finance and medicine, because annotators with specific domain expertise were unavailable.
