Generation-Augmented Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering

Yuning Mao, Pengcheng He, Xiaodong Liu, Yelong Shen, Jianfeng Gao, Jiawei Han, Weizhu Chen · 2021 · 11 sections
"Generation-Augmented Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering" by Yuning Mao, Pengcheng He, Xiaodong Liu, Yelong Shen, Jianfeng Gao, Jiawei Han, Weizhu Chen (2021), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2021.acl-long.316

Abstract written by a model

This document presents Generation-Augmented Retrieval (GAR), a method for open-domain question answering that enriches a question before retrieval. In a retriever-reader system over a large collection such as Wikipedia, GAR trains sequence-to-sequence models to generate three kinds of likely relevant context: answers, answer-containing sentences, and passage titles. These generated contexts, derived from knowledge in pretrained language models and trained with in-domain contexts rather than external supervision or retrieved passages, are appended to the original question. BM25 retrieves passages for the expanded queries, and results from the context types can be fused. GAR is positioned against conventional query expansion, query reformulation, dense retrieval such as DPR, and generative QA. Its focus is retrieval: it extends sparse retrieval beyond the original question while retaining sparse-representation efficiency. The work evaluates retrieval and question answering on Natural Questions and TriviaQA, comparing BM25, DPR, GAR, and RM3-based expansion. It uses Anserini retrieval, BART-large for query-context generation, a BERT-base-initialized extractive reader, a DPR-style span-voting reader, and a BART-large generative reader. Experiments find that generated answer, sentence, and title contexts raise lexical overlap with relevant passages and work complementarily when combined. GAR substantially improves BM25 retrieval, approaches or exceeds DPR at larger retrieval cutoffs, and delivers strong extractive and generative end-to-end QA performance. GAR with BM25 can match or outperform dense retrieval, while fusing GAR with DPR improves results further. The document reports state-of-the-art extractive OpenQA results on Natural Questions and TriviaQA, competitive generative results, and lower training, indexing, and retrieval computation than DPR. It concludes with possible improvements to generation and fusion, joint optimization, hyperparameter tuning, and application to other text-matching tasks.

Generation-Augmented Retrieval for Open-Domain Question Answering front matter

Abstract body

The abstract proposes Generation-Augmented Retrieval (GAR) for open-domain question answering. GAR generates diverse, heuristically discovered relevant contexts without external resources as supervision and uses them to augment queries. GAR with BM25 achieves performance comparable to or better than dense retrieval methods such as DPR, and combining GAR with DPR improves performance further. GAR achieves state-of-the-art results on Natural Questions and TriviaQA under extractive question answering and consistently outperforms other retrieval methods with the same generative reader.

Introduction body

The unit concerns Open-domain question answering (OpenQA) and introduces Generation-Augmented Retrieval (GAR), which expands questions with contexts generated by a pre-trained language model before retrieval. GAR trains sequence-to-sequence models to generate answers, answer-containing sentences, or passage titles, then appends those contexts to the original questions for retrieval. Experiments on Natural Questions and TriviaQA show that GAR with BM25 improves retrieval and question-answering performance, can match or outperform dense methods such as DPR, and can complement DPR when their results are fused.

Related Work body

The unit compares GAR with conventional query expansion and recent query reformulation methods. GAR expands queries using knowledge stored in PLMs, generated through standard seq2seq learning with in-domain contexts such as passage titles, rather than relying on retrieved passages or external resources. For OpenQA, GAR extends sparse retrieval beyond question information and can achieve comparable or better performance than dense methods while retaining sparse-representation efficiency. The unit also distinguishes GAR from generative QA, which focuses on generating answers or improving the reading stage.

Generation-Augmented Retrieval / Task Formulation .. Generative Reader body

The unit describes GAR, a generation-augmented retrieval method for OpenQA using a retriever-reader architecture over a large document collection such as Wikipedia. GAR generates answers, answer-containing sentences, and relevant passage titles as query contexts, appends them to the question, and retrieves passages with BM25 before fusing the results. It evaluates both extractive and generative readers, including a DPR-style extractive reader with passage-level span voting and a BART-large generative reader. The method is intended to improve retrieval effectiveness and efficiency and thereby improve question-answering performance.

Experiment Setup / Datasets .. Implementation Details body

The experiments evaluate open-domain question answering methods on Natural Questions and TriviaQA using retrieval and reading metrics. Passage retrieval is compared across BM25, DPR, GAR, and RM3-based query expansion, while passage reading uses extractive and generative readers. GAR uses Anserini for retrieval, BART-large for query-context generation, and BERT-base initialization for its extractive reader.

Experiment Results .. Efﬁciency of GAR body

The unit evaluates GAR for query-context generation, passage retrieval, passage reading, model generalizability, and computational efficiency in OpenQA, primarily on NQ and Trivia. GAR generates answer, sentence, and title contexts that increase lexical overlap with relevant passages, and combining these contexts improves retrieval because they are complementary. GAR substantially improves BM25 retrieval, approaches or exceeds DPR at larger retrieval cutoffs, and achieves strong end-to-end extractive and generative QA performance, especially when combined with DPR. GAR is also reported to require substantially less training, indexing, and retrieval computation than DPR.

Conclusion .. Future Extensions body

The unit concludes that Generation-Augmented Retrieval (GAR) uses relevant contexts generated by pretrained language models without external supervision to enrich query semantics and improve retrieval accuracy. GAR with sparse representations performs similarly to or better than state-of-the-art methods using dense representations of the original queries, and combining sparse and dense representations improves results further. GAR achieves state-of-the-art end-to-end performance on extractive OpenQA and competitive performance under the generative setup. Future work includes improving context generation and fusion, jointly optimizing generation and retrieval, tuning hyperparameters, and applying GAR to other text-matching tasks.
