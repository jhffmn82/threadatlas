Robust Retrieval Augmented Generation for Zero-shot Slot Filling

Michael Glass, Gaetano Rossiello, Md Faisal Mahbub Chowdhury, Alﬁo Gliozzo · 2021 · 15 sections
"Robust Retrieval Augmented Generation for Zero-shot Slot Filling" by Michael Glaß, Gaetano Rossiello, Md Faisal Mahbub Chowdhury, Alfio Gliozzo (2021), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2021.emnlp-main.148

Abstract written by a model

The document presents Knowledge Graph Induction (KGI), an end-to-end zero-shot slot-filling system for automatically building high-quality knowledge graphs from document collections. Slot filling, a Knowledge Base Population task, identifies predetermined relations for an entity and finds their fillers in a corpus to populate infobox-like structures. KGI combines Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR), which retrieves supporting passages, with Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG), which generates the target tail entity from retrieved evidence. The document begins by positioning KGI against language-model and retrieval-based methods including RAG, KILT, Multi-task DPR, DensePhrases, and GENRE. Although RAG is a strong KILT baseline for generating fillers, its retrieval is weaker than BM25. KGI trains DPR and RAG in two phases using KILT provenance ground truth and target tail entities. DPR uses separate BERT-based query and passage encoders, positive passages, hard negatives, and batch negatives; passages are encoded and indexed with HNSW in FAISS. Rather than mining hard negatives with BM25 alone, KGI introduces Dense Negative Sampling, which draws difficult negatives from a learned dense index. RAG retrieves five passages and trains its query encoder through the retrieved passages’ effect on generating the correct entity. Experiments evaluate zero-shot slot filling on T-REx and zsRE using accuracy, F1, provenance quality, and combined KILT metrics. KGI1 surpasses earlier systems, especially when correct provenance is required. DPRDNS+RAG achieves the best reported results on both datasets, and KGI ranks first on the KILT leaderboard. Reported KILT-F1 improvements over previously submitted systems are 38.24% on T-REx and 21.25% on zsRE. The analysis shows that KGI1 performs best when given gold passages but worst with random passages, indicating strong use of explicit retrieved knowledge and less dependence on implicit knowledge. Explicit evidence is particularly useful for relations such as performer, author, and place of birth, while taxonomy and language relations are often handled by the language model. Finally, the work adapts a pretrained DPR to a new TACRED slot-filling variant by indexing a new corpus. KGI1 exceeds ranking baselines in zero-shot adaptation and improves further with rapid few-shot fine-tuning. The authors release pretrained models, source code, and the zero/few-shot TACRED benchmark.

Abstract body

The unit concerns automatically inducing high-quality knowledge graphs from document collections through advances in slot filling. It presents a zero-shot slot-filling approach that extends dense passage retrieval with hard negatives and robust training procedures for retrieval augmented generation models. The model improves passage retrieval and slot value generation on the T-REx and zsRE datasets, ranks first on the KILT leaderboard, and demonstrates domain adaptation on a new TACRED slot-filling variant.

Introduction body

Slot filling is described as a sub-task of Knowledge Base Population (KBP). Its goal is to recognize a predetermined set of relations for an entity and use them to populate infobox-like structures. A system can do this by locating the entity in a corpus and gathering information about slot fillers from the surrounding context. A slot filling system processes and indexes documents before being prompted with an entity and relations.

opening body

The unit concerns slot filling systems and introduces KGI (Knowledge Graph Induction), an end-to-end system using Dense Passage Retrieval (DPR) and Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG). KGI improves KILT performance on T-REx and zsRE, with gains of 38.24% and 21.25% KILT-F1, respectively, over previously submitted systems. The system uses hard negative mining to improve DPR context-encoder training and supports domain adaptation by indexing a new corpus with a pretrained DPR. The authors evaluate this approach on a zero/few-shot variant of TACRED and release KGI’s pretrained models and source code.

Related Work .. DPR for Slot Filling body

This unit reviews language-model and retrieval-based approaches to zero-shot slot filling, including RAG, KILT, DPR, Multi-task DPR, DensePhrases, and GENRE. KILT's strongest baseline for producing slot fillers is RAG, which uses DPR to retrieve passages and a BART-initialized generator to produce answers, although its retrieval metrics are worse than BM25. Multi-task DPR improves retrieval by training DPR encoders on multiple KILT tasks. Knowledge Graph Induction (KGI) combines DPR and RAG, training both for slot filling in two phases using KILT provenance ground truth and target tail entities.

opening body

The unit describes DPR training and hard negative mining as used by Karpukhin et al. (2020) and Multi-DPR (Maillard et al., 2021). Anserini retrieves candidate passages with BM25, and a remaining top-ranked passage becomes a hard negative for each query. DPR trains separate BERT-based query and passage encoders using positive passages, hard negatives, and batch negatives, with a row-wise softmax and negative log-likelihood loss. The trained passage encoder produces vectors for approximately 32 million KILT passages, which are indexed with HNSW in FAISS using 8-bit scalar quantization.

opening .. Dense Negative Sampling body

This unit describes how the query encoder is trained for slot filling and then injected into RAG for Natural Questions, where it can be updated without disrupting generation. RAG retrieves five passages with an ANN index, combines their weighted BART predictions, and trains the query encoder through the passages’ impact on generating the correct tail entity. The unit introduces Dense Negative Sampling (DNS), which mines DPR hard negatives from a learned dense index rather than with BM25. DNS and subsequent RAG training improve retrieval, with DPRDNS+RAG achieving the best reported results on both T-REx and zsRE.

KILT Experiments .. Slot Filling Evaluation body

This unit describes KILT experiments evaluating zero-shot slot filling on T-REx and zsRE. It explains passage segmentation of the KILT knowledge source, training settings for DPR, RAG, and the KGI systems, and their computational requirements. The evaluation compares retrieval methods and reports slot-filler accuracy, F1, provenance quality, and combined KILT metrics. KGI1 outperforms earlier systems, particularly on slot-filler accuracy and metrics requiring correct provenance.

Analysis .. Slot Filling Evaluation body

The unit evaluates retrieval and knowledge use in slot filling, comparing KGI1, KGI0, BARTLARGE, and other systems on KILT, zsRE, and T-REx. KGI1 performs best with gold passages but worst with random passages, showing that it uses explicit knowledge effectively while relying less on implicit knowledge. The analysis finds that explicit knowledge helps most for relations such as performer, author, and place of birth, while taxonomy and language relations are often handled by the language model. The unit then studies domain adaptation from Wikipedia and Wikidata-based tasks to TACRED, where KGI1 outperforms ranking baselines in zero-shot and improves further with few-shot training.

Conclusion body

The paper presents KGI, a novel approach to zero-shot slot filling that improves Dense Passage Retrieval with hard negatives from the dense index and uses robust training procedures for Retrieval Augmented Generation. KGI was evaluated on the T-REx and zsRE slot filling datasets, ranking first on the KILT leaderboard with KILT-F1 improvements of 38.24 and 21.25 percentage points, respectively. The authors also proposed and released a zero/few-shot slot filling benchmark based on TACRED for evaluating domain adaptation, where KGI achieved much better zero-shot results than the baselines. KGI also showed significant improvements after rapid few-shot fine-tuning.
