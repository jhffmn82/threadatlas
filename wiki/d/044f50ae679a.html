Unified Active Retrieval for Retrieval Augmented Generation

Qinyuan Cheng · 2024 · 18 sections
"Unified Active Retrieval for Retrieval Augmented Generation" by Qinyuan Cheng, Xiaonan Li, Shimin Li, Qin Zhu, Zhangyue Yin, Yunfan Shao, Linyang Li, Tianxiang Sun, Hang Yan, Xipeng Qiu (2024), licensed CC BY. This document is the EMNLP 2024 paper “Unified Active Retrieval for Retrieval Augmented Generation,” by Qinyuan Cheng, Xiaonan Li, Shimin Li, Qin Zhu, Zhangyue Yin, Yunfan Shao, Linyang Li, Tianxiang Sun, Hang Yan, and Xipeng Qiu, affiliated with Fudan University, Shanghai AI Laboratory, and Shanghai Collaborative Innovation Center of Intelligent Visual Computing. It addresses when Retrieval-Augmented Generation should retrieve external documents rather than relying on a large language model alone. Retrieval can improve factual reliability, but unnecessary or poor-quality retrieval may increase cost, interfere with responses, or harm creativity. The paper introduces Unified Active Retrieval (UAR), a plug-and-play framework that freezes the underlying large language model and uses lightweight single-layer MLP classifiers over its final input-token hidden state. UAR makes retrieval decisions through four orthogonal criteria: retrieval intent, knowledge intensity, time sensitivity, and model familiarity or self-awareness. Its Unified Active Retrieval Criteria (UAR-Criteria) organizes these judgments in a prioritized decision tree. If retrieval is needed, retrieved documents are appended to the instruction; otherwise, the original generation format is preserved. After reviewing prior active retrieval, temporal reasoning, mutable-fact, and self-awareness work, the paper evaluates UAR on the new Active Retrieval Benchmark (AR-Bench) and six downstream datasets with 7B and 13B Llama2 models. UAR exceeds FLARE, Self-RAG, and SKR in retrieval-timing accuracy across intent-aware, knowledge-aware, time-aware, and self-aware cases. It also achieves the strongest overall downstream results while retrieving selectively, notably for time-sensitive TAQA and FreshQA questions and unfamiliar TriviaQA and WebQuestions facts. Further analysis finds self-aware retrieval decisions most difficult. Fine-tuning the whole model yields only slight self-awareness gains over value-head classification while increasing latency and parameter requirements. Results also vary with the number of reference documents and with retrieval frequency. The paper concludes that multifaceted, low-cost retrieval timing can avoid needless or harmful augmentation while preserving model capabilities. Limitations include a focus on short-text, mainly knowledge-question-answering settings; only one retrieval call; potentially incomplete criteria; and an unexplored deeper classifier design. The work was supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China and Fudan University’s CFFF platform.

Unified Active Retrieval for Retrieval Augmented Generation front matter

“Unified Active Retrieval for Retrieval Augmented Generation” is a work concerning Retrieval Augmented Generation. It is authored by Qinyuan Cheng, Xiaonan Li, Shimin Li, Qin Zhu, Zhangyue Yin, Yunfan Shao, Linyang Li, Tianxiang Sun, Hang Yan, and Xipeng Qiu. The authors are affiliated with Fudan University, Shanghai AI Laboratory, and Shanghai Collaborative Innovation Center of Intelligent Visual Computing.

Abstract body

The unit concerns Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and the problem of deciding when retrieval is useful. It introduces Unified Active Retrieval (UAR), which uses four orthogonal criteria as plug-and-play classification tasks to make multifaceted retrieval timing judgements with negligible extra inference cost. It also introduces the Unified Active Retrieval Criteria (UAR-Criteria) to handle diverse active retrieval scenarios through a standardized procedure. Experiments on four representative types of user instructions show that UAR improves retrieval timing judgement and the performance of downstream tasks over existing methods.

Introduction body

The unit concerns large language models (LLMs), hallucination, and how retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) can improve factual reliability without unnecessarily increasing cost or harming creativity. It introduces Unified Active Retrieval (UAR), which uses intent-, knowledge-, time-sensitivity-, and self-awareness criteria to decide when retrieval is needed. UAR-Criteria combines these criteria into a prioritized decision tree, while lightweight classifiers make the decisions using the last-layer hidden states of a fixed LLM. Experiments on the new Active Retrieval benchmark, AR-Bench, and downstream tasks show that UAR improves retrieval-timing accuracy and enables more efficient RAG.

2 / 2.1 .. 2.3 body

The unit reviews active retrieval, time-awareness, and self-awareness in large language models. It explains that active retrieval can preserve model versatility, reduce retrievals, and avoid low-quality retrieval interference, while noting that existing methods often address only single scenarios. It describes TimeQA and MULAN as benchmarks for temporal reasoning and mutable facts, alongside work on aligning model knowledge to target years. It also summarizes findings that language models can assess and express what they know or do not know, with training methods improving this self-awareness.

3 .. 3.3 body

The unit presents Unified Active Retrieval (UAR), a plug-and-play active retrieval framework that freezes the large language model’s parameters and trains lightweight classifiers for different retrieval criteria. UAR evaluates user instructions for retrieval intent, knowledge intensity, time sensitivity, and model familiarity, then invokes retrieval only when appropriate. It appends retrieved documents to instructions requiring retrieval and otherwise preserves the model’s original generation format. The framework uses single-layer MLP classifiers over the final input-token hidden state, adding negligible computational cost.

4 / 4.1 .. 4.7 body

The unit evaluates UAR, an active retrieval method, on the Active Retrieval Benchmark and six downstream datasets using 7B and 13B Llama2 models. UAR substantially outperforms FLARE, Self-RAG, and SKR on retrieval-timing accuracy across intent-aware, knowledge-aware, time-aware, and self-aware scenarios. On downstream tasks, UAR achieves the best overall results while selectively invoking retrieval, especially for time-sensitive questions in TAQA and FreshQA and unknown facts in TriviaQA and WebQuestions. The experiments indicate that multifaceted retrieval decisions can avoid unnecessary or harmful retrieval augmentation while preserving model capabilities.

5 / 5.1 .. 5.3 body

This unit analyzes UAR against single classifiers, compares value-head classification with fine-tuning the whole large language model, and studies the effect of reference document number. The self-aware scenario is the most difficult, with the self-aware classifier achieving the lowest single-classifier accuracy and limiting UAR performance. Fine-tuning the whole large language model improves self-aware classification only slightly over a value head while increasing inference latency and parameter requirements. On TriviaQA and WebQuestions, UAR changes retrieval frequency and performs differently from always-retrieval and never-retrieval methods as the number of reference documents varies.

6 body

The paper introduces UAR, a unified active retrieval framework for retrieval-augmented generation. UAR combines four orthogonal criteria in plug-and-play classification tasks to judge retrieval timing with minimal inference cost and no loss of model capabilities. The paper also introduces UAR-Criteria and curates the Active Retrieval Benchmark (AR-Bench) for evaluating retrieval timing across scenarios. Experiments show that UAR outperforms existing methods on AR-Bench and downstream tasks, benefiting downstream applications.

Limitations body

The limitations concern experiments on short-text generation, especially knowledge-based question answering, using only a single retrieval call. The work does not yet implement multiple active retrieval calls within longer text responses. Its active retrieval criteria are based mainly on practical application experience and may miss some active retrieval scenarios. The classifier uses a single-layer MLP network, and the potential benefits of a deeper network remain unexplored.
