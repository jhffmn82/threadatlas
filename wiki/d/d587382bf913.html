This February 23, 2024 research article, “Merging Mixture of Experts and Retrieval Augmented Generation for Enhanced Information Retrieval and Reasoning,” is by Xingyu Xiong and Mingliang Zheng of the Xiamen Artificial Intelligence Research Institute. It studies whether combining Mixture of Experts (MoE) with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) can improve Large Language Model information retrieval and reasoning. The work uses Mistral 8x7B, an open-source model with a scalable MoE architecture, and evaluates it on Google BIG-Bench. The article begins by identifying shortcomings of Large Language Models in complex retrieval and reasoning: factual errors, outdated knowledge, weak logical deduction, and difficulty using structured knowledge bases. Its related-work discussion presents MoE as a way to distribute work among specialized sub-models through routing, potentially improving efficiency, adaptability, domain performance, and updating. It presents RAG as retrieval plus generation, allowing models to use external, current information and potentially improve factual accuracy, relevance, specialized question answering, and reliability. The methodology integrates both components into Mistral. Query features are extracted, a gating network dynamically routes work and weights specialized expert outputs, relevant documents are retrieved, and retrieved information is incorporated into the generated response. The authors use accuracy, precision, recall, F1 score, qualitative assessment, and task-specific measures for evaluation. Implementation includes data preprocessing, iterative training, and efforts to address expert-gating, retrieval, and computational-efficiency challenges. Results show that Mistral+MoE+RAG outperforms both baseline Mistral and Mistral+MoE on accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score. Qualitatively, it produces more detailed, relevant, contextually rich, accurate, and nuanced responses. The discussion frames the combined approach as useful for AI application development and research, improving retrieval, reasoning, contextual richness, and factual accuracy. The article closes by recognizing limits: reliance on Google BIG-Bench and the computational complexity of the integrated model. It proposes future work on scalability, efficient retrieval, and broader language and cultural coverage.
