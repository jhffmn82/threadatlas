Abstract written by a model

"Retrieval augmented generation for large language models in healthcare: A review" is a PRISMA-based systematic review of how retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is used to address large language model hallucinations and improve access to current, evidence-grounded medical information. Lameck Mbangula Amugongo, Pietro Mascheroni, Jan Seidel, Steven Brooks, and Stefan Doering review English-language full-text studies published from January 2020 to February 2025, searching Google Scholar and PubMed. From 2,139 records, they include 70 studies and extract information on models, embeddings, retrieval methods, outcomes, and ethical concerns. The review first maps datasets, largely for question answering and information retrieval and commonly drawn from the Web, PubMed, and UMLS; most are English or Chinese, limiting representativeness. It then explains RAG’s indexing, retrieval, and generation workflow and compares Naive RAG with Advanced RAG, which improves retrieval and context processing, and Modular RAG, which combines components such as query reformulation, knowledge graphs, fine-tuning, and LLM readers. Applications include medical question answering, clinical phenotyping, radiology support, trial screening, biomedical dialogue, disease prediction, and personalized care-related uses. GPT-3.5/4 and OpenAI embeddings are prominent, alongside work on knowledge graphs, multiple retrievers, embedding fine-tuning, prompting, and embeddings-free retrieval. The review next finds that evaluation commonly considers correctness, completeness, faithfulness, relevance, and fluency, using tools including RAGAs, continuous-eval, TrueLens, and DeepEval, but lacks a harmonized healthcare-specific framework that evaluates both retrieval and generation. Finally, it emphasizes that RAG can reduce hallucination and memorized-data risks but cannot eliminate incorrect answers, bias, privacy leakage, or safety concerns. It calls for diverse benchmarks, stronger retrieval and evidence grounding, scalable standardized evaluation, privacy protection, transparency, accountability, bias mitigation, and human oversight. Supporting files provide collected-paper data, extracted data, and evaluation metrics.

Retrieval augmented generation for large language models in healthcare: A systematic review front matter

Abstract body

The review, titled "Retrieval augmented generation for large language models in healthcare: A review," examines how retrieval augmented generation (RAG) is used with Large Language Models (LLMs) in healthcare. It assesses datasets, RAG methodologies, retrieval, augmentation, generation, and evaluation frameworks, finding that most datasets are in English or Chinese and that Naive RAG, Advanced RAG, and Modular RAG are used. GPT-3.5/4 are the most frequently used models in the reviewed healthcare applications. The review identifies a lack of standardized evaluation frameworks and limited attention to ethical considerations, highlighting the need for responsible adoption of RAG in healthcare.

Introduction body

The unit concerns the use of Large Language Models (LLMs) in healthcare and their limitations, especially the generation of inaccurate or nonsensical information known as model hallucinations. It describes Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), which combines LLMs with external knowledge sources to improve factuality, transparency, and access to current medical information. The review examines RAG methodologies, architectures, evaluation frameworks, ethical concerns, and applications in healthcare. It also identifies future research directions and open issues in current RAG implementations.

Methods .. Exclusion criteria body

The RAG in healthcare review examines retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) techniques used with large language models (LLMs) in the medical domain. It uses a systematic and transparent methodology based on the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA), covering English-language articles published from January 2020 through February 2025. The review searched Google Scholar and PubMed, screened studies by title, abstract, and full text, and excluded reviews, papers without available full text, and short conference papers. For selected studies, multiple reviewers extracted information about models, embeddings, retrieval methods, outcomes, and ethical concerns.

Results / Included studies .. Datasets body

The review on retrieval-augmented generation for large language models in healthcare included 70 studies published between 2020 and 2025, selected from 2,139 records retrieved through Google Scholar and PubMed. It examines RAG approaches for deciding what information to retrieve, when to retrieve it, and how to use retrieved information to ground medical LLMs. The reviewed datasets mainly support question answering and information retrieval, drawing on sources such as the Web, PubMed, and UMLS. The unit describes medical dialogue, QA, retrieval, and benchmark datasets, including their domains, sizes, limitations, and uses in evaluating or improving medical LLMs.

RAG overview .. Advanced RAG body

The unit explains how retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) grounds large language models (LLMs) in external, up-to-date knowledge through indexing, retrieval, and generation. It distinguishes Naive RAG, which follows these three basic steps, from Advanced RAG, which adds pre-retrieval and post-retrieval processes to improve chunk quality, ranking, context integration, and answer reliability. Healthcare applications include medical question answering, clinical phenotyping, radiology decision support, clinical trial screening, and biomedical dialogue, with reported improvements in accuracy, factuality, consistency, and efficiency. The unit also describes the use of knowledge graphs, multiple retrievers, metadata, similarity thresholds, and context reordering to address retrieval noise, irrelevant information, and incomplete answers.

Modular RAG body

The unit describes Modular RAG as a customizable framework that combines retrieval, query reformulation, fine-tuning, knowledge graphs, LLM readers, and other modules to improve healthcare applications. Wang, Ma and Chen used hybrid retrieval, query augmentation, and an LLM reader with medical knowledge, while Jin et al. combined feature extraction, RAG, LlamaIndex, and XGBoost for disease prediction. Prompt-RAG used an LLM for direct document retrieval without vector embeddings and performed well on Korean medicine documents, although it was slower. The unit also discusses Hypothesis Knowledge Graph Enhanced, Clinfo.ai, and agentic and multi-agent systems as approaches for improving medical question answering and other complex medical tasks.

Evaluation metrics and frameworks body

This unit concerns the evaluation of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems, focusing on user prompts, context retrieval, and generated outputs. It describes metrics including accuracy/correctness, completeness, faithfulness/consistency, relevance, and fluency, and discusses frameworks such as Retrieval Augmented Generation Assessment (RAGAs), continuous-eval, TrueLens, and DeepEval. It also covers PubMedRS-200, a dataset of question-answer pairs derived from systematic reviews, and studies evaluating medical question answering and lay-summary generation with automatic and human-based measures. The unit concludes that RAG evaluation lacks harmonization and needs scalable methods that assess both document selection and summary generation, while adapting information-retrieval benchmarks to RAG.

Ethical considerations body

The unit examines ethical considerations surrounding retrieval augmented generation (RAG)-based large language models (LLMs) in healthcare, focusing on correctness, data leakage, privacy, safety, bias, and hallucination. It reports that RAG can ground LLM responses and reduce hallucinations and memorized-data risks, but it does not eliminate incorrect answers, bias, privacy risks, or safety concerns. Most reviewed studies assessed accuracy, while relatively few evaluated privacy, intentional or unintentional harms, or bias. The unit concludes that healthcare applications require robust privacy measures, transparency, accountability, bias mitigation, human oversight, and human autonomy, particularly because medical-purpose systems may face approval challenges under US and EU laws, including the EU AI Act.

Data analysis body

The unit analyzes retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems and datasets used in healthcare and the medical domain. It reports that most common retrieval datasets are in English, while most healthcare studies use proprietary models, especially OpenAI’s GPT-3.5/4 models, and OpenAI’s text-embedding-ada-002 for embeddings. The text discusses privacy concerns around patient data, the trade-offs of open-source models, and evidence that GPT-4o-based RAG performs strongly with knowledge graph integration. It also reviews embedding fine-tuning, embeddings-free RAG, dense passage retrievers, pre-retrieval strategies, and prompting techniques.

Discussion body

The unit reviews recent advances, applications, evaluation practices, limitations, and future directions for retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) with large language models in healthcare. It reports that proprietary models, especially GPT models, dominate the reviewed studies, while English-language external knowledge datasets and inconsistent evaluation metrics limit representativeness and comparability. The discussion identifies retrieval quality, multi-hop reasoning, context-window constraints, hallucination, multimodal data integration, patient privacy, safety, and ethics as major challenges. It calls for diverse benchmarks, standardized healthcare-specific evaluations, stronger retrieval and evidence-grounding methods, and ethically developed systems.

Conclusion body

The paper reviews how RAG advances the grounding and capabilities of large language models in the medical domain. It examines healthcare datasets, models, retrieval and augmentation techniques, and evaluation approaches used in existing RAG studies. The results show growing interest in RAG for healthcare, frequent use of proprietary LLMs, and no standardized framework for evaluating medical RAG pipelines. RAG could support applications from drug development and disease prediction to personalized care management, but challenges involving information integration, noise, source factuality, and ethics remain.
