Retrieval Augmented Generation or Long-Context LLMs?

Zhuowan Li · 2024-11-12/2024-11-16 · 17 sections
"Retrieval Augmented Generation or Long-Context LLMs? A Comprehensive Study and Hybrid Approach" by Zhuowan Li, Cheng Li, Mingyang Zhang, Qiaozhu Mei, Michael Bendersky (2024), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-industry.66

This document is a study comparing retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) with long-context (LC) prompting for large language models processing lengthy inputs. It examines the trade-off between answer quality and computational cost, while also noting a visiting researcher associated with Google DeepMind. RAG retrieves query-relevant external information and provides it to an LLM, making it comparatively easy to set up, potentially host on the customer side, and use at low cost, though retrieval itself can add cost. LC models such as Gemini-1.5, GPT-4, and Claude-3 can handle much larger contexts, Gemini 1.5 is cited at up to 1 million tokens, but long-context prompting is expensive because transformer computation grows quadratically with input length. The study benchmarks RAG and LC on nine LongBench and ∞Bench datasets, using Gemini-1.5-Pro, GPT-4O, and GPT-3.5-Turbo, alongside Contriever and Dragon retrievers. LongBench contexts average 7k words and ∞Bench contexts average 100k tokens; tasks are scored with F1, accuracy, or ROUGE. In contrast with earlier findings that LC models lagged behind RAG, the reported results show that sufficiently resourced LC models generally achieve higher average performance and better long-context understanding than RAG. Analyses address retrieval failures, retriever choice, synthetic-data artifacts, and attempts to exclude models’ internal knowledge. Dragon-retriever and non-commonsense-subset results broadly support the main trend, while internal knowledge differs by model: on MuSiQue, GPT-3.5-Turbo scores 14.53 and Gemini-1.5-Pro 23.58 using only internal knowledge. To combine LC quality with RAG efficiency, the authors propose SELF-ROUTE. Through model self-reflection, it sends a query to RAG when retrieved context appears sufficient and otherwise uses LC processing. SELF-ROUTE approaches LC performance while using fewer tokens and lowering cost, reported as a 65% reduction for Gemini-1.5-Pro and 39% for GPT-4O. The document concludes that RAG remains valuable for lower-cost use and inputs beyond a model’s context window, while dynamic routing offers a practical route to LC-comparable performance at substantially reduced computation cost. The authors acknowledge Weize Kong, Tao Chen, Jeffrey Dudek, Spurthi Amba Hombaiah, and anonymous reviewers.

opening .. Exclusion of LLM’s internal knowledge body

The unit compares RAG, LC, and SELF-ROUTE across Gemini-1.5-Pro, GPT-4O, and GPT-3.5-Turbo on LongBench datasets, finding that LC generally outperforms RAG while SELF-ROUTE approaches LC performance with fewer tokens. SELF-ROUTE routes queries to RAG when the retrieved context appears sufficient and sends other queries to LC, reducing token usage while preserving accuracy. The analysis examines retrieval failures, retriever variation, synthetic-data artifacts, and methods for limiting models’ use of internal knowledge. Results using the Dragon retriever and non-commonsense subsets broadly support the reported trends.

conclusion body

The paper compares RAG and LC in terms of performance and computational cost. LC performs better in long-context understanding, while RAG remains useful because it costs less and handles inputs that exceed the model’s context window. The proposed dynamic routing method uses model self-reflection to select between RAG and LC, achieving performance comparable to LC at significantly lower cost. The findings inform practical applications of long-context LLMs and future optimization of RAG techniques.

notes notes

Different models may learn different internal knowledge, which can lead to different numbers of non-commonsense questions. GPT-3.5-Turbo achieves a performance of 14.53 on MuSiQue using only internal knowledge. Gemini-1.5-Pro achieves a performance of 23.58 on MuSiQue under the same condition.
