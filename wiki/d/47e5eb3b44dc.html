Abstract written by a model

“Ask in Any Modality: A Comprehensive Survey on Multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation,” by Mohammad Mahdi Abootorabi and coauthors, published in Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: ACL 2025, surveys Multimodal RAG: systems that retrieve and generate across text, images, audio, video, and documents. It begins from limitations of large language models, hallucinations, outdated knowledge, and unverifiable reasoning, and frames RAG as grounding generation in external knowledge. Multimodal RAG extends this pipeline to heterogeneous modalities, raising problems of modality selection, fusion, and cross-modal relevance. The survey then develops a taxonomy of the field. Retrieval covers multimodal encoders including CLIP, BLIP, MARVEL, and Uni-IR; similarity search; modality-specific and document retrieval; and re-ranking, filtering, and evidence selection. Fusion methods include score and alignment approaches, attention-based cross-modal interaction, and unified representations; MegaPairs illustrates combined CLIP-based and MLLM-based retrieval but adds inference complexity. Augmentation enriches retrieved context and uses adaptive or iterative retrieval, with systems such as EMERGE, MiRAG, Video-RAG, Img2Loc, UniversalRAG, SKURG, OmniSearch, OMGM, IRAMIG, OMG-QA, and RAGAR. Generation methods encompass in-context learning, reasoning and verification, instruction tuning, citation and attribution, and agentic interaction. Training is described as pretraining on paired data followed by task-specific fine-tuning. REVEAL combines Prefix Language Modeling, contrastive, disentangled regularization, and alignment regularization losses before downstream cross-entropy training; InfoNCE and related contrastive methods support cross-modal alignment. The survey also reviews datasets, benchmarks, metrics, evaluation, applications, and agent-based scenarios. It concludes that major unresolved issues include generalization, explainability, attribution, robustness to adversarial inputs and poor or outdated sources, modality bias, alignment, compositional reasoning, scalability, and retrieval quality. It emphasizes unified embedding spaces, knowledge graphs, feedback-driven retrieval, cross-modal reasoning, and interactive agents, including possible uses involving real-world sensory data, robotics, navigation, physics-informed reasoning, and embodied AI. The authors note that the review prioritizes recent major-venue and arXiv work, omits comparative performance evaluation because settings differ widely, and will be updated as the field develops. It uses public research and acknowledges concerns about bias, misinformation, privacy, and intellectual property.

front matter .. Abstract front matter

Mohammad Mahdi Abootorabi and coauthors present “Ask in Any Modality: A Comprehensive Survey on Multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation” in Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: ACL 2025. The survey examines datasets, benchmarks, metrics, evaluation, methodologies, and innovations in retrieval, fusion, augmentation, and generation for Multimodal RAG. It also reviews training strategies, robustness enhancements, loss functions, agent-based approaches, and diverse Multimodal RAG scenarios. The authors identify open challenges and future directions for developing more capable and reliable AI systems that use multimodal dynamic external knowledge bases.

Introduction & Background body

This unit explains how large language models have advanced while remaining vulnerable to hallucinations, outdated knowledge, and unverifiable reasoning. It describes Retrieval-Augmented Generation as a retriever-generator pipeline that grounds responses in external knowledge, and presents multimodal learning as a way to integrate text, images, audio, and video. It defines multimodal RAG as extending retrieval and generation across heterogeneous modalities, with challenges involving modality selection, fusion, and cross-modal relevance. The unit also introduces a mathematical formulation for multimodal RAG and outlines datasets, benchmarks, evaluation methods, applications, and the survey’s taxonomy and contributions.

Key Innovations and Methodologies / 3.1 body

This unit concerns retrieval strategies for multimodal RAG systems, including efficient similarity search, modality-specific retrieval, document retrieval, and re-ranking. It describes multimodal encoders such as CLIP, BLIP, MARVEL, and Uni-IR, as well as maximum inner product search, adaptive quantization, hybrid sparse–dense retrieval, and distributed approximate nearest-neighbor methods. It surveys text-, vision-, video-, audio-, and document-centric retrieval techniques, including systems that use layout, temporal, acoustic, or lexical information. It also explains how re-ranking, relevance scoring, filtering, and optimized example selection improve the quality and robustness of retrieved evidence.

3.2 body

The unit categorizes multimodal fusion methods into Score Fusion and Alignment Models, Attention-Based Mechanisms, and Unified Frameworks and Projections. Score Fusion and Alignment Models use shared embedding spaces, score fusion, feature fusion, and projection strategies to align modalities, with MegaPairs combining CLIP-based and MLLM-based retrieval at the cost of increased inference complexity. Attention-Based Mechanisms dynamically regulate cross-modal interactions through cross-attention, co-attention, memory retrieval, and related strategies, although some require task-specific supervision or substantial computation. Unified Frameworks and Projections consolidate multimodal inputs through unified tensors, joint-grained retrieval, captioning, shared decoders, and dense-to-sparse projection.

3.3 body

Augmentation techniques refine retrieved data before generation to improve multimodal interpretation, structuring, and integration. Context Enrichment expands or refines retrieved knowledge with additional contextual elements, as shown by EMERGE, MiRAG, Video-RAG, and Img2Loc. Adaptive and Iterative Retrieval dynamically adjusts retrieval based on query complexity, modality, granularity, relevance, and prior feedback. Systems including UniversalRAG, SKURG, OmniSearch, OMGM, IRAMIG, OMG-QA, and RAGAR use these strategies to improve retrieval precision and contextual consistency.

3.4 body

This unit reviews generation techniques for multimodal RAG, including in-context learning, reasoning, instruction tuning, source attribution, and agentic interaction. In-context learning methods use retrieved multimodal content or examples to improve reasoning without retraining, while reasoning methods apply chain-of-thought, evidence curation, verification, and compositional strategies. Instruction-tuning approaches train or preference-tune models to improve visual understanding, retrieval decisions, ranking, and resistance to overreliance on retrieved context. The unit also discusses evidence citation and attribution limitations, as well as agent-driven systems for user interaction, specialized applications, and hierarchical multimodal reasoning.

3.5 body

Training Strategies for multimodal RAG models use multiple stages to capture cross-modal interactions. Pretraining on paired datasets establishes cross-modal relationships, while fine-tuning adapts models to task-specific objectives. REVEAL combines Prefix Language Modeling Loss with contrastive, disentangled regularization, and alignment regularization losses before using cross-entropy for downstream tasks. Contrastive learning methods, including InfoNCE loss and several refinements, improve multimodal retrieval and generation by aligning positives and separating negatives.

Open Problems and Future Directions body

The unit identifies open challenges for multimodal RAG systems in generalization, explainability, robustness, reasoning, alignment, retrieval, and agent-based operation. It reports that multimodal RAG systems can exhibit domain-adaptation difficulties, modality biases, weak attribution, vulnerability to adversarial perturbations, and degradation from low-quality or outdated sources. It highlights compositional reasoning, modality alignment, knowledge graphs, unified embedding spaces, and feedback-driven retrieval as important research directions. It also connects agent-based multimodal RAGs, real-world sensory data, and embodied AI with future applications in robotics, navigation, physics-informed reasoning, and AGI.

Conclusion body

The study provides a comprehensive review of multimodal RAG. It categorizes advancements in retrieval, multimodal fusion, augmentation, generation, training strategies, and agents, and examines applications, datasets, benchmarks, and evaluation methods. It highlights open challenges and promising future directions. The study particularly encourages work on cross-modal reasoning and retrieval, agent-based interactive systems, and unified multimodal embedding spaces.

Limitations body

The study comprehensively examines multimodal RAG systems, with extended discussions, dataset and benchmark details, and additional relevant work provided in the Appendices. Its selection focuses primarily on recent research from major venues and arXiv, but may overlook emerging or domain-specific contributions. It does not include a comparative performance evaluation because task definitions, metrics, implementation details, and computational requirements vary substantially. The study identifies open questions in multimodal RAG, including multimodal fusion and scalability, and plans to update the survey and repository as the field develops.

Ethical Statement body

The survey reviews research on multimodal RAG systems and offers insights for researchers in the field. It analyzes publicly available studies, datasets, and benchmarks, with only a small number of papers requiring institutional access. The work involves no personal data or user interactions and introduces no new models, datasets, or experimental methodologies. It acknowledges ethical concerns involving bias, misinformation, privacy, and intellectual property in multimodal RAG systems.
