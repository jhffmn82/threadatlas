TRACE the Evidence: Constructing Knowledge-Grounded Reasoning Chains for Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Jinyuan Fang · 2024 · 21 sections
"TRACE the Evidence: Constructing Knowledge-Grounded Reasoning Chains for Retrieval-Augmented Generation" by Jinyuan Fang, Zaiqiao Meng, Craig Macdonald (2024), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.findings-emnlp.496

Abstract written by a model

“TRACE the Evidence: Constructing Knowledge-Grounded Reasoning Chains for Retrieval-Augmented Generation,” by Jinyuan Fang, Zaiqiao Meng, and Craig Macdonald of the University of Glasgow, is an EMNLP 2024 Findings paper on improving retrieval-augmented generation for multi-hop question answering. It addresses the problem that retrieved Wikipedia document sets contain substantial irrelevant or noisy material. Given a question and retrieved documents, TRACE first uses a Knowledge Graph Generator to turn documents into knowledge triples, then uses a triple ranker and LLM-based autoregressive selector to construct logically connected reasoning chains. These chains either become the answer generator’s context (TRACE-Triple) or identify and order a smaller set of useful original documents (TRACE-Doc). Beam search, multiple candidate chains, and adaptive chain termination support the process. The paper evaluates TRACE on HotPotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and MuSiQue, whose questions require two to four reasoning hops. TRACE-Triple and TRACE-Doc outperform naive RAG, document-ranking methods, and IRCoT; compared with using all retrieved documents, they improve Exact Match by average margins of 14.03% and 13.46%, respectively. TRACE-Triple often attains strong results with substantially fewer context tokens, while TRACE-Doc achieves the best reported performance among listed systems with Mistral-7B-v0.1. Ablations find that the KG generator, chain constructor, triple ranker, triple selector, demonstrations, suitable chain counts, and adaptive termination all matter. Adaptive chain lengths outperform fixed four-step chains by avoiding redundant triples. Further analyses report that HotPotQA graphs average 83.21 entities and 79.06 triples, while chains average about three steps and identify about 2.6 documents; these selected documents have much lower error rates than the original retrieved sets. Case studies show that multiple chains can provide complementary evidence and avoid noisy documents. TRACE has higher indexing latency than IRCoT because it generates triples, but comparable or lower inference runtime because its selector uses a single token rather than generating multi-token thoughts. The paper concludes that grounded reasoning chains generalize across reader models, while noting limits: Wikipedia-focused retrieval, unavailable annotations for directly evaluating triples and chains, title-to-text-entity-focused triples, and a black-box LLM setting that may yield only one chain without selector logits.

front matter .. TRACE the Evidence: Constructing Knowledge-Grounded Reasoning Chains for Retrieval-Augmented Generation front matter

The unit identifies the paper “TRACE the Evidence: Constructing Knowledge-Grounded Reasoning Chains for Retrieval-Augmented Generation.” It was written by Jinyuan Fang, Zaiqiao Meng, and Craig Macdonald of the University of Glasgow. The paper appears in Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EMNLP 2024, pages 8472–8494, and concerns constructing knowledge-grounded reasoning chains for retrieval-augmented generation.

Abstract body

The unit concerns TRACE, a retrieval-augmented generation approach for improving multi-hop question answering. It addresses noise caused by imperfect retrievers by constructing knowledge-grounded reasoning chains from retrieved documents. TRACE uses a Knowledge Graph Generator to create a knowledge graph and an Autoregressive Reasoning Chain Constructor to build logically connected chains of knowledge triples. On three multi-hop QA datasets, TRACE improved average performance by up to 14.03% compared with using all retrieved documents, and reasoning chains were often sufficient as context for correct answers.

Introduction body

The unit concerns retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) models and TRACE, a method for improving their performance on multi-hop question answering (QA). TRACE converts retrieved documents into a knowledge graph (KG) of knowledge triples and autoregressively constructs reasoning chains to identify and integrate supporting evidence. The method provides these chains directly as context or uses them to select useful documents before answer generation. Experiments on three multi-hop QA datasets found that TRACE-Triple and TRACE-Doc improved Exact Match over using all retrieved documents by average margins of 14.03% and 13.46%, respectively.

Problem Formulation body

This unit concerns multi-hop questions and their answers, denoted as q and a. Each question is associated with a document set Dq containing N documents obtained using a retriever model. The documents are often retrieved from Wikipedia and comprise a title and text. Given the question and document set, the goal is to correctly generate the answer.

TRACE / Overall Framework .. Answer Generation body

TRACE is a framework for answering multi-hop questions from retrieved documents. It generates a knowledge graph from the documents, constructs reasoning chains from graph triples using a triple ranker and an LLM-based triple selector, and then generates an answer. Its answer generation either uses the reasoning chains directly or retrieves and orders the original documents associated with the triples. The framework includes adaptive chain termination and beam search to produce multiple candidate reasoning chains.

Experiments / Experimental Setup .. Results and Analysis body

The experiments evaluate TRACE on HotPotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and MuSiQue, three multi-hop question-answering datasets requiring two to four reasoning hops. TRACE constructs reasoning chains from knowledge-graph triples or documents and is compared with naive RAG, document-ranking baselines, and IRCoT. TRACE-Triple and TRACE-Doc outperform the baselines across the datasets, with TRACE-Triple using substantially fewer context tokens while achieving the best performance on most metrics. Ablation studies show that the knowledge-graph generator, reasoning-chain constructor, triple ranker, and triple selector all contribute to performance.

Related Work body

RAG models use retriever-reader pipelines to retrieve relevant information and generate answers, and they have recently been applied to multi-hop questions. The text contrasts TRACE with IR-CoT, noting that TRACE grounds its reasoning chains in retrieved documents rather than relying on potentially inaccurate generated CoT sentences. TRACE uses in-context learning to reduce the effects of irrelevant documents and creates a knowledge graph from retrieved documents for multi-hop reasoning without additional training. Unlike GraphRAG, which generates summaries for open-ended global questions, TRACE addresses multi-hop QA questions with clear answers.

Conclusion body

The paper proposes TRACE to improve the multi-hop reasoning ability of RAG models. TRACE uses a knowledge graph generator to create a knowledge graph from retrieved documents and an autoregressive reasoning chain constructor to build reasoning chains from it. It uses these reasoning chains either as context for answer generation or to identify a subset of relevant documents. On three multi-hop QA datasets, TRACE achieves an average EM improvement of up to 14.03% compared with using all retrieved documents, and its reasoning chains generalize across reader models.

Limitations body

The work identifies limitations concerning its focus on documents retrieved from Wikipedia, the evaluation of generated KG triples and reasoning chains, and the use of TRACE with a black-box LLM. The KG generator primarily creates triples between document titles and entities within the texts rather than between all possible entities. Because annotated data is unavailable, the work evaluates these components through final QA performance, while TRACE may produce only a single reasoning chain when it cannot access triple-selector logits.

A appendix

The unit concerns the algorithm for constructing R reasoning chains from documents Dq to answer question q. Algorithm 1 describes this construction process, while omitting the adaptive chain termination strategy for clarity. Without that strategy, the computational complexity is O(N + LR), where N is the number of documents, L is the reasoning-chain length, and R is the number of reasoning chains or relevant documents as stated in the text. The LR term generally dominates N when L and R are relatively large, although adaptive termination can further improve actual efficiency.

Prompts / Prompt for Generating KG Triples .. Prompt for Generating Answers / Demonstration Data / Experimental Details appendix

This unit describes the prompts and experimental details used in TRACE. The KG generator produces KG triples from each document, while the triple selector selects triples to construct reasoning chains and the reader generates answers from contexts. Algorithm 1 formalizes knowledge triple-grounded reasoning-chain construction using retrieved documents, a triple scorer, and iterative selection. The unit also lists retrieval and reranking models and notes that 20 demonstrations were manually labeled for each dataset.

Datasets appendix

TRACE uses the HotPotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and MuSiQue multi-hop question answering datasets. Each dataset supplies Wikipedia documents containing relevant and irrelevant material, along with annotations identifying the relevant documents. TRACE uses these documents as inputs to identify supporting evidence and perform multi-hop reasoning to generate answers. Because the test sets are unavailable, performance is reported on the original development sets, while 500 training questions from each dataset are sampled for development-set hyperparameter tuning.

Baselines appendix

The unit describes baselines for evaluating TRACE's ability to identify supporting evidence, including document retrieval methods and reader configurations. It presents prompts for generating knowledge triples, selecting triples that form reasoning paths, and answering questions from contexts. It also reports dataset statistics and ablation results for TRACE-Doc on HotPotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and MuSiQue. Retrieval baselines include vanilla RAG, bi-encoders, cross-encoders, and IRCoT.

Statistics and Analyses of the Generated appendix

The unit reports statistics and qualitative analyses of generated knowledge graphs and reasoning chains across experimental datasets, including HotPotQA. In the HotPotQA test set, the generated knowledge graphs average 83.21 entities, 79.06 triples, and 1.20% density, while reasoning chains average about three steps and identify about 2.6 documents from Dq. These identified documents have substantially lower error rates than the original documents in Dq, such as 21.06% versus 79.66% for HotPotQA. The results support TRACE-Doc's advantage over the vanilla RAG model, although the generated knowledge graphs and reasoning chains cannot be quantitatively evaluated because annotated data is unavailable.

Additional Implementation Details appendix

The experiments are conducted in a zero-shot setting, with context such as reasoning chains or documents prepended to questions for answer generation by the reader. Demonstrations for the KG generator come from 50 manually labelled training documents per dataset, while demonstrations for the reasoning chain constructor come from 20 manually labelled training questions per dataset. E5-Mistral retrieves the top three similar labelled documents or questions as demonstrations. The experiments use a maximum reasoning chain length of 4, five reasoning chains, five beams, and select the number of candidate triples from 15, 20, 25, or 30 using the development set.

Additional Experimental Results and .. MuSiQue appendix

This unit reports additional experimental results and analyses for TRACE, including evaluations with Mistral-7B-v0.1 and Gemma-7B readers, ablations of TRACE-Doc, and studies of maximum chain length, demonstrations, chain count, adaptive termination, case studies, and efficiency. Using Mistral-7B-v0.1 as the reader, TRACE-Doc achieves the best reported performance among the listed systems on HotPotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and MuSiQue, while TRACE-Triple also outperforms IRCoT. The results indicate that TRACE's reasoning chains generalize across readers and that TRACE-Doc's components are important for retrieving relevant documents. On MuSiQue, the results further show that an appropriate maximum chain length and adaptive chain termination are important for performance.

Chain Constructor .. Chains R appendix

This unit examines TRACE, especially its reasoning chain constructor, demonstrations, and the number of reasoning chains used. Table 9 shows that removing demonstrations or using fixed demonstrations generally reduces TRACE-Triple performance, while adding more than the default three demonstrations does not consistently help. Figure 10 shows that increasing the number of reasoning chains initially improves TRACE-Triple and TRACE-Doc performance on HotPotQA and 2WikiMultiHopQA before performance stabilizes. Table 8 reports that TRACE-Triple and TRACE-Doc outperform the listed baselines across HotPotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and MuSiQue under the reported evaluation settings.

Effects of Adaptive Chain Termination appendix

The unit concerns TRACE and its adaptive chain termination strategy for determining the optimal lengths of reasoning chains. An ablation variant called Fixed-Length Reasoning Chains sets all chain lengths to a fixed value of 4 and is compared with adaptive-length TRACE-Triple and TRACE-Doc on three multi-hop QA datasets. Adaptive-length reasoning chains significantly outperform the fixed-length variant because they avoid unnecessary and redundant triples.

Case Study appendix

The Case Study reports results in Table 14, which presents the top-five reasoning chains and the relevant documents identified through them. It finds that generating multiple reasoning chains enriches the context by adding complementary information, as illustrated by the question about the birth date of a Spanish footballer added as a holding midfielder in the 2021-13 FC Bayern Munich season. It also finds that reasoning chains effectively identify relevant supporting documents while avoiding noisy ones, as shown by examples involving Blaise Cendrars and Julian Barnes. These findings are consistent with empirical results in Appendix D.5.

Efficiency Analysis appendix

The unit evaluates TRACE against IRCoT, reporting indexing and inference runtimes on HotPotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and MuSiQue using the same LLaMA3-7B model. TRACE has higher indexing latency because it generates knowledge-graph triples, but its inference runtime is similar to or lower than IRCoT because it selects triples with a single token rather than autoregressively generating multi-token thoughts. The unit also presents TRACE reasoning-chain examples involving The Wittenburg Door, Sports Collectors Digest, A Girl’s Gotta Do (What a Girl’s Gotta Do), and Mindy McCready. Additional examples show generated knowledge triples and reasoning chains for entities including Ellen Glasgow, Julian Barnes, Blaise Cendrars, Christina Stead, Nuruddin Farah, and Javi Martínez.
