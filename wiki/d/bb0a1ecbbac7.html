Retrieval-augmented Generation across Heterogeneous Knowledge

Wenhao Yu · 2022 · 9 sections
"Retrieval-augmented Generation across Heterogeneous Knowledge" by Wenhao Yu (2022), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2022.naacl-srw.7

This unit explains retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) for NLP and argues that RAG should retrieve across heterogeneous knowledge sources rather than depend mainly on one corpus such as Wikipedia. It begins with large pre-trained language models, including T5 and GPT-3, which store knowledge implicitly through pre-training and can be fine-tuned in a close-book setting. It then identifies their limitations: stale knowledge, limited interpretability, weak domain specificity, and costly pre-training. In the open-book setting, models instead receive resources such as encyclopedias and books. RAG combines a document retriever and a text generator. A retriever such as DPR ranks and selects top-K documents using query-document embedding relevance; a generator such as BART or T5 produces an output from the input and retrieved references. FiD independently encodes retrieved passages and attends across them, avoiding some sequential-concatenation limits. RAG can outperform larger PLMs on open-domain QA while using fewer parameters because it explicitly retrieves supporting knowledge. The central problem is that retrieval from a single homogeneous source restricts coverage. The unit presents evidence that combining text, tables, knowledge-graph triples, encyclopedic knowledge, and commonsense knowledge improves open-domain QA and commonsense reasoning. It describes three approaches: converting diverse sources into text, multi-virtual-hops retrieval across sources, and enriching retrieved documents through structured-knowledge reasoning. Relevant systems and datasets include UniK-QA, DPR+FiD, NaturalQuestions, TriviaQA, HotpotQA, and WebQuestions. Abstract body

The unit concerns retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) methods and their use in NLP. It states that RAG methods achieve state-of-the-art performance on many NLP downstream tasks and offer easier knowledge acquisition, stronger scalability, and lower training costs than conventional pre-trained generation models. Existing RAG models have been applied to knowledge-intensive NLP tasks such as open-domain QA and dialogue systems, but most retrieve unstructured text documents from Wikipedia. The unit discusses obstacles in retrieving knowledge from a single-source homogeneous corpus and presents evidence and multiple solutions for retrieval-augmented generation across heterogeneous knowledge.

1 body

Large pre-trained language models (PLMs), including T5 and GPT-3, have transformed natural language processing and achieved strong results on various downstream tasks. Through pre-training, these models acquire substantial in-depth knowledge from their corpora. They can therefore predict downstream outputs without external memory or raw text, functioning as a parameterized implicit knowledge base. Fine-tuning PLMs using only target-data input-output pairs is called the close-book setting.

opening body

The unit concerns retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) as a way to address limitations of large-scale pre-trained language models (PLMs), including stale knowledge, limited interpretability, weak domain specificity, and costly pre-training. It explains that the open-book setting augments PLM inputs with open-world resources such as encyclopedias and books, and identifies RAG as a method combining PLMs with traditional information retrieval. RAG models are described as outperforming larger PLMs on several open-domain question answering tasks while using fewer parameters, because they explicitly retrieve knowledge and generate from references. The unit argues that reliance on a single homogeneous source such as Wikipedia limits coverage and motivates retrieval across heterogeneous knowledge.

2 body

RAG is a framework that predicts an output y from a source input x by using a document reference set Z and two components: a document retriever and a text generator. The document retriever, such as DPR, narrows millions of documents to the top-K documents using relevance scores based on query-document embeddings. The text generator, such as BART or T5, conditions on the input and retrieved documents to produce y. FiD processes retrieved passages independently in the encoder and performs attention over all of them, avoiding some limitations of sequential concatenation.

3 / 3.1 .. 3.3.3 body

This unit proposes grounding retrieval-augmented generation across heterogeneous knowledge sources rather than relying on a single homogeneous corpus such as Wikipedia. It presents evidence that combining text, tables, knowledge graph triples, encyclopedic knowledge, and commonsense knowledge can improve open-domain question answering and commonsense reasoning. Three solutions are described: homogenizing different sources into text, using multi-virtual hops retrieval across sources, and enriching retrieved documents through structured-knowledge reasoning. The discussion concerns systems and datasets including UniK-QA, DPR+FiD, NaturalQuestions, TriviaQA, HotpotQA, and WebQuestions.
