GRAG: Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Yuntong Hu, Zhihan Lei, Zheng Zhang, Bo Pan, Chen Ling, Liang Zhao · 2025 · 13 sections

Abstract written by a model

This document presents Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation (GRAG), by Yuntong Hu, Zhihan Lei, Zheng Zhang, Bo Pan, Chen Ling, and Liang Zhao. GRAG extends Retrieval-Augmented Generation for networked documents, where relevant evidence is not merely isolated text but interconnected textual graphs whose nodes and edges have text attributes. The paper argues that Large Language Models can use textual attributes but do not inherently capture complex topology, while exhaustive search for an optimal subgraph is NP-hard. GRAG frames the task as retrieving an optimal query-relevant textual subgraph from a graph and supplying it, with the query, to an LLM for generation. Its retrieval stage uses a divide-and-conquer process: it indexes K-hop ego-graphs with pre-trained language-model embeddings, ranks them by cosine similarity to the query, merges the top-N candidates, and applies Textual Subgraph Soft Pruning. Two multilayer perceptrons assign query-dependent scaling factors to nodes and edges, reducing redundant material and yielding a tailored subgraph with linear complexity. For generation, GRAG supplies complementary text and graph views. The text view turns retrieved ego-graphs into hierarchical descriptions that distinguish tree edges from additional edges. The graph view applies a Graph Neural Network to the softly pruned graph, and an MLP aligns graph embeddings with the LLM text-vector space. The resulting graph and text embeddings are concatenated with the query context for generation. Experiments on GraphQA, using WebQSP for knowledge-graph question answering and ExplaGraphs for common-sense reasoning, find that GRAG outperforms standard RAG retrievers and LLM baselines in graph multi-hop reasoning. Without LLM fine-tuning, it reaches 0.7236 Hit@1 on WebQSP and 0.9223 accuracy on ExplaGraphs; frozen LLMs with GRAG outperform fine-tuned LLMs on all reported tasks. Ablations emphasize the value of graph-aware retrieval, graph encoding, soft pruning, and hierarchical descriptions. Two-hop ego-graphs generally beat one-hop versions, but overly large subgraphs or too many retrieved entities can add irrelevant information and impair generation. The paper closes by noting that GRAG depends on effective initial node ranking and pruning, so performance can suffer when graph structure or node importance is hard to estimate.

front matter .. GRAG: Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation / Abstract front matter

Yuntong Hu, Zhihan Lei, Zheng Zhang, Bo Pan, Chen Ling, and Liang Zhao introduce Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation (GRAG). GRAG addresses the limitations of Naive Retrieval-Augmented Generation in handling networked documents by retrieving textual subgraphs and integrating textual and topological information into Large Language Models. The authors propose a divide-and-conquer strategy for efficient linear-time subgraph retrieval and use text and graph views for graph context-aware generation. Experiments on graph reasoning benchmarks show that GRAG significantly outperforms current state-of-the-art RAG methods in multi-hop reasoning on textual graphs.

Introduction body

This unit concerns how Large Language Models (LLMs) can use networked documents for retrieval and generation. It introduces Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation (GRAG), which extends Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) by incorporating graph context during both retrieval and generation. GRAG retrieves relevant textual subgraphs through ego-graph retrieval followed by graph soft pruning, and provides their information to LLMs through hierarchical text descriptions and graph-based soft prompts. Experiments on graph multi-hop reasoning benchmarks show that GRAG outperforms RAG-based retrievers and LLM baselines, with frozen LLMs using GRAG outperforming fine-tuned LLMs on all tasks.

This unit reviews prompt tuning, emphasizing soft prompts that adapt task-specific behavior while preserving model parameters and extensions to multi-modal contexts. 3 body

The unit formalizes Textual Graphs as graphs with text-attributed nodes and edges. It defines Textual Subgraphs as subgraph structures formed from subsets of a textual graph's nodes and edges. Graph Retrieval Augmented Generation integrates graph context into retrieval and generation to improve the relevance of generated content. Given a query over a textual graph G, GRAG aims to retrieve an optimal textual subgraph ˆg and provide it with the query to LLMθ for answer generation.

4 .. 4.1 body

This unit introduces GRAG, a method for textual subgraph retrieval and generation. GRAG uses a divide-and-conquer strategy to retrieve important ego-graphs, merge the top-N relevant ones, and apply soft pruning to reduce redundant nodes and edges. It represents textual graphs through complementary graph and text views to preserve both topology and textual information. For retrieval, it indexes K-hop ego-graphs with embeddings from a pre-trained language model, ranks them by cosine similarity to the query, and selects promising candidate subgraphs for further refinement.

opening body

The unit concerns retrieving and refining subgraphs from graph G according to their semantic relevance to a query. The subset SN(G) contains the N subgraphs with the highest relevance. Textual Subgraph Soft Pruning uses two Multilayer Perceptrons to assign scaling factors to nodes and edges based on their element-wise distance from the query. The masked subgraphs are merged into an optimal subgraph structure tailored to the query with linear complexity.

4.2 body

Textual Graph Augmented Generation provides large language models with two complementary views of a textual graph: a text view and a graph view. The text view converts retrieved ego-graphs into hierarchical descriptions by separating tree edges from additional edges, while the graph view uses a Graph Neural Network with relevance scaling factors to encode a soft pruned subgraph. An MLPϕ3 aligns graph embeddings with the large language model’s text-vector space, and the query plus hierarchical description are converted into text embeddings. Generation uses the concatenated graph and text embeddings conditioned on the retrieved subgraph and the query.

5 / 5.1 .. 5.4 body

The Experiments evaluate GRAG on the GraphQA benchmark, using WebQSP for knowledge graph question answering and ExplaGraphs for common-sense reasoning. GRAG outperforms standard RAG retrievers and LLM baselines, achieving WebQSP Hit@1 of 0.7236 and ExplaGraphs accuracy of 0.9223 without LLM fine-tuning. Results show that graph-aware retrieval, graph encoding, soft pruning, and textual graph descriptions are important for graph multi-hop reasoning. The study also finds that 2-hop ego-graphs generally outperform 1-hop ego-graphs, while retrieving too many subgraphs or using larger subgraphs can introduce irrelevant information and reduce performance.

6 body

The paper introduces Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation (GRAG) to extend Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) to graph-based scenarios. GRAG enhances Large Language Models (LLMs) by retrieving query-relevant textual subgraphs through a divide-and-conquer strategy using K-hop ego-graphs and soft pruning. It provides graph and text views of a textual graph, improving contextual understanding and performance, especially for detailed, multi-hop reasoning. The approach addresses the NP-hardness of exhaustive subgraph search, and a frozen LLM enhanced by GRAG can outperform fine-tuned LLMs at lower training cost.

7 body

This unit discusses a limitation of GRAG, a framework for graph context-aware generation. Its textual subgraph retrieval depends on the quality of the initial node ranking and pruning mechanism. When graph structure or node importance is difficult to estimate, retrieval performance may be suboptimal. The work was supported by multiple National Science Foundation and NIH grants.

A / A.1 .. A.4 appendix

This unit describes how a 2-hop ego-graph is converted into a hierarchical textual representation and compares RAG-based retrievers on WebQSP and ExplaGraphs. It reports retrieval results for BM25, MiniLM-L12-v2, LaBSE, mContriever-Base, and E5-Base, using Hit@1 on WebQSP and Accuracy on ExplaGraphs. It details implementation settings involving SentenceBert, GAT, Llama-2-7b-hf, and LoRA, and explains that GRAG uses graph topology and retrieved K-hop ego-graphs. The experiments find that increasing the number of retrieved entities generally helps until performance plateaus or declines because irrelevant information can impair generation.
