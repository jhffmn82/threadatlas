Part of The Sherlock Holmes Canon

The Return of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1903-09-26 · 44 sections

This document is a sequence of Sherlock Holmes cases narrated or accompanied by Dr. John Watson, following Holmes’s return after the Reichenbach Fall through investigations involving murder, fraud, abduction, blackmail, stolen property, and political danger. It begins with Holmes’s reappearance to Watson during the Park Lane Mystery: Holmes explains his escape from Professor Moriarty, uses a wax likeness as bait, and captures Colonel Sebastian Moran, who killed Ronald Adair with an air-gun. Holmes then clears John Hector McFarlane when Jonas Oldacre’s supposed murder proves a revenge scheme staged by Oldacre himself. The cases broaden across England and beyond London. Holmes deciphers threatening dancing-men messages, exposing Abe Slaney after Hilton Cubitt is killed and Elsie Cubitt wounded. He saves Violet Smith from Jack Woodley, Williamson, and a forced marriage plot connected to her inheritance, while recognizing Bob Carruthers’s partial effort to protect her. He finds Lord Saltire, exposes James Wilder and Reuben Hayes in the Duke of Holdernesse’s secret kidnapping scheme, and resolves Captain Peter Carey’s death by proving Patrick Cairns acted in self-defense, freeing John Hopley Neligan. Holmes and Watson also confront Charles Augustus Milverton, destroying his blackmail papers after an unnamed woman kills him; recover the black pearl of the Borgias from Napoleon busts smashed by Beppo; and uncover Gilchrist’s theft of examination papers, aided by Bannister’s loyalty to Gilchrist’s father. Holmes solves Willoughby Smith’s killing through Anna and Professor Coram’s hidden history, then finds Godfrey Staunton mourning his secretly married wife, promising discretion. The later cases stress Holmes’s judgment as much as detection. At Abbey Grange, he rejects a fabricated burglary narrative, accepts Captain Crocker’s account that he killed Sir Eustace Brackenstall while defending Mary Fraser, and informally acquits him. The document ends with its largest political threat: a stolen letter could provoke European war. Holmes links it to Eduardo Lucas’s murder and Lady Hilda Trelawney Hope’s blackmail, recovers the letter, secretly restores it to the despatch-box, and preserves both the state’s security and Lady Hilda’s secret.
