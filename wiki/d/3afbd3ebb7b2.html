The Patchwork Girl of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1913-07-01 · 31 sections

Abstract written by a model

The Patchwork Girl of Oz is an Oz adventure framed by the Royal Historian's account, sent from Oz by wireless telegraphy after Ozma had made the country invisible and cut off ordinary communication. It follows the young Munchkin Ojo, whose hunger and poverty lead him and his uncle, Unc Nunkie, to visit Dr. Pipt, the Crooked Magician. There they meet Dame Margolotte's unfinished patchwork servant. Ojo secretly adds every available quality, including Cleverness, to her brains; after Dr. Pipt uses his Powder of Life, she becomes the lively Patchwork Girl, Scraps. A spilled Liquid of Petrifaction turns Unc Nunkie and Margolotte into marble, and Ojo undertakes a quest for ingredients needed to restore them, accompanied first by Scraps and the Glass Cat, Bungle. The travelers gather the Woozy's tail hairs, survive dangerous plants with help from the Shaggy Man, and seek the Emerald City. Ojo secretly picks a forbidden six-leaved clover, is arrested, and learns that Oz's kindly prison system aims to help offenders reject wrongdoing. Ozma forgives him and authorizes the restoration effort, while Dorothy, the Scarecrow, Toto, and Scraps join his search for water from a dark well. Their travels take them through the Tottenhots, past the captive giant Mister Yoop, and among the one-legged Hoppers and horned Horners, whose threatened war ends when a misunderstood joke is resolved. In the Horner mine Ojo obtains the dark-well water. Crossing the Trick River into the Winkie Country, the party obtains oil from the Tin Woodman's knee. But the Tin Woodman refuses to sacrifice a yellow butterfly, leaving the charm incomplete. Back in the Emerald City, Ozma reveals that Glinda had foreseen Ojo's failure and arranged help. The Wizard removes Dr. Pipt's magic and restores his body, then, using Glinda's knowledge, restores Margolotte and Unc Nunkie. Ozma gives Ojo and his uncle a home near the Emerald City under her protection, bringing Ojo's quest from hunger and loss to security and reunion.

The Patchwork Girl of Oz / Prologue body

The prologue explains how the Royal Historian of Oz resumed writing Oz stories after learning that Ozma of Oz had ordered Oz rendered invisible and communication with it cut off. Children suggested using wireless telegraphy to reach Princess Dorothy, so the Historian built a tower, learned wireless telegraphy, and sent wireless messages into the air. Glinda learned of the messages through her book of events, and a Shaggy Man in Oz sent a wireless reply, allowing Dorothy to obtain Ozma's permission to share the latest news. The volume is titled The Patchwork Girl of Oz and includes chapters about Ojo, the Patchwork Girl, and their adventures in Oz.

Chapter One body

Ojo and his uncle, Unc Nunkie, live alone in a round house surrounded by woods in the Land of Oz. Their cupboard contains only bread, and the bread tree has just two unripe loaves left, so Ojo worries that they are poor and will soon have nothing to eat. Ojo suggests that they leave home to find food in the Munchkin Country, but Unc Nunkie thinks Ojo is too young. Unc Nunkie stays awake after Ojo falls asleep, silently considering the situation.

Chapter Two body

At dawn, Ojo and Unc Nunkie leave their isolated home and travel through the Munchkin Country to visit Dr. Pipt, the Crooked Magician. Dame Margolotte welcomes them, feeds them, and explains Dr. Pipt's Powder of Life, which can bring objects to life. Dr. Pipt is nearly finished making a new batch of the powder for Margolotte, who has made a servant from her grandmother's old patchwork quilt. Margolotte brings the Patchwork Girl into the workshop to show Ojo and Unc Nunkie.

Chapter Three body

Margolotte prepares a patchwork servant girl, giving her a stuffed body, sewn features, and selected qualities for brains. Ojo secretly adds powder from every bottle on the Brain Furniture shelf, including Cleverness, before the Patchwork Girl is brought to life. Dr. Pipt completes the Powder of Life after nearly six years of work and locks it away until morning. While Ojo, Margolotte, Unc Nunkie, and Dr. Pipt talk about magic, a strange cat enters the house.

Chapter Four body

Dr. Pipt introduces Ojo and Unc Nunkie to the Glass Cat, a transparent animal with jeweled features, a ruby heart, and unusually advanced brains. The Glass Cat complains about Dr. Pipt, Margolotte, and its existence, while Margolotte argues with it about the Patchwork Girl's appearance and intended role as a servant. Ojo worries that he secretly added extra qualities to the brains sewn into the Patchwork Girl, contrary to Margolotte's wishes. After breakfast, Dr. Pipt prepares to bring the Patchwork Girl to life and sprinkles the Powder of Life over her head and arms.

Chapter Five body

The Liquid of Petrifaction is accidentally spilled on Margolotte and Unc Nunkie, turning both into marble statues, while the Powder of Life brings a phonograph to life. Dr. Pipt discovers that the Powder of Life is exhausted and identifies a compound that could restore the petrified pair. Ojo agrees to search Oz for the required ingredients, accompanied by the Patchwork Girl and Bungle. The three leave with food and charms so Ojo can try to save Unc Nunkie and Margolotte.

Chapter Six body

Ojo, Scraps, and the Glass Cat leave the Magician’s house and travel through the forest toward the Emerald City and the Country of the Winkies. They meet a Munchkin woodchopper, who warns that unauthorized magic is against the law and that their journey will cross lonely regions, rivers, and dark forests. At night they enter a roadside house where an unseen voice offers them beds but commands them to keep quiet. When Scraps refuses, an unseen hand throws her outside, while Ojo falls asleep inside.

Chapter Seven body

Ojo wakes in a Munchkin house, eats breakfast, and continues traveling with the Glass Cat and Patchwork Girl. They are joined by a living phonograph that has run away from Dr. Pipt after quarreling with him. The phonograph plays classical music and rag-time, but the travelers find both unbearable and send it away. Ojo, the Glass Cat, and Patchwork Girl then continue along the widest path toward the Emerald City.

Chapter Eight body

Ojo, Scraps, and the other travelers stop at the Foolish Owl and Wise Donkey's house to seek advice. The Wise Donkey explains that the Foolish Owl is extremely foolish but says her poems contain advice, while he gives directions to the Emerald City. Ojo learns to follow their path until it reaches the yellow brick road, which leads directly to the Emerald City. The travelers accept the advice to move on and resume their journey.

Chapter Nine body

Ojo, Scraps, and the Glass Cat enter the Woozy's fenced forest to obtain three hairs from the end of the Woozy's tail for the Crooked Magician's magic charm. The Woozy agrees to help after Ojo feeds it bread and cheese, but its three hairs cannot be pulled out. Scraps discovers that the Woozy's eye fire can burn through the fence when the group makes it angry by shouting "Krizzle-Kroo!" The Woozy escapes with them after promising not to eat honey-bees, and they continue seeking the road of yellow bricks and the other things Ojo needs.

Chapter Ten body

Ojo and his companions reach the yellow brick road and head west toward the Emerald City. They are captured by monstrous plants whose leaves fold around them, leaving Ojo despairing that no one can save them. The Shaggy Man arrives, uses whistling to control the plants, and cuts Ojo and his companions free. He introduces himself to Ojo and rescues Scraps, the Glass Cat, and the Woozy.

Chapter Eleven body

The Shaggy Man joins Ojo, Scraps, the Glass Cat, and the Woozy on their journey toward the Emerald City to help them find the things required by the Crooked Magician to restore Unc Nunkie and Margolotte. He explains that a six-leaved clover may be found near the Emerald City, while a yellow butterfly, a dark well, and a drop of oil from a live man's body may be difficult to find. The travelers encounter the damaged live phonograph, whose song irritates the Shaggy Man and causes it to flee. That night, the group shelters in a deserted cabin, where the Shaggy Man sings about the Land of Oz and its unusual inhabitants.

Chapter Twelve body

Ojo and his companions travel toward the Emerald City and must walk backward to cross a section of the road of yellow bricks that slides them backward. They encounter Chiss, a giant porcupine whose quills can be thrown in any direction, and the Woozy's attempted growl fails to frighten him. Chiss attacks with a shower of quills, injuring the Shaggy Man and Scraps, but the travelers collect all of Chiss's quills and leave him harmless. Ojo uses a charm for flesh wounds to heal the Shaggy Man's leg before they continue their journey.

Chapter Thirteen body

Scraps, Ojo, the Shaggy Man, the Woozy, and the Glass Cat pass through an optical-illusion wall and continue toward the Emerald City. Ojo searches for a six-leaved clover despite being warned that picking one violates Ozma's law, then agrees to avoid the clover. They meet the Scarecrow of Oz riding the Sawhorse, and Ojo explains that the Woozy's three tail hairs are needed to restore Unc Nunkie from a marble statue. The Scarecrow advises Ojo to seek Dorothy's help because Ozma may not permit the Crooked Magician's restoration magic, and he leaves for a visit with Jinjur before planning to return to the Emerald City.

Chapter Fourteen body

Ojo and his companions travel toward the Emerald City while discussing the Scarecrow, Dorothy, Ozma, and the Crooked Magician. Ojo secretly plucks a six-leaved clover from beside the road because he believes it will help restore Unc Nunkie to life. At the Emerald City gate, the Soldier with the Green Whiskers arrests Ojo for allegedly breaking one of the Laws of Oz. Ojo is placed in jeweled golden handcuffs despite Scraps's claim that he has broken no law.

Chapter Fifteen body

Ojo the Unlucky is arrested by the Soldier with the Green Whiskers for breaking a Law of Oz by picking the six-leaved clover. He is taken to the only prison in Oz, a comfortable prison house run by Tollydiggle, where he is fed, entertained, and treated kindly. Tollydiggle explains that Ozma uses kindness to help prisoners become strong and brave enough to resist wrongdoing. Ojo begins to understand his fault, though he remains uncertain about the justice of his arrest.

Chapter Sixteen body

Dorothy Gale receives the Shaggy Man, Scraps, the Woozy, and the Glass Cat at the royal palace and learns about Ojo's quest to restore Unc Nunkie and Margolotte after their accidental petrification. The Shaggy Man explains that Ojo was arrested by the Soldier with the Green Whiskers for allegedly breaking the Law of Oz by picking a six-leaved clover. Scraps secretly hides the six-leaved clover in a golden vase and declares that she will help prove Ojo innocent. Dorothy arranges lodging for the Woozy and the Glass Cat and keeps Scraps nearby while she learns more about her.

Chapter Seventeen body

Ozma hosts a banquet for Dorothy, the Shaggy Man, the Scarecrow, and the Wizard, where they discuss their recent adventures and companions. The Scarecrow praises the Patchwork Girl, while the group talks about the Woozy, the Glass Cat, and the Powder of Life. Ozma explains that Mombi's Powder of Life brought Jack Pumpkinhead and the Sawhorse to life and ultimately helped her escape, regain her proper identity, and become the rightful Ruler of Oz.

Chapter Eighteen body

Ojo is brought before Ozma of Oz for picking a six-leaved clover, admits that he broke the law, and is forgiven and released. Ozma permits Dr. Pipt to make a magic charm to restore Unc Nunkie and Margolotte, provided the Wizard of Oz can help Ojo find the required ingredients. Dorothy, the Scarecrow, and Scraps join Ojo's search for a gill of water from a dark well, while the Glass Cat and Woozy remain in the Emerald City. Ojo's party prepares to leave the next day and search the Land of Oz for the missing ingredient.

Chapter Nineteen body

Dorothy, Ojo, Toto, the Scarecrow, and the Patchwork Girl visit Jack Pumpkinhead at his pumpkin house before traveling south toward the Quadling Country in search of water from a naturally dark well. Jack explains that the dark well will be difficult to find and advises them to seek mountains, rocks, and caverns. Near the edge of the Quadling Country, the travelers encounter the Tottenhots, who toss the Scarecrow, the Patchwork Girl, and attempt to toss Ojo until Dorothy and Toto stop them. The travelers then sleep in one of the Tottenhots' circular houses while the Tottenhots play outside.

Chapter Twenty body

Dorothy, the Scarecrow, Ojo, Scraps, and Toto leave the Tottenhot and travel through a rocky mountain rift. They encounter signs warning of Yoop and find Mister Yoop, a huge captive giant who tries to eat them. The Scarecrow’s plan allows Dorothy, Ojo, Scraps, and Toto to escape past the cave while Yoop grabs and throws the Scarecrow and Scraps. The travelers continue their journey through the mountains, relieved that Mister Yoop remains caged.

Chapter Twenty-One body

Dorothy, Ojo, Scarecrow, Scraps, and Toto climb a steep mountain while searching for a dark well. They follow a rock path into a hidden tunnel, where they meet Hip Hopper, a one-legged champion wrestler. Hip Hopper explains that the Hoppers and Horners live on opposite sides of the mountain and are at war. He leads the travelers toward the Hopper city, saying the Horner Country may contain a dark well.

Chapter Twenty-Two body

Dorothy, the Scarecrow, Scraps, Ojo, and the Champion reach a great cave containing the one-legged Hoppers and the horned Horners, whose countries are separated by a marble fence. The Hoppers are preparing for war because a Horner joked that their single legs gave them less “under-standing,” but the Scarecrow suggests that the insult may be resolved without fighting. The Champion’s attempt to throw the Scarecrow over the fence leaves him caught on a picket, while Scraps lands in Horner Country and speaks with Jak, the Chief of the Horners. Jak explains that Diksey made the joke and agrees to wait for him so he might explain it to the Hoppers.

Chapter Twenty-Three body

Scraps Patchwork visits Chief Horner's radium-decorated home and meets his nineteen daughters, who are raised according to strict rules of ladylike behavior. Diksey explains that his joke about the Hoppers having less "standundering" was not meant as an insult, and Dorothy persuades the Hoppers to laugh so the proposed war will end. Peace is declared, and the Horners use a ladder to rescue the Scarecrow from the marble picket fence. Ojo then learns from Diksey that a dark well exists in the Horners' radium mine, so the group goes there to investigate.

Chapter Twenty-Four body

Diksey leads Ojo, Dorothy, the Scarecrow, Scraps, and Toto through the great cave and into a mine. After sliding down a steep, dark tunnel, they reach a vast cave and find the dark well. Ojo fills the gold flask with water from the dark well and puts it in his pocket. The group returns safely to the Horner city with the water secured.

Chapter Twenty-Five body

Dorothy, Scarecrow, Ojo, Scraps, and Toto travel through the red Country of the Quadlings seeking a route to the Country of the Winkies, where Ojo can find a yellow butterfly. They reach a strong river with no bridge or boat and meet a lazy Quadling man who agrees to help build a raft in exchange for Square Meal Tablets. The travelers and the Quadling man construct the raft from logs, clothesline, and boards, while Scraps minds the children. After the Quadling man's wife returns and becomes angry about the materials used, Dorothy reassures her with promises of repayment, and the travelers stay overnight to begin their river journey the next morning.

Chapter Twenty-Six body

Dorothy, the Scarecrow, Ojo, Scraps, and Toto travel by raft down the Trick River toward the Winkie Country, but the river repeatedly reverses its current. Ojo catches a great fish to help pull the raft forward, and the travelers later use a tree, a branch, and a rock to resist the backward current. They finally reach the Winkie Country, land near the Tin Woodman's tin castle, and walk through fields of yellow lilies to the castle grounds. The Tin Woodman welcomes Toto, the Scarecrow, and Dorothy, then marvels at Scraps.

Chapter Twenty-Seven body

The Tin Woodman welcomes Ojo, Scraps, Dorothy, and their companions in the Winkie Country, where Ojo obtains a drop of oil from the Tin Woodman's knee joint for the magic charm. The Tin Woodman refuses to let Ojo obtain the left wing of a yellow butterfly because doing so would torture and kill it, so the party travels to the Emerald City to seek Ozma's advice. Ozma reveals that Dr. Pipt has been brought to the palace, his Powder of Life equipment and recipes have been destroyed, and marble statues of Unc Nunkie and Margolotte have been brought there. She also says that Glinda the Good knew Ojo would fail to find all the ingredients and has arranged for something to happen in the palace.

Chapter Twenty-Eight body

Ojo arrives and finds the Wizard, Ozma, Dr. Pipt, and his friends gathered beside the statues of Unc Nunkie and Margolotte. The Wonderful Wizard of Oz removes Dr. Pipt's magical powers, restores his crooked limbs, and explains the arrangements for the Glass Cat, the Woozy, and the Patchwork Girl. Using knowledge from Glinda the Good, the Wizard restores Margolotte and Unc Nunkie from petrification. Ozma gives Ojo and Unc Nunkie a house outside the Emerald City, where they will live under her protection.
