R2AG: Incorporating Retrieval Information into Retrieval Augmented Generation

Fuda Ye, Shuangyin Li, Yongqi Zhang, Lei Chen · 2024 · 11 sections
"R2AG: Incorporating Retrieval Information into Retrieval Augmented Generation" by Fuda Ye, Shuangyin Li, Yongqi Zhang, Lei Chen (2024), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.findings-emnlp.678

This paper introduces R2AG, an enhanced retrieval-augmented generation framework by Fuda Ye, Shuangyin Li, Yongqi Zhang, and Lei Chen. It addresses a semantic gap between retrievers, which rank external documents for a query, and large language models (LLMs), which must use those documents to generate answers. Standard RAG places retrieved documents with the query, while other enhanced approaches may add noise, cost, information loss, or require joint retriever–LLM training. R2AG is positioned as a cost-effective, non-destructive alternative, especially for low-resource settings where retrievers and LLMs are frozen. R2AG computes list-wise retrieval features from a query and its ranked documents, including relevance, precedent similarity, and neighbor similarity information. A trainable R2-Former transforms these features, and retrieval-aware prompting injects the resulting information into the LLM input embeddings. This retrieval information acts as an anchor for the LLM, helping it understand retrieved documents and reduce retriever–LLM miscommunication. The R2-Former is trained with both a query-document matching task and a language-modeling objective; the LLM may remain frozen or be trained. Experiments on Natural Questions, HotpotQA, MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and DuReader use accuracy, F1, and Rouge. Across frozen and fine-tuned settings and multiple LLMs, R2AG generally improves on its foundation LLMs and most enhanced RAG methods. Combining R2AG with RAFT produces the strongest overall results. Ablations and discussions attribute gains to retrieval features, query-document matching, retrieval-aware prompting, and stronger retrievers and LLMs. The paper reports robustness and efficiency, including only a 0.8% increase in inference latency. The paper notes limits: R2AG depends on encoder-based retriever representations; sparse and cross-encoder use is untested; performance depends on the foundation LLM and may not extend to more powerful closed-source LLMs; and other useful retrieval features may be absent. Evaluation assumes relevant documents are available, leaving no-relevant-document cases for future work, potentially with self-RAG. The authors state that public datasets and models were used, report no conflicts, and describe support from WT145-39, 2023A1515012073, and No. 62006083.

Related Works / Retrieval Augmented Generation .. Enhanced RAG body

Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) combines retrievers with LLMs by placing retrieved documents alongside the original query to generate responses, helping address hallucinations and outdated knowledge. Enhanced RAG methods improve retrieval through supervision, ranking, selection, truncation, or document compression, but may introduce noise, computational costs, or information loss. Other approaches incorporate documents into latent representations or jointly optimize retrievers and LLMs, but they can be limited to encoder-decoder models or require additional training. R2AG is presented as a cost-effective, non-destructive approach for bridging the semantic gap between retrievers and LLMs.

R2AG / Problem Formulation and Overview .. Training Strategy body

This unit introduces R2AG, a framework that connects a retriever with an LLM by extracting and transforming retrieval information before generation. It computes list-wise retrieval features from the query and ranked documents, processes them with the R2-Former, and injects the resulting information into the LLM’s input embeddings through retrieval-aware prompting. R2AG jointly trains the R2-Former with a query-document matching task and language-modeling objective, while allowing the LLM to remain frozen or be trained. The framework is designed to reduce the semantic gap and miscommunication between retrievers and LLMs.

Experiments / Datasets and Metrics .. Discussions body

The experiments evaluate R2AG on Natural Questions, HotpotQA, MuSiQue, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and DuReader using accuracy, F1 score, and Rouge. R2AG is compared with standard RAG baselines using multiple LLMs and enhanced RAG methods, including RAFT, across frozen and fine-tuned settings. R2AG generally improves over its foundation LLMs and most enhanced RAG methods, while R2AG combined with RAFT achieves the strongest overall results. Ablation and discussion experiments show that retrieval features, query-document matching, stronger retrievers, stronger LLMs, and retrieval-aware prompting contribute to performance.

Conclusion and Future Work body

The paper proposes R2AG, an enhanced retrieval-augmented generation framework designed to bridge the semantic gap between retrievers and LLMs. R2AG incorporates retrieval information from retrievers into the LLMs’ generation process to capture a comprehensive understanding of retrieved documents. Experimental results show that R2AG outperforms other competitors, and further analysis confirms its robustness and effectiveness. Future work may apply more retrieval features to the R2AG framework.

Limitations body

The unit describes limitations of R2AG. R2AG depends on semantic representations from encoder-based retrievers, and its suitability with sparse and cross-encoder retrievers remains unexplored. It also relies on the capabilities of the foundation LLM, may not work with more powerful closed-source LLMs, and may omit informative retrieval features beyond relevance, precedent similarity, and neighbor similarity scores. R2AG was evaluated on five datasets with relevant documents provided, but situations without relevant documents require further consideration, potentially using techniques such as self-RAG.

Ethics Statement body

The statement concerns LLMs, which can generate incorrect and potentially harmful answers. The proposed method aims to alleviate this issue by providing LLMs with retrieved documents and retrieval information to enhance their generation capability. The work followed ethical guidelines established by the academic and open-source community. All datasets and models used were publicly available, and the authors reported no conflicts of interest.
