LongRAG: A Dual-Perspective Retrieval-Augmented Generation Paradigm for Long-Context Question Answering

Qingfei Zhao, Ruobing Wang, Yukuo Cen, Daren Zha, Shicheng Tan, Yuxiao Dong, Jie Tang · 2024 · 18 sections

This is the front matter and paper record for “LongRAG: A Dual-Perspective Retrieval-Augmented Generation Paradigm for Long-Context Question Answering,” in the Proceedings of the 2024 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, held November 12–16, 2024. Qingfei Zhao, Ruobing Wang, Yukuo Cen, Daren Zha, Shicheng Tan, Yuxiao Dong, and Jie Tang present LongRAG for long-context question answering, where models must reason accurately over long documents. The paper begins by identifying two central difficulties: long-context language models may suffer from “lost in the middle,” while Vanilla RAG can fragment global context through chunking and retrieve noisy or low-quality evidence. After reviewing long-context LLM scaling, RAG, advanced RAG, and domain-specific fine-tuning, the authors introduce LongRAG as a Vanilla RAG-based system augmented with a Long-Context Extractor and a CoT-guided Filter. LongRAG first uses a hybrid retriever, combining chunking, overlapping windows, dual-encoder and cross-encoder retrieval, and FAISS. It then restores source paragraphs, extracting global information while identifying factual details. The CoT-guided Filter selects question-relevant factual chunks, and an LLM-augmented Generator combines the global information and filtered details to produce an answer. To fine-tune components, the authors create LRGinstruction with ChatGLM3-32B-128k using HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, MusiQue, and QASPER. The evaluation uses standardized HotpotQA, 2Wiki-MultiHopQA, and MusiQue samples and F1-score comparisons against long-context LLMs, advanced RAG methods, and vanilla RAG. LongRAG generally surpasses LongAlign, LongLoRA, CRAG, Self-RAG, and vanilla RAG; fine-tuned ChatGLM3-6B-32k exceeds non-fine-tuned GPT-3.5-Turbo. LongRAG-GLM-4 obtains the highest reported average score, 52.56%. Ablations favor combining extraction and filtering, which preserves global information, selects relevant evidence, and reduces generator input length. The paper concludes that LongRAG is effective, robust, adaptable, and deployable with smaller local models, while acknowledging dependence on one-time retrieval and possible annotation bias in ChatGLM3-generated fine-tuning data. The work received support from the Natural Science Foundation of China, Tsinghua University, Siemens Ltd., China, and the New Cornerstone Science Foundation.

LongRAG: A Dual-Perspective Retrieval-Augmented Generation Paradigm for Long-Context Question Answering front matter

“LongRAG: A Dual-Perspective Retrieval-Augmented Generation Paradigm for Long-Context Question Answering” is a work by Qingfei Zhao, Ruobing Wang, Yukuo Cen, Daren Zha, Shicheng Tan, Yuxiao Dong, and Jie Tang. The authors are affiliated with the Institute of Information Engineering, the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences, Tsinghua University, and Zhipu AI. The work concerns a dual-perspective retrieval-augmented generation paradigm for long-context question answering.

Abstract body

The unit concerns Long-Context Question Answering (LCQA), which requires reasoning over long documents to answer questions accurately. It explains that existing long-context large language models struggle with the "lost in the middle" issue, while Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) can introduce factual evidence but may disrupt global context and retrieve noisy or low-quality information. The authors propose LongRAG, a general and robust RAG paradigm designed to improve understanding of both global information and factual details in complex long-context knowledge. Experiments on three multi-hop datasets show that LongRAG outperforms long-context LLMs, advanced RAG, and Vanilla RAG, while ablation studies analyze its components and fine-tuning strategies.

Introduction body

The unit concerns LongRAG, a dual-perspective RAG system for long-context question answering (LCQA), and compares it with long-context LLMs and Vanilla RAG. It explains that long-context LLMs can suffer from the “lost in the middle” issue, while Vanilla RAG can lose global information through chunking and retrieve low-quality evidence in documents with low evidence density. LongRAG addresses these problems with a hybrid retriever, an LLM-augmented information extractor, a CoT-guided filter, and an LLM-augmented generator. Experiments on three multi-hop LongBench datasets report that LongRAG outperformed long-context LLMs, advanced RAG methods, and Vanilla RAG.

2 / 2.1 .. 2.3 body

This unit reviews methods for scaling Long-Context LLMs, including long-context training or fine-tuning and non-fine-tuned approaches such as restricted attention and context compression. It describes Retrieval-Augmented Generation as a way to improve LLM responses by incorporating external knowledge, while noting problems with chunking, incomplete information, and noise. It discusses advanced RAG models that filter, re-rank, avoid chunks, or actively retrieve knowledge. It also covers Domain-Specific Fine-Tuning for RAG, including fine-tuning retrieval components, generators, and multiple components collaboratively.

3 / 3.1 .. 3.2 body

This unit defines LongRAG as a Vanilla RAG-based system that adds a Long-Context Extractor and a CoT-guided Filter after retrieval. Given a question and long-context corpus, it retrieves relevant chunks, extracts global information from their source paragraphs, identifies factual details, and uses both to generate an answer. It also describes LRGinstruction, a small instruction-following dataset built with ChatGLM3-32B-128k from HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, MusiQue, and QASPER. The dataset supports supervised fine-tuning of LongRAG components through long-context extraction, CoT-guided filtering, and task-oriented question-answering data.

4 / 4.1 .. 4.5 body

The LongRAG System combines hybrid retrieval, LLM-based information extraction, CoT-guided filtering, and generation for long-context multi-hop question answering. The Hybrid Retriever uses chunking, overlapping windows, dual-encoder and cross-encoder retrieval, and FAISS, while the extractor restores source paragraphs and produces global information. The CoT-guided Filter identifies relevant factual chunks, and the LLM-augmented Generator combines global information with filtered details to answer questions. Experiments on HotpotQA, 2WikiMQA, and MusiQue show that LongRAG improves performance over vanilla RAG baselines, with LongRAG-GLM-4 achieving the highest reported average score of 52.56%.

5 / 5.1 .. 5.4 body

The unit evaluates LongRAG on standardized HotpotQA, 2Wiki-MultiHopQA, and MusiQue samples using F1-score, comparing it with long-context LLM methods, advanced RAG methods, and vanilla RAG systems. LongRAG generally outperforms LongAlign, LongLoRA, CRAG, Self-RAG, and vanilla RAG, with LongRAG using fine-tuned ChatGLM3-6B-32k surpassing non-fine-tuned GPT-3.5-Turbo. Ablation results show that the Extractor & Filter strategy usually performs best by combining global information extraction with question-relevant filtering while reducing generator input length. The experiments also report that ChatGLM3-6B-32k can serve as a lower-cost built-in model for extraction and filtering while achieving results that surpass GPT-3.5-Turbo and rival GLM-4.

6 body

The unit presents LongRAG, an effective and robust RAG system paradigm for improving performance on long-context question answering (LCQA) tasks. LongRAG addresses incomplete collection of long-context information and difficulty identifying factual information amid noise. Experiments show that LongRAG outperforms long-context LLMs, advanced RAG methods, and Vanilla RAG across various LLMs. Its plug-and-play components support low-cost local deployment with small parameter-size LLMs, and its automated fine-tuning data pipeline facilitates adaptation to specific-domain data.

7 body

The paper presents LongRAG as a general-purpose, corpus-level retrieval-augmented generation system paradigm for long-context question answering. It reports that the system is effective but identifies limitations involving one-time retrieval dependency and dataset annotation bias. The study examined its information extractor and CoT-guided filter only in a one-time retrieval setting, making performance dependent on single-pass retrieved chunks. It used ChatGLM3 to generate fine-tuning datasets but notes that self-generated datasets may contain annotation biases.
