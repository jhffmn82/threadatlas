The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~ · 54 sections

This document is The Odyssey, tracing Ulysses’ return from Troy, his son Telemachus’s search for him, and the recovery of Ulysses’ household in Ithaca. Ulysses is detained by Calypso while the gods, led by Jove and aided by Minerva, decide he must go home; Neptune remains hostile because Ulysses blinded his son Polyphemus. Minerva first strengthens Telemachus, who confronts Penelope’s suitors and travels to Pylos and Lacedaemon. Nestor, Menelaus, and Helen confirm Ulysses’ importance and suffering, while the suitors, led especially by Antinous, plot to kill Telemachus. Mercury orders Calypso to release Ulysses. After Neptune wrecks his raft, Ulysses reaches Scheria, where Nausicaa, Alcinous, Arete, and the Phaeacians receive him. At Alcinous’s court, Ulysses reveals himself and recounts his long wanderings: disasters among the Cicons and Lotus-eaters; escape from Polyphemus, whose prayer brings Neptune’s vengeance; Aeolus’s winds; the Laestrygonians; Circe; the journey to Hades and Teiresias’s warnings; the Sirens, Scylla and Charybdis; and the destruction of his crew after they kill Hyperion’s cattle. Alone, he reaches Calypso. The Phaeacians finally carry Ulysses to Ithaca. Minerva disguises him as a beggar, and he finds loyalty in Eumaeus the swineherd. Telemachus returns safely, meets his disguised father, and joins him in planning against the suitors. In the palace, Ulysses endures insults, is recognized by his dog Argos and later by Euryclea through an old scar, but keeps his identity hidden. Penelope, still faithful despite the suitors’ pressure, announces that whoever can string Ulysses’ bow and shoot through twelve axes will marry her. Ulysses strings the bow effortlessly, reveals himself, and with Telemachus, Eumaeus, Philoetius, and Minerva kills the suitors. The disloyal women and Melanthius are punished, and the house is purified. Penelope accepts Ulysses only after he proves knowledge of their immovable bed. In the end, Ulysses reunites with Laertes; when Eupeithes leads Ithacans against him, Laertes kills Eupeithes, and Minerva and Jove impose peace.

The Odyssey / BOOK I body

The unit concerns Ulysses, who has not returned home after sacking Troy and is being detained by Calypso. The gods meet in Olympian Jove’s house and agree that Ulysses should return, although Neptune still persecutes him for blinding Polyphemus. Minerva plans to visit Ithaca, encourage Telemachus to confront the suitors, and send him to Sparta and Pylos for news of his father. Mercury is to tell Calypso that the gods have decided Ulysses must go home.

So saying she bound on her glittering golden sandals, imperishable, body

Minerva visits Ithaca disguised as Mentes and advises Telemachus to challenge the suitors and search for his missing father, Ulysses. Telemachus welcomes her, learns that Ulysses may still be alive, and resolves to call an assembly and undertake a voyage to Pylos and Sparta. He then confronts the suitors, orders them to leave Ulysses’ house, and asserts his authority. Penelope mourns Ulysses, while the suitors continue feasting and competing for her hand.

BOOK II body

Telemachus calls an assembly of the people of Ithaca and demands that the suitors stop consuming his household and allow him to seek news of Ulysses. Antinous blames Penelope for delaying remarriage, while Halitherses prophesies that Ulysses will soon return and destroy the suitors. Minerva, disguised as Mentor, encourages Telemachus, promises to accompany him, and arranges a ship and crew for his voyage to Pylos and Sparta. Telemachus secretly gathers wine and barley meal, while Minerva prepares the ship and summons the crew.

Furthermore she went to the house of Ulysses, and threw the suitors body

Minerva causes the suitors at the house of Ulysses to become so drowsy that they leave for the town to sleep. Taking the form and voice of Mentor, she calls Telemachus outside and leads him to the ship. Telemachus and the crew load stores, set sail, and make offerings to the immortal gods, especially Minerva. The ship travels through the night from dark until dawn.

BOOK III body

Telemachus and Minerva arrive at Pylos, where Nestor and the Pylian people are offering sacrifices to Neptune. Telemachus asks Nestor about Ulysses and learns about the Achaeans’ suffering at Troy, their divided homeward voyage, and the fate of Agamemnon. Nestor explains that Aegisthus killed Agamemnon while Menelaus was delayed on his return, and that Orestes later avenged his father. Nestor advises Telemachus to visit Menelaus in Lacedaemon, offering ships, horses, and an escort.

As he spoke the sun set and it came on dark, whereon Minerva said, body

Minerva directs Nestor and his household to complete their religious offerings, then departs in the form of an eagle. Nestor welcomes Telemachus into his house, arranges a sacrifice to Minerva, and sends Telemachus toward Lacedaemon with Pisistratus. After the sacrifice and a communal meal, Telemachus and Pisistratus travel by chariot from Pylos, spend the night at Pherae, and continue their journey.

BOOK IV body

Telemachus and Pisistratus arrive in Lacedaemon and receive lavish hospitality at Menelaus’s palace during wedding celebrations. Menelaus and Helen recognize Telemachus as Odysseus’s son and recount Odysseus’s hardships, his disguised entry into Troy, and his bravery in the Trojan Horse. The company grieves for the dead and for Odysseus, who remains missing, until Helen drugs the wine to relieve their sorrow. Telemachus and Pisistratus then retire to sleep in the forecourt.

When the child of morning, rosy-fingered Dawn appeared, Menelaus rose body

Menelaus tells Telemachus how Idothea helped him capture Proteus in Egypt and learn how to complete his sea voyage home. Proteus reveals that Ulysses is alive but held by Calypso, that Ajax drowned, and that Agamemnon was murdered by Aegisthus. Menelaus says that the gods will grant him a blessed afterlife and offers Telemachus gifts, but Telemachus declines the horses because Ithaca lacks suitable open ground. Menelaus agrees to give him a precious silver mixing bowl instead.

Meanwhile the suitors were throwing discs or aiming with spears at a body

The suitors learn that Telemachus has sailed from Ithaca to Pylos and Lacedaemon to seek news of Ulysses, so Antinous organizes an attempt to murder him on his return. Medon tells Penelope about the plot, while Euryclea admits that she helped Telemachus depart without Penelope’s knowledge. Penelope prays to Minerva, who sends her a reassuring dream promising that Telemachus will return, though the goddess does not reveal whether Ulysses is alive. The suitors sail to Asteris and wait in ambush between Ithaca and Samos.

BOOK V body

Jove orders Mercury to tell Calypso that Ulysses must leave her island and return home through Scheria, where the Phaeacians will help him continue his journey. Calypso helps Ulysses build a raft and supplies him with food, water, clothing, and a favorable wind. Neptune raises a violent storm that destroys the raft, while Ino gives Ulysses an enchanted veil to protect him as he swims toward the Phaeacian coast. Ulysses abandons the wreckage and begins swimming for shore.

But Minerva resolved to help Ulysses, so she bound the ways of all the body

Minerva helps Ulysses by calming the winds except for a northern breeze that carries him toward the land of the Phaeacians. After floating for two nights and two days, Ulysses reaches a rocky, surf-beaten coast but struggles to find a safe landing. A wave tears his hands on the rocks, but he swims to a river mouth and prays to its god, who stills the current and waves. Ulysses reaches shore, hides in the woods beneath olive shoots, and falls asleep under Minerva's protection.

BOOK VI body

Minerva directs Nausicaa to arrange a washing day, leading her to the shore with her maids, clothes, a waggon, and mules. The noise of their ball game wakes Ulysses, who approaches them after his shipwreck and asks Nausicaa for help. Nausicaa provides him with clothes, oil, food, and directions to the Phaeacian town and royal house. Ulysses then prays in Minerva’s sacred grove for a friendly reception, while Minerva continues to aid him without appearing openly.

BOOK VII body

Ulysses follows Minerva through Scheria to the palace of King Alcinous, where he petitions Alcinous and Arete for help returning home. The Phaeacians receive him, give him food and drink, and agree to arrange an escort after a sacrificial banquet. Arete recognizes the clothes made by herself and her maids, and Ulysses recounts his voyage from Ogygia and his rescue by Nausicaa. Alcinous offers Ulysses shelter, an estate, and even marriage to Nausicaa, while promising that Phaeacian ships will carry him home.

BOOK VIII body

Alcinous gathers the Phaeacians to arrange Ulysses’ escort home, ordering a new ship prepared and a banquet held. During the feast, Demodocus sings of the quarrel between Ulysses and Achilles, causing Ulysses to weep privately. The Phaeacians then hold athletic games, where Ulysses proves his strength by throwing a heavy discus farther than any of them. Dancing and Demodocus’ song about Mars, Venus, and Vulcan conclude the gathering.

Then Alcinous told Laodamas and Halius to dance alone, for there was no body

Alcinous praises the Phaeacians' dancers and orders them to give Ulysses clothing, gold, and other gifts, while Euryalus apologizes by giving him a sword. Arete packs the gifts, Ulysses bathes and is clothed, and Nausicaa asks him not to forget her. During the feast, Ulysses honors the bard Demodocus and asks him to sing about the wooden horse and the sack of Troy. Demodocus's song causes Ulysses to weep secretly, and Alcinous notices his grief.

“Aldermen and town councillors of the Phaeacians, let Demodocus cease body

A Phaeacian speaker asks Demodocus to stop singing because the guest has been groaning and lamenting during the song. He says that the Phaeacians’ festivities, escort, and gifts are being given in the guest’s honour and asks the guest to reveal his name, homeland, and identity. He also asks where the guest has travelled, what peoples and cities he encountered, and why stories of the Argive Danaans returning from Troy make him unhappy. The speaker explains that Phaeacian ships need no pilots or rudders and can cross mist-covered seas, though Neptune may punish the Phaeacians for escorting others so readily.

BOOK IX body

Ulysses declares his identity to King Alcinous and describes Ithaca, his homeland, before recounting his return from Troy. He tells how he and his men raided Ismarus, were defeated by the Cicons after delaying their departure, and lost many comrades. Storms then drove the ships off course for nine days until they reached the land of the Lotus-eaters. Some men ate the lotus and lost all desire to return home, but Ulysses forced them back to the ships and ordered the others to depart.

“We sailed hence, always in much distress, till we came to the land of body

Odysseus and the Achaeans reach the land of the Cyclopes after being driven off course from Troy. Odysseus enters Polyphemus’s cave with twelve companions, but Polyphemus traps them, kills six men, and eats them. Odysseus gets Polyphemus drunk, gives his name as “Noman,” and blinds him with a heated olive-wood club. Odysseus and the surviving men escape beneath the sheep, take some animals, and sail away while Odysseus taunts Polyphemus.

“He got more and more furious as he heard me, so he tore the top from body

Ulysses and Ulysses's men escape from the Cyclops after the Cyclops throws rocks at their ship. Ulysses identifies himself as Ulysses, son of Laertes, from Ithaca, and taunts the Cyclops about the blinding of the Cyclops. The Cyclops recalls an old prophecy about the Cyclops and prays to Neptune, his father, to prevent Ulysses from reaching home safely. Neptune hears the prayer, and the Cyclops throws a second rock that drives the ship toward the shore.

“When at last we got to the island where we had left the rest of our body

The narrator and his comrades return to the island where they had left the rest of their ships and find their comrades lamenting and awaiting them. They land the vessel and the Cyclops’ sheep, dividing the sheep equitably, while the ram is given to the narrator as an extra share. The narrator sacrifices the ram to Jove, but Jove does not heed the sacrifice and instead considers destroying the ships and comrades. After feasting and camping on the beach, the men depart by ship at dawn, sorrowful over their losses but glad to have escaped death.

BOOK X body

Ulysses reaches the Aeolian island, where Aeolus gives him an ox-hide sack containing the winds to aid his return voyage. After nine days of sailing, Ulysses's men release the winds while he sleeps, and the resulting storm drives them back to Aeolus, who refuses further help. They then reach Telepylus, the stronghold of the Laestrygonians, who destroy every ship except Ulysses's and kill the crews caught in the harbor. Ulysses and the surviving men escape into open water.

“Thence we sailed sadly on, glad to have escaped death, though we had body

Ulysses and his surviving men reach the Aeaean island, where Circe lives, after losing comrades and escaping other dangers. Circe transforms the men led by Eurylochus into pigs, but Mercury gives Ulysses the herb Moly and instructions that protect him from her magic. Ulysses forces Circe to swear not to harm him, and she restores the transformed men and welcomes the company into her house. Circe then urges Ulysses and his men to recover their strength before continuing their voyage home.

“Thus did she speak and we assented. We stayed with Circe for a whole body

Ulysses and his men stay with Circe for a whole year before deciding to resume their journey home. Circe tells Ulysses that they must first voyage to the house of Hades and Proserpine to consult the dead prophet Teiresias. She explains the route, the sacrifices required, and how Ulysses must protect Teiresias's access to the blood. As the men prepare to leave, Elpenor falls from the roof and dies, and Circe brings a ram and an ewe to the shore for the voyage.

BOOK XI body

Ulysses sails with his companions to Oceanus and performs a necromantic sacrifice so that he can question the dead. Elpenor asks to be buried, while Teiresias warns Ulysses about Neptune, the Thrinacian island, the suitors in Ithaca, and his eventual death. Ulysses then speaks with his mother Anticlea, who tells him that Penelope remains faithful, Telemachus holds the estate, and his father suffers in the countryside. He also sees many famous women among the ghosts before deciding to leave the underworld.

Here he ended, and the guests sat all of them enthralled and speechless body

Ulysses finishes recounting his underworld journey to the Phaeacians, describing the dead he encountered after visiting Hades. Agamemnon son of Atreus tells Ulysses that Aegisthus and Clytemnestra murdered him after welcoming him home from Troy. Achilles asks about his son Neoptolemus and father Peleus, while Ajax refuses to speak to Ulysses because of their dispute over Achilles’ armor. Ulysses also describes seeing Minos, Orion, Tityus, Tantalus, Sisyphus, and the phantom of Hercules before fleeing Hades and returning to his ship.

BOOK XII body

Circe tells Ulysses and his crew about the dangers ahead on their journey home. She warns them about the Sirens, the Wanderers, Scylla, and Charybdis, explaining how to pass each danger. She also warns them not to harm the cattle and sheep of the sun-god on the Thrinacian island. Ulysses and his crew have returned from the house of Hades after burying Elpenor on the Aeaean island.

“Here she ended, and dawn enthroned in gold began to show in heaven, body

Ulysses and his men sail past the Sirens and through the Straits of Scylla and Charybdis, where Scylla seizes six men. They land on the island of the sun-god Hyperion despite warnings, and after their provisions run out, Eurylochus persuades the men to slaughter the sun-god's cattle. Jove destroys their ship with lightning, killing all the men except Ulysses. Ulysses survives on a makeshift raft and is eventually carried to the Ogygian island, where Calypso receives him.

BOOK XIII body

Ulysses is carried from Scheria to Ithaca by the Phaeacians, who leave him sleeping with his gifts in the harbour of Phorcys. Neptune transforms the returning Phaeacian ship into stone and threatens the Phaeacians with further punishment. Minerva reveals Ithaca to Ulysses, helps him hide his treasure in the Cave of the Naiads, and urges him to conceal his return while preparing revenge upon the suitors. She disguises Ulysses as an old beggar and sends him to the swineherd, while she goes to Sparta to see his son.

“But why,” said Ulysses, “did you not tell him, for you knew all about body

Ulysses asks Minerva why she did not tell Telemachus everything, since Telemachus might otherwise suffer hardship while others consume Ulysses's estate. Minerva says Telemachus is staying comfortably with Menelaus, while the suitors lie in wait at sea to kill him before he returns. She predicts that the suitors may die before they succeed. Minerva then disguises Ulysses as an aged, poor wanderer, and the two part, with Minerva going to Lacedaemon to fetch Telemachus.

BOOK XIV body

Ulysses reaches Eumaeus’s hut in Ithaca, where the swineherd protects him from the dogs and welcomes him with food and drink. Eumaeus explains his loyalty to Ulysses, the wealth of Ulysses’s estate, and the suitors’ abuse of it. Ulysses promises that he will return before the end of the year and take vengeance on the suitors, but Eumaeus does not believe him. Eumaeus also worries about Penelope, Laertes, and Telemachus, while Ulysses prepares to tell the story of his sufferings.

“I am by birth a Cretan; my father was a well to do man, who had many body

Ulysses, disguised as a Cretan stranger, tells Eumaeus a false account of his birth, warfare, travels, shipwreck, and alleged news of Ulysses. He claims that Jove caused his force to be defeated in Egypt, that he was later betrayed and nearly sold into slavery, and that he escaped to Ithaca. Eumaeus does not believe the story about Ulysses but offers hospitality out of respect for Jove. Eumaeus sacrifices a pig, shares supper with the stranger and the swineherds, gives him shelter, and then goes out to guard the pigs during the storm.

BOOK XV body

Minerva tells Telemachus to leave Lacedaemon, return to Ithaca, avoid the suitors’ ambush, and go first to Eumaeus’s hut. Telemachus receives valuable gifts from Menelaus and Helen, travels with Pisistratus to Pylos, and then boards his ship. At Pylos he accepts the exiled seer Theoclymenus as a suppliant and brings him aboard. The ship sails toward Ithaca with a favorable wind, while Telemachus wonders whether he will escape capture or death.

Meanwhile Ulysses and the swineherd were eating their supper in the body

Ulysses tests Eumaeus by proposing to beg in the city, but Eumaeus urges him to remain at the hut until Telemachus returns. Eumaeus recounts how he was carried from Syra by Phoenicians and sold to Laertes. Meanwhile, Telemachus reaches Ithaca, sends Theoclymenus to Piraeus for hospitality, and goes to the swineherd’s homestead. A hawk carrying a dove appears on Telemachus’s right, and Theoclymenus interprets it as an omen of Telemachus’s future power.

BOOK XVI body

Telemachus returns from Pylos to the swineherd's station, where he meets Ulysses in disguise. Minerva transforms Ulysses into a youthful form, and he reveals himself to Telemachus as his father. Father and son make plans to destroy the suitors, conceal Ulysses's identity, and remove the household's arms. Eumaeus is sent to tell Penelope that Telemachus has returned, while Ulysses and Telemachus prepare to go to the city.

Thus did they converse, and meanwhile the ship which had brought body

Telemachus returns from his voyage to Pylos, while his ship reaches Ithaca and is drawn into the harbour. The suitors learn that he has escaped their ambush and discuss killing him, but Amphinomus urges them to seek the gods' guidance. Penelope, informed of the plot by Medon, rebukes Antinous and the suitors for consuming Ulysses's property and seeking her marriage. At the countryside house, Eumaeus reports the ship's arrival to Telemachus and Ulysses, while Minerva disguises Ulysses so that Eumaeus will not recognize him.

BOOK XVII body

Telemachus returns home from his journey to Pylos and Lacedaemon, reunites with Penelope, and tells her that Menelaus learned Ulysses is alive but held by Calypso. Theoclymenus declares that Ulysses is already in Ithaca, investigating the suitors’ crimes and preparing revenge. Ulysses and Eumaeus travel toward the city, where Melanthius insults Ulysses and kicks him. Ulysses endures the abuse and prepares to enter his own house as a beggar.

As they were thus talking, a dog that had been lying asleep raised his body

Ulysses, disguised as a poor beggar, enters the house and is recognized by his old dog Argos, who dies after recognizing him. Telemachus gives Ulysses food, while Antinous insults him and strikes him with a footstool. Penelope hears of the assault and sends for the stranger so she can question him about Ulysses, but he asks to wait until sundown. Eumaeus returns to the pigs, while Telemachus remains among the suitors and the suitors continue their singing and dancing.

BOOK XVIII body

Irus, a notorious beggar, challenges the disguised Ulysses to a fight at the doorway of the house of Ulysses. Ulysses defeats Irus with a single controlled blow and drags him outside, while the suitors laugh and reward Ulysses with food. Amphinomus offers Ulysses bread and wine, and Ulysses warns him that the suitors are dishonoring Penelope and wasting the estate because Ulysses will soon return. Amphinomus becomes serious but is still destined to be killed by Telemachus.

Then Minerva put it into the mind of Penelope to show herself to the body

Minerva inspires Penelope to appear before the suitors and makes her exceptionally beautiful, while Penelope reproaches Telemachus and persuades the suitors to give her presents. The suitors bring her elaborate clothing and jewelry before she withdraws upstairs. Ulysses, still disguised as a stranger, is mocked by Melantho and Eurymachus, but he answers defiantly and warns that his position will change if he regains his household. Eurymachus attacks him with a footstool, and Telemachus orders the suitors to end the disturbance and go home.

BOOK XIX body

Ulysses and Telemachus secretly remove the armour from the hall while Minerva lights their way, preparing for the killing of the suitors. Penelope questions the disguised Ulysses, who tells a fabricated story about being Aethon of Crete and claims to have recently heard that Ulysses is alive and returning. Penelope describes how she delayed the suitors by weaving and secretly unweaving a burial cloth for Laertes, and she continues to grieve because she believes Ulysses will not return. At Penelope's request, the old nurse Euryclea begins washing the stranger's feet and notices how closely he resembles Ulysses.

Then the old woman took the cauldron in which she was going to wash his body

Ulysses is recognized by Euryclea when she washes his leg and feels the scar left by a wild boar on Mount Parnassus. He makes Euryclea promise to keep his identity secret, while she offers to identify the women in the house who have behaved well or badly. Penelope tells the disguised Ulysses about her dream of an eagle killing her geese and announces a tournament in which the suitors must string Ulysses’ bow and shoot an arrow through twelve axes. Ulysses assures her that he will return before any suitor can accomplish the feat, and Penelope retires in sorrowful anticipation of leaving his house.

BOOK XX body

Ulysses lies awake planning how to kill the suitors, while Minerva reassures him that he will soon escape his troubles. Penelope prays to Diana for death, and Jove answers Ulysses's request for omens with thunder and a miller's prayer that the suitors will dine in the house for the last time. Eumaeus and Philoetius express loyalty to Ulysses, while Telemachus warns the suitors not to insult their guest. During the feast, Ctesippus throws an ox's foot at Ulysses, and Theoclymenus sees frightening signs of the suitors' approaching destruction before leaving the house.

BOOK XXI body

Penelope brings out Ulysses’s bow and proposes that the suitors compete by stringing it and shooting an arrow through twelve axes, with the winner marrying her. Telemachus arranges the axes and tries to string the bow, but Ulysses signals him to stop before he succeeds. The suitors fail one after another, while Eumaeus and the stockman weep at the sight of Ulysses’s bow. Antinous orders the bow to be warmed and greased so that the remaining suitors, especially himself and Eurymachus, can try again.

Then the swineherd and the stockman left the cloisters together, and body

Ulysses reveals his identity to Eumaeus and Philoetius after learning that they would support him, and he directs them to prepare the house for conflict with the suitors. With Telemachus's authority, Eumaeus gives Ulysses the bow, while the women’s doors and the outer gates are secured. Ulysses strings the bow easily and shoots an arrow through all the axe handles, revealing his strength and signaling that the contest is over. He then arms Telemachus and prepares to confront the suitors.

BOOK XXII body

Ulysses reveals himself, kills Antinous, and begins destroying the suitors with help from Telemachus, Eumaeus, and Philoetius. Melanthius secretly brings armour to the suitors but is captured, bound, and suspended in the store-room. Minerva protects Ulysses and his companions during the battle, which ends with the suitors dead, while Phemius and Medon are spared. Ulysses then asks Euryclea to identify the twelve women who have misconducted themselves, ordering that they be brought to him.

Euryclea left the cloister to tell the women, and make them come to body

Euryclea summons the household women after Ulysses orders Telemachus, the stockman, and the swineherd to remove the dead and clean the cloisters. The women clean the tables and seats, while Telemachus and the others remove blood and dirt; afterward Telemachus oversees the execution of the women by hanging. Melanthius is mutilated and killed, and Ulysses purifies the cloisters and both courts with fire and sulphur. Ulysses then sends Euryclea to summon Penelope and the other women, who come with torches to embrace him.

BOOK XXIII body

Euryclea tells Penelope that Ulysses has returned and killed the suitors, but Penelope remains doubtful until he proves his identity through knowledge of their immovable bed. Ulysses and Penelope recognize one another and share their sufferings, while Ulysses recounts his return journey and the future task foretold by Teiresias. Before dawn, Ulysses, Telemachus, Eumaeus, and Philoetius arm themselves and leave the town to visit Laertes, while Penelope is told to remain upstairs.

BOOK XXIV body

Mercury of Cyllene leads the ghosts of the suitors into Hades, where they meet Achilles and Agamemnon. Agamemnon hears from Amphimedon how Ulysses, aided by Telemachus and the gods, killed the suitors after Penelope delayed remarriage with her weaving ruse. Agamemnon praises Penelope’s faithfulness and condemns the daughter of Tyndareus for killing her husband. The account also recalls Achilles’ funeral and Agamemnon’s murder by Aegisthus and his wicked wife.

Thus did they converse in the house of Hades deep down within the body

Ulysses reunites with his aged father Laertes at Laertes's farm after returning to Ithaca, and Laertes recognizes him through his scar and his knowledge of the vineyard. The townspeople, led by Eupeithes, prepare to fight Ulysses over the massacre of the suitors, but Minerva and Jove intervene. Laertes kills Eupeithes, and Minerva stops the fighting and establishes a peace covenant between Ulysses and the opposing Ithacans.
