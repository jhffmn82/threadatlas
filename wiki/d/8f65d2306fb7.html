Fact, Fetch, and Reason: A Unified Evaluation of Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Satyapriya Krishna, Kalpesh Krishna, Anhad Mohananey, Steven Schwarcz, Adam Stambler, Shyam Upadhyay, Manaal Faruqui · 2025 · 17 sections
"Fact, Fetch, and Reason: A Unified Evaluation of Retrieval-Augmented Generation" by Satyapriya Krishna, Kalpesh Krishna, Anhad Mohananey, Steven Schwarcz, Adam Stambler, Shyam Upadhyay, Manaal Faruqui (2025), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.naacl-long.243

FRAMES (Factuality, Retrieval, And reasoning MEasurement Set) is a benchmark and evaluation dataset for Large Language Models and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems. It is designed to jointly test factuality, information retrieval, reasoning, and synthesis in realistic end-to-end settings, rather than assessing these abilities separately as benchmarks such as NaturalQuestions, TriviaQA, and ELI5 tend to do. FRAMES contains 824 human-annotated questions that require combining information from two to fifteen Wikipedia articles, including multi-hop and temporal reasoning, and are labeled across five reasoning types. The researchers turned to human annotation after LLM-generated synthetic questions and answers showed more than 30% hallucination. Quality checks considered correctness, temporal ambiguity, guesswork, interpretability, and possible data contamination. The empirical analysis compares naive single-step prompting, BM25-retrieved context, oracle prompting, and iterative multi-step retrieval. Gemini-Pro-1.5-0514 scores 0.408 with naive prompting and 0.729 with oracle information, while still making numerical, tabular, and post-processing errors. A multi-step search-and-reasoning approach, using five iterations, five queries per iteration, and ten retrieved documents, reaches 0.66 accuracy, more than a 50% improvement over single-step inference and close to the roughly 0.73 oracle result. The results show that current LLMs still struggle with complex multi-document reasoning and that both retrieval and reasoning need improvement. The document also notes limits: pretraining-data contamination may inflate results, and FRAMES may not cover the full range of real-world queries. It calls for newer or synthetic data, contamination measurement, and broader coverage. Ethical concerns include source bias, misuse, computational environmental costs, efficiency, accessibility, and transparency. The authors thank Abhimanyu Goyal, Tsendsuren Munkhdalai, and Dipanjan Das for feedback on writing and presentation.

Abstract body

The abstract concerns Large Language Models (LLMs) and their use in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems. It introduces FRAMES (Factuality, Retrieval, And reasoning MEasurement Set), a dataset for evaluating factual responses, retrieval capabilities, and reasoning in end-to-end RAG scenarios. FRAMES contains challenging multi-hop questions that require integrating information from multiple sources. Baseline LLM accuracy is 0.408 without retrieval, while a multi-step retrieval pipeline raises accuracy to 0.66, an improvement of more than 50%.

The Empirical Analysis evaluates state-of-the-art large language models on FRAMES questions involving factuality, retrieval, and reasoning. Single-step evaluations compare naive prompting, BM25-retrieved context, and an Oracle Prompt, while multi-step evaluations require iterative query generation and retrieval. Gemini-Pro-1.5-0514 improves from 0.408 with naive prompting to 0.729 with oracle information, but retains errors in numerical, tabular, and post-processing tasks. Multi-step search planning raises performance to 0.66 with five iterations, five queries per iteration, and ten retrieved documents, approaching the oracle result of 0.73.

4 body

The unit concerns the evaluation of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems, which combine retrieval and generative capabilities. It compares NaturalQuestions, TriviaQA, and ELI5, noting that each emphasizes a particular aspect but lacks comprehensive assessment. It presents FRAMES as a unified framework covering factual retrieval, reasoning, and synthesis, including complex multi-hop queries and temporal disambiguation. FRAMES also evaluates the synthesis of information into coherent responses in realistic, multifaceted scenarios.

5 body

This unit concerns FRAMES, a comprehensive evaluation dataset for testing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems. It evaluates factuality, retrieval accuracy, and reasoning through realistic, multi-document queries. Experiments with state-of-the-art LLMs show that they struggle with complex, multi-hop reasoning tasks, achieving only moderate improvements from multi-step retrieval and reasoning strategies. The findings emphasize the need to improve both retrieval mechanisms and reasoning capabilities.

Limitations body

The unit describes limitations of FRAMES as a comprehensive evaluation framework for RAG systems. It highlights pretraining data contamination as a concern because information in the dataset may have been seen by large language models during pretraining, inflating performance metrics and weakening measures of true generalization. It also notes that the dataset may not represent the full range of real-world queries and scenarios. Future iterations should consider newer or synthetic data, contamination measurement methods, and broader coverage to improve evaluation robustness and reliability.

Ethical Considerations body

The developers of FRAMES recognize the importance of addressing ethical considerations in retrieval-augmented generation research. These considerations include possible biases in information sources, safeguards against misuse of advanced RAG systems, and the environmental impact of computationally intensive approaches. Researchers using FRAMES are encouraged to explore efficient, accessible implementations and maintain methodological transparency. The research community is invited to engage in ongoing dialogue about these issues.
