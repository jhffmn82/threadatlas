Medical Graph RAG: Towards Safe Medical Large Language Model via Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Junde Wu, Jiayuan Zhu, Yunli Qi, Jingkun Chen, Min Xu, Filippo Menolascina, Vicente Grau · 2024-10-15 · 9 sections
"Medical Graph RAG: Towards Safe Medical Large Language Model via Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation" by Junde Wu, Jiayuan Zhu, Qi Yang, Chen, Jingkun, Xu, Min, Menolascina, Filippo, Grau, Vicente (2024), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2408.04187

This document presents MedGraphRAG, a graph-based retrieval-augmented generation framework for safer, more reliable medical large language model responses. Authored by Junde Wu, Jiayuan Zhu, Yunli Qi, Jingkun Chen, Min Xu, Filippo Menolascina, and Vicente Grau, it addresses medical LLM limitations including restricted context, specialized terminology, insufficiently verifiable evidence, and the need to handle private medical data safely. MedGraphRAG builds medical knowledge graphs from documents through semantic chunking, entity extraction, and relationship linking. Its Triple Graph Construction connects user documents to credible RepoGraph medical sources and controlled-vocabulary definitions, producing Meta-MedGraphs. These graphs are summarized with hierarchical medical tags. Its U-Retrieval process then uses top-down precise retrieval, entity-neighbor retrieval, and bottom-up response refinement by a response LLM, aiming to provide traceable, evidence-based answers with source documentation and definitions. The work evaluates the system against standard RAG, GraphRAG, and no-retrieval baselines using Llama2, Llama3, Gemini-pro, and GPT-4. It uses MIMIC-IV as RAG data, MedC-K and evidence publications as upper repository data, and the UMLS graph as bottom repository data. Across nine medical Q&A benchmarks, two health fact-checking benchmarks, and a long-form generation dataset, MedGraphRAG improves accuracy, particularly for smaller models, and reaches new state-of-the-art results with larger models. Human assessments find its long-form answers more pertinent, correct, citation-supported, and understandable. Ablations show gradual gains from Med-MetaGraph, Triple Graph Construction, and U-Retrieval, with especially large improvements from Triple Graph Construction. Comparisons adding Med-Paper and Med-Dictionary data indicate that both retrieval method and data matter, with reported gains exceeding 6% and 8%. The document situates the framework among medical LLMs, RAG, citation systems, and GraphRAG, arguing that existing approaches insufficiently support attributed medical responses and term explanations. It concludes that MedGraphRAG is effective, while future work should support real-time updates and validation on real-world clinical data.

Abstract body

MedGraphRAG is a graph-based Retrieval-Augmented Generation framework designed for the medical domain to help Large Language Models generate evidence-based medical responses and handle private medical data more safely and reliably. It introduces Triple Graph Construction, which links user documents with credible medical sources and controlled vocabularies, and U-Retrieval, which combines Top-down Precise Retrieval with Bottom-up Response Refinement. The framework was evaluated on 9 medical Q&A benchmarks, 2 health fact-checking benchmarks, and a collected long-form generation dataset. MedGraphRAG consistently outperformed state-of-the-art models and produced responses with credible source documentation and definitions.

Introduction body

The unit examines challenges faced by large language models in medicine, including limited context windows, specialized terminology, and the need for verifiable evidence. It introduces Medical GraphRAG, a medical-domain graph-based retrieval-augmented generation method that uses Triple Graph Construction and U-Retrieval to improve evidence-based responses. MedGraphRAG links user data to medical papers and dictionaries, supports traceability, and retrieves information through hierarchical medical tags. Evaluations on medical Q&A and health fact-checking benchmarks, along with clinician assessments, report improvements over classic RAG, GraphRAG, and many specifically trained medical LLMs.

Method / Medical Graph Construction / Step1: Semantic Document Chunking .. Step6: U-Retrieval body

MedGraphRAG constructs medical knowledge graphs from documents, organizes them with tag summaries, and retrieves information to answer user queries. Its medical graph construction process includes semantic document chunking, entity extraction, triple linking to RepoGraph sources and definitions, and relationship linking into Meta-MedGraphs. Graph tagging summarizes Meta-MedGraphs with predefined medical tags and hierarchically clusters related summaries. U-Retrieval combines Top-down Precise Retrieval, entity-neighbor retrieval, and Bottom-up Response Refinement through a response LLM.

The experiment evaluates MedGraphRAG against standard RAG, GraphRAG, and no-retrieval baselines across biomedical question-answering and public-health fact-checking datasets using Llama2, Llama3, Gemini-pro, and GPT-4. It uses MIMIC-IV as RAG data, MedC-K and evidence publications as upper repository data, and the UMLS graph as bottom repository data. MedGraphRAG improves accuracy across the evaluated datasets, with especially pronounced gains for smaller language models, and achieves new state-of-the-art results when applied to larger models. Human evaluation also finds that MedGraphRAG produces more pertinent, correct, citation-supported, and understandable long-form responses, while an ablation study attributes gains to Med-MetaGraph, Triple Graph Construction, and U-Retrieval.

Analysis / Ablation Study based on GraphRAG .. Which is important? Data or Method body

The ablation study evaluates GraphRAG, Med-MetaGraph, Triple Graph Construction, and U-Retrieval on the same RAG dataset to isolate methodological effects. Adding the proposed modules produces gradual performance improvements, with especially large gains from Triple Graph Construction and further gains from U-Retrieval. A separate comparison examines RAG, GraphRAG, and MedGraphRAG with external RAG data, showing that data and retrieval method must work together. MedGraphRAG achieves strong improvements as Med-Paper data and Med-Dictionary data are added, including gains of over 6% and 8%, respectively.

Related Work / LLM for Medicine .. Retrieval-augmented generation body

This unit reviews medical applications of large language models (LLMs), including BioGPT, PMC-LLaMA, BioMedLM, and Med-PaLM 2. It notes that prompt engineering offers a cost-efficient, non-fine-tuned approach, while retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is less explored in medicine and lacks support for evidence-based responses and term explanations needed in clinical settings. The unit describes RAG as improving accuracy and reducing hallucinations by allowing models to use specific datasets without additional training. It also discusses citation-generating systems and GraphRAG, which enhances complex reasoning through knowledge graphs but lacks features for attributed responses and has limited medical application.

Conclusion body

The conclusion concerns MedGraphRAG, a graph-based retrieval-augmented generation framework for medical response generation. It uses Triple Graph Construction and U-Retrieval to improve evidence-based, context-aware responses and performs strongly on benchmarks and human evaluations. Future work will address real-time data updates and validation on real-world clinical data.
