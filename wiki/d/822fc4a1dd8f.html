HyPA-RAG: A Hybrid Parameter Adaptive Retrieval-Augmented Generation System for AI Legal and Policy Applications

Abstract written by a model

This document presents Hybrid Parameter-Adaptive Retrieval-Augmented Generation (HyPA-RAG), an RAG system for high-stakes AI legal and policy question answering. It focuses on the reliability problems of Large Language Models, including hallucinations, outdated knowledge, and retrieval failures, and uses New York City Local Law 144 (LL144) of 2021 as an AI Legal and Policy Corpus for compliance retrieval and interpretation. HyPA-RAG combines adaptive parameter selection with BM25 sparse retrieval, vector retrieval, reciprocal rank fusion, optional query rewriting and reranking, and knowledge graph retrieval. Its related Parameter-Adaptive RAG (PA-RAG) version omits the knowledge graph. The system uses a fine-tuned DistilBERT Query Complexity Classifier to select query rewrites and top-k retrieval settings based on question complexity. Evaluation uses synthetic, expert-reviewed questions spanning simple, complex, situational, comparative, vague, and rule-conclusion forms, alongside RAGAS measures and adapted correctness assessment. After comparing chunking approaches, the study adopts sentence-level chunking for later experiments, although pattern-based chunking leads on several metrics. PA-RAG generally improves over a fixed-k baseline, especially in faithfulness and absolute correctness, while HyPA-RAG yields more mixed results; its reported 2-class version provides only modest correctness improvement over fixed-k. Ablations show trade-offs: adaptive k alone gives the highest answer relevancy; adaptive k with query rewriting and reranking gives the highest faithfulness; and a configuration combining k, knowledge graph, another adaptive parameter, query rewriting, and reranking gives the highest correctness score. Overall, adaptive methods tend to improve faithfulness and answer quality, but reranking and knowledge graphs can help some measures while harming others. The work notes limits in evaluator dependence, knowledge graph construction, parameter mappings, classifier data, and baselines, and proposes broader human evaluation, improved retrieval and classification, human feedback, preference optimization, and iterative refinement. It stresses transparency, reproducibility, efficiency, and the need for domain-expert oversight: outputs are for research and not legal advice or decision-making. The project was supported by Holistic AI and the Center for Artificial Intelligence at University College London.

Abstract body

The text concerns Large Language Models (LLMs), Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), and AI legal and policy applications. It introduces Hybrid Parameter-Adaptive RAG (HyPA-RAG), which combines adaptive parameter tuning with dense, sparse, and knowledge graph retrieval. HyPA-RAG is evaluated using NYC Local Law 144 (LL144) and improves retrieval accuracy, correctness, faithfulness, and contextual precision. The work addresses the need for adaptable NLP systems in complex, high-stakes AI legal and policy applications.

Introduction body

The unit concerns Large Language Models and their use in specialised fields such as law and policy. It explains that hallucinations, outdated knowledge, and several RAG failure points limit the reliability of AI question-answering systems. The proposed HyPA-RAG system addresses these issues through adaptive parameter selection, a hybrid search system, and an end-to-end evaluation framework. HyPA-RAG is tailored to the AI policy domain and uses NYC Local Law 144 as its primary corpus.

Background and Related Work body

Recent advancements in large language models have expanded their use in law and policy for judgment prediction, document drafting, and contract analysis. Retrieval-Augmented Generation integrates external knowledge through indexing, retrieval, and generation, combining sparse and dense retrieval to improve specificity and accuracy. Advanced RAG methods, including hybrid retrieval, query rewriters, rerankers, knowledge graph retrieval, Self-RAG, and KG-RAG, address retrieval inaccuracies, improve comprehensiveness, and reduce hallucinations. Evaluation frameworks such as Ragas, Giskard, and ARES assess RAG performance using reference-free metrics, synthetic question-answering datasets, and prediction-powered inference.

System Design .. AI Legal and Policy Corpus body

The hybrid parameter-adaptive RAG system combines BM25, vector retrieval, reciprocal rank fusion, and a knowledge graph retriever to improve retrieval accuracy and response relevance. It can optionally use query rewriting and reranking before processing selected chunks and knowledge graph triplets within an LLM context window. The system has two variations: Parameter-Adaptive RAG without knowledge graph retrieval and Hybrid Parameter-Adaptive RAG with it. The AI Legal and Policy Corpus uses Local Law 144 (LL144) of 2021, enacted by the New York City Department of Consumer and Worker Protection, as a challenging corpus for evaluating AI compliance retrieval and interpretation.

Performance Evaluation / Dataset Generation .. Classifier Results body

The performance evaluation concerns an RAG system for AI policy and legal question-answering, using synthetic questions, human review, RAGAS metrics, and an adapted correctness evaluation. Dataset Generation introduces simple, complex, situational, comparative, complex situational, vague, and rule-conclusion questions, which are deduplicated and reviewed by human experts. The study compares sentence-level, semantic, and pattern-based chunking and adopts sentence-level chunking for later experiments despite pattern-based chunking achieving the strongest overall results on several metrics. It also develops a Query Complexity Classifier and evaluates fixed, parameter-adaptive, and hybrid parameter-adaptive RAG configurations, with fine-tuned DistilBERT achieving the best reported classifier F1 scores.

RAG System Architecture / Parameter-Adaptive RAG (PA-RAG) .. RAG Results body

This unit describes Parameter-Adaptive RAG (PA-RAG), which uses a fine-tuned DistilBERT model to classify query complexity and adapt query rewrites and top-k retrieval settings. It also presents Hybrid Parameter-Adaptive RAG (HyPA-RAG), which adds a knowledge graph representation of LL144 to address missing or fragmented context in legal-document retrieval. PA-RAG generally outperforms the fixed-k baseline, achieving the strongest faithfulness and improved absolute correctness, while answer relevancy remains similar or slightly lower. HyPA-RAG produces more varied results, with the reported 2-class configuration showing only a modest correctness improvement over a fixed-k configuration.

System Ablation Study body

The System Ablation Study evaluates adaptive parameters, bge-reranker-large, and a query rewriter in PA and HyPA RAG methods with 2-class and 3-class classifiers. Varying k alone achieves the highest Answer Relevancy, while the k, Q + reranker configuration achieves the highest Faithfulness. The k, Q + reranker configuration slightly lowers Absolute Correctness compared with k, Q configuration but improves Correctness Score. The k, K, S, Q + reranker configuration achieves the highest Correctness Score, although it does not lead in Faithfulness, Answer Relevancy, or Absolute Correctness.

Overall Results and Discussion body

The unit concerns an ablation study of adaptive methods and configurations of adaptive k in a 3-class setting. It compares fixed and adaptive parameters, including query rewrites, reranking, and knowledge graph components, using faithfulness, answer relevancy, absolute correctness, and thresholded correctness. Adaptive methods generally improve faithfulness and answer quality, while the combination of adaptive parameters with a reranker achieves the highest thresholded correctness. The results also show trade-offs in which reranking or a knowledge graph can improve some measures while reducing overall response quality or other correctness scores.

Limitations and Future Work body

The study identifies limitations in its evaluation, knowledge graph construction, parameter mappings, classifier training data, and baseline methods. Correctness evaluation relies on a single evaluator familiar with the policy corpus, while parameter mappings lack rigorous quantitative validation. The study proposes larger-scale human evaluation, improved retrieval and classification methods, and more granular parameter-selection categories. Future work includes integrating human feedback, fine-tuning the LLM with RLHF, RLAIF, or other preference optimisation methods, and refining the query rewriter and iterative answer refinement to improve performance metrics.

Ethical and Societal implications body

The unit concerns the deployment of the Hybrid Parameter-Adaptive RAG (HyPA-RAG) system in AI legal and policy contexts. It highlights ethical and societal concerns about the accuracy, reliability, and potential misinterpretation of AI-generated responses, especially because legal information is high stakes. The system promotes transparency and reproducibility through documentation of data generation, retrieval methods, and evaluation metrics, while adaptive retrieval strategies aim to reduce computational and environmental costs. The findings are intended for research purposes only, and HyPA-RAG outputs should not be used for legal advice or decision-making without domain expertise and oversight.
