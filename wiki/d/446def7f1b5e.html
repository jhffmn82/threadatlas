SEAKR: Self-aware Knowledge Retrieval for Adaptive Retrieval Augmented Generation

Zijun Yao · 2025 · 18 sections
"SeaKR: Self-aware Knowledge Retrieval for Adaptive Retrieval Augmented Generation" by Zijun Yao, Weijian Qi, Liangming Pan, Shulin Cao, Linmei Hu, Weichuan Liu, Lei Hou, Juanzi Li (2025), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.1312

This document is a long paper in the Proceedings of the 63rd Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics, titled “SEAKR: Self-aware Knowledge Retrieval for Adaptive Retrieval Augmented Generation,” by Zijun Yao, Weijian Qi, Liangming Pan, Shulin Cao, Linmei Hu, Weichuan Liu, Lei Hou, and Juanzi Li. It presents SEAKR, an adaptive retrieval-augmented generation system for answering natural-language questions with large language models. The paper begins by framing two central problems for adaptive retrieval: deciding when an LLM needs external knowledge and determining how retrieved knowledge should be incorporated. It situates the approach among retrieval-augmented generation, retrieval-augmented reasoning, and research showing that Transformer internal representations can reveal uncertainty or hallucination-related signals. SEAKR combines a search engine, an LLM, and a self-aware uncertainty estimator. During Chain-of-Thought-style iterative reasoning, it derives uncertainty from EOS-token hidden representations across sampled generations, using a Gram matrix and regularized Gram determinant. High uncertainty triggers retrieval. Retrieved snippets are then re-ranked according to their ability to reduce uncertainty, and for complex questions the system compares reasoning based on generated rationales with reasoning based on retrieved knowledge, selecting the less uncertain route for the final answer. The evaluation uses LLaMA-2-chat and BM25 retrieval over a 2018 English Wikipedia dump, without fine-tuning. It tests complex multi-hop QA on 2WikiMultiHopQA, HotpotQA, and IIRC, and simple QA on NaturalQuestions, TriviaQA, and SQuAD. SEAKR achieves the strongest overall complex-QA results, the best simple-QA F1 results on TriviaQA and SQuAD, and performance comparable to FLARE on NaturalQuestions. Ablations show that removing or changing uncertainty estimation, retrieval, re-ranking, or reasoning generally harms results. Further analyses compare LLM backbones and tune generation count, internal-state layer, and uncertainty threshold. A HotpotQA case study shows the system recognizing insufficient knowledge about Alejandro Jodorowsky and retrieving information for a comparison with Philip Saville. The paper concludes that self-aware uncertainty can guide both retrieval decisions and dynamic knowledge integration. It notes limits: dependence on internal model states, short-form QA evaluation, 20 pseudo-generations for uncertainty computation, models of at most 8 billion parameters, and possible future improvements in re-ranking. Ethical considerations emphasize improved factual correctness but acknowledge misuse risks; the authors plan to release code for transparency and risk monitoring.

Abstract body

The paper introduces Self-aware Knowledge Retrieval (SEAKR), an adaptive retrieval model for large language models (LLMs). SEAKR extracts self-aware uncertainty from LLM internal states, activates retrieval when uncertainty is high, and re-ranks retrieved knowledge snippets according to how much they reduce uncertainty. It also uses self-aware uncertainty to select reasoning strategies for complex tasks requiring multiple retrievals. Experiments on complex and simple Question Answering datasets show that SEAKR outperforms existing adaptive retrieval methods.

3 .. 3.4 body

This unit describes SEAKR, a system that combines a search engine, a large language model, and a self-aware uncertainty estimator for answering natural language questions. SEAKR uses Chain-of-Thought-style iterative reasoning, deciding when to retrieve external knowledge based on uncertainty and selecting knowledge that reduces that uncertainty. Its self-aware uncertainty estimator uses hidden representations of the EOS token, a Gram matrix, and a regularized Gram determinant to measure consistency across sampled generations. SEAKR concludes by comparing reasoning with generated rationales against reasoning with retrieved knowledge and generates the final answer using the less uncertain strategy.

This unit evaluates SEAKR against retrieval-augmented generation baselines on knowledge-intensive question answering tasks. It covers complex QA datasets requiring multi-hop reasoning, 2WikiMultiHopQA, HotpotQA, and IIRC, and simple QA datasets, NaturalQuestions, TriviaQA, and SQuAD. Using LLaMA-2-chat with BM25 retrieval over a 2018 English Wikipedia dump, SEAKR adaptively retrieves and integrates knowledge without fine-tuning. SEAKR achieves the strongest overall complex QA results and the best simple QA F1 scores on TriviaQA and SQuAD, while remaining comparable to FLARE on NaturalQuestions.

5 / 5.1 .. 5.4 body

This unit analyzes SEAKR through ablation studies, backbone-LLM comparisons, hyper-parameter searches, and a HotpotQA case study. Removing or replacing SEAKR's self-aware uncertainty estimation, retrieval, re-ranking, or reasoning components generally reduces performance on 2Wiki, HPQA, and NQ. SEAKR performs better with LLaMA-3 and alignment-tuned models, while its hyper-parameter search identifies preferred settings for generation count, internal-state layer, and uncertainty threshold. In the case study, SEAKR detects insufficient knowledge about Alejandro Jodorowsky and selects retrieved knowledge that supports the correct comparison with Philip Saville.

The paper proposes self-aware knowledge retrieval (SEAKR) for adaptive retrieval. SEAKR extracts self-aware uncertainty from LLMs’ internal states, uses it to decide when to invoke knowledge retrieval, and dynamically integrates retrieved knowledge. Experiments on complex and simple QA tasks show that SEAKR outperforms existing adaptive baselines.

Limitations body

The unit discusses limitations of SEAKR. SEAKR requires access to the internal states of large language models, is mainly evaluated on short-form question answering, and requires 20 pseudo-generations to compute a Gram determinant. Its evaluation is limited to models with no more than 8 billion parameters, including a comparison between LLaMA-2 and LLaMA-3, and its Self-aware Re-ranking component could eventually be surpassed by advanced information retrieval methods.

Ethical Considerations body

This unit discusses the ethical considerations and broader impact of SEAKR. SEAKR is described as a retrieval augmented generation system intended to improve the factual correctness and trustworthiness of LLMs. The text identifies potential misuse, including using its self-aware uncertainty estimator for deceptive model training and integrating LLMs with information retrieval systems to automate cyber manhunt. SEAKR is built on open-sourced LLMs, and its authors plan to release the code to support transparency and risk monitoring.
