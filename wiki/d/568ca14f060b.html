Toto's Merry Winter

Laura E. Richards · 1887 · 20 sections

This document is a winter-time animal tale centered on Toto’s cottage, where Toto, Bruin, the raccoon, the blind grandmother, Miss Mary, Cracker, and other animal friends live companionably. It opens on a snowy evening with supper in the warm kitchen and the grandmother beginning a story inherited from her own grandmother. Her tale follows Chop-Chin, who rescues his father Ly-Chee from an unjust death sentence by taking the Golden Dragon from its temple, using it to terrify Emperor Wah-Song, and winning pardons and rewards for them both. The cottage’s winter life then unfolds through domestic mishaps, visits, adventures, and stories. Mice raid the kitchen; a saved squash pie is eaten; Toto and a companion arrange for brown mice to move to the forest and attend a woodmouse wedding. The raccoon tells of the Useful Coal that reveals a thief to King Sligo, while Toto’s dancing lesson ends with Bruin sitting on the grandmother’s custard pie. Toto tells a cautionary comet story after seeing a comet, and Bruin and Toto aid Baldhead after he breaks his leg. When a robber invades the cottage, Miss Mary raises the alarm and the raccoon attacks him, escaping safely after being carried away. Stories within the story continue: Cracker tells of a cow pursuing the moon; Miss Mary tells of a princess limited to three sayings who outwits danger and marries a young king; and the hermit tells Eileen Macarthy’s tale. Eileen earns a jewel-producing charm by freeing a Green Man, is exploited by Dr. Michael O’Shaughnessy, escapes, and learns that kind words and deeds are the true pearls and diamonds; the doctor is punished, while Eileen prospers and marries the Count of Kilmoggan. The hermit also becomes part of the winter circle, and Bruin shows mercy to the hungry Ger-Falcon after capturing him. Near spring, the household holds its merriest, and understood to be last, great party. In April the forest animals prepare to leave the cottage, but Bruin cannot leave Toto or the grandmother. Toto finds him hiding in the wood-shed, brings him back, and Bruin remains permanently, while the friends continue their happy life between cottage and forest.

Toto's Merry Winter / CHAPTER I. body

On a snowy winter evening, Toto, Bruin, the raccoon, the grandmother, and their animal friends gather in the warm kitchen of Toto's cottage. Bruin prepares a hearty supper while Toto and the raccoon set the table, and the grandmother joins them with the squirrel on her shoulder. The animals live together happily, although the woodchuck sleeps through the winter in a cupboard. After supper, the group asks the grandmother for a story, and she begins one said to have been told to her by her own grandmother.

Chop-Chin learns that his father, Ly-Chee, has been sentenced to death after accidentally soaking the Emperor's clothing with a bucket of water. He disguises his purpose by claiming to carry Tong-Ki-Tcheng's prayers and drugged wine to the great temple of the Golden Dragon. After the guards and the priests of the Saki-Pan fall asleep, Chop-Chin removes the Golden Dragon from its shrine and carries it out of the Temple of the Golden Dragon.

Ten o'clock of a dark night. body

Chop-Chin secretly carries the repaired Celestial Footstool into the royal palace, hides inside the Golden Dragon, and frightens Emperor Wah-Song into obeying the Dragon's commands. The Emperor orders a free pardon and rewards for Ly-Chee, including his appointment as Chief Sweeper and Chop-Chin's appointment as Second Sweeper. The Golden Dragon is returned to the Temple of the Saki-Pan, while Ly-Chee is publicly honored and Wah-Song becomes meek after his terrifying dream.

CHAPTER III. body

The grandmother's family discusses her story, while Miss Mary describes her father Polacko, a parrot who escaped from China. At bedtime, the grandmother sends the family to sleep, and Bruin secures the cottage before falling asleep on the hearth-rug. Two mice, Squeak and Scrabble, enter the kitchen, eat squash pie, accidentally consume hot ginger, and cool themselves in a basin. They mistake the sleeping Bruin for a black hill, flee when he moves and yawns, and return to their cellar.

CHAPTER V. body

The raccoon tells the family the story of the Useful Coal, which helps King Sligo expose the High Cellarer as the thief of his treasure and is made into a black diamond in the king's crown. Afterward, Toto conducts a dancing-school lesson for Cracker, the raccoon, and Bruin. The lesson becomes chaotic when Bruin falls, Cracker waltzes with Miss Mary, and Bruin accidentally sits on the grandmother's custard pie. Bruin concludes that waltzing is not his forte and declares that the lesson will be his last.

" CHAPTER VI. body

Toto, Cracker, and the raccoon attend the wedding of the woodmouse's eldest son in a cave filled with woodland creatures. Mr. Woodmouse, Senior, welcomes the large visitors, while the bride and groom endure the guests' intimidating presence. A star-nosed mole performs the marriage ceremony, after which the wedding party dances and shares a lavish feast of nuts, berries, roots, bark, gum, corn, and root-beer. The bride and groom leave for their new home, and the guests depart after a final dance.

CHAPTER VII. body

Toto, <DW53>, and Cracker see a comet while walking home and discuss what comets are. Toto then tells them the story of The Naughty Comet, Short-Tail Comet No. 73, who disobeys the Comet Master's orders and is devoured by the Sun. The Comet Master sends No. 1 Express to find the runaway meteor and take it to the planet Bungo for punishment. The story is meant to teach the other comets to obey their orders.

CHAPTER VIII. body

Cracker tells Bruin and the blind grandmother that Baldhead has broken his leg and is trapped without food in his cave. Bruin and Toto bring food, medicine, and bandages, and Jim Crow leads them to the cave. Bruin's attempts to help frighten Baldhead, who faints, but Pigeon Pretty revives him and explains Bruin's kindness. Baldhead then welcomes Bruin, Toto, and Jim Crow, and they all laugh together.

CHAPTER IX. body

A robber enters the cottage kitchen and begins stealing food and silver while the grandmother naps. Miss Mary calls out "Stop thief!", and the raccoon attacks the robber, while the kettle spills boiling water over his legs. The robber flees down the road carrying the raccoon, who later returns safely. Miss Mary and the raccoon thank each other, while Cracker helps restore the kitchen and learns that the kettle may be alive.

CHAPTER X. body

Toto returns home with Jim Crow, a crow he has brought for a visit, and the household animals greet and humorously question him. After supper, Cracker tells a story about a young cow who believes her calf Bossy has been taken to the moon and becomes a moon-calf. The cow leaps toward the moon, overshoots it, and is never seen again. Toto jokes about the story, while the raccoon praises it and asks for nuts.

" CHAPTER XI. body

Miss Mary tells the story of a beautiful princess who can speak only three remarks: “What is the price of butter?”, “Has your grandmother sold her mangle yet?”, and “With all my heart!” The princess uses the first two remarks to frighten a butterman impostor posing as king and a band of robbers, receiving bags of rubies and sapphires from them. At a palace, she meets a young king whom she loves and finally answers his marriage proposal, “With all my heart!”

The hermit, recovering in his cave, plays backgammon with Bruin and discusses winter amusements, reading, and hibernation. Bruin captures Ger-Falcon after the hawk pursues Pigeon Pretty, but the hermit and Pigeon Pretty persuade him to spare the starving bird and feed him broth. Ger-Falcon explains the decline of his once-honored family and agrees to stay with the hermit through the winter, while the crow reluctantly accepts his presence. Toto brings news and provisions from the cottage, and the hermit agrees to tell Toto, Bruin, and Pigeon Pretty an Irish fairy story.

" And settling himself comfortably on his moss-pillows, the hermit began the story of-- CHAPTER XIII. body

Eileen Macarthy enters the forest to gather sticks and frees a tiny Green Man from a spider’s web. The Green Man grants Eileen’s wish that a diamond and a pearl fall from her mouth whenever she speaks, but warns that the charm can be removed at the toadstool with holly and the words “Slanegher Banegher, Skeen na Lane!” When Eileen returns to the Macarthy cottage, her family mistakes the jewels for stolen property or evidence of bewitchment. Dr. O’Shaughnessy claims the jewels are only glass and takes Eileen to his house to attempt a cure.

Eileen Macarthy is taken by Dr. Michael O'Shaughnessy to his house, where he confines her in the best room and forces her to speak so that diamonds and pearls will fill it. After three exhausting days, Eileen distracts Mrs. O'Shaughnessy with a long story, escapes, and returns to the forest to find the Green Man. The Green Man removes the charm and explains that kind words are pearls and kind deeds are diamonds. Dr. O'Shaughnessy's wish to become King of Ireland is not fulfilled, though the Green Man says he has come into his kingdom by that time.

Dr. Michael O'Shaughnessy returns home dreaming of becoming King Michael and borrowing Queen Victory's crown. Mrs. O'Shaughnessy tells him that Eily has disappeared, and he finds the room filled not with jewels but with toads, snakes, newts, and other reptiles. The reptiles pursue him across County Kerry for thrice three days before he collapses in a bog. Eily returns home with an apron full of pearls and diamonds, is believed, and later marries the young Count of Kilmoggan.

CHAPTER XIV. body

Toto gathers eggs from Mrs. Speckle and Dame Clucket to help prepare a party at the cottage. The cottage is decorated, the grandmother and animals prepare an abundant supper, and the old hermit arrives with a crow and a hawk. The guests eat, play games, and enjoy music and dancing, while the hermit reports that spring is approaching. Although everyone calls it their merriest gathering yet, they know it is their last.

CHAPTER XV. body

In April, Toto and the animals prepare to leave the little cottage and return to the forest after spending the winter together. The squirrel, wood-pigeon, raccoon, and woodchuck depart, but Bruin secretly remains because he cannot bear to leave Toto or the blind grandmother. Toto discovers Bruin in the wood-shed, where he has been splitting far more kindling than necessary, and brings him back to the cottage. Bruin settles permanently into cottage life, while he, Toto, the grandmother, and their forest friends continue to share happy days between the cottage and the forest.
