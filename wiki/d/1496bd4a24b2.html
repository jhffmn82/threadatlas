DRAGIN: Dynamic Retrieval Augmented Generation based on the Information Needs of Large Language Models

Weihang Su, Yichen Tang, Qingyao Ai, Zhijing Wu, Yiqun Liu · 2024 · 18 sections
"DRAGIN: Dynamic Retrieval Augmented Generation based on the Real-time Information Needs of Large Language Models" by Weihang Su, Yichen Tang, Qingyao Ai, Zhijing Wu, Yiqun Liu (2024), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.acl-long.702

This paper presents DRAGIN, a lightweight dynamic retrieval-augmented generation framework intended to reduce factually incorrect but plausible output from large language models (LLMs). Rather than retrieving once from the initial input or using fixed intervals, recent text, or uncertainty alone, DRAGIN identifies information needs during generation across the full context, deciding both when retrieval should occur and what query should be issued. DRAGIN has two central components. Real-time Information Needs Detection (RIND) determines when to retrieve by combining token entropy, maximum attention from following tokens, and a binary semantic indicator for stopwords. Query Formulation based on Self-attention (QFS) then selects highly attended preceding tokens at the detected position to construct a query. Retrieved documents are incorporated through a prompt after the model truncates its output, and generation continues; this process can repeat when another need is detected. The paper contrasts this approach with single-round and multi-round methods including REPLUG, UniWeb, RETRO, ICRALM, IRCot, and FLARE, emphasizing limitations of fixed-interval and uncertainty-based retrieval. Experiments evaluate DRAGIN on 2WikiMultihopQA, HotpotQA, IIRC, and StrategyQA, covering multihop reasoning, reading comprehension, and commonsense reasoning. Comparisons include wo-RAG, SR-RAG, FL-RAG, FS-RAG, and FLARE. Using LLaMA-2-Chat, Vicuna-13B-v1.5, Llama2-13b-chat, and Llama2-7b-chat, BM25 retrieval over Wikipedia passages, and greedy decoding, DRAGIN generally delivers the strongest results, particularly on multihop reasoning tasks. Analyses attribute substantial gains to retrieval timing and query formulation, find DRAGIN relatively efficient in retrieval calls, and report that BM25 outperforms SGPT in the tested dynamic setting. The paper concludes that RIND and QFS enable superior dynamic RAG performance across the benchmarks. Its main limitation is dependence on Transformer self-attention scores, preventing use with APIs that do not expose them. The work reports no personally identifiable information or manually annotated datasets, releases models, data, and code on GitHub, and acknowledges support from Quan Cheng Laboratory under Grant No. QCLZD202301.

Abstract body

The text introduces DRAGIN, a framework for dynamic retrieval augmented generation based on the information needs of Large Language Models (LLMs). It addresses when to activate the retrieval module and what query to use during text generation. Unlike existing methods that rely on static rules and recent text, DRAGIN considers information needs across the entire context. Experiments on four knowledge-intensive generation datasets show that DRAGIN achieves superior performance on all tasks.

Introduction body

The unit concerns large language models (LLMs), their tendency to produce factually incorrect but plausible text, and retrieval-based methods for mitigating this problem. It explains that traditional Retrieval-Augmented Generation uses single-round retrieval, while dynamic RAG retrieves multiple times during generation by deciding when and what to retrieve. The authors introduce DRAGIN, a lightweight framework that uses Real-time Information Needs Detection (RIND) to determine when retrieval is needed and Query Formulation based on Self-attention (QFS) to determine what to retrieve. Experiments on four knowledge-intensive generation benchmarks show that DRAGIN outperforms existing dynamic RAG frameworks and achieves state-of-the-art performance.

2 / 2.1 .. 2.2 body

This unit reviews single-round and multi-round retrieval-augmented methods for large language models. Single-round retrieval augmentation uses the initial input to retrieve external information, while multi-round methods retrieve information during generation for complex tasks. It discusses methods including REPLUG, UniWeb, RETRO, ICRALM, IRCot, and FLARE, noting the limitations of fixed-interval or uncertainty-based retrieval. The unit also illustrates the DRAGIN framework, which detects information needs during generation and formulates retrieval queries, using an Einstein example.

QFS formulates retrieval queries by selecting the highest-attended preceding tokens at the detected position. The unit describes experiments evaluating DRAGIN and other retrieval-augmented generation methods on 2WikiMultihopQA, HotpotQA, IIRC, and StrategyQA. It specifies dataset settings, prompt templates, answer-generation procedures, and evaluation metrics for multihop reasoning, reading comprehension, and commonsense reasoning. It compares DRAGIN with wo-RAG, SR-RAG, FL-RAG, FS-RAG, and FLARE, focusing on when retrieval occurs and how queries are formed. Experiments use LLaMA-2-Chat and Vicuna-13B-v1.5, BM25 retrieval over Wikipedia passages, and greedy decoding.

5 / 5.1 .. 5.5 body

The unit reports experiments evaluating DRAGIN and several baselines on 2WikiMultihopQA, HotpotQA, StrategyQA, and IIRC using Llama2-13b-chat, Llama2-7b-chat, and Vicuna-13b-v1.5. DRAGIN generally achieves the strongest performance, especially on the multihop reasoning datasets, while its retrieval timing and query formulation contribute substantially to its results. The experiments also compare retrieval-call efficiency, RIND thresholds, and BM25 versus SGPT, finding that DRAGIN is relatively efficient and BM25 outperforms SGPT in the tested dynamic RAG setting.

6 body

The unit presents DRAGIN, a dynamic RAG framework for addressing the real-time information needs of LLMs during text generation. DRAGIN integrates RIND for timely retrieval activation and QFS for precise query formulation. The framework significantly outperforms existing dynamic RAG methods across various knowledge-intensive benchmarks.

7 body

The paper acknowledges limitations concerning its reliance on the self-attention mechanism of Transformer-based LLMs. This mechanism is used for Real-time Information Needs Detection (RIND) and Query Formulation based on Self-attention (QFS). Although self-attention scores are accessible for all open-source LLMs, the method cannot be applied to certain APIs that do not provide access to those scores, motivating future work on overcoming this constraint.

The research prioritizes ethical considerations throughout its conduct to support the responsible development and application of AI technologies. It does not rely on personally identifiable information or manually annotated datasets. The researchers value open research and the scientific value of reproducibility, and they have publicly released the models, data, and code associated with the paper on GitHub. This transparency enables community verification and encourages use of the methods in other contexts.
