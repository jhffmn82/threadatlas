This document is a 2024 NAACL Human Language Technologies Industry Track paper, “Optimizing LLM Based Retrieval Augmented Generation Pipelines in the Financial Domain,” by Yiyun Zhao, Hanoz Bhathena, Prateek Singh, Saket Sharma, Bernardo Ramos, Aviral Joshi, and Swaroop Gadiyaram of JPMorgan Chase & Co.’s Machine Learning Center of Excellence. It studies Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) for financial question answering, aiming to ground large language model outputs in current, domain-specific material. The authors benchmark six LLMs, including GPT-4, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and Llama-2 models, over 15 retrieval conditions, nine prompts, and two banking datasets: webpages and policy guides. These datasets pair real user questions with relevant document chunks and human-labeled answer spans. The evaluation measures answer quality with token F1 and measures instruction following through structured formatting and direct quotation. Retrieval conditions vary gold-document availability, document number, ranking, and Retriever-Only versus Retriever-W-GT settings. The study first establishes that generative LLMs generally beat extractive baselines, while showing that missing relevant material, more distractors, and lower-ranked relevant documents degrade answers. Its policy-guide experiments further show that retrieving the gold document helps, but that models remain vulnerable to noise, ordering, and document count. GPT-4 performs best overall; GPT-4 and GPT-3.5-Turbo are more robust to prompt changes and more accurate at structured-output instructions than Llama-2 models. The paper concludes that RAG design should prioritize retrieval recall to avoid pseudo-helpful answers without relevant evidence, while also improving precision through re-rankers or fine-tuned retrievers. It stresses that optimization must consider document ordering, not recall alone. For smaller Llama-2 models, it recommends simple instructions. The work states compliance with the ACL Code of Ethics and says user data was anonymized.

Abstract body

The study investigates Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) for grounding large language model generations in up-to-date and domain-specific knowledge. It benchmarks 6 LLMs across 15 retrieval scenarios and evaluates 9 prompts on 2 real-world financial domain datasets. The study examines how the retrieval pipeline, prompts, and generation models affect answer generation quality. It also provides specific recommendations for designing RAG systems.

1 body

This unit examines Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines for Question Answering in the specialized financial domain, using two datasets from the banking sector containing real user queries. It evaluates six LLMs, including GPT-4, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and Llama-2 models, across different retrieval qualities, prompts, and answer-generation settings. The study finds that generative LLMs generally outperform extractive baselines, but answer quality declines when relevant information is missing, distractor documents increase, or relevant documents are ranked lower. GPT-4 performs best overall, while GPT-4 and GPT-3.5-Turbo show stronger instruction compliance and greater resilience to prompt variations than Llama-2 models.

2 / 2.1 .. Evaluation Metrics and Aspects body

The Experiment Framework evaluates six LLMs across 15 retrieval conditions, nine prompts, two datasets, and two performance aspects: answer quality and instruction following ability. The datasets concern Banking webpages and Banking policy guides, with user questions paired to relevant document chunks and human-labeled answer spans. The study manipulates retrieval quality by varying whether a gold document is present, the number and order of retrieved documents, and uses Retriever-Only and Retriever-W-GT conditions. It evaluates answer generation models using token F1 scores and assesses structured formatting and direct quoting as instruction-following measures.

3 / 3.1 .. 3.4 body

This unit examines how retrieval conditions, generation models, prompts, and instruction-following affect answer generation quality on the Banking Policy guides dataset. Performance generally improves when the gold document is retrieved, but models remain sensitive to retrieval noise, document count, and document order. GPT-4 and GPT-3.5-turbo outperform the Llama-2 models, show greater prompt robustness, and follow structured-output instructions more accurately. The results indicate that retrieval optimization should consider more than recall, including document ordering and re-ranking.

4 body

The investigation examines how components of a RAG pipeline influence generation quality. It recommends prioritizing retrieval recall because LLMs may exhibit pseudo-helpfulness when relevant gold documents are not retrieved, and improving retrieval precision through re-rankers or fine-tuned retrievers. The study finds that RAG systems are sensitive to distractors and that vendor LLMs from OpenAI outperform smaller-scale open-source alternatives in sensitivity to prompting, overall quality, and instruction following for domain-specific use cases. For smaller-sized Llama-2 models, it recommends using simple instructions.
