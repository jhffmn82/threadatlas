Hippolytus; The Bacchae

Euripides · 427 BC · 11 sections

Abstract written by a model

The document contains summaries of two tragedies, HIPPOLYTUS and THE BACCHAE, each centered on a god’s punishment of mortals who reject or dishonor divine power. In HIPPOLYTUS, Aphrodite resolves to punish Hippolytus because he honors Artemis and refuses Aphrodite. Phaedra, Hippolytus’s stepmother, wastes away from a secret, unwanted love for him. After the Nurse draws out and then betrays Phaedra’s secret, Hippolytus angrily rejects the accusation and swears silence. Phaedra hangs herself but leaves a tablet that makes Theseus believe Hippolytus violated her. Theseus curses and exiles his son; Hippolytus is then fatally injured when a supernatural Sea-Bull terrifies his horses and wrecks his chariot. Artemis reveals Phaedra’s false accusation and Aphrodite’s role. Hippolytus forgives Theseus before dying, and Artemis promises him lasting honors and vengeance against Cypris. In THE BACCHAE, Dionysus arrives at Thebes to establish his worship, vindicate Semelê, and punish those, including Pentheus, who deny his divinity. He has driven the women of Thebes, including Agave, into ecstatic mountain worship. Cadmus and Teiresias support Dionysus, but Pentheus condemns the rites, arrests the stranger who is Dionysus, and resists repeated warnings. Dionysus escapes imprisonment, while reports show the Bacchae performing wonders and violent acts. He then deceives Pentheus into dressing as a woman to spy on the worshippers on Kithaeron. There, Dionysus causes Agave, Ino, Autonoë, and the Maenads to kill Pentheus. Agave returns carrying her son’s head, believing it a lion’s, until Cadmus forces her to recognize the truth. Dionysus declares the catastrophe punishment for rejected divinity and foretells exile and transformation for Cadmus and his family. Both works end in grief, ruined households, and reflections on divine justice exceeding human understanding.

HIPPOLYTUS body

Aphrodite declares that she will punish Hippolytus for rejecting her and honoring Artemis instead. Hippolytus returns from the hunt, worships Artemis, and refuses to honor Aphrodite except from afar. Phaedra, wasting from a secret illness and love for Hippolytus, is brought outside by the Nurse, and the Nurse eventually draws the truth from her. Phaedra reveals that her grief resembles the destructive loves of her mother and sister, while the Nurse condemns Aphrodite for bringing ruin upon the household.

CHORUS body

Phaedra explains to the women of Troezen that, after realizing she loved Hippolytus, she tried silence, self-control, and finally resolved to die rather than shame her husband, children, and herself. The Nurse argues that love is a divinely imposed force and secretly attempts to help Phaedra, but Hippolytus overhears the disclosure, condemns women and the Nurse, and swears to remain silent only because of his oath. Phaedra blames the Nurse for betraying her and decides that death is the only remaining way to preserve some honour, while also resolving to bring Hippolytus down with her. The Chorus mourns Phaedra's fate and foresees her hanging herself in the bridal room.

[_The Voice of the_ NURSE _is heard from within, crying, body

Phaedra is found dead by hanging in the Castle, and Theseus returns to discover her body and the tablet she left behind. The tablet leads Theseus to accuse Hippolytus of violating Phaedra, curse him through Poseidon, and exile him from his realms. Hippolytus denies the charge, swears that he never touched Phaedra, and leaves Athens in grief after Theseus refuses to believe him. The Chorus mourns the collapse of the royal household and questions how good and evil receive their rewards.

[A HENCHMAN _enters in haste_.] body

A henchman tells Theseus that Hippolytus has been gravely injured when his terrified horses, driven by a supernatural Sea-Bull sent from the sea, wreck his chariot. Artemis reveals that Phaedra falsely accused Hippolytus after Cypris caused her love for him, and that Theseus killed his innocent son by invoking Poseidon’s curse. Hippolytus is brought in dying, forgives Theseus, and dies in his father’s arms. Artemis promises Hippolytus lasting honors in Trozên and vengeance against Cypris.

THE BACCHAE body

Dionysus comes to Thebes in human form to establish his worship and vindicate his mother, Semelê. He says that Hera's hatred caused Semelê's death and that Semelê's sisters have denied his divine birth, so he has driven them and the women of Thebes into ecstatic wandering. Dionysus accuses Pentheus of rejecting him and declares that Pentheus and Thebes will suffer until they recognize his divinity. He summons his Eastern followers to sing and then goes to lead the worshippers toward Mount Kithaeron.

CHORUS body

The Chorus praises Dionysus and describes the ecstatic rites, songs, dances, and mountain worship of his followers. Teiresias and Cadmus, dressed in ivy and fawn-skins, defend the new god, while Pentheus condemns the worship and orders Dionysus's followers and the visiting stranger to be arrested. Teiresias warns Pentheus that his resistance is madness and that Dionysus will become great in Greece. Pentheus rejects their counsel and begins persecuting Dionysus, while the Chorus prays for the god's protection and celebrates the release from care found in wine, dancing, and worship.

[_As the Chorus ceases, a party of the guards return, leading in the body

Dionysus is captured and brought before Pentheus, but he remains calm while Pentheus cuts his hair, takes his wand, and orders him imprisoned. Dionysus escapes when the god shakes Pentheus's Castle with an earthquake and sets fire to Semele's tomb, leaving Pentheus to attack an illusion. A Messenger reports that the wild women, led by Agave, have performed wonders, slaughtered cattle, resisted armed men, and ravaged neighboring villages. Pentheus rejects the warnings and orders his soldiers to assemble against the Bacchae.

DIONYSUS body

Dionysus overcomes Pentheus's resistance and persuades him to spy on the Bacchae while disguised as a woman. Pentheus is dressed in a rich trailing robe, a snood, a dappled fawn-skin, and given a wand; Bacchic madness clouds his judgment. Dionysus leads Pentheus toward Kithaeron and calls on Agave and the Cadmeian sisters to bring about his doom. The Chorus praises freedom from fear, Wisdom, Loveliness, and the enduring strength of the Spirit of God, while calling for Justice and the Wrath of the Lord against Pentheus.

[_Enter hastily a_ MESSENGER _from the Mountain, pale and distraught._] body

A Messenger reports that Pentheus was killed on Kithaeron by Agave, Ino, Autonoë, and the Maenads after Dionysus revealed him and commanded his punishment. Agave returns carrying Pentheus's head, believing it to be a lion's head, but Cadmus leads her to recognize the truth and shows her Pentheus's body. Dionysus then appears, declares that the suffering is punishment for rejecting his divinity, and announces the future exile and transformation of Cadmus and his family. Agave and Cadmus leave Thebes in grief, while the Chorus reflects that divine outcomes often differ from human expectation.
