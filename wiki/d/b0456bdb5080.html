Knowledge Graph-Guided Retrieval Augmented Generation

Xiangrong Zhu · 2025-04-29 to 2025-05-04 · 11 sections
"Knowledge Graph-Guided Retrieval Augmented Generation" by Xiangrong Zhu, Yuexiang Xie, Yi Liu, Yuanman Li, Wei Hu (2025), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.naacl-long.449

Abstract written by a model

This unit presents Knowledge Graph-Guided Retrieval Augmented Generation (KG2RAG), a retrieval-augmented generation framework intended to improve the factual reliability of large language model responses and reduce problems associated with hallucinations. It begins by identifying a weakness of ordinary semantic retrieval: retrieved document chunks may be redundant or only weakly connected. In contrast, knowledge graphs capture fact-level relationships among chunks, including across documents, and can support more diverse, coherent context. KG2RAG operates through offline document processing, KG-enhanced retrieval, and KG-based context organization. Documents are split into chunks and connected to a knowledge graph using extracted entities, relations, and triplets. For a query, semantic retrieval first selects seed chunks. Graph-guided expansion then retrieves chunks associated through connected entities, and a context-organization module filters and arranges the expanded material with maximum spanning trees into organized paragraphs for large language models. The unit situates KG2RAG among RAG methods, including sentence-window retrieval, KGP, and GraphRAG, distinguishing its use of factual associations for both expansion and organization. Experiments use HotpotQA and entity-shuffled variants. A graph built from 66,581 HotpotQA documents contains 211,356 extracted triplets linked to source chunks. Response and retrieval quality are evaluated with F1, precision, and recall. KG2RAG generally exceeds Semantic RAG, Hybrid RAG, LightRAG, and GraphRAG, particularly in fullwiki and shuffled settings. Ablations find that graph-guided expansion and context organization complement one another, and robustness tests cover top-k, expansion hops, and randomly dropped triplets. The conclusion emphasizes that the framework produces related, self-consistent, well-organized context. Its stated limitation is that it focuses on retrieval optimization rather than query rewriting or multi-turn dialogue, though it is compatible with other RAG modules. The work acknowledges support from the National Natural Science Foundation of China, grant 62272219.

It introduces Knowledge Graph-Guided Retrieval Augmented Generation (KG2RAG), which uses knowledge graphs to capture fact-level relationships between chunks. The unit describes KG2RAG, a retrieval-augmented generation workflow that combines document offline processing, KG-enhanced chunk retrieval, and KG-based context organization. Documents are split into chunks, which are linked to a knowledge graph through extracted entities, relations, and triplets. KG2RAG first uses semantic-based retrieval to select seed chunks, then performs graph-guided expansion to find related chunks through connected entities. Its KG-based context organization module filters and arranges the expanded chunks with maximum spanning trees before providing organized context to LLMs.

3 / 3.1 .. 3.3 body

The experiments evaluate KG2RAG against several retrieval-augmented generation baselines on HotpotQA and entity-shuffled variants. The study constructs a knowledge graph from 66,581 HotpotQA documents using Llama-3, linking 211,356 extracted triplets to their source chunks, and evaluates response and retrieval quality with F1 score, precision, and recall. KG2RAG generally outperforms Semantic RAG, Hybrid RAG, LightRAG, and GraphRAG, especially in fullwiki and shuffled settings. Ablations show that KG-guided expansion and KG-based context organization complement each other, while KG2RAG remains robust to changes in top-k, graph-expansion hops, and randomly dropped triplets.

4 body

This unit reviews retrieval-augmented generation and approaches that use knowledge graphs to enhance large language model applications. It explains that typical retrieval-augmented generation systems retrieve relevant document chunks, while sentence window retrieval expands context using physically neighboring chunks. It presents KG2RAG as a method that uses factual associations among chunks, including chunks across documents, to expand retrieval and organize results into paragraphs. It also compares KG2RAG with KGP and GraphRAG, which use document-level or automatically extracted graph structures for retrieval expansion or query-focused summarization.

5 body

The conclusion presents KG2RAG, a framework for enhancing retrieval-augmented generation through integration with knowledge graphs. KG2RAG links chunks to a specific knowledge graph and uses retrieved seed chunks to guide chunk expansion and context organization. These processes produce diverse, related, self-consistent, and well-organized paragraphs for large language models. Comparisons and ablation studies show improved response and retrieval quality, with both modules contributing collaboratively to KG2RAG's effectiveness.

Limitations body

The unit discusses limitations of retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and the scope of KG2RAG. RAG can be refined through query rewriting, retrieval optimization, and multi-turn dialogue, while KG2RAG focuses only on retrieval optimization. KG2RAG performs KG-guided retrieval expansion and KG-based context organization using structured factual knowledge from knowledge graphs, and is described as compatible with other RAG modules.
