Retrieval Augmented Generation with Rich Answer Encoding

Wenyu Huang, Mirella Lapata, Pavlos Vougiouklis, Nikos Papasarantopoulos, Jeff Z. Pan · 2023 · 15 sections
"Retrieval Augmented Generation with Rich Answer Encoding" by Wenyu Huang, Mirella Lapata, Pavlos Vougiouklis, Nikos Papasarantopoulos, Jeff Z. Pan (2023), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2023.ijcnlp-main.65

“Retrieval Augmented Generation with Rich Answer Encoding,” by Wenyu Huang, Mirella Lapata, Pavlos Vougiouklis, Nikos Papasarantopoulos, and Jeff Z. Pan, presents a framework for Knowledge-Intensive Generation, where a system retrieves external passages and uses them to produce natural-language answers or conversational responses. The work targets hallucination and a weakness in conventional retrieval-augmented generation (RAG): training through retrieval marginalization can favor passages that overlap with the label answer rather than passages containing the underlying knowledge needed to produce it. This label-relevant bias can overfit retrievers and miss knowledge-relevant passages with little token overlap with an answer. The proposed RAG-based retrieve-generate model uses a Dense Passage Retriever and a BART encoder-decoder, augmented with two objectives. Retriever as Answer Classifier (RAC) trains the retriever to determine whether a retrieved passage contains knowledge expressed in the answer. Dense Knowledge Similarity (DKS) aligns question-passage representations with knowledge representations extracted from answers. Generation, answer-classification, and knowledge-similarity losses are jointly optimized with balancing weights; a multi-task variant combines the objectives. The paper situates this approach among knowledge-intensive generation and retrieval methods, including RAG, FiD, KG-FiD, KGI, RE2G, and Hindsight, while emphasizing false-negative passages, passage selection, and the need for robust retrievers. It evaluates retrieval, generation, and combined performance on MSMARCO generative QA and KILT Wizard of Wikipedia informative conversation, using MSMARCO’s 8.8M passages and about 24.5M passages from the 2019/08/01 Wikipedia dump. Metrics include recall, ROUGE-L, F1, BLEU, METEOR, R-Precision, and KILT scores. Results show improvements over RAG in retrieval and end-to-end generation. The multi-task method is strongest overall on the MSMARCO development set and on several MSMARCO metrics, while RAC obtains the highest KILT-WoW retrieval and end-to-end ROUGE-L results, and DKS achieves the highest combined scores and best KILT-WoW R-Precision. Smaller train-to-development drops indicate greater robustness. In a Gouda cheese example, RAC and DKS give knowledge-matched answers whereas RAG hallucinates goat’s milk. The authors conclude that RAC and DKS mitigate label-relevant overfitting, while noting fixed passage-encoder parameters and unavailable MSMARCO test data as limitations; future work may combine passages with structured knowledge.

Retrieval Augmented Generation with Rich Answer Encoding / Abstract body

The work, titled "Retrieval Augmented Generation with Rich Answer Encoding," concerns improving knowledge-intensive generation by incorporating answer information into retrieval-augmented generation. Wenyu Huang, Mirella Lapata, Pavlos Vougiouklis, Nikos Papasarantopoulos, and Jeff Z. Pan propose Dense Knowledge Similarity (DKS) and Retriever as Answer Classifier (RAC) to retrieve passages that are relevant to both questions and answers. They evaluate the approach on open domain question answering using MSMARCO and on conversation using Wizard of Wikipedia. The best models improve retrieval and generation metrics over a baseline, including Recall@1 and BLEU-4 on MSMARCO and R-Precision and KILT-RL on the KILT-WoW leaderboard.

Introduction body

The unit introduces Knowledge-Intensive Generation (KIG), which uses external knowledge sources to generate responses and addresses hallucination in Large Language Models. It explains that retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) retrieves passages before generating answers, but conventional training favors label-relevant passages over other knowledge-relevant passages. The proposed framework adds Retriever as Answer Classifier (RAC) and Dense Knowledge Similarity (DKS), using rich answer encoding to train retrievers to identify knowledge-relevant passages. Experiments on MSMARCO and Wizard of Wikipedia through the KILT benchmark report improvements in retrieval and generation performance over RAG.

Related Works body

This unit discusses knowledge-intensive generation, where models must access external knowledge and produce natural-language responses, and the hallucination problem affecting pretrained language models. It examines retrieval marginalization and the false-negative passage problem, especially in multi-document question tasks. It reviews retrieval augmented generation methods including RAG, FiD, KG-FiD, KGI, RE2G, and Hindsight, emphasizing their effects on retrieval quality and passage selection. It also describes retrieval augmented generation with LLMs, which uses retrieved passages or knowledge graph triples to improve factuality and highlights the importance of powerful, robust retrievers.

Problem Statement body

The unit concerns Knowledge-Intensive Generation, in which a question and a knowledge source are used to retrieve relevant passages and generate a natural language response. It explains that marginalizing the retrieval step during training biases the retriever toward label-relevant passages, causing it to overfit. The unit distinguishes these passages from knowledge-relevant passages, which contain the knowledge needed to generate the label answer and may have little token overlap with it. It proposes a training framework focused on knowledge-relevant passages to improve retriever robustness and generalization.

Methodology / Retrieve-Generate Backbone .. Overall Architecture body

The proposed model is a retrieve-generate framework for knowledge-intensive generation that adds Retriever as Answer Classifier and Dense Knowledge Similarity objectives to a RAG-based backbone. The retrieve-generate backbone uses a Dense Passage Retriever to select passages and a BART encoder-decoder to generate a label sentence. Retriever as Answer Classifier trains the retriever to identify whether retrieved passages contain knowledge expressed in an answer, while Dense Knowledge Similarity aligns question-passage representations with knowledge representations extracted from answers. The model jointly optimizes generation, answer-classification, and knowledge-similarity losses with balancing weights.

Experimental Setup / Datasets .. Comparison Models body

The experiments evaluate the proposed methods on generative question answering and informative conversations using the MSMARCO QA dataset and KILT-WoW. They assess retrieval, end-to-end generation, and combined performance with metrics including recall, ROUGE-L, F1, BLEU, METEOR, R-Precision, and KILT scores. The knowledge sources contain 8.8M MSMARCO passages and approximately 24.5M passages from the 2019/08/01 Wikipedia dump. On the MSMARCO development set, the multi-task method performs best overall, while on KILT-WoW, RAC achieves the highest retrieval and end-to-end ROUGE-L results and DKS achieves the highest combined scores.

Results / RQ1: Effectiveness / Generative QA .. Case Studies body

The unit evaluates RQ1: Effectiveness and RQ2: Robustness for Generative QA on MSMARCO and KILT-WoW. The proposed methods improve retrieval and end-to-end generation over RAG, with the multi-task model performing best on several MSMARCO metrics and DKS performing best on KILT-WoW R-Precision. The proposed methods also show smaller train-to-development performance drops, indicating better robustness. A Gouda cheese case study shows that RAC and DKS produce knowledge-matched answers, while RAG hallucinates that Gouda cheese is made from goat’s milk.

Conclusion body

The work introduces a training framework for retrieval augmented generation models that trains more robust retrievers using the knowledge-constrained objectives RAC and DKS. Evaluations on MSMARCO and KILT Wizard of Wikipedia show improvements over the baseline model in retrieval and generation metrics, including relative gains in Recall@1, R-Precision, KILT-RL, and KILT-F1. The authors find that focusing on label-relevant passages can cause overfitting and that their framework mitigates this issue. They identify fixed passage-encoder parameters and the lack of access to the MSMARCO test set as limitations, and propose combining passages with structured knowledge for future retrieval augmented generations.
