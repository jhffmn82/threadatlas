Evidence-Driven Retrieval Augmented Response Generation for Online Misinformation

Zhenrui Yue, Huimin Zeng, Yimeng Lu, Lanyu Shang, Yang Zhang, and Dong Wang · 2024 · 12 sections
"Evidence-Driven Retrieval Augmented Response Generation for Online Misinformation" by Zhenrui Yue, Huimin Zeng, Yimeng Lu, Lanyu Shang, Yang Zhang, Dong Wang (2024), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.naacl-long.313

Abstract written by a model

This unit presents retrieval augmented response generation (RARG), a system for producing timely, polite, factual counter-responses to online misinformation. It addresses limits of misinformation detection, social correction, and language-generation approaches: user or expert corrections may not scale or include facts, while language models can lack fine-grained knowledge and hallucinate. RARG therefore grounds generation in retrieved scientific evidence. Given a misleading claim, RARG searches a database of more than 1 million academic publications. Its two-stage retrieval pipeline uses BM25 to select a small document subset, then a dense retriever, fden, to rerank evidence. For COVID-19, evidence comes from CORD and LitCovid; the dense retriever is fine-tuned with Check-COVID and additional sampled documents. The retrieved claim-evidence material is passed to fgen, the response generator. Generation begins with supervised fine-tuning, producing an initial and reference model. Human-feedback-based reward models assess refutation, factuality, politeness, claim relevance, and evidence relevance. Reinforcement learning with PPO then updates the actor model, while a KL-divergence penalty keeps it close to the reference model. The resulting actor generates evidence-based responses from the claim and collected documents. Experiments focus on COVID-19 misinformation. The retrieval component outperforms sparse and dense baselines on Check-COVID, and the response component performs strongly on MisinfoCorrect, Constraint, and ANTiVax in both in-domain and cross-domain settings. The RLHF-tuned version generally improves response quality and reportedly outperforms state-of-the-art methods, producing factual, polite, relevant counter-responses. The unit also identifies limits: performance may weaken in domains substantially unlike COVID-19; unreliable evidence can cause inaccurate outputs; and no human evaluation of overall response quality was conducted because of limited scope and budget. Future work aims for a more generalized, domain-adaptive system and improved evaluation.

Abstract body

The unit concerns online misinformation and the development of retrieval augmented response generation for online misinformation (RARG) to produce counter-misinformation responses. RARG retrieves and reranks evidence documents from a database containing over 1 million academic articles, then uses large language models to generate responses based on the evidence. Its response generation is aligned through reinforcement learning from human feedback, with a reward function that promotes evidence use and text quality. Experiments focused on COVID-19 found that RARG outperformed baselines on in-domain and cross-domain datasets.

Introduction body

The unit concerns the growing spread of online misinformation and the need for timely, evidence-based intervention. It introduces retrieval augmented response generation (RARG), a framework that retrieves scientific evidence from over 1M academic publications and generates polite, factual counter-responses. RARG uses a two-stage retrieval pipeline and aligns large language models through reinforcement learning from human feedback. Experiments on COVID-19 misinformation evaluate RARG in both in-domain and cross-domain settings, where it reportedly outperforms state-of-the-art methods.

Related Work / Detecting and Countering Misinformation body

The unit reviews methods for detecting misinformation and countering its spread. Misinformation detection is categorized into content-based detection, which classifies claims using input content and sometimes additional modalities, and evidence-based detection, which uses external knowledge to verify claims. Social correction by users or experts is common but may lack politeness and supporting facts and may not scale to large volumes of misinformation. Language-based methods generate counter-responses or explanations, but often neglect factuality, motivating a counter-misinformation response generation framework that collects relevant documents for response reasoning and generation.

Retrieval Augmented Generation body

Recent progress in large language models has improved language understanding and generation, but these models still struggle with fine-grained knowledge and often hallucinate. Retrieval augmented generation incorporates up-to-date knowledge by generating text conditioned on retrieved documents, though current methods mainly target knowledge-intensive NLP tasks. REALM is an example that retrieves documents such as Wikipedia for conditional generation tasks like question answering. The work described explores retrieval augmented response generation using scientific evidence and RLHF alignment to produce evidence-based counter-responses to online misinformation.

Methodology / Preliminary .. Response Generation body

The methodology concerns counter-misinformation response generation using RARG, which retrieves scientific evidence for a misleading claim and generates an evidence-based response. Its two-stage evidence retrieval pipeline first uses BM25 to select a small subset of documents and then applies a dense retriever for reranking. The response generation model is trained end-to-end and further optimized with reinforcement learning toward refutation, factuality, politeness, claim relevance, and evidence relevance. For COVID-19 evidence, RARG uses academic articles from CORD and LitCovid and fine-tunes its dense retriever with Check-COVID and additional sampled documents.

opening body

The unit describes how RARG optimizes fgen to generate counter-misinformation responses from an input claim and retrieved evidence. It first applies supervised fine-tuning to create an initial generation model and reference model, then uses human-feedback-based reward models to evaluate refutation, factuality, politeness, and relevance. Reinforcement learning with PPO updates the actor model while a KL-divergence penalty limits its deviation from the reference model. After training, the actor model generates responses using the input claim and collected evidence.

Experiments / Experiment Design .. Response Generation Results body

The unit evaluates RARG, an evidence-driven retrieval-augmented response generation framework for countering online misinformation. Its evidence retrieval component combines BM25 with fden and outperforms sparse and dense retrieval baselines on the Check-COVID dataset. Its response generation component uses retrieved documents and performs strongly on the MisinfoCorrect, Constraint, and ANTiVax datasets, including in-domain and cross-domain evaluations. RARG produces factual, polite, relevant counter-responses, and its RLHF-tuned version generally improves generation quality.

Limitations body

The unit describes limitations of RARG for evidence-based counter-response generation against online misinformation. It notes that test domains differing substantially from COVID may hinder deployment in more generalized applications, while unreliable evidence sources may introduce inaccuracies into generated responses. Human evaluations were not conducted to assess the overall response quality of RARG because of limited research scope and budgets. The authors plan to develop a more generalized and domain-adaptive solution with an improved evaluation protocol.
