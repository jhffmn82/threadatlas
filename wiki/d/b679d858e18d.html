FACTS About Building Retrieval Augmented Generation-based Chatbots

Rama Akkiraju, Anbang Xu, Deepak Bora, Tan Yu, Lu An, Vishal Seth, Aaditya Shukla, Pritam Gundecha, Hridhay Mehta, Ashwin Jha, Prithvi Raj, Abhinav Balasubramanian, Murali Maram, Guru Muthusamy, Shivakesh Reddy Annepally, Sidney Knowles, Min Du, Nick Burnett, Sean Javiya, Ashok Marannan, Mamta Kumari, Surbhi Jha, Ethan Dereszenski, Anupam Chakraborty, Subhash Ranjan, Amina Terfai, Anoop Surya, Tracey Mercer, Vinodh Kumar Thanigachalam, Tamar Bar, Sanjana Krishnan, Jasmine Jaksic, Nave Algarici, Jacob Liberman, Joey Conway, Sonu Nayyar and Justin Boitano · 2024-07-10 · 12 sections

Abstract written by a model

This paper presents NVIDIA’s practitioner-oriented approach to building secure, effective, and cost-efficient enterprise chatbots using large language models (LLMs) and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). It traces enterprise chatbots from fragile dialog-flow systems toward conversational enterprise search enabled by Chat-GPT, vector databases, and RAG, while stressing that enterprise deployments require fresh knowledge, access controls, personal-information protection, and optimization of quality, speed, and cost. The central contribution is the FACTS framework: Freshness, flexible Architectures, Cost economics, Testing, and Security. It is grounded in NVIDIA’s experience with the NVBot platform and three bots: NVInfo Bot, which answers questions over about 500 million enterprise documents while enforcing document permissions; NVHelp Bot, for IT help and HR benefits using about 2,000 multimodal documents; and Scout Bot, for financial earnings using about 4,000 public multimodal documents. The discussion begins with freshness, explaining that frozen foundation models lack enterprise-specific and current knowledge and may hallucinate. RAG addresses this, but retrieval quality depends especially on metadata enrichment, chunking, query rephrasal, and reranking. Hybrid search, agentic architectures, multimodal handling, fine-tuning choices, permissions, evaluation, and ongoing RAGOps monitoring also matter. The paper identifies fifteen RAG pipeline control points. It then describes NVBot’s modular, pluggable architecture, designed to avoid duplicated work in authentication, guardrails, interfaces, monitoring, security, and evaluation. It supports different models, databases, agents, and evaluation frameworks, as well as citizen development, specialized bots, enterprise-wide bots, copilots, and multibot orchestration. On economics, the paper compares large commercial LLMs with smaller and open-source models, finding that smaller models can often offer comparable accuracy with lower cost and latency. It discusses an internal LLM Gateway for auditing, subscriptions, cost control, and vendor-API security, alongside NVHelp Bot evaluations and TensorRT-LLM optimization. Finally, it covers testing and security. Chatbots need security, prompt-change, and regression testing; representative ground-truth data; feedback loops and RLHF; and human oversight because LLM-as-a-judge can reinforce bias. Security requires access control, governance, sensitive-data protection, classification, filtering, and pre- and post-processing controls. NVIDIA adopted Nemo Guardrails because prompt-only guardrails were inconsistent. The paper closes with future work on agentic architectures, enterprise-data summarization, auto-ML optimization, and evaluation of subjective responses and conversations.

FACTS About Building Retrieval Augmented Generation-based Chatbots front matter

ABSTRACT body

The unit concerns enterprise chatbots powered by generative AI and the engineering required to make them effective and secure. It presents the FACTS framework, developed from NVIDIA’s experience building chatbots for IT and HR benefits, company financial earnings, and general enterprise content. The framework focuses on content freshness, architectures, cost economics of LLMs, testing, and security, while the paper also identifies fifteen RAG pipeline control points. Empirical results examine accuracy-latency tradeoffs between large and small LLMs using enterprise data.

INTRODUCTION body

The unit describes the evolution of enterprise chatbots from fragile, dialog-flow-based systems to LLM- and retrieval-augmented systems that can support conversational enterprise search. It explains that Chat-GPT, vector databases, and retrieval augmented generation helped enable chatbots that understand natural-language intent, retrieve fresh content, and synthesize coherent responses. NVIDIA shares lessons from building enterprise chatbots, including the need to engineer RAG pipelines, manage enterprise knowledge and document access control permissions, protect personal information, and optimize quality, speed, and cost. The paper presents the FACTS framework and fifteen control points of RAG pipelines as practitioner-oriented approaches to building effective, secure, and cost-efficient enterprise generative AI-based chatbots.

CASE STUDY body

NVIDIA has built three bots using retrieval-augmented generation systems (RAGs) and large language models (LLMs) on its in-house NVBot platform. NVInfo Bot answers questions about approximately 500 million enterprise documents while enforcing document access controls. NVHelp Bot focuses on IT help and HR benefits using approximately 2,000 multimodal documents, and Scout Bot answers questions about financial earnings from approximately 4,000 public multimodal documents. The FACTS framework presents challenges and lessons learned from building these bots, beginning with the challenge of delivering fresh enterprise content.

ENSURING FRESHNESS OF ENTERPRISE .. 3.1 body

This unit concerns ensuring fresh enterprise data in LLM-powered chatbots through Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG), RAG control points, and ongoing RAGOps monitoring. It explains that foundation models can lack enterprise-specific knowledge, remain frozen after training, and hallucinate when answering questions about enterprise content. The unit identifies metadata enrichment, chunking, query rephrasal, and query reranking as especially influential on retrieval relevancy and chatbot answer quality, while hybrid search and agentic architectures improve entity matching and complex-query handling. It also discusses fine-tuning decisions, multi-modal data handling, document access control permissions, and evaluation practices for secure and accurate chatbot responses.

BUILDING FLEXIBLE ARCHITECTURES FOR body

NVIDIA developed the NVBot platform to support its growing ecosystem of generative AI chatbots and avoid duplicated work across security, guardrails, authentication, user interfaces, monitoring, and evaluations. NVBot uses a modular, pluggable architecture that lets developers select different models, databases, agents, and evaluation frameworks while sharing common platform components. The platform also supports citizen development and the coexistence and orchestration of multiple chatbots. NVIDIA is exploring domain-specific chatbots, an enterprise-wide chatbot, and copilots, including a multibot approach in which enterprise-wide chatbots direct inquiries to specialized bots.

COST ECONOMICS OF CHATBOT body

The unit explains the cost economics of generative AI-based chatbot deployments, focusing on model costs, data security, latency, and performance. It compares large commercial LLMs with smaller and open-source models, noting that smaller models can provide comparable accuracy, lower costs, and better latency for many use cases. It describes how an internal LLM Gateway can support data auditing, subscription management, cost control, and security when vendor APIs are used. NVIDIA’s NVHelp Bot evaluation and TensorRT-LLM optimization are cited as examples of model performance and inference improvements.

TESTING RAG-BASED CHATBOTS (T) .. 6.1 body

This unit concerns testing RAG-based chatbots and improving their safety, accuracy, and adaptability over time. It describes security testing, prompt change testing, regression testing, and the use of feedback loops and RLHF for continuous improvement. It cautions that LLM-as-a-judge evaluations may reinforce model biases and recommends supplementing automated evaluations with human oversight. The unit also emphasizes long test cycles, representative ground truth datasets, and systematic human feedback.

SECURING RAG-BASED CHATBOTS (S) .. 7.1 body

The unit explains how to secure RAG-based chatbots, especially internal enterprise chatbots, against security and derivative risks. It emphasizes enterprise content access control, sensitive data protection, data governance, and enterprise-specific guardrails. NVIDIA applied these practices in its NVInfo bot journey, including document sensitivity classification, sensitive data filtering, and pre- and post-processing controls. The unit also notes that Nemo Guardrails was adopted because LLMs did not consistently follow guardrails expressed only in prompts.

RELATED WORK body

The unit compares Our work with selected research on improving RAG quality across the FACTS dimensions of freshness, architecture, costs, testing, and security. It discusses failure points, pain points, domain adaptation, HYDE, FLARE, Self-refine, ReAct, RAGAS, ARES, and broader surveys of augmented generation. Our work contributes 15 control points, techniques for complex queries, testing and evaluation, document security, and reference architectures for agentic systems and a flexible chatbot platform. The unit concludes that the FACTS framework offers a distinctive perspective on building secure enterprise-grade chatbots.

CONCLUSIONS body

The paper presents an approach to developing effective RAG-based chatbots based on experiences building three chatbots at NVIDIA. It introduces the FACTS framework, which emphasizes content freshness, architecture, LLM cost management, testing, and security. The paper identifies fifteen critical control points in RAG pipelines and analyzes accuracy and latency trade-offs between large and small LLMs. It also highlights future work on agentic architectures, enterprise-data summarization, auto-ML optimization, and evaluation frameworks for subjective responses and conversations.
