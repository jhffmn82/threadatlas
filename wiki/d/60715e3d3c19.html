This is a 2024 article in Volume 15, Number 3 of the International Journal of Advanced Computer Science and Applications, titled “Retrieval-Augmented Generation Approach: Document Question Answering using Large Language Model.” Kurnia Muludi, Kaira Milani Fitria, Joko Triloka, and Sutedi of the Informatics Engineering Graduate Program at Darmajaya Informatics and Business Institute present a document question-answering system designed to reduce the time, errors, and resource demands of manual document processing and to limit hallucinations associated with relying only on large language models. After reviewing the evolution of QA methods, including semantic parsing, knowledge-based methods, dynamic memory networks, BiDAF, QANet, BERT-Base, T5, and RAG, the study selects GPT-3.5-turbo for implementation. The proposed system lets users upload external text documents, including multiple PDFs; it loads, splits, embeds, and retrieves document information through LangChain and FAISS, then supplies relevant context to ChatGPT through Retrieval-Augmented Generation. The authors introduce the DocuQA dataset and also test modified SQuAD 1.1 documents, comparing results with other commercial QA applications against ground-truth answers. Evaluation uses accuracy, ROUGE, BLEU, BERTScore, and Jaccard Similarity. The system achieves 96% accuracy on the authors’ dataset and 95.5% on the SQuAD development dataset, and exceeds the comparison application on ROUGE-L, BERTScore, BLEU precision, Jaccard Similarity, and reported precision, recall, and F1-Score measures. The article attributes these results to the RAG combination of LangChain, FAISS, and GPT-3.5. It concludes by proposing future work on external-data use, scalability, user feedback, continuous learning, real-time processing, and compatibility with varied document formats.
