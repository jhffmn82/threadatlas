Generate-then-Ground in Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Multi-hop Question Answering

Zhengliang Shi, Shuo Zhang, Weiwei Sun, Shen Gao, Pengjie Ren, Zhumin Chen, Zhaochun Ren · 2024 · 18 sections
"Generate-then-Ground in Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Multi-hop Question Answering" by Zhengliang Shi, Shuo Zhang, Weiwei Sun, Shen Gao, Pengjie Ren, Zhumin Chen, Zhaochun Ren (2024), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.acl-long.397

This is a long paper from the Proceedings of the 62nd Annual Meeting of the Association for Computational Linguistics, titled “Generate-then-Ground in Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Multi-hop Question Answering,” by Zhengliang Shi, Shuo Zhang, Weiwei Sun, Shen Gao, Pengjie Ren, Zhumin Chen, and Zhaochun Ren. It addresses Multi-Hop Question Answering, where answering requires information from multiple sources and multi-step reasoning. The paper argues that retrieve-then-read systems can be harmed by retrieval failures, noisy documents, and misleading content, while large language models can reason from stored knowledge but may hallucinate. The authors introduce GenGround, a generate-then-ground framework that alternates through iterations. First, answer deduction decomposes a complex question into fine-grained, simpler single-hop questions and generates answers using a large language model’s parametric knowledge. Next, instructional knowledge grounding retrieves documents, cites relevant evidence, and revises generated answers to correct errors. A batch grounding strategy supports this process. To extend the approach to smaller open-source models, the paper develops instructional grounding distillation: trajectories from ChatGPT are used to create synthetic training data. From 50,000 Natural Questions samples paired with ground-truth and noise documents, filtering produces 45,710 examples for training a student model to cite documents and revise incorrect immediate answers. Experiments evaluate GenGround on HotpotQA, MuSiQue, 2Wikimultihopqa, and StrategyQA, primarily using gpt-3.5-turbo and ColBERTv2, with Mistral-7B as a smaller-model comparison. Across F1, Accuracy, semantic accuracy, and human evaluation, GenGround reports the strongest main benchmark results and outperforms DSPy, GRG w/ dq, RetGen, and SearChain under several retrievers, including BM25 and Google Search. Removing deduction, grounding, or batch processing reduces performance. Distillation improves Mistral-7B, sometimes allowing it to approach or exceed ChatGPT-based baselines such as ReAct. Further analysis reports consistent repeated runs, high lexical similarity in trajectories, a 53.2% overall success rate and 5.6% error rate after revision, and lower token use than GRG w/ decomposition and RetGen. The paper concludes that GenGround effectively combines model knowledge and external documents, while acknowledging dependence on task/domain, difficult question decomposition, and risks from unreliable or incomplete documents.

This unit presents the title “Generate-then-Ground in Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Multi-hop Question Answering.” It concerns the Generate-then-Ground approach in Retrieval-Augmented Generation and its application to Multi-hop Question Answering. The listed authors are Zhengliang Shi, Shuo Zhang, Weiwei Sun, Shen Gao, Pengjie Ren, Zhumin Chen, and Zhaochun Ren, affiliated with institutions in China, the United Kingdom, and the Netherlands.

Abstract body

The abstract concerns Multi-Hop Question Answering and the difficulty it poses for large language models because it requires extensive knowledge. It introduces GenGround, a generate-then-ground framework that combines the parametric knowledge of large language models with external documents. GenGround alternates between generating answers to simpler single-hop questions and grounding those question-answer pairs in retrieved documents to correct errors. The abstract also presents instructional grounding distillation for extending the method to smaller models and reports superior results on four datasets.

Introduction body

The unit concerns Multi-Hop Question Answering (MHQA) and a proposed generate-then-ground framework for combining large language models with retrieved evidence. It explains that the retrieve-then-read paradigm decomposes complex questions, retrieves documents for sub-questions, and uses the documents to derive answers, but can be limited by retrieval failures and misleading content. The proposed generate-then-ground method first lets large language models generate answers using their world knowledge and reasoning, then grounds and revises those answers with retrieved documents. The authors also introduce instructional grounding distillation to help smaller models use this method, reporting strong results on four MHQA benchmarks.

2 / 2.1 .. 2.2 body

Multi-Hop Question Answering concerns questions that require information from multiple sources and multi-step reasoning. Existing methods use large language models to decompose questions and reason step by step, but these models can generate non-factual statements. Retrieval-augmented generation adds retrieved documents to support knowledge-intensive tasks, although retrieval noise and limited retriever accuracy can mislead models. The work proposes instructional knowledge grounding and a generate-then-ground framework to help large language models identify relevant evidence and better combine parametric knowledge with external documents.

3 .. 3.3 body

This unit describes GenGround, a framework in which LLMs alternate between answer deduction and instructional knowledge grounding over multiple iterations to answer multi-hop questions. Answer deduction breaks a complex question into fine-grained sub-questions and generates answers using the LLMs' stored world knowledge. Instructional knowledge grounding retrieves documents, cites evidence, and revises generated answers to address hallucinations. The unit also introduces a batch grounding strategy and grounding distillation for adapting the framework to smaller open source models.

4.1 .. 4.2 body

This unit describes how instructional grounding distillation constructs a synthetic training dataset from trajectories produced by LLMs, including ChatGPT, during instructional knowledge grounding. It samples 50k questions from the Natural Questions dataset, pairs each question with a ground-truth document and noise documents, and adds an immediate answer from a smaller model and a detailed revision trajectory from ChatGPT. After heuristic filtering, the resulting synthetic dataset contains 45,710 examples. The training objective uses a standard language modeling loss to train a student model to cite relevant document content and revise incorrect immediate answers.

5 / 5.1 .. 5.4 body

The Experimental Setup evaluates GenGround and multiple retrieval-based and non-retrieval-based methods on HotpotQA, MuSiQue, 2Wikimultihopqa, and StrategyQA. The study compares methods using F1, Accuracy, and semantic accuracy, with additional human evaluation on 120 sampled cases. Experiments use gpt-3.5-turbo as the main backbone, Mistral-7B for comparison, and ColBERTv2 primarily as the retriever. GenGround achieves the strongest reported results across the main benchmark metrics, while its ablations show declines when deduction, grounding, or batch processing is removed.

6 / 6.1 .. 6.3 body

The unit evaluates GenGround against retrieval and generation baselines across multiple datasets, retrievers, and model sizes. GenGround outperforms DSPy, GRG w/ dq, RetGen, and SearChain, including when BM25 or Google Search is used as the retriever. Experiments with Mistral-7B show that instructional grounding distillation further improves performance, including on HotpotQA and MusiqueQA. Ablations indicate that the answer deduction phase, instructional knowledge grounding phase, and batch grounding strategy each contribute to performance.

7 / 7.1 .. 7.3 body

The authors' framework is evaluated for consistency, knowledge incorporation, and efficiency on the HotpotQA dataset. Its repeated-run results show no statistically significant differences, and its answer deduction and instruction knowledge grounding trajectories exhibit high lexical similarity. The framework combines knowledge from large language models' parameters with external documents, achieving a 53.2% overall success rate and a 5.6% error rate after revision. It also consumes fewer tokens than GRG w/ decomposition and RetGen, while instructional grounding distillation enables Mistral-7B to approach or sometimes exceed strong ChatGPT-based baselines such as ReAct.

8 body

The unit presents GenGround, a generate-then-ground framework for multi-hop question answering. It combines the parametric knowledge of LLMs with external documents and alternates between simplifying a multi-hop question into a single-hop question and grounding the resulting question-answer pair in retrieved documents. The framework is extended to smaller models through instructional grounding distillation. Experiments on four datasets demonstrate the framework's superiority.

Limitations body

The unit describes limitations of the framework presented in the paper. It notes that generating an initial answer may be highly dependent on the task and that the model may struggle across diverse problem domains. It also identifies question decomposition as a challenging, insufficiently explored step and warns that unreliable or incomplete external documents may compromise the framework's effectiveness.

Ethics Statement body

The research conducted in this paper focuses on developing a generate-then-ground framework for multi-hop question answering. The framework enables Language Learning Models to alternate between deducing answers and using established evidence to revise them across several iterations. The study uses questions from existing benchmarks and Wikipedia as an open source corpus to support its retrieval system. The researchers state that they followed ethical standards and sought to avoid harm, deception, and potential misuse of information.
