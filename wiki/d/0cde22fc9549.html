Abstract written by a model

This document presents Fidelis Alu and Sunkanmi Oluwadare’s conceptual framework for an auditable, source-verified AI-powered clinical decision support system. It aims to make AI recommendations traceable to authoritative clinical guidelines and peer-reviewed evidence, while recording the patient inputs, retrieved sources, reasoning pathway, and outputs for later review. The authors frame the proposal around evidence-based medicine, clinician trust, U.S. Food and Drug Administration guidance, and the European Union AI Act. The paper first contrasts deterministic rule-based evidence systems with probabilistic large language model systems, emphasizing risks such as hallucinated or unstable citations. It argues that verified retrieval, provenance metadata, and complete audit trails should be embedded in AI inference. The proposed design has three main elements: a Data and Knowledge Layer containing curated medical evidence and provenance; a Retrieval and Reasoning Engine using semantic mapping and retrieval-augmented generation; and a User Interface with audit logging. A simulated suspected-sepsis case illustrates the framework: hypotension leads to a fluid-resuscitation recommendation linked to the Surviving Sepsis Campaign International Guidelines. The system would then create a forensic audit record using cryptographic hashes and a permissioned distributed ledger, Hyperledger Fabric. The discussion argues that verified sources, transparent citations, and logs could improve accountability, monitoring, and clinician confidence, but identifies major unresolved issues: knowledge-base curation, retrieval-augmented-generation design, privacy, usability, and biased evidence sources. Diversity-Audit and Evidence Equity are proposed to address unequal demographic representation in clinical evidence. The article ends by stressing that the system remains theoretical, with no new algorithm, prototype, implementation, or testing; simulation studies, clinician-centered trials, broader evaluation, and continuous monitoring are needed.

1 Introduction body

Alu and Oluwadare propose a conceptual framework for AI-powered clinical decision support that combines retrieval of curated medical evidence, source verification, and audit logging. The framework is intended to help clinicians verify AI-generated recommendations by linking claims to authoritative medical references and recording the data, sources, and reasoning used. The authors position the proposal within evidence-based medicine, U.S. Food and Drug Administration guidance, and the European Union AI Act, while identifying future simulation studies and clinical user trials as needed for evaluation.

2 Background and related work body

This unit reviews source verification, transparency, provenance, and auditability in clinical decision support, especially for large language model-based systems. It contrasts deterministic, rule-based Evidence-Based Medicine systems with probabilistic systems that face hallucination and unstable citation risks. It argues that the proposed framework embeds verified retrieval and provenance into inference while recording the full decision pathway for auditing. It also connects clinician trust, OpenEvidence, regulatory guidance, data provenance tools, audit logs, and blockchain-based mechanisms to the development of safer and more accountable clinical AI.

3 Methods (framework design) .. 3.3.4 Step 4: Forensic audit logging body

The unit presents a conceptual, future-oriented AI-CDSS framework designed to support trustworthy clinical decision-making through authoritative evidence, source-linked recommendations, and comprehensive audit logging. It describes a Data and Knowledge Layer, a Retrieval and Reasoning Engine, and a User Interface with Audit Logging, supported by semantic mapping, retrieval-augmented generation, and provenance mechanisms. The framework is illustrated through a clinical simulation involving suspected sepsis, hypotension, and a fluid resuscitation recommendation based on the Surviving Sepsis Campaign International Guidelines. The simulation concludes with forensic audit logging using cryptographic hashes and a permissioned distributed ledger, specifically Hyperledger Fabric.

4 Discussion .. 4.2 Bias mitigation and evidence equity body

The proposed system is a conceptual medical AI design that combines verified sources, transparent citations, and audit logging to support trustworthy clinical recommendations. The unit explains that clinical guidelines and other evidence sources could reduce unsupported outputs, while transparency and audit logging could improve trust, accountability, and monitoring. It also identifies limitations involving knowledge-base curation, RAG architectures, privacy, usability, and source bias, and proposes future implementation and evaluation. The discussion introduces Diversity-Audit and Evidence Equity as approaches to address unequal demographic representation in clinical evidence and improve bias mitigation.

5 Conclusion body

Alu and Oluwadare conclude that trustworthy AI for medicine requires careful design and propose a theoretical, auditable, source-verified AI-powered clinical decision support system. The proposed framework links recommendations to explicit medical evidence and logs reasoning steps so that claims and decisions can be traced and examined retrospectively. Although it has not yet been implemented or tested, the authors suggest that broader evaluation and continuous monitoring could help AI enhance rather than undermine medical decision making.
