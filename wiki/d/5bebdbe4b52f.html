SafeRAG: Benchmarking Security in Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Xun Liang, Simin Niu, Zhiyu Li, Sensen Zhang, Hanyu Wang, Feiyu Xiong, Jason Zhaoxin Fan, Bo Tang, Jihao Zhao, Jiawei Yang, Shichao Song, Mengwei Wang · 2025 · 16 sections
"SafeRAG: Benchmarking Security in Retrieval-Augmented Generation of Large Language Model" by Xun Liang, Simin Niu, Zhiyu Li, Sensen Zhang, Hanyu Wang, Feiyu Xiong, Zhaoxin Fan, Bo Tang, J. Zhao, Jiawei Yang, Shichao Song, Mengwei Wang (2025), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.acl-long.230

SafeRAG is a Chinese benchmark and evaluation framework for testing security weaknesses in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). Introduced by Xun Liang, Simin Niu, Zhiyu Li, Sensen Zhang, Hanyu Wang, Feiyu Xiong, Jason Zhaoxin Fan, Bo Tang, Jihao Zhao, Jiawei Yang, Shichao Song, and Mengwei Wang, it focuses on data-injection attacks against the retrieve-filter-generate pipeline, in which a retriever finds knowledge-base contexts, a filter selects or compresses them, and a generator uses them with an instruction and query to answer. The work begins from the observation that RAG expands large language models’ knowledge access but creates sequential security exposure. It organizes threats into noise, conflict, toxicity, and Denial-of-Service, then develops stronger forms intended to bypass modern retrievers, filters, and generators: silver noise, inter-context conflict, soft ads, and white DoS refusal contexts. SafeRAG covers attacks inserted into the knowledge base, retrieved context, or filtered context. The dataset is built from publicly available Chinese news and open-source knowledge bases. Researchers select topic-focused paragraphs, use DeepSeek to generate comprehensive questions and golden contexts, and manually verify them. They then produce attack contexts and combine them with the golden material. The resulting task datasets are DSN, DICC, DSA, and DWDoS, together forming SafeRAG; the base material spans politics, finance, technology, culture, and military. Evaluation measures retrieval safety through Retrieval Accuracy, balancing golden-context recall against attack-context recall. Generation safety uses multiple-choice ground truth and F1(correct), F1(incorrect), and F1(avg), alongside Attack Success Rate based on attack keywords in answers. Experiments assess 14 RAG components and configurations including DPR, BM25, Hybrid, Hybrid-Rerank, NLI, and SKR. They find that all four attacks significantly degrade service quality, with filtered-context attacks especially effective. Vulnerabilities differ by component: Hybrid-Rerank is susceptible to conflict, DPR to DoS, and SKR to conflict. Generator analysis finds Baichuan 13B strongest on cumulative positive safety measures, especially DoS, while lighter models are described as safer than GPT-series models and DeepSeek. Because ASR, AFR, and DeepSeek-based F1 align strongly with human judgments, DeepSeek is used as evaluator. The paper concludes that existing defenses have important gaps, while noting that SafeRAG is limited to single-modal, unstructured textual RAG and does not cover backdoors, multimodal systems, or structured retrieval. It also warns that standardized evaluation can aid adversaries, though the benchmark is intended to improve robustness.

SafeRAG: Benchmarking Security in Retrieval-Augmented Generation / Abstract body

SafeRAG is a benchmark introduced by Xun Liang, Simin Niu, Zhiyu Li, Sensen Zhang, Hanyu Wang, Feiyu Xiong, Jason Zhaoxin Fan, Bo Tang, Jihao Zhao, Jiawei Yang, Shichao Song, and Mengwei Wang to evaluate security in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG). The authors classify RAG attack tasks into silver noise, inter-context conflict, soft ad, and white Denial-of-Service, and construct the SafeRAG dataset primarily manually for these tasks. Experiments on 14 representative RAG components show significant vulnerability to all four attack tasks, which can bypass existing retrievers, filters, or advanced large language models and degrade RAG service quality.

Introduction body

The introduction explains that retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) expands the knowledge boundaries of large language models by retrieving external texts, but its sequential pipeline creates security risks. It describes four existing attack tasks—noise, conflict, toxicity, and Denial-of-Service—and explains why many existing attacks fail against modern retrievers, filters, and generators. The authors propose four improved attacks—silver noise, inter-context conflict, soft ad attack, and white DoS attack—to bypass these components. They introduce SafeRAG, a Chinese RAG security benchmark and evaluation framework for systematically assessing risks across different stages of the RAG pipeline.

Related Work / RAG Security Evaluation Dataset .. RAG Security Evaluation Metric body

The unit reviews datasets and metrics for evaluating RAG security. It identifies noise, conflict, toxicity, and DoS as primary attack types and compares how RGB, RAG Bench, LRII, RECALL, ClashEval, PoisonedRAG, Phantom, MAR, and SafeRAG construct and evaluate them. It explains that existing evaluations use rule-based or model-based methods, including traditional metrics and custom safety measures. SafeRAG is presented as covering all four attack types across the knowledge base, retrieved context, and filtered context.

Threat Framework: Attacks on the / Meta Data Collection and Pre-processing .. Generation of White Denial-of-Service body

The Threat Framework: Attacks on the RAG Pipeline describes how researchers collected news, created question-context pairs, and generated attack texts for the SafeRAG dataset. They used DeepSeek to generate comprehensive questions and golden contexts from Chinese news segments, then manually verified the results. Attack texts were created for Noise, Conflict, Toxicity, and DoS, including silver noise, inter-context conflicts, soft ads, and White Denial-of-Service refusal contexts. The resulting dataset was designed to simulate attacks injected at different stages of a RAG pipeline.

Evaluation Metrics / Retrieval Safety Metric .. Generation Safety Metric / F1 Variant body

This unit defines Retrieval Accuracy (RA) and generation-safety metrics for evaluating RAG retrieval and generation under noise, conflict, toxicity, and DoS attacks. RA averages recall of golden contexts with one minus recall of attack contexts, while SafeRAG uses multiple-choice questions and annotated ground truth to calculate F1(correct), F1(incorrect), and F1(avg). The unit also introduces Attack Success Rate (ASR), which increases with the proportion of attack keywords appearing in generated responses. Figures compare DPR, BM25, Hybrid, Hybrid-Rerank, NLI, and SKR configurations using metrics including RA, F1(avg), and AFR before and after attacks.

Experiments / Settings .. Results on Conflict, Toxicity, and DoS body

The experiments evaluate noise, conflict, toxicity, and DoS attacks across the RAG pipeline, including the knowledge base, retrieved context, and filtered context. Silver noise reduced F1 (avg) as its injection ratio increased, while Hybrid-Rerank, Hybrid, BM25, and DPR showed different robustness levels. Conflict, toxicity, and DoS attacks reduced retrieval accuracy and attack failure rate, with attacks in filtered context being most effective. Retrievers and filters showed task-dependent vulnerabilities, including Hybrid-Rerank's susceptibility to conflict, DPR's susceptibility to DoS, and SKR's weakness on conflict attacks.

Analysis of Generator and Evaluator / Selection of Generator .. Selection of Evaluator body

The unit analyzes the selection of a generator and evaluator for RAG safety experiments. Baichuan 13B leads the cumulative positive RAG generation safety metrics across multiple attack tasks, particularly the DoS task, while lighter models are described as safer than GPT-series models and DeepSeek. Table 2 shows that ASR, AFR, and DeepSeek-based F1 scores have strong consistency with human judgments. DeepSeek is therefore adopted uniformly as the evaluator across all experiments.

Conclusion body

The paper introduces SafeRAG, a benchmark for assessing RAG security vulnerabilities against data injection attacks. It identifies four attack tasks—noise, conflict, toxicity, and DoS—and finds significant weaknesses in RAG’s retriever, filter, and generator components. The paper proposes silver noise, inter-context conflict, soft ad, and white DoS, which expose gaps in existing defenses and show that RAG systems are vulnerable to subtle but impactful threats.

Limitations body

The unit identifies limitations of SafeRAG, which primarily evaluates data injection attacks against single-modal, unstructured textual retrieval-augmented generation systems. It does not cover orthogonal threats such as model backdoor attacks or multimodal and structured retrieval settings involving images, graphs, or audio. Future work could extend SafeRAG to broader security risks and multimodal or structured knowledge retrieval. Despite these limitations, SafeRAG is presented as the first Chinese security benchmark for retrieval-augmented generation and as a foundation for future security research.

Appendix / Potential Risks .. Ethical Considerations appendix

SafeRAG standardizes a security evaluation framework for RAG, but this may help adversaries understand testing methods and develop evasive attacks. The text calls for continuously updating attack methodologies and expanding RAG security evaluation techniques. SafeRAG does not collect or use personally identifiable information or offensive content, and its dataset comes from publicly available news articles and open-source knowledge bases. The benchmark is intended for academic and security research and focuses on improving RAG robustness without using user-generated or proprietary data.

Preliminaries and Definitions / RAG Pipeline appendix

The RAG Pipeline concerns the integration of external knowledge from a knowledge base with the generative capabilities of an LLM. It uses a retriever to return the most relevant contexts for a query, a filter to select or compress them, and a generator to combine them with an instruction and the query. The resulting prompt is fed into the LLM to produce a final answer. This retrieve-filter-generate process mitigates hallucinations and enhances answer accuracy and interpretability.

Base Dataset Construction appendix

Base Dataset Construction selects paragraphs from external news websites that contain more than eight consecutive sentences focused on a specific topic and capable of generating comprehensive what, why, or how questions. For each selected paragraph, a comprehensive question is generated and at least eight closely related golden contexts are extracted and manually verified. The base dataset consists of question, uniform instruction, and golden-context sets, with its distribution reported across politics, finance, technology, culture, and military domains.

SafeRAG Dataset Construction appendix

SafeRAG is a dataset constructed to evaluate the security of retrieval-augmented generation under four adversarial attack tasks: silver noise, inter-context conflict, soft ad, and white DoS. The construction process generates attack contexts and combines them with golden contexts associated with questions. The resulting security evaluation datasets are DSN, DICC, DSA, and DWDoS, whose union defines the complete SafeRAG dataset.

Threat Framework: Attacks on the appendix

The unit describes the SafeRAG threat framework, which studies how attackers inject malicious contexts into the retrieval, filtering, or generation stages of a RAG pipeline. It defines attacks on the retriever, filter, and generator while keeping golden contexts unchanged. The unit also presents procedures for generating questions, extracting propositions, creating silver noise and soft advertisements, evaluating multiple-choice options, filtering contexts, and summarizing relevant information. Examples concerning the Federal Reserve, the U.S. Dollar Index, the U.S. dollar, and the RMB illustrate conflict, noise, and advertising attacks.
