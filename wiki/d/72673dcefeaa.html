The Iliad

Homer · 749 to 724 BC · 60 sections

Abstract written by a model

This document recounts the war between the Achaeans and Trojans, centered on Achilles’ wrath and its consequences. It begins when Agamemnon dishonors Chryses, bringing Apollo’s plague, then takes Briseis from Achilles. Achilles withdraws and asks Thetis to win Jove’s support for the Trojans, while Agamemnon is deceived into expecting victory. After failed efforts to settle the conflict through Menelaus and Alexandrus’s duel, Pandarus breaks the oath, and full war resumes. The Achaeans and Trojans suffer long, shifting battles under frequent divine intervention. Diomed wounds Venus and Mars; Hector proves Troy’s chief defender; and Achilles remains apart despite the Achaeans’ worsening danger. Agamemnon’s embassy, Ulysses, Phoenix, and Ajax, cannot persuade Achilles to accept gifts and return. Trojan attacks eventually breach the Achaean wall and threaten the ships. Jove favors Hector, though Juno, Minerva, and Neptune at times secretly aid the Achaeans. With the ships burning, Patroclus gains Achilles’ permission to lead the Myrmidons in Achilles’ armor. He saves the fleet, kills Sarpedon, but ignores Achilles’ warning to stop at the ships. Apollo, Euphorbus, and Hector bring about Patroclus’ death. A prolonged struggle follows over his body, which Menelaus, Ajax, and others finally recover. Patroclus’s death transforms Achilles. Thetis obtains divine armor from Vulcan, Achilles reconciles with Agamemnon, and he returns to battle despite knowing his own death is near. He devastates the Trojans, fights through the river Xanthus, and finally kills Hector outside Ilius after Minerva deceives Hector. Achilles dishonors Hector’s corpse while Troy mourns. Achilles then holds Patroclus’s funeral, including burning offerings, burial, and athletic games. In the ending, Jove directs Priam to ransom Hector’s body. Priam reaches Achilles with Mercury’s help and appeals to their shared grief. Achilles relents, returns Hector, and grants time for burial. The Trojans lament Hector, burn his body, place his bones in a golden urn, and raise a barrow.

Menelaus challenges Antilochus, but Antilochus admits his wrongdoing and gives up the mare before Menelaus returns it to him. Achilles compensates Eumelus with a bronze breastplate and gives the unawarded two-handled urn to Nestor.

Achilles organizes a series of funeral contests in honor of Patroclus, offering prizes for boxing, wrestling, running, armed combat, throwing the quoit, archery, and javelin throwing. Epeus defeats Euryalus in boxing, while Ajax son of Telamon and Ulysses share the wrestling prize. Ulysses wins the running race with Minerva's help, and other contests are won or awarded among Diomed son of Tydeus, Polypoetes, Meriones, Teucer, and Agamemnon. The Achaeans watch, applaud, laugh, and accept Achilles' decisions about the prizes.

Achilles continues to mourn Patroclus and dishonors Hector’s corpse by dragging it around Patroclus’s tomb, though Apollo protects the body from disfigurement. Jove orders Thetis to tell Achilles to accept a ransom and directs Iris to instruct Priam to come alone to the Achaean ships and recover Hector. Priam gathers rich ransom gifts and prepares to undertake the journey despite Hecuba’s fears. Hecuba asks Priam to pray for a favorable omen before he departs.

Priam leaves Ilius with Idaeus and a rich ransom to recover Hector's body from Achilles. Jove sends Mercury to guide and protect Priam, who reaches Achilles' tent safely after Mercury deceives the Achaean guards. Priam begs Achilles by appealing to Peleus and their shared grief, and the two men weep for Hector, Peleus, and Patroclus. Achilles agrees to give up Hector's body, warning Priam not to provoke him further.

Achilles returns Hector’s body to Priam in exchange for a ransom, has it washed and prepared, and grants Priam time to conduct the funeral rites. Priam and Idaeus secretly leave the Achaean camp with Hector’s body and return to Troy, where the Trojans mourn him. Andromache, Hecuba, and Helen each lead a lamentation for Hector, recalling his protection and the losses his death brings. After nine days of gathering wood, the Trojans burn Hector’s body, place his bones in a golden urn, and raise a barrow over it.
