The Emerald City of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1910 · 33 sections

This document is an Oz fairy tale framed by L. Frank Baum’s note that children’s letters and ideas have helped shape the adventures of Dorothy, Aunt Em, Uncle Henry, and others. It begins with Roquat the Red, the Nome King, furious that Dorothy possesses his Magic Belt. Seeking revenge, he orders a secret tunnel dug beneath the Deadly Desert and sends General Guph to recruit the Whimsies, Growleywogs, and Phanfasms for an invasion of Oz. Meanwhile, Dorothy’s Kansas relatives face the loss of their farm. Dorothy asks Princess Ozma to bring Aunt Em and Uncle Henry to Oz, where Ozma gives them a permanent home and declares Dorothy a Princess of Oz. Wanting the older couple to have useful activity, Ozma sends them traveling with Dorothy, the Wizard, the Shaggy Man, and others. Their journey introduces them to many unusual places and peoples: the athletic college led by Professor H. M. Wogglebug, T.E.; the paper-doll Cuttenclips; the scattered Fuddles; Utensia’s living utensils; Bunbury’s edible inhabitants; Bunnybury’s rabbit kingdom; Rigmarole Town; and the fear-filled Flutterbudgets. Dorothy also helps the unhappy Rabbit King value his duty. During the journey, Ozma discovers through the Magic Picture that the Nome King’s tunnel is approaching the Emerald City. Dorothy’s companions, including the Tin Woodman, Scarecrow, and Jack Pumpkinhead, return to stand with Ozma. Although the invaders are immensely dangerous and Ozma refuses either to fight or flee, the Scarecrow devises a peaceful defense using the Forbidden Fountain’s Water of Oblivion. When the Nome King, Guph, the Nomes, and their treacherous allies emerge from the tunnel, they drink the water and forget who they are and why they came. Ozma sends the Nomes home under their king’s own forgotten orders, then uses the Magic Belt to return the remaining invaders safely to their lands. Finally, Glinda the Good protects Oz by making it invisible to all outsiders. In the closing message, Dorothy tells the writer that Oz is forever cut off from the rest of the world, and he wishes its people lasting happiness.

Author's Note body

L. Frank Baum explains that he has used many ideas sent in letters by children and therefore considers himself more an editor or private secretary than solely an author of fairy tales. He says children's suggestions have influenced what happens to Dorothy, Aunt Em, and Uncle Henry, as well as subjects for future stories. Baum believes children love the stories because they have helped create them, and he is proud of his partnership with them. He hopes the alliance will continue for a long time.

1. How the Nome King Became Angry body

Roquat the Red, the Nome King, is enraged because Dorothy took his Magic Belt, which he believes has reduced his power. His Chief Counselor, Chief Steward Kaliko, and General Blug all fail to satisfy or encourage him, and he orders the Counselor and General thrown away. General Blug explains that the Nomes cannot simply march across the deadly desert to the Land of Oz, so Roquat decides to build a secret tunnel under it and invade the Emerald City. Roquat intends to recover the Belt, destroy Oz and its Emerald City, enslave Princess Ozma and Dorothy, and take revenge, while Ozma and Dorothy do not know he remains a dangerous enemy.

2. How Uncle Henry Got Into Trouble body

Dorothy Gale lives with Aunt Em and Uncle Henry on a poor Kansas farm threatened by an unpaid mortgage. The banker gives Uncle Henry thirty days to pay or lose the farm, leaving the older couple afraid they cannot support Dorothy. Dorothy says Ozma can summon her to the Land of Oz, and she plans to go there while finding a way to help her aunt and uncle. At four o’clock, Dorothy makes the secret signal and disappears from her room, leaving Uncle Henry and Aunt Em unable to find her.

3. How Ozma Granted Dorothy's Request body

Dorothy Gale asks Ozma to let Uncle Henry and Aunt Em leave their failing Kansas farm and live with her in the Land of Oz. Ozma agrees to give them a comfortable place in Oz and invites Dorothy to live in the palace as her constant companion. Because Uncle Henry and Aunt Em may not believe in Oz and could become nervous, Ozma plans to transport them without warning using the Magic Belt. Their arrival is planned for the next morning, after rooms are prepared in the Emerald City of Oz.

4. How The Nome King Planned Revenge body

King Roquat the Red plans to conquer the Land of Oz, destroy the Emerald City, enslave its people, and recover his Magic Belt. He appoints Guph as General after General Crinkle refuses the mission and is taken to the torture chamber. Guph warns that Oz's magic powers make a direct attack impossible and proposes gathering evil creatures as allies. The Nome King orders Guph to arrange this alliance while he begins digging an underground tunnel to Oz.

5. How Dorothy Became a Princess body

Dorothy returns to the Emerald City, where the people warmly welcome her and Princess Ozma declares that she must assume her rightful rank as a Princess of Oz. Ozma uses the Magic Belt to transport Dorothy's Uncle Henry and Aunt Em from Kansas to the Emerald City. Although they arrive in their work clothes and are startled by the palace and its creatures, Ozma welcomes them as honored subjects and gives them a permanent home in the Land of Oz. Dorothy is delighted that her relatives will share in the happiness and comforts of Oz.

6. How Guph Visited the Whimsies body

General Guph, the new General of the Nome King's army, plans carefully to help the Nomes conquer the Land of Oz. Roquat the Red orders a large tunnel built beneath the desert from the Nome country toward the Emerald City so the Nome army can attack by surprise. Guph visits the Whimsies and promises that the Nome King will give them large natural heads if they help fight for him. The Whimsies accept the bargain and agree to join the Nome conquest of Oz.

7. How Aunt Em Conquered the Lion body

Dorothy shows Aunt Em and Uncle Henry their luxurious rooms in Ozma's palace and introduces them to the palace and its inhabitants. They visit Billina, who explains that her large chicken family lives safely and comfortably in the Land of Oz. While exploring the palace grounds, Aunt Em and Uncle Henry encounter the Cowardly Lion and frighten him with Aunt Em's fierce gaze. The Lion explains that he is Dorothy's friend, serves Ozma as a body guard, and remains frightened despite winning his battles.

8. How the Grand Gallipoot Joined The Nomes body

Guph crosses the Ripple Land and reaches the domain of the Growleywogs, where the Grand Gallipoot hears his proposal to help the Nomes conquer the Land of Oz. The Growleywogs agree to provide eighteen thousand warriors in exchange for twenty thousand people of Oz as slaves, while secretly planning to conquer the Nomes afterward. Guph is imprisoned and tortured before being released with the promise of assistance. Having secured the Whimsies and Growleywogs as allies, he decides to seek still more powerful support.

9. How the Wogglebug Taught Athletics body

Dorothy asks Ozma to find work for Uncle Henry and Aunt Em, so Ozma sends them on a journey through the Land of Oz with Dorothy and an escort. The party travels in a red wagon drawn by the wooden Sawhorse and visits the Royal Athletic College of Oz, directed by Professor H. M. Wogglebug, T.E. The college emphasizes sports, while students use the Wizard's School Pills to learn subjects such as algebra, geography, Latin, grammar, and spelling without ordinary study. After meeting the Wogglebug and seeing the college, the travelers continue their journey.

10. How the Cuttenclips Lived body

Dorothy, the Wizard, the Shaggy Man, and their traveling party visit the Cuttenclips, a village of living paper dolls ruled by Miss Cuttenclip. Miss Cuttenclip explains that Glinda gave her magic live paper, which makes the dolls she cuts out alive and able to think and talk. The visitors observe the village and its delicate inhabitants, but the Shaggy Man’s sneeze blows many paper dolls over and damages two houses. The party leaves after helping restore the dolls, while Miss Cuttenclip warns that visitors must avoid making breezes.

11. How the General Met the First and Foremost body

General Guph travels to the Mountain of Phantastico to persuade the Phanfasms to join the Nomes, Whimsies, and Growleywogs in conquering the Land of Oz. He reaches the First and Foremost Phanfasm and describes the Nome King's plan to plunder Oz and enslave its people. The Phanfasms agree to help because they enjoy making happy people unhappy, though they secretly intend to destroy the Nomes and their allies afterward. They plan to use King Roquat the Red's tunnel to lead the invasion of Oz.

12. How they Matched the Fuddles body

Dorothy and her fellow travelers visit Fuddlecumjig on their journey through the Land of Oz to see the peculiar Fuddles. The Fuddles are made of many painted pieces and scatter themselves whenever strangers approach, requiring others to match them together. The travelers assemble the cook, Lord High Chigglewitz, Grandmother Gnit, and several other Fuddles, then share a meal and continue their journey. The visit entertains the travelers, although Aunt Em questions what use the Fuddles are.

13. How the General Talked to the King body

General Guph tells Roquat the Red that the Whimsies, Growleywogs, and Phanfasms will join the Nome invasion of Oz. The Whimsies are promised large heads, the Growleywogs are promised Oz people as slaves, and the Phanfasms demand nothing because they want the pleasure of destroying the Oz people. Roquat worries that the Phanfasms and other allies may become dangerous, but Guph insists that the allies are needed to conquer Oz. The Nomes are drilling a tunnel through solid rock and expect it to reach the Emerald City in three days, while Guph plans to send the allies ahead so the Nomes will not face the eggs in Oz.

14. How the Wizard Practiced Sorcery body

Dorothy and the travelers leave Fuddlecumjig to find the Rigmaroles, following a southwest path that eventually ends in a meadow. The Sawhorse carries them across the meadow to a forest, where they camp for the night. The Wizard uses sorcery learned from Glinda to turn handkerchiefs into furnished tents and produce a hot supper and other food. The travelers eat well, and the dishes wash and dry themselves.

15. How Dorothy Happened to Get Lost body

A zebra brings a soft-shell crab to Dorothy and her companions to settle their dispute over whether the world contains more land or water, and the Wizard confirms that the crab is right about there being more water. The zebra and crab return to the forest after quarreling over the judgment. The next morning, Dorothy walks into the forest with Billina and Toto, loses her way, and encounters the Spoon Brigade. The spoons, servants of King Kleaver of Utensia, declare Dorothy and her companions their prisoners and begin taking them to their king.

16. How Dorothy Visited Utensia body

Dorothy, Billina, and Toto are brought by Captain Dipp and the Spoon Brigade into the Kingdom of Utensia as prisoners. King Kleaver convenes his Counselors, Courtiers, High Priest, and Judge to decide their fate, but the utensils cannot identify any wrongdoing. Judge Sifter orders Dorothy discharged because the only objection concerns the stove-hearth where she is sitting. Dorothy and her companions are released and return to the forest to search for their camp and for blackberries.

17. How They Came to Bunbury body

Dorothy, Billina, and Toto follow a path to Bunbury, a town inhabited by people made of buns, bread, crackers, and other baked foods. The Bunbury inhabitants allow Dorothy to eat discarded or spare objects, including a wheelbarrow, waffles, a piano, and milk, while C. Bunn shows her around the town. Trouble begins when Toto eats three Crumpets and a Salt-rising Biscuit, and Billina is accused of pecking out a Raisin Bunn's eyes. Mr. Bunn orders the travelers to leave and threatens to put them in the great ovens if they refuse.

18. How Ozma Looked into the Magic Picture body

Princess Ozma considers suitable occupations for Uncle Henry and Aunt Em while managing the affairs of the Land of Oz. She uses the Magic Picture to watch Dorothy and her companions at Fuddlecumjig, then looks for Roquat the Red and discovers an underground tunnel being dug toward the Emerald City. Ozma realizes that the Nomes are preparing to attack the Land of Oz and continues monitoring the tunnel construction each day. The tunnel steadily advances beneath the Deadly Desert toward the Emerald City.

19. How Bunnybury Welcomed the Strangers body

Dorothy and Billina return to the signpost and follow the other road to Bunnybury, where a white marble wall and guarded entrance block their way. The Keeper of the Wicket admits Dorothy after reading a letter from Ozma of Oz, but he requires her to leave Billina and Toto outside and reduce herself to the rabbits' size. Inside Bunnybury, Dorothy sees an enclosed city of white marble buildings, green lawns, richly dressed rabbit inhabitants, and a statue of Glinda the Good. The Keeper announces Dorothy's arrival, and the rabbits respectfully welcome her as she approaches the Royal Palace.

20. How Dorothy Lunched With a King body

Dorothy is welcomed to Bunnybury by the Rabbit King and has luncheon with him in his palace. The Rabbit King explains that he hates being forced to rule, wear royal clothing, and live in luxury instead of living freely in the forest. Dorothy promises to ask Glinda the Good to replace him as King, which briefly makes him extremely happy. He then prepares to entertain Dorothy and presents her to the nobility of Bunnybury.

21. How the King Changed His Mind body

The King of Bunnybury entertains Princess Dorothy with the Whiskered Friskers, his Bodyguard of Royal Pikemen, the Royal Jugglers, and the Winsome Waggish Warblers. Dorothy refuses to ask Glinda to let him take the performers or Bodyguard away, and she observes that he is the only rabbit unhappy in Bunnybury. After Dorothy prepares to leave, the King changes his mind about returning to the wild forest and promises to remain cheerful and do his duty by his subjects. Dorothy then leaves Bunnybury with Toto and Billina, and the Keeper of the Wicket credits her with helping the King appreciate ruling the city.

22. How the Wizard Found Dorothy body

Dorothy reunites with the Wizard, Aunt Em, Uncle Henry, the Shaggy Man, and Omby Amby after visiting Utensia, Bunbury, and Bunnybury. The Wizard explains that he found her by following her and Billina's tracks, and the group resumes its journey in the enchanted red wagon. The wagon carries them to Rigmarole Town, where the inhabitants speak in excessively long speeches that say very little. After observing the Rigmaroles, Dorothy decides to use only enough words to express what she means.

23. How They Encountered the Flutterbudgets body

The Wizard, Dorothy, and their companions reach Flutterbudget Center and discover that its inhabitants are consumed by exaggerated fears and unlikely possibilities. Several Flutterbudgets panic over harmless events or imagined dangers, leading the Wizard to conclude that foolish fears, nervousness, worries, and too many “ifs” can make anyone a Flutterbudget. The travelers leave the village and cross from the purple Gillikin Country into the yellow Country of the Winkies. They continue toward Nick Chopper’s Tin Castle, hoping to lunch with the Tin Woodman.

24. How the Tin Woodman Told the Sad News body

The Tin Woodman welcomes Dorothy, Uncle Henry, Aunt Em, the Wizard, and their companions to his tin castle, but he is troubled by news from Ozma. He explains that the Scarecrow has moved into a farm mansion and that the Nome King has ordered a tunnel dug beneath the deadly desert toward the Emerald City. The Nome King plans to send powerful evil spirits through the tunnel before his Nomes follow to conquer Oz, enslave its people, and plunder the country. Ozma refuses to fight because she believes Oz's inhabitants cannot overcome the invading forces, while the Tin Woodman decides to go to the Emerald City and perish beside her if necessary.

25. How the Scarecrow Displayed His Wisdom body

The Scarecrow welcomes Dorothy, the Tin Woodman, and their companions to his mansion after they learn that the Nome King plans to conquer and destroy Oz. He displays his wisdom by advising them not to worry before the disaster occurs, while still deciding to accompany them to the Emerald City to support Ozma. The Scarecrow's mansion and farm are described, including his method of replacing the straw in his body. Jack Pumpkinhead joins the group, and they reach the Emerald City before twilight.

26. How Ozma Refused to Fight for Her Kingdom body

Ozma learns through the Magic Picture that the Nome King, the Whimsies, the Growleywogs, and the Phanfasms will invade Oz through a completed tunnel at daybreak. She refuses to fight or abandon the Oz people, choosing instead to share their fate and seek a peaceful solution. Dorothy and the others agree to remain with Ozma, while they learn that the Forbidden Fountain contains the Water of Oblivion, which erases all knowledge and memories. The Scarecrow says his brains have found a way to save the kingdom and plans to reveal it to Ozma before the invasion.

27. How the Fierce Warriors Invaded Oz body

The Nome King and his allies begin their invasion of Oz after midnight, marching through the tunnel toward the Emerald City. The First and Foremost, Grand Gallipoot, and Chief of the Whimsies each secretly plan to betray the others and seize Oz's treasures and slaves. The Nome King and General Guph plan to obtain the Magic Belt and send the allied forces back to their own countries. As the armies advance, thick dust fills the tunnel, causing the invaders to cough, sneeze, choke, and thirst, but they continue toward Oz.

28. How They Drank at the Forbidden Fountain body

The Scarecrow, Tin Woodman, Tiktok, Jack Pumpkinhead, Ozma, Dorothy, and their allies wait beside the Forbidden Fountain as the Phanfasms, Growleywogs, Whimsies, Nome King, General Guph, and Nomes invade Oz. The invaders drink from the Water of Oblivion and forget their identities, purposes, and hostility toward Oz, while the Nome King is thrown into the fountain and also loses his memory. Ozma persuades the Nome King to order the Nomes to march home, and General Guph follows them. She then uses the Magic Belt to wish the remaining invaders safely back to their own homes, causing them all to disappear from Oz.

29. How Glinda Worked a Magic Spell body

Ozma decides that the Land of Oz needs stronger protection from outsiders and plans to seek Glinda the Good's advice. At Glinda's castle, Glinda explains that her magic charm can make Oz invisible to everyone outside it. Glinda reveals that she has already performed the Magic Spell, ensuring that outsiders cannot see or find Oz while its inhabitants remain unaffected.

30. How the Story of Oz Came to an End body

The writer of these Oz stories receives a note from Princess Dorothy of Oz saying that Oz is cut off forever from the rest of the world. Dorothy says that she and Toto will always love the writer and the children who love them. Although the writer is initially saddened, he reflects that six story books have already told enough of the history of Oz. He wishes Dorothy and her companions a long and happy life in their invisible country.
