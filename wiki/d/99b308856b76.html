Question Decomposition for Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Paul J. L. Ammann · 2025-07-28/2025-07-29 · 14 sections
"Question Decomposition for Retrieval-Augmented Generation" by Paul Ammann, Jonas Golde, Alan Akbik (2025), licensed CC BY. Shown here as extracted text, with facts drawn from it. https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.acl-srw.32

The document presents a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipeline for complex and multi-hop question answering that combines LLM-based question decomposition (QD) with cross-encoder reranking (RR). Building on the RAG framework of Lewis et al. (2020), it retrieves documents by encoding queries and documents into a shared vector space, then uses a pretrained autoregressive LLM to generate answers from the query and selected passages. The proposed extension has an LLM split an original complex question into simpler subqueries, retrieve passages for both the original query and subqueries, merge and deduplicate the candidates, and rerank them against the original question before selecting the top-k passages for answer generation. The central claim is that QD broadens coverage of evidence distributed across documents, while reranking removes noisy or weakly relevant material and improves precision. Unlike approaches requiring additional training or specialized indexing, the system uses general-purpose LLM decomposition and an off-the-shelf cross-encoder reranker in a single-shot pipeline. Experiments compare Naive RAG, RAG + QD, RAG + Reranker, and RAG + QD + Reranker on MultiHop-RAG and HotpotQA. Results, taken from Tang and Yang (2024) and evaluated on development data because the official test set is hidden, show that the combined QD+RR system delivers the strongest retrieval and answer results. On MultiHop-RAG it reaches 87.2% Hits@10 and 0.635 MRR@10; overall, the approach reports gains of 36.7% in MRR@10 retrieval and 11.6% in F1 answer accuracy over standard RAG baselines. On HotpotQA, QD+RR has the best answer EM, F1, precision, and recall, though RR alone has the highest supporting-fact EM and F1. The document also identifies tradeoffs. QD+RR has the greatest latency, mainly from LLM inference for decomposition, although caching may help. Decomposition often creates five subqueries even when only two or three gold evidence sentences are needed, and subquery count has near-zero correlation with gold evidence count. Performance can suffer for already precise queries, depends on prompts, the underlying LLM, and a strong domain-aligned reranker, and the pipeline is not iterative. Future work includes applying QD conditionally and using more efficient models.

Abstract body

The unit concerns a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipeline designed to improve answers to multi-hop questions by using question decomposition and reranking. An LLM decomposes each original query into sub-questions, passages are retrieved for each sub-question, and the merged candidate pool is reranked before answer generation. The approach pairs an off-the-shelf cross-encoder reranker with LLM-driven question decomposition without extra training or specialized indexing. Evaluations on MultiHop-RAG and HotpotQA report gains of 36.7% in MRR@10 retrieval and 11.6% in F1 answer accuracy over standard RAG baselines.

Introduction body

The unit concerns a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipeline that combines question decomposition with reranking for multi-hop question answering. It explains that question decomposition uses large language models to split complex questions into focused subqueries, improving coverage of evidence distributed across multiple documents. Retrieved passages from all subqueries are aggregated, then a pretrained reranker scores them against the original query to reduce noise and improve precision. The approach is evaluated on MultiHop-RAG and HotpotQA, where it improves retrieval recall, ranking metrics, and final answer accuracy over standard RAG and single-component variants.

Methodology .. Generation. body

The methodology follows the retrieval-augmented generation framework of Lewis et al. (2020), combining a retriever with a generative language model to answer a natural language query. A query encoder and document encoder independently project queries and documents into a shared vector space, where inner-product similarity selects the top-k documents. A pre-trained cross-encoder reranker then scores the candidate query-document pairs and retains the top-k passages. A pretrained autoregressive LLM concatenates the query with these passages and generates an answer token by token, conditioning on the complete retrieved context.

RAG with Question Decomposition .. QD Module / Reranker Module body

The unit concerns a proposed RAG pipeline that extends naive retrieval with question decomposition and reranking for complex and multi-hop questions. The question decomposition module generates simpler sub-queries, retrieves documents for the original query and sub-queries, and merges and deduplicates the results. The reranking module scores the candidate documents against the original complex query and filters noisy or weakly relevant passages. Algorithm 1 then selects the top-k passages for the language model.

Experiments / Datasets .. HotpotQA body

The unit evaluates a question decomposition pipeline for retrieval in multi-hop question answering using MultiHop-RAG and HotpotQA. It compares Naive RAG, RAG + QD, RAG + Reranker, and RAG + QD + Reranker, using dense retrieval, reranking, and answer generation components. On MultiHop-RAG, the combined system achieves the strongest reported results, including 87.2% Hits@10 and 0.635 MRR@10. On HotpotQA, the combined system also performs best on answer EM, F1, precision, and recall, although the reported evaluation includes answer, supporting-fact, and joint metrics.

opening .. Efficiency body

This unit evaluates QD+RR, RR, QD, and Naive RAG on retrieval, answer, supporting-fact, and joint metrics for MultiHop-RAG and HotpotQA. QD+RR achieves the strongest retrieval results and the highest HotpotQA answer scores, while RR achieves the highest supporting-fact EM and F1. Question decomposition usually produces five subqueries, despite most questions requiring only two or three gold evidence sentences, and the number of subqueries has near-zero correlation with the number of gold evidences. QD+RR has the highest retrieval quality but also the greatest latency, largely because of LLM inference for decomposition, although caching can reduce this overhead.

Related Work / RAG augments LLMs with access to body

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) gives large language models access to external information at inference time and supports knowledge-intensive tasks such as open-domain and multi-hop question answering. Its performance depends heavily on retrieved content, since irrelevant or misleading passages can impair answer quality. Recent work uses question decomposition to split complex queries into subqueries targeting multiple evidence sources, but sequential resolution can increase latency and cascading errors. This work adopts single-step decomposition with general-purpose LLMs without task-specific training.

Reranking for Precision Retrieval. / Complementary Approaches. / Conclusion .. Future work. Employing QD only when a query body

The unit examines how LLM-based question decomposition and cross-encoder reranking affect retrieval-augmented generation for complex and multi-hop question answering. Across four system variants and two datasets, combining question decomposition with reranking produced the largest gains in retrieval and answer correctness without extra training or domain-specific tuning. Question decomposition broadened evidence coverage by creating focused sub-queries, while reranking promoted the most relevant passages. The approach adds computational overhead and can introduce noise when queries are already precise, motivating future work on conditional question decomposition and more efficient models.

Limitations body

The limitations concern an approach for improving multi-hop retrieval through question decomposition and reranking. Question decomposition can hurt performance for specific queries, while subquery quality depends on prompt design and the underlying large language model. The approach also increases computational overhead, latency, GPU requirements, and pipeline complexity. Its gains depend on a strong, domain-aligned cross-encoder reranker, and its single-shot pipeline does not support iterative evidence-based retrieval.
