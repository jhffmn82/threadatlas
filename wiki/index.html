ThreadAtlas

A human-navigable knowledge graph grounded in source text

ThreadAtlas is an attempt to build a knowledge graph that can be verified and that keeps a person in the loop. A model organizes the documents into the graph you can walk here. In this design it is never the source of truth: the source text is. Every fact on this site links to the verbatim quote it was read from, every quote opens the source text at the spot it was taken, and the judgement stays with the person reading. The summaries, abstracts and section notes around the facts are written by a model, and every page marks them so.

Nothing is merged. When the same person, place or idea appears in two works, one page links both, and each claim stays with the work that made it. Connections between entities are facts a work states, so every line in a figure opens to its quotes.

Retrieval is built the same way. Each fact the search on this site returns, and each fact the pipeline hands a model as context, comes with the passage it was read from, so an answer can be traced back to the source text. Its benchmark results are below.

About

ThreadAtlas is Justin Hoffman's IT 494 project in the M.S. in Computer Science at Illinois State University, advised by Dr. Xing Fang.

ThreadAtlas is a proof of concept built on one position: a language model organizes a collection of documents, but it is not the collection's source of truth and it does not replace the person who reads it. The model finds the entities, reads the facts and writes the summaries; the ground truth stays the source text. Every fact carries a verbatim quotation that code has located in its document, every narrative belongs to the section it was written from, and the same entity in two works is joined on one page without moving any claim out of the work that makes it. A reader can go from any claim to its passage and judge it there.

The idea came from audit and oversight work, where a person moves through many kinds of material and a statement that goes into a report must trace to a source the reader can examine. The same graph also gives a language model its context, with the source passages attached; its results on public retrieval benchmarks are below.

The store this site was built from holds 24,071 documents. 189 are the books and papers on this site: Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), 98 works; Classical Greek and Roman Literature, Mythology, and History, 30 works; Oz series, 28 works; The Sherlock Holmes Canon, 9 works; Balfour, Frances Campbell, Lady, 1858–1931, 2 works; L. Frank Baum Collection, 2 works; Ovid's Amatory Elegies, 2 works; and 18 works in no larger collection. The other 23,882 are chat sessions, the LongMemEval benchmark's generated conversation histories between a user and an assistant, which hold 1,127,024 of the store's 1,231,253 facts; they are not published here. Across all of it, 131,345 entities link their appearances in different documents.

Follow one claim to its source

Every page works this way. What a model wrote is marked written by a model; a quotation is the source’s own words, and one click opens the text it was read from.

  1. An entity. Ozma has one page. It joins the entity’s accounts in 24 works without merging them.
  2. A work. In The Marvelous Land of Oz it is one of 123 entities, with its own section on that page.
  3. A claim. Tip is identical to Princess Ozma (in transformed form). worded by a model
  4. Its quotation. ““that is the Princess Ozma—the child brought to me by the Wizard who stole her father’s throne. That is the rightful ruler of the Emerald City!” and she pointed her long bony finger straight at the boy.” the source’s own words
  5. Its source. The quotation opens Princess Ozma of Oz in the viewer, highlighted inside the full text of that section, where it can be read in context and judged.

How it works

  1. Phase 0, extraction. Each file is read on its own and cut into dated units: chapters, sections, or chat turns. Every boundary must copy text found on the cited line.
  2. Phase 1, ingestion. Per document: the entities, the facts with verbatim quotes, a narrative cell per entity per unit, and abstracts. A fact is kept only if its quote is found in its unit's text.
  3. Phase 2, global merge. Which entities in different documents are the same thing? A judge model rules on each offered pair; a parent links the instances without merging them. Bodies of work are found the same way.
  4. Retrieval. A question is answered from the graph: vector and keyword search fused by rank, each hit packed with the passage its facts came from.
  5. Wiki. This site, rendered from the store by one deterministic pass: no page is written by hand and no model is called.
