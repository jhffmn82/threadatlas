the Enchanted Forest in The Yellow Knight of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1930

The Enchanted Forest is a centuries-old enchanted forest of giant trees, a silvery stream, and unusual plants and animals. It contains the magic palm grown from a planted date seed, the King of the Golden City and that city’s subjects in enchanted forms, and later concealed transformed people, horses, and the court of Corumbia as trees, bushes, and vines. Sir Hokus, the Comfortable Camel, and the jester hurry along its stream to escape possible further spells. Speedy and Marygolden rest and walk there before leaving for a yellow river and orange grove. They return to seek the magic date seed; after Speedy buries the red seed, the forest’s transformed inhabitants regain their forms.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER TEN

The enchanted forest contains giant trees, a silvery stream, and plants and animals that behave in unusual ways. A lyre bush produces music, a willow appears to weep and call to Sir Hokus, and a flowering vine laughs through the birds resting on it. The jester warns that the forest may cast further evil spells, so Sir Hokus, the Comfortable Camel, and the jester hurry along the stream to leave it.

CHAPTER FOURTEEN

The Enchanted Forest is where Speedy and Marygolden rest, walk, and encounter magical transformations. They leave it on Stampedro’s back and reach a yellow river and an orange grove.

CHAPTER SIXTEEN

The enchanted forest is shown through Chinda's magical telescope, including a hollow tree near a horse chestnut tree. Speedy and his companions return there to search for the magic date seed and to plan the release of the transformed people.

CHAPTER SEVENTEEN

The enchanted forest conceals the transformed people, horses, and court of Corumbia as trees, bushes, and vines. After Speedy buries the red date seed, the trees and plants change back into the King, Queen, Corumbians, Knights, horses, children, courtiers, and musicians.
