Nymphs in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

Nymphs are immortal beings associated with a sacred cave in the Paphlagonian hills and its monument’s sacred water, from which they create a stream. They mourn Alexander, marvel at Oenone’s leap onto his pyre, condemn Paris, and praise Oenone’s devotion.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Then, while her father and her maidens slept,

The Nymphs mourn around Alexander's corpse and marvel when Oenone leaps onto the pyre beside him. They judge Paris evil-hearted and foolish for neglecting the wife who loved him more than life.
