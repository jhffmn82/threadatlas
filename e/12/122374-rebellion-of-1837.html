rebellion of 1837 in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

The rebellion of 1837 in Lower Canada was an armed, historical rebellion and the only occasion described as resembling civil war between French and English Canadians during a hundred and fifty years of British rule. It followed months of political agitation, mass meetings, separatist rhetoric, and the formation of armed Patriote companies. Though Papineau was associated with it, he later denied primary responsibility, and the text judges that he played no important role in planning it. The movement was impromptu, based on sudden resolution rather than a well-reasoned plan, hopeless from the outset, and later regarded as a fiasco. It was mainly confined to the county of Two Mountains and the Richelieu valley. Political conflict became armed conflict in the Chambly–Longueuil skirmish and at St Denis and St Charles; the murder of Lieutenant Weir and the killing of Chartrand deepened hostility between French and English Canadians. French-Canadian and English-Canadian veterans fought on opposing sides. At St Denis, the rebels checked Colonel Gore's column, their only victory. Their defeat at St Charles effectively ended the rebellion around the Richelieu, but fighting continued at Moore's Corners and St Eustache. The aftermath included extensive burning and pillaging by irregular loyalist forces. Presented as belonging to the dead past, the rebellion is to be judged impartially, with blame assigned both to English opponents' arrogance and lawlessness and to Patriote leaders' irreconcilability and carelessness.

The rebellion of 1837 in Lower Canada was an impromptu, poorly planned movement that was considered hopeless from the outset. It was confined mainly to the county of Two Mountains and the valley of the Richelieu, and it was easily defeated.

The rebellion is presented as belonging to the dead past. The text argues that Canadians should judge its events impartially, recognizing both the arrogance and lawlessness of many English opponents and the irreconcilability and carelessness of many Patriote leaders.
