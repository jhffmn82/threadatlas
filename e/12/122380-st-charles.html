St Charles in The 'Patriotes' of '37

Alfred D. Decelles · 1916

St Charles is a place central to the Patriote rebellion of 1837. On October 23 it hosted the largest and most imposing Patriote meeting described, attended by thousands; armed men guarded it, a column was dedicated to Papineau, and participants swore fidelity to their country and to conquer or die. It contained the manor-house of Debartzch, later fortified and held by an armed Patriote force under Thomas Storrow Brown, with a cure who encouraged the rebellion. After St Denis, Wetherall's force advanced on St Charles. On November 25 British troops attacked the fortified position and defeated its poorly armed defenders in less than an hour, prompting refugees to gather at Swanton and weakening support elsewhere.

{82} CHAPTER IX _FORCE MAJEURE_ The check administered to Colonel Gore's column at St Denis, in the first engagement of the rebellion, was the only victory which fell to the rebel forces.

St Charles was the site of a fortified rebel position at the manor-house of Debartzch. British troops attacked there on November 25 and defeated the poorly armed rebel force in an engagement lasting less than an hour.

{89} But the end of rebellion was not yet in sight.

The collapse and defeat at St Charles prompted Patriote refugees to gather at Swanton. News of the defeat also moderated support for the rebels at St Eustache.
