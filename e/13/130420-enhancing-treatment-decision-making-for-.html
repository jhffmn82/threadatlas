Enhancing treatment decision-making for low back pain: a novel framework integrating large language models with retrieval-augmented generation technology in Enhancing treatment decision-making for low back pain: a novel framework integrating large language models with retrieval-augmented generation technology

Rong Chen, Siyun Zhang, Yiyi Zheng, Qiuhua Yu and Chuhuai Wang · 2025-05-14

This article presents CLBP-GPT, a clinical decision-support framework intended to improve personalized diagnosis and treatment decisions for chronic low back pain (CLBP). Chen, Zhang, Zheng, Yu, and Wang address shortcomings of existing decision-support systems and Patient Decision Aids, particularly their difficulty combining complex clinical, psychosocial, occupational, and lifestyle information while providing unbiased recommendations. The proposed system integrates GPT-4.0, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and least-to-most (LtM) prompting to improve recommendation accuracy, interpretability, and personalization. The framework begins with multi-source patient complaints from Internet medical platforms and hospitals. The authors compiled 80 de-identified CLBP complaint samples and built a knowledge base from 4,612 CLBP-related PubMed articles and medical guidelines published from 2021 to 2025. GPT-4.0 extracts patient features, retrieves relevant evidence and guidelines, and generates individualized recommendations; progressive LtM prompting in Chinese is used to guide clinical reasoning. The study then evaluates CLBP-GPT against GPT-4.0, ERNIE Bot, and DeepSeek. Using 80 representative questions, three CLBP experts scored responses for accuracy, relevance, clarity, benefit, and completeness on five-point scales. CLBP-GPT achieved the highest results in every category, with a reported total score of 4.40, and showed lower score variability than the comparison models, indicating more stable performance. In discussion, the authors characterize CLBP-GPT as a hybrid LLM-RAG system that dynamically retrieves clinical evidence and adapts recommendations to individual circumstances. They conclude that it could provide technical support for efficient, personalized CLBP treatment strategies. They also note unresolved needs for long-term clinical-outcome validation, continual knowledge updating, cross-cultural adaptability, multicenter clinical studies, and better doctor-AI workflow integration. The work received support from the Guangdong-Hong Kong Technology and Innovation Cooperation Funding, the National Key Research and Development Program of China, and the National Natural Science Foundation of China; the authors report no conflicts of interest.

1 Introduction

This unit concerns chronic low back pain (CLBP) and the development of CLBP-GPT, a clinical decision-supporting system for personalized diagnosis and treatment. It describes the limitations of existing decision-supporting systems and Patient Decision Aids, including difficulties integrating complex clinical and psychosocial information and providing unbiased recommendations. The study combines GPT-4.0, retrieval-augmented generation, and LtM prompt engineering to improve the accuracy, interpretability, and personalization of clinical recommendations. It also constructs a CLBP dataset and a medical knowledge base from research papers and clinical guidelines.

2 Materials and methods / 2.1 Framework .. 2.4 LtM prompting

This unit describes a clinical decision framework for chronic low back pain (CLBP) that combines large language models, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), and prompt engineering. The framework uses multi-source patient complaints from Internet medical platforms and hospitals, a CLBP knowledge base built from recent PubMed literature and guidelines, GPT-4.0 feature extraction, knowledge retrieval, and personalized recommendations. The study compiled 80 de-identified CLBP patient complaint samples and collected 4,612 relevant PubMed articles and medical guidelines from 2021–2025. It also applies progressive LtM prompting in Chinese to improve GPT-4.0-based clinical decision-making.

3 Experiment .. 3.1 Evaluation metrics

The experiment evaluates CLBP-GPT for supporting clinical decision-making in low back pain and compares it with GPT-4.0, ERNIE Bot, and DeepSeek. An automated evaluation system based on the GPT-4.0 API was developed for CLBP-GPT, while the other models were assessed through manual questioning and recording because of interface limitations. The evaluation process involved standardized data preparation, answer collection, scoring by three experts, averaging the scores, and comparing model performance. Evaluation metrics covered accuracy, relevance, clarity, benefit, and completeness, each scored on a five-point scale.

4 Results

The systematic evaluation experiment compared CLBP-GPT with GPT-4.0, ERNIE Bot, and DeepSeek using 80 representative questions reviewed by three CLBP experts. CLBP-GPT achieved the highest scores for accuracy, relevance, clarity, Benefit, Completeness, and total score. It also had lower standard deviations than the other models, indicating more stable performance.

5 Discussion

Chen et al. evaluated CLBP-GPT, a hybrid LLM-RAG framework for clinical decision support in chronic low back pain (CLBP) management. The framework combined GPT-4-based semantic feature extraction, dynamic retrieval of clinical guidelines and evidence, and personalized adaptation using occupational, psychosocial, and lifestyle factors. CLBP-GPT received higher mean scores than GPT-4.0, ERNIE Bot, and DeepSeek for accuracy, relevance, clarity, benefit, completeness, and total performance. The authors identified limitations involving long-term clinical outcome validation, knowledge updating, and cross-cultural adaptability, and proposed multicenter clinical studies and improved doctor-AI workflow integration.
