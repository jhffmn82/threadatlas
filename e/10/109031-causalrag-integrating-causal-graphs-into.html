Abstract

The abstract concerns large language models (LLMs) and their use in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) for natural language processing. It identifies limitations in traditional RAG systems, including disrupted contextual integrity from text chunking and over-reliance on semantic similarity for retrieval. It introduces CausalRAG, which incorporates causal graphs into the retrieval process by constructing and tracing causal relationships. The framework is reported to outperform regular RAG and graph-based RAG across several metrics, improving retrieval precision, contextual continuity, and response accuracy and interpretability.

Introduction

It explains that reliance on pre-trained knowledge limits LLMs’ ability to integrate and reason over dynamically updated external information, especially in academic research. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is presented as a framework that combines retrieval mechanisms with generative capabilities to improve contextual understanding and response quality. Recent research is described as focusing on two primary directions for improving RAG, although the unit provides only the beginning of the first direction.

Related Work / Retrieval-Augmented Generation .. Rethinking Graph-based RAGs

This unit reviews retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), graph-based RAG, and the emerging use of causal graphs in RAG. It argues that regular RAG can disrupt linguistic coherence through chunking, retrieve superficially similar but causally irrelevant information through semantic search, and amplify bias during generation. It reports that GraphRAG and related graph-based methods generally improve context precision through graph ranking and summarization but may reduce context recall by omitting relevant information. The unit introduces CausalRAG as a framework intended to address these limitations through causally grounded retrieval.

Methodology .. Retrieving Context Causally

This unit introduces CausalRAG, a framework that integrates retrieval-augmented generation with causality to improve context retrieval from uploaded documents. It constructs a text-based graph whose nodes and edges represent entities, concepts, and relationships, then matches a user query to initial nodes and expands along graph edges to identify causal paths. A large language model estimates these paths, builds a refined causal graph, and generates a causal summary as grounded context. The summary is combined with the user query in a refined prompt for the final retrieval-augmented generation response.

Experiment / Experimental Setup .. Parameter Study

The experiments evaluate CausalRAG against Regular RAG, GraphRAG, and HippoRAG2 on academic papers sampled from OpenAlex, using answer faithfulness, context recall, and context precision. CausalRAG achieves the strongest overall balance, with the highest answer faithfulness and context precision while maintaining competitive context recall. A case study shows that causal retrieval identifies the relationship among influence tactics, buyer attention, and sales contract award more accurately than semantic or community-based retrieval. A parameter study finds that performance improves as k and s increase, with diminishing gains at higher settings and competitive results at k = s = 3.

Conclusion and Future Work

The unit concerns CausalRAG, a framework that integrates causal reasoning into retrieval-augmented generation. It identifies limitations of existing RAG systems and reports that CausalRAG improves generation quality, reduces hallucinations, and strengthens alignment between queries and retrieved content across diverse domains and context lengths. It discusses future work on scalable graph-based causal retrieval for long-context reasoning. It also notes limitations involving LLM knowledge, specialized domains, and the computational cost of identifying causal paths during inference.
