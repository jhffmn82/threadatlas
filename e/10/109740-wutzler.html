Otto Wutzler in Dragon's Blood

Henry Milner Rideout · 1909

Otto Wutzler is a German outcast and guide, related by marriage to Wutzler's wife's cousin. Disguised as a small coolie in a wicker hat and dirty blue clothes, with mangrove-bark coloring, he warns Heywood that riots are coming and explains the rivalry between the Triad and White Lotus. Knowing the White Lotus lodge-room's listening place, he leads Heywood there but refuses to stay. He later helps disguise another fugitive's escape, guides Heywood through alleys out of town, identifies the Black Dog as a cannon, and seeks to learn when it will fire.

Wutzler in Dragon's Blood

Henry Milner Rideout · 1909

Wutzler is a nervous, shy, isolated European resident of Stink-Chau who lives in Wutzler's house. Described as a German-speaking coolie, a white man, and a German-Chinese hybrid in appearance and dress, he has a frail, black-haired body resembling a puny ape and a Chinese wife whom he regards as shameful. He owns a repaired but damaged mechanical laughing box and considers himself a coward, as does Herr Heywood. He first greets Rudolph at the club and withdraws to his paper, then warns that the rats' behavior is ominous. At home, dressed in Chinese clothing, he anxiously feeds Rudolph and Heywood, praises Heywood's loyalty, fears being forgotten amid plague and violence, and blesses them brokenly. During the attack, he revives Rudolph with rice-brandy, guides and hides him through smoke and fire, and reports that Rudolph's friends are trapped. He disguises himself in a dead musketeer's clothing, leads Rudolph onward, then fires to confuse pursuit. He writes a coded message on an arrow, and ultimately the hostile forces display his head and body after his death.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II THE PIED PIPER A gray smudge trailing northward showed where the Fa-Hien--Scottish Oriental, sixteen hundred tons--was disappearing from the pale expanse of ocean.

Wutzler is a nervous European resident who greets Rudolph briefly at the club before retreating to read his paper. He later appears outside among the rats and calls their behavior a warning, but the others largely ignore him.

CHAPTER V IN TOWN Night, in that maze of alleys, was but a more sinister day.

Wutzler receives Rudolph and Heywood at his small home, dresses in Chinese clothing, and anxiously prepares them a meal and drinks. He praises Heywood’s loyalty to him, fears that the plague and violence may cause people to forget him, and gives them a broken benediction when they leave.

CHAPTER XVI THE GUNWALE Rudolph's mission began quietly, with a glimpse which he afterward recalled as incredibly peaceful.

Wutzler finds Rudolph sitting in the mud, gives him rice-brandy, and restores him to alertness. He leads Rudolph through smoke and fire, hides with him among oil-jars, and reports that Rudolph's friends are hemmed in.

CHAPTER XVII LAMP OF HEAVEN The white men peered from among the oil-jars, like two of the Forty Thieves.

Wutzler crawls out from among the oil-jars and disguises himself in a dead musketeer's jacket and scarlet turban. Carrying the dead man's musket and equipment, he leads Rudolph through the smoke before separating from him and firing to confuse the chase.

CHAPTER XX THE HAKKA BOAT "Pretty fair," Captain Kneebone said.

Wutzler's head appears to the defenders by torchlight above the wall, apparently displayed on a bamboo pole. Later, his white body is seen lying on a framework like a bier in the field, revealing that the hostile forces have displayed his remains.
