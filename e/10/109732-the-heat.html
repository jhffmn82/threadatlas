the heat in Dragon's Blood

Henry Milner Rideout · 1909

The heat is an oppressive condition affecting the night and the men. It intensifies Rudolph's agitation, makes the air stagnant, sour, and breathless, spreads bad temper through the club, and contributes to reckless loss of self-control.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VIII THE HOT NIGHT Rudolph paced his long chamber like a wolf,--a wolf in summer, with too thick a coat.

The heat oppresses the entire night, leaving the air stagnant, sour, and breathless. It torments Rudolph, fills the club with bad temper, contributes to the men's reckless behavior, and is repeatedly blamed for their loss of self-control.
