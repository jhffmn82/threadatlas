Iobates in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Iobates, king of Lycia and father of Philonoe, receives Bellerophon with Proetus’s death order but sets him deadly tasks instead. After Bellerophon succeeds, Iobates reveals the letter, gives him Philonoe, and leaves him the kingdom at death.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE LIBRARY, 11. 1. 5—u. 1

Iobates received Bellerophon and the letter from Proetus ordering Bellerophon's death. Instead of killing him directly, he assigned Bellerophon the tasks of killing the Chimera, fighting the Solymi and the Amazons, and ambushing the bravest Lycians; after Bellerophon succeeded, Iobates showed him the letter, asked him to remain, gave him his daughter Philonoe, and left him the kingdom when he died.

Iobates in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Iobates, king of Lycia, receives Bellerophon with a secret request for his death, sends him against the Chimaera, then gives him his daughter and succession after recognizing divine favor.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter X

Iobates, king of Lycia, sought a hero to destroy the Chimaera and received Bellerophon with a letter that secretly requested his death. He sent Bellerophon against the monster and later gave him his daughter in marriage and made him his successor after seeing that the gods favored him.
