1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VIII THE HOT NIGHT Rudolph paced his long chamber like a wolf,--a wolf in summer, with too thick a coat.

Political unrest is growing as the natives are said to be lashing themselves into a state of mind, and the Sword-Pen remains active. At dawn, Rudolph and the others watch Yamen runners escort Chok Chung, a Christian merchant, to answer for the death of the cormorant-fisher; Heywood warns that another act of trouble is coming.
