the plague in Dragon's Blood

Henry Milner Rideout · 1909

The plague is a pestilence and the immediate danger threatening Stink-Chau, present in the surrounding settlement and aboard the Fa-Hien. Its first notice comes with rats emerging and leaping strangely. It causes fear among the expatriate community, dominates Rudolph's fears, and fatally strikes Monsieur Jolivet; Dr. Chantel reports his sudden death and his own disinfection. Quarantine shapes shore activity, while Captain Kneebone keeps two afflicted coolies topside under the boats. In town, reports say people are dying in Silk-Weafer Street, and residents hang nets and thorns against plague-goblins, imagined as sheltering frightened women and children. After explanations involving weather, soldiers, a kite, a paper effigy, and geomantic forces, the pestilence disappears. Rudolph is relieved, while an uncounted number of dead join the host of ancestors.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER II THE PIED PIPER A gray smudge trailing northward showed where the Fa-Hien--Scottish Oriental, sixteen hundred tons--was disappearing from the pale expanse of ocean.

The plague is identified by Heywood as the danger threatening Stink-Chau. The rats' strange emergence and leaping are described as its first notice.

" CHAPTER III UNDER FIRE The desert was sometimes Gobi, sometimes Sahara, but always an infinite stretch of sand that floated up and up in a stifling layer, like the tide.

The plague dominates Rudolph's fears and is described as an immediate danger in the city. Dr. Chantel announces that Monsieur Jolivet has died suddenly from it and says that he himself has been disinfected.

CHAPTER IV THE SWORD-PEN "Wutzler was missing last night," said Heywood, lazily.

The plague is present ashore and aboard Captain Kneebone's steamer. Kneebone says that two coolies on board have plague and that they are kept topside under the boats, while Heywood and Rudolph's shore activities are affected by the quarantine situation.

CHAPTER V IN TOWN Night, in that maze of alleys, was but a more sinister day.

The plague is a feared presence in the town, though the unit does not establish its actual extent. Wutzler says people are dying in Silk-Weafer Street and describes residents hanging nets and thorns to keep plague-goblins away, while Heywood imagines terrified women and children sheltering behind them.

" CHAPTER VI THE PAGODA He was spared that inconvenience.

The pestilence disappears after several competing explanations, including weather, soldiers, a kite, a paper effigy, and geomantic forces. Its passing relieves Rudolph and leaves behind an uncounted number of dead who join the host of ancestors.
