Neil Gibson in The Case-Book of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1921-10

Neil Gibson is an American senator and the world's greatest gold-mining magnate, a masterful, formidable millionaire whose business power can make or break communities, cities, and nations, usually breaking them. He owns Thor Place in Hampshire, employs Marlow Bates as estate manager, has enemies, is violent, and keeps a loaded revolver beside his bed amid a large firearms collection. He married Maria Pinto after meeting her in Brazil, but his love faded; he became harsh toward her, with Bates and others calling his treatment brutal. Grace Dunbar then entered his life. Gibson passionately desired her, told her he wanted her love and possession, and, though unable to marry her, offered money to secure her comfort. Grace nevertheless influenced him powerfully for good, and Gibson later says he is thankful his plans failed and promises she will never again be molested. When Mrs. Gibson dies near the bridge at Thor Place, Gibson returns, calls for a doctor and police, and is said to have been in his library rather than outside after returning from town. A missing matching pistol and his alleged offences toward Grace raise suspicion, but he comes to Sherlock Holmes seeking evidence to prove Grace innocent. He initially denies a personal connection to her, then admits his feelings, accepts Holmes's reproach calmly, and expects Holmes to establish her innocence.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

THE PROBLEM OF THOR BRIDGE

Neil Gibson, an American senator and major gold-mining magnate, asks Holmes to prove that Miss Dunbar is innocent. He is described as powerful, harsh, and formidable, and Marlow Bates accuses him of treating his wife brutally. Gibson first denies having any personal relationship with Miss Dunbar, then admits that he had loved and married Maria Pinto before his feelings faded.

"Then came Miss Grace Dunbar. She answered our advertisement and

The millionaire admits that he developed a passionate regard for Grace and told her that he wanted her love and possession. He says he could not marry her but offered to do whatever money could accomplish to make her happy and comfortable, and he objects when Holmes criticizes his morals.

To my surprise the Gold King took the reproof with equanimity.

The Gold King says he is grateful that his plans did not succeed as intended. He explains that Miss Dunbar influenced him for good and that his wife remained partly to use that influence, and he discusses the possibility that his wife attacked Miss Dunbar.

Mr. Neil Gibson owns Thor Place and had a pair of pistols, although the other matching pistol has not been found. He summoned the police after Mrs. Gibson was discovered and, according to the available evidence, was in his library and had not been outside since returning from town.
