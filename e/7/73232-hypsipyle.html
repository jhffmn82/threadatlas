Hypsipyle in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Hypsipyle, daughter of Thoas and granddaughter of Bacchus, possesses Lemnos as her marriage portion. She welcomed and married Jason, bore their twin sons, then writes reproaching his silence and marriage to Medea, asserting her lawful bond and wishing Medea similar suffering.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

VI

Hypsipyle writes to Jason, reproaching him for failing to send word of his return and for taking Medea as his wife. She recalls that she welcomed Jason on Lemnos, bore his twin sons, and remains his lawful wife; she also prays that Medea will suffer as she has suffered.

Hypsipyle in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · -0299/-0200

Hypsipyle, Thoas’s daughter, spares him during the Lemnian massacre and sends him away hidden in a chest, then becomes queen of Lemnos. She leads the women’s assembly, proposes gifts for the Minyae, welcomes Jason, conceals the killings, and tearfully offers him Lemnos and Thoas’s sceptre.

Hypsipyle, daughter of Thoas, alone spared her aged father during the massacre and became queen after Thoas was presumed dead. She summoned the Lemnian women’s assembly, proposed sending gifts to the Minyae, welcomed Jason, concealed the full truth of the killings, and parted from him tearfully while offering him Lemnos and her father’s sceptre if he returned.

Hypsipyle in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Hypsipyle, Thoas’s daughter, ruled Lemnos after its men were gone, saving her father while others killed fathers and husbands. She slept with Jason and bore Euneus and Nebrophonus. Later, as a purchased bondwoman in Lycurgus’s house, she led champions to water, leaving infant Opheltes, who was killed by a serpent.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Πελίον θυγατέρα Αλκηστιν. ἐκείνου 1 δὲ δώσειν

Hypsipyle, daughter of Thoas, ruled Lemnos when it was without men. She alone saved her father during the killing of the fathers and husbands by hiding him. She slept with Jason and bore Euneus and Nebrophonus.

VI. ᾿Ἑτεοκλῆς δὲ καὶ ἸΠολυνείκης περὶ τῆς

Hypsipyle, who serves in Lycurgus's household, leads the champions to a spring while leaving behind the infant Opheltes whom she nurses. During her absence, a serpent kills Opheltes.
