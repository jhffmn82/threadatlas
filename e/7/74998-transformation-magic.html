transformations in The Tin Woodman of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1918

Transformations are magical changes caused and used by Mrs. Yoop through yookoohoo magic to alter creatures and objects. She changes herself, field-mice, and a wasp's nest, then transforms travelers into a Brown Bear, Tin Owl, Green Monkey, and Canary-Bird. The altered travelers are unhappy, while Ozma and Dorothy witness the results and Ozma seeks to break the enchantments.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Five

Mrs. Yoop uses magic transformations to change living beings and objects into other forms. She says she transformed herself into a mouse to hide from the people who captured Mr. Yoop, and she turns field-mice into biscuits and a wasp's nest into honey.

Chapter Eleven

The transformations have changed the Scarecrow into a Brown Bear, Nick Chopper into a Tin Owl, Woot into a Green Monkey, and Polychrome into a Canary-Bird. The travelers are unhappy in these forms and seek a way to recover their original shapes.

Chapter Twelve

The transformations inflicted by Mrs. Yoop change the forms of the Tin Woodman, the Scarecrow, Woot, and another person. Ozma and Dorothy watch them with horror, and Ozma resolves to attempt to break the enchantments.

transformation magic in Glinda of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1920

Transformation magic, used by Reera the Red for her own selfish amusement, enables wonders without spoken incantations. It unfolds through Reera changing her own form, animals, and a meal table, apparently by touch, then turning a wolf into a singing bird, fish into young women and Adepts at Magic, and herself into her natural form; she usually refuses to help others.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Eighteen

Reera's magic allows her to change her own form, transform animals, create a fully set meal table, and perform other wonders without spoken incantations. Ervic observes that she seems to accomplish the creature transformations by touching the creatures, while he claims not to understand magic.

Chapter Nineteen

Reera's transformation magic changes a wolf into a colorful singing bird, the three fish into beautiful young women and Adepts at Magic, and Reera into her natural form. Reera says she normally refuses to use magic to assist others because she does not want crowds seeking her help.
