Heidegger’s murder in The Return of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1903-09-26

Heidegger’s death at Lower Gill Moor appears to have been caused by a savage blow, yet no bicycle tracks or human footprints initially explain it. It is later identified as Reuben Hayes’s killing during the escape after Lord Saltire’s kidnapping; James Wilder did not directly take part but is held morally responsible because it arose from his crime.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

When the nobleman and his secretary had left, my friend flung

The text presents Heidegger’s death as the result of a savage blow from a vigorous arm, though Holmes has not established how it occurred. The absence of another bicycle’s track or human footprints makes the apparent murder impossible to explain from the evidence then available.

At eleven o’clock next morning my friend and I were walking up

Heidegger’s murder was committed by Reuben Hayes during the escape after Lord Saltire’s kidnapping. The Duke argues that James Wilder did not know of or directly participate in the killing, but Holmes holds Wilder morally responsible because the murder arose from his crime.
