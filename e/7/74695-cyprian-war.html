Cyprian War in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Cyprian War between Artaxerxes and Evagoras lasted nearly ten years, with about two years of active warfare, ending when Evagoras retained Salamis as a tribute-paying king subject to Artaxerxes.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian / BOOK XV.

The Cyprian War between Artaxerxes and Evagoras lasted nearly ten years, although only about two years were spent in active warfare. It ended when Evagoras agreed to pay annual tribute and rule Salamis as a king subject to Artaxerxes.

Cyprian war in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Cyprian war was a conflict against Persian power for the liberties of Greek cities in Asia. Prepared by Artaxerxes against Euagoras, it began with an Athenian expedition to Cyprus and ended in peace between Persia and Athens, followed by Athenian withdrawal.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

DIODORUS SICULUS. / BOOK XIL

The Cyprian war began with the Athenian expedition against Persian power in Cyprus. It ended when peace was made between Persia and the Athenians, after which the Athenians withdrew their forces.

CHAP. XIII.

The Cyprian War was prepared by Artaxerxes after peace was made with the Grecians. It was directed against Euagoras, who had raised a large army throughout Cyprus.
