Mithridates in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Mithridates is a Persian nobleman and soldier, son of Ariobarzanes and descendant of one of the seven Persians who slew Smerdis; he is also recorded as son of Ariarathes and Antiochide and lawful heir after an imposture. As king, he rules Pontus and later Asia. While engaged in Asia he declines Italian aid, then defeats Roman generals, frees supplied prisoners, and is acclaimed god and deliverer.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XXXVIL

Mithridates was asked by the Italians to invade Italy and oppose Rome. He replied that he would march into Italy after subduing Asia, where he was then engaged. His answer deprived the Italians of the assistance and financial support they hoped to receive.

BOOK XXXVII.

Mithridates, after defeating Roman generals in Asia, sent Roman prisoners home clothed and supplied with provisions. His clemency won widespread praise, and many cities welcomed him as a god and deliverer.

Mithridates in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Mithridates, king of barbarians near the Euxine Sea, used Aristion as ambassador, fought Rome through Archelaus and Taxilus, won support from part of Athens, and was linked with Menophanes in Delos’s destruction. After Roman defeat and exile, he died by his own hand according to a divine interpretation, or was killed by a mercenary.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIX.

Mithridates was king of the barbarians near the Euxine Sea and employed Aristion as an ambassador to the Greek states. He fought the Romans, sending Archelaus and Taxilus as generals, and gained the support of part of Athens.

CHAPTER XXIII.

Mithridates was associated with Menophanes in the destruction of Delos. After his power was destroyed and he was driven about by the Romans, he was compelled by the god to become his own executioner, though another tradition says that one of his mercenaries killed him.
