Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Dryope becomes a lotus tree as roots and woodiness overtake her; briefly retaining face, voice, tears, and warmth, she is embraced before protecting it from an axe.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter V

The lotus tree is the form Dryope takes after her feet become rooted and woodiness spreads through her body. Her face, voice, tears, and warmth remain briefly, and her family embraces the trunk and kisses its leaves before the transformation is complete.
