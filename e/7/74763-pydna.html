Pydna in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Pydna is a town with grounds, territories, walls, and a harbour. Philip captured it after Amphipolis and delivered it to the Olynthians. Later Olympias entered with relatives and noncombatants; Cassander enclosed and blockaded it by land and sea. During winter famine weakened its defenders, and the city surrendered.

CHAP. 11.

Philip captured Pydna after taking Amphipolis. He later delivered Pydna, together with its grounds and territories, to the Olynthians.

Olympias entered Pydna with a large group of relatives and noncombatants despite inadequate provisions for a long siege. Cassander surrounded the town with a mud wall from sea to sea and planned to blockade it by land and sea.

Since we have treated of the afiisiirs of Asia, we shall now pass over

Pydna was blockaded by Cassander during the winter, with its walls surrounded, a mud-wall drawn from sea to sea, and its harbour guarded by ships. The city eventually surrendered after its defenders were weakened by famine.
