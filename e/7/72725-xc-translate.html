XC-Translate

benchmark · 1 work · 32 quoted facts · 5 connections
Also called: Cross-Culture Translate, XC-Translate annotations, benchmark, our benchmark, the benchmark
  • In Towards Cross-Cultural Machine Translation with Retrieval-Augmented Generation from Multilingual Knowledge Graphs: XC-Translate is a large-scale, manually created and verified machine-translation evaluation benchmark for cross-cultural translation of text containing potentially culturally nuanced entity names. It covers 10 English-to-X language pairs, with Arabic, Chinese, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Spanish, Thai, and Turkish as target languages. It contains about 5,000 sentences per language pair, more than 58,000 instances, and over 100,000 references, averaging two translations per sentence. Human annotators selected entity names likely to require treatment beyond transliteration. The benchmark is designed to reveal weaknesses in MT systems and multilingual large language models when entity names vary culturally. Evaluation results show that BLEU and COMET can remain high despite incorrect entity translations, while M-ETA reports substantially lower entity-aware performance. It also evaluates a knowledge retriever, which reaches 85.9% hits@1 and 92.1% hits@3; KG-MT significantly outperforms the state of the art. Although diverse, XC-Translate is not exhaustive: it omits Uralic, Dravidian, and Niger-Congo families, many Indo-European languages, and dialectal and regional variation; its Wikidata-based entity selection may also exclude culturally relevant entities.

It covers 10 English-to-X language pairs, with Arabic, Chinese, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Spanish, Thai, and Turkish as target languages.
