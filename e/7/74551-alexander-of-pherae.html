Alexander of Pherae in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Alexander of Pherae became ruler of Thessaly after poisoning and succeeding his brother Polydorus. He reigned for eleven years as a tyrant, ruling with oppression and becoming hated by all men. After Alexander, king of Macedonia, captured Larissa and Cranon, he fled to Pherae. He imprisoned Pelopidas and Ismenias, massacred the people of Scotussa, besieged Peparethos, fought the Thessalians, and received Athenian assistance against the Thebans. Though he won repeated victories and initially held the advantage, he was forced into peace: he restored captured towns, ceded Magnesia and Phthiotis to the Boeotians, and was confined to Pherae. He was ultimately murdered by his Theban wife, with help from his brothers Lycophron and Tisiphonus.

Alexander the Pheraean poisoned his brother Polydorus, succeeded him, and reigned for eleven years. He ruled tyrannically, became hated, and fled to Pherae after Alexander, king of Macedonia, captured Larissa and Cranon.

CHAP. VIIL

Alexander imprisoned Pelopidas and Ismenias, then fought the Boeotians in Thessaly with support from the Athenians and other allies. His cavalry attacked the Boeotian army during its retreat, but Epaminondas repulsed the attack and brought the army safely away. Alexander later massacred the inhabitants of Scotussa after surrounding them at a common assembly.

CHAP. IX,

Alexander of Pherae fought the Thessalians and defeated them repeatedly before Pelopidas arrived. He held the higher ground and initially prevailed, but after further defeat he was forced to make peace, restore captured towns, deliver Magnesia and Phthiotis to the Boeotians, and retain only Pherae.

CHAP. III.

Alexander of Pherae was murdered by his wife, who was a Theban, and by his two brothers, Lycophron and Tisiphonus. His killers were initially praised but then established their own rule with foreign soldiers and force.

BOOK XIV.

Alexander of Pherae imprisoned Pelopidas and Ismenias and acted cruelly at Scotussa. He was later murdered by his wife and two brothers.

Alexander the king of the Pheræans in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Alexander, king of the Pheræans, seized Scotusa in peacetime, killed men gathered in its theatre, enslaved women and children, and funded mercenaries with the proceeds.

CHAPTER V.

Alexander the king of the Pheræans attacked Scotusa during a time of peace while its people were gathered in the theatre. He killed the men who were there and sold the women and children, using the proceeds to maintain a mercenary army.

Alexander in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Alexander, Roxana’s son and a ruler in Thessaly, is associated with early death, victories over Darius and in India without trophies, hostility toward Thebes, and actions against Antipater. Though friendly to Pelopidas and the Thebans, he treacherously imprisoned Pelopidas; when Epaminondas approached with a Theban force, he lost confidence and released him. Cassander later sought to destroy Alexander’s house.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VII.

Alexander is said to have died early. The text suggests that Philip’s disregard for the principle that a conscientious man’s descendants will prosper left no reason for the gods to preserve Alexander’s life.

CHAPTER VII.

Alexander expelled the Thebans from Thebes. Cassander restored Thebes chiefly out of hatred for Alexander and sought to destroy Alexander's house.

CHAPTER XV.

Alexander, the ruler in Thessaly, treacherously imprisoned Pelopidas, who had come to his court as a personal and public friend. When Epaminondas took command of the Theban force near Pylæ, Alexander lost confidence in victory and released Pelopidas.
