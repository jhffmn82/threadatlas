Also called: RE-RAG framework, RE-RAG model, RE-RAGFlan−base, RE-RAGFlan−large, RE-RAGbase, RE-RAGlarge, our model, our system
  • In RE-RAG: Improving Open-Domain QA Performance and Interpretability with Relevance Estimator in Retrieval-Augmented Generation: RE-RAG is a proposed framework, model, and RAG system for open-domain question answering that extends traditional retrieval-augmented generation with an external relevance estimator (RE). Its central feature is the RE module: it evaluates question-context relevance and confidence, reranks initially retrieved contexts, and supplies more precise relevance scores for answer generation. RE-RAG replaces similarity-based marginalization weights with normalized RE scores and jointly trains its components. The estimator’s information is used in question answering and decoding, supporting the handling of unanswerable or low-confidence context sets while improving interpretability. At inference, the system uses an extra context-evaluation module and 25 contexts. It was evaluated on Natural Questions and TriviaQA, where reported results generally outperform comparable FiD-KD and other baselines; RE-RAGFlan-large reached 62.5 F1 on Natural Questions and 78.7 F1 on TriviaQA among the small-generator group. With the same total parameter count, it achieves higher accuracy than traditional RAG and performs competitively with FiD-based models. Ablations indicate that both improved context selection through reranking and improved context weighting through relevance scores contribute to performance. The framework is reported to enhance traditional RAG, but its effectiveness has not yet been sufficiently evaluated on multi-hop QA tasks; extending it to such tasks is future work.

RE-RAG in RE-RAG: Improving Open-Domain QA Performance and Interpretability with Relevance Estimator in Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Kiseung Kim and Jay-Yoon Lee · 2024

RE-RAG is a proposed framework, model, and RAG system for open-domain question answering that extends traditional retrieval-augmented generation with an external relevance estimator (RE). Its central feature is the RE module: it evaluates question-context relevance and confidence, reranks initially retrieved contexts, and supplies more precise relevance scores for answer generation. RE-RAG replaces similarity-based marginalization weights with normalized RE scores and jointly trains its components. The estimator’s information is used in question answering and decoding, supporting the handling of unanswerable or low-confidence context sets while improving interpretability. At inference, the system uses an extra context-evaluation module and 25 contexts. It was evaluated on Natural Questions and TriviaQA, where reported results generally outperform comparable FiD-KD and other baselines; RE-RAGFlan-large reached 62.5 F1 on Natural Questions and 78.7 F1 on TriviaQA among the small-generator group. With the same total parameter count, it achieves higher accuracy than traditional RAG and performs competitively with FiD-based models. Ablations indicate that both improved context selection through reranking and improved context weighting through relevance scores contribute to performance. The framework is reported to enhance traditional RAG, but its effectiveness has not yet been sufficiently evaluated on multi-hop QA tasks; extending it to such tasks is future work.
