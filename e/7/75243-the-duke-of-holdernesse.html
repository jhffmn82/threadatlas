the Duke of Holdernesse in The Return of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1903-09-26

The Duke of Holdernesse is the 6th Duke, a wealthy former Cabinet Minister, owner of extensive land, Holdernesse Hall, and Carston Castle, and Lord Lieutenant of Hallamshire since 1900. His family life is central: his unhappy marriage to the Duchess ended in separation, Lord Saltire is his legitimate younger son and heir, and James Wilder is his biological but publicly unacknowledged son and secretary. When Lord Saltire disappears after receiving the Duke’s letter, the Duke offers major rewards, seeks to avoid scandal, considers that the boy may have gone south with Heidegger, and permits Holmes to investigate while denying ransom demands or damaging matter in the letter. He initially conceals Wilder’s part in the kidnapping and Heidegger’s murder, and aids Reuben Hayes’s escape through money given to Wilder. Ultimately, he admits Wilder’s parentage and his concealment, agrees to recover Saltire, sends Wilder to Australia, and writes to the Duchess to resume their relationship.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

THE ADVENTURE OF THE PRIORY SCHOOL

The Duke of Holdernesse is the wealthy former Cabinet Minister and father of Lord Saltire. He offers five thousand pounds for information locating his son and another thousand for identifying those who took him, wants to avoid public scandal, and initially favors the possibility that Lord Saltire fled toward the south of France with Heidegger. He visits the Priory School with James Wilder, agrees that Holmes may investigate, and denies knowing of any ransom demand or harmful content in his letter to his son.

When the nobleman and his secretary had left, my friend flung

The Duke of Holdernesse is the father of the lost boy and is expected to be glad to receive news about him. His purse is mentioned as a possible means of persuading the arrested gipsies to reveal what they know.

At eleven o’clock next morning my friend and I were walking up

The Duke admits that James Wilder is his illegitimate son and that he concealed Wilder’s role in Lord Saltire’s kidnapping and Heidegger’s murder. He acknowledges aiding the escape of Reuben Hayes, agrees to retrieve Lord Saltire, sends James Wilder to Australia, and writes to the Duchess to resume their relationship.
