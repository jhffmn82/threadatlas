Archagathus in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Archagathus, son of Agathocles, is a daring commander who governs Africa and commands the Greeks after Agathocles departs. Enraged by Lyciscus’s insults and accusation concerning Alcia, he kills Lyciscus with a lance, provoking a mutiny and threats against both him and Agathocles. As commander in Libya, he divides his army after the Carthaginians divide theirs, loses forces under Eumachus and Eschrion, withdraws from Tunis, and seeks aid. Learning that Agathocles plans to leave Africa, he protests, but soldiers kill him and his brother after Agathocles escapes.

CHAP. IL

He became enraged when Lyciscus insulted him and accused him of an incestuous relationship with his stepmother, Alcia. Archagathus seized a lance and killed Lyciscus, provoking a mutiny in which the soldiers threatened to kill him and Agathocles.

Archagathus discovered Agathocles's plan to leave Africa with Heraclides and informed the captains and officers. He opposed being left behind after sharing the hardships and dangers of the war, but the soldiers later killed him and his brother after Agathocles escaped.

BOOK XX.

Archagathus, the son of Agathocles, kills Lyciscus and is placed in great danger by the army. He also carries out actions in Africa.
