kitchen (Toto's cottage) in Toto's Merry Winter

Laura E. Richards · 1887

The kitchen is Toto's cottage's warm, bright household center, with a great hearth fire, kettle, cookware, food stores, and silverware. In winter the family and animals gather there for supper and stories; later a robber searches it, but Miss Mary, the raccoon, and the kettle drive him out.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Toto's Merry Winter / CHAPTER I.

The kitchen is warm, bright, and inviting despite the cold snow outside. Its fire, shining cookware, kettle, and soup-kettle make it the center of the household's evening activities. The family and animals gather there for supper and storytelling.

CHAPTER IX.

The kitchen contains the fire, kettle, parrot, raccoon, food stores, and the drawer holding the silver teaspoons and porringer. The robber eats and searches there, but is driven out after Miss Mary raises the alarm and the raccoon and kettle attack him.
