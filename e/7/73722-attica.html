Also called: Attic soil, Attlea, country, τῆς ᾿Αττικῆς, ᾿Αττικήν, ᾿Αττικὴν
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Attica is described as a Greek and Athenian territory that suffered invasions, famine, devastation, and repeated Lacedaemonian attacks.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, Attica is the place where Polyperchon encamped near the Piraeus and left forces under Alexander when he marched into the Peloponnesus.
  • In The Library, Attica is described as the territory formerly called Acte and Cecropia, awarded to Athena, and later associated with Cranaus, Pandion, Dionysus, Demeter, and Icarius.
  • In Description of Greece, Attica is described as a promontory region of mainland Greece containing Athens, Sunium, harbours, fortified places, and many notable landscapes and monuments.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Attica is identified as the place where Ion died and was buried and where the Ionians later settled.

Attica in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Attica is a Greek region and Athenian territory, described by Endius as less extensively cultivated than the Peloponnese. Its record begins with an Amazon invasion: they encamped at the later-named Amazonian Field, but Theseus and the Athenians defeated and expelled them. It then suffered drought and famine after Minos’s dispute, alongside the murder of Androgeus near Oenoe and Theseus’s killing of Procrustes at Corydallus. Mardonius later devastated the abandoned region, followed by repeated Lacedaemonian invasions that burned towns, crops, and countryside, including campaigns under Archidamus and Agis.

Attica in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Attica is Attic soil, a promontory region on the mainland of Greece facing the Cyclades and the Ægean sea. Formerly called Actæa, it was said to be named for Atthis, daughter of Cranaus. It contains Athens, Sunium, the harbours of Piraeus, Munychia, and Phalerum, fortified places including Panactus, Salamis, Eleusis, and Munychia, and the setting of the Marathonian bull. Its numerous townships, mountains, islands, temples, altars, tombs, and monuments include Pentelicus, Parnes, Hymettus, and Alimus.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Description of Greece / BOOK I.--ATTICA.

Attica contains the promontory of Sunium, the harbours of Piraeus, Munychia, and Phalerum, and the city of Athens. It was formerly called Actæa, and the name Attica was said to derive from Atthis, the daughter of Cranaus.

CHAPTER XXV.

Attica included the fortified places of Panactus, Salamis, Munychia, the Piraeus, and Eleusis mentioned in the unit’s account of Macedonian control and Athenian resistance. It was also the setting of the Marathonian bull, which Theseus was said to have driven to the Acropolis and sacrificed to Athene.

CHAPTER XXXI.

Attica contains numerous townships, mountains, islands, temples, altars, tombs, and monuments. Its townships include Alimus, Zoster, Prasiæ, Marathon, Brauron, Rhamnus, Oropus, and others, while its mountains include Pentelicus, Parnes, Hymettus, and Anchesmus.
