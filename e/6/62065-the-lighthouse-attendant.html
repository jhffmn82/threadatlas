the lighthouse attendant in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

A young, intelligent lighthouse attendant of working-man status, sickly-faced and coughing, guides women through the Lizard Lights, explains its instruments, and recounts wrecks he has witnessed during their limited visit.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

" DAY THE EIGHTH And seven days were all we could allow ourselves at the Lizard, if we meant to see the rest of Cornwall.

The lighthouse attendant, described as an intelligent young man scarcely above the grade of a working man, conducts the women through the Lizard Lights. He explains the instruments and recounts several wrecks he has witnessed, despite appearing sickly and having a short cough.
