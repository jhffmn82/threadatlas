  • In Tik-Tok of Oz, Betsy Bobbin is a shipwrecked Oklahoma girl who travels through Oz, helps her companions, and is welcomed as a resident of the Emerald City.
  • In The Scarecrow of Oz, Betsy Bobbin lives in Ozma's palace, accompanies Dorothy, welcomes Trot and Cap'n Bill, and befriends Trot.
  • In The Lost Princess of Oz, Betsy Bobbin joins Dorothy and others in searching for the missing Ozma and stolen magical possessions.
  • In The Magic of Oz, Betsy Bobbin attends Ozma's birthday feast, seated next to Aunt Em and before the Shaggy Man.
  • In Glinda of Oz, Betsy joins a rescue expedition, helps wind Tik-Tok, notices Button Bright is lost, and recognizes the danger drying the lake poses to its fishes.
  • In The Royal Book of Oz, Betsy Bobbin reads with Princess Ozma and helps Ozma, Jack Pumpkinhead, and the Wizard settle the Horners-Hoppers war.
  • In The Lost King of Oz, Betsy Bobbin, shipwrecked with Hank and taken in by Ozma, joins events at the castle and travels toward the Emerald City.
  • In The Hungry Tiger of Oz, Betsy Bobbin joins Carter Green and other companions on an adventure through Rash, helps recover the lost rubies, and is promised the future role of Queen.
  • In Jack Pumpkinhead of Oz, Betsy Bobbin finds a magic belt and hides in a butterfly bush while Mogodore's army captures others.
  • In The Yellow Knight of Oz, Bettsy Bobbin helps Dorothy announce visitors and seeks missing companions while living in Oz with her mule Hank.

Betsy Bobbin in Tik-Tok of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1914

Betsy Bobbin is a small, ordinary mortal girl from Oklahoma whose shipwreck begins her journey. Thrown into the sea when her ship strikes a rock, she saves herself by holding Hank's tail, reaches a floating hatch-cover with him, and rides the storm to land. Hungry and without a home, she and her faithful mule seek food and shelter, and Betsy consistently defends and cares for Hank. After meeting Shaggy Man, Betsy joins his search for his lost brother. She is brave, curious, independent, and kind: she refuses Queen Ann's claim that the party is conquered or must be enslaved; helps rescue and wind Tik-Tok; crosses dangers with Hank and her companions; asks questions of queens, dragons, and others without fear; and remains interested in what will happen even when others are frightened. She travels through the Rose Kingdom, the Rubber Country, the Hollow Tube, Queen Erma's land, and the Nome King's cavern, often riding Hank when tired. In the Nome King's cavern, Kaliko hides and kindly feeds Betsy, then restores her to her friends. She approves of Kaliko as king, judges Ruggedo harshly for his deeds but still urges leniency when he seems harmless, helps him gather jewels, brings him food and drink, and asks Kaliko not to be hard on him. She also bravely kisses the Ugly One to test whether the act will undo a charm. At the end, Betsy leaves the cavern with Hank, Shaggy Man, and Shaggy Man's brother. The Wizard brings her to the Land of Oz, where Dorothy welcomes her, Ozma gives her rooms, and the animals recognize her as Hank's beloved little mistress. Amazed by talking animals and delighted with Oz, she wishes every child could live there.

Narrative, section by section

20 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Four

Betsy is thrown from the ship into the sea when it strikes a rock. She grabs Hank's tail, helps him aboard a floating hatch-cover, and rides with him through the storm. After sleeping on the raft, she wakes at dawn and sees land ahead.

Chapter Five

Betsy wades ashore from the raft with Hank and enters the greenhouse seeking people, food, and shelter. She explains that she and Hank survived a shipwreck by floating ashore on a raft, and she defends Hank when the Roses and the Royal Gardener are frightened by him.

Chapter Six

Betsy meets Shaggy Man after his fall and learns that he comes from the Land of Oz and knows Ozma and Dorothy. She helps pick Princess Ozga from the Royal Gardens and correctly observes that the Roses have no hearts, so the Love Magnet cannot affect them. After being driven from the Rose Kingdom, she agrees to help Shaggy Man find his lost brother.

Chapter Seven

Betsy discovers Polychrome and invites her to join the group. With Shaggy Man, she investigates the old well, helps pull out Tik-Tok, reads the inscriptions on his body, and winds his thought, speech, and action mechanisms.

Chapter Eight

Betsy objects when Queen Ann claims that her party has been conquered and refuses to become anyone's slave. She restrains Hank by his forelock and promises to keep Tik-Tok wound up if he becomes a soldier.

Chapter Nine

Betsy laughs at the officers' bouncing in the Rubber Country and uses Polychrome's suggestion to cross the brook by bouncing over it. She then calls Hank to follow her.

Chapter Ten

Betsy rides Hank backward when the mule suddenly begins sliding down the Hollow Tube, and she holds tightly to his neck. She speaks with Queen Ann, Shaggy Man, and Polychrome during the journey, emerges safely, and explains to Tubekins that they arrived through the Hollow Tube.

Chapter Eleven

Betsy is brought before the Private Citizen with the party and identifies herself when questioned. She is worried when her companions react fearfully to the name of the Great Jinjin, but she remains curious about what will happen to Ruggedo. Queen Erma invites her to be a guest, and Betsy asks that Polychrome accompany her.

Chapter Twelve

Betsy admires the six light maidens, asks Erma about the dragon emblem and Electra's age, and enjoys a lavish fairy feast. She becomes sleepy after eating and falls asleep in a soft bed.

Chapter Thirteen

Betsy accepts the return journey because Tititi-Hoochoo has promised to make it easier. She is astonished by Quox's enormous size, rides in the front seat with Hank, and questions him about his age, fire, and the nature of dragons.

Chapter Fifteen

Betsy talks with Quox and says that she loves onions after the dragon compares their smell with brimstone. She asks questions about gravity and cheers when the Army of Oogaboo advances.

Chapter Sixteen

Betsy Bobbin enters the Nome King's cavern on Hank and calmly asks whether Ruggedo has been conquered and for something to eat. Kaliko hides her in his room, where she eats bread and mushrooms and waits for possible visitors.

Chapter Seventeen

Betsy Bobbin is believed by Ruggedo to be in the Slimy Cave with Hank, but the executioners find no one there.

Chapter Nineteen

Betsy Bobbin is brought safely from Kaliko's private room and rejoins her friends. She approves of Kaliko becoming King and notices that the disappearance of Queen Ann and the Officers of Oogaboo seems like magic.

Chapter Twenty

Betsy thanks Quox with the others and later rides on the mule after becoming tired from walking. She says Ruggedo deserves no sympathy but agrees that he must be allowed to take as much treasure as he can carry in his pockets.

Chapter Twenty-One

Betsy is shocked by Queen Ann and the officers' ruined condition and helps Ruggedo gather jewels. She suggests that the Ugly One wear a mask and offers her handkerchief, though Shaggy Man decides it is too small.

Chapter Twenty-Two

Betsy learns about the Hotel Trees and Breakfast Trees in the natural forest and argues that Ruggedo should undo the ugliness he caused. She bravely kisses the Ugly One, then helps determine that her kiss did not break the charm and encourages Polychrome to try.

Chapter Twenty-Three

Betsy discovers Ruggedo hiding in a corner and softens toward him, bringing him food and drink. She asks Kaliko not to be hard on Ruggedo and later says goodbye sadly to Polychrome before returning to the cavern.

Chapter Twenty-Four

Betsy Bobbin leaves the Nome King's cavern with Hank, Shaggy Man, and Shaggy Man's brother. The Wizard later transports her to the Land of Oz, where Dorothy eagerly goes to greet her and Ozma arranges rooms for her.

Chapter Twenty-Five

Betsy is identified by Hank as his beloved little mistress and is welcomed as a friend by the animals. She is amazed that animals in Oz can talk, enjoys herself greatly in Oz, and wishes that every child could live there.

Betsy Bobbin in The Hungry Tiger of Oz

Ruth Plumly Thompson · 1926

Betsy Bobbin is a little, kind-hearted, polite girl from Oklahoma in the United States, an earth traveler living and staying in the Royal Palace of the Emerald City in Oz. She is friends with the Hungry Tiger and is known for marvelous adventures. At her birthday celebration in the Palace garden, she enjoys the guests and embraces the Hungry Tiger. Soon afterward, she meets Carter Green in the Emerald City, pays for strawberries with one of her emerald rings, and accompanies him in his vegetable cart after the Quick Sandals carry them toward and across the Deadly Desert. In Rash, Betsy and Carter are imprisoned after Carter angers Pasha Irashi. Betsy recognizes the Hungry Tiger, reassures Carter that he is harmless, learns of Irashi's rule, and intends to tell Ozma. Escaping with Carter and others, she meets Prince Evered of Rash and immediately befriends him. Their company passes through Down Town, Cave Inn, the Lost Labyrinth, and other dangers while seeking the lost Rash rubies and a way back to Oz. Betsy refuses to become a cash girl, warns against a dangerous ruby test, explains their journey to Kaliko, survives Guph's attack and the fire-fall, and fears for her companions during encounters with Giants and Immense City. While she is missing, Ozma worries and searches for her. Betsy reunites with Reddy, Carter, and the Hungry Tiger, later meets Ozma, recounts the adventure, and rejoices when the last ruby is found. She supports Reddy's conquest of Rash, holds his crown, and suggests punishment for Irashi and Ippty. With Reddy promised the future rule of Rash and Betsy promised the role of Queen, she returns to the Emerald City, where the travelers' adventures are celebrated. She still misses Dorothy and Ozma when away and wants to see Hank.

Betsy Bobbin encourages Reddy to conquer Rash and helps during the confrontation. She holds the crown of Rash for him, celebrates the victory, and suggests that Irashi and Ippty deserve punishment.

CHAPTER 20
