the men of Regos and Coregos in Rinkitink in Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1916

The men of Regos and Coregos are Northmen: fierce, unruly, wild, lawless warriors belonging to King Gos and based in the City of Regos when not on expeditions. They conquer Pingaree, seize its people as slaves for mines and grain fields, plunder its palace of pearls, fabrics, and metals, destroy the palace and houses, and take its boats. They continue raiding islands and enslaving conquered people, but cannot injure Inga, Rinkitink, or Bilbil; Bilbil’s charge makes them flee, and they abandon Regos with King Gos for Coregos. Afterward they are especially reckless and deliberately annoy common people, though they avoid Bilbil after he bruises them. Captain Buzzub later commands them and keeps them silent and orderly as Inga, Rinkitink, and Bilbil depart.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Three

The men of Regos and Coregos land on Pingaree, capture its people, remove valuables from the palace, destroy the palace and every house they find, and take all the boats they can locate. They leave the island with hundreds of slaves, rich plunder, and pearls.

Chapter Seven

The warriors of Regos raid and pillage other islands and enslave conquered people. They cannot injure Inga, Rinkitink, or Bilbil, flee from Bilbil's charge, abandon the City of Regos with King Gos, and cross to Coregos.

Chapter Eleven

King Gos’s warriors are described as wild, lawless, reckless, and fond of annoying common people. Zella fears encountering them and avoids both the path they use and the city of Regos.

Chapter Twelve

The warriors of Regos are identified by Bilbil as dangerous foes. When Bilbil attacks, they tumble down the stairs, become bruised and sore, and mostly avoid interfering with his escape.

Chapter Sixteen

Captain Buzzub is given command of the warriors of Regos and promises to keep them quiet and orderly if he can. They silently watch Inga, Rinkitink, and Bilbil depart and appear glad to see them leave.

the warriors of Regos and Coregos in Rinkitink in Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1916

The warriors of Regos and Coregos are fierce, skilled, numerous invaders. They ravage Pingaree, enslave its people, seize valuables and boats, and depart with Prince Inga’s parents captive. Later, Rinkitink fears they may kill or capture three travelers attempting to confront them.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Four

The invaders depart Pingaree in a fleet carrying Prince Inga's parents, the people of Pingaree, and everything valuable they can take. They leave the island's houses ruined or burned and remove all the boats from the shore.

Chapter Six

The warriors of Regos and Coregos are described as fierce, skilled, and numerous. Rinkitink doubts that three travelers could conquer them and fears they may kill or capture the group.
