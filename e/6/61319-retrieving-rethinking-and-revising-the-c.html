This is the EMNLP 2024 paper “Retrieving, Rethinking and Revising: The Chain-of-Verification Can Improve Retrieval Augmented Generation,” by Bolei He, Nuo Chen, Xinran He, Lingyong Yan, Zhenkai Wei, Jinchang Luo, and Zhen-Hua Ling, published in Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EMNLP 2024, pages 10371–10393. It addresses hallucinations in Large Language Model question answering, arguing that Retrieval Augmented Generation can still fail when queries are unsuitable, retrieved context is erroneous, or generated answers exceed internal knowledge boundaries. The authors propose CoV-RAG, which couples a generator and retriever with a Chain-of-Verification mechanism. At inference, the retriever obtains five references, the language model creates an initial answer, then evaluates references and answer, produces verification results, and can revise the question. When re-retrieval is indicated, updated references are retrieved and a replacement answer is generated. For training, vanilla RAG data are split between RAG and verification tasks; RAG sampling and verification-data synthesis create positive and negative examples. GPT-4 evaluates references and answers, producing judgments, rationales, and revised queries, and verified augmented training uses multi-task learning for generation and verification outputs. Experiments on Natural Questions, Web Questions, TriviaQA, and Mintaka compare CoV-RAG with Naive LLMs, RAG models, and verification/rewriting systems including ChatGPT, WebGLM, Self-RAG, and RRR. Results report that CoV-RAG surpasses WebGLM and other methods, while multi-iteration CoV-RAG performs best across tested models and datasets. Analyses attribute improvements to post-retrieval revision and the score-to-judge-to-revise structure; GPT-4 evaluations also favor CoV-RAG on citation, correctness, truthfulness, bias, and conciseness. The paper concludes that verification prompting and revised-query re-retrieval improve both external retrieval and internal generation. Limitations include reliance on a small LM distilled from GPT-4 for training data, efficiency costs, and cases where revised questions remain incorrect and may require multi-round validation. The authors state that criteria were standardized and data and code are publicly available.

Abstract

The abstract concerns Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) and its use with Large Language Models (LLMs). It identifies query-unsuitability as a cause of erroneous retrieved context and knowledge-boundary limitations as a cause of answers inconsistent with external references. It proposes CoV-RAG, which integrates a verification module for scoring, judgment, and rewriting to improve external retrieval correctness and internal generation consistency. Experiments across various LLMs show that CoV-RAG is effective and adaptable, significantly surpassing state-of-the-art baselines with different LLM backbones.

Introduction

The unit explains how Large Language Models support question answering but can produce hallucinations caused by factual errors and knowledge limitations. Retrieval augmented generation reduces these problems by supplying externally retrieved documents, although errors can still arise from the retriever or the generator. The authors introduce CoV-RAG, which uses a Chain-of-Verification to assess references and answers, revise queries, re-retrieve information, and improve generation during training and inference. Experiments on four public datasets report improved factuality across multiple models and retrieval tools.

2 / Methods .. Algorithm 1 CoV-RAG Inference

This unit describes CoV-RAG, a retrieval-augmented generation model that combines a generator with a Chain-of-Verification mechanism to improve factuality and consistency. In its inference process, the retriever module R retrieves five references for a question, and the language model M generates an initial answer. M then evaluates the references and answer, produces verification results, and may revise the question. If the re-retrieval indicator is true, R retrieves updated references and M generates a replacement answer.

4 / Results and Analysis / 4.1 / Main Results .. 4.4 / Further Analysis on Retriever

This unit evaluates CoV-RAG, a retrieval-augmented generation method, on open-domain question answering across Natural Questions, WebQuestions, Mintaka, and TriviaQA. CoV-RAG outperforms WebGLM and other comparison methods, with multi-iteration CoV-RAG achieving the strongest results across the tested models and datasets. Ablation studies find that revising after retrieval and using a score-to-judge-to-revise Chain of Verification structure improve performance. GPT-4-based evaluation further reports that CoV-RAG achieves better citation, correctness, truthfulness, bias, and conciseness results, while multi-iteration retrieval improves retrieval accuracy with Bing and Chrome.

5 / Related Work

The unit discusses hallucinations in large language models and methods for addressing them, including detection, data augmentation, and retrieval-augmented generation. It describes the components of a typical RAG framework, including the retriever and generator, and reviews techniques for improving retrieval and generation. It compares WebGLM and Self-RAG, which jointly optimize the RAG process, with CoV-RAG, which uses chain of thought training and query revising. The unit states that CoV-RAG aims to improve generation quality, retrieval reliability, and performance on questions unsuitable for retrieval.

6 / Conclusion

The paper introduces CoV-RAG, a retrieval-augmented generation method designed to mitigate hallucinations during internal generation and external retrieval. It integrates chain of verification prompting into fine-tuned RAG generators to identify and mitigate generation errors. The prompting also refines external contextual knowledge by re-retrieving a revised query. Experiments across different language model backbones show that CoV-RAG detects generation errors and significantly improves generation quality.

Limitations

The unit discusses limitations of the CoV-RAG framework and offers insights for future research. A small size LM distilled from GPT-4 generates training data for the generator to reduce time and financial costs, although using GPT-4 for all training data may improve performance against baselines. For efficiency, the retriever re-retrieves relevant references during verification before the LM predicts and outputs the final answer. Because a revised question may not yield the correct answer, multi-round validation may be needed and is left for future work.
