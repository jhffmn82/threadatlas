  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, fire, called Vulcan, became known when lightning ignited a mountain tree, and its usefulness aided human generation, perfection, and security.
  • In The Trojan Women of Euripides, fire is flung into Troy’s heart by the Greeks, consuming the city’s houses and walls while its flames and smoke rise into the sky.
  • In The Library, fire is a valuable substance obtained through theft or recovery, enabling warmth and mechanical arts and serving protective, purifying, courageous, and changeling-removing purposes.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, flames spread from Phaëton’s chariot through heaven, earth, and sea, threatening the universe until Jupiter restrains them; afterward, they provide light when the Sun is hidden.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, fire is lighter than air, rises toward the highest regions, can become dense air, and may arise volcanically from wind, friction, bituminous matter, or sulphur before eventually dying when its fuel is exhausted.

fire in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Fire is a valuable substance, often first possessed or withheld by a deity, animal, bird, or other supernatural being. It reaches people through theft or recovery: Beaver steals it from the Sky people, brings glowing coals after rain extinguishes fire on earth, and fire is made on earth for the first time by the Lame Boy. Its transfer explains its continuing presence in trees, wood, and stones, from which it can be elicited by friction, boring, rubbing sticks, or striking stones. Once obtained, it lets Earth people keep warm in the upper air, enables the mechanical arts, and is preserved for later use. In legend and custom, it also serves Demeter’s attempted gift of immortality and protects, purifies, gives courage, or removes fairy changelings when children pass over, around, or through fire or smoke.

flames in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Flames, produced from flint and steel, spread from the chariot through heaven, earth, and sea, burning clouds, lands, peoples, waters, and Phaëton’s yellow hair. They threaten the universe and world’s destruction until Jupiter restrains them with ruthless fires; afterward they provide light when the Sun’s face is hidden.
