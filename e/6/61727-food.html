food in Scientific American Supplement No. 360, November 25, 1882

Various · 1881

Food is any substance taken into the body to build or repair tissues, provide energy and animal heat, or control or assist these functions. Broadly, it includes water and air, though they are not ordinarily recognized as food.

food in Child's Health Primer for Primary Classes

Jane Andrews · 1885

Food includes beef and supplies the body with water, lime, salt, flesh-making substances, and fat-making substances. Much comes from the earth through grains, grass, vegetables, and animals that eat them. Meat, vegetables, and bread require cooking; food is then chewed, swallowed, cut and ground by teeth, mixed with gastric juice, and changed and worked over in the stomach. The liver and other organs partly prepare it for passage from the intestines into the blood. Blood carries it to every part of the body, feeding bones, muscles, nerves, brain, skin, finger nails, hair, and eyes. It makes and mends bone, helps children grow, repairs worn-out parts in grown people, and, when good and simple, supports strength. Good food helps make good blood; named foods serve muscles, fat, and heat.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER X. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Food is cut and ground by the teeth, then mixed with gastric juice and changed in the stomach. Afterward, it is sent through the body to feed the bones, muscles, nerves, brain, skin, finger nails, hair, and eyes. Children need food to grow, while grown people need it to mend parts that wear out.

] CHAPTER XI. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

CHAPTER XII. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Food is cooked, chewed, swallowed, worked over in the stomach, and prepared for passage into the blood. The blood carries its nourishment to every part of the body.

CHAPTER XIII. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

Food supplies the blood with materials to carry to every part of the body. Meat, milk, eggs, wheat, corn, and oats are named as foods for muscles, while sugar, starch, fat, cream, and oil are named for fat and heat.

CHAPTER XIV. .. REVIEW QUESTIONS.

The stomach prepares food, after which the blood takes it up and carries it throughout the body. Good food helps make good blood.
