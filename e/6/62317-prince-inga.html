  • In Rinkitink in Oz: Prince Inga is the only child and heir of King Kitticut and Queen Garee, prince of Pingaree: a small but grave, thoughtful, industrious boy trained in learning, just rule, and pearl-fishing. Before disaster, he studies his father’s manuscripts, values teachings about goodness, and learns that his family’s three magical pearls must be guarded. When invaders destroy Pingaree and carry off his parents and people, Inga survives by hiding in his favorite tree. He rescues King Rinkitink, joins him and Bilbil, recovers the Blue, Pink, and White Pearls from the ruins, and follows the White Pearl’s guidance toward Regos and Coregos. The pearls define his rescue mission: the Blue gives him strength, the Pink protects him, and the White advises and guides him. With Rinkitink and Bilbil, Inga takes the silver-lined boat to Regos, conquers it despite his innocent appearance, and rules there kindly. Though he loses the magical shoes containing the pearls and is captured by Queen Cor, he endures, regains the shoes, frees enslaved women and children of Pingaree, and continues seeking his parents and people. He liberates slaves, organizes the people’s return and rebuilding, appoints leaders for Regos and Pingaree, and refuses to abandon the search when his parents are taken to the nome caverns. Inga faces imprisonment and deadly trials underground, breaking chains through the Blue Pearl’s strength and escaping dangers through courage and invention. He survives the Three Trick Caverns and remains determined until Dorothy helps secure his parents’ release. He returns to Pingaree to see its rebuilding, restores the three Magic Pearls to their hidden place, and later prepares to use them again when a fleet approaches.

Prince Inga in Rinkitink in Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1916

Prince Inga is the only child and heir of King Kitticut and Queen Garee, prince of Pingaree: a small but grave, thoughtful, industrious boy trained in learning, just rule, and pearl-fishing. Before disaster, he studies his father’s manuscripts, values teachings about goodness, and learns that his family’s three magical pearls must be guarded. When invaders destroy Pingaree and carry off his parents and people, Inga survives by hiding in his favorite tree. He rescues King Rinkitink, joins him and Bilbil, recovers the Blue, Pink, and White Pearls from the ruins, and follows the White Pearl’s guidance toward Regos and Coregos. The pearls define his rescue mission: the Blue gives him strength, the Pink protects him, and the White advises and guides him. With Rinkitink and Bilbil, Inga takes the silver-lined boat to Regos, conquers it despite his innocent appearance, and rules there kindly. Though he loses the magical shoes containing the pearls and is captured by Queen Cor, he endures, regains the shoes, frees enslaved women and children of Pingaree, and continues seeking his parents and people. He liberates slaves, organizes the people’s return and rebuilding, appoints leaders for Regos and Pingaree, and refuses to abandon the search when his parents are taken to the nome caverns. Inga faces imprisonment and deadly trials underground, breaking chains through the Blue Pearl’s strength and escaping dangers through courage and invention. He survives the Three Trick Caverns and remains determined until Dorothy helps secure his parents’ release. He returns to Pingaree to see its rebuilding, restores the three Magic Pearls to their hidden place, and later prepares to use them again when a fleet approaches.

Narrative, section by section

21 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter One

Prince Inga is King Kitticut's only child and heir to Pingaree. He is thoughtful, industrious, skilled at pearl fishing, and taught reading, writing, arithmetic, and the principles of just rule by his father. Kitticut tells him about the three magical pearls and instructs him to guard them if the King dies.

Chapter Two

Prince Inga follows King Kitticut to the beach and observes King Rinkitink and Bilbil. He receives gifts from Rinkitink, listens to the parchment, and says that a teaching that makes one good must be worth its weight in pearls.

Chapter Three

Prince Inga sleeps in his favorite tree when the invasion begins. He hides among its upper branches, watches his parents and the people being captured, and witnesses the destruction of the palace and the rest of Pingaree.

Chapter Four

Prince Inga remains hidden in a tree until the invaders' fleet disappears, then searches the ruins for food and water. He rescues King Rinkitink from the well with Bilbil's help and explores Pingaree with them, grieving the loss of his parents, home, and country.

Chapter Five

Prince Inga returns with King Rinkitink and Bilbil to the ruins of Pingaree and clears a room for shelter. He moves marble blocks to uncover the hiding place of the three pearls, secretly retrieves them, and places the Blue Pearl and Pink Pearl in his shoes while wearing the White Pearl in a bag around his neck. The White Pearl advises him to go to the Islands of Regos and Coregos to liberate his parents, and he resolves to follow that advice.

Chapter Six

Prince Inga finds the beautiful silver-lined boat and claims it as his own. He bargains with King Rinkitink, then rows the boat effortlessly toward Regos and Coregos, trusting the Magic Pearls to guide and protect him as he attempts to rescue his parents and people.

Chapter Seven

Prince Inga arrives at Regos to free his parents and people and boldly demands that the invaders surrender their city and island. Protected by the Pink Pearl and strengthened by the Blue Pearl, he advances through attacks, breaks the iron gates, and conquers Regos.

Chapter Eight

Inga takes formal possession of the palace in Regos and shares a bedroom with King Rinkitink for protection. After losing both magic shoes, he follows the Voice of the Pearl's advice and keeps the loss secret, then begins governing Regos with kindness.

Chapter Ten

Prince Inga tells Queen Cor that he intends to free his parents and people and recover the goods taken from Pingaree. Queen Cor captures and binds him, takes him to Coregos, and orders him to serve as her page; the White Pearl advises him to endure bravely and promises that conditions will soon improve.

Chapter Thirteen

Prince Inga is forced to serve Queen Cor but remains concerned for the abused people of Pingaree. He recognizes his magical shoes on Zella, exchanges shoes with her, and regains the Magic Pearls' power. He takes the keys from Queen Cor's slave driver, frees the women and children, and begins searching for Queen Garee.

Chapter Fourteen

Inga uses the White Pearl to learn how to summon the boat from Pingaree, then sails with Rinkitink, Bilbil, and Zella to Regos to rescue his father and the people of Pingaree. At the mines, he learns that King Gos has taken King Kitticut away, breaks the guards' chains, liberates the slaves, and organizes them to march toward the City of Regos.

Chapter Fifteen

Prince Ingato intends to liberate his parents and all the people of Pingaree and return them to their island. After learning that his parents have been taken away, he vows to follow them and search the world for them.

Chapter Sixteen

Prince Inga appoints Captain Buzzub to command the warriors of Regos, frees most of King Gos's former slaves, and sends the people of Pingaree home with goods to rebuild their island. He appoints Nikobob to oversee the restoration of Pingaree and then follows King Gos's boat.

Chapter Eighteen

Inga follows the White Pearl's guidance to the caverns of the nomes in order to rescue King Kitticut and Queen Garee. He refuses to abandon the effort despite King Kaliko's warnings, explores the caverns with King Rinkitink, and is eventually deceived, separated from his companions, and chained in a small underground chamber.

Inga breaks his chains and handcuffs with the strength given by the Blue Pearl. He defeats the giant with an iron post, avoids falling into the abyss when the cavern floor collapses, and leaps across the gulf to reach the opposite passageway.

Prince Inga finds the last cavern filled with glowing coals and initially fears that he cannot escape. He invents a plan, tears rocks from the corridor to make stepping-stones, crosses the cavern, and eventually rejoins King Kaliko, Klik, King Rinkitink, and Bilbil.

Chapter Nineteen

Inga undergoes dangerous trials in the Three Trick Caverns while Rinkitink and Bilbil face Kaliko's magic. Kaliko expects the trials may have destroyed him, but Inga is found still alive.

Chapter Twenty

Inga escapes the destruction of Pingaree with King Rinkitink and the talking goat, finds the Magic Pearls, and rows the silver-lined boat to Regos to rescue his parents. He later follows the King of Regos and Queen of Coregos to the Nome King's country and enters the caverns, where Dorothy sees him in danger and helplessness.

Chapter Twenty-One

Inga remains determined to rescue his captive parents from the Nome King's caverns. He is overjoyed when Dorothy helps secure their release and returns with them and the others to the Land of Oz.

Chapter Twenty Three

Inga returns with the others to Pingaree because he wants to see how the people have rebuilt their homes. He places the three Magic Pearls back in their secret cavity beneath the banquet hall floor.

Chapter Twenty-Four

Prince Inga joins the watchers when the fleet approaches. Rinkitink asks him to get his pearls so they can protect him from the people of Gilgad.
