the Lizard Lights in An Unsentimental Journey through Cornwall

Dinah Maria Craik · 1884

The Lizard Lights are an early lighthouse experiment: a huge, dazzling-white low building resembling Sydenham Crystal Palace, with a tower at either side. At sunset its two powerful lights, each stated at 20,000 candles, replace the suns and send beams across sea and landscape. They are visible far into darkness from the sea and moonlit countryside, claimed at twenty miles and perhaps thirty, but fog hides them; then a fog-horn sounds. First watched nightly from afar, they become the narrator’s only visible illumination during the storm’s darkest phase and later a familiar yet beautiful sight. On the eighth day, the women visit and see the enormous lamps, ladders, lighting apparatus, and fog-horn machinery, whose workings they cannot understand.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

"Shall we get a boat?

The Lizard Lights consist of two powerful lights that become visible when the sun sets. Their beams extend far across the ocean and are said to be visible twenty or even thirty miles away, except in fog, when a fog-horn sounds.

DAY THE FOURTH Sunday, September 4th--and we had started on September 1st; was it possible we had only been travelling four days?

The Lizard Lights gleam across the landscape and sea at night. During the storm's darkest phase, their ghostly light is the only illumination visible to the narrator.

DAY THE SIXTH And a day absolutely divine!

The Lizard Lights are seen from the moonlit countryside after the evening service. Their far-gleaming appearance has become familiar to the narrator and companions but remains beautiful.

" DAY THE EIGHTH And seven days were all we could allow ourselves at the Lizard, if we meant to see the rest of Cornwall.

The women visit the Lizard Lights after watching them night after night from a distance. They see the huge white building, its enormous lamps, ladders, lighting apparatus, and fog-horn machinery, though they admit they cannot understand the mechanisms.
