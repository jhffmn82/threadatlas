the Battle of Plataea in Four Plays of Aeschylus

Aeschylus · -0471

The Battle of Plataea is a land battle and Persian defeat, foretold as punishment for destroying Greek shrines and temples. Near Plataea, Lacedaemonians, Athenians, and Tegeans defeat Mardonius’s Persian force, destroying especially its rear guard.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE PERSIANS

The battle near Plataea destroys the three hundred thousand men left by Xerxes under Mardonius. The victorious forces are the Lacedaemonians, Athenians, and Tegeans.

The GHOST OF DARIUS rises.

The Plataea battle is foretold as the place where the Persian rear-guard will receive its final and terrible punishment. Darius connects its future slaughter with the Persian destruction of Greek shrines and temples.

Battle of Platæa in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

At Platæa, Pausanias led the Lacedæmonians as Greeks fought Mardonius, Medes, and barbarians. After Mardonius fell, the barbarians were annihilated; Pausanias protected his body and returned a Coan woman to Cos. Greeks later commemorated victory at Olympia with a Zeus statue.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK III.--LACONIA.

At the battle of Platæa, Pausanias led the Lacedæmonians and the barbarians were annihilated after Mardonius fell. Pausanias protected Mardonius’ body from mutilation and sent a Coan woman associated with the Persian camp back to Cos.

CHAPTER XXII.

The Battle of Platæa is commemorated at Olympia by a statue of Zeus dedicated by the Greeks who fought against Mardonius and the Medes. The dedication lists the states that participated in the action.
