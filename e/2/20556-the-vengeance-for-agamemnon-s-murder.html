the vengeance for Agamemnon’s murder in The House of Atreus; Being the Agamemnon, the Libation-Bearers, and the Furies

Aeschylus · -0457

The vengeance for Agamemnon’s murder is a divinely required act of justice, commanded by Apollo and carried out by Orestes. Cassandra first foretells that a banished wanderer will return to kill his mother and complete the house’s wrongdoing. Orestes then undertakes the planned deaths of Clytemnestra and Aegisthus, driven by Apollo’s oracle, grief, and lost rule. The Chorus presents the punishment as justice aided by divine powers.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

[_Exeunt all but Cassandra and the Chorus._

Cassandra foretells that a banished wanderer will return to avenge his father’s blood and complete the house’s cycle of wrongdoing by killing his mother. She says that the high gods have sworn that this vengeance will occur.

ORESTES (_approaching suddenly_)

The vengeance for Agamemnon’s murder is required by Apollo’s oracle, by grief for the dead king, and by the loss of his wealth and rule. Orestes undertakes it by planning the deaths of Clytemnestra and Aegisthus.

[_Enter Orestes and Pylades, in guise of travellers_.

The Chorus presents Orestes’ deed as an act of justice and vengeance directed by Apollo and aided by divine powers. It says that Justice and Craft have brought punishment upon the wrongdoers and that divine law forbids service to wrongdoing.
