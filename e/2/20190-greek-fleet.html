Greek fleet in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Greek fleet, first commanded by Eurybiades with Themistocles directing other matters, sailed to Artemisium to oppose the Persian fleet. After fighting there, it withdrew to Salamis and defeated the Persians in the narrow straits. Later, under Leotychides and Xantippus, it sailed from Delos to Mycale, then returned to Samos.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

LAST TEN BOOKS / BOOK XI.

The Grecian fleet was ordered to sail to Artemisium in Euboea to oppose the Persian fleet. Eurybiades, a Lacedaemonian, served as its admiral.

Having now said enough of these gallant men, we shall return

The Greek fleet fought the Persian fleet near Artemisium and later withdrew to Salamis. Under Eurybiades and Themistocles, it was arranged to exploit the narrow straits and defeated the Persians there.

CHAP. IV.

The Greek fleet, commanded by Leotychides the Lacedaemonian and Xantippus the Athenian, sailed from Delos toward Mycale with 250 galleys. It supported the battle against the Persians and afterward sailed back to Samos.

Aeschylus · -0471

Greek naval force of about three hundred galleys and ten select ships advances in order, exploits confined waters, and destroys the larger Persian fleet by ramming and encirclement at Salamis.
