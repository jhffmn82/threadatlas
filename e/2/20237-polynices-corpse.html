  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, it is ordered to remain unburied and exposed, later found untouched by animals, thinly dust-covered, and given the required burial rites.

Polynices' corpse in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Polynices' corpse is ordered to remain unburied and exposed, suffering dogs and vultures and polluting the altars; later it is found untouched by animals, thinly dust-covered and given the required burial rites.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ANTIGONE

Polynices’ corpse is found outside a grave, with no signs that animals have touched it. It has been covered with a thin layer of dust and treated with the rites required for burial.

CHORUS

Polynices' corpse is left without obsequies and exposed above the ground. Dogs and vultures feed on it, and its pollution reaches the altars and causes the gods to refuse sacrifices.
