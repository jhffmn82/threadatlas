The unnamed narrator began working for the Daily Minnesotian in St. Paul in 1858, when Geo. W. Moore was part owner and manager. The paper issued an extra about Minnesota's admission into the Union after the Pioneer had already published the news.

THE FIVE MILLION LOAN ELECTION.

The Minnesotian, edited by Dr. Thomas Foster, was the only prominent paper identified as opposing the Five Million Loan measure. It later denounced Gov. Sibley and the railroad bonds as fraudulent and illegal, and its attacks caused concern that eastern investors would view them as reflecting local opinion.

The Minnesotian was a Republican paper whose editor, Dr. Foster, engaged in a bitter rivalry with Thomas M. Newson of the Times. It was temporarily consolidated with the Times as the Minnesotian and Times, then returned to Dr. Foster's control after the partnership ended. The paper later faced competition from the St. Paul Daily Press, and Dr. Foster eventually sold the entire plant to its projectors.

TERRITORIAL PRINTERS IN CIVIL LIFE.

The Minnesotian was published by George W. Moore and John P. Owens beginning in 1852. George W. Moore sold his interest in it to Dr. Foster in 1860, and J.Q.A. Ward served as its business manager during its prosperous period.

HALF A CENTURY WITH THE PIONEER PRESS.

The Minnesotian employed Frank Moore as a printer’s “devil” beginning April 17, 1858. The Pioneer Press absorbed the Minnesotian in 1861, and Moore continued with the resulting paper.
