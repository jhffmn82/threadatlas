Dorothy and the Wizard in Oz in Dorothy and the Wizard in Oz

Narrative, section by section

21 sections; each heading opens the text

To My Readers

L. Frank Baum explains that children have persuaded him to write another story about the Land of Oz. The current book follows Dorothy on another adventure and includes the Wizard, Princess Ozma, Jim the Cab-Horse, the Nine Tiny Piglets, and Eureka the Kitten. Baum says the children’s suggestions helped shape the book, and he considers it partly theirs. He also expresses gratitude for their letters, friendship, and support.

1. The Earthquake

Dorothy Gale arrives at Hugson's Siding from San Francisco with her kitten Eureka and is met by Zebediah, who takes her toward Hugson's Ranch in a buggy. The train was delayed because repeated earthquakes made the engineer fear the rails would spread apart. As Dorothy and Zebediah travel, a powerful earthquake opens cracks in the earth, frightening them and stopping Jim. A second crack opens beneath the horse, pulling Jim, the buggy, Dorothy, and Zebediah into a pit.

2. The Glass City

Dorothy, Zeb, and Jim fall through a great crack in the earth with the buggy and land safely on the roof of a glass city. They discover six colored suns, talking animals, and air that supports people and animals as they walk through it. The expressionless people of the Glass City blame the newcomers for the Rain of Stones and send them to the House of the Sorcerer to prove their innocence.

3. The Arrival Of The Wizard

Dorothy and Zeb arrive with their horse and buggy in the Land of the Mangaboos, where the Prince questions them about the Rain of Stones. The thorn-covered Sorcerer Gwig claims the newcomers caused the damage, but the man with the star challenges his predictions. A balloon brings Oz, the Great and Terrible, who explains that an earthquake opened a crack through which he descended from the surface of the earth. Oz defeats Gwig in a contest of magic, but Gwig tries to stop Oz's breath, prompting Oz to cut him in two; the Prince then explains that the Mangaboos are vegetable beings.

4. The Vegetable Kingdom

The Prince leads Dorothy, Zeb, the Wizard, Jim, and Eureka through the glass city’s gardens in the Land of the Mangaboos. The Mangaboos plant the two halves of the Sorcerer in a planting-ground, expecting him to sprout into a bush producing more sorcerers. The travelers learn that Mangaboos grow on plants, become alive when picked, and usually live about five years without eating. The Prince reveals that the Royal Princess will succeed him, but he plans to destroy the strangers rather than let them remain in his country.

5. Dorothy Picks the Princess

Dorothy and the Wizard pick the Royal Princess of Mangaboo, who receives the Star of Royalty and becomes the ruler after accusing the Prince of selfishly delaying her picking. Dorothy, Zeb, the Wizard, Eureka, and Jim explore the vegetable gardens, where they eat fruits and discover that the Wizard's tiny piglets can talk. The Wizard catches a fish for the hungry Eureka, then the group returns to the city and takes shelter in the House of the Sorcerer. They find that the people of Mangaboo walk in the air and that the country has no apparent night or need for stairs.

6. The Mangaboos Prove Dangerous

The Wizard, Zeb, and Dorothy learn that the Princess intends to destroy them for trespassing in the Land of the Mangaboos, while sending Jim the cab-horse and Eureka to the Black Pit. The Wizard uses lanterns and kerosene oil to create a fire test that scorches the Mangaboo advisors and convinces the Princess to spare the three humans. For several days they remain undisturbed in the House of the Sorcerer, until the Mangaboos drive Jim, Eureka, and the nine tiny piglets toward the mountain. Jim and Eureka fight back, but the numerous Mangaboos eventually force them to continue toward the Black Pit.

7. Into the Black Pit and Out Again

The Mangaboos imprison Jim and the piglets in the Black Pit by blocking its entrance with glass rocks. Eureka escapes and alerts Dorothy and the Wizard, who return with Zeb to attempt a rescue but become trapped inside the mountain with the prisoners. They use lanterns to guide Jim and the buggy through a steep tunnel, eventually emerging into a bright and charming new country.

8. The Valley of Voices

Dorothy, Zeb, the Wizard, Jim, Eureka, and the piglets enter a beautiful but seemingly deserted valley after escaping through a mountain from the Country of the Mangaboos. The piglets eat a strange fruit and become invisible, leading the Wizard to warn the others not to eat it. At a cottage, the travelers discover that the people of Voe are also invisible because they eat the fruit, which protects them from invisible bears that hunt them. The people of Voe welcome the travelers and offer them food.

9. They Fight the Invisible Bears

Dorothy, the Wizard, Zeb, Eureka, and Jim meet an unseen family in the Valley of Voe and learn that the dama-fruit makes people and animals invisible. The family warns them about the bears and tells them that the route home leads through Pyramid Mountain, its spiral staircase, and the Land of Naught, where Gargoyles live. When invisible bears attack, an unseen girl gives them a broad-leaved plant that lets them walk on water, and the travelers escape across the broad river. They later leave the river, race Jim to Pyramid Mountain, and arrive safely at its foot.

10. The Braided Man of Pyramid Mountain

Dorothy, the Wizard, Jim, and Zeb begin climbing Pyramid Mountain to escape the Valley of Voe. They travel up broad, winding stairs through darkness and stop at landings overlooking the Valley of Voe and a black sea below the clouds. In a cavern halfway up the mountain, they meet a braided old inventor who makes Assorted Flutters and Rustles. Dorothy gives him a blue hair-ribbon, and he gives the travelers boxes of his products before they continue their journey.

11. They Meet the Wooden Gargoyles

The Wizard, Dorothy, Zeb, Jim, and Eureka complete a difficult climb and emerge from a spiral stairway into the Country of the Gargoyles. They discover a silent wooden land inhabited by small flying Gargoyles and prepare to defend themselves. Jim scatters the first attackers with his iron-shod hooves, while the Wizard fires a revolver during a second attack. Zeb captures a crowned Gargoyle and ties it inside the buggy.

12. A Wonderful Escape

The Gargoyles overwhelm Dorothy, Zeb, the Wizard, Jim, and Eureka after the Wizard's revolver ammunition is exhausted, then carry them to a tall prison house in a wooden city. Eureka discovers that the Gargoyles remove and store their wings while resting, allowing Zeb to steal eight wings. The Wizard, Zeb, Dorothy, and Eureka attach the wings to Jim and the buggy and escape by flight toward a rock containing an archway, while the Gargoyles pursue them.

13. The Den of the Dragonettes

Dorothy, the Wizard, and Zeb escape the Gargoyles by burning their wooden wings and doors, then travel through difficult passages inside a mountain. They reach a vast cavern inhabited by dragonettes, young dragons whose tails are tied to rocks while their mother hunts. The dragonettes explain that they eat people and other large creatures but cannot reach the travelers. Dorothy, the Wizard, and Zeb find two paths and hurry away before the mother dragon returns.

14. Ozma Uses the Magic Belt

Dorothy, Zeb, the Wizard, Jim the cab-horse, Eureka, and the nine tiny piglets become trapped in an underground cave after a moving rock blocks their passage. Dorothy explains that Princess Ozma can see her through an enchanted picture and use the Nome King's Magic Belt to rescue her. At four o'clock Dorothy gives the agreed signal, and Ozma transports Dorothy and Eureka to the Land of Oz, then sends for the Wizard, Zeb, Jim, and the piglets. They arrive safely in the Emerald City and enter the courtyard of the royal palace.

15. Old Friends are Reunited

Oz returns to the Emerald City with Zeb and is welcomed by Jellia Jamb and the royal servants, though he does not wish to rule again. Princess Ozma offers him a permanent home and appoints him the Official Wizard of the Land of Oz. Oz explains his origins, his former rule, and how his initials led the people to call him Oz. Dorothy is reunited with the Scarecrow, Billina, Nick Chopper, and H. M. Woggle-Bug, T. E.

16. Jim, The Cab-Horse

Jim the Cab-horse is treated as an honored guest in the Land of Oz, where he demands oats and sleeps on a bed of rugs. He meets the Sawhorse and boasts about being a real horse before encountering the Cowardly Lion and Hungry Tiger. Dorothy arrives and greets the Lion and Tiger, then learns that the talking Gump's Head is mounted in the palace hall. Princess Ozma welcomes Dorothy to breakfast.

17. The Nine Tiny Piglets

Princess Ozma orders a holiday in the Emerald City to honor her visitors, featuring a grand procession, the Wizard’s performance, and afternoon games and races. The Wizard delights the people by producing nine tiny piglets and performing additional tricks, while Ozma keeps one piglet as a pet. At the races, the Sawhorse defeats Jim the Cab-horse, who angrily damages it before apologizing. Ozma rewards the Sawhorse by naming it Prince of Horses.

18. The Trial of Eureka the Kitten

Ozma’s white piglet disappears from her boudoir after Eureka is seen leaving the room, leading Ozma to suspect that the kitten ate it. Eureka is arrested and imprisoned for alleged murder, and Ozma summons the Court to hold a trial with the Tin Woodman as her defender and the Woggle-Bug as accuser. The Wizard, believing Eureka may be guilty but wanting to spare Dorothy’s unhappiness, gives the Tin Woodman another identical piglet to produce if the jury finds Eureka guilty.

19. The Wizard Performs Another Trick

In the Emerald City Throne Room, Princess Ozma presides over the trial of Eureka, who is accused of murdering and eating Ozma's lost piglet. The jury finds Eureka guilty, but the Tin Woodman produces a substitute piglet supplied by the Wizard, apparently proving her innocent. Eureka reveals that the real piglet fell into a vase, and Ozma's officers recover it by breaking the vase. Eureka is freed, and the citizens leave pleased with the day's amusement.

20. Zeb Returns to the Ranch

Eureka remains in disgrace and confinement after the trial because the people of Oz know she tried to eat the piglet. Dorothy uses the enchanted picture to learn that Uncle Henry and Aunt Em have returned to the farm in Kansas and believe she was killed by the earthquake. Ozma sends Dorothy and Eureka to Kansas and Zeb and Jim to Hugson's Ranch, using the Magic Belt. Zeb arrives safely at the ranch, where Uncle Hugson is astonished to see him.
