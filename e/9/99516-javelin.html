javelin in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Javelin is a beautiful weapon of unknown wood with a golden point. Given by Cynthia to Procris and then by Procris to Cephalus, it always strikes what it is aimed at, cannot be escaped, and returns to its thrower bloodstained without retrieval. During a hunt, Cephalus throws it at what he believes is a wild beast; it strikes and kills Procris, marking their reconciliation.

The javelin is a beautiful weapon of unknown wood with a golden point, given to Procris by Cynthia and later given by Procris to Cephalus. Whatever it is aimed at, it strikes, does not depend on chance, and returns stained with blood without anyone retrieving it.

opening

The javelin had previously been given to Cephalus by Procris. While hunting, Cephalus hurled it at what he thought was a wild beast, and it struck Procris in the breast.

notes .. notes

Procris's javelin in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Procris's javelin was a weapon that never failed to hit its mark and was given to Procris by Diana. Procris gave it to Cephalus, who later threw it at the bushes and accidentally killed her.
