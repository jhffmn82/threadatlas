Greek mythology in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Greek mythology is presented through differing accounts of Niobe’s family and the deaths of her children, interpreted through natural or historical events such as plague and the destructive powers of Apollo and Diana. It is also proposed to derive partly from Egyptian notions of the future state and from real places, animals, plants, and customs.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

notes .. notes

Ancient Greek mythology preserves differing accounts of Niobe's parentage, number of children, and their deaths. The unit explains several myths through natural or historical events, especially the association of plague with the destructive powers of Apollo and Diana.

notes .. notes

The explanation suggests that much of Greek mythology may have been derived from Egyptian mythology, particularly Greek ideas about the Elysian Fields and the Infernal Regions. It also proposes that some myths, including those concerning Cerberus and poisonous plants, may have developed from real places, animals, plants, or customs.

Greek mythology in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Greek mythology is a body of Greek stories, shaped by Greek imagination and legends borrowed from Egypt and the East, often preserved in differing Greek and Roman forms. It developed through Scriptural, Historical, Allegorical, Astronomical, and Physical explanations, including attempts to explain nature and names, and later supplied subject matter for Renaissance and later painters and sculptors.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter I

Greek mythology contains stories shaped by Greek imagination and by legends borrowed from Egypt and the East. The same myth may occur in several different forms in Greek and Roman authors.

Chapter XXVIII

Pagan mythology is explained through Scriptural, Historical, Allegorical, Astronomical, and Physical theories. The unit concludes that all these sources contributed to its development, along with attempts to explain natural phenomena and the names of places and persons.
