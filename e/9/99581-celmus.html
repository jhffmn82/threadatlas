Celmus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Celmus is a remarkably wise, moderate young man, untouched by passions. Formerly attached to Jupiter in Jupiter’s youth, he is said to have been Jupiter’s foster-father. His resistance to passion changes him into adamant; in another account, Jupiter encloses him in an impenetrable tower for revealing the immortality of the Gods.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Celmus is described as now being adamant. He was formerly most attached to Jupiter when Jupiter was little.

notes .. notes

Celmus was described by Pliny as a remarkably wise and moderate young man whose passions made no impression on him, causing him to be changed into adamant. Another account identifies him as Jupiter's foster-father, imprisoned by Jupiter in an impenetrable tower for revealing the immortality of the Gods.
