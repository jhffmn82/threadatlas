Brother Morris in The Valley of Fear

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1914-09/1915-05

Brother Morris is a drygoods-store owner on Market Square, husband and father of three, and a reluctant, troubled member of Vermissa lodge and the Freeman’s society. Coerced into crime, including his employer’s murder, he fears for his wife and children and is excommunicated because he is a Scowrer. He opposes Boss McGinty and the lodge’s criminal rule, warning that attacks such as the proposed killing of James Stanger will bring police, troops, courts, and the lodge’s destruction. Threatened by McGinty, he recants publicly, but privately trusts McMurdo, offers him a clerkship, and describes Vermissa as ruled by fear. Finally, Morris gives McMurdo a telegraph-service friend’s letter warning of a Pinkerton detective and asks him to handle the danger.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

When the drinks which followed the ceremony of initiation had all been

Brother Morris warns that the lodge is driving small business owners from the district and replacing them with large companies that may have the resources to destroy the organization through the courts. He also argues against killing James Stanger because Stanger is respected and his death would provoke widespread demands for police and troops. After McGinty threatens him, Morris apologizes and promises not to speak against the lodge again.

Chapter IV

Brother Morris tells McMurdo that the Freeman’s society has forced him into crime and threatens his life and family if he leaves. He describes taking part in a murder and warns McMurdo that the lodge’s violence has made Vermissa a valley of fear.

Chapter VI

Morris, a weaker and troubled member of the order, tells McMurdo that a Pinkerton detective is investigating the Scowrers. He gives McMurdo a letter from a friend and asks him to take responsibility for dealing with the threat.
