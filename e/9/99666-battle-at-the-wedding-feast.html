battle at the wedding feast

event · 2 works · 1 quoted fact · 1 connection
Also called: a furious combat, a general conflict, the combat, the contest, the unequal contest, this outrage on the rights of hospitality
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the battle is fought between Perseus and Phineus and ends when Perseus displays the Gorgon head, petrifying many combatants.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the battle begins when Phineus throws a javelin at Perseus and ends when Perseus holds up Medusa's head, petrifying the attackers.

battle at the wedding feast in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

is_fought_between Perseus and Phineus [with Phineus and his adherents attacking Perseus] The wedding celebration becomes a violent battle between Perseus and Phineus’s followers. Numerous combatants are killed by swords, spears, and improvised weapons, while Pallas protects and encourages Perseus. The battle ends when Perseus displays the Gorgon head and petrifies many of the combatants.

battle at the wedding feast in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

The battle at the wedding feast begins when Phineus throws a javelin at Perseus and his followers attack the guests. Perseus turns the battle in his favor by warning his friends to look away and holding up Medusa's head, which petrifies the attackers.
