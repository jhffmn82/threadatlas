Also called: Amyclides, Hyacinth, Son of Œbalus, descendant of Amycla, the Tænarian youth, the dying boy, the youth Hyacinthus, Œbalides, Ὕακινθον
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Hyacinthus is Apollo’s beloved companion, accidentally killed by a rebounding discus, from whose blood a hyacinth springs.
  • In The Library, Hyacinth is Apollo’s beloved, the son of Clio and Pierus, accidentally killed by Apollo’s discus.
  • In Description of Greece, Hyacinthus is a handsome, small-bearded figure buried beneath Apollo’s statue at Amyclæ, honored with offerings before sacrifices during Hyacinthia.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Hyacinthus is a youth beloved by Apollo, killed by a discus during quoits, whose blood Apollo turns into a flower marked with grief.

Hyacinthus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Hyacinthus, a youth of Laconia born in Sparta and Apollo’s most beloved companion, is accidentally killed by a rebounding discus; from his blood springs a lament-marked hyacinth.

FABLE III. [X.106-142]

Hyacinthus was a youth of Laconia and Apollo’s beloved companion. Apollo accidentally killed him when a discus rebounded from the ground and struck his head; afterward, a flower sprang from his blood and bore marks of lamentation.

Hyacinthus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Hyacinthus is a very handsome, small-bearded figure with a virgin sister, Polybœa. He is buried beneath Apollo’s statue at Amyclæ, where offerings precede sacrifices to Apollo during Hyacinthia. An altar shows gods carrying him and Polybœa to heaven; traditions report his accidental death by Apollo but may be baseless.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIX.

Hyacinthus is said to be buried beneath the base of Apollo’s statue at Amyclæ, and offerings are made to him through a brazen door before sacrifices are offered to Apollo at his festival. The altar depicts the gods carrying Hyacinthus and his virgin sister Polybœa to heaven, and Hyacinthus is shown with a small beard and as very handsome. Traditions concerning his accidental death by Apollo, Zephyr, and the hyacinth flower are reported as possibly baseless.

Hyacinthus is a youth loved by Apollo. He dies when a discus strikes him in the forehead during a game of quoits, and Apollo preserves his memory by turning his blood into a flower bearing signs of grief.
