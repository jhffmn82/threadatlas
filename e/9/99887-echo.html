Echo in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Echo is a noisy Nymph, also described by poets as daughter of the Air and the Tongue. She distracted Juno with stories so Jupiter’s mistresses could escape, and Juno punished her to repeat only others’ last words. After seeing Narcissus in the forest, Echo loved him, but his rejection drove her to hide, waste away, and transform into sound; only Echo’s voice survived. She still echoes Narcissus’s cries and final words, grieving him despite anger over his treatment of her.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Echo was once a noisy Nymph who distracted Juno with long stories so Jupiter's mistresses could escape. Juno punished her by allowing her only to repeat the last words spoken by others. After Narcissus rejected her, Echo hid in the woods, wasted away, and was never seen again, though she continued to be heard.

notes .. notes

The poets represented Echo as the daughter of the Air and the Tongue. The explanation suggests that the story may have arisen from a Nymph who became lost in the woods, while those searching for her heard only echoes of their own voices.

opening

Echo repeats Narcissus's cries, words, and sounds. Although she is angry and remembers how he treated her, she grieves as he wastes away and laments his death.

Echo in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Echo is a beautiful woodland nymph who attends Diana and loves Narcissus unreturned. Punished by Juno for delaying her with talk, she can only repeat last words; after fading away, she becomes a voice that answers calls.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter VIII

Echo was a beautiful nymph who loved woodland sports but was punished by Juno for using talk to delay her. She could no longer speak first and eventually faded away until only her voice remained, which still replies to anyone who calls.
