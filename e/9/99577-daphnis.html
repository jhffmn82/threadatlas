Daphnis in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Daphnis is a shepherd of Ida, associated with Crete or Phrygia. He has a Nymph as paramour and affronts Thalia. In the end, the Nymph’s resentment turns him into stone, an event also described as allegorically expressing insensibility.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Daphnis is identified as the shepherd of Ida. The resentment of his paramour, a Nymph, turned him into a stone.

notes .. notes

Daphnis was a shepherd associated with Ida, which could refer to either Crete or Phrygia. He was said to have been turned into stone, perhaps as an allegory for an individual's insensibility, after affronting Thalia.
