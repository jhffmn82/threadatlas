Ovid · 0008~

The flaying of Marsyas is Apollo’s punishment for Marsyas’s presumption after overcoming him: Apollo fastens Marsyas to a pine or plane tree and strips his skin, leaving his nerves, veins, bowels, and lungs exposed; accounts add that the skin is suspended in Celenæ or Marsyas is hewn to pieces.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

After overcoming Marsyas, Apollo punishes him by stripping the skin from the surface of his limbs. Marsyas is left as one entire wound, with his nerves, veins, bowels, and lungs exposed.

notes .. notes

After Marsyas lost to Apollo, Apollo fastened him to a pine tree or, according to Pliny the Elder, a plane tree, and stripped off his skin. The skin was later suspended in Celenæ, while Hyginus says that Apollo hewed Marsyas to pieces.
