Scylla in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Scylla, royal daughter of Nisus, often inhabits a regal tower in peace. During Minos's siege of Megara, she passionately loves him and betrays her father and country by cutting his purple lock. Rejected, she follows Minos's fleet and becomes Ciris.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK THE EIGHTH.

Scylla, the daughter of Nisus, falls passionately in love with Minos and betrays her father and country by cutting off Nisus's purple lock. Minos rejects her, and she follows his departing fleet before being transformed into Ciris.

Scylla in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Scylla, daughter of Nisus, loves Minos, betrays Megara by cutting Nisus's purple lock, is rejected and cursed by Minos, then pursues his ship and is changed into a bird.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter VIII

Scylla, daughter of Nisus, fell in love with Minos during the siege of Megara and cut off her father's purple lock to give Minos victory. Minos rejected and cursed her, and after she pursued his ship, a deity changed her into a bird.
