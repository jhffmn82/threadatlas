Anaxarete in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Anaxarete, a noblewoman descended from Teucer, cruelly rejects Iphis’s repeated appeals. After witnessing his funeral procession, she is fixed in place and transformed into stone; Salamis preserves her statue beneath a temple called Venus, the looker-out.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLES XII. AND XIII. [XIV.698-851]

Anaxarete, a noble woman descended from the race of Teucer, rejects Iphis's repeated appeals with contempt and deprives him of hope. When she looks upon his funeral procession, she becomes fixed in place and is transformed into stone; Salamis preserves her statue and a temple called Venus, the looker-out.

Anaxarete in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Anaxarete is a noble lady descended from Teucer who cruelly mocks and rejects Iphis. After watching his funeral procession, she is punished by transformation into stone; her statue remains in Venus's temple at Salamis.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter VI

Anaxarete is a noble lady of the ancient family of Teucer who mocks and rejects Iphis without giving him hope. When she watches his funeral procession, she is transformed into stone, and her statue remains in the temple of Venus at Salamis.
