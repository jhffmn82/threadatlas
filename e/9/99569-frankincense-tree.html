frankincense tree in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The frankincense tree, proposed as the natural subject symbolized by Leucothoë, grows from her dissolved body after the Sun sprinkles nectar on earth. Its shoot roots through clods and breaks a hillock, is odoriferous, produces an aromatic drug used in physic, and may be killed by a nearby sunflower.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

The frankincense tree grows from Leucothoë's body after the Sun sprinkles her and the earth with heavenly nectar. Its shoot takes root through the clods and breaks through the hillock.

notes .. notes

The frankincense tree is proposed as the natural subject symbolized by Leucothoë. It was said to have been introduced into Persia by Orchamus, and a claim that the sunflower kills it may underlie Clytie's jealousy.
