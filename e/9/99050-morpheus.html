Morpheus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Morpheus, Sleep's most dexterous son, imitates human shapes, movements, faces, voices, clothing, and speech. Silent-winged, he is sent to Halcyone in a dream, takes Ceyx's pale, wet, shipwrecked form, and tells her Ceyx has died.

Morpheus is skilled at imitating human forms, movements, faces, voices, clothing, and speech. Sleep sends him to Halcyone, and he assumes the form of Ceyx, appearing pale, wet, and shipwrecked as he tells her that Ceyx has died.
