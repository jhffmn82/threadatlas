Cygnus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Cygnus, Neptune’s Trojan son, kills a thousand men and resists Achilles’ weapons without wounds. Achilles defeats him by forcing him down and tightening his helmet straps around his throat; afterward Neptune transforms him into a white swan.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK THE TWELFTH.

Cygnus, the son of Neptune and a Trojan, kills a thousand men and proves invulnerable to Achilles's spears and sword. Achilles overcomes him by forcing him to the ground and tightening his helmet straps around his throat, after which Neptune transforms him into a white swan.

FABLES III. AND IV. [XII.146-535]

Cygnus is defeated by Achilles. His body can be pierced by no weapon and receives no wounds, astonishing the Grecian chiefs.
