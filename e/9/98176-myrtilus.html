Myrtilus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Myrtilus, son of Hermes and charioteer of Oenomaus, loves Hippodamia and sabotages Oenomaus’s chariot by omitting linchpins, causing Oenomaus to lose; dying, Oenomaus curses him and wishes Pelops to kill him.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

II. Ὅτι ὁ Τάνταλος ἐν “Atdov? κολάζεται,

Myrtilus, the son of Hermes, was Oenomaus’s charioteer. Because he loved Hippodamia and wanted to please her, he failed to put the linchpins into the wheels, causing Oenomaus to lose the race; Oenomaus cursed him as he died, wishing Pelops to kill him.

Myrtilus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Myrtilus, said to be Hermes’ son and Œnomaus’ charioteer, betrays Œnomaus for Pelops’ promises. Pelops throws him from a ship; washed ashore at Pheneus, he is buried and annually honored at night, while the claimed Myrtoan Sea connection is rejected.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIV.

Myrtilus was said to be the son of Hermes and the charioteer of Œnomaus. After betraying Œnomaus because of Pelops' promises, he was thrown from a ship by Pelops, and his body was washed ashore and buried by the people of Pheneus. They honored him annually at night, but Pausanias rejected the claim that the Myrtoan Sea was named after him.
