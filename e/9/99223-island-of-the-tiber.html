An island within the Tiber at Rome, formed by two equal river channels, becomes Æsculapius’s chosen habitation, introducing his cult and ending the city’s mourning.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE VII. [XV.622-744]

The Island of the Tiber is formed where the river divides into two equal channels around a strip of dry land. The serpent carrying Æsculapius chooses this island as his habitation, and the city’s mourning ends there.
