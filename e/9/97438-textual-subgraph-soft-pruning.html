Textual Subgraph Soft Pruning in GRAG: Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Yuntong Hu, Zhihan Lei, Zheng Zhang, Bo Pan, Chen Ling, Liang Zhao · 2025

Textual Subgraph Soft Pruning is a mechanism that minimizes irrelevant nodes and edges in retrieved textual subgraphs before they are encoded into graph tokens. Using Multilayer Perceptrons, it ranks entities by distance from the query and softly masks farther ones with scalars nearer 0. It is especially important for dense graphs, though difficult importance estimates can impair retrieval.

opening

Textual Subgraph Soft Pruning minimizes the influence of irrelevant nodes and edges that remain in retrieved subgraphs. It uses distance from the query to softly mask entities, with farther nodes or edges receiving scalars closer to 0.

Soft pruning removes irrelevant entities when retrieved textual subgraphs are encoded into graph tokens. Removing soft pruning lowers WebQSP Hit@1 from 0.7275 to 0.5671, indicating that pruning is especially important for dense graphs.

7

The initial node ranking and pruning mechanism affects the efficiency of GRAG’s textual subgraph retrieval. If graph structure or node importance is difficult to estimate, the mechanism may lead to suboptimal retrieval performance.

Textual Subgraph Soft Pruning in GRAG: Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Yuntong Hu, Zhihan Lei, Zheng Zhang, Bo Pan, Chen Ling, Liang Zhao · 2025-07-12

Textual Subgraph Soft Pruning is a method that minimizes irrelevant and redundant nodes and edges in retrieved subgraphs, producing a query-tailored optimal subgraph structure with linear complexity. During graph-token encoding, it removes irrelevant entities; with K-hop ego-graphs in divide-and-conquer, it approximates the optimal textual subgraph and is especially crucial for dense graphs.
