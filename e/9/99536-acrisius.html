Acrisius

person · 4 works · 18 quoted facts · 7 connections
Also called: Acrisius the son of Abas, he, his, the cruel king, the old king, ᾿Ακρίσιον, ᾿Ακρίσιος
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Acrisius is son of Abas and king of Argos who opposes Bacchus, confines Danaë because of a prophecy, and exposes her and Perseus in a boat.
  • In The Library, Acrisius is king of Argos, twin brother of Proetus, husband of Eurydice, father of Danae, and grandfather of Perseus; he confines Danae, casts her and Perseus into the sea, and is accidentally killed by Perseus.
  • In Description of Greece, Acrisius is son of Abas who retains Argos after dividing the kingdom with Prœtus and is unintentionally killed by Perseus with a quoit at Larissa.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Acrisius is king of Argos and Danae's father who tries to prevent her children after a prophecy and dies when Perseus's discus strikes his foot.

Acrisius in The Library

Apollodorus · 0070~/0200~

Acrisius is king of Argos, twin brother of Proetus, husband of Eurydice, and father of Danae, making him Perseus’s grandfather. After an oracle foretells Danae’s son would kill him, he confines Danae and casts her and Perseus into the sea in a chest. Fleeing Argos in fear, he is accidentally killed by Perseus and buried outside the city.

Acrisius in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Acrisius is king of Argos and Danae's father. Warned that Danae's child will kill him, he tries to prevent her children, but dies when Perseus's discus strikes his foot.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

PERSEUS AND MEDUSA

Acrisius, king of Argos, learns from an oracle that Danae's child will kill him and tries to prevent her from having children. The prophecy is fulfilled when Perseus's discus strikes Acrisius's foot during a contest at Larissa, causing his death.
