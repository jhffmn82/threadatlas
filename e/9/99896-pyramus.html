Pyramus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Pyramus is the most beautiful youth and Thisbe’s lover. In a tale of youthful love and parental resentment, he meets her near Ninus’s tomb in Babylon; believing she has been killed, he mourns, wounds himself with his sword, and dies beneath their agreed-upon tree.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK THE FOURTH.

Pyramus is described as the most beautiful of youths and as the lover of Thisbe. Believing that Thisbe has been killed, he mourns her, wounds himself with his sword, and dies beneath the agreed-upon tree.

notes .. notes

Pyramus was one of the two lovers in the story of Pyramus and Thisbe. The lovers met near the tomb of Ninus in Babylon, and the tale is presented as a moral story about youthful engagements and parental resentment.

Pyramus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Handsomest youth in Babylonia, Pyramus loved Thisbe despite parental opposition; believing a lioness killed her after finding her bloody veil, he stabbed himself.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

PYTHON

Pyramus was described as the handsomest youth in Babylonia and loved Thisbe despite their parents' opposition. Believing that a lioness had killed Thisbe after finding her bloody veil, he stabbed himself beneath the appointed tree.
