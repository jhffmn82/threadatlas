Nymph Lotis in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Nymph Lotis, pursued by Priapus because of his lust, flees and is transformed into a lotus near a lake, preserving her identity in its name.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE IV. [IX.324-425]

The Nymph Lotis, fleeing from the lust of Priapus, was transformed into the lotus near the lake. Her identity remained preserved in the plant's name, though Dryope did not know its history.

Lotis in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Lotis, a nymph fleeing a base pursuer, is changed into a lotus plant; Dryope unknowingly plucks its flowers, and blood falls from the stems.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter V

Lotis is a nymph who had been changed into a lotus plant while fleeing from a base pursuer. Dryope unknowingly breaks flowers from her transformed body, causing blood to fall from the stems.
