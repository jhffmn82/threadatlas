Pygmalion's ivory statue in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Pygmalion carves a snow-white ivory ideal virgin, adorns and loves it as a companion, and Venus transforms it into a living woman.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE VI. [X.220-242]

Pygmalion carves the statue from snow-white ivory and gives it the beauty of an ideal virgin. He dresses it, adorns it with jewels, treats it as his companion, and falls in love with it; Venus later makes it a living woman.

Galatea in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Galatea begins as an ivory statue, becomes a living woman through Venus's favor, awakens in her niche to learn love and life, and marries Pygmalion in Venus-blessed union.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter V

Galatea is the living statue in William Morris's version of the Pygmalion story. She describes opening her eyes in the niche where she had stood and hearing a woman tell her to come down, learn to love, and be alive.
