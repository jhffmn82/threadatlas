PART I / Chapter I

Sherlock Holmes and Dr. Watson receive a cipher message from Fred Porlock warning that danger threatens a wealthy man named Douglas at Birlstone. Holmes identifies it as a book cipher and decodes it using an older edition of Whitaker’s Almanac, obtaining a warning about Douglas at Birlstone Manor House. Fred Porlock’s accompanying letter says that Professor Moriarty suspects him and asks Holmes to burn the cipher. Inspector Alec MacDonald then arrives and reveals that Mr. Douglas of Birlstone Manor House was horribly murdered the previous night.

Chapter II

Sherlock Holmes explains to Inspector MacDonald that the anonymous warning from Porlock foretold the death of John Douglas at Birlstone and may connect the murder to Professor Moriarty. Holmes argues that Moriarty secretly controls a large criminal organization, with Colonel Sebastian Moran as his chief of staff, and suggests that Moriarty’s wealth is acquired illegally. MacDonald and Holmes travel to Birlstone after receiving White Mason’s report describing a perplexing murder involving a shotgun wound to Douglas’s head. Holmes avoids forming a premature theory and intends to trace the connection between Moriarty in London and the dead man in Sussex.

Chapter III

The unit introduces Birlstone, its ancient Manor House, and the household of John Douglas and Mrs. Douglas. Cecil James Barker discovers that Douglas has been murdered in the Manor House by a close-range blast from a sawed-off shotgun, while the raised drawbridge appears to make escape impossible. Sergeant Wilson, Dr. Wood, and Ames examine the scene and find evidence that someone hid in the room, escaped through the open window across the moat, left a card marked “V.V. 341,” and removed Douglas’s wedding ring. The unexplained brand on Douglas’s forearm and the other unusual evidence deepen the mystery.

Chapter IV

White Mason meets Holmes, Dr. Watson, and Inspector MacDonald at Birlstone and briefs them on the murder of Mr. Douglas. The investigators examine the Manor House, its moat, the window, the weapons, the footprints, the blood mark, the missing wedding ring, and the card marked V.V. 341. White Mason and MacDonald debate whether the killer came from inside or outside, while Holmes develops questions about a possible secret society and other unexplained clues. Cecil Barker then reports that the suspected killer’s bicycle has been found concealed near the Manor House.

Chapter V

Ames, Mrs. Allen, and Cecil Barker give evidence about the events surrounding the murder of Jack Douglas at Birlstone. Barker says Douglas feared an implacable secret society connected with his past in California, where threatening men sought him after he left. Inspector MacDonald questions Barker about Douglas's marriage, his friendship with Mrs. Douglas, the missing wedding ring, and the alleged connection between the marriage and the tragedy. Sherlock Holmes also questions Barker about the candle and lamp in the study, finding the change in lighting remarkable.

Mrs. Douglas tells Inspector MacDonald and Sherlock Holmes that Mr. Douglas feared a danger connected with his American past, though he refused to explain it. She recalls his repeated references to the “Valley of Fear” and his fevered mention of Bodymaster McGinty. The investigators also learn that Mr. Douglas's wedding ring was taken, and Holmes discovers that Cecil Barker's bloodstained carpet slipper matches the blood mark on the study window sill. This suggests that Barker himself made the apparent outside footprint, though Holmes and MacDonald do not yet understand why.

Chapter VI

Dr. Watson encounters Mrs. Douglas and Barker laughing together in the garden, then learns they are anxious about whether Sherlock Holmes would share confidential information with the police. At the village inn, Holmes argues that Barker and Mrs. Douglas are lying about the murder and conspiring to conceal the truth, though he is not certain they committed it. Holmes reasons that the gunshot was probably fired around a quarter to eleven, half an hour before the alarm, and considers the possibility that an outsider murdered Mr. Douglas and threatened the couple into silence. He plans to spend an evening alone in the study and borrows Dr. Watson’s umbrella while the investigation of the bicycle continues.

Inspector MacDonald and White Mason identify the bicycle as belonging to an American calling himself Hargrave, who stayed at the Eagle Commercial in Tunbridge Wells and is described as a roughly fifty-year-old, fierce-looking man carrying a concealed sawed-off shotgun. MacDonald theorizes that Hargrave cycled to the house, hid his bicycle, shot Mr. Douglas, and escaped through the moat or by another route. Sherlock Holmes maintains that the murder occurred earlier than reported and that Mrs. Douglas and Barker helped conceal what happened and fabricated evidence of an escape through the window. Holmes later makes a solitary investigation focused on Mr. Douglas's single dumb-bell and returns asking Dr. Watson whether he fears sleeping with a lunatic.

Chapter VII

Sherlock Holmes tells Inspector MacDonald and White Mason to abandon their search for the mysterious cyclist because they do not understand what they are investigating. Holmes discovers that the missing dumb-bell had been used to weight a bundle containing American boots, a knife, clothing, and a yellow overcoat, and he traps Cecil Barker into retrieving it from the moat. Mrs. Douglas then reveals that John Douglas is alive and has been hiding in the Manor House. Douglas gives Dr. Watson his written account, identified as the story of the Valley of Fear.

John Douglas explains that Ted Baldwin attacked him at Birlstone Manor, but Baldwin was accidentally killed when his own gun discharged during their struggle. Douglas, Barker, and Douglas's wife disguised Baldwin's body as Douglas's and hid Douglas in a secret hiding place, hoping to make his American enemies believe Baldwin had killed him. Sherlock Holmes had already inferred the deception after finding Douglas's clothes in the moat and discovering that the body was not Douglas's. Holmes warns that the mystery is not finished and that Douglas may face dangers beyond English law, leading the narrative back to the origins of the past conflict in America.

PART II / Chapter I

On February 4, 1875, John McMurdo travels by evening train from Chicago to Vermissa, a grim coal- and iron-working settlement in Vermissa Valley. He reveals that he is armed, belongs to the Eminent Order of Freemen, and has fled Chicago because of serious trouble with the law. After befriending Brother Scanlan and provoking two policemen, McMurdo arrives in Vermissa and is warned about the violent Scowrers and their leader, Jack McGinty. He takes lodging at the Shafter boarding house, where he meets Jacob Shafter and his daughter Ettie.

Chapter II

McMurdo quickly becomes prominent at Shafter's boarding house, openly courts Ettie Shafter, and refuses to be intimidated by her existing connection to Teddy Baldwin. Mike Scanlan urges him to report to Boss McGinty, while Mr. Shafter reveals that the local Eminent Order of Freemen is also known as the murderous Scowrers and orders McMurdo to leave. Baldwin threatens McMurdo and Ettie, displaying a branded circle-and-triangle mark, but McMurdo declares that he is a Freeman and decides to visit McGinty immediately.

Boss McGinty, the powerful Bodymaster of Lodge 341 and a feared official in the Vermissa Valley, questions McMurdo when he arrives at McGinty's saloon. McMurdo proves his lodge membership and admits that he formerly made counterfeit dollars and killed Jonas Pinto in Chicago. McGinty accepts him as a potentially useful brother after discovering McMurdo's nerve and ability to make counterfeit money. McMurdo and Ted Baldwin quarrel over a young lady, but McGinty forces them to settle the dispute under lodge discipline and instructs McMurdo to attend Lodge 341's Saturday meeting.

Chapter III

Jack McMurdo moves to the Widow MacNamara’s and lodges with Scanlan, while continuing his relationship with Ettie and showing the lodge members his counterfeit-money moulds. Captain Marvin recognizes McMurdo as a suspected Chicago criminal, but agrees to keep silent if he lives honestly, and this revelation makes McMurdo a hero among the local gang. McMurdo is initiated into Lodge 341, Vermissa, where he endures tests of courage and pain, accepts the Bodymaster’s authority, and receives a lodge brand on his forearm. The lodge is part of the Scowrers, a large and openly criminal organization whose members commit murders with little fear of conviction.

At a Vermissa lodge meeting, McGinty assigns members to carry out violent acts, including an attack requested by Merton County Lodge and a warning against editor James Stanger. Brother Morris warns that the lodge's violence and extortion are driving out small businesses and provoking powerful companies and public opposition. McMurdo gains popularity by singing, volunteers for the attack on Stanger, and stops Ted Baldwin from continuing to beat the editor after the assault begins. The lodge members attack Stanger in the Vermissa Herald office and escape while maintaining an alibi at the Union Bar.

Chapter IV

McMurdo learns from Brother Morris that the lodge’s activities include murder and that members live under Boss McGinty’s control. McGinty warns McMurdo against associating with Morris, but police Captain Marvin arrests McMurdo for involvement in the attack on the Herald office. McGinty arranges a defense, and the court releases McMurdo and the other accused Scowrers because the evidence is inconclusive and citizens provide an alibi. The acquittal is applauded by lodge members but condemned by others who call the men murderers and threaten revenge.

Chapter V

Jack McMurdo’s arrest and acquittal increase his popularity among the Scowrers, while Ettie Shafter unsuccessfully urges him to abandon the criminal organization. Lawler and Andrews, assisted by other men, murder Crow Hill manager Josiah H. Dunn and engineer Menzies, and the lodge celebrates the killings along with Ted Baldwin’s murder of William Hales. Boss McGinty then assigns McMurdo to kill Chester Wilcox, the chief foreman of the Iron Dike Company, despite Wilcox’s wife, children, and hired help being in the house.

McMurdo organized an attempt to kill Chester Wilcox with the help of Manders and Reilly by placing quarry powder against Wilcox's isolated house and blowing it up. Wilcox had moved his family and arranged police protection the day before, so the explosion destroyed an empty house. McMurdo vowed to continue pursuing Wilcox, and newspapers later reported that Wilcox had been shot at from an ambuscade. The unit describes the Society of Freemen and the Scowrers as responsible for numerous killings and acts of terror across the Valley of Fear.

Chapter VI

McMurdo learns from Morris’s letter that Pinkerton detective Birdy Edwards is investigating the Scowrers in the valley and warns the lodge. He identifies Edwards as the man using the name Steve Wilson and proposes luring him to Widow MacNamara’s house so the lodge can kill him. McMurdo also begins an escape plan with Ettie Shafter, promising to send for her if he must flee. The lodge accepts his plan and prepares to attack Edwards.

Chapter VII

Birdy Edwards, posing as McMurdo, lures the Scowrers into a trap at his house and reveals that he is a Pinkerton’s detective. Captain Marvin and armed Mine Police surround the house, disarm the gang, and arrest its members. Edwards explains how he infiltrated the Scowrers and secretly prevented or limited several of their crimes. The Scowrers are later tried, with McGinty and eight chief followers executed, while Edwards eventually flees repeated vengeance attempts.

Epilogue

John Douglas was acquitted at Quarter Sessions after being referred to a higher court, but Sherlock Holmes warned that he was unsafe in England. Two months later, Cecil Barker reported that Douglas had been lost overboard from the Palmyra near St. Helena, though Holmes concluded that the death had been staged and was murder. Holmes attributed the crime to Professor Moriarty, whose London-based organization had taken over the effort to eliminate Douglas. Holmes said that Moriarty could be defeated, but asked for time to act.
