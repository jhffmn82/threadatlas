the ship in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

A swift, many-thwarted sea-going ship carries trade and profit when suitably loaded. If sea-faring is desired, it is readied with tackle, wings, rudder, and measured cargo; otherwise it is hauled ashore, drained, protected, and its equipment stored.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

(ll. 618-640) But if desire for uncomfortable sea-faring seize you;

A ship should be hauled onto land, packed around with stones, drained through its bilge-plug, and protected from rain. Its tackle and fittings should be stored in the house, its wings stowed, and its rudder hung over the smoke; cargo should be kept within a safe measure.
