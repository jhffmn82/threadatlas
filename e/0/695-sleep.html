Sleep in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Sleep, a soft, dream-bearing god and death's half-brother, descends as Phorbas, offers to relieve Palinurus, then uses a Lethe-soaked branch to induce fatal sleep and send him into the sea.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Thus far the sacred sports they celebrate:

Sleep descends during the night in the form of Phorbas and offers to take Palinurus’s place at the rudder. It throws a Lethe-soaked branch at Palinurus, overcomes him, and causes him to fall into the sea.

Sleep in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Sleep is a divine personification and brother of Death who fears Jupiter after punishment. Initially refusing Juno’s request, he accepts Pasithea as bride, hides on Mount Ida, puts Jupiter to sleep, and tells Neptune to aid the Danaans.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK XIV.

Sleep initially refuses to lull Jupiter because Jupiter had previously punished him for helping Juno against Hercules. After Juno promises him Pasithea in marriage and swears by the Styx, Sleep hides on Mount Ida, puts Jupiter to sleep, and reports to Neptune that he can now aid the Danaans.

Sleep in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Sleep, a God of sleep, dwells in a dark, silent Cimmerian cave surrounded by dreams and Lethe. At Iris’s request, he sends his son Morpheus to fulfill Juno’s command, then returns to drowsiness.

Sleep dwells in a dark, silent cave near the Cimmerians, surrounded by dreams and the waters of Lethe. At Iris’s request, he selects Morpheus from among his sons to carry out Juno’s command, then sinks back into drowsiness.
