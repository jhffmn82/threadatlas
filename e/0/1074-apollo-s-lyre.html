Apollo’s lyre

object · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Apollo's lyre music, high-pitched lyre, hollow lyre, lovely thing, lyre, playing of Apollo, sweet lyre, sweetness, the lyre
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Apollo’s lyre is a high-pitched seven-stringed instrument made by Hermes, tested and played by him, kept by his cradle, and later declared by Apollo to be forever dear alongside his curved bow.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, Apollo’s lyre music is played with a plectrum and skilful thumb, charming Tmolus and leading him to judge Apollo’s performance superior to Pan’s.

Apollo’s lyre in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Apollo’s lyre is a high-pitched instrument with seven sheep-gut strings. Hermes makes it from a tortoise shell and other materials, tests its marvelous sound and sings with it, then keeps it by his cradle; Apollo later declares it always dear alongside his curved bow.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

III. TO DELIAN APOLLO

Apollo declares that the lyre will always be dear to him, together with the curved bow. The lyre therefore forms part of his announced divine identity and musical power.

IV. TO HERMES

Hermes makes the lyre from the tortoise's shell, reeds, ox hide, horns, a cross-piece, and seven sheep-gut strings. He tests it, finds that it sounds marvelously, and sings about Zeus, Maia, and his own birth. He keeps it beside him when he returns to his cradle.
