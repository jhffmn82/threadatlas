Cyllene

place · 3 works · 7 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: Mount Cyllene, bright crests of Cyllene, mountain of Cyllene, the mountain, Κυλλήνην, Κυλλήνης
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Cyllene is identified as Hermes’s domain and as the forest-clad mountain containing Maia’s deep-shadowed cave, where Hermes is born and to which he returns after stealing Apollo’s cattle.
  • In The Library, Cyllene is described as the place where the Pleiades were born and where Maia gives birth to Hermes in a cave; Hermes returns there after stealing Apollo’s cattle, and Apollo goes there to accuse him.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Cyllene is described as Arcadia’s highest mountain, named for Cyllen, with a ruined temple of Cyllenian Hermes on its summit, near Æpytus’s tomb and an arsenal of Elis, and noted for entirely white blackbirds.

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XVII.

Cyllene is the highest of the mountains in Arcadia and contains the ruins of a temple of Cyllenian Hermes on its summit. Its name is said to derive from Cyllen, son of Elatus, and it is noted for blackbirds that are entirely white.
