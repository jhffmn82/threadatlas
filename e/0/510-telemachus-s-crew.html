Telemachus's crew in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Telemachus’s crew is a ship’s company of twenty young men, about his own age, belonging to him and loyal out of friendship. Recruited as volunteers around Ithaca by Minerva, they accompany Telemachus in his search for Ulysses. They wait by the water, load stores, prepare the vessel, set its mast and sails, and depart. During a sacrifice, two guard the ship; later the crew again prepares, boards, and sails with Telemachus and Theoclymenus toward Ithaca.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK II

Telemachus asks for a crew of twenty men to accompany him in search of Ulysses. Minerva recruits volunteers around Ithaca and brings them to the ship, where she encourages them.

Furthermore she went to the house of Ulysses, and threw the suitors

The crew waits by the water side, brings the stores aboard as Telemachus directs, and prepares the ship for departure. They set the mast, raise the sails, and make the ship fast.

As he spoke the sun set and it came on dark, whereon Minerva said,

Minerva says that Telemachus’s crew consists of young men who have taken the voyage out of friendship. At Nestor’s request, they leave the ship and attend the sacrifice, while two men remain in charge of the vessel.

BOOK XV

Telemachus’s crew prepares the ship, boards it, and follows his orders. The men set up the mast, raise the sails, and sail with him and Theoclymenus toward Ithaca.
