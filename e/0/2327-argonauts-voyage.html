golden fleece quest in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · -0299/-0200

The golden fleece quest is an expedition commanded by Pelias and undertaken by Jason, the Argonauts, and assembled heroes in the Argo toward Colchian land. Their journey includes deeds and wanderings, and they leave Lemnos after Heracles warns that remaining there will bring neither renown nor fleece. After mooring on the river Phasis, Jason and his companions pursue the fleece while Hera, Athena, Cypris, and Eros arrange aid. With Medea’s aid, the heroes obtain the fleece after Aeetes’ contests, then flee pursuing Colchians through a covenant, Apsyrtus’s deception, and violence.

Argonauts’ voyage in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · -0299/-0200

The Argonautic expedition is the voyage undertaken by the Argonauts to obtain the golden fleece, intended to atone for the intended sacrifice of Phrixus and guided by the Cyprian goddess toward a glorious outcome. It begins when Jason and the heroes leave Pagasae after preparing and launching Argo and sacrificing to Apollo, then sail toward Lemnos. Phineus foretells their route through the Cyanean rocks and Pontus to Aea and warns them to obey divine signs. After Idmon and Tiphys die, they continue past rivers, headlands, islands, and many peoples, reach the island of Ares using a shield-and-noise defense, and later enter the Phasis near Aeetes. As they approach Aeetes, Medea fears great evil but agrees to help the stranger complete the contest.

Argonautic expedition in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The Argonautic expedition is a legendary voyage from Thessaly to Colchis and back seeking the Golden Fleece. It is interpreted as an early important maritime, perhaps half-piratical, expedition for rich spoils, or as a corrupted tradition of Noah and the ark.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XI

The Argonauts' voyage is presented as a legendary expedition from Thessaly to Colchis and back in search of the Golden Fleece. The unit suggests that it may preserve a substratum of truth as an early important maritime expedition, possibly involving half-piratical activity and rich spoils. Another interpretation presented is that it may be a corrupted tradition of Noah and the ark, supported by the name Argo and the incident of the dove.
