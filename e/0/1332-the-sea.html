the sea in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

The sea breaks on African shores and controls passage, at times granting and denying the way. It may prevent Danaans from returning or bar their fleet, yet fails to stop Protesilaus’s departure; it becomes a source of fear and separation as Laodamia watches it.

The sea failed to prevent Protesilaus's departure when Laodamia wished it would, but later it bars the Danaan fleet from sailing. Laodamia watches the sea after losing sight of Protesilaus and finds its waters associated with fear and separation.

the sea in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

The sea is a barrier between Leander and Hero, dividing the Hellespont’s two lands. Uncertain, swollen, rough, dark, wind-tossed, and stormy, it endangers ships and Leander’s swimming voyage. For a few nights it prevents and delays his crossing, despite prayers and Hero’s hope that it will calm; its waves beat the shores and dark clouds conceal daylight.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

XVIII..

The sea is rough, dark, wind-tossed, and difficult for ships and swimmers. It delays Leander, frustrates his prayers, and repeatedly threatens to overwhelm him.

183 Am I, then, never to embrace you except when

The sea is uncertain and can be swollen, stormy, and difficult to cross. The speaker asks it to calm briefly while he is crossing, but wishes winter to continue after he reaches the addressee’s shore.

XIX

The sea separates Hero and Leander and is repeatedly described as stormy, rough, and dangerous for swimming. Its waves beat the shores, conceal the daylight beneath dark clouds, and prevent Leander from making the crossing, though Hero hopes it will become calm enough for his journey.

sea in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

The sea is a fruitless, deep natural feature ruled and moved by Poseidon. It is harmless during the steady-wind sailing season and passable in spring, but becomes dangerous with autumn storms; ignorant or overloaded sailors risk death.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

(ll. 618-640) But if desire for uncomfortable sea-faring seize you;

The sea becomes dangerous when the Pleiades plunge into it and autumn storms arise. It is relatively harmless fifty days after the solstice, passable in spring, and a place where men risk death when they sail in ignorance or overload their ships.

XXII. TO POSEIDON

The sea is described as fruitless and deep, and Poseidon is its mover and lord.
