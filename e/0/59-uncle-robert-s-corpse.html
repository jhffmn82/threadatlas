Samuel Pepys’s uncle’s will and papers become the central business of the unit. His estate is believed by Thomas Pepys to contain more than the roughly £500 already found but little money overall; a debt of £164 from Piggott may pass with the debts to the heir at law, and his surrender of Sturtlow is delivered to Samuel through William Luffe.
