John Ferrier in A Study in Scarlet

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1886

John Ferrier is a solitary, starving traveller on the great Alkali Plain who survives after his companions die and protects Lucy Ferrier, carrying her in a grey shawl and claiming her as his daughter. Rescued by Mormons, he accepts their religious terms to live, adopts Lucy, and becomes a useful guide and indefatigable hunter during their migration. Granted fertile land, he builds Ferrier farm into a large, prosperous holding and becomes a rich, practical farmer of Salt Lake City. Though he otherwise conforms to the Mormon religion, he remains unmarried and strictly celibate. When the Elders demand that Lucy marry, Ferrier refuses the authority of Brigham Young and rejects Joseph Stangerson and young Drebber, preferring Lucy dead to either marriage. Threatened by daily warnings, he prepares his loaded shotgun, gathers two thousand dollars in gold, and plans flight with Lucy and Jefferson Hope. They escape into the mountains, but Ferrier is captured while Hope hunts. Stangerson shoots and kills him; he is buried as a former resident of Salt Lake City. Hope later says that killing Drebber and Stangerson repaid Ferrier’s debt.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

PART II. / CHAPTER I.

John Ferrier is a gaunt, starving traveler who has crossed the desert with Lucy Ferrier after the rest of their party died. He protects Lucy, claims her as his child because he saved her, and agrees to join the Mormons and accept their conditions when they rescue him.

CHAPTER II.

John Ferrier travels with the Mormons as a useful guide and indefatigable hunter and receives a large, fertile tract of land. He builds a substantial home, becomes one of the wealthiest men in Salt Lake City, and remains strictly celibate while otherwise conforming to the religion.

CHAPTER III.

John Ferrier is warned by Brigham Young that Lucy must marry a Mormon. He intends to send Jefferson Hope a message, sell or abandon the farm, and leave Utah if necessary; he also prepares his house and shotgun for danger.

CHAPTER IV.

John Ferrier rejects the marriage proposals of Joseph Stangerson and young Drebber and declares that he would rather see Lucy dead than married to either of them. After receiving mysterious threats and daily countdown warnings, he prepares to flee with Lucy and Jefferson Hope, carrying their money and riding one of the horses through the mountains.

CHAPTER V.

John Ferrier escapes with Lucy and Jefferson Hope but is captured by the pursuing party while Hope is away hunting. Stangerson shoots him, and he is buried at the mountain camp with a brief inscription identifying him as formerly of Salt Lake City.
