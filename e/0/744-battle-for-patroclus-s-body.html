battle for Patroclus's body in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

The battle for Patroclus’s body is a fight near the ships between Achaeans and Trojans: the Trojans seek to carry the body to Ilius, while the Achaeans seek to take it to their ships. Fighting continues from Menelaus and Euphorbus through massed combat with spears, swords, and chariots. Ajax organizes the defense; Hector repeatedly tries to seize the body. Jove and Minerva influence the struggle, until Achilles’s appearance confuses the Trojans.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XVII.

The battle for Patroclus’s body continues from the encounter between Menelaus and Euphorbus through the massed fighting of Trojans, allies, Danaans, and Achaeans. Ajax organizes the defense, while Hector and the Trojans repeatedly attempt to remove the body to Ilius.

Such fearful turmoil of men and horses did Jove on that day

Achaeans and Trojans fight continuously around Patroclus's body with spears, swords, and chariots. Jove and Minerva influence the battle, which turns repeatedly as the Trojans pursue and the Achaeans defend the dead.

BOOK XVIII.

A fierce battle takes place around Patroclus’s exposed body as the Achaeans defend it and the Trojans try to drag it away. Hector repeatedly attempts to seize the body, while the two Ajaxes hold him off until Achilles’s appearance forces the Trojans into confusion.
