agriculture

topic · 3 works · 4 quoted facts · 3 connections
Also called: corn, ears of corn, farra, frugibus, grain and agriculture, harvest, messis, sata, seeds of Ceres, semen, sustenance, the art of tilling the ground and sowing corn, wheat
  • In The Amores, or Amours, corn is described as a crop given by the earth that Ceres taught people to grow and cut.
  • In Heroides and Amores, grain and agriculture are described through Ceres’s teaching of cultivation, harvesting, and plowing with oxen and sickles.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, agriculture is treated through Ceres, whose buried seeds and teaching of tilling and sowing corn support her interpretation as goddess of the Earth and corn.

corn in The Amores, or Amours

Ovid · -0015

Corn is a crop given by the earth; Ceres taught people to grow and cut it, and it dwindled when fields were neglected while flourishing in Crete.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ELEGY X.

Ceres taught people to make corn swell in the fields and cut its coloured locks with the sickle. When she neglected the fields, the crops emerged with very little wheat, while the corn in Crete flourished.

grain and agriculture in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Ceres teaches grain’s cultivation, harvesting, and plowing with oxen and sickles, replacing acorns and turf herbs; when she leaves for love, crops fail except in fertile Crete.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

IX

Ceres teaches the cultivation, harvesting, and plowing of grain, replacing earlier foods such as acorns and turf herbs. When she abandons the fields for love, crops fail across the land, while Crete remains fertile and even the wild boar is said to reap grain there.

agriculture in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Agriculture is treated through Ceres, whose buried seeds, teaching of tilling and sowing corn, and returning crops support her interpretation as goddess of the Earth and corn. Though linked to its introduction into Greece, the account favors the introduction of Ceres's worship, while allowing improved tilling methods from Egypt.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

notes .. notes

Agriculture is central to the interpretation of Ceres as a goddess of the Earth and corn. Ceres is said to have taught her subjects to till the ground and sow corn, while the return of crops allegorically explains Jupiter’s settlement of the dispute between Ceres and Pluto.

notes .. notes

Some have connected the story with the introduction of agriculture into Greece. The unit considers it more probable that the story concerns the introduction of Ceres's worship, while allowing that improved methods of tilling the ground may also have been learned from Egypt.
