uncle Robert in The Diary of Samuel Pepys

Samuel Pepys · 1660/1669

Uncle Robert, the diarist’s uncle, suffers serious dizziness and episodic stupidity and speechlessness while his business needs the diarist’s father’s attention. He offers adjoining land for purchase. He dies on the 5th; the diarist goes to Brampton for the funeral and affairs, finding a reasonably supportive will but disappointing estate and papers.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

Robert is suffering from dizziness in his head, and his condition is considered serious. His business requires the diarist's father's attention, and the diarist wants his father to discuss buying land with him.

opening

Uncle Robert died on the 5th, and the narrator traveled to Brampton for his funeral and to examine his affairs. His will was found to provide reasonably well for the narrator's family and other relatives, although his estate and papers were less satisfactory than expected.
