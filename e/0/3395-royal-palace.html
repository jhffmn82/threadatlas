  • In The Hungry Tiger of Oz, the Royal Palace contains Ozma’s garden and later fades below Ozma in the sky.

  • In The Yellow Knight of Oz, Ozma’s palace hosts the gathering where Sir Hokus announces his quest and its inhabitants prepare to join it.

Palace of Oz in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04

The Palace of Oz is a large green palace in the middle of the City of Oz, with spires and a dome above the Emerald City. Guarded by soldiers and attended by a green girl, it contains the great Throne Room of Oz and plenty of silk for making a balloon. After Dorothy and her companions enter the Emerald City, the Guardian leads them to the Palace, where they receive rooms and wait for separate audiences with Oz, who appears in different forms. Later, the travelers return with a crowd and again stay as Oz's guests while he considers helping Dorothy.

Chapter X

The Great Oz sits day after day in the great Throne Room of his Palace and does not allow people to see him face to face. The Guardian of the Gates leads Dorothy and her companions toward the Palace after they enter the Emerald City.

Chapter XI

The Palace of Oz is a large green building in the middle of the City of Oz, guarded by soldiers and attended by a green girl. Dorothy and her companions are given pleasant rooms there while they wait for their separate audiences with Oz. The Palace includes the Throne Room where Oz appears in different forms.

Chapter XV

The travelers are followed by a crowd to the Palace of Oz and are given their old rooms while waiting to see him. Oz offers to treat them as guests in the Palace while he considers how to help Dorothy.

royal palace in The Marvelous Land of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1904-06

The royal palace is a palace in the Emerald City, with arched hallways containing a throne room and surrounding a courtyard. It is occupied by the Scarecrow and companions while girl soldiers surround it in two rows after the city is conquered. Its entrances are barred, bolted, and locked; an outer passage and gateway permit an escape. Rebels tear emeralds from its marble steps, and Nick Chopper plans an aerial machine on its roof when doors are unsafe.

3 sections; each heading opens the text

The Scarecrow Plans an escape

The palace is surrounded by the invading girls after the Emerald City is conquered. The Soldier bars its doors and windows, but an outer passage and gateway allow the Scarecrow’s group to escape.

The Prisoners of the Queen

The royal palace has marble steps from which the Army of Revolt has torn the emeralds, leaving tiny holes. After the mouse panic, the Scarecrow's party closes, bolts, and locks its entrances against the returning rebels.

The Scarecrow Takes Time to Think

Jinjur’s palace is occupied by the Scarecrow and his companions, but it is surrounded by two rows of girl soldiers. The group cannot safely escape through its doors, so Nick Chopper plans to build the aerial machine on its roof.

The Emerald City palace is a gorgeous, emerald-studded royal palace of Oz, normally located in the Emerald City above a cave. It contains Ozma, most of the Courtiers, Dorothy and her friends, a sitting-room, and the Magic Picture in one of Ozma’s rooms. Its crisis begins when it shivers, its lights fail, furniture falls, and the whole castle seems to rise into the air. It then disappears, leaving a deep palace-shaped hole in the gardens and a giant footprint nearby. The cause is tied to Glegg’s tunnel beneath the palace and the Mixed Magic he buried there: Ruggedo becomes a giant, bursts through the cave ceiling, and the palace is impaled on the spikes of his gray head. The occupants are endangered whenever Ruggedo moves, while others seek the giant and the one responsible. The Question Box says the palace will settle firmly on its foundations when Ruggedo returns to the Emerald City. Afterward, Ozma and the others set the topsy-turvy palace right.
