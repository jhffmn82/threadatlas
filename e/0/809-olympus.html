  • In The Argonautica, Olympus contains Cypris's palace and Zeus's orchard, where Cypris finds Eros before he travels down toward earth.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Olympus is the glorious snowy dwelling of Zeus, the Muses, and the undying gods, where they assemble and from which Zeus acts.
  • In The Iliad, Olympus is the snowy heavenly dwelling of the gods, chiefly the seat of Jove, where he rules, convenes councils, watches events, and receives the gods.

  • In The Fall of Troy, Olympus is where the gods observe the contest and where Eos returns to Zeus's threshold through the heavenly gates.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, Olympus is the abode of the gods and, on its summit, the site of the palace and separate dwellings of the Olympians.

Olympus in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Olympus is a high, glorious, snowy home and dwelling place of Zeus, the Muses, and the undying gods. The Muses sing there, and its snowy heights hold assemblies of the immortals, Zeus’s scales of judgment, and the gods’ oaths by Styx. Zeus calls the deathless gods there before fighting the Titans, comes from it hurling lightning, and it reels during that battle. Styx first arrives there with her children in support of Zeus. Hermes and Apollo reach it after their dispute; Heracles lives there happily after his works. Finally, it reels horribly when Pallas Athene springs from Zeus’s head armed for war.

Olympus in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Olympus is a snowy, heavenly, many-delled mountain and divine dwelling: the home of the gods and immortals, with mansions, summits, topmost ridges, an assembly, and Jove’s bronze-floored mansion. It is chiefly the seat of Jove, who sits on its highest ridges or golden throne, convenes councils, watches Troy and battle, directs the gods, and sends commands. Thetis petitions him there, and Olympus reels when he confirms his promise or enters its assembly; Juno also makes it quake in rage. From its summits Minerva descends to obey Jove, while Juno looks down and later departs to pursue her deception. Gods flee or return there after conflict: Mars arrives wounded, and Diana reaches Jove’s mansion weeping. During battle, Jove remains on Olympus as other gods go among Trojans and Achaeans, and it receives defeated gods. At the end, the gods assemble there again as Jove instructs Thetis and Iris, and Iris carries divine commands to Priam.
