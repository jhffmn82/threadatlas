Pandarus in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Pandarus, Lycaon’s son from Zelea and Lycia, is a Trojan archer whose chief weapon is an ibex-horn bow. Persuaded by Minerva disguised as Laodocus, he prays to Lycian Apollo and shoots Menelaus with an unused winged arrow.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK IV.

Pandarus, son of Lycaon, is persuaded by Minerva in the form of Laodocus to shoot Menelaus. He takes up his ibex-horn bow, prays to Lycian Apollo, and fires the arrow that wounds Menelaus.
