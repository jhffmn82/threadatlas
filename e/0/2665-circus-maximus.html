Circus Maximus

place · 2 works · 3 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: Circus, circus
  • In The Amores, or Amours, Circus Maximus is a setting where arranged seating brings the speaker close to his mistress, with barriers, open space, a procession, a crowd, and a chariot race unfolding around them.
  • In Heroides and Amores, the circus is a racing venue with assigned seating and a starting line that places the poet and woman close together, hosts four-horse chariot races and a gods’ procession, and lets its crowd demand restarts by waving togas.

ELEGY II.

The Circus Maximus provides the setting where the speaker sits close to his mistress because of the arrangement of its seating. Its barriers, turning-place, open space, procession, crowd, and chariot race shape the events of the unit.

circus in Heroides and Amores

A racing venue with assigned seating and a starting line, circus places the poet and woman close together, hosts four-horse chariot races and a gods’ procession, and lets its crowd demand restarts by waving togas.

1 section; each heading opens the text

LIBER TERTIUS

The circus places the poet and the woman close together under its seating rules and provides the setting for the chariot race and the procession of the gods. Its crowd can demand that a race be restarted by waving their togas.
