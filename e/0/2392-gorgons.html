Gorgons in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Gorgons were a powerful African nation of women, enemies of the Amazons who troubled the Atlantides; Merina captured many, Perseus conquered them under Medusa, and Hercules extirpated them.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAP. IV.

The Gorgons were an African nation of courageous and powerful women who were enemies of the Amazons and frequently troubled the Atlantides. Merina defeated them and captured three thousand of them, but they later remained powerful until Medusa's reign, when Perseus conquered them. Hercules eventually extirpated the Gorgons.

Gorgons in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

Gorgons include Medusa and Euryale, ravening sisters with maiden and serpent heads; after Perseus' attack, their fierce death-lament becomes the model for Athene's flute music.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

XII.

The Gorgons are described as having maiden heads and unapproachable serpent heads, and their lament was heard during the anguish following Perseus' attack. Their death-lament became the model for Athene's flute music.

Gorgons in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The Gorgons are a group variously described as female warriors near Lake Tritonis, wild women, wealthy sisters, beautiful women whose appearance petrifies, or a deadly animal. They are said to be daughters of Phorcus or Phorcys, numbering three in one account and two sisters in another, with shared possessions including islands, a golden statue of Minerva, a minister, and an eye of the Graeae. They inhabit Gorgades, possibly Cape Verde Islands. In the opening, Perseus reaches their fortified abode beneath Atlas and finds Medusa’s victims turned to stone.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

notes .. notes

The Gorgons were variously described as female warriors near Lake Tritonis, savage women living in caves and forests, wealthy sisters who owned islands and a golden statue, beautiful women whose appearance turned people into statues, or a deadly animal called a gorgon. They were said to inhabit the Gorgades in the Æthiopian Sea, possibly the Cape Verde Islands.

opening

The Gorgons dwell in a remote, fortified place beneath Atlas. Perseus reaches their abode and sees statues of people and wild beasts turned to stone by the sight of Medusa.

the Gorgons in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The Gorgons are three monstrous females with huge teeth, brazen claws, and snaky hair; two are immortal, one mortal, and they may personify open-sea terrors.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter IX

The Gorgons are three monstrous females with huge teeth, brazen claws, and snaky hair; two are immortal and one is mortal. The text presents them as possible personifications of the strong billows of the open sea.
