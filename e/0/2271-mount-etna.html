  • In The Aeneid, Mount Etna is a volcanic mountain beside the secure port on the Cyclopian shores, continually emitting smoke, embers, flames, rocks, and burning sulphur, while its caverns echo with the cries of Polyphemus and the Cyclopes.
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Mount Etna is a source of fire whose eruptions damaged and laid waste to Sicilian territory; Ceres lit her firebrands at its eruptions, and it became the Cataneans’ refuge after they were forced to leave Catana.
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Mount Aetna is a Sicilian mountain overlooking plains, with rocky slopes and Cyclops-inhabited caves; it supplies Polyphemus’s feast implements, and he hunts on its summit as its shepherd.
  • In The Extant Odes of Pindar, Aitna is a snowy volcanic mountain honored through Zeus, Chromios, and Aitnaian Zeus, with Typhon beneath it and fire and lava emerging from it; Zeus is asked to favor its fruitful land.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Ætna is a sulphurous, bituminous mountain pressing on Typhœus’s head to imprison him beneath the earth, emitting flames and associated with violent earth shocks.

Mount Etna in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Mount Etna is described as a source of fire and as a place whose eruptions damaged and laid waste to Sicilian territory. Ceres is said to have lit her firebrands at its eruptions while searching for Proserpine. Etna, formerly called Eunetia, became the refuge of the Cataneans after they were forced to leave Catana.

Mount Aetna in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Mount Ætna is a mountain in Sicily overlooking its plains, with rocky slopes and caves inhabited by Cyclops. It supplies Polyphemus’s feast implements; he hunts on its summit and is called its shepherd.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

THE CYCLOPS.

Mount Ætna in Sicily is the setting of the drama. Its rocky slopes and caves are inhabited by Cyclops, and Polypheme hunts on its summit.

ULYSSES, CHORUS.

Ætna supplies the hatchets and thorn stems used by Polyphemus in preparing his feast. Ulysses later calls Polyphemus the shepherd of Ætna.

Aitna in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

Aitna is a snowy volcanic mountain, honored in Nemean Ode I through Zeus, Chromios, and Aitnaian Zeus; Typhon lies beneath it, fire and lava emerge, and Zeus is asked to favor its fruitful land.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

I.

Aitna is described as the mountain beneath which Typhon lies imprisoned and from which fire and lava emerge. The ode asks Zeus to favor the mountain and its fruitful land.

Nemean Ode I

Aitna is honored through Chromios’s victory and through the praise of Aitnaian Zeus. Chromios is identified as a man of Aitna.

Ætna in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Ætna, a mountain, presses on Typhœus’s head to imprison him beneath earth; rich in sulphur and bituminous matter, it often emits flames and is linked to violent earth shocks.

Ætna was often seen emitting flames, and the forces within it were associated with violent shocks in the earth. According to the fable, the Gods threw Mount Ætna onto Typhœus or Enceladus.
