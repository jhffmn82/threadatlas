wine in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Wine is a natural product of the vine, made by pressing grapes, and a pleasant, strengthening drink. Some naturalists treat it as the substance represented by Bacchus. Osiris and Dionysius are credited with discovering or teaching the vine’s cultivation, grape harvest, wine preservation, production, and use. Wine serves as a sacred pledge in oaths of fidelity, military service, pardons, truces, and wartime leagues through pouring and drinking. Boiling wine was understood to strengthen and fragrance it; pure wine was believed to cause madness, while wine mixed with water was said to cheer, refresh, and cure intoxication. Gaul, unable to produce it because of cold, imports it eagerly, sometimes at the price of a boy for a hogshead.

wine in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Wine, Bacchus's gift to mortals, springs from the ground when a Bacchant sets her wand upon it. Consumed by mortals, it relieves and ends grief, brings sleep and forgetfulness of daily troubles, enables Venus and pleasures, and is honored in libations and Bacchic worship.

wine in Child's Health Primer for Primary Classes

Jane Andrews · 1885

Wine is changed grape-juice containing water, alcohol, and grape flavor. It can also be made from currants, elderberries, and other fruits in much the same way as from grapes. It contains a little alcohol compared with distilled liquor, costs more than beer, and can be used to make distilled liquors, wine-jelly, and wine-sauce. Its alcohol may lead drinkers to become drunkards; if left warm long enough, it turns into vinegar.

Wine contains a little alcohol. Wine-jelly and wine-sauce can teach a person to like the taste of alcohol.
