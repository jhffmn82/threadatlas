the sea in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

The sea is a body of water and a dangerous realm, tossed by wind and wave, bordering Phyllis at her coast and harbor. It matters most as a treacherous barrier and threat to travelers, ships, and sailors: stormy, deep, and raging, with contrary winds, rocks, Scylla, Charybdis, the Syrtes, and the Ceraunian mountains. It keeps Demophoon from Phyllis, surrounds Ariadne’s island, and may punish faithlessness and broken promises. From watched waters and delayed return, it unfolds into fears of shipwreck, isolation, rough waves, and death as the shore recedes.

Narrative, section by section

II

The sea has kept Demophoon from returning, although Phyllis repeatedly watches its winds and waters for signs of his approach. She threatens to throw herself into it so that its waves may carry her body to Demophoon's shores.

The sea is described as stormy, dangerous, and capable of punishing those who have broken faith. Dido fears that Aeneas may be shipwrecked and asks him to wait until the waters become calm.

The sea surrounds Ariadne’s island and prevents her access to land. It threatens her with dangerous waters and is said to cast up large seals.

The sea is presented as treacherous, with storms, contrary winds, deep waters, dangerous rocks, Scylla, Charybdis, and the Syrtes. A sailor fears death when the shore recedes and the waves are roughened.

Ocean in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Ocean is an unnavigable sea and expanse of water that, after separating from earth, heaven, and air, sinks to the lowest place and surrounds Earth. It expands in the deluge, overwhelms hills and mountain heights, loses distinct shores, and carries away people, animals, buildings, and vegetation. Neptune raises or calms it and orders its retreat, after which it again has a shore. Jupiter later crosses it as a bull carrying Europa, who fears the passage and looks back toward shore.

the sea in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

The sea is a stormy, dangerous domain inhabited by sea Gods. Winds drive it into violent upheaval until it rises, breaks a ship, enters its hull, and engulfs vessel and crew. It later receives Æsacus and Glaucus after their plunges; Æsacus loves it, while its Gods honor and transform Glaucus.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

FABLE VII. [XI.410-748]

The sea becomes white with swelling waves and is driven into violent upheaval by the winds. It rises toward the heavens, breaks against the ship, enters its hull, and finally engulfs the vessel and much of its crew.

FABLE VIII. [XI.749-795]

The sea receives Æsacus when he hurls himself from a rock. After his transformation, he loves the sea and repeatedly plunges into it, while his feathers break his falls.

FABLE VIII. [XIII.898-968]

The sea receives Glaucus after he plunges into it. Its Gods grant him kindred honors, while Oceanus and Tethys purify him and oversee the removal of his mortality through repeated charms and immersion beneath many streams.

The sea is a broad natural expanse, bounded by America at the horizon, resembling the Mediterranean in blueness, and visible from Mullion Cove before its inner sandy nook. It unfolds through changing shore views: first dark, rough, and difficult to cross, with hills, valleys, foaming whirlpools, and beautiful sunset, moonrise, and reflected lights; then rolling over silver sand beneath a setting sun. It becomes calm and brilliantly blue with birds and pools, receives the day’s last glow, and in storm merges with the sky into darkness. Pacific Ocean in The Sea Fairies

L. Frank Baum · 1911

Pacific Ocean is a body of water below the bluff, a beloved companion to Trot and Cap'n Bill and a home for sea people. It can be stormy and shore-breaking or calm; in Giant's Cave it appears sapphire and azure beneath a rock dome. Clear and bright near the surface, it becomes darker blue with descent, while phosphorescent creatures and some fish provide light below. Its rugged, mountainous bottom includes white sand, rough rocks, sea shrubs, flowering weeds, kelp beds, rocky canyons, open sandy spaces, sea gardens, coral palaces, Crabville, deep ocean caverns, Zog's castle, and the undiscovered island nearby. Mid-water offers faster but less safe travel; the deepest route holds mermaid palaces and other remarkable, dangerous beings. The ocean contains schools of fish, crabs, sharks, devilfish, sea serpents, jellyfish, singing barnacles, swordfish guards, sea pigs, snapping eels, starfishes, sea horses, sea spiders, and King Anko. Air enables mermaids and visitors to breathe, but its deep water could crush unprotected bodies, making it unsuitable and dangerous for earth people; magic protects Trot and Cap'n Bill. Trot, Cap'n Bill, and companions descend through its waters, explore its creatures and palaces, rise near the undiscovered island, and face darkness from cuttlefish ink while being carried through a clear passage. Captives seek the broad, clear ocean beyond a dome, and Queen Aquareine makes the Yell-Maker disappear forever from it. In the end, mermaids, Trot, and Cap'n Bill swim straight through middle water, passing fish toward the Giant's Cave entrance near the sea bottom.
