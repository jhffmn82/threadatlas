Trojan Cycle in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

The Trojan Cycle is a set of component epics that, with the Homeric poems, connects events before and after the Iliad and Odyssey. It unfolds through Cyprian Lays, Aethiopis, Little Iliad, Sack of Troy, Returns, and Telegony; evidence survives chiefly as fragments and Proclus’s abridgment preserved by Photius.

The Ionic School

The Trojan Cycle connected the events before and after the Iliad and Odyssey through the Cyprian Lays, Aethiopis, Little Iliad, Sack of Troy, Returns, and Telegony, together with the Homeric poems. Its surviving evidence is chiefly fragments and Proclus’s abridgment preserved by Photius.
