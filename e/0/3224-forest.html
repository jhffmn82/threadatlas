forest in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04

The forest is a thick, dangerous part of the route to Emerald City. It contains a cottage, spring, rusted Tin Woodman, wild animals, Kalidahs, and a camp. Dorothy, Scarecrow, and Tin Woodman travel through it along a leaf-covered yellow brick road, camp overnight, cross a ditch and gulf, face Kalidahs, and pass from its dark, gloomy thickness to thinner trees and the river.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter V

The forest contains the cottage, a spring, and the tree beside which the Tin Woodman has rusted. Dorothy, the Scarecrow, and the Tin Woodman travel through it before reaching the road of yellow brick.

Chapter VI

The forest is thick and dangerous, with a yellow brick road covered by branches and dead leaves. Few birds live there, and wild animals growl from among the trees.

Chapter VII

The travelers camp in the forest, cross a first ditch and a second gulf within it, and encounter the Kalidahs. The forest becomes thick, dark, and gloomy before its trees grow thinner and the travelers reach the river.
