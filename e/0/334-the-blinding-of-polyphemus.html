Also called: blinded an eye of Polyphemus, blinded him, blinding of Polyphemus, blinding of the Cyclops, bore it into the monster’s eye, burn out the Cyclops’ eye, burned my visual nerves, deprived of sight, got him down, managed to blind my eye, put your eye out, spoiled your beauty, the stroke, this vengeance
and 1 moreἐξετύφλωσεν αὐτόν
  • In The Aeneid, Ulysses and his companions blind Polyphemus by driving a flaming brand into his single eye while he sleeps, avenging the Greeks whom he killed and enabling their escape from the cave.
  • In Hecuba and other plays, the blinding of Polyphemus is accomplished through Ulysses’ craft, wine, sleep, and a heated olive pole, while the Chorus celebrates the deed without directly undertaking the dangerous work.
  • In The Odyssey, Odysseus and four companions drive a heated, sharpened olive-wood beam into the eye of the drunken, sleeping Polyphemus; his cry that “Noman” attacks him misleads the other Cyclopes, and the injury provokes Neptune’s continuing anger and obstructs Odysseus’ journey home.
  • In The Library, Odysseus and four companions sharpen and heat a club and use it to blind Polyphemus while he sleeps after drinking Maron's wine, then escape beneath the sheep.

the blinding of Polyphemus in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

The blinding of Polyphemus is Ulysses’ attack on the king of the Cyclopes. After getting him drunk and asleep, Odysseus and four companions drive a heated, sharpened olive-wood beam into his eye. Polyphemus’s cry that “Noman” attacks him misleads other Cyclopes. The injury cannot be healed except by Neptune and provokes Neptune’s continuing anger, obstructing Ulysses’ journey home.
