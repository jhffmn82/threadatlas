Brixton Mystery in A Study in Scarlet

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1886

The Brixton Mystery is a murder investigation at Lauriston Gardens, conducted by Tobias Gregson and Lestrade while Sherlock Holmes unofficially observes and assists. Holmes closely examines the grounds, room, grey dust, and inscription, and seeks constable John Rance's address. He soon believes the main facts are clear enough to continue, despite obscure details. Newspapers widely cover the strange crime and speculate about political refugees or revolutionists.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER III.

The investigation at Lauriston Gardens is conducted by Tobias Gregson and Lestrade, with Sherlock Holmes and the Doctor observing and assisting. Holmes studies the grounds and room in detail, collects grey dust, examines the inscription, and asks for the address of John Rance, the constable who found the body.

CHAPTER IV.

Holmes says his mind is made up about the main facts of the case, though some details remain obscure. He believes the evidence provides a working basis for continuing the investigation.

CHAPTER VI.

The newspapers describe the Brixton Mystery as an unusually strange crime and speculate that political refugees or revolutionists were responsible. Sherlock Holmes and Doctor Watson discuss the reports, while Tobias Gregson and G. Lestrade investigate the case.
