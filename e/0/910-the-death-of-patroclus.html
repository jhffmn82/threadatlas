the death of Patroclus in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

Patroclus’s death is a battle death in the Trojan War and a disaster for Achilles. After Apollo strikes him and he loses his helmet and armour, Euphorbus wounds him with a spear and Hector gives the final thrust through his belly. Patroclus dies after warning Hector that Achilles will kill him, and his soul goes to Hades. His body becomes the point of fighting at the ships: Menelaus and Ajax defend it while Hector takes Achilles’ armour and rallies the Trojans. The death brings grief to Achilles, the Myrmidons, and the Achaeans. When Antilochus reports it to Achilles, Achilles abandons his anger toward Agamemnon, returns to battle, and seeks vengeance on Hector.

Patroclus's death is known to the fighters around his body but not to Achilles. It causes grief among the Achaeans, motivates Menelaus to send urgent word to Achilles, and becomes the reason for the fierce struggle to recover his body.

BOOK XVIII.

Patroclus’s death is reported to Achilles by Antilochus, who says that Hector killed him and took his armour. His death drives Achilles to abandon his anger toward Agamemnon, return to battle, and seek vengeance against Hector.

death of Patroclus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The death of Patroclus is a major event in the Trojan War. Apollo disarms him, an unnamed Trojan wounds him, and Hector kills him and takes Achilles' armor. Ajax and Menelaus defend the body until it reaches the ships and Achilles learns of it, reproaches himself, seeks revenge, and the Greeks hold funeral rites and games.

Patroclus dies after Apollo removes his helmet and lance, an unnamed Trojan wounds him from behind, and Hector pierces him with a spear. Hector takes Achilles' armor, while Ajax and Menelaus defend the body until the Greeks carry it back to the ships and Antilochus informs Achilles.
