Jason, son of Aeson and nephew of Pelias, is an ambitious heroic adventurer and captain of the Argonaut expedition. Seeking fame, he leads the Argonauts to Colchis, promises marriage to Medea for her aid, helps seize the Golden Fleece, and is wounded during the escape before Medea cures him. Jason in The Argonautica

Apollonius Rhodius · -0299/-0200

Jason is Aeson and Alcimede’s son, Cretheus’s grandson, and a hero who becomes leader of the Argonauts and commander of Argo. After crossing the Anaurus and losing a sandal, he comes before Pelias, whose response to an oracle sends him on the dangerous quest for the Golden Fleece. Guided by Apollo’s oracle, favored by Hera for carrying her when she appeared as an old crone, and comforted by Athena, he gathers the heroes, accepts their election as leader, sacrifices to Apollo, and departs from Pagasae. On the voyage Jason declines Hypsipyle’s sovereignty, manages conflicts and losses among the Argonauts, including the departure of Heracles, and leads dealings with Phineus, the sons of Phrixus, and others. He experiences fear for his comrades and their return to Hellas, but repeatedly regains resolve. At the Phasis he approaches Aeetes for the fleece; Aeetes imposes the contest of the bronze-footed, fire-breathing bulls, the serpent’s teeth, and the Earthborn men. As a stranger in Colchis, Jason receives Medea’s magical help. He promises her honor, remembrance, and marriage in Hellas, receives her dread charm, sacrifices to Hecate Brimo, and uses the charm to withstand and yoke the bulls, plough with the adamant plough, sow the teeth, and destroy the Earthborn men by turning them against one another. He then receives Medea, carries off the Golden Fleece, orders the return, and defends the ship from pursuit. During the return Jason and Medea deceive Apsyrtus, whom Jason ambushes and kills. He undergoes Circe’s purification for the killing, remains bound to Medea by oaths, and marries her in Macris’s sacred cave. He continues leading the Argonauts through sea and desert dangers, follows the counsel of Libyan nymphs, assists Medea against Talos, and calls on Phoebus during the Pall of Darkness. His course runs from Pelias’s command, through the fleece’s capture, to a perilous return shaped by leadership, divine aid, Medea’s help, and the consequences of Apsyrtus’s death.

Jason in Medea

Euripides · -0430

Jason is Aeson’s son, Pelias’s nephew, and the kingly heir of Iolcos in Thessaly. A famous, ambitious Greek hero and chief of the Argonauts, he seeks the Golden Ram’s fleece and ancestral honour, accepting Medea’s help and flight from Colchis. He becomes Medea’s husband and the father of their two children, though the introduction says their marriage is not legal. Later, valuing political security, prosperity, and career above love, he abandons Medea and their sons to marry Creon’s daughter, a royal bride. Medea, the Nurse, and the Attendant condemn this as betrayal; Medea calls him evil and a thief and blames his new marriage for her exile. Jason defends himself by claiming her curses caused the exile and that his marriage will secure a prosperous household and future for the children. He offers Medea money and support, which she rejects. When she appears reconciled, he agrees to protect the children and let them bring gifts to his bride. After Medea kills the bride, the king, and their children, Jason arrives to save the sons, learns they are dead, curses Medea, grieves his childlessness, and is denied their burial.

INTRODUCTION

Jason is the son of Aeson and a famous Argonaut who seeks the Golden Ram’s fleece and his ancestral honour. He accepts Medea’s help and flight from Colchis, but later abandons her for a Corinthian marriage in pursuit of political security and a great career; the introduction presents him as ambitious, calculating, and fundamentally unchanged from his heroic past.

MEDEA

Jason has forsaken Medea and his two sons in order to marry Creon's daughter and live in a king's chamber. The Nurse and the Attendant describe him as no longer acting like a husband or father, while Medea calls him evil and vows to repay his betrayal.

MEDEA.

Jason is identified by Medea as the man who gave the bride and as one of the three people she intends to kill. Medea says she once shaped her plans for his sake but now regards him as a thief whose marriage has brought about her exile.

_Enter from the right_ JASON.

Jason claims that Medea's angry speech and curses caused her exile, while he presents his marriage to another woman as a prudent effort to secure a prosperous royal household and future for his children. He offers Medea provisions, money, and recommendations to friends abroad, but she rejects his help.

CHORUS.

Jason answers Medea’s call and accepts her apparent repentance. He praises her wisdom, promises to protect the children, agrees to ask that they remain in Corinth, and allows them to carry gifts to his new bride.

[_Enter_ MESSENGER.

Jason comes to save his children from the dead king's kinsmen, but learns that Medea has killed them. He curses Medea, grieves that he is childless, and begs to touch and bury the children; Medea refuses him.

Chapter XI

Jason led the expedition to obtain the Golden Fleece and assembled the Argonauts aboard the Argo. In Colchis, he yoked the fire-breathing bulls, sowed the dragon's teeth, and defeated the armed men that sprang from them by using Medea's charm. He then took the fleece from the sleeping dragon, escaped with Medea and the Argonauts, returned safely to Thessaly, and delivered the fleece to Pelias.

MEDEA AND AESON

Jason asks Medea to add some of his years to Aeson's life, but she promises to lengthen Aeson's life without shortening his. He later wishes to marry Creusa and puts Medea away.
