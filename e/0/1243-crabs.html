crabs in Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica

Hesiod · -0699~

Crabs are mailed, shell-covered warriors with curving claws, eight legs, feelers, and backward-looking eyes; Zeus sends them to aid Frogs by attacking Mice, nipping their tails, paws, and feet until they flee.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

THE BATTLE OF FROGS AND MICE

The crabs are mailed, shell-covered creatures with curving claws, eight legs, feelers, and eyes that look behind them. Zeus sends them to aid the Frogs, and they nip off the Mice's tails, paws, and feet until the Mice flee.

crabs in The Sea Fairies

L. Frank Baum · 1911

Crabs are sea creatures with many families, related to lobsters and crawfish, living among rocks and kelp in Crabville and serving, performing, or hiding.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

[Illustration: _Chap. 7._

The crabs live among rocks and kelp in Crabville and display many different behaviors. Some serve as household servants for mermaids, while others perform music, jokes, acrobatics, or hide in shells.
