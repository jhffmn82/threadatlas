Ulysses's presumed death in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Ulysses's presumed death is the suitors' and Eumaeus's belief that he is long absent or dead, used to justify consuming his estate. Eurymachus places his death in a far country; Eumaeus imagines beasts or fish destroying him. Telemachus remains uncertain and seeks news before funeral arrangements.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK II

Antinous and Eurymachus treat Ulysses as absent or dead, and Eurymachus says that he died in a far country. Telemachus does not know whether he is alive, so he plans to seek news before deciding what funeral rites and arrangements should follow.

BOOK XIV

Eumaeus believes that Ulysses has been dead and gone for a long time. He says that wolves, birds of prey, or fish may have destroyed him and that his bones may lie buried on a foreign shore, although he continues to honor and grieve for him.
