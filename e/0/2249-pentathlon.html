The Pentathlon in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

The Pentathlon is a five-contest athletic event comprising the jump, discus throw, javelin throw, foot-race, and wrestling. Victory could be secured by winning at least three contests. Xenophon won it at Olympia, and Sogenes won the boys’ prize.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

XIII.

The Pentathlon consisted of the Jump, throwing the Disk, throwing the Javelin, the Foot-race, and Wrestling. Xenophon won the Pentathlon at Olympia, probably by winning at least three of its five contests.

Nemean Ode VII

The pentathlon is the five-game athletic contest in which Sogenes wins the boys' prize. Its events are identified as the jump, discus throw, javelin throw, foot-race, and wrestling.

notes

The Pentathlon is identified in a footnote, which directs the reader to the Introduction to Ol. xiii and the note on Nem. vii, p. 129 for further information.

pentathlum in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Pentathlum was an Olympic and athletic contest held at Olympia and other Greek games, including Delphi and Nemea; at Olympia it included a piper. It was remembered and added in the 18th Olympiad, when Lampis won. A boys’ pentathlum began in the 38th Olympiad but ended after Eutelidas of Lacedæmon won because Elis did not want boys training for it. Many athletes won it, including Gorgus, who won four Olympic victories, and Timon, who won at all Greek games except the Isthmian.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VIII.

The pentathlum was remembered and added to the games in the 18th Olympiad, when Lampis was victorious. A pentathlum for boys was established in the 38th Olympiad but was discontinued after Eutelidas of Lacedæmon won it, because Elis did not wish its boys to train for the event.

BOOK VI.--ELIS. PART II.

The pentathlum appears among the contests recorded at Olympia, Delphi, and Nemea. Athletes such as Stomius, Hysmon, Eupolemus, Antiochus, and Dicon are described as winning pentathlum victories, while Hysmon trained for it to strengthen his constitution after suffering a discharge.

CHAPTER XIII.

The pentathlum is recorded as an event in which Gorgus of Elis won four Olympic victories, as well as one victory in the double course and one in the race in heavy armour. Timon won the pentathlum at all the Greek games except the Isthmian, and several other athletes in the unit are recorded as pentathlum victors.
