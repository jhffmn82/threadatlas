Jefferson Hope's confession in A Study in Scarlet

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1886

Jefferson Hope’s police-station confession, made while an aortic aneurism threatens his life, explains his pursuit of justice and revenge. He recounts using poison pills to kill Enoch Drebber, describes his responsibility for Joseph Stangerson’s death, and refuses to identify his accomplice so as not to implicate another person.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

Jefferson Hope gives his confession at the police-station after John Watson, M.D., confirms that his aortic aneurism creates an immediate danger of death. He explains his motives, pursuit, use of poison pills, killing of Enoch Drebber, and responsibility for Joseph Stangerson's death.

“That was how Enoch Drebber came to his end. All I had to do then was

Jefferson Hope confesses to killing Enoch Drebber and Stangerson and explains how he carried out both confrontations. He refuses to reveal the identity of his accomplice, saying that he does not get other people into trouble.
