  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, Achilles' armour is contested after Achilles' death, awarded to Odysseus rather than Aias, and later sought unsuccessfully by Neoptolemus.
  • In Hesiod, the Homeric Hymns, and Homerica, Achilles' arms are disputed by Odysseus and Aias, awarded to Odysseus through Athena's contriving, and later given to Neoptolemus.
  • In The Iliad, the armour of Achilles is lent to Patroclus, taken and worn by Hector after Patroclus falls, and later worn again by Achilles, whom it protects from Hector's spear.
  • In The Library, Achilles' arms are set up by Hephaestus as a prize for the bravest after Achilles' death and are awarded to Odysseus rather than Ajax.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the arms of Achilles are made by Vulcan, become the prize in a dispute between Ajax and Ulysses, and are awarded to Ulysses by the Argive leaders.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV, the armour of Achilles is the object of the rivalry between Ajax Telamon and Ulysses, is claimed by Ajax, and is obtained by Ulysses, after which Ajax kills himself with Hector's sword.
  • In The Fall of Troy, Achilles' armor is divine, immortal battle-gear made by Hephaestus; after being awarded to Odysseus, it is promised to Neoptolemus, whom it fits and protects in battle.

Achilles' armour in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Achilles' armour, made by Hephaestus, becomes contested after Achilles' death. Though Aias is most valiant, surviving Greek judges award it to Odysseus as wisest, enraging Aias. Neoptolemus later seeks it, but Odysseus claims he saved it and Achilles; Neoptolemus returns to Scyros deprived.

AIAS

After Achilles' death, his armour is to be awarded to the worthiest surviving Greek. Although Aias is described as the most valiant, the judges award the armour to Odysseus because he is considered the wisest, provoking Aias' rage.

armour of Achilles in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

The armour of Achilles is immortal armour given by the gods to Peleus and later to Achilles. Achilles lends it to Patroclus, who wears all but Achilles’ great spear so Trojans may mistake him for Achilles. After Patroclus falls, Hector takes, wears, and boasts in the armour, appearing like Achilles and leaving Achilles unable to arm himself. Later, Achilles again wears the gleaming armour while pursuing Hector; it protects him when Hector’s spear strikes the shield and rebounds without piercing it.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK XVI.

Achilles gives his armour to Patroclus so that the Trojans will mistake him for Achilles and retreat. Patroclus wears the greaves, cuirass, sword, shield, and helmet, but does not take Achilles’ great spear because no other Achaean can wield it.

BOOK XVII.

The armour of Achilles is the immortal armour given by the gods to Peleus and later to his son. Hector takes it after killing Patroclus, puts it on, and appears to the allies like Achilles himself.

Such fearful turmoil of men and horses did Jove on that day

Hector possesses Achilles' armour after Patroclus's death and vaunts himself in it. Menelaus tells Antilochus that Hector already has the armour, and Achilles cannot yet come to the fight because he lacks it.

BOOK XVIII.

Achilles’s armour, formerly given to Peleus by the gods, has been taken from Patroclus by Hector. Because the armour is in Trojan hands, Achilles cannot arm himself when Iris urges him to enter the battle.

BOOK XXII.

Achilles' armor gleams as he pursues Hector and is described as protecting him during the fight. Hector's spear strikes its shield but rebounds without piercing it.

the arms of Achilles in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

The arms of Achilles are made by Vulcan at Thetis’s request and belong to Achilles during his life. After Achilles dies, they become the prize in a dispute between Ajax, son of Telamon, and Ulysses. Ajax cites kinship and battlefield service; Ulysses cites discovering Achilles, carrying his body and arms, and aiding Troy’s conquest. Agamemnon leaves the decision to Argive leaders, who award the arms to Ulysses.

Achilles' armor in The Fall of Troy

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

Achilles' armor is divine, immortal battle-gear made by Hephaestus, including a shield, helmet, corslet, greaves, sword, and spear. Its gold-worked shield depicts earth, heaven, sea, and living creatures. After becoming the prize contested by Aias and Odysseus and awarded to Odysseus, it is promised to Achilles' son, Neoptolemus, whom it fits and protects in battle.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK V

Achilles' immortal arms include the shield, helmet, corslet, greaves, sword, and spear. The arms become the prize in a rivalry between Aias and Odysseus, and the Trojans award them to Odysseus.

BOOK VII

Achilles' armor is described as godlike battle-gear fashioned by Hephaestus, with gold worked over a shield depicting earth, heaven, sea, and living creatures. Odysseus promises to give it to Achilles' son when he comes to Troy.

So came they to the heavy-plunging sea,

Achilles' armor, made by Hephaestus, fits Neoptolemus closely even though it would have been oversized for another man. Neoptolemus wears the armor, helmet, and great Pelian spear in battle, and the equipment protects him from the many shafts thrown at him.
