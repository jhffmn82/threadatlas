  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, the Myrmidons are a race or group descended from ants or surviving subjects of Æacus, known for thrift, industry, and courage in war.
  • In The Fall of Troy, the Myrmidons are Achilles’s battle-cunning followers and mourners who preserve his funeral rites, support his son Neoptolemus, and aid the Greeks.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the Myrmidons are Achilles’s soldiers, originally an industrious and peaceful people who follow Cephalus to war and later signify zealous political followers.
  • In Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable, the ant-born people of Aegina are a diligent, acquisitive race who hail Æacus as king, become known as Myrmidons, and grow into bold soldiers.

Myrmidons in The Iliad

Homer · -0749/-0724

The Myrmidons are warriors, famed horsemen, and Achilles’s trusted companions, brought to Troy in fifty ships. Led by Achilles and, at key moments, by Patroclus, they arm in ordered companies, surge from the ships, attack the Trojans, and help drive them from the fleet. They fight beside the Achaeans over Sarpedon’s body, losing Epeigeus and carrying Sarpedon’s armour away. After Patroclus dies, they mourn with Achilles, tend his body, accompany him to the pyre, and follow Achilles’s funeral orders.

The Myrmidons arm themselves under Patroclus and form tightly ordered companies under their captains. They surge from the ships, attack the Trojans, and help drive them back from the fleet.

The Myrmidons fight alongside the Achaeans over Sarpedon’s body. Epeigeus, one of their best men, is killed by Hector, and Patroclus later gives Sarpedon’s armour to his men to carry to the ships.

BOOK XVIII.

The Myrmidons mourn Patroclus with Achilles throughout the night. They prepare a tripod, wash and anoint his body, close his wounds, and lay him on a bier.

BOOK XXIII.

The Myrmidons remain with Achilles instead of returning to their ships, drive their chariots around Patroclus's body, and mourn him. They cut their hair, accompany the body to the pyre, and carry out Achilles's orders during the funeral.

Myrmidons in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The Myrmidons are a race or group named from the Greek word for ants. In one account, severe pestilence leads to ants being transformed into men; in another, they are surviving subjects of Æacus, or a barbarous, industrious people of Thessaly brought by Æacus to his island. They are thrifty, patient of toil, tenacious in acquiring and storing goods, and receive Æacus’s city and lands. When the East wind turns south, they attend war with equal years and courage.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

The transformed ants are called Myrmidons, a name derived from the Greek name for those insects. They retain traits associated with their origin: they are thrifty, patient of toil, and tenacious of what they acquire and store.

notes .. notes

The Myrmidons are explained either as surviving subjects of Æacus or as a barbarous, industrious people of Thessaly whom Æacus brought to his island. Their name resembles the Greek word for ‘an ant,’ which likely inspired the story that Jupiter changed ants into men.

Quintus Smyrnaeus · 0200~/0299~

The Myrmidons are a battle-cunning Argive host, Achilles’ followers and mourners. They possess his immortal horses, mourn him continuously, conduct his funeral rites and preserve his bones, then rejoice in his son Neoptolemus, follow him as successor in battle, and are urged to aid the desperate Greeks.

Myrmidons in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The Myrmidons are Achilles’ soldiers in the Trojan War, originally an industrious, peaceful people who follow Cephalus to war after Aeacus offers aid; later, their name denotes zealous, unscrupulous political followers.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter VII

The Myrmidons are the soldiers of Achilles in the Trojan War, and their name later describes zealous and unscrupulous followers of a political chief. They are an industrious and peaceful people who follow Cephalus to war after Aeacus offers them as assistance.

ant-born people of Aegina in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

The ant-born people of Aegina emerge after Aeacus’s prayer to Jupiter, hail Aeacus as king, and are called Myrmidons; diligent and acquisitive like ants, they become young, bold soldiers.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter VII

The ant-born people of Aegina emerge after Aeacus's prayer to Jupiter and are named Myrmidons from the word for ant, myrmex. They resemble ants in being diligent, industrious, eager to gain, and tenacious of their gains, and they hail Aeacus as their king.
