Trojan camp

place · 3 works · 3 quoted facts · 6 connections
Also called: camp, lines, the Trojan camp, the camp, the far end of the camp, trenches
  • In Hecuba and other plays, the Trojan camp is threatened by Rhesus’s Thracian army, entered by Ulysses and Diomede at night, and occupied by Rhesus’s troops while its guards fail to prevent the raid.
  • In The Rhesus of Euripides, the Trojan camp lies near the Greeks, with watch-fires among sleeping and watching allies, and hosts night councils, Guards’ movements, Rhesus’s attack, and the discovery of disaster.
  • In The Iliad, the Trojan camp lies behind Dolon as he runs toward the ships, is the place near which Hector and the councillors confer, and is thrown into an uproar after Rhesus’s horses are gone and soldiers are dying.

NOTES

The Trojan camp lies on the battlefield close to the Greek lines, with Hector's tent or hut near the front and watch fires around the camp. It is the setting for the night councils, the movement of the Guards, the attack on Rhesus, and the discovery of the disaster.
