Themis in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

Themis is a sacred prophetic Goddess, daughter of Cœlus and Terra, whose Greek name means what is just and right. She gives sacred oracular responses, precedes Apollo at Delphi, has a temple and an ancient oracle in Bœotia, and instructs men to ask the Gods what is lawful. After Deucalion and Pyrrha reach Parnassus, they consult her neglected temple; moved by prayer, she gives the response renewing humanity.

Themis in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Themis is a prophetic goddess who prevents Hebe’s promise and foretells Theban conflict, several fates, and Calirrhoë’s future request for youth for her sons.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE IV. [IX.324-425]

Themis prevents Hebe from promising that she will never again grant renewed youth. She prophesies the Theban civil war, the fates of Capaneus, the two brothers, Amphiaraüs, and Alcmæon, and the future request of Calirrhoë for youth for her infant sons.
