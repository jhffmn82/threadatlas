Pythian Games in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

The Pythian Games are an event held at Delphi, at the temple and games of Pytho, and located in the rich cornlands of Pylades in Phokis. They confer crowns, glory, public honor, and joyful returns through athletic victories. Hieron wins a chariot-race as Aitna is proclaimed; Karrhotos wins another beside Kastalia; Aristomenes wins wrestling; Telesikrates wins an armed foot-race; and Hippokleas is linked to a crown and armored racing. Timodemidai have four victories, and the account ends by predicting Timodemos’s conquest there.

Narrative, section by section

6 sections; each heading opens the text

I.

At the Pythian Games, Hieron won the chariot-race. The herald proclaimed Aitna’s name aloud in connection with the victory.

V.

The Pythian games granted Arkesilas glory through his horses and supplied the subject of the triumphal song. At the games, Karrhotos won the chariot-race and received a crown beside the waters of Kastalia.

VIII.

The Pythian games award Aristomenes the victory in the wrestling-match. The Pythian feast grants him the glad return and welcomes him to his mother's side, while his defeated competitors lack that joy.

IX.

The Pythian games provided the setting for Telesikrates’s victory in the foot-race in full armour. His success there was celebrated as a public honour for Kyrene.

X.

The Pythian Games are associated with Hippokleas's crown at Pytho and with victories won beneath the cliff of Kirrha. The text also refers to a race in full armor at Pytho and to Hippokleas's father being twice victorious at Olympia in armor.

Nemean Ode II

The ode predicts that Timodemos will conquer in the Pythian games.

Pythian games in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The Pythian games are sacred contests, probably originally a religious ceremonial, named for Python and instituted by Phœbus as a memorial of Python’s death; other accounts credit Diomedes, Eurylochus the Thessalian, Amphictyon, or Adrastus. Celebrated near Delphi, they honor Apollo with a hymn accompanied by cithara. They may first have consisted only of singing, then included singing, dancing, instrumental music, boxing, running, and chariot-racing; gymnastic and equestrian exercises came later. Held every eight years before the 48th Olympiad and every four years afterward, they continued in the time of Emperor Julian. Prizes changed from beechen-leaf crowns to laurel chaplets and palm branches.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

opening

The Pythian games are sacred contests instituted by Apollo in memory of his defeat of Python. Young men compete in boxing, running, and chariot-racing, and the victors receive crowns of beechen leaves.

notes .. notes

The Pythian games were celebrated near Delphi and included musical contests, as well as singing, dancing, instrumental music, gymnastic exercises, and equestrian events. They were originally held every eighth year and later every fourth year; their victors first received crowns of beechen leaves and later laurel chaplets with palm branches.

Pythian games institution in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII

Ovid · 0008~

The Pythian games arose, by historical interpretation, from destruction of a robber near Parnassus; probably a religious ceremony or musical contest honoring the Pythian God, later adding gymnastic and equestrian competitions.

1 section; each heading opens the text

notes .. notes

The institution of the Pythian games is attributed in the explanation to the destruction of the serpent Python or the robber near Parnassus. The games were probably originally a religious ceremony or a musical contest honoring the Pythian God, though later accounts added gymnastic and equestrian competitions.

Pythian games in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The Pythian games began with singing a hymn in honour of Apollo and later included musical, athletic, chariot, and horse-racing contests. Over time, the games discontinued singing to the pipes, introduced contests such as the race in armour and the pancratium for boys, and awarded victors a laurel crown.

Pythian games in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Apollo instituted the Pythian games near Delphi to commemorate victory over Python; strength, foot-race, and chariot-race victors received beech wreaths before laurel became his tree.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

PYTHON

Apollo instituted the Pythian games to commemorate his victory over Python. Victors in feats of strength, foot races, or chariot races were crowned with wreaths of beech leaves because the laurel had not yet become Apollo's tree.
