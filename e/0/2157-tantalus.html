  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Tantalus is depicted under a winejar, undergoing Homer’s punishments while fearing that an overhanging stone will fall on him.

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Tantalus, rich renowned son of Zeus, ruled part of Asia later called Phrygia and fathered Pelops and Niobe; after revealing gods’ secrets to men, he was punished in life and eternally after death.

Tantalos in The Extant Odes of Pindar

Pindar · -0497

Tantalos, father of Pelops, hosted and was exceptionally honored by the gods at beloved Sipylos. His excess and wrongdoing ended that fortune: he stole nectar and ambrosia from the immortals for mortals, and the gods punished him with a great stone hanging above his head.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

OLYMPIAN ODES. / I.

Tantalos is said to have enjoyed exceptional honor from the guardians of Olympus but lost his fortune through excess and wrongdoing. He is punished with a great stone hanging above his head and is also condemned for stealing nectar and ambrosia from the immortals and giving them to mortals.

Tantalus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Tantalus is a king and Niobe’s father. He reveals divine mysteries to men and tries to share ambrosia, then is punished in Hades: standing in a lake beneath a hanging stone beside fruit trees. Another record calls him Thyestes’ son, Clytaemnestra’s former husband, and Agamemnon’s victim.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

II. Ὅτι ὁ Τάνταλος ἐν “Atdov? κολάζεται,

Tantalus is punished in Hades with a stone hanging over him, while he stands in a lake and sees fruit-bearing trees beside it. Some say he is punished because he revealed the gods’ mysteries to humans and tried to share ambrosia with his companions.

Tz 15 «Τὸν δ᾽ ᾿Αγαμέμνονα ὃ τροφὸς μετὰ τοῦ Μενελάου

Tantalus, son of Thyestes and the former husband of Clytaemnestra, was killed by Agamemnon together with his newborn child.
