Polypheme the Cyclops in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Polypheme is a one-eyed, savage Cyclops, son of Neptune, who rules and lives in the Cyclops’ caves on Mount Ætna with his flocks. He eats animals, strangers, and Ulysses’ companions, rejects hospitality and the gods’ authority, and values wealth and bodily pleasure. Armed with a club, he orders Ulysses and the strangers prepared as food, then feasts, drinks wine, and falls asleep. Ulysses and his companions blind him with a burning brand. Polypheme recognizes Ulysses, recalls the oracle, and threatens him and his crew.

Polyphemus in The Odyssey

Homer · -0632~/-0599~

Polyphemus is a huge, inhuman Cyclops, king of the Cyclopes, son of Neptune and Thoosa, and a lawless outlaw who lives alone in a cave, shepherding sheep and goats. Resembling a mountain crag and carrying a massive olive-wood club, he rejects gods, laws, social order, and hospitality, eating six of Ulysses’s men rather than welcoming them. Ulysses makes him drunk and blinds his single eye; because Ulysses calls himself “Noman,” Polyphemus cannot identify the escaping men. Furious, he hurls rocks at Ulysses’s ship, recognizes an old prophecy has been fulfilled, and prays to Neptune for vengeance and healing. His blinding keeps Neptune angry with Ulysses.

Polyphemus in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

Polyphemus is a huge, wild, man-eating Cyclops and giant, said to be the son of Poseidon and the nymph Thoosa. He has one eye on his forehead, lives in a giant’s cave, and has a thousand sheep. In the Homeric story, he eats some of Odysseus’s companions. Ulysses deceives him by calling himself “Nobody,” blinds him, and escapes; after learning Ulysses’s name, Polyphemus throws rocks at the departing ship. Accounts differ: the Dolopathos narrative calls its giant blear-eyed and has episodes unlike the Odyssey, while other discussions present Polyphemus as the comparable one-eyed cave giant. His story is repeatedly linked with Finnish, Karelian, Lithuanian, German, Yorkshire, Breton, Esthonian, Lapp, and Russian tales. He is treated as a fairyland being; Wilhelm Grimm interprets him as the sun, though another text rejects physical or moral interpretations.

Polyphemus in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Polyphemus is a one-eyed Cyclops, Neptune’s son, a Sicilian shepherd with a mountain cave and many herds. He loves Galatea, changes himself to please her, and boasts of his wealth and strength. Jealous when she loves Acis, he kills Acis with a mountain fragment. After devouring Ulysses’ companions and being blinded by Ulysses, he rages around Ætna, hurling rocks and threatening the escaping Greeks.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

FABLE VII. [XIII.719-897]

Polyphemus, one of the Cyclops, falls in love with Galatea and changes his appearance and behavior in an effort to please her. He boasts of his cave, flocks, wealth, and strength, but becomes enraged because Galatea loves Acis; he pursues Acis and kills him by hurling a fragment of a mountain.

FABLE IV. [XIV.154-247]

Polyphemus, the Cyclop, devoured Ulysses’ companions and was blinded by Ulysses. After the Greeks escaped, he raged around Ætna, hurled rocks, and threatened to devour or mutilate Ulysses or any of his companions.

Polyphemus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Polyphemus is a fierce one-eyed Cyclops, a giant, terror of the woods, and master of a cave. Passionately loving Galatea, he changes while pursuing her but kills Acis with a hurled rock after finding them together. Later he traps Ulysses and companions, eats several, is blinded by Ulysses, and hurls rocks and curses as they escape.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter XIX

Polyphemus was a fierce Cyclops who fell in love with Galatea and changed his behavior while pursuing her. When he saw Galatea with Acis, he threatened them, pursued Acis, and hurled a rock that overwhelmed and killed him.

Chapter XXII

Polyphemus traps Ulysses and his companions in his cave, kills and eats several of them, and promises that Noman will be eaten last. Ulysses blinds him with a burning stake and escapes beneath the flock, but then reveals his name, causing Polyphemus to hurl rocks at the vessel and curse him.
