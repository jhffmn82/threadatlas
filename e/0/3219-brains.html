brains in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1900-04

Brains are what the Scarecrow seeks from the Great Oz because he believes his straw-stuffed head leaves him foolish and unable to know things. He values brains above all else, encouraged by an old crow’s claim that they would make him as good as any man. Though the Tin Woodman doubts brains bring happiness, the Scarecrow continues his request. In the end, Oz fills his head with bran, pins and needles, and straw; the Scarecrow believes this makes him wise.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter III

The Scarecrow believes he has no brains because his head is stuffed with straw. He wants the Great Oz to give him brains so that people will not call him a fool and so that he can know things.

Chapter IV

The Scarecrow believes brains are the only things worth having and wants them because he feels like a fool without them. An old crow told him that brains would make him as good as any man and better than some.

Chapter V

Brains are what the Scarecrow wants the Great Oz to give him. The Tin Woodman believes that brains do not make a person happy and would rather have a heart.

Chapter VI

The Scarecrow wants Oz to give him brains because his head is stuffed with straw. He tells the Cowardly Lion that Oz could give him courage as easily as brains.

Chapter XVI

Oz makes the Scarecrow's brains from bran mixed with pins and needles, using straw to fill the remaining space. The Scarecrow believes the mixture has made him wise.
