  • In The Trojan Women of Euripides, Cassandra is assigned to Agamemnon as a bride despite her sacred status and lack of consent, and foretells that the marriage will bring death to his house.
  • In Hecuba and other plays, Cassandra's enslavement to Agamemnon is presented as her assignment as his concubine; she performs a mock bridal celebration and prophesies disaster for him and his house.

Cassandra's forced marriage in The Trojan Women of Euripides

Euripides · -0414

Cassandra is assigned to Agamemnon as a bride, despite her sacred status as Apollo's chosen priestess and her lack of consent. She treats the marriage as a wedding while foretelling that it will bring death to Agamemnon's house.
