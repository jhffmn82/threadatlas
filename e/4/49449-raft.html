raft in The Patchwork Girl of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1913-07-01

The raft is a log craft made of logs bound with the Quadling woman's clothesline and reinforced by wooden strips. Completed by evening, it begins carrying the travelers the next morning down the Trick River. Reversing currents impede it, but a fish, a pole, and holds on a tree and rock help it survive a steep wave and reach the Winkie Country safely.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

Chapter Twenty-Five

The raft is built from logs bound together with the Quadling woman's clothesline and reinforced with strips of wood nailed across the top. It is completed by evening, but the travelers do not begin using it until the following morning.

Chapter Twenty-Six

The raft carries the travelers down the Trick River but is repeatedly swept backward when the current reverses. A fish pulls it forward, and the travelers later hold it against a tree and a rock and guide it with a pole. It survives a steep wave of water, eventually reaches the Winkie Country, and lands safely near the Tin Woodman's castle.
