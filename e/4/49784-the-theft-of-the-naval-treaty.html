XI. The Naval Treaty

The treaty theft occurs while Percy Phelps is copying the document at the Foreign Office. After a bell rings from his room, he returns to find the original missing; he concludes that the thief entered through the side door, while Holmes notes the importance of the timing and the lack of footprints.

“We examined the room also. There is no possibility of a secret

The theft occurred while Percy Phelps was working alone on a special Foreign Office task. The police investigated Mrs. Tangey, her husband, and Gorot but found no evidence, so the matter remained unsolved until Phelps appealed to Holmes.

“Where are we going to now?” I asked, as we left the office.

The theft of the naval treaty remains unresolved. Sherlock Holmes considers that the thief may be waiting for a better price or may have been prevented from delivering the treaty, while Lord Holdhurst confirms that no one should have known in advance that it was to be copied.

It was seven o’clock when I awoke, and I set off at once for

Joseph Harrison stole the State papers from Percy Phelps’s office after entering through the Charles Street door and finding Phelps’s room empty. He recognized their value, concealed them at Briarbrae, and intended to retrieve and sell them, possibly to the French embassy.
