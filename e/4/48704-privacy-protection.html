privacy protection in Mitigating the Privacy Issues in Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) via Pure Synthetic Data

Shenglai Zeng, Jiankun Zhang, Pengfei He, Jie Ren, Tianqi Zheng, Hanqing Lu, Han Xu, Hui Liu, Yue Xing, Jiliang Tang · 2025

Privacy protection is a central goal of synthetic retrieval data generation for RAG systems. The approach seeks to reduce the privacy risks of retrieval-based language models while retaining performance comparable to original retrieval data. Privacy concerns can remain in initial synthetic data, including explicit personally identifiable information and details inferable or linkable to individuals. The work evaluates privacy through targeted and untargeted attacks that attempt to extract verbatim or closely similar retrieval content. Results indicate strong protection, especially after refinement. Protection increases with fewer attributes and agent-based refinement, with attribute counts selected through evaluation-set testing.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

Introduction

Privacy protection is the central concern of the proposed synthetic-data approach for RAG. The unit reports that using synthetic data as retrieval data can substantially reduce privacy risks while maintaining performance comparable to using original data.

Related Works

Privacy issues arise in retrieval-based language models and RAG systems. Huang et al. (2023) identified privacy leakage in KNN-LM, while Zeng et al. (2024) and Qi et al. (2024) showed that RAG systems, including production models, are vulnerable to extraction and other attacks.

Methods

Privacy concerns may remain in Stage-1 synthetic data because no privacy controls are initially applied. They include explicit personally identifiable information and personal details that can be inferred or linked to specific individuals.

Privacy of using synthetic data

Privacy is assessed by measuring whether attacks can extract verbatim or closely similar content from retrieval data. The results indicate that the synthetic data, especially after refinement, has strong privacy protection.

Ablation Studies

Privacy protection is stronger when fewer attributes are used or when data undergoes agent-based refinement. The study recommends selecting an appropriate attribute count for each dataset through evaluation-set testing.
