He ruled with Moo, described variously as his sibling and co-ruler and as his wife, sister, and consort. His identity was marked by a shield painted with round green spots, a spotted-tiger totem, leopard representations, and a leopard-skin shield. His statue, found in a buried monument, holds an empty vase; workers believed he would return to inhabit it. Accounts of the statue and mausoleum describe filed teeth, paintings, bas-reliefs, urns, jade ornaments, and other objects. The records interpret the urn contents as his brains, heart, and viscera, the carvings as evidence of enemies' hearts being devoured, and blood near the statue as possible evidence of funeral sacrifices. Chaacmol was allegedly killed by Aac, his brother, with three spear thrusts in the back out of jealousy. He was buried in a mausoleum at Chichen-Itza, which the author says he opened, identifying Chaacmol's effigy and name and finding his heart and jade ornaments.
