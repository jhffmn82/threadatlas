CLIP in Wiki-LLaVA: Hierarchical Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Multimodal LLMs

Davide Caffagni · 2024

CLIP is a pre-trained visual model used in a hierarchical retrieval pipeline. With the CLIP ViT-L/14@336 backbone, it encodes images and text to compute image–title similarity, retrieve target entities from query images, and provide LLaVA’s visual encoder. It retrieves more successfully from the 100k-entry InfoSeek knowledge base than the 2M-entry Encyclopedic-VQA knowledge base; errors can yield irrelevant passages.

2. Related Work

CLIP is used in the hierarchical retrieval scheme to help identify the target entity from the query image. In Wiki-LLaVA, it forms part of the retrieval pipeline together with Contriever.

3. Proposed Method .. 3.2. Training

CLIP-based visual and textual encoders produce embeddings used to compute image–title similarity for the first retrieval stage. LLaVA uses a pre-trained CLIP-based visual encoder.

4. Experiments / 4.1. Datasets .. 4.5. Limitations and Future Works

CLIP ViT-L/14@336 is used both as LLaVA's visual encoder and to extract image features for entity retrieval. Its retrieval recall is substantially higher on the 100k-entry InfoSeek knowledge base than on the 2M-entry Encyclopedic-VQA knowledge base, and retrieval errors can supply irrelevant passages to the MLLM.

CLIP in Ask in Any Modality

Mohammad Mahdi Abootorabi, Amirhosein Zobeiri, Mahdi Dehghani, Mohammadali Mohammadkhani, Bardia Mohammadi, Omid Ghahroodi, Mahdieh Soleymani Baghshah, Ehsaneddin Asgari · 2025

CLIP is a multimodal encoder that aligns visual and textual modalities through contrastive learning, embedding images and queries in a shared space. Early methods faced retrieval-precision and computational-cost trade-offs; later it supports retrieval and score fusion in named approaches.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

3.2

CLIP is used to embed images and queries into a shared space in Wiki-LLaVA, C3Net, Riedler and Langer, and MegaPairs. It also contributes to score fusion in the interleaved image-text approach of Sharifymoghaddam et al. (2024), while MegaPairs combines CLIP-based retrieval with MLLM-based retrieval.
