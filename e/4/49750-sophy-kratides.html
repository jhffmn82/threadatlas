Sophy Kratides in The Memoirs of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1892~/1893~

Sophy Kratides is a wealthy Greek lady, Paul Kratides’s sister, who speaks Greek and visits England as a young woman. There she becomes involved with Harold Latimer, who persuades her to flee with him. Unaware that Latimer and Wilson Kemp are holding Paul prisoner at The Myrtles, she sees him during an interrogation, recognizes him despite his plaster disguise, and embraces him after he tears it away. Harold removes her, and she later flees with the two men. Holmes suspects that she avenges the two Englishmen’s murder.

X. The Greek Interpreter

Sophy is a tall, graceful woman with black hair who enters the room during Kratides’s interrogation. She recognizes Kratides as Paul, and he tears away the plaster and rushes into her arms before Harold forcibly removes her.

Sophy Kratides is a wealthy Greek family's daughter who came to England to visit friends and was persuaded by Harold Latimer to flee with him. Latimer and Wilson Kemp keep her brother prisoner without her knowledge, but she recognizes him through the plaster disguise when she sees him during Mr. Melas's visit. She flees with the two men, and Sherlock Holmes suspects that she later avenged herself and Paul.
