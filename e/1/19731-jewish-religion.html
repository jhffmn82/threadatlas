religion of the Atlas Jews in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

The religion of the Atlas Jews differs from Jewish religion in the Pentateuch's order and law, resembles Caraaites, names the Supreme Being Shadai, lacks Ezra and Christ, and has a written law omitting the deluge and recounting Ishmael.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER I.

The religion of the Atlas Jews is described as differing from that of Jews generally: they are said to resemble Caraaites, to be unacquainted with Ezra or Christ, and to call the Supreme Being Shadai. Their written law is said to omit the deluge, describe distinct human species, and recount God's appearance to Ishmael.
