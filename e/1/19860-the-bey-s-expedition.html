the Bey's expedition in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

The Bey's expedition, with mounted guards, Turkish Infantry, artillery, followers, animals, and attendants, passes through Manouba and travels south through the Jereed, repeatedly breaking camp, marching, saluting, hunting, and encamping.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IX.

The Bey's expedition traveled southward through the Jereed with mounted guards, Turkish Infantry, artillery, camp followers, baggage animals, and attendants. It followed a set routine of striking camp before daylight, marching, saluting successive groups, hunting, and encamping at places such as Beereen, Tfeefleeah, Heelet-el-Gazlen, Ben Sayden, and Jebanah.
