Sappho in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Sappho is a slight, dark-complexioned Lesbian woman, Pelasgida, poet, singer, and vates, renowned across every land and associated with a lyre and great poetic genius. She loves Lesbian women and passionately loves Phaon. In his absence, grief impedes her art until she feels bereft of genius. Writing to him, she recalls intimacy, neglect, family hardships, and her daughter, begs him to return, and concludes that, if he will not, she will seek escape from passion by leaping from the Leucadian cliff into death.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

XV

Sappho writes to Phaon in grief over his absence and says that she cannot stop loving him. She recalls their former intimacy, describes her disordered and neglected appearance, and blames her continuing troubles on her love, her family hardships, and her daughter. After seeing a Naiad, she decides to go to the Leucadian cliff and leap from it to escape her passion.

Cur tamen Actiacas miseram me mittis ad oras, 185

Sappho asks Phaon why he sends her to Actium instead of returning himself. She says that Phaon's presence would help her more than the Leucadian waters, and she asks him to return.

Sappho in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Sappho, an early Greek poetess whose surviving fragments establish her genius, passionately loves Phaon without return and is associated with a leap from Leucadia.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XVIII

Sappho was a poetess of a very early period of Greek literature, and the surviving fragments of her work establish her poetical genius. She is commonly associated with an unreturned love for Phaon and a leap from Leucadia.
