Toser in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

Toser is a town in the Jereed, identified with ancient Tisurus, with about six thousand people and surrounding villages behind a mud wall. Though poor in appearance, it has mosques, schools, baths, inns, markets, and brick and mud dwellings often built from Roman ruins. Its greatest wealth is its abundant watered date gardens and finest-quality dates, alongside household weaving of burnouses. Its wells are insufficient, so it relies on Wad Meshra. Travelers calculated it at 200 miles from Ghabs; its dates were later taken as tribute.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER X.

Toser, identified with the ancient Tisurus, is described as a town of about six thousand people, surrounded by villages and a mud wall. It contains mosques, schools, baths, inns, markets, detached brick houses, date gardens, and numerous inhabitants engaged in weaving burnouses. The travelers find it poor in appearance but rich in gardens, dates, and some wealthy residents.

On our march back to Tunis, we encamped for two days by the foot of a range of h

Santa Maria calculated the distance from Ghabs to Toser at 200 miles. The party later marched from Byrlafee, about ninety-one miles from Toser, and dates collected at Toser formed part of the tribute.
