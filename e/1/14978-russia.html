Russia in Dr. Elsie Inglis

Lady Frances Balfour · 1918

Russia is a war-torn place and the route, field of service, and eventual danger zone for Dr. Inglis, Elsie, and a Scottish Women’s Hospitals unit serving alongside the Serbian army. Before the unit arrived to assist the Jugoslav Division, also described as the Serbian Division in Southern Russia, that force depended on Russian equipment and medical help. The unit traveled through Archangel and Moscow toward Odessa, receiving trains, food, repairs, transport, and other aid from Russian officials and civilians. Elsie traveled toward Russia, sought a copy of the Euchologion there, and worked under retreat conditions demanding rest and self-command. Russia is also a major war area marked by the Serbian Division’s battle, retreats, bombardments, disease, and uncertainty. Its people are portrayed as religious, interested in England, and hopeful after the war, but the Russian Revolution and a wider upheaval changed soldiers’ attitudes and made political and military conditions unstable. As the service ended, Inglis sought removal of the Serbian Army from the Russian front, while the Foreign Office feared for the unit’s safety amid the chaotic sufferings of the Russian people and evacuation.
