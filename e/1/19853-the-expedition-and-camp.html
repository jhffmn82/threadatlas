the expedition and camp in Travels in Morocco

James Richardson · 1860

The Bey’s camp, used for tribute collection, comprised about 2,660 people: Mamelukes, 300 Turkish Infantry, Spahis, roughly 2,000 Arab camp followers, attendants, two artillery pieces, and about 2,000 camels. Tents surrounded the Bey’s central tent, with water supplied from Tunis. The expedition marched ten to twenty miles at a time through varied country, encamping near several places and gaining Arabs. Along the way members hunted, bathed, inspected ruins and houses, and observed local natural features.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER IX.

The Bey's camp contained about 2,660 men, including Mamelukes, Turkish Infantry, Spahis, and Arab camp followers, as well as two artillery pieces. Its tents were arranged around the Bey's central tent, and its attendants included guards of the pipe, gun, coffee, shoes, and water, together with a story-teller and a supply of water brought from Tunis.

Marched to a place called Gilma, near which the road passes through an ancient t

The expedition marched between roughly ten and twenty miles at a time through plains, hills, and salt-covered country, encamping near Wady Tuckah, Ghafsa, Ghorbatah, Furfouwy, and Dra-el-Hammah. The camp traveled with about two thousand camels and was joined by increasing numbers of Arabs. Its members hunted, bathed in a shallow stream, inspected ruins and local houses, and observed animals, birds, locusts, springs, and desert vegetation.
