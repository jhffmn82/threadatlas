Hecuba's revenge in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Hecuba’s revenge is her planned and completed vengeance for Polydore. With captive Trojan women, and with Agamemnon’s consent to help or suppress opposition subject to the Greek army’s approval, she lures Polymestor to her. The women murder Polymestor’s children, and Hecuba blinds him with clasps; she declares that he will never see again or see his children alive and rejoices in her vengeance.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

HECUBA.

This unit does not describe Hecuba’s revenge, but the argument states that she will later kill Polymestor’s sons and blind Polymestor after luring him to her.

FEMALE ATTENDANT, CHORUS, HECUBA.

Hecuba plans to avenge Polydore with the captive Trojan women. Agamemnon consents to assist or suppress opposition, provided that his actions do not appear to be a private attack on the Thracian king.

POLYMESTOR, HECUBA, CHORUS.

Hecuba's vengeance consists of the murder of Polymestor's children and the blinding of Polymestor. She tells him that he will never regain his sight or see his children alive, and she rejoices that she has avenged herself.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLES III. AND IV. [XIII.439-622]

Hecuba's revenge begins when she discovers that Polymnestor has murdered Polydorus. She falsely promises him a hidden treasure, then attacks him with the captive matrons and tears out his eyes.
