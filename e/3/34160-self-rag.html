SELF-RAG in SELF-RAG: Learning to Retrieve, Generate, and Critique through Self-Reflection

Akari Asai, Zeqiu Wu, Yizhong Wang, Avirup Sil, Hannaneh Hajishirzi · 2023-10-17

SELF-RAG, also called Self-Reflective Retrieval-Augmented Generation, is a retrieval-augmented generation framework that improves language-model quality, factuality, factual accuracy, and citation accuracy through on-demand retrieval and self-reflection. It trains a single arbitrary large language model end to end to retrieve when useful, generate text, evaluate retrieved passages, critique its own generations, choose better outputs, and provide citations for each generated segment. Its central mechanism is reflection tokens: intermittent special tokens generated during training and generation. These tokens signal whether continued generation would benefit from retrieval, guide the model in assessing passage relevance, support, and utility, and enable critique of generated text. A critic model inserts reflection tokens offline into the original corpus for training. The resulting generator model learns to retrieve, generate, and critique rather than relying on retrieval for every query; it can selectively use retrieved passages and filter irrelevant ones while processing passages in parallel. At inference time, SELF-RAG first determines whether retrieval would help. After it outputs a retrieval token, it calls a retriever model on demand, evaluates retrieved passages in parallel, and continues generation with reflection-guided assessment. It supports adaptive retrieval, critique-based ranking, hard or soft controls, and customizable decoding. Segment-level beam search can use critique-type weighting and ISSUP; reported settings include beam width 2, greedy token-level decoding, vllm for faster inference, a retrieval threshold of 0.2 for most tasks, and 0 for ALCE because of citation requirements. Across diverse tasks, including open-domain question answering, reasoning, fact verification, long-form factuality, and citation accuracy, 7B and 13B SELF-RAG models outperform evaluated baselines in many results. They outperform Llama2-chat and Alpaca on all evaluated tasks, outperform retrieval-augmented ChatGPT on four tasks, and substantially exceed supervised fine-tuned models without retrieval. SELF-RAG significantly improves long-form factuality and citation accuracy relative to ChatGPT and retrieval-augmented Llama2-chat, while improving factual accuracy without hurting versatility. Its training cost is reported as lower than RLHF, and its performance benefits from training scale, adaptive retrieval, and ISSUP-guided beam search.

ABSTRACT

SELF-RAG is a framework that improves language-model quality and factuality through retrieval and self-reflection. It trains a single arbitrary language model to retrieve passages on demand and to generate and reflect on retrieved passages and its own generations using reflection tokens. SELF-RAG models with 7B and 13B parameters outperform state-of-the-art language models and retrieval-augmented models across diverse tasks, including open-domain question answering, reasoning, fact verification, long-form factuality, and citation accuracy.

INTRODUCTION

SELF-RAG is introduced to improve generation quality and factual accuracy through on-demand retrieval and self-reflection. It learns to decide whether retrieval is useful, evaluate retrieved passages, critique generated text, select better outputs, and provide citations.

RELATED WORK

SELF-RAG trains an arbitrary language model to use retrieval on demand for diverse instruction-following queries and to select the best possible model output through fine-grained self-reflection. It processes passages in parallel, filters irrelevant passages through self-reflection, evaluates output qualities including factuality, and supports customizable inference with reflection tokens.

SELF-RAG: LEARNING TO RETRIEVE, GENERATE AND CRITIQUE / 3.1 / PROBLEM FORMALIZATION AND OVERVIEW .. 3.3 / SELF-RAG INFERENCE

SELF-RAG enables an LLM to retrieve passages when useful, generate text, and assess relevance, support, and utility with reflection tokens. It supports adaptive retrieval, critique-based ranking, and hard or soft controls during inference.

EXPERIMENTS / 4.1 / TASKS AND DATASETS .. 4.3 / EXPERIMENTAL SETTINGS

SELF-RAG is evaluated against diverse proprietary and non-proprietary baselines across six tasks. Its 7B and 13B versions achieve strong overall results, including the best non-proprietary results on several reported metrics.

RESULTS AND ANALYSIS / 5.1 / MAIN RESULTS .. 5.2 / ANALYSIS

SELF-RAG substantially outperforms supervised fine-tuned language models without retrieval and achieves the best performance among non-proprietary language-model-based systems across the evaluated tasks. It learns to retrieve, generate, and critique passages and its own generations using reflection tokens, and its performance benefits from training scale, adaptive retrieval, and ISSUP-guided beam search.
