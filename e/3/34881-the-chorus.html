Chorus of Village Guardians in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

The Chorus of Village Guardians of Colonos, old and weak, protects Oedipus and supports Athens in prayer. It first searches for him in the sacred grove and orders him away, then pities him and defers to Theseus. It praises Colonus and Athens, confronts Creon over Antigone, anticipates battle, celebrates the daughters’ return, reflects on suffering and death, and pities Polynices after his curse.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

OEDIPUS AT COLONOS

The Chorus of Village Guardians searches for Oedipus after he enters the sacred grove and orders him to leave the forbidden ground. After hearing his identity and Antigone's plea, it expresses pity but defers judgment to Theseus.

_Enter_ THESEUS.

The Chorus praises Colonus and Athens, welcomes Oedipus's promised protection of the land, and initially supports Theseus's decision. It confronts Creon, orders him to release Antigone, and calls for the ruler of the land when Creon threatens further violence.

CHORUS.

The Chorus anticipates a battle between the forces of Theseus and the opposing army, praises Athena's champions, and celebrates the return of Oedipus's daughters. It later reflects that prolonged life brings suffering and that death ultimately ends the struggle, then pities Polynices after Oedipus curses him.

the Chorus in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

The Chorus is a group of Athenian citizens that compassionately guides Oedipus and his daughters. It advises rites for the grove and questions his past, then reads thunder as divine signs, prays for his peaceful passage, and comforts and protects Antigone and Ismene after his disappearance.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

_Enter_ ISMENE.

The Chorus expresses compassion for Oedipus and his daughters, advises him to propitiate the holy powers of the grove, and gives detailed instructions for the offering. It also questions Oedipus about his incest and the killing of his father.

CHORUS.

The Chorus interprets the thunder and lightning as divine signs and prays to Zeus and the powers of the Underworld for Oedipus's peaceful passage. After his disappearance, it urges Antigone and Ismene to stop mourning and assures them that they are protected in Athens.

the Chorus in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912

The Chorus is a body of citizens of Colonus and Athens, representing the Elders and defending local law. It reverences the gods, guards the sacred grove, and initially searches for, fears, and orders the stranger Oedipus to leave. After hearing his appeal, it pities him and his daughters, counsels atonement through offerings, and supports him as a wronged suppliant. It presses him to disclose his suffering and recognizes the tangled relation of his daughters to him. When Creon seizes the maidens, it denounces him, calls the people to defend the State, and supports Theseus’s intervention. Finally, it learns of Oedipus’s death, comforts Antigone and Ismene, and points them toward acceptance, refuge, and safety.

Narrative, section by section

5 sections; each heading opens the text

OEDIPUS AT COLONUS

The Chorus, composed of citizens of Colonus, searches for the stranger who has entered the sacred grove. After learning that he is Oedipus, the Chorus is horrified and orders him and Antigone to leave, but it listens to his appeal and agrees to await the decision of the ruling powers.

[Enter ISMENE]

The Chorus pities Oedipus and his daughters and advises him to make atonement to the deities whose grove he profaned. It gives detailed instructions for the offerings and promises to support Oedipus if he follows them.

CHORUS.

The Chorus presses Oedipus to reveal the truth about his suffering, his marriage, and the blood he has shed. It identifies his daughters as both his offspring and his sisters, and announces the arrival of Theseus.

[Enter CREON with attendants]

The Chorus of Colonus rejects Creon's seizure of the maidens, calls him a wrongdoer, and threatens to fight if he does not release them. It calls the people to rally and defend the State, then supports Oedipus and welcomes Theseus's intervention.

[Enter MESSENGER]

The Chorus questions the Messenger about Oedipus's death and later comforts Antigone and Ismene. It urges them to accept their fate and assures them that refuge and safety have been found.
