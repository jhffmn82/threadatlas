Chorus in Alcestis

Euripides · -0437

Chorus is a chorus of citizen elders of Pherae and friends of Admetus’s household. Outside Admetus’s castle, it fears and mourns Alcestis’s death, wishes but cannot save her, and praises her as a brave, true wife who died for her lover. It imagines lasting fame for her, condemns Admetus’s parents’ failure, and, after consoling Admetus, insists that Fate is irresistible and grief must be endured.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

ALCESTIS

The Chorus waits outside the Castle of Admetus, fears Alcestis's death, and praises her as the bravest and truest of wives. It prays for help, mourns her death, and urges Admetus to endure the grief that follows.

CHORUS.

The chorus praises the Daughter of Pelias and imagines that the dead, minstrels, and singers will proclaim her fame. Its leaders also grieve that they cannot reach or save her, while the elders honor her sacrifice and condemn the failure of her husband’s parents.

Chorus of Pherœans in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

The Chorus of Pherœans is a collective of mourners for Alcestis. Waiting outside Admetus's palace, it seeks news of whether she lives, laments her approaching death, and praises her virtue and sacrifice. It consoles Admetus about death, condemns his parents' failure to die for him, and commends his hospitality toward Hercules.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

ALCESTIS.

The Chorus of Pherœans waits outside Admetus's palace and tries to learn whether Alcestis is alive or dead. It laments her coming death, praises her as the best of women, and fears that Admetus's life will no longer be worth living after her loss.

CHOR. And I indeed with thee, as a friend with a friend, will bear this

The Chorus consoles Admetus, saying that death is a debt all mortals must pay, and mourns Alcestis as an exceptional wife. It praises her sacrifice, condemns the failure of Admetus's parents to die for him, and praises Admetus's hospitality toward Hercules.
