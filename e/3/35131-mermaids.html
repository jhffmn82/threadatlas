mermaids in The Sea Fairies

L. Frank Baum · 1911

Mermaids are fairies and sea people: a powerful underwater society living deep in the Pacific Ocean, in their own kingdom, palaces, and Queen Aquareine's palace. They have lady-like arms, long soft hair, scales and tails, and wear transparent or gauzy pearl-decorated gowns. Although they live in water without gills, water does not touch them or their gowns; they use air to keep belongings dry. They are beautiful, merry, playful, sweet-tempered, long-lived, and reportedly immortal, with none of their race ever destroyed. They sing, play harps, dance while swimming, travel faster than sea horses, warn fish about hooks, and possess fairy magic that can protect or transform visitors. Cap'n Bill describes them as singing, smiling, winking, and laughing when mortals drown after being charmed, yet the records chiefly follow their friendship with Trot. They invite Trot and Cap'n Bill from Giant's Cave to visit their home, protect them through ocean travel, host them with food, music, and entertainment, and show them the palaces. Ruled by Aquareine, they later face Zog after he captures their friend King Anko; they are held in the Rose Chamber, while Aquareine considers opposing him. They remain trusted protectors of Trot, regret exposing earth people to ocean dangers, and finally carry Trot and Cap'n Bill swiftly back to Giant's Cave after restoring their usual forms.

Narrative, section by section

16 sections; each heading opens the text

[Illustration: TROT AND CAP'N BILL

Mermaids are described by Cap'n Bill as fairies that live deep in the water. They are said to sing sweet songs, smile and wink at people, and laugh when people drown.

[Illustration: _Chap 2._

The mermaids appear in Giant's Cave as beautiful, friendly water fairies. They say that they love little girls, can use magic to protect or transform visitors, and invite Trot and Cap'n Bill to see their underwater home.

[Illustration: _Chap. 3._

The mermaids travel with Trot and Cap'n Bill through the ocean and protect the mortal travelers from harm. They wear flowing, transparent gowns decorated with pearls, and their hair floats freely around them. They are fairies who cannot be injured by anything in the ocean and are portrayed as joyful, playful, and long-lived.

[Illustration: The PALACE OF QUEEN AQUARINE

The mermaids accompany Trot and Cap'n Bill through the ocean, laugh at Trot's observation that they must be breathing by magic, and escort the visitors to the palaces. They are described as beautiful, jeweled, and dressed in gauzy robes, and they rest on coral couches because of their tails.

[Illustration: _Chap. 6._

The mermaids host Trot and Cap'n Bill, question them about human life, and share sea fruit and a formal dinner with them. They recline on couches while eating, and their palaces have separate tables for other mermaids. They use sunlight or hot springs for cooking and employ lobsters as slaves to serve meals.

[Illustration: _Chap. 7._

Mermaids have warned the fish that hooks bring death rather than happiness, but the fish do not heed the warnings. The mackerel regard Merla as someone who can explain what happens in the sea.

[Illustration: _Chap. 8._

The mermaids guide Trot to her room, help her dress and arrange her hair, serve the guests, and entertain them with harp music, songs, and swimming-dances. They are described as fresh, lovely, sweet-tempered, and merry.

[Illustration: _Chap. 9._

The mermaids travel through the sea with Trot and Cap'n Bill. Butterfly fish greet them, and the octopus addresses them as neighbors.

[Illustration: _Chap. 10._

The mermaids travel with Trot and Cap'n Bill near the undiscovered island. The seals can swim more quickly than sea horses, but the sea horses cannot swim as the mermaids do.

[Illustration: _Chap. 12._

The mermaids possess fairy powers and have a queen named Aquareine. Aquareine says that the mermaids have never before measured their strength against Zog, though she believes she may be able to defeat him if King Anko could do so.

[Illustration: _Chap. 13._

The mermaids are friends of King Anko whom Zog has captured for revenge. They claim that none of their race has ever died or been destroyed, and Zog intends to experiment on them.

[Illustration: _Chap. 14._

The mermaids occupy the Rose Chamber, which contains golden rose decorations and couches made with mother-of-pearl frames and soft white sponges. They examine and admire the room while staying as prisoners in Zog's castle.

[Illustration: _Chap. 15._

Queen Aquareine identifies herself as the ruler of the mermaids, and Princess Clia is identified as a mermaid princess. The mermaids keep their belongings dry by surrounding them with air, unlike those living under the water in Zog's castle.

[Illustration: _Chap. 16._

The fairy mermaids are trusted by Trot to protect the group from danger.

[Illustration: _Chap. 20._

The mermaids are described as faithful friends who protected and helped Trot and Cap'n Bill during their dangers. Aquareine and Clia regret only that inviting earth people to visit the ocean exposed them to dangers unsuitable for their nature.

[Illustration: _Chap. 22._

The mermaids carry Trot and Cap’n Bill swiftly across the ocean and bring them to the Giant’s Cave. They enjoy Trot’s visit and leave after helping her and Cap’n Bill return to their usual forms.
