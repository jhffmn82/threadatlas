children

group · 2 works · 2 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: Phaedra’s children, Phædra's children, children (Phædra's children), my babes, my children, orphans, the babes I bare, the young, thy children
  • In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, Phaedra’s children are destined to live in Athens as free and honourable men; she fears her disgrace would shame them and makes their future reputation a reason for choosing death.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Phædra's children are her children and heirs of their paternal house, whose share in the paternal mansion is threatened if she dies; Phædra loves them and fears her disgrace would damage their future and reputation.
