Orestes' trial and sentence in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Orestes' trial and sentence is the Argive city's decree against Orestes and Electra for killing Clytemnestra. Orestes anticipates a stoning sentence, urged by Tyndarus. The citizens condemn both to death; Orestes forestalls immediate stoning by promising suicide, though they seek safety through a plot against Helen and Hermione's seizure.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

MENELAUS, ORESTES, CHORUS.

Orestes says that the city will decide his case that day. He expects the Argives to sentence him to death by stoning, while Tyndarus plans to encourage the assembled multitude to impose that sentence on Orestes and Electra.

PYLADES, ORESTES, CHORUS.

The Argive citizens impose a death sentence on Orestes and Electra. Orestes avoids being immediately stoned by promising that he and Electra will end their lives that day.

ELECTRA, ORESTES, PYLADES, CHORUS.

The land has decreed that Orestes and Electra die. They believe death is unavoidable unless they can obtain safety through their plot against Helen and their seizure of Hermione.
