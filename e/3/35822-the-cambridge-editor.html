The Cambridge editor in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

The Cambridge editor is a textual critic of Iphigenia in Aulis and Iphigenia in Tauris. The editor’s central work is proposing and supporting emendations, omissions, rearrangements, restorations, and explanations of difficult passages. The editor strongly condemns the final scene of Iphigenia in Aulis, suspects interpolations, and is often praised for judgment, taste, and choral treatment despite some rejected suggestions.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

notes

The Cambridge editor supplies or supports many textual emendations, including readings involving dispatches, place names, grammatical forms, and lines restored from other sources. The notes repeatedly cite this editor’s explanations, while also reporting that the editor condemns or suspects interpolation in numerous passages, especially the final scene.

notes

The Cambridge editor repeatedly proposes readings, omissions, rearrangements, and explanations for difficult or corrupt passages. The notes praise the editor’s judgment, taste, and treatment of the choruses, while sometimes declining to follow particular suggestions.
