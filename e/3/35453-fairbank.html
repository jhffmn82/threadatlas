Fairbank in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

Fairbank is Mr. Holder’s modest white-stone residence in Streatham, with a carriage-sweep, paths, stable lane, garden, dressing-room, and hall window. Alexander Holder brings the coronet from the bank and locks it in the dressing-room bureau overnight as household members and servants move through the house. Mary takes it and hands it through the hall window; Holmes examines the house and surroundings.

XI. THE ADVENTURE OF THE BERYL CORONET

Holder's house at Streatham contains the coronet overnight after Alexander carries it there from the bank. Alexander locks it in his dressing-room bureau, while members of the household and servants remain in or move through the house.

“Do you receive much company?” he asked.

Fairbank is Mr. Holder’s modest white-stone residence in Streatham. It has a front carriage-sweep, a tradesmen’s path, a stable lane, a garden, and the dressing-room where the coronet was kept.

“For Heaven’s sake, tell me, then, what is this extraordinary mystery!”

Holder's house contains the dressing-room from which Mary takes the coronet and the hall window through which it is handed outside. Holmes examines the house and its surroundings for evidence of the theft.
