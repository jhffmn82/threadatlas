Oedipus's exile in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

Oedipus's exile is his permanent banishment from Thebes after his crisis. Though he first requests immediate exile and Creon says the god must be consulted, Creon later orders his expulsion with the consent of Oedipus's sons. Oedipus says the State had earlier denied him death and Creon had refused banishment, but then drove him from his native land. Abandoned by his sons, he wanders homeless with Antigone and Ismene until reaching Colonos.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS.

Oedipus requests immediate exile from Thebes and asks to range the mountain where he was exposed. Creon says that exile cannot be granted until the god has been consulted.

OEDIPUS AT COLONOS

Oedipus's banishment from Thebes is attributed to the command of Creon, with the consent of Oedipus's sons. The banishment leads Oedipus to wander until he reaches Colonos.

_Enter_ ISMENE.

Oedipus says that when he was first suffering, the State did not grant his wish for death but later drove him permanently from his native land. His sons failed to help him, while Antigone and Ismene supported him in homeless banishment and beggary.

_Enter_ THESEUS.

Oedipus says that he was expelled from his native land by his own offspring and that return is barred to him as the man who slew his sire. He recalls that Creon once refused his request for banishment but later expelled him when Oedipus wished to remain at home.

Oedipus's exile in Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone

Sophocles · 1912

Oedipus’s exile is a punishment and forced separation from Thebes, first feared as the fulfillment of prophecy, then sought by Oedipus himself, and ultimately imposed by his own family and city. Teiresias foretells that the curse of Oedipus’s parents will drive him beyond Thebes to a strange land. Oedipus considers banishment a consequence of guilt and repeatedly asks to be sent away, though Creon initially refuses pending consultation with the gods. After Oedipus’s fall, Thebes enforces his expulsion; he says his sons and Polyneices did nothing or drove him from his sovereignty, while his daughters support him. He wanders for years as a beggar with Antigone, aided also by Ismene, until reaching Colonus. There he seeks final refuge, rejects Creon’s later call to return to Thebes, and remains under Theseus’s protection.

Narrative, section by section

8 sections; each heading opens the text

CHORUS.

Teiresias prophesies that the curse of Oedipus's mother and father will drive him beyond the borders. He says Oedipus will grope his way toward a strange land.

CREON.

Oedipus considers exile one possible consequence if he is guilty of Laius’s murder and the other crimes foretold by the prophecy. He imagines being banished from his native land and separated from those he loves.

[Enter SECOND MESSENGER.]

Oedipus repeatedly asks to be sent away from Thebes and to live where no mortal voice can reach him. Creon does not immediately grant the request, saying that the gods must be consulted.

OEDIPUS AT COLONUS

Oedipus has been banished from Thebes and has wandered for many years with Antigone. His wandering brings him to Colonus, where he seeks a final refuge and protection.

[Enter ISMENE]

Oedipus recalls that his sons did nothing when he was forcibly expelled and left to wander as a beggar. His daughters instead provided him with food, shelter, guidance, and filial care.

[Enter THESEUS]

Oedipus says that his own flesh and blood expelled him from his country and that he can never return there as a parricide. Theseus treats his continued banishment as acceptable and offers him a secure place in the land under his protection.

[Enter CREON with attendants]

Oedipus recalls that he once sought exile as a release, but says that Creon refused him then and later expelled him from his country. Creon now urges him to return to Thebes, while Oedipus chooses to remain where he is under the protection of Athens.

[Enter ANTIGONE and ISMENE with THESEUS]

Oedipus recalls that Polyneices drove him from his sovereignty and made him an exile without a city. He says that he has since lived in a foreign land begging for bread, aided by Antigone and Ismene.
