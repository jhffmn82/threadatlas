the alleged theft of the Beryl Coronet in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

The alleged theft of the Beryl Coronet begins when Alexander Holder finds Arthur Holder beside the damaged coronet at night and accuses him of taking three jewels. Arthur denies stealing them and refuses to explain, leading to questioning and custody amid fears of public scandal over national property. Holmes investigates and establishes that Mary took the coronet from the dressing-room and gave it to Sir George Burnwell. Arthur discovered the theft and recovered the coronet. Holmes retrieves the three gems, still attached to a triangular piece of gold, and returns them to Holder for a £4,000 check; the gems are concealed to avoid prosecution and scandal.

Narrative, section by section

XI. THE ADVENTURE OF THE BERYL CORONET

Alexander Holder alleges that Arthur stole three jewels from the coronet after finding him beside it at night. Arthur denies the theft and says that no jewels are missing, but he refuses to provide further information.

“By this time the whole house was astir, for I had raised my voice in

Arthur is questioned about whether he will be charged with theft. The narrator treats the matter as a public theft because the ruined coronet is national property, and Arthur is taken into custody.

“Do you receive much company?” he asked.

The stolen coronet gems are the three gems removed with a triangular piece of gold from the coronet. Sherlock Holmes finds them during his investigation and gives them to Mr. Holder in exchange for a £4,000 check.

“For Heaven’s sake, tell me, then, what is this extraordinary mystery!”

The coronet theft is carried out by Mary and Sir George Burnwell, with Mary taking the coronet from the dressing-room and handing it to him. Arthur discovers the theft and recovers the coronet, while Holmes establishes the truth and retrieves the missing gems.
