moral tests in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

Acer's moral tests judge justice and benevolence by whether public figures tenderly recognize disadvantaged, overlooked claimants' undefended claims, though the narrator warns these tests may be delusive.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

VIII.

Acer's moral tests focus on whether a public figure treats poorly placed or overlooked people tenderly and recognizes claims that others will not defend. The narrator replies that such tests may be as delusive as ignorant people's haphazard tests of intellect and learning.
