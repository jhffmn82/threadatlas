Aztec pito in Musical Instruments

Carl Engel · 1875-08

The Aztec pito is a reddish-pottery pipe, called pito by the Mexican Spaniards, resembling a flageolet and fitted with four finger-holes. Examined specimens varied from six to nine inches yet shared one pitch and a pentatonic interval order; it was used in religious observances and appeared in Aztec religious symbolism.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VI.

The Aztec pito was a reddish-pottery pipe resembling a flageolet and equipped with four finger-holes. Specimens examined by the writer, ranging from about six to nine inches in length, had the same pitch and an order of intervals conforming to the pentatonic scale; the instrument was used in religious observances and appeared in Aztec religious symbolism.
