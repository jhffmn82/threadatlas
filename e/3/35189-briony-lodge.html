Briony Lodge in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

Briony Lodge is Irene Adler’s two-story bijou villa on Serpentine Avenue in St. John’s Wood, close to the road with a garden behind it. Its notable features include a Chubb-locked door, a well-furnished sitting-room with long windows, a passage window reachable from the coach-house, and a sliding panel above the right bell-pull. It is the place Holmes investigates for a photograph, stages an operation by entering the sitting-room while feigning injury, and plans to revisit with the King. After Adler departs, the open, searched house yields her photograph and letter from behind the sliding shutter.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

I. A SCANDAL IN BOHEMIA

Briony Lodge, on Serpentine Avenue in St. John’s Wood, is Irene Adler’s address. Holmes records it as the place where he must investigate the photograph.

II.

Briony Lodge is Irene Adler's two-story villa, built close to the road with a garden behind it. Holmes examines its Chubb-locked door, sitting-room windows, passage window, and coach-house, and later selects it as the scene of his planned operation.

It was a quarter past six when we left Baker Street, and it still

Briony Lodge is Irene Adler's house and the location where the photograph is hidden. Holmes is carried into its sitting-room after pretending to be injured, and the doctor observes the events through an uncurtained window. Holmes intends to return there with the King the following morning.

III.

Briony Lodge is found with its door open after Irene Adler’s departure. Its furniture, shelves, and drawers are scattered as though Irene searched the house before fleeing, and Holmes finds her photograph and letter hidden behind a sliding shutter.
