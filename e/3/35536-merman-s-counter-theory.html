Merman's counter-theory in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

Merman's counter-theory concerns Magicodumbras and Zuzumotzis. Merman defends its fundamental correctness despite collateral mistakes, rejects minute learning as an obstacle to judgment, favors history and varied observation, and later sees its main idea adopted without credit.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Meanwhile, how did he like it?

Merman maintains that his counter-theory is fundamentally correct despite collateral mistakes. He argues that minute learning can obstruct clear judgment, especially concerning the Magicodumbras and Zuzumotzis, and that history and varied observation of people provide better preparation; he later sees the main idea behind his theory adopted and respected without acknowledgment of his role.
