the Tauri in Iphigenia in Tauris

Euripides · -0413/-0411

The Tauri are a savage people who possess Artemis’s heaven-fallen image and sacrifice strangers reaching their shores, actions contrasted with Orestes and Pylades’s bravery and unselfishness.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

PREFACE

The Tauri possess an image of Artemis that has fallen from heaven and sacrifice strangers who reach their shores. They are described as savage people, and the Taurian mob is contrasted with the bravery and unselfishness of Orestes and Pylades.

Taurian attendants in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Taurian attendants are portrayed as slavish barbarians, feared to harm or carry off Greek women; Iphigenia opposes their rule over free Greeks.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

IPH. Mother, do thou hear my words, for I perceive that thou art vainly

The barbarians are described as people who might harm or carry off women from prosperous Greece. Iphigenia argues that they should not rule the Greeks, because the barbarians are characterized as slavish while the Greeks are free.

Taurians in The Library

Apollodorus · 0001/0200

The Taurians, part of the Scythians, customarily kill strangers by throwing victims into sacred fire; they capture Orestes and Pylades for King Thoas, but Orestes escapes with his sister and the wooden image.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

ES 23 [᾿Αγαμέμνων δὲ καταντήσας εἰς Μυκήνας μετὰ

The Taurians are described as a part of the Scythians who murder strangers and throw them into a sacred fire. They capture Orestes and Pylades and bring them before King Thoas, but Orestes escapes with his sister and the wooden image.
