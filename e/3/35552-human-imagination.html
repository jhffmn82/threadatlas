human imagination in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

Powerful imagination begins as intense inward representation, not confusion of fact with fancy. Fed by susceptibility to minutiae of experience, it uses definite knowledge for creative inward construction, combines ideal and associative power with clear ordinary reality, and does not measure posterity by present poverty.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

opening

Powerful imagination is described as intense inward representation and creative construction fed by close attention to real experience. It is not habitual confusion of fact with fancy, but an ideal and associative power that remains compatible with clear recognition of ordinary reality.
