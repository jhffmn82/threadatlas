the journey across the desert in The Road to Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1909

The journey across the desert is a crossing that cannot be made on foot because the sands destroy living flesh. Johnny Dooit builds a sand-boat; wind drives it rapidly over the sand until it crashes at the rocks and ends on the grassy tableland.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

11. Johnny Dooit Does It

The desert crossing cannot be made on foot because the sands destroy living flesh. Johnny Dooit provides a proposed crossing method by building a sand-boat and telling the Shaggy Man to sail it.

12. The Deadly Desert Crossed

The journey across the desert begins when the wind catches the sand-boat's sail and drives it forward. The travelers move rapidly across the uneven sand, then reach the rocks at the desert's edge and crash onto the grassy tableland beyond.
