  • In The Seven Plays in English Verse, the blind stranger's death is a peaceful, mysterious, fortunate end in a foreign refuge; free from sickness, war, violence, or ordinary burial, he is taken from the Earth without a visible grave, and his resting place blesses and benefits Athens while harming Thebes and bringing victory to Thebes's enemies.
  • In Plays of Sophocles: Oedipus the King; Oedipus at Colonus; Antigone, Oedipus's death and burial secures the benefits offered to Theseus: the benefit will be realized when he is dead and Theseus has buried him, making this final service the guarantee of all the benefits his presence will bring.

the blind stranger's death in The Seven Plays in English Verse

Sophocles · -0449/-0429

The blind stranger's death is a peaceful, mysterious, fortunate end in a foreign refuge. Free from sickness, war, violence, or ordinary burial, he is taken from the Earth without a visible grave. His resting place blesses and benefits Athens while harming Thebes and bringing victory to Thebes's enemies.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

OEDIPUS AT COLONOS

The introductory account says that Oedipus's death in a foreign land will bring victory to the enemies of Thebes. An earlier oracle also promises that his troubled life will end after he reaches a place of refuge among revered Powers.

CHORUS.

The blind stranger's death is described as peaceful, mysterious, and free from sickness, war, or violence. He is taken from the Earth without a visible grave or ordinary burial, and the Chorus calls his end fortunate.
