Messenger

person · 3 works · 5 quoted facts · 2 connections
Also called: MESS., MESSENGER, man
  • In The Bacchae of Euripides, the Messenger, Pentheus’s mountain attendant and witness, reports Pentheus’s death after Dionysus summons the Maenads, who tear Pentheus apart despite his appeal to Agave.
  • In Hippolytus; The Bacchae, the Messenger reports Pentheus’s death to the Leader and Chorus, describing how Dionysus bends a pine tree for Pentheus to climb and reveals him to the Maenads, who tear him apart.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, the Messenger, Pentheus’s slave and attendant, reports Pentheus’s death on Cithaeron and the Maenads’ tearing him apart, then leaves to avoid Agave’s return.

Messenger in The Bacchae of Euripides

Euripides · -0406/-0405

Messenger, Pentheus’s mountain attendant and witness, arrives to report Pentheus’s death: Dionysus summons the Maenads, whose violence tears Pentheus apart despite his appeal to Agave.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHORUS.

The Messenger arrives from the mountain and reports that Pentheus is dead. He describes the expedition, Dionysus's summoning of the Maenads, Pentheus's attempted appeal to Agave, and the tearing apart of his body.
