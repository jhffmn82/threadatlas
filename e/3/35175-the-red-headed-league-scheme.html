the Red-headed League scheme in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

The Red-headed League scheme is a possible prank in Jabez Wilson’s view, but Sherlock Holmes suspects a serious purpose. Devised by John Clay and an accomplice, it uses an advertisement, temporary office, and £4-a-week copying job to draw Wilson from his shop. While he works, the accomplice digs a tunnel from the cellar to the bank, preparing a robbery. The scheme ends abruptly, costing Wilson his position.

The Red-headed League scheme gave Mr. Jabez Wilson a temporary position copying the Encyclopaedia and cost him the opportunity to earn four pounds a week when it ended. Wilson regards it as a possible prank costing its perpetrators two and thirty pounds, while Sherlock Holmes suspects that it concealed a more serious purpose.
