Nome King's tunnel in The Emerald City of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1910

Nome King's tunnel is a large, arched underground passage planned and built by King Roquat the Red and his workmen beneath the Deadly Desert. It is drilled through solid rock from the Nome King's cavern and domain toward the Emerald City, designed high and broad enough for the Nome army and its allies to bypass the desert, enter Oz without warning, and invade and destroy the country. As it is built, it reaches nearly halfway beneath the desert and is expected to be finished in three days. It then becomes nearly complete, leaving only a thin crust of earth before the gardens of the royal palace. Completed beneath Ozma's palace grounds, it ends in front of the Forbidden Fountain, with only earth separating the invaders from Oz. The Phanfasms, Growleywogs, Whimsies, the Nome King, General Guph, and the Nomes march through it toward the Emerald City. Dust becomes increasingly thick, filling their throats, eyes, and noses; it was placed there by Ozma through the Magic Belt and makes them cough and choke. The Nome King's army ultimately marches back through the tunnel to its underground home.

Narrative, section by section

11 sections; each heading opens the text

4. How The Nome King Planned Revenge

King Roquat plans to bore an underground tunnel from the Nome kingdom to the Emerald City. He says he will begin digging it while Guph makes arrangements with evil powers.

6. How Guph Visited the Whimsies

The tunnel is being built beneath the desert from the Nome King's mountains toward the Emerald City. It is designed to be high and broad enough for the Nome army to pass through and emerge without warning.

8. How the Grand Gallipoot Joined The Nomes

The Nome King is building a tunnel under the deadly desert so that the Nomes and their allies can march into the Land of Oz and reach the Emerald City.

11. How the General Met the First and Foremost

The tunnel to Oz is being built by King Roquat the Red's Nomes. The Phanfasms consider it a clever means of bypassing the deadly desert and plan to use it to lead their legions into Oz.

13. How the General Talked to the King

The tunnel is being drilled through solid rock beneath the desert toward the walls of the Emerald City. It is nearly halfway under the desert and is expected to be completed in three days.

18. How Ozma Looked into the Magic Picture

The underground tunnel is a large, arched passage being dug beneath the Deadly Desert toward the Emerald City. It is intended to let the army of Nomes march into the Land of Oz.

24. How the Tin Woodman Told the Sad News

The tunnel beneath the deadly desert is nearly complete, with only a thin crust of earth remaining at its end. It leads to the gardens of the royal palace in the Emerald City and is intended to carry the Nome King's invading forces into Oz.

25. How the Scarecrow Displayed His Wisdom

The Nome King's tunnel leads toward Oz and is part of his plan to invade and destroy the country. The Tin Woodman tells the Scarecrow about it when the travelers stop at the Scarecrow's mansion.

26. How Ozma Refused to Fight for Her Kingdom

The Nome King's tunnel is completed beneath Ozma's palace grounds and ends in front of the Forbidden Fountain. Only a crust of earth remains between the invaders and Oz, which they expect to break through easily.

27. How the Fierce Warriors Invaded Oz

The tunnel carries the Phanfasms, Growleywogs, Whimsies, the Nome King, General Guph, and the Nomes toward the Emerald City. It becomes increasingly dusty, filling the invaders' throats, eyes, and noses, although no one stops or turns back.

28. How They Drank at the Forbidden Fountain

The tunnel contains dust placed there by Ozma through the Magic Belt, causing the approaching invaders to cough and choke. It is also filled with the Nome King's army, which marches back through it to its underground home.
