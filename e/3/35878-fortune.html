human fortune and misfortune in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Human experience encompasses both fortune and misfortune; although troubles vary among people, none is wholly free from misfortune, like ships weathering unequal storms and Homer’s jars of blessings and woes.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIV.

The account argues that one person may suffer less trouble than another, but that no person can remain entirely free from misfortune. It compares human experience to ships facing different degrees of storm and to Homer’s two jars, one filled with blessings and the other with woes.
