  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books, Jason and Medea's marriage lasts ten years in Corinth, during which they live as husband and wife and have three children, before ending when Jason marries Glauce.
  • In The Argonautica, Jason and Medea's marriage occurs in Macris's sacred cave out of immediate necessity, with completion intended in Jason's father's hall at Ioleus; it enables Alcinous to refuse to surrender Medea to the Colchians.
  • In Medea, Jason and Medea's marriage is presented by Medea as involving Jason's false wooing, betrayal, and new marriage, while Jason describes Medea as a wife who made him suffer and Medea declares that neither Jason nor the princess was destined for a happy married life.
  • In The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I, Jason and Medea's marriage produces their sons and begins with Medea leaving her home and foreign land for Jason, but is destroyed by Jason's betrayal and new marriage to the princess, provoking hostility, Medea's revenge, and Jason's denunciation of the union.
  • In The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books I-VII, Jason and Medea's marriage is promised by Jason in return for her aid, and he returns to Iolcos with Medea as his wife after obtaining the Golden Fleece.
  • In The Library, Jason and Medea's marriage is a conditional agreement in which Medea will help Jason and give him the Golden Fleece, while Jason swears to marry her and take her to Greece.

Medea and Jason's marriage in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Medea and Jason’s marriage produces their sons and begins with Medea leaving her home and foreign land for Jason. It is later destroyed by Jason’s betrayal and new marriage to the princess, provoking hostility, Medea’s revenge, and Jason’s denunciation of the union as hateful and destructive.

JASON, MEDEA, CHORUS.

Medea recalls the children, care, and hopes connected with her marriage to Jason. Jason's decision to leave her for another union becomes the cause of their hostility and of the revenge she carries out.

JASON, CHORUS.

Jason and Medea’s marriage has been destroyed by Jason’s betrayal and new marriage. Medea recalls that she left her home and foreign land to marry Jason, while Jason calls the union hateful and destructive and blames her for its consequences.
