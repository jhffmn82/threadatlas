the compromising photograph in The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes

Arthur Conan Doyle · 1891-06-25

The compromising photograph is a cabinet-size photograph depicting Irene Adler with the King of Bohemia. It matters because the King fears Adler will send it to his intended bride’s family, threatening his planned marriage, while both have reason to conceal it. The King asks Holmes to recover it, making it central to the investigation. Holmes considers whether Adler gave it to Godfrey Norton, but its location is not then established. He later finds it hidden behind a sliding panel above the right bell-pull in the sitting-room at Briony Lodge, yet does not take it during the ruse.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

I. A SCANDAL IN BOHEMIA

The compromising photograph shows Irene Adler and the King of Bohemia together. The King believes Irene will use it to ruin him by sending it to the family of his intended bride, and he asks Holmes to recover it.

II.

The photograph is the object Holmes is trying to locate or recover in the investigation into Irene Adler. Holmes considers whether Adler may have transferred it to Godfrey Norton's keeping, but this unit does not establish where it is.
