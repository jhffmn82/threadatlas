Macaria in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Macaria, Hercules’ virgin daughter of noble birth, leaves her chamber after Iolaus’s lament and freely offers herself for death rather than be chosen by lot. Seeking heroic fame, she saves her brothers and Athens, requests proper rites and burial honors, and entrusts her family to survivors.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

DEMOPHOON, IOLAUS, CHORUS.

Macaria comes from her chamber after hearing Iolaus’s lament and voluntarily offers herself as the victim. She rejects selection by lot, saying that a free choice will preserve her merit, and asks to be conducted to death with proper rites. She entrusts her brothers and Alcmena to the survivors and requests that her brothers later honor her burial.

Macaria in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Macaria is a noble, unmarried daughter of Hercules by Deianira and sister of the children of Hercules. When an oracle requires one of Hercules’s descendants to be devoted to secure victory, she freely offers herself for sacrifice to Ceres rather than let others die for her family and the city. Rejecting selection by lot, she faces death willingly, entrusts her brothers to Iolaus, and is afterward commemorated in the name of Macaria’s fountain.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHOR. This land is ever willing to aid in a just cause those in difficulty;

Macaria comes forward and freely offers to die for her brothers and the city, arguing that it would be shameful for suppliants of Hercules to avoid death while others risk themselves for them. She rejects death by lot, asks to be killed by women if possible, and bids farewell while entrusting her brothers to Iolaus.

notes

Macaria, the daughter of Hercules by Deianira, voluntarily offered herself for sacrifice after the oracle required one of Hercules's descendants to be devoted. Her name was afterward given to a fountain.
