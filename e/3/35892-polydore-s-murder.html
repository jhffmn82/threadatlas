Polydore’s murder in Hecuba and other plays

Euripides · -0429~

Polydore’s murder is Polymestor’s killing of the boy Priam entrusted to him, motivated by Priam’s gold and violating hospitality. Polymestor throws the body into the sea; it reaches shore and is brought to Hecuba. Polymestor later confesses, Hecuba’s account convinces Agamemnon, and the murder prompts justice and revenge.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

HECUBA.

Polydore’s murder was committed by Polymestor, who wanted to keep the gold that Priam had sent with him. Polymestor then threw Polydore’s body into the sea.

CHORUS.

Polydore’s murder is attributed to the Thracian king, who kills the boy entrusted to his protection in order to obtain gold. His corse is cast onto the shore, where an attendant finds it and brings it to Hecuba.

CHORUS.

Polymestor confesses to the murder of Polydore, whom Priam had entrusted to him. Hecuba argues that he killed Polydore for gold and only after Troy had fallen, and Agamemnon finds this explanation convincing.

murder of Polydore in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

The murder of Polydore is committed by Polymestor, who had received him from Priam and Hecuba for protection. Polymestor kills him, allegedly to prevent the Greeks finding Priam’s surviving son, but Hecuba says greed for entrusted gold motivated him. He mutilates Polydore’s body, casts it into the sea untombed, and Hecuba seeks punishment.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

HECUBA.

Polymestor murders Polydore, despite having received him from Priam as a charge to protect. He kills him for the gold and casts his body into the sea.

FEMALE ATTENDANT, CHORUS, HECUBA.

Polymestor murders Polydore for the gold entrusted to him and casts the mutilated body into the sea without a tomb. Hecuba calls the act impious and asks Agamemnon to punish its perpetrator.

POLYMESTOR, HECUBA, CHORUS.

Polymestor murdered Polydore after receiving him from Priam and Hecuba. He claims that the murder was intended to prevent the Greeks from discovering a surviving son of Priam, while Hecuba argues that greed for the gold caused it.
