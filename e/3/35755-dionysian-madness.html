Dionysian madness in The Tragedies of Euripides, Volume I

Euripides · -0437

Dionysian madness is a delusion that distorts perception and mind: Bacchus imposes it on Pentheus, making him willingly don women's clothing and act a Bacchant; he sees two Thebes and a bull-like transformation before the madness leaves him to recognize his fate.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

MESSENGER. Pentheus, ruler o'er this Theban land, I come, having left

Bacchus intends to drive Pentheus out of his right mind so that he will willingly wear women's clothing. Pentheus then experiences distorted visions and behaves as though he has become a Bacchant.

notes

Dionysian madness causes delusion and distorted perception, as illustrated by Pentheus’s belief that he sees two Thebes and by his apparent transformation or perception as a bull. A passage from Nonnus cited in the notes describes Dionysus’s madness leaving Pentheus before he recognizes his fate.
