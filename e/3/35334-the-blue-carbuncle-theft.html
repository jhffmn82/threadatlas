VII. THE ADVENTURE OF THE BLUE CARBUNCLE

The blue carbuncle theft is attributed by the police-court evidence to John Horner, who was working in the Countess of Morcar's dressing-room when the jewel disappeared. The recovery of the stone in a goose leads Sherlock Holmes to investigate the sequence connecting the theft to Henry Baker.

“So much for Mr. Henry Baker,” said Holmes when he had closed the door

Ryder’s theft was the deliberate stealing of the Countess of Morcar’s blue carbuncle after he and Catherine Cusack helped implicate Horner. Ryder hid the stone in a goose but never recovered it because he took the wrong bird.
