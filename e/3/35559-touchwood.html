Touchwood in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

Touchwood is generally regarded as a good fellow, honourable gentleman, steadfast friend, defender of the oppressed, and affectionate-hearted person with high character, gifts, and abilities. Yet his chronic, severe, capricious, pugnacious temper dominates his conduct: he is insolent, quarrelsome, haughty, neglectful, antagonistic, harsh without provocation, and inconsistently opposed even to benevolent measures he sympathizes with. Though thought capable of excellent public service or a great book, his temper makes intimacy insupportable and raises doubt that he could preserve sound judgment and integrity as a public man.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

VI.

Touchwood is regarded as a good fellow, a steadfast friend, a defender of the oppressed, and an affectionate-hearted person, but his insolent, quarrelsome, haughty, neglectful, and antagonistic behaviour makes close intimacy insupportable. His bad temper causes him to contradict others, resist expectations, adopt opinions suddenly, and treat people harshly without provocation. He is thought capable of excellent public service or a great book, but the unit doubts whether his temper would allow him to maintain sound judgment and integrity in public life.
