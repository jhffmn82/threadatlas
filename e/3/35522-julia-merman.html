Julia Merman in Impressions of Theophrastus Such

George Eliot · 1879

Julia Merman is Merman’s supportive wife, affectionate and heroic amid domestic hardship. Guided by his ideas, she avoids disagreement and tries not to increase his troubles. As his controversy leaves the household with declining resources and he grows indifferent, she preserves the home’s charm, reduces wants, manages finances, and secretly sells her best wedding jewel to pay rent.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

III.

Julia Merman is Merman's supportive wife, described as a 'nice little woman' with discernment and wisdom. After Merman explains the importance of his new ideas, she avoids contradicting him and chiefly worries about increasing his troubles.

Meanwhile, how did he like it?

Julia tries to preserve the charm of home and soothe Merman's excitement while managing the household's declining resources. She sells the best jewel among her wedding presents to pay the rent without telling Merman that the sacrifice resulted from his unsuccessful controversy, and she remains affectionate and resigned despite his increasing indifference.
