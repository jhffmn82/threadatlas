Lausus in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Lausus is Mezentius’s youthful son, a pious, virtuous, heroic warrior skilled in horsemanship and hunting. Though his father is called unworthy, Lausus is worthy of a happier throne and is second only to Turnus in graceful appearance. He leads a thousand men and the Latins against Trojan forces, rallies fighters after a renowned warrior’s death, kills Abas, and meets Pallas’s troops; fate denies both youths a return home. Wearing Palmas’s crest and armour, Lausus shields his wounded father by standing between Mezentius and Aeneas. Aeneas kills him despite warning him to stop, then praises his courage and devotion. Mezentius mourns his beloved son.

Narrative, section by section

4 sections; each heading opens the text

And now the mighty labour is begun

Lausus, the youthful son of Mezentius, rides beside his father in the ranks. He is second only to Turnus in graceful appearance, is a skilled horseman and huntsman, and leads a thousand men despite having an unworthy father.

BOOK X

Lausus is not involved in an action in this unit, but the argument identifies him as a pious and virtuous youth whose episode and death will be treated.

But, in another part, th’ Arcadian horse

Lausus prevents panic among the enemy after the death of a renowned warrior and encourages the fight by his example. He kills Abas and leads the opposing forces as they meet Pallas’s troops. He and Pallas are described as equally youthful and beautiful, but fate forbids both from returning to their native lands.

Meantime, by Jove’s impulse, Mezentius arm’d,

Lausus protects his wounded father, Mezentius, by stepping between him and Aeneas and keeping Aeneas at a distance with the help of the surrounding troops. Aeneas kills Lausus after warning him to stop, and later praises his courage and filial devotion.

Lausus in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Mezentius's son defends his wounded father against Aeneas, is killed, and receives Aeneas's pity and funeral return.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

NISUS AND EURYALUS

Lausus, Mezentius's son, interposes himself when Aeneas attacks his wounded father. Aeneas kills him but pities him and orders that his body be returned to his friends for funeral honors.
