Harringford's father's letter in Gallegher and Other Stories

Richard Harding Davis · 1890

Harringford’s father writes to Young Harringford, offering peace, forgiveness, and a welcome home. Invoking the Prodigal Son, he says Cecil, his first-born, is dearer than the other ninety-nine and that the girl from Surrey still asks about him. In Monte Carlo, Harringford reads the letter for the last time and tears it up.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

"THERE WERE NINETY AND NINE" Young Harringford, or the "Goodwood Plunger," as he was perhaps better known at that time, had come to Monte Carlo in a very different spirit and in a very different state of mind from any in which he had ever visited the place before.

Harringford’s father’s letter urges him to come home and forgive the past, invoking the story of the Prodigal Son and promising him peace and welcome. It says that Cecil is the first-born, dearer to him than the other ninety-nine, and that the girl from Surrey still asks about him; Harringford reads it for the last time and tears it into pieces.
