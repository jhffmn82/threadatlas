Abstract

The unit concerns Retrieval-augmented Large Language Models (LLMs) and their use in knowledge-intensive scenarios. It introduces BlendFilter, an approach that combines query generation blending with knowledge filtering to address complex inputs and noisy retrieval. BlendFilter integrates external and internal knowledge with the original query and uses the LLM’s intrinsic capabilities to remove extraneous data. Experiments on three open-domain question-answering benchmarks show that BlendFilter significantly surpasses state-of-the-art baselines.

1

The unit concerns Generative Large Language Models and retrieval-augmented generation for improving answers to complex questions. It explains that direct retrieval can miss essential information when task inputs are multifaceted, motivating query decomposition, rewriting, and augmentation. It introduces BlendFilter, which blends original, external knowledge-augmented, and internal knowledge-augmented queries and filters irrelevant retrieved knowledge without an extra language model. Experiments on three open-domain question answering benchmarks show that BlendFilter surpasses baseline models across three backbones.

The unit reviews retrieval-augmented generation methods that use Large Language Models (LLMs) to interact more actively with retrievers. It categorizes LLM-assisted retrieval into question decomposition, query rewriting, and query augmentation, while describing limitations such as misleading sub-questions, training costs, and reliance on a single augmentation source. It also discusses knowledge filtering methods designed to prevent irrelevant or harmful retrieved knowledge from degrading answers. The figure presents BlendFilter as a framework combining internal and external knowledge augmentation, knowledge filtering, and answer generation.

The methodology uses a pre-trained Large Language Model, a knowledge base, and a retriever to improve responses without fine-tuning. BlendFilter combines query generation blending with knowledge filtering, using external and internal knowledge augmentation to produce enhanced queries. The unit evaluates BlendFilter against retrieval-augmented baselines on HotPotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and StrategyQA using GPT3.5-turbo-Instruct, Vicuna 1.5-13b, and Qwen-7b. BlendFilter consistently improves performance across backbones and datasets, and it also outperforms baselines when BM25 replaces ColBERT v2. Its query blending and knowledge filtering improve retrieval precision, recall, and S-Precision, while removing any of its original, externally augmented, or internally augmented queries reduces performance. Increasing the number of retrieved documents initially helps and then slightly hurts performance, while sampling multiple answers benefits all methods but produces smaller gains for BlendFilter.

5

The paper introduces BlendFilter, a framework for enhancing retrieval-augmented generation within LLMs. It combines query generation blending with knowledge filtering to address complex inputs and reduce noise in retrieved knowledge. Its self-reliant knowledge filtering module uses an LLM’s capabilities to remove extraneous content, while external and internal knowledge augmentation support retrieval. Experiments on three benchmarks show that BlendFilter outperforms state-of-the-art baselines and generalizes to GPT3.5-turbo-Instruct, Vicuna 1.5-13b, and Qwen-7b.

Limitations

The unit describes a limitation of the BlendFilter framework concerning the hyper-parameter K. K controls how many documents the framework needs to retrieve and may require additional tuning effort. However, model performance is not very sensitive to K, so the paper fixes it at a value that achieves good performance.
