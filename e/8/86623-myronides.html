Myronides in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Myronides, son of Callias, is an Athenian general. He commanded aid to Megara against Corinth, winning hard-fought victories over the Corinthians. Later, despite fewer soldiers than expected, he led Athenians against the Thebans, defeated them, captured and demolished Tanagra, and conquered much of Boeotia. He defeated Opuntians, Locrians, and Phocians, took hostages, failed to capture Pharsalia, and returned to Athens acclaimed.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. XIX.

Myronides commanded the Athenian forces sent to assist Megara against Corinth. His army defeated the Corinthians in a hard-fought battle and won another victory at Cimolia.

CHAP. XXIV.

Myronides led the Athenians against the Thebans despite having fewer soldiers than expected, arguing that those who appeared on the appointed day showed greater courage. He defeated the Thebans, captured and demolished Tanagra, ravaged Boeotia, overcame the Locrians and Phocians, besieged Pharsalus unsuccessfully, and returned to Athens with great acclaim.
