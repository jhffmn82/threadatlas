public education in writing

topic · 1 work · 1 quoted fact · 1 connection
Also called: all the children of the citizens should learn to write, education, instruction
  • In The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books: teaches writing [to all citizens' children] Charondas required all citizens’ children to learn writing, with the city paying the teachers’ wages. He considered writing especially important because it preserved votes, decrees, letters, wills, laws, knowledge, and the memory of the dead for future generations. The measure gave poor children access to instruction that their families could not otherwise afford.
