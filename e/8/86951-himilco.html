Himilco in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Himilco was a Carthaginian king and general appointed under Carthaginian law. He led the campaign against Dionysius, capturing Motya and Messana, defeating the Sicilian fleet near Catana, and besieging Syracuse. Disease afflicted his forces; after their defeat, he paid Dionysius’s agents, escaped to Carthage with few survivors, lived in disgrace, and killed himself in poverty.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

In the time of their governments, IMonyrfus, lord of Syracuse,

Himilco was made king by the Carthaginians and commanded the campaign against Dionysius. He captured Motya and Messana, destroyed Messana, defeated the Sicilian fleet near Catana, and brought his army and fleet before Syracuse. He ravaged the country around Syracuse, established forts, and prepared to maintain the siege, but disease later afflicted his forces.

The Carthaginians being thus routed both by sea and knd, seat

Himilco delivered three hundred talents to Dionysius’s agents and later filled forty galleys with Carthaginian citizens in an attempt to escape. He fled to Carthage with a few followers after abandoning the rest of the army, and afterward lived in disgrace, eventually murdering himself in extreme want and poverty.
