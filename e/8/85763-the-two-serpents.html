the two serpents

animal · 2 works · 5 quoted facts · 1 connection
Also called: A hideous serpent's fangs, Two serpents, serpents, serpents twain, the monsters, the serpents, their flaming crests, their speckled tails, those fearsome dragons, those monsters
  • In The Aeneid, the two serpents emerge from the sea as a terrifying portent, kill Laocoon and his children, then retreat to Minerva’s tower beneath her protection.
  • In The Fall of Troy, the serpents are deadly dragons of Typhon’s brood from Calydna, summoned by Athena and sent toward Troy; they terrify island and sea, kill Laocoon’s sons, then disappear beneath earth into Apollo’s temple in Pergamus.

the two serpents in The Aeneid

Virgil · -0028/-0018

Two serpents emerge from the sea as a terrifying portent, kill Laocoon and his children, then retreat to Minerva’s tower beneath her protection.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK II

The two serpents emerge from the sea and swim toward the shore, where they coil around Laocoon’s children and then around Laocoon himself. After killing them, the serpents retreat to Minerva’s tower and lie beneath her protection.
