Roman tribunes of the people in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

The Roman tribunes of the people are a magistracy of ten, created by a settlement. They hold sovereign authority over the city’s other magistrates, including the consuls, and protect the people’s liberty. Each year they must choose successors or face death by burning; if they cannot agree, they remain in office until successors are chosen.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

great and high birth, and the glory of their families, with inaaflierabk

The settlement required ten tribunes of the people to be chosen with sovereign authority over the other magistrates and power to protect the people's liberty. They were to appoint successors at the end of each year, under penalty of being burned alive if they neglected this duty; if they could not agree on a choice, they were to remain in office meanwhile.
