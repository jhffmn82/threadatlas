Amilcar in The Historical Library of Diodorus the Sicilian, in Fifteen Books

Diodorus Siculus · -0059/-0029

Amilcar, son of Hanno and Hannibal’s grandfather, was a Carthaginian general with great authority. Joined with Hannibal in command, he helped plan recruitment, then led an invasion of Sicily and besieged Himera by land and sea. At Himera, Gelon’s disguised horsemen killed him during a sacrifice and burned ships.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAP. U.

Amilcar was chosen as general of the Carthaginian forces and led the invasion of Sicily. Gelon sent horsemen disguised as Selinuntian allies into the Carthaginian naval camp, where they killed Amilcar during a sacrifice and set fire to the ships.

CHAP. XII.

Because Hannibal pleaded old age, Amilcar, son of Hanno and a member of the same family, was joined with him in command. Amilcar and Hannibal consulted together and arranged the recruitment of soldiers from several regions.
