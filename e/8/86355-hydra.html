Hydra in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Hydra is a mythical serpent-monster associated with Lerna’s marshes; its cut heads regrow, and Hercules destroys it, perhaps by draining marshes and burning thickets to end serpent replacement.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

EXPLANATION.

The Hydra of Lerna was the poetic form given to Hercules’s destruction of the serpents infesting the marshes of Lerna. The unit suggests that Hercules may have drained the marshes and burned the surrounding thickets, while the Hydra’s regenerating heads represented the replacement of the serpents.

hydra in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The hydra, a monster said to be reared beneath a plane-tree near the Amymone’s source, infested the river; it was larger than ordinary water-snakes, venomous, and described by Pisander as many-headed.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXXV.

The hydra was said to have been reared beneath a plane-tree near the source of the Amymone. The account suggests that it was larger than ordinary water-snakes and had highly venomous poison, while Pisander described it as having many heads.

Hydra in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Hydra ravages Argos from a swamp near Amymone; its nine heads include one immortal, and severed heads multiply until Hercules and Iolaus burn them away and bury the immortal head beneath a rock.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter XII

The Hydra lived in a swamp near the well of Amymone and ravaged the country of Argos. It had nine heads, including one immortal head; when Hercules struck off a head, two grew in its place, but with Iolaus's help he burned away the heads and buried the immortal one beneath a huge rock.
