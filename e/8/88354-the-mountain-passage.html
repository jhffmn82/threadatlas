the mountain passage in Glinda of Oz

L. Frank Baum · 1920

The mountain passage is a rock-cut, dark passage inside the mountain, wide enough for Dorothy and Ozma abreast. Its repeated flights rise and fall; every tenth step rings a warning bell, and it eventually reaches the summit.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Chapter Five

The mountain passage is cut through the rock and is only wide enough for Dorothy and Ozma to walk abreast. It becomes dark inside the mountain, follows the repeated up-and-down stair pattern, and eventually leads to the summit.
