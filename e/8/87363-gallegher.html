Gallegher is a young office-boy at the Press with extensive street knowledge and a strong interest in crime. He recognizes a man with a possibly artificial finger as Stephen S. Hade, follows him from the Kensington railroad station to Torresdale and the Eagle Inn, and telegraphs Detective Hefflefinger. He reports the discovery to Michael E. Dwyer and insists on accompanying him and Hefflefinger to the planned arrest.

Gallegher travels with the sporting editor and gives the cab driver directions to Torresdale. He arranges for the cab to have a clear escape route, enters Keppler’s barn through a window with Hefflefinger, watches the fight from a haymow, and helps identify and secure Hade. He praises Hefflefinger’s handling of the arrest and is told not to forget his share of the payment.

Gallegher takes Dwyer's notebook, tricks the officers guarding the gate by pretending that Keppler is his imprisoned father, and escapes in a cab. He drives through the night toward Philadelphia, evades two policemen with a false explanation, and is finally stopped by cabmen near the Press office.

Gallegher arrives wet, exhausted, and nearly unable to stand after being delayed and having his cab taken from him. He reports that Mr. Dwyer was arrested, says that Hade was captured, and delivers Mr. Dwyer's copy. He asks whether he is in time and proudly says that he beat the town.
