Phaon in Heroides and Amores

Ovid · -0024~/-0015~

Phaon is a young, beautiful man, praised by Sappho for a face, youth, kindness, and poetic genius that inspire her powers. Once her lover, he leaves her tears and kisses behind, departs toward the women of Sicily, and delays returning. Sappho portrays his heart as churlish and unmoved: his absence has taken away what others admired in her, while his return could restore her poetry; she fears he may cause her death.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

XV

Phaon has left Sappho without taking her tears or kisses and has gone toward the women of Sicily. Sappho praises his beauty, youth, and attractiveness, saying that even Apollo, Bacchus, Phoebus, Venus, and Mars could be drawn to him.

Cur tamen Actiacas miseram me mittis ad oras, 185

Phaon is described as more savage than cliffs or waves if he can endure having Sappho's death attributed to him. He has taken away what the Lesbian women once admired in Sappho, and his return would restore her poetic power.
