knowledge graph in REANO: Optimising Retrieval-Augmented Reader Models through Knowledge Graph Generation

Jinyuan Fang · 2024-08-11/2024-08-16

A knowledge graph stores relational information about real-world entities as knowledge triples, consisting of entities and relations. In REANO, it is generated from relevant retrieved passages for a question, addressing incompleteness in existing knowledge graphs such as Wikidata. The generated graph combines intra-context and inter-context relation triples, is treated as a latent variable, and is used with passages by an answer predictor, which selects relevant graph information to generate answers for ODQA.

knowledge graph G in SimGRAG: Leveraging Similar Subgraphs for Knowledge Graphs Driven Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Yuzheng Cai, Zhenyue Guo, Yiwen Pei, Wanrui Bian, Weiguo Zheng · 2025

A knowledge graph is a set of triples (h, r, t), with entity nodes in V and relations in R. It can be read by humans and, when human-understandable, interpreted by large language models; its schemas are presented as aligning with human cognition. It matters as an external knowledge source for retrieval-augmented generation, supporting tasks such as construction, completion, embedding, question answering, and fact verification, while its intricate structures challenge traditional pipelines. In SimGRAG, the knowledge graph begins as the input source of entities, relations, triples, and candidate nodes. Candidate subgraphs are retrieved and expanded through neighboring triples, then compared with a query-derived pattern graph P: entities and relations are embedded, and subgraphs isomorphic to P with small Graph Semantic Distance are sought. Experiments use movie, Freebase, sports-domain, and DBpedia knowledge graphs, including retrieval from one at a 10-million scale. The approach ends by aligning desired patterns with knowledge-graph structures, but depends on input graphs aligning with human cognition; domain-specific schemas can make pattern alignment and entity linking harder.

knowledge graphs in A Survey of Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Customized Large Language Models

Qinggang Zhang, Shengyuan Chen, Yuanchen Bei, Zheng Yuan, Huachi Zhou, Zijin Hong, Hao Chen, Yilin Xiao, Chuang Zhou, Junnan Dong, Yi Chang, Xiao Huang · 2025-09-29

Knowledge graphs are structured representations of entities, concepts, relations, and facts, often modeling domain concepts as nodes and semantic relationships as edges. They represent hierarchical relationships and complex knowledge dependencies, and can be extracted from corpora or drawn from domain-specific and general-purpose resources. In GraphRAG, they supply fine-grained information and relevant subgraphs for retrieval, logic-guided chain retrieval, reasoning-path planning, and multi-step reasoning over long spans. Combined with structured and unstructured sources, they support contextual understanding, graph construction, question answering, dialog generation, decision support, response verification, and factual refinement that can reduce hallucination. Their usefulness depends on timeliness, accuracy, completeness, and correct intermediate processing, while high-quality graph shortages and cross-domain ontology inconsistency remain limitations. Their deployment also raises privacy-preserving integration needs and requires efficient matching, traversal, reasoning, and specialized hardware for large-scale graphs.
