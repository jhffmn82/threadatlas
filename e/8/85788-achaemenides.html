Achæmenides in The Metamorphoses of Ovid, Books VIII-XV

Ovid · 0008~

Achæmenides, an Ithacan Greek, is deserted near Ætna after Polyphemus, survives hidden on wild food, then is rescued by a Trojan ship; he praises Æneas and recounts Polyphemus’ terrifying cannibalistic rage.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

FABLE IV. [XIV.154-247]

Achæmenides, an Ithacan, was left behind among the crags of Ætna when Ulysses and his companions fled from Polyphemus. He hid for many days, surviving on acorns, grass, and leaves, until a Trojan ship found and rescued him. He praises Æneas for preserving his life and relates the terror of witnessing Polyphemus’ rage and cannibalism.
