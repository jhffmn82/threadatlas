Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The grove of Pelops, traditionally dedicated by Hercules, lies north of the Temple of Zeus at Olympia. Enclosed, tree-planted, and filled with statues and offerings, it is entered from the west; black-ram sacrifices restrict entry to the temple.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XI.

The grove of Pelops lies north of the Temple of Zeus and extends from the middle of the temple toward its rear. It is enclosed by a stone wall, planted with trees, filled with statues and offerings, and entered from the west; sacrifices there include a black ram, whose flesh imposes restrictions on entry to the Temple of Zeus.
