oracles and ambiguous place-names in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Several cases show oracle misunderstandings caused by ambiguous place-names: Epaminondas read Pelagos as sea, Hannibal read Libyssa as Libya, and Athenians chose distant Sicily over a nearby hill.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XI.

The unit presents several cases in which people misunderstood an oracle because a place-name had more than one possible reference. Epaminondas interpreted Pelagos as the sea, Hannibal interpreted Libyssa as Libya, and the Athenians interpreted Sicily as the distant island rather than the small hill near Athens.
