Onatas the Æginetan in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Onatas, son of Micon and an Æginetan, made Hercules and Idomeneus among votive offerings at Olympia; an inscription credits him with many works, and he is ranked among the greatest sculptors since Dædalus and the Attic school.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXII.

Onatas the son of Micon is identified as the maker of the statue of Idomeneus among the Achæan votive offerings. An inscription on Idomeneus’ shield says that Onatas made that statue and many others, and the account ranks the Æginetan Onatas among the greatest sculptors since Dædalus and the Attic school.

Onatas in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Onatas, son of Mico, was a sculptor of Ægina. He made notable brazen statues of Demeter for Phigalia and Apollo for Pergamus, and, with Calynthus, votive offerings of the Tarentines. His Demeter followed an ancient painting or copy, perhaps aided by a dream.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XLII.

Onatas of Ægina, the son of Mico, made a brazen statue of Demeter for the people of Phigalia after discovering a painting or copy of the ancient statue, possibly with additional guidance from a dream. He was a contemporary of the Athenian Hegias and the Argive Ageladas.
