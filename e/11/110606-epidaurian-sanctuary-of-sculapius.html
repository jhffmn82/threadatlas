Epidaurian sanctuary of Æsculapius in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The Epidaurian sanctuary of Æsculapius is a walled sacred district where no births or deaths occur, though sacrifices are consumed. Centered on Æsculapius’s temple and ivory-and-gold statue, it includes a sleeping-place for suppliants, healing inscriptions, Rotunda paintings, Polycletus’s theatre, Artemis and Maleatian Apollo temples, stadium, fountain, mountains, and Antonine additions. Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXIV.

The Epidaurian sanctuary of Æsculapius is enclosed by walls, and no births or deaths take place within its precincts, although sacrifices are consumed there. It contains a temple and an ivory-and-gold statue of Æsculapius by Thrasymede, a sleeping-place for suppliants, the Rotunda with paintings by Pausias, healing inscriptions, a theatre designed by Polycletus, temples, a stadium, a fountain, and buildings added or restored by Antonine. The sanctuary also includes the mountains Titthion and Cynortion, with a temple of Maleatian Apollo, and records traditions of healing, including the restoration of Hippolytus to life.
