Tisamenus

person · 3 works · 13 quoted facts · 7 connections
Also called: Tisamenus of Elis, Tisamenus the son of Orestes, Tisamenus, son of Orestes
  • In The Library, Tisamenus is reigning over the Peloponnesians when the Heraclids attempt to return, and is later killed by them after their victory by land and sea.
  • In Description of Greece, Tisamenus is described both as Orestes and Hermione’s son who ruled Argos and Sparta until the Heraclidae expelled him and his sons to Achaia, and as Thersander’s Iamide son, a prophet whose oracle-reading brought five wartime victories.
  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Tisamenus is described as the son of Orestes and Thersander by Demonassa, king of the Achaeans after their expulsion, who fell fighting the Ionians and was buried at Helice before being reburied at Sparta.

Tisamenus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Tisamenus is described both as Orestes and Hermione’s son, who succeeded Orestes and ruled Argos and Sparta until the Heraclidæ expelled him and his sons to Achaia, and as Thersander’s Iamide son, a prophet whose oracle-reading brought five wartime victories.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XVIII.

Tisamenus, the son of Orestes and Hermione, succeeded Orestes as king. During his reign the Heraclidæ returned to the Peloponnese, expelled him from Lacedæmon and Argos, and drove him and his sons to Achaia with their army.

Tisamenus in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Tisamenus, son of Orestes and Thersander by Demonassa, became king of the Achæans after their expulsion from Argos and Lacedæmon. Unaffected by the Furies of Laius and Oedipus, he fell fighting the Ionians, was buried at Helice, then reburied at Sparta.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

Description of Greece, Volume II / BOOK VII.--ACHAIA. / CHAPTER I.

Tisamenus, son of Orestes, was king of the Achæans after the Dorians expelled them from Argos and Lacedæmon. He fell in the battle against the Ionians, was buried at Helice, and his remains were later moved by the Lacedæmonians to Sparta.
