Erythræ in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Erythræ was a former city in the Platæan district near Mount Cithæron. Founded by Erythrus, son of Rhadamanthys, it was first associated with settlers from Crete, then occupied by Lycians, Carians, Pamphylians, and other Greeks. Cleopus formed an Ionian colony there. It had temples of Hercules and Athene Polias, Hercules’ wooden raft, and Chalcis, whose sea baths were considered Ionia’s most beneficial.

Narrative, section by section

2 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER III.

Erythræ was founded by Erythrus, son of Rhadamanthys, and was initially associated with settlers from Crete. Lycians, Carians, Pamphylians, and other Greeks later occupied it, and Cleopus gathered people from the Ionian towns into a colony there.

CHAPTER V.

Erythræ possessed a temple of Hercules, a temple of Athene Polias, and a notable ancient work of art. It also had Chalcis, a promontory and sea baths considered the most beneficial in Ionia.
