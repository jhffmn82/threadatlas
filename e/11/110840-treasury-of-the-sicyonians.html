treasury of the Sicyonians in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

The treasury of the Sicyonians at Olympia was an offering built by Myron after his chariot victory. It had two brass chambers, one Doric and one Ionic, and contained Myanes armour, Pelops’ gold-hilted sword, Amalthea’s ivory horn, and a boxwood Apollo with a gilt head.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XVII.

The treasury of the Sicyonians was the offering of Myron, king of the Sicyonians, after his chariot victory in the 33rd Olympiad. It had Doric and Ionic chambers of brass and contained athletic equipment, armour dedicated by the Myanes, the sword of Pelops, the horn of Amalthea, and a boxwood statue of Apollo.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XI.

The treasury of the Sicyonians stands near the votive offering of the Tarentines, but it contains no money, like the other treasuries. Statues brought by the Cnidians stand beside it.
