Lichas in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Lichas, a Lacedæmonian son of Arcesilaus, entered and won an Olympic chariot-race as a Theban during Lacedæmonian exclusion, was scourged for binding the charioteer, prompted an invasion of Elis, and later erected a statue at Olympia.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

BOOK VI.--ELIS. PART II.

Lichas entered the chariot-race as a Theban while the Lacedæmonians were excluded, then bound the victorious charioteer with a riband. The umpires scourged him, and the Lacedæmonians under Agis invaded Elis because of this incident; Lichas later erected his statue at Olympia.
