Aristodemus in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Aristodemus is presented as a king and member of the Æpytidæ, with records linking him both to Lacedæmon and to the Messenians. After offering and killing his daughter for a required sacrifice, he becomes king of the Messenians following Euphaes’s death. In the first Messenian War, he commands and deploys the Messenian forces from Ithome, seeks and interprets an omen from Delphi, then despairs of saving Messenia and kills himself at his daughter’s grave after six years and a few months. Another account says Pylades’ sons killed him.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

BOOK III.--LACONIA.

Aristodemus was the father of Procles and Eurysthenes and died at Delphi before the Dorians returned to the Peloponnese. The text also reports a tradition that he was murdered by the sons of Pylades and Electra.

CHAPTER VI.

Aristodemus, a member of the Æpytidæ, offers his daughter for the divinely required sacrifice. After killing her in anger over a false pregnancy claim, he is nevertheless elected king of the Messenians after Euphaes’s death.

CHAPTER XI.

Aristodemus commanded the Messenian forces in the later stages of the first Messenian War, positioning the heavy-armed troops and concealing light-armed troops, slingers, and archers in Ithome. He later sent to Delphi, interpreted the omen concerning Ophioneus, and, believing that there was no hope of saving Messenia, cut his throat at his daughter’s grave after a reign of six years and a few months.
