Sulla in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Sulla led the Roman capture of Athens during war against Mithridates, shut adversaries in the Ceramicus to be decimated by lot, was blamed for savagery, and burned the original imitation of Xerxes’ tent.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIX.

Sulla led the Roman attack that captured Athens after the city supported Mithridates. He shut Athenian adversaries in the Ceramicus, ordered them to be decimated by lot, and was blamed for treating many Athenians with exceptional savagery; he also burned the original imitation of Xerxes’ tent.

Sulla in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Sulla is a person and military leader in a war with Mithridates. He begins the war, invades Bœotia, and punishes the Thebans despite their attempt to renew friendship with Rome by taking half of Thebais. He takes votive offerings from Olympia, Epidaurus, and Delphi for his troops, gives half of Thebais to the gods in return, and offers a statue of Dionysus. He also removes an old ivory Athene statue, attacks Greek cities, and erects a trophy at Chæronea after defeating Taxilus and Mithridates’ army. At life’s end, he is afflicted with lice disease.

Narrative, section by section

3 sections; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER VII.

Sulla invaded Bœotia during the war with Mithridates, causing panic among the Thebans when they repented of their alliance and sought renewed friendship with Rome. He remained angry with them, took half their territory, and gave the gods half of Thebais in exchange for the votive offerings he had distributed to his troops.

CHAPTER XXXIII.

Sulla removed the old ivory statue of Athene from the temple at Alalcomenae. The text criticizes his cruelty toward the Athenians, Thebans, and Orchomenians, and says that after attacking Greek cities and their gods he was afflicted with a disease that covered him with lice.

CHAPTER XL.

Sulla erected a trophy at Chæronea for victory over Taxilus and the army of Mithridates.
