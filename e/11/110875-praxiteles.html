  • In Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II, Praxiteles is a maker of statues, including the Pentelican marble Eros and Hera and the stone Aphrodite and Phryne, and is Phryne’s lover.

Praxiteles in Description of Greece

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Praxiteles created or was credited with the Satyr and Cupid and designed the Brauronian Artemis statue; he especially prized the Satyr and Cupid, reportedly revealing them as his finest works to Phryne.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XIX.

Praxiteles was credited with a Satyr and with the design of the statue of Brauronian Artemis. He reportedly prized his Satyr highly and accidentally revealed to Phryne that his finest works were the Satyr and Cupid.

Praxiteles in Pausanias' Description of Greece, Volume II

Pausanias · 0155~/0175~

Praxiteles is a maker of statues, notably Pentelican marble Eros and Hera and stone Aphrodite and Phryne; he is Phryne’s lover, and her ingenious trick concerning him is noted.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

CHAPTER XXVII.

Praxiteles made a Pentelican marble statue of Eros and stone statues of Aphrodite and Phryne. The account also refers elsewhere to Phryne's ingenious trick concerning him.

Praxiteles in Bulfinch's Mythology: The Age of Fable

Thomas Bulfinch · 1855

Praxiteles was a Greek sculptor of the later Greek type, known through the surviving Hermes of Olympia and copies of other works. His art moved beyond Pheidias’s restraint toward sensual loveliness, graceful curves, tender figures, and gods portrayed through human experience.

Narrative, section by section

1 section; each heading opens the text

In Athens, after Pheidias, the greatest sculptures were those

Praxiteles is known through the surviving Hermes of Olympia and copies of several other works. His art departed from the restraint of Pheidias through sensual loveliness, tender and effeminate figures, graceful feminine curves, and gods conceived as figures of human experience.
