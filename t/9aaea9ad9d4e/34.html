This section, in brief. Penthesilia, daughter of Otrere and Ares, accidentally killed Hippolyte, was purified by Priam, and later killed many in battle before Achilles killed her; Achilles then killed Thersites after falling in love with her after death. Memnon came to Troy with a large force of Ethiopians, killed many Achaeans including Antilochus, and was killed by Achilles, who was later fatally shot in the ankle by Alexander and Apollo. Achilles was buried with Patroclus in the White Isle, and games were held in his honour. Ajax and Odysseus then competed for Achilles’ arms; Odysseus won, Ajax went mad, slaughtered cattle and their herdsmen, and killed himself.

The contest between Ajax and Ulysses for the arms of Achilles was also related in the Aethiopis of Arctinus. See Epicorum Graecorum Fragmenta, ed. G. Kinkel, p. 84. It was known to Homer (Qd. xi. 542 sqq.), who tells us that the Trojans and Pallas Athena acted as judges and awarded the arms to Ulysses. A Scholiast on this passage of Homer (v. 547) informs us that Agamemnon, unwilling to undertake the invidious duty of deciding between the two competitors, referred the dispute to the decision of the Trojan prisoners, inquiring of them which of the two heroes had done most harm to the Trojans. The prisoners decided that Ulysses was the man, and the arms were therefore awarded to him.
