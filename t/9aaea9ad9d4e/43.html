This section, in brief. Ulysses passes the Sirens, Scylla, and Charybdis, reaches Thrinacia, loses his ship after his companions slaughter the Sun’s cattle, and eventually arrives at Ogygia and then among the Phaeacians. Calypso keeps Ulysses for five years, after which he reaches Ithaca and finds suitors consuming his property and pressing Penelope to marry. With the help of Telemachus, Eumaeus, and Philoetius, Ulysses kills the suitors and returns to Penelope and Laertes. He later follows Tiresias’s directions, propitiates Poseidon among the Thesprotians, and remains there with Callidice before returning to Ithaca.

The prophet, according to this account, was a fisherman who, long buffeted by storms, conceived a horror of the sea, and, putting an oar on his shoulder, took to the hills and walked till he met a man who did not know what an oar was. There the prophet planted his oar in the ground, and there he resolved to abide. That is why all the prophet’s chapels are on the tops of hills.
