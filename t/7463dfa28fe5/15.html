This section, in brief. The unit explains why Egyptians worship crocodiles and other creatures, offering several accounts involving protection, gratitude, political division, and ancient tribal standards. It describes Egyptian religious divisions, laws, and funerary customs, including three grades of burial, bodily preparation, mourning, and judgment before burial. The deceased may be accused before forty judges at a lake crossing governed by Charon, while the successful dead are praised for their piety, justice, chastity, and other virtues. The account contrasts these visible Egyptian practices with what it presents as the Greeks’ largely fictional teachings about rewards and punishments after death.
