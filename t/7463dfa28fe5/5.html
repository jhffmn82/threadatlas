This section, in brief. The unit presents Egyptian priests' and Egyptians' accounts of Osiris, Isis, their worship, and the antiquity of Egyptian history, contrasting them with Greek traditions. It claims that Orpheus transferred Egyptian accounts of Osiris and Bacchus into Greece, associating Dionysius with the Greek story of Semele, Cadmus, and Jupiter. It distinguishes an ancient Egyptian Hercules from the later son of Alcmena and attributes to Egypt the origins of several Greek figures, rites, customs, and colonies. It also describes Isis's healing powers, the reigns and chronology of Egyptian gods, and Egyptian claims about colonization, while noting that many of these reports lack sufficient evidence.
