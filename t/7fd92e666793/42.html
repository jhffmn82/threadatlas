This section, in brief. The notes explain passages, textual difficulties, and mythological parallels in the Bacchae, a play attributed to Euripides. They discuss Bacchanalian rites, including drums, cymbals, ivy, animal skins, dancing, and initiation, as well as Dionysus’s associations with prophecy, night, caves, and human sacrifice. The central catastrophe concerns Pentheus, whose madness and delusion lead to his death and dismemberment by Agave and the Mænads. The notes compare the story with accounts by Hyginus, Ovid, Oppian, Nonnus, and other ancient and later authors.
