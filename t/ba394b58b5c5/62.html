This section, in brief. The unit praises Hestia as honored in the dwellings of deathless gods and earth-walking men. It says mortals hold no banquet without pouring sweet wine to Hestia first and last. Hermes is invoked as the slayer of Argus, son of Zeus and Maia, messenger of the blessed gods, bearer of the golden rod, and giver of good. Hestia and Hermes are asked to dwell together in the glorious house and aid the wisdom and strength of men.

XXIX. TO HESTIA (ll. 1-6) Hestia, in the high dwellings of all, both deathless gods and men who walk on earth, you have gained an everlasting abode and highest honour: glorious is your portion and your right. For without you mortals hold no banquet,—where one does not duly pour sweet wine in offering to Hestia both first and last. (ll. 7-10) 2533 And you, slayer of Argus, Son of Zeus and Maia, messenger of the blessed gods, bearer of the golden rod, giver of good, be favourable and help us, you and Hestia, the worshipful and dear. Come and dwell in this glorious house in friendship together; for you two, well knowing the noble actions of men, aid on their wisdom and their strength. (ll. 12-13) Hail, Daughter of Cronos, and you also, Hermes, bearer of the golden rod! Now I will remember you and another song also.
