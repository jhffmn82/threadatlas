This section, in brief. The unit recounts Agathocles's rise from obscure origins to military prominence and his seizure of power at Syracuse. After gaining command of an army, Agathocles used a supposed threat from the six hundred to incite his soldiers against them and their supporters, leading to a massacre in which more than four thousand people were killed and more than six thousand exiled. He then presented himself as a protector of the democracy, secured sole and absolute command, and ruled Syracuse as a monarch while maintaining a conciliatory public manner. The unit also reports contemporary events in Sicily and Italy, including Roman operations against the Samnites and conflicts involving Crotona and the Brutians.
